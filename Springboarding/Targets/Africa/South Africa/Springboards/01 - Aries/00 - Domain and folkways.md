## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nguni speakers (amaZulu, amaXhosa, amaSwati, amaNdebele):** check whether clan-name address (isiduko, sibongo) and personal names carry the same weight in each, since naming is the Aries folkway.
- **Sotho-Tswana peoples (Basotho, Batswana, Bapedi):** compare the seriti and botho vocabularies with Nguni terms before treating "personhood" as one idea.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga of Limpopo:** separate their naming and initiation sequences, often grouped with neighbors in older surveys.
- **Khoe and San (‡Khomani, Nama, Griqua, !Xun and Khwe):** look at how revival movements and archival accounts each frame the person, kept apart.
- **Coloured communities and Cape Malay families (Western and Northern Cape):** trace naming lines back through Cape slavery and Cape Muslim naming at once.
- **Afrikaners and English-speaking South Africans:** compare family-name, Bible-name and grandparent-name customs across rural Free State and Natal settler records.
- **Jewish, Portuguese and Chinese South Africans:** test how a heritage name sits beside an anglicized one in each community's own records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for naming and Nguni or Sotho praise forms; mixed for measured self-concept; thin for VhaVenda, Vatsonga and amaNdebele outside art history. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; this sign's sheets are written for the present South African lands.
- **Languages needed:** all twelve listed, plus archival Dutch (VOC-era Cape records) and German (mission sources on Bapedi and Batswana).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race-classification history is painful; initiation knowledge (ulwaluko, koma, domba) is closed to outsiders; San material falls under the South African San Institute's San Code of Research Ethics *check*.
- **Held for review:** sources on how South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands define the person and the body, set aside under the standing rule.
