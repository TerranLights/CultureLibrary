*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Married women in rural KwaZulu-Natal and Eastern Cape homesteads:** check where hlonipha speech still applies and where it has lapsed, since it sets limits on direct claims toward in-laws.
- **Batswana and Bafokeng communities in North West:** look at who may speak, and in what order, at the kgotla, the forum that frames public claims.
- **Basotho of the Free State and Bapedi of Sekhukhuneland:** compare the self-praise of initiation graduates with chiefly praise, noting what of it is closed to outsiders.
- **Kaaps-speaking Coloured communities of the Cape Flats:** trace language pride, hip hop and theater as routes of claiming voice.
- **Afrikaans and English school systems:** compare what each school culture rewards in oratory and debate.
- **Vatsonga and VhaVenda listeners in Limpopo:** test whether request and complaint genres on their stations differ from the Nguni and Sotho ones.
- **Township and suburban workplaces in Gauteng:** check how "speaking up" is coached in corporate training against shop-steward practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for praise poetry and protest song; mixed for measured assertiveness; thin for Tshivenda and Xitsonga talk genres beyond radio.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for hlonipha and praise; Setswana for kgotla speech; Kaaps beside standard Afrikaans, since the contrast is itself the topic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest assertion ties to recent violence; initiation-graduate praises may be closed knowledge; workplace power talk touches labor disputes (rules to Libra, legal culture to Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** oratory, protest and complaint traditions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; not scouted here, awaiting the author's call.
