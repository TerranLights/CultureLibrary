## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bafokeng and other Batswana communities in North West:** check how a group's control over land and mineral income bears on each member's own choices.
- **Orania residents and other Afrikaans-speaking towns in the Northern Cape and Free State:** separate group self-rule from personal latitude inside that group.
- **Young women at the umhlanga reed dance and urban youth in eThekwini and Pietermaritzburg:** look into who decides on bodily practices in each setting.
- **amaMpondo households in the Eastern Cape with migrant earners:** check how decisions move when the main earner works in Gauteng mines or cities.
- **Vatsonga families in Limpopo:** compare the Junod-era record with present panel data, reading the early source for its biases.
- **Live-in domestic workers in Gauteng and Western Cape suburbs:** test how much of their time and space is their own, by their own accounts.
- **Cape Muslim congregations in Bo-Kaap and the Cape Flats:** see how far a mosque runs itself and where wider ulama bodies decide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for older ethnography of family authority and for panel data; mixed for bodily autonomy; thin for Tshivenda and siSwati sources on personal choice.
- **Languages needed:** Setswana for Bafokeng records; isiZulu for umhlanga debates; Afrikaans for Orania's own material; Xitsonga to read Junod's terms against present usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** virginity testing, ukuthwala and reproductive choice are contested and painful; the rules on them go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); Orania and traditional authority over land are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** household decision-making and marriage-choice studies centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not used here.
