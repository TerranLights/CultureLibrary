## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Township traders in Soweto, Khayelitsha and Mamelodi:** check whether people describe a start as need or as opportunity, using GEM's split and their own words.
- **Rural households of Limpopo and Mpumalanga (Bapedi, VhaVenda, Vatsonga, amaSwati):** look for whether calling a work party still happens and who calls it.
- **Afrikaner farm districts of the Free State and North West:** compare the old group self-help bodies with present farmers' cooperatives and study groups.
- **Cape Flats and Bo-Kaap troupe and choir families:** trace how leadership of a troupe passes or is seized, as a measure of acting first.
- **Mission-educated amaKholwa in KwaZulu-Natal and Barolong in the Northern Cape:** set the founder biographies against each other, checking the role of church networks.
- **Women in informal trade and stokvels across provinces:** separate the initiative of club organizers from that of sole traders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for entrepreneurship surveys and township-economy studies; mixed for civic volunteering; thin for work-party customs today.
- **Languages needed:** Setswana and Sesotho for letsema; isiXhosa for the Peires sources' oral traditions; Afrikaans for self-help movement papers; Kaaps for troupe lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informal trading meets bylaws and xenophobic attacks on foreign-born traders (rules to Libra); taxi-industry conflict can be violent; trader data may expose people.
- **Held for review:** any enterprise or civic-initiative source from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands stays parked under the standing rule.
