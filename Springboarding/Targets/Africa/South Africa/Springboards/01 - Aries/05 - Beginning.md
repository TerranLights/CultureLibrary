## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Basotho and Batswana against Nguni peoples:** compare origin-place stories (Ntsoana-Tsatsi, the reed bed) to see how each places its beginning.
- **Slave-descended and Cape Malay families of the Western Cape:** check how 1 and 2 January divide between them and what each day opens.
- **Afrikaans-speaking communities:** trace how 1838 and 1938 founding commemorations are kept, changed or dropped since 1994.
- **English-speaking families of the Eastern Cape:** look at how the 1820 story is told now by descendants, schools and museums.
- **Khoe and San descendants (Nama, Griqua, ‡Khomani):** examine "who was here first" claims in heritage forums, kept apart from colonial narratives.
- **Zionist and Nazarite congregations against mainline churches:** compare baptism and conversion as fresh starts.
- **Chinese South Africans in Johannesburg and Gqeberha:** check how Lunar New Year sits beside 1 January in community calendars.
- **Removed communities of District Six, Sophiatown and South End:** compare how families describe starting again elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century origin texts (read for collectors' framing) and removal histories; mixed for present new-year customs; thin for Tshivenda and Xitsonga seasonal openings.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Callaway's texts; Sesotho for clan origins; Khoekhoegowab terms in Hahn; 17th-century Dutch for the *Daghregister*; Kaaps for Tweede Nuwejaar lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "first arrival" claims bear on land restitution (rules to Libra); founding days such as 16 December are contested; initiation as a new start is partly closed knowledge.
- **Held for review:** arrival narratives and new-year festivals of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept back here.
