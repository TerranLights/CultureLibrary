## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiXhosa-speaking families in the Eastern Cape:** check whether initiation endurance and wartime bravery are named with the same words.
- **Bapedi of Sekhukhuneland, VhaVenda and Ndzundza amaNdebele:** compare how each keeps its own 19th-century resistance in praise, monuments and schooling.
- **Afrikaner families with 1899–1902 war memory:** trace whether "hendsopper" and "bittereinder" still carry weight, and in which districts.
- **Migrant mine-worker communities in North West and Gauteng compounds:** look at endurance talk underground against home-village courage terms.
- **!Xun and Khwe at Platfontein near Kimberley:** examine how this community frames its members' past military service, a contested history.
- **Women's organizations in Gauteng and the Eastern Cape:** see whether women's endurance is called courage, using 1956 and later commemorations.
- **Coastal volunteers in the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal:** check which rescues are honored and how citations word bravery.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and struggle histories; mixed for civilian bravery records; thin for Tshivenda and isiNdebele resistance praise in print.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for iimbongi; Afrikaans for Boer war memory; Sepedi and Tshivenda for resistance traditions; isiNdebele for Nyabela.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation deaths and injuries; apartheid-era military service, including San soldiers; struggle violence and its victims on all sides.
- **Held for review:** resistance heroes and wartime memoirs belonging to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, withheld per the rule.
