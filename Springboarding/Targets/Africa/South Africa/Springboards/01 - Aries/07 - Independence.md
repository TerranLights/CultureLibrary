## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Residents of the former Transkei, Bophuthatswana, Venda and Ciskei:** check how people now speak of the homeland "independence" years, and whether the word itself carries a stain.
- **Grant-receiving households in rural Eastern Cape and Limpopo against wage earners in Gauteng:** compare how taking help is described and justified.
- **Afrikaner families with poor-white history in the Free State and Karoo:** look for how that memory shapes talk of not needing help.
- **Western Cape residents of every background:** separate who backs or rejects regional independence, without assuming a group's position.
- **Khoe and San councils in the Northern and Western Cape:** examine collective self-reliance projects alongside individual livelihoods.
- **Bafokeng women and other migrant women in North West towns:** trace household independence gained through earning away from home.
- **amaSwati and Vatsonga homesteads in Mpumalanga:** measure how much food comes from their own gardens and fields against purchase.
- **Jewish communal institutions in Johannesburg and Cape Town:** compare internal welfare with reliance on public services.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for grants, poverty inquiries and homeland histories; mixed for self-provisioning; thin for siSwati and Xitsonga terms on self-reliance.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for the 1932 inquiry and farm press; Setswana for Bafokeng sources; isiXhosa for Transkei and Ciskei memory; Tshivenda for Venda.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "dependency" talk can stigmatize grant recipients; homeland collaboration is a sore subject; secession talk is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** welfare, self-help and remittance studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set apart under the rule.
