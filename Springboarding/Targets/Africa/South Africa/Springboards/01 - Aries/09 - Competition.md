## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hostel-based isicathamiya and ngoma teams in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng:** check how judging, prizes and home-district loyalty are organized.
- **Black rugby and cricket clubs of the Eastern Cape:** trace rivalries older than unified sport, from club minutes and memory.
- **Afrikaans and English high schools:** compare how inter-school rugby and Craven Week rank boys and schools.
- **Coloured and Cape Malay sports bodies in the Western Cape:** look at the SACOS-era refusal to play under apartheid as a choice about competing at all.
- **Basotho and Batswana morabaraba players:** test how village rules differ from formal mind-sport rules.
- **VhaVenda children:** compare Blacking's recorded games with games now played in Limpopo schools.
- **Free State and KwaZulu-Natal farming families:** examine agricultural-show prizes as a status contest among neighbors.
- **Jewish sports clubs (Maccabi South Africa):** check how competing for a community and for the country relate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** sport, choral and school-exam rivalry are well documented; Indigenous games and Tshivenda or Xitsonga contest terms are thinly covered.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for isicathamiya and ngoma; isiXhosa for Eastern Cape clubs; Sesotho for morabaraba; Afrikaans for school sport and farm shows.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** apartheid-era sport segregation and boycotts; doping and match-fixing cases; business rivalry rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** sporting clubs, school rivalries and contests run within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted pending the author.
