# South Africa: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English; Khoekhoegowab for possessive grammar. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hostel dwellers and their rural homesteads (Gauteng and the Eastern Cape):** check how belongings are split between the city bed and the rural home, and which side holds the valued things.
- **Families uprooted in apartheid-era removals:** compare District Six, Sophiatown and Northern Cape removals for what each kept as proof of the home lost.
- **VhaVenda households of Limpopo:** look into whether items such as drums and cloth (minwenda) are held personally or for the lineage.
- **Cape Muslim and Coloured families of the Cape Flats:** examine the front-room display cabinet and its contents in memoirs and house museums.
- **Afrikaner farm families of the Karoo and Free State:** trace heirloom furniture, Bibles and kis chests passed through generations.
- **‡Khomani and other San communities:** set the film's outsider portrait against how the communities themselves speak of sharing and keeping, recorded with consent.
- **English-speaking and Jewish South Africans of Johannesburg suburbs:** study security walls and insurance inventories as the guarded side of possession.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theft statistics, museums and removal testimony; mixed for everyday belongings; thin for siSwati- and isiNdebele-speaking households.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for impahla and the spirit-possession sense; Sesotho and Setswana for thepa and dithoto; Afrikaans for heirloom and museum labels.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spirit possession and diviners' callings are sacred and often private; removals testimony carries grief; San communities set research terms under their own code.
- **Held for review:** keepsake and memory-object studies among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted here by rule.
