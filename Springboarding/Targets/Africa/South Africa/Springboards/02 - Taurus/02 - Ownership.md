# South Africa: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Xitsonga, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab and N|uu for Khoe and San land vocabulary; archival Dutch for the VOC period. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Households on Ingonyama Trust land and freehold owners in the same KwaZulu-Natal districts:** check how each speaks of "my land" when one holds a permit and the other a deed.
- **Royal Bafokeng and Bakgatla communities of North West:** compare how mineral income is claimed as collective property.
- **Makuleke and other restored communities of Limpopo:** trace what a returned title means when the land is not lived on.
- **Mission-station residents of Genadendal, Elim and Mamre:** examine church-held plots and how residents describe their own claim to them.
- **!Xun, Khwe, ‡Khomani and Nama communities:** set title to land beside claims to plant knowledge and to heritage sites, kept apart by community.
- **Afrikaner farm families and Orania residents:** separate family title from share-block membership as two routes to owning.
- **Chinese South Africans:** look for family accounts of holding shops and homes when they could not hold title in their own names.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land audits, tenure studies and land disputes; mixed for Indigenous knowledge claims; thin for everyday talk of owning goods outside land.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Ingonyama Trust testimony; Setswana for Bafokeng sources; Xitsonga for Makuleke records; archival Dutch for the VOC journal; Afrikaans for Orania and farm-family sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land is the most charged topic in the nation; claims are often in dispute; San knowledge is governed by community protocols. The legal rules of title and expropriation belong to Libra (the letter of the law) and Capricorn (its spirit), not here.
- **Held for review:** land- and shop-holding histories of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (not scouted).
