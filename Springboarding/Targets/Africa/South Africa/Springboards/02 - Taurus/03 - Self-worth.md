# South Africa: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English, Xitsonga; Khoekhoegowab for Nama and Griqua heritage sources. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Initiated and uninitiated young men among amaXhosa and Basotho:** check what public standing changes after initiation, using only what communities share openly.
- **Domestic workers from Lesotho-border, Eastern Cape and Limpopo villages working in Gauteng suburbs:** compare how they describe their own worth with how employers addressed them.
- **Coloured communities of the Cape Flats:** follow the respectability vocabulary (ordentlik) and the skollie label as tools that raise or lower a person.
- **Zulu-speaking migrant workers in Johannesburg hostels:** study weekend dress and display as a source of standing apart from the job.
- **Afrikaans-speaking men in former mining and railway towns of the Free State:** look at how job losses after 1994 are narrated in terms of worth.
- **Khoe and San revival groups in the Eastern and Northern Cape:** see how restored names and remains are used to speak of a people's worth.
- **Vatsonga workers and their families in Mpumalanga:** examine whether outsider names applied to them carried a slight that they answer in their own terms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** political writing and public testimony on dignity should be plentiful; scale-based self-esteem studies come in patches; personal worth as discussed in Tshivenda or siSwati will be hard to find.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for imbongi poetry and initiation talk; isiZulu for migrant-worker interviews; Afrikaans and Kaaps for respectability language and post-1994 Afrikaner writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation knowledge (ulwaluko, lebollo) is closed; suicide and mental-health data need care; Sarah Baartman's history is a wound for Khoe communities and should be handled on their terms.
- **Held for review:** dignity and self-image studies focused on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back by rule.
