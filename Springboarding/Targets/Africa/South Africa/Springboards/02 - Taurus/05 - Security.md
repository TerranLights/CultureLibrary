## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Residents of Gauteng security estates and township street committees:** check which protections are bought and which are arranged among neighbors.
- **Coloured neighborhoods of the Cape Flats (Manenberg, Hanover Park, Mitchells Plain):** follow how parents plan children's routes and hours around gang territory.
- **Farm owners and farm workers in Limpopo and the Free State:** take each side's account of rural danger separately, since each fears different things.
- **Grandmothers raising grandchildren on old-age grants in the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal:** see how one grant secures a whole household.
- **Basotho and amaMpondo mineworker families:** trace savings and remittances from the mines as protection for the rural home.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga households of rural Limpopo:** examine stored and gathered foods (mopane worms, dried greens) as a food reserve.
- **Jewish communities of Johannesburg:** look at how a community-run security body and a burial society (Chevra Kadisha) together frame safety.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** grants, crime surveys and food-security data are strong and national; ritual protection of the home is poorly documented outside older ethnography; pre-colonial security rests on archaeology.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for Cape Town mutuals; Sesotho for mineworker remittance sources and difaqane memory; Kaaps for Cape Flats interviews; Afrikaans for farm-safety and insurance histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** farm attacks, gang violence and gender-based violence are political and traumatic; protective medicine belongs to healers and families. Policing and the rules of security belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** savings, insurance and safety studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left for the author's decision.
