# South Africa: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps; archival Dutch for old Cape family registers. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vatsonga villages around Bushbuckridge and Sepedi villages near Polokwane:** check whether "home" stays rural through decades of town work in each surveillance site.
- **Basotho migrants and their Free State and Lesotho-border homes:** read lifela and interviews for how they keep two places steady.
- **Old Cape families, Afrikaans and Coloured, on Boland farms and mission towns:** compare long tenure on one farm or plot as each side records it.
- **Bo-Kaap residents and newer buyers:** test how long-settled households describe a changing street.
- **Chinese South African families of Gqeberha and Johannesburg:** look for generational continuity in shops, clubs and burial grounds.
- **Portuguese South Africans of Madeiran origin:** examine how families tie continuity to parishes, clubs and the island of origin, and to repeated return visits.
- **amaXhosa and amaMpondo families with town and rural homes in the Eastern Cape:** trace which home holds the graves and the umbilical cords, and why that anchors belonging.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** demographic surveillance and census data are unusually strong; family continuity is well recorded for old Cape families and thin elsewhere in writing; oral sources fill the gap.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho for lifela; isiXhosa for Jordan's novel and Eastern Cape interviews; Xitsonga and Sepedi for the surveillance-site communities; archival Dutch and Afrikaans for family registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** removals, migrant labor and broken households are painful histories; burial sites tied to land claims are contested; umbilical-cord customs are family matters.
- **Held for review:** settlement, continuity and family-business histories of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, outside this sheet by rule.
