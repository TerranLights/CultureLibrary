# South Africa: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Setswana, Sesotho, Sepedi, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English; Khoekhoegowab for Nama herding sources. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New middle-class households of Gauteng suburbs and their rural kin in Limpopo and the Eastern Cape:** check how earners describe what they owe home and how kin describe what they expect.
- **Cattle-holding households of the Eastern Cape and Bapedi villages:** compare whether herd size still marks a rich man next to cash and cars.
- **Afrikaans families shaped by the volkskapitalisme era and Afrikaans farm families:** separate business wealth built through group institutions from wealth held in land.
- **Township youth and their elders in Soweto and Katlehong:** look at how display such as izikhothane is judged by each generation.
- **Charismatic and mainline congregations:** set prosperity teaching beside Methodist, Catholic and Zionist sermons on riches.
- **Nama herders of the Richtersveld and Namaqualand:** examine how wealth in goats and sheep is counted and spoken of in their own terms.
- **Cape Muslim families:** trace how the costs of weddings and religious feasts are shared among kin and neighbors, as families describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** wealth distribution and elite history are well covered; attitudes to the rich are covered in surveys; how rural households reckon wealth in herds is mostly in older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Setswana and Sesotho for cattle proverbs and tales; Afrikaans for the volkskapitalisme sources; isiZulu for township display; Khoekhoegowab for Nama herding words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wealth by population group is a raw subject; the Randlord period has been used for ethnic caricature, so cite only what the sources show about named people; "black tax" debates are personal.
- **Held for review:** trade and business fortunes of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept out of this sheet.
