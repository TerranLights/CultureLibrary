# South Africa: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Households with co-wives in rural KwaZulu-Natal and monogamous couples in Durban and Pietermaritzburg:** check how jealousy is named and managed in each arrangement.
- **Soweto residents and VhaVenda or Vatsonga villagers of Limpopo:** compare how fear of a neighbor's envy is voiced in a township and in rural villages.
- **Taxi owners and drivers in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal:** trace how a route becomes "ours" and how the claim is enforced.
- **Coloured fishing families of Hawston and the Overberg coast:** see how guarding abalone grounds is described next to the loss of fishing rights.
- **amaSwati and amaNdebele families of Mpumalanga:** look into how gifts and inheritance shares are spread among children to head off rivalry.
- **Afrikaans-speaking couples in church counseling:** examine how pastors frame jealousy in marriage courses and sermons.
- **Residents and foreign-born shopkeepers in informal settlements:** take each side's account of trade claims apart, without assuming either.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** partner-control surveys and witchcraft-and-envy ethnography are rich; childhood sharing and object-guarding are hardly studied; the territorial side is strong for taxis and fisheries.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Callaway's tales, maskandi and co-wife sources; Tshivenda and Xitsonga for Limpopo villages; Afrikaans for soap and church material; Kaaps for Overberg fishers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence, ukuthwala, witchcraft accusations and xenophobic attacks are traumatic and politically charged. The legal side of protection orders belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** jealousy, rivalry and family-control studies about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held off by rule.
