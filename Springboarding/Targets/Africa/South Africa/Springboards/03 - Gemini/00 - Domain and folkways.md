## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu speakers in rural KwaZulu-Natal and in Gauteng townships:** test whether hlonipha speech and clan-name address keep the same rules after migration to Johannesburg, since urban mixing may change the rules.
- **N|uu speakers near Upington, !Xun and Khwe at Platfontein, Nama of the Richtersveld:** read each community's language-revival and home-language figures separately, because each language stands at its own point of survival.
- **Coloured communities of the Western and Northern Cape, and Cape Malay families:** compare where Kaaps and standard Afrikaans are each spoken (home, school, mosque, church) to map register shifts.
- **Afrikaners and English-speaking South Africans in suburbs and farm districts:** compare kuier visiting customs, neighborhood-watch groups and street WhatsApp groups as settings for talk near home.
- **VhaVenda, Vatsonga and Bapedi in Limpopo:** check villages where three home languages share one school, a test of how early learning handles several mother tongues.
- **amaNdebele and amaSwati of Mpumalanga:** check whether children are schooled in isiNdebele or siSwati or moved to another language, since this shapes early learning.
- **Deaf South Africans using SASL:** treat them as a language community of their own, with its own schools and clubs.
- **Jewish, Portuguese and Chinese South Africans:** look at community schools and Saturday language classes (Hebrew, Portuguese, Cantonese or Mandarin) as places where the young learn close to home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census, travel and Gauteng surveys; mixed for speech-ways ethnography; thin for Khoe and San languages outside revival projects. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled, so the Gemini sheets are written for the nine present-day provinces.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for praise poetry and hlonipha; Afrikaans and Kaaps for visiting and neighbor words; Sesotho and Setswana for travel and migrant routes; SASL for Deaf sources; N|uu and Khoekhoegowab terms for Northern Cape revival work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language and race categories overlap in older records; some Khoe and San knowledge is held by communities and shared on their terms; hlonipha touches on marriage norms speakers may not want recorded.
- **Held for review:** speech-ways, schooling and neighborhood material on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted under the rule.
