# South Africa: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English, Kaaps, South African Sign Language; Khoekhoegowab for Nama sources. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mpondo and Thembu speakers of the rural Eastern Cape:** test eye contact, greeting length and respect speech toward elders in village settings against what school and clinic staff expect, where misreadings may arise.
- **Batswana of North West and Northern Cape:** compare speaking order and turn-taking in public meetings with Nguni-language gatherings, since meeting talk is a strong field for this concept.
- **Cape Flats Kaaps speakers:** check whether speakers frame Kaaps as a separate language, a dialect or a style, because the label shapes every study of their speech.
- **Bapedi, VhaVenda and Vatsonga of Limpopo:** gather greeting formulas and requests in each language rather than borrowing Nguni models.
- **Afrikaners of the Free State and Northern Cape farm towns:** compare directness and indirectness in Afrikaans speech between town, farm and church settings.
- **Deaf South Africans in Worcester, Johannesburg and Durban:** compare SASL varieties by school of origin.
- **Nama communities of the Richtersveld and Namaqualand:** check how Khoekhoegowab and Afrikaans divide between home, church and the outside world.
- **Portuguese South Africans of Gauteng and Chinese South Africans of Johannesburg and Port Elizabeth (Gqeberha):** look at children as language brokers for older relatives, worth testing here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for multilingualism and code-switching research; mixed for politeness pragmatics (strong in isiZulu, weaker elsewhere); thin for Tshivenda, Xitsonga and siSwati conversation studies.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for politeness and imbongi material; Setswana for Plaatje's proverbs and kgotla talk; Kaaps beside Afrikaans for Cape Flats speech; SASL for Deaf sources; mine-era Fanakalo for workplace history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Fanakalo carries memories of coercion and racial hierarchy; clinic interpreting research involves patients' private data; Kaaps carries class and racial stigma that speakers name themselves.
- **Held for review:** politeness, code-switching and broadcasting research centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, outside this sheet by rule.
