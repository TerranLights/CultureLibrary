## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural households of the former Transkei and Ciskei and Gauteng households:** compare data costs, signal and the channels each uses for news, since access gaps decide what information arrives at all.
- **‡Khomani, !Xun and Khwe communities of the Northern Cape:** check how each applies its consent rules to researchers, as these rules govern every later study of their knowledge.
- **Readers of Afrikaans broadsheets and of the tabloid *Son*:** test whether news sources split by class inside the Afrikaans-reading public.
- **Basotho of the Free State and Vatsonga and VhaVenda of Limpopo:** compare the reach of Lesedi FM, Munghana Lonene FM and Phalaphala FM, to see how far SABC language stations carry information locally.
- **Cape Muslim listeners of Radio 786 and Voice of the Cape:** look at faith-community radio as a separate news and notice channel.
- **Jewish South Africans:** examine the *SA Jewish Report* and community archives as a minority's own record-keeping.
- **amaNdebele and amaSwati of Mpumalanga:** check whether Ikwekwezi FM and Ligwalagwala FM are their main source of news in home language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history, censorship and fact-checking; mixed for audience data (paywalled); thin for how news moves within Khoe and San and smaller-language communities.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for the Stuart testimonies; isiXhosa and Setswana for the founding papers; Afrikaans for censorship files and tabloids; Khoe and San languages where communities allow access to knowledge sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** San and other Indigenous knowledge is governed by community protocols; destroyed apartheid records leave gaps that are themselves a subject; leaks and state-capture material involve living people.
- **Held for review:** press, broadcasting and archive material produced by or about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside here by rule.
