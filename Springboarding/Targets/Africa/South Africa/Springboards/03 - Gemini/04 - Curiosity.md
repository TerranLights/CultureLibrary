## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karoo towns near the SKA (Carnarvon, Vanwyksvlei):** compare how residents frame the telescope's science against grazing and land use, a local test of curiosity's price.
- **Batswana of North West and Northern Cape:** check sky names and seasonal star-reading reported by elders, and how much of it survives in schools.
- **Khoe and San descendants in Hankey and the Northern Cape:** ask how the history of display and collecting affects their willingness to be studied today.
- **isiZulu-speaking families in rural KwaZulu-Natal:** look for riddling sessions and children's question games and see who still runs them.
- **Afrikaans naturalist and farming circles of Limpopo and the Free State:** compare amateur natural history (Marais's legacy; birding clubs) across generations.
- **Soweto and Langa residents who host tourists:** check what visitors may photograph or ask and who sets those rules.
- **Vatsonga and VhaVenda households of Limpopo:** gather questions children may and may not ask of elders, case by case, without borrowing other groups' patterns.
- **Coloured communities of the Cape Flats:** examine science-outreach reach in Cape Flats schools versus city-center schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science outreach and astronomy projects; mixed for folk riddling; thin for how far children may question adults in each language group.
- **Languages needed:** |xam and Khoekhoegowab terms in the Bleek and Lloyd notebooks; Setswana for sky knowledge; Afrikaans for Marais; isiZulu and Sesotho for riddles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains and the Baartman history are painful and governed by community wishes; some sky and plant knowledge may be restricted; township tourism touches on privacy and exploitation.
- **Held for review:** science, festival and inquiry material connected to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; not used here.
