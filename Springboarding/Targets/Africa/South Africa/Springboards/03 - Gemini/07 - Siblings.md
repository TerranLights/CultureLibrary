# South Africa: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English, Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab for Nama family terms. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu-speaking homesteads of KwaZulu-Natal:** compare the eldest son's place in rural homes with that of urban households where daughters may head families.
- **VhaVenda families of Limpopo:** check what the makhadzi does today in weddings, funerals and family quarrels, from the families' own accounts.
- **Batswana of North West and Northern Cape:** test how far the brother-sister (kgaitsadi) pairing still links sisters' marriages to brothers' obligations.
- **Xhosa households of the Eastern Cape:** look at twin rites and the naming of firstborn and last-born children in villages and towns.
- **Child-headed households in KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo and the Eastern Cape:** compare where siblings raise siblings and where grandparents or neighbors take over.
- **Afrikaner farm families:** check whether farms pass to one son or are split among siblings, and the family quarrels each choice brings (inheritance law to Libra).
- **Coloured families of the Cape Flats and Namaqualand:** compare kin terms and sibling care duties in Kaaps-speaking homes with standard Afrikaans usage.
- **Vatsonga and Bapedi families with members working in Gauteng:** check which sibling migrates and which stays, and how money moves among them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household makeup, orphanhood and child-headed homes; mixed for twin beliefs; thin for sibling terms and practice in siSwati and isiNdebele.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for Mqhayi; Tshivenda for makhadzi and *Muvhango*; Setswana for kgaitsadi; Sepedi for *Skeem Saam*; Afrikaans for *Triomf* and farm-family sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** HIV/AIDS orphanhood; incest in *Triomf*; inheritance disputes and the courts' rulings on male heirs belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here; some twin rites may be private.
- **Held for review:** sibling, birth-order and family-business studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted under the rule.
