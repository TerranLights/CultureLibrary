# South Africa: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English, Kaaps; seventeenth-century Dutch for VOC-era records. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping sides settle a conflict or reach agreement without imposing a decision, and more broadly the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors). Court-based settlement belongs to Libra and Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **KwaZulu-Natal and East Rand areas hit by 1990s political violence:** compare peace-committee records across rival-aligned communities, since one model met very different conditions.
- **Zwelethemba and other Boland townships:** check how residents rate peace-committee outcomes against calling the police.
- **Coloured neighborhoods of the Cape Flats:** look at who is trusted to broker gang ceasefires and who is not, and why.
- **Bapedi and Basotho families:** test whether the maternal uncle (malome) is still the first go-between in family quarrels and marriage talks.
- **Vatsonga and VhaVenda households of Limpopo:** compare how diviners and church leaders each stand between families and ancestors or God.
- **Afrikaner congregations:** examine ministers as family peacemakers and the church's part in 1980s back-channel talks.
- **Khoe descendants in the Western Cape:** check how Krotoa is claimed and remembered, since the go-between's memory is itself contested.
- **Gauteng taxi associations:** gather who mediates route disputes and what each truce required.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for TRC, peace accord and 1990s negotiations; mixed for marriage negotiators and peace committees; thin for go-betweens in Xitsonga, Tshivenda and siSwati families.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Dutch for VOC records; isiZulu for Ngubane and izinduna studies; isiXhosa and Sesotho for marriage-delegate terms; Afrikaans for church and Dakar material; Kaaps for Cape Flats truce sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** TRC testimony on torture and killing; gang and taxi violence involve living people and danger; diviners' intercession may be private; any courtroom, customary-court or labor-law aspect goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** peacemaking, brokerage and reconciliation material involving South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held aside by rule.
