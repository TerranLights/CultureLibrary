## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **amaMpondo and abaThembu of the Eastern Cape against isiZulu speakers in KwaZulu-Natal:** check how each lays out the homestead and assigns its huts, since the two old ethnographies frame them differently.
- **Balobedu, Bapedi, VhaVenda and Vatsonga in Limpopo:** compare the courtyard household (lapa, muti, mudi) and its head, to see whether Limpopo has one pattern or several.
- **Batswana of North West and Basotho of the Free State:** check how the lelapa ties to the village ward, and how far labor migration reshaped both.
- **Nama of the Richtersveld, \|xam descendants, !Xun and Khwe at Platfontein:** read each people's own account of camp, house and kin, as Khoe and San family forms differ among themselves.
- **Afrikaner farm families and English-speaking townspeople:** compare the werf and the suburban house as family settings, and who counted as part of the household.
- **Coloured families of the Cape Flats and Cape Malay families of the Bo-Kaap:** check how each gathers its wider family after removals scattered households.
- **amaNdebele and amaSwati of Mpumalanga:** compare the painted homestead and the household it held with siSwati homesteads often filed under Eswatini.
- **Jewish, Portuguese and Chinese South Africans:** check what part community associations and family firms play in who counts as family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household surveys and older ethnography; mixed for family memory and hospitality; thin for Khoe and San family life in their own words. The author has not yet settled the nation's exact post-war lands at Stage 0, so the Cancer sheets cover the nine provinces as they stand today.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for homestead and clan terms; the three Sotho-Tswana languages for the lelapa; Afrikaans and Dutch for estate records and magazines.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the old ethnographies wrote under segregation and use its categories; records of enslaved households and of removals carry grief; family rites may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** household, kin and home-building material about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left unscouted on this sheet.
