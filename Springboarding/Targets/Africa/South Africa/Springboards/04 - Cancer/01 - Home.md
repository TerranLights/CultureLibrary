# South Africa: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Kaaps and South African English; Khoekhoegowab for Nama homes in the Northern Cape. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xitsonga-speaking households of Bushbuckridge and migrants in Gauteng:** check which of two houses people call "home" when asked, since surveys may record only one.
- **Bafokeng and other Batswana villages in North West:** compare how a home village and a mine town are each named and described, to see where a person feels they live.
- **Nama of the Richtersveld and farm-based Coloured families of the Northern Cape:** check the matjieshuis camp against the fixed farm cottage as a person's base.
- **Former residents of District Six, Sophiatown and South End (Gqeberha):** compare how removed families still name the old street as home, case by case.
- **amaXhosa families split between East London townships and rural villages:** check the age at which each generation stops "going home" for holidays and rites.
- **Afrikaner families tied to a farm and English-speaking families in cities:** compare home as a family farm handed down with home as a house bought and sold.
- **Cape Malay families in the Bo-Kaap and Basotho of the Free State Highlands:** check how each tells the history of its homes when outsiders move in or land changes hands.
- **Portuguese South Africans from Madeira and Mozambique, and Chinese South Africans:** look at what each calls "home" when an older homeland is also in view (diaspora material as comparison only).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing statistics and removals memory; mixed for the rural home in migrants' own words; thin for San homes outside archives and heritage sites.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for going-home and ancestor terms; Xitsonga for Bushbuckridge interviews; Afrikaans for the farm novel; Kaaps for Cape Flats home talk; Nama for the Richtersveld.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced removals and lost homes are raw for living families; ukubuyisa is a family rite and may not be shared; RDP allocation is a source of local disputes.
- **Held for review:** dwelling and neighborhood histories of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept off this page.
