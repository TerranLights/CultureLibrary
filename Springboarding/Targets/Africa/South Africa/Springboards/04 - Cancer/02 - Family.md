*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **VhaVenda of Limpopo:** check what authority the makhadzi holds in naming, marriage talks and rites today, against Stayt's account, to see how a kin office survives.
- **Vatsonga of Limpopo and Mpumalanga:** compare kin terms in Junod's record with speakers' usage now, to find which relatives still get their own word.
- **Bapedi of Sekhukhune:** check whether the senior house and cousin marriage still shape who counts as close kin.
- **amaZulu and amaXhosa clans:** compare how a stranger is placed by clan name and praises, and what follows (in-law avoidance, marriage limits).
- **Coloured families of the Western Cape:** look at the line between family and household in places such as Ross's settlement, where kin and non-kin share yards.
- **Afrikaner families and their stamvader lines:** check the role of family reunions, family associations and printed genealogies in deciding who belongs.
- **Jewish South Africans and Chinese South African families:** compare family-name associations, burial societies and community rolls as records of kin.
- **Batswana and Basotho in North West and the Free State:** check the paternal aunt's and maternal uncle's roles at weddings and funerals, region by region.
- **Nama of Namaqualand (Steinkopf, Leliefontein):** check mission registers for how older Nama clan names gave way to the surnames families carry now.
- **LGBTQ South Africans:** check how chosen families and birth families each turn up in memoirs and archive interviews.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy and older ethnography; mixed for present-day kin obligations; thin for kin terms among Khoe and San speakers beyond Nama.
- **Languages needed:** Tshivenda and Xitsonga for kin offices; isiZulu and isiXhosa for clan praises; Dutch for the earliest Cape registers; Hebrew and Yiddish for some Jewish family records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** customary marriage and ilobolo disputes belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); some clan praises are family property; outing LGBTQ relatives in records must be avoided.
- **Held for review:** kinship, marriage and genealogy sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, parked pending the author's call.
