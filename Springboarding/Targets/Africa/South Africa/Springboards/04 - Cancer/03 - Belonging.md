# South Africa: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Setswana, Sesotho, Sepedi, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele; Khoekhoegowab and N|uu for Khoe and San revival groups. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Griqua communities of the Northern Cape and the Free State:** check how membership in a Griqua body is decided, and how that differs from the state's categories.
- **Khoe and San revival groups in the Western Cape and !Xun and Khwe at Platfontein:** compare self-declared belonging in the revival with belonging by birth in a living San community.
- **Coloured people of the Cape Flats and of Namaqualand:** check whether "Coloured", "Khoe", "bruin" or a town name is the first answer to "who are your people?", place by place.
- **Cape Muslims of the Bo-Kaap:** look at how membership in a congregation and a neighborhood is passed down, now that outsiders are buying in.
- **Afrikaners in Orania, in farm districts and in cities:** compare who each setting treats as one of its own, including Afrikaans speakers who are not white.
- **amaZulu in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng migrants from there:** check how far clan and chiefdom still decide belonging away from home.
- **Bapedi and other ZCC members in Limpopo:** check church membership as a tie that crosses ethnic lines.
- **Jewish, Chinese and Portuguese South Africans:** compare community schools, clubs and congregations as gates of membership, and how mixed marriages are received.
- **Foreign-born Africans in Gauteng and the Western Cape:** read migrants' own accounts of exclusion alongside survey data, as a check on how the boundary is drawn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for identity politics and history; mixed for everyday belonging in neighborhoods; thin for rural Limpopo and Mpumalanga.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for the Stuart testimony and clan histories; Afrikaans and Kaaps for Coloured and Afrikaner sources; Setswana and Sesotho for ubuntu-adjacent terms; Khoekhoegowab for Khoe revival writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** classification by race is a live wound; xenophobic violence (2008, 2015 and after) is traumatic; Khoe and San recognition is a political claim; initiation (ulwaluko, lebollo) as entry into manhood is closed knowledge.
- **Held for review:** community-membership and identity sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, withheld from this sheet by the standing rule.
