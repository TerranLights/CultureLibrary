## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **VhaVenda of Limpopo:** check children's song repertoires and who leads them, to see how far children teach children.
- **amaXhosa of the Eastern Cape:** compare iintsomi told at home with stories children now meet in school readers, to see what moved.
- **Basotho of the Free State and QwaQwa:** check the tales told to children against Postma's collection, to trace what is still told.
- **Soweto families in the Bt20+ cohort:** look at grandmothers, aunts and older siblings as day-to-day carers, and how often the mother is away.
- **Coloured families in Paarl and the Cape winelands:** check what the Drakenstein data shows on caregiving under farm and town work hours.
- **Afrikaans- and English-speaking parents:** compare advice in *Baba & Kleuter* and *Your Baby* on sleep, feeding and spanking, as two strands of print advice.
- **San and Nama communities in the Western and Northern Cape:** check how heritage centers and schools pass on knowledge of plants and tracking to the young.
- **amaNdebele and amaSwati in Mpumalanga:** look for how beadwork and wall painting are taught to girls, and who teaches.
- **Jewish and Portuguese South African community schools:** check how each pairs home teaching with community schooling in the early years.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cohorts and early-childhood statistics; mixed for home teaching in African languages; thin for San and Nama child-rearing outside heritage programs.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa and Sesotho for tale collections; Tshivenda for children's songs; Afrikaans for magazines and old rhymes; isiZulu for Gauteng interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation teaching (domba, ulwaluko, lebollo) is closed; spanking and its rules go to Capricorn (spirit) and Libra (letter); child-malnutrition data can stigmatize places.
- **Held for review:** upbringing, lullaby and early-schooling sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside on this sheet.
