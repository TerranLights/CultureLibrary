## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu-speaking homesteads in rural KwaZulu-Natal:** check which protective rites (intelezi, lightning medicine) families still use and who performs them, against Berglund's record.
- **Basotho and Batswana families in the Free State and North West:** compare how long botsetsi seclusion lasts and who may enter, to see local variation in shielding newborns.
- **!Khomani San of the Kalahari and Nama of the Richtersveld:** check how each frames guardianship of land in its own documents and land settlements.
- **Afrikaner farm families in the Free State and Limpopo:** check whether memories of the commando and the laager come up when families talk about guarding a farm, and in what terms.
- **Coloured families of Manenberg, Hanover Park and Mitchells Plain:** check how parents arrange children's routes and hours around gang territory.
- **Vatsonga and Bapedi villages near Kruger's western edge:** compare villagers' views of rangers who protect wildlife with their views of who protects them.
- **Men in Gauteng townships and Eastern Cape towns:** check how men describe being a protector, and where that turns to control, in Sonke's and other programs' material.
- **Jewish, Chinese and Portuguese South African communities:** check each one's own volunteer patrols, crisis lines and community halls as protective bodies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime and child-protection statistics; mixed for protective rites; thin for San protective practice in living communities.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for protective-medicine terms; Sesotho and Setswana for post-birth seclusion; Afrikaans for Voortrekker and commando sources; Kaaps for Cape Flats safety talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protective medicine belongs to healers and families; violence against women and children is traumatic and the rules on it belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); rangers and poaching touch armed conflict.
- **Held for review:** protective rites and community-safety sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; whether to admit them is the author's decision.
