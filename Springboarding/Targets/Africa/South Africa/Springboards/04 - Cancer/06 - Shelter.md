# South Africa: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** Afrikaans, Kaaps, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English; Khoekhoegowab and San languages for rock-shelter heritage. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shack dwellers in eThekwini (isiZulu and isiXhosa speakers from rural areas):** check how residents describe a shack as a temporary or a lasting shelter, in Abahlali's own records.
- **Backyarders in Coloured areas of Cape Town and Gqeberha:** compare yard rooms with family members and with landlords, to see how kin and rent mix.
- **People sleeping rough in Tshwane and Johannesburg:** check where people from Limpopo, Mpumalanga and abroad end up, and which shelters they use.
- **Basotho women in the Free State:** look at who decorates the house wall and when it is redone, as part of making shelter.
- **San heritage groups and Drakensberg rock-art guides:** check how the old rock shelters are interpreted today, and by whom.
- **Afrikaners in informal camps on the West Rand and near Pretoria:** compare how poor white families describe living in tents and caravans with other camp residents' accounts.
- **Farm-worker families in the Northern Cape and the winelands:** check the tie between a job and a roof, and what happens to shelter when the job ends.
- **Families sheltered by churches during the 2008 and 2015 attacks on foreign Africans:** read the shelter records and testimonies for how refuge was arranged.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for informal settlements and housing policy; mixed for homelessness; thin for counts of people sheltered by kin.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for shack-movement records; Sesotho for refuge and house terms; Afrikaans and Kaaps for Cape and camp sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** evictions and shack fires are recent trauma; the 1900-1902 camps carry grief for Boer and Black families alike; refuges for abused women keep their addresses confidential; eviction rules go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** lodging, refuge and housing-movement sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held pending a ruling from the author.
