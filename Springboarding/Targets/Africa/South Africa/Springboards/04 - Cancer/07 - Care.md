# South Africa: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiXhosa, isiZulu, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English, Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab (Nama). **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Grandmothers in Khayelitsha and the Eastern Cape:** check how care for orphaned grandchildren is split among grants, kin and community groups.
- **Older Vatsonga in Bushbuckridge:** compare care from co-resident kin with support from children working elsewhere (HAALSI data).
- **isiZulu-speaking families in KwaZulu-Natal:** check when a family turns to an isangoma, a clinic or both, and who pays.
- **Domestic workers from Limpopo and the Eastern Cape in the suburbs:** look at care given to employers' families and arranged for their own children.
- **Coloured farm workers in the Overberg and on the West Coast:** check who tends the sick and old in cottages and farmhouse; compare *Agaat* with workers' oral histories, since a novel is one view.
- **Afrikaner congregations in the Free State and the Karoo:** compare church-run frail care with home care, and who in the family gives it.
- **Nama of Namaqualand and Cape herbalists:** check which plant remedies are given at home and who keeps the knowledge.
- **Cape Muslim and Jewish communities:** compare how each arranges care of the sick and washing of the dead, through community bodies and families.
- **Pondoland villages:** look at who takes the sick to clinics and how families choose who goes.
- **Disabled South Africans:** check where DPSA's members want care to come from (family, state, paid help).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for time use, HIV care and domestic work; mixed for elder care; thin for the knowledge healers keep.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for healer and care terms; Xitsonga for Agincourt interviews; Afrikaans for ACVV records and *Agaat*; Kaaps for Cape Muslim usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge may be closed and bound by calling; HIV status is private; domestic workers' accounts can expose both sides; care law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** caregiving and healing sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held until the author rules.
