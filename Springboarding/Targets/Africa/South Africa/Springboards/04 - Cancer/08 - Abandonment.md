# South Africa: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** Sesotho, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English; !Xun, Khwe and Afrikaans for Platfontein sources. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Basotho families in the Free State and QwaQwa:** check how wives and children left by mine migrants describe the wait, alongside Murray's Lesotho work.
- **Residents of former dumping grounds (Dimbaza, Limehill, Onverwacht):** compare how each settlement tells the story of being placed there and left.
- **!Xun and Khwe families at Platfontein:** check how residents speak of the army that moved them in 1990 and then withdrew.
- **Street children and youth in Cape Town and Hillbrow:** look at what home and family ties they keep, from their own accounts.
- **Coloured and Afrikaner forest families around Knysna:** compare the lost-child story in fiction with local memory of the woodcutter communities.
- **Vatsonga and Bapedi grandmothers in Limpopo:** check how they take in children whose parents left for Gauteng, and what they call that.
- **Portuguese South Africans who left Mozambique and Angola in 1975:** look at what they say of homes and kin they left behind (comparison only for the countries they left).
- **Jewish families tied to Arcadia:** check how former residents recall being placed and visited.
- **Fathers in the Eastern Cape and Gauteng, across language groups:** check what reasons men themselves give for living apart from their children, in the fatherhood reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for removals history and child statistics; mixed for absent fathers; thin for elders left alone.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho for tales and migrant families; isiXhosa for resettlement testimony; Afrikaans for *Fiela se Kind* and forest-community memory; !Xun and Khwe for Platfontein.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abandoned infants, street children and orphans are minors and must not be identified; removals carry grief; the absent-father debate can stigmatize; the legal side of adoption and child safes is for Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not this sign.
- **Held for review:** desertion, orphanhood and family-separation sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, out of scope for this page by rule.
