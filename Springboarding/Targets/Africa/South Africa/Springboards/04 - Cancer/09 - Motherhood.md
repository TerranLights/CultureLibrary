# South Africa: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Afrikaans, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Kaaps, South African English; Dutch for early Cape slave records; Khoekhoegowab where Nama women's groups publish. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Afrikaner women in the Free State:** check how the camp-mother memory at the Vrouemonument is taught today, and how younger women relate to the volksmoeder ideal.
- **Methodist and Anglican women's unions in Gauteng and the Eastern Cape:** compare manyano and Mothers' Union rules on who may wear the uniform (married mothers only, or all women).
- **amaNdebele of Mpumalanga:** check which beadwork pieces mark marriage and motherhood, and whether that is still read the same way.
- **Basotho and Batswana women:** compare the "little mother" and "big mother" roles of a mother's sisters, case by case, in Free State and North West families.
- **Coloured mothers in Cape Town and descendants of the enslaved:** look at how family histories carry memories of enslaved mothers, against Shell's records.
- **isiZulu-speaking mothers in rural KwaZulu-Natal:** check how confinement after birth (umdlezana) is kept in clinic-era practice.
- **Cape Muslim and Portuguese Catholic families:** compare the place of Marian devotion (Our Lady of Fátima) and of mosque women's circles in the life of mothers.
- **Jewish South African women:** check the Union of Jewish Women's records for the mother's role in community welfare.
- **Nama women of Namaqualand and !Xun and Khwe women at Platfontein:** check what each community's own women's groups record about birth and raising children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for maternal health and the politics of motherhood; mixed for everyday mothering in African languages; thin for Khoe and San mothers' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for volksmoeder sources and Poppie Nongena; isiZulu and isiXhosa for birth customs; Sesotho and Setswana for mothers' sisters' roles; Dutch for Cape slave records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal deaths and teenage pregnancy can shame individuals and places; the camps and slavery carry deep grief; "Mother of the Nation" titles are politically contested; abortion belongs to Aries and its rules to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** mother-figure, birth-custom and maternal-health sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not taken up on this sheet pending the author.
