## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu speakers in KwaZulu-Natal and isiXhosa speakers in the Eastern Cape:** compare praise poetry, dance gatherings and choir contests, the best-documented oral forms of this domain.
- **Khoe and San peoples (Nama of the Richtersveld; !Xun and Khwe at Platfontein; ‡Khomani of the Kalahari):** check each people's own account of dance, song and image-making, apart from rock-art scholarship about them.
- **Coloured communities of the Western and Northern Cape, in their own self-designation:** check carnival troupes, riel dancing and Kaaps comedy as distinct circuits of performance.
- **Cape Malay communities:** check choir competitions and festive meals as occasions with their own calendar and judging.
- **Afrikaners in the Free State, Northern Cape and Western Cape:** check arts festivals in Afrikaans, rugby and jukskei as separate settings for display and pleasure.
- **Bapedi, VhaVenda and Vatsonga of Limpopo, amaNdebele and amaSwati of Mpumalanga:** check tshikona and xibelani dancing and painted homesteads against the better-recorded coastal forms.
- **Basotho of the Free State and Batswana of North West:** compare choir music, praise forms (dithoko) and horse and cattle shows.
- **Gauteng township youth:** check izikhothane, pantsula and amapiano scenes as city-made display, separately from rural forms.
- **English-speaking, Jewish, Portuguese and Chinese South Africans:** check club sport and community festivals, and, for Chinese South Africans, the story told of the fahfee numbers game.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music history, sport and festivals; mixed for measured leisure; thin for Khoe and San creative life in their own words and for Tshivenda and Xitsonga arts. The nation's exact post-war lands await the author's Stage 0 decision; until then the Leo sheets take the nine present-day provinces as their ground.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for praise and dance terms; Afrikaans and Kaaps for festivals and carnival; Sesotho and Setswana for dithoko and choir; Tshivenda and Xitsonga for Limpopo dance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation dances (VhaVenda domba, for one) are closed to outsiders; rock-art sites and San healing dances need community consent; old ethnography and the carnival's past names carry racial labels.
- **Held for review:** festival, music and sport material centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside for the Leo domain under the standing rule.
