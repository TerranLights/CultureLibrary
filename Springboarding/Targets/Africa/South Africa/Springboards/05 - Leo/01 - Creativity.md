## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu-speaking beadworkers of KwaZulu-Natal and isiXhosa-speaking beadworkers of the Eastern Cape:** check whether a pattern is invented by the maker or inherited, and how each names the designer.
- **amaNdebele painters of Mpumalanga and Gauteng's north:** check how a new motif enters the shared repertory and who may sign it.
- **Basotho of the Free State:** check litema as seasonal renewal against one-off design.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga carvers and potters of Limpopo:** check whether a sculptor's vision is credited to the person, a dream or the ancestors.
- **!Xun and Khwe painters at Platfontein and ‡Khomani makers in the Kalahari:** check their own accounts of how a picture is conceived, kept apart from older San rock-art readings.
- **Cape Malay and other Cape artisans:** check how builders, tailors and silversmiths of the early Cape are credited in records versus memory.
- **Afrikaners of the farm districts:** check where the "make a plan" proverb is used and whether it marks pride in improvising or simple need.
- **Gauteng township youth:** check Iscamtho coinage and amapiano production as creativity in speech and sound.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check the Irma Stern Museum and similar estates for how an immigrant artist's originality was received.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for art history, beadwork and township art; mixed for measured creative work; thin for technical invention outside patent counts and for Tshivenda and Xitsonga terms for "art".
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for bead vocabularies; isiNdebele and Sesotho for mural terms; Afrikaans for the proverb and early Cape craft records; Kaaps for coinage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who owns designs and plant knowledge belongs to Taurus and, for the law, Libra; some beadwork and carving serves ancestors and should not be read as gallery art; collectors' catalogs labeled makers "tribal".
- **Held for review:** crafts, design and invention traditions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left out of this creativity sheet.
