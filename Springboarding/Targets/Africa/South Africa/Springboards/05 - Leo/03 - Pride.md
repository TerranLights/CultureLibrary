# South Africa: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab and Griqua sources in Afrikaans for Khoe revival material. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Afrikaners in Gauteng, the Free State and the Western Cape:** check how monuments, language festivals and family histories carry pride after 1994, and the disputes over them.
- **isiZulu speakers and the royal house in KwaZulu-Natal:** check how pride in the kingdom is voiced at royal events against a wider national pride.
- **isiXhosa speakers of the Eastern Cape:** check how iimbongi and older writers praise clan and people, and how far iintsomi punish arrogance.
- **Coloured communities, in their own self-designation, and Griqua of the Northern Cape and Free State:** check the shift from imposed labels to chosen ones as a source of self-respect.
- **Mineworkers from the Eastern Cape, Limpopo and North West:** check pride in labor and its loss in retrenchment memoirs.
- **Black Consciousness generations in Gauteng and the Eastern Cape:** check what "Black is beautiful" meant on campuses and in townships.
- **English-speaking South Africans of the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal:** check settler commemoration and how its pride is being renegotiated.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga of Limpopo:** check whether pride is framed through the old polities and leaders or through new provincial institutions.
- **Portuguese South Africans of Gauteng:** check clubs and 10 June celebrations for how an immigrant group shows pride in two homelands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nationalism, monuments and sport; mixed for measured national pride; thin for personal pride in work or children outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for Afrikaner commemoration; isiXhosa and isiZulu for praise and tales; Sesotho and Setswana for the many words between pride and arrogance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** group pride under apartheid included white supremacy, and monuments from that era are disputed; ethnic pride can feed xenophobic or separatist talk, to record without adopting; the line between pride and humiliation runs through this sign, see Humiliation and Dignity.
- **Held for review:** heritage-pride events and writing from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left out of this pride sheet.
