# South Africa: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; !Xuntali and Khwedam (via XK FM) and Khoekhoegowab for Northern Cape peoples. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **!Xun and Khwe at Platfontein and ‡Khomani in the Kalahari:** check how radio, heritage centers and land claims change who looks at whom.
- **Former residents of District Six and the Bo-Kaap, including Cape Malay families:** check how removals and later tourism changed who sees the neighborhood and how residents want to be seen.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga in Limpopo:** check greeting postures (kneeling, clapping, lowered gaze) as rules about who may look at whom.
- **Farmworkers in the Western Cape winelands and Northern Cape:** check their own reports on being overlooked by owners, tourists and the state.
- **Women in rural KwaZulu-Natal and Eastern Cape homesteads:** check where eye contact with in-laws is avoided, and how city life changes it.
- **Black lesbian and trans South Africans in townships:** check the tension between being visible and being safe.
- **Afrikaners in small Karoo and Free State towns:** check how press photographs and later television depicted them.
- **Undercounted areas in Gauteng informal settlements and the Eastern Cape:** check census and voter-roll gaps as a measure of who is officially unseen.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check the South African Jewish Museum's photographs for how a minority documented its own presence.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for photography, removals and census quality; mixed for media representation; thin for everyday gaze and greeting norms outside isiZulu and Tshivenda material.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu and isiXhosa for rites of presentation; Tshivenda and Xitsonga for greeting postures; !Xuntali and Khwedam through XK FM; Afrikaans for photo captions and small-town press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gender-based violence and named victims; photographs of San people taken without consent; some rites of presentation involve ancestors and are private.
- **Held for review:** representation and erasure material on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted on the being-seen sheet.
