# South Africa: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab for Nama and Griqua testimony. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sotho-Tswana speakers of the Free State, North West and Limpopo:** check how seriti is spoken of when a person is shamed or recovers.
- **Shack dwellers in eThekwini and Ekurhuleni:** check how residents describe what housing and toilets do to their dignity, and in which languages.
- **Platinum and gold miners in North West and Gauteng:** check whether respect from managers or pay comes first in their own accounts.
- **Grant recipients in rural Eastern Cape and Limpopo:** check queue conditions and the words people use about being processed.
- **Afrikaners in poverty, then and now:** check the 1932 framing against present informal settlements of white South Africans and how residents describe themselves.
- **Khoe and San descendants in the Northern Cape:** check how reburials are performed and who decides the rites.
- **Families of psychiatric patients in Gauteng:** check testimony from the Life Esidimeni hearings on care and respect.
- **Cape Muslim and Jewish South Africans:** check burial and welfare societies as community ways of keeping the dying and the dead in dignity.
- **amaSwati and isiNdebele speakers of Mpumalanga:** check how sitfunti and related terms appear in elders' talk and church language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest, labor and poverty reports; mixed for philosophy of ubuntu and seriti; thin for Tshivenda, Xitsonga and siSwati terms in use.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho and Setswana for seriti; isiZulu and isiXhosa for isithunzi and isidima; Dutch for the VOC journal; Afrikaans for the Carnegie report and its reception.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in care and at Marikana involve named victims; human remains and their reburial need community consent; the constitutional right to dignity belongs to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not this sheet.
- **Held for review:** labor, housing and burial dignity sources centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held out of the dignity scouting.
