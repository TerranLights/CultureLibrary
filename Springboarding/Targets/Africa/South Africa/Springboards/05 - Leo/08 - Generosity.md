## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black professionals in Gauteng and their families in the Eastern Cape and Limpopo:** check how black tax is described by givers and receivers, and where it turns into resentment.
- **Basotho of the Free State and Batswana of North West:** check mafisa and letsema in memory and present use, as giving that builds clients and neighbors.
- **isiZulu and isiXhosa speakers in rural districts:** check ilima work parties and cattle loans, and what the host owes the helpers.
- **Afrikaners in Western and Northern Cape towns:** check church offerings and ACVV welfare as organized generosity, and who receives it.
- **Cape Muslim communities of the Bo-Kaap and Cape Flats:** check boeka exchange and zakah as giving across neighbors and within the community.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga in Limpopo:** check harvest and first-fruits sharing and who is fed at feasts.
- **!Xun and Khwe at Platfontein:** check their own accounts of sharing food and goods within a camp or settlement.
- **City drivers and car guards in Cape Town, Durban and Johannesburg:** check tipping amounts and the talk around giving or refusing.
- **Portuguese South Africans:** check community welfare societies and church feasts as channels of giving in Gauteng.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household transfers, remittances and black tax writing; mixed for formal philanthropy; thin for sharing customs among Khoe and San and for Tshivenda and Xitsonga terms.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho and Setswana for mafisa, letsema and mosa; isiZulu and isiXhosa for ilima and isisa; Afrikaans for church and welfare records; Kaaps for boeka.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family giving can hide coercion and debt; cattle loaning touches ownership (Taurus) and reciprocity (Libra); church and mosque giving records are private.
- **Held for review:** charity, relief and festival giving by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept out of the generosity sheet.
