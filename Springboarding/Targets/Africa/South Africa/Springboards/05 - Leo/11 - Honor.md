# South Africa: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Dutch for early Cape military records; Khoekhoegowab for Nama memory. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu speakers of KwaZulu-Natal:** check how regimental honors and the reed dance are remembered and contested as honor for men and for girls.
- **Afrikaners of the former republics:** check how bittereinder ancestry and the hensopper stigma pass down in families.
- **Families of Mendi soldiers in the Eastern Cape:** check how the restored honor is marked locally and in praise poetry.
- **Coloured communities of the Western Cape, in their own self-designation:** check memories of the Cape Corps alongside the prison and street codes that claim the language of honor.
- **Cape Muslim communities:** check kramat pilgrimages and who tends the shrines.
- **English-speaking South Africans:** check regimental associations and school honors systems as keepers of rank and reputation.
- **Bapedi and Vatsonga in Limpopo:** check the tombstone unveiling and the debts of honor families take on to stage it.
- **Basotho and Bapedi initiates:** check only what communities publish on how initiation confers standing, since the schools are closed.
- **Struggle generation in Gauteng townships:** check how "sell-out" accusations worked as loss of honor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military history and prison-gang studies; mixed for family honor and funerals; thin for honor terms in Tshivenda, Xitsonga and isiNdebele.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for iziqu and the reed dance; Afrikaans for bittereinder memory and the handslag; Dutch for the Pandour records; Kaaps and prison argot for the Number.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence over insult, gang killings and attacks on alleged sell-outs; virginity testing and girls' privacy; initiation knowledge is closed. Revenge and feuds go to Scorpio; damages such as inhlawulo, as law, go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** military, religious and family-honor sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted for honor.
