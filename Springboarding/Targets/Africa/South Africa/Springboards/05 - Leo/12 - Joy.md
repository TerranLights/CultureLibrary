## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **VhaVenda of Limpopo:** check malende and children's songs as everyday sources of gladness, against festival dance.
- **Vatsonga in Limpopo and Gauteng:** check Shangaan electro and xibelani skirt dancing as rural and city expressions of the same delight.
- **isiZulu speakers and the royal house:** check first-fruits and other royal festivals as sanctioned joy, and who may attend.
- **Basotho of the Free State and Sesotho-speaking migrants:** check famo and mokete feasts where joy and boasting mix.
- **Afrikaners and Coloured communities, in their own self-designation:** compare sokkie and langarm halls and the kuier visit as forms of shared enjoyment.
- **Cape Muslim families:** check Labarang visiting and how joy is shown after the fast.
- **Gospel congregations across provinces:** check praise singing as spiritual joy, and how it differs between mainline and Zionist churches.
- **Township youth in Gauteng:** check amapiano parties and after-tears gatherings, and generational disputes over both.
- **Nama of the Northern Cape:** check festive songs and dances recorded by their own heritage groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music, festivals and Gauteng well-being surveys; mixed for national happiness measures; thin for Khoe and San expressions of delight and for isiNdebele and siSwati festive terms.
- **Languages needed:** Tshivenda for Blacking's song texts; Sesotho for famo lyrics; Xitsonga for Shangaan electro; Afrikaans and Kaaps for sokkie, langarm and Labarang.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some royal festivals have closed parts; alcohol at celebrations links to harm, which belongs elsewhere (Pisces); help-line data on depression is aggregate only.
- **Held for review:** music, dance and festive gatherings of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not scouted for joy.
