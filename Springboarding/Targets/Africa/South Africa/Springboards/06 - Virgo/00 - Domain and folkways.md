# South Africa: The domain: work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** the nine official African languages (isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele), Afrikaans, South African English and Kaaps, with Khoekhoegowab, N|uu and !Xun or Khwe sources for the Northern Cape. **Concept in one line:** work, service, the daily round and health, with the kept house, chores, craft and rest beside them; the domain question is what counts as good work and a well-kept life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern Cape homesteads against Gauteng hostels:** check whether migrant work splits one household's day across two places, and how each end tells it.
- **Batswana of the North West platinum belt:** check whether talk of work around Rustenburg and Phokeng still separates tiro from mmereko.
- **‡Khomani of the Kalahari and Nama of the Richtersveld:** check the round of gathering, stock posts and piece work as the communities tell it.
- **Coloured mission-village families of the Overberg, in their own self-designation:** check the church-bell day beside the rhythm of farm labor.
- **Cape Malay households of the Bo-Kaap:** check how prayer times sit inside the working day, and which trades pass from parent to child.
- **Afrikaner farm families of the Free State and Karoo, and Bapedi, VhaVenda and Vatsonga villages in Limpopo:** check who is expected to do which task in the farm year in each setting.
- **Jewish, Portuguese and Chinese South Africans:** check family shops and market gardens where home and workplace share one routine, and the Sabbath's place in the Jewish week.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history and household surveys; mixed on whether health is grouped with work, faith or kin; thin for Khoe and San routines told in their own words. The post-war nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the Virgo sheets are written for the nine present-day provinces. Labor law and rules for healers go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Languages needed:** Setswana to weigh tiro against mmereko; isiZulu for the Stuart testimonies; old Dutch for the Cape slave registers; Afrikaans for *Huisgenoot*; |xam in the Bleek and Lloyd notebooks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' training and medicines are not public; slave-descended families' records touch living memory; old ethnography uses racial labels.
- **Held for review:** daily-round, household and work-ethic sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept off the Virgo domain sheet.
