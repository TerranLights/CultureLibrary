# South Africa: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vatsonga of the Limpopo and Mpumalanga lowveld:** check how a first mine contract and a man's return are named and sung in Xitsonga, and whether that marks a stage of life.
- **Basotho of QwaQwa, Thaba Nchu and the Free State goldfields:** check lifela and shaft-team vocabulary as a worker's own record of the job.
- **Bapedi of Sekhukhuneland:** check how home-boy networks and burial societies organized who went to which mine.
- **Griqua, Nama and Coloured mining families of Kimberley, Okiep and Alexander Bay:** check how company-town work is told in their own accounts.
- **Afrikaner railway and mine workers from 1922 on:** check how union and church papers described manual work and its standing.
- **Western Cape farmworkers, Coloured and isiXhosa-speaking, around De Doorns and the winelands:** check what the 2012 strikers said about pay, housing and the worth of their labor.
- **Gauteng informal traders and minibus-taxi workers:** check whether informal earning is called work, a piece job or a hustle, and by whom.
- **Chinese and Portuguese South Africans:** check how families remember the 1904-1910 mine contracts and the later shops and market gardens.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mining and migrant labor, among the best-documented subjects in the country's social history; mixed for farm work; thin for women's unpaid field and household work outside surveys, and for Khoe and San piece work in the Kalahari.
- **Languages needed:** Xitsonga for magayisa and Harries's songs; Sesotho for lifela; Afrikaans for Solidarity's materials; Chinese-language sources for the 1904-1910 contracts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths at work and the 2012 Marikana killings in the North West touch grieving families; labor law and job reservation go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** histories of jobs, trades and workers' movements among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; this work sheet leaves them for the author.
