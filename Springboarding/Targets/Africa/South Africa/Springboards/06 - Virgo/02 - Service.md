## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu speakers in KwaZulu-Natal:** check how ukukhonza moves between serving God, serving a chief and serving an employer, and whether speakers keep the senses apart.
- **Basotho of the eastern Free State and Batswana of the North West:** check how service to a chief's court is remembered against service to church and state.
- **Domestic workers from the Eastern Cape and Limpopo in Gauteng and Cape Town suburbs:** check the terms of address they use and are given, in each language.
- **Afrikaner and English-speaking men conscripted before 1994:** check how memoirs treat *diensplig* and the choice to refuse it.
- **Cape Malay and other Cape Muslim congregations:** check how service at mosque, funeral and fast-breaking meals is organized and who does it.
- **Coloured congregations of the Moravian and Dutch Reformed Mission churches, in their own self-designation:** check how deacons and women's groups describe their service.
- **Vatsonga and VhaVenda of Limpopo:** check service owed at royal courts (for example the khoro) beside service in rural clinics staffed by community-service doctors.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check whether synagogue and burial-society volunteers speak of serving their own congregation differently from serving outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public-service delivery, domestic service and church organization; mixed for volunteering beyond 2010; thin for Khoe and San terms for serving others.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for the senses of ukukhonza; Sesotho for Casalis's terms read against present usage; Afrikaans for *grensliteratuur* and church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic workers' testimony needs care and consent; conscription memories divide families; service-delivery protests can turn violent. Military law and labor law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** religious, welfare and household-service material concerning South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside on the service sheet.
