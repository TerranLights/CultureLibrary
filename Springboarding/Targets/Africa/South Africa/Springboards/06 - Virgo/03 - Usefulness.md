# South Africa: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** Setswana, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, Kaaps, South African English and South African Sign Language (SASL); !Xun, Khwe and Khoekhoegowab for plant and tool knowledge. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or a piece of knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Grandmothers drawing the older persons' grant in Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal homesteads:** check whether they speak of themselves as providers, carers or burdens, and in which words.
- **Children in Limpopo and Mpumalanga villages:** check the age-graded chores named in Xitsonga, Tshivenda and Sepedi, and how a helpful child is praised.
- **Deaf South Africans who sign SASL, and disability organizations:** check how they answer or reject usefulness as a measure of a person.
- **Reclaimers in Johannesburg and Tshwane:** check how they name their work and the things they gather, and how passers-by name them.
- **!Xun and Khwe of Platfontein and the ‡Khomani:** check which plant, tool and tracking knowledge the communities themselves want handed on as useful.
- **Afrikaner farm families of the Karoo and Northern Cape:** check stories of improvising with wire and scrap, and who tells them.
- **Coloured residents of the Cape Flats, in their own self-designation:** check what "skarrel" covers and whether it is praise or complaint.
- **Portuguese and Chinese South African shopkeeping families:** check which tasks fell to children and to elders behind the counter.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for grants, public works and household surveys; mixed for disability; thin for how the old and the disabled describe their own usefulness, and for Khoe and San terms.
- **Languages needed:** Setswana for mosola; Sepedi for mohola; isiZulu and isiXhosa for the help and burden words; SASL for Deaf testimony; Kaaps for skarrel.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** words for disabled people can wound; reclaimers face harassment; Khoe and San plant knowledge falls under benefit-sharing agreements, whose legal side goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** elder-role, reuse and practical-skills sources tied to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not drawn on for usefulness.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the latest EPWP and CWP annual reports and pull participant counts and job types by province.
2. Search Stats SA for "Survey of Activities of Young People" and its chores tables.
3. Search library catalogs for "older persons grant" with "household" and "intergenerational".
4. Search the Iziko and McGregor catalogs for "ostrich eggshell" and "digging stick".
5. Search Plaatje's *Sechuana Proverbs* for idleness and help.
