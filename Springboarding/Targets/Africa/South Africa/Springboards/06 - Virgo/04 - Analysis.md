## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **!Xun and Khwe trackers of Platfontein and the ‡Khomani of the Kalahari:** check how trackers themselves explain an inference from a track, in their own languages, against how outside researchers wrote it up.
- **Cape Muslim congregations in Cape Town:** check how the moon-sighting argument is conducted and who is trusted to settle it.
- **Mathematics classrooms in KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State:** check how teachers and learners move between isiZulu or Sesotho and English when reasoning through a problem.
- **Afrikaans-medium campuses (Stellenbosch, Pretoria, Potchefstroom):** check how scientific terms were coined and taught in Afrikaans across the twentieth century.
- **amaNdebele and Basotho mural painters:** check whether painters describe planning symmetry and pattern in their own words, apart from geometric readings by others.
- **VhaVenda and Bapedi diviners in Limpopo:** check how the reading of a cast of dice or bones is explained step by step, where diviners agree to share.
- **Gauteng township residents:** check how "clever" street knowledge and school learning are ranked against each other by age group.
- **Jewish South Africans in Johannesburg:** check yeshiva and study-house traditions of close textual argument as a separate line of analysis.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school mathematics, audits and statistics; mixed for ethnomathematics; thin for reckoning words in Tshivenda, Xitsonga and the San languages.
- **Languages needed:** !Xun and Khwe for trackers' explanations; Afrikaans for the Akademie's terms; isiZulu and Sesotho for classroom reasoning; Tshivenda for thangu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** diviners' methods are partly secret; school results reported by old racial categories reflect unequal schooling, not ability, and must be read that way.
- **Held for review:** mathematics, science and reasoning traditions linked to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, outside this analysis sheet.
