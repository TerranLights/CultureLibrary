# South Africa: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Tshivenda, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, siSwati, isiNdebele, Kaaps and South African English; tonic sol-fa choir usage in several African languages. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **VhaVenda potters and carvers of Limpopo:** check the words makers use for a piece that is done right and for one that failed, and whether flaws are kept or destroyed.
- **Christmas-band members of the Cape Flats and Boland, Coloured in their own self-designation:** check what judges mark down and how bands rehearse toward a clean march.
- **isiXhosa- and isiZulu-speaking school and church choirs:** check how conductors talk about exact pitch and diction in sol-fa rehearsal.
- **Basotho litema painters of the eastern Free State:** check how evenness of incised wall patterns is judged among the women who make them.
- **Afrikaner church bakers and show entrants:** check which faults cost a prize and who passes the standard on.
- **High-achieving learners in Gauteng and Western Cape schools, township and suburban:** check how students and parents describe pressure for distinctions and the fear of a lower mark.
- **Pentecostal and Dutch Reformed congregations:** check which one stresses holiness and which grace, and how members feel each.
- **Cape Malay and Bo-Kaap cooks:** check how family recipes are held to an exact standard across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for standards bodies, magazines and medical literature; mixed for craft standards; thin for perfection words in Tshivenda and Xitsonga.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for *Kook en Geniet*, *Sarie* and church sources; Tshivenda for potters' terms; Kaaps for band talk; isiXhosa and isiZulu for sol-fa rehearsal language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** student distress, eating disorders and suicide need care; the "chokers" label can wound players; some craft families guard their methods.
- **Held for review:** exacting-craft, ritual-precision and academic-pressure sources concerning South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept out of the perfectionism sheet.
