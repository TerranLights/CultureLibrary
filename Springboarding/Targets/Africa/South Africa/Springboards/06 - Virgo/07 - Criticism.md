# South Africa: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiXhosa, isiZulu, Setswana, Sesotho, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, Kaaps and South African English. **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, and the forms this takes: feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiXhosa-speaking communities of the former Transkei and Ciskei:** check when an imbongi may still censure a leader, and whether the license holds at state and university ceremonies.
- **Batswana and Basotho with kgotla and pitso traditions:** check how open fault-finding with a chief is handled in assembly today.
- **isiZulu-speaking families in KwaZulu-Natal:** check how hlonipha limits criticism of in-laws and elders, and how young people work around it.
- **Afrikaner writers, clergy and journalists:** check how criticism from inside the group was received, from the 1930s to the 1980s.
- **Coloured comedians and Kaaps performers, in their own self-designation:** check how self-mocking humor criticizes both insiders and outsiders.
- **VhaVenda and Vatsonga of Limpopo:** check whether joking partners may criticize each other in ways others may not.
- **Black township readers and tabloid audiences in Gauteng:** check which faults readers expose in letters and who they target.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check how communal papers and synagogues handled members' dissent over national politics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press, satire and church history; mixed for workplace feedback; thin for criticism within Khoe and San communities and in Tshivenda and Xitsonga.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for iimbongi; Setswana and Sesotho for kgotla sayings; Afrikaans for Louw, *Vrye Weekblad* and church documents; isiZulu for *Isolezwe* letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whistleblowers have been threatened and killed; criticism of the Zulu monarchy and of traditional leaders is touchy; defamation and press law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** cartoons, satire and dissent by or about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; the criticism sheet sets them aside until the author decides.
