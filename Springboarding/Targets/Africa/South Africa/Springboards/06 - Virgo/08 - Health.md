# South Africa: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiXhosa, isiZulu, Setswana, Sesotho, Sepedi, Tshivenda, Xitsonga, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, Kaaps and South African English; Khoekhoegowab and N|uu for plant remedies; Dutch for eighteenth-century Cape medicine. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu speakers in KwaZulu-Natal:** check how impilo works in greetings and clinic talk, and whether an illness is described as a body matter or a matter of relations with the living and the dead.
- **isiXhosa speakers in the Eastern Cape:** check how speakers keep apart igqirha the diviner and igqirha the doctor, and when they use each.
- **Bapedi and other members of the Zion Christian Church in Limpopo:** check how blessed water, tea and prayer sit beside clinic visits.
- **Afrikaner families in rural Free State and Northern Cape towns:** check which complaints go to home remedies, which to the GP, and who in the family decides.
- **Nama, ‡Khomani and Cape Khoe revivalist groups:** check how communities describe buchu, hoodia and other plant knowledge as theirs.
- **Returned mineworkers in the Eastern Cape and Basotho in the Free State:** check how tuberculosis and silicosis are told as illnesses of the job.
- **Cape Malay and Cape Muslim households:** check what a doekoem is asked to treat and how that sits with doctors.
- **Chinese South Africans in Johannesburg:** check how Chinese medicine shops and practitioners serve the community and others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemiology, mining disease and HIV; mixed for healers' practice; thin for San healing in the community's own words and for mental health in African languages.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for igqirha and intwaso; isiZulu for healer terms; Afrikaans for *boererate*; Khoekhoegowab and N|uu for plant names; Dutch for Cape medical records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** HIV status and mental illness carry stigma; healers' training and medicines are guarded; the Life Esidimeni deaths and the AIDS-denial years are raw. The law on healers and on health insurance goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** healing, diet and illness-belief sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, excluded from the health sheet.
