*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Township residents in Gauteng and the Eastern Cape with street-committee pasts:** check how they recount the move from people's courts to police stations after 1994.
- **Coloured families of the Cape Flats, in their own self-designation, and Cape Muslim congregations:** check how PAGAD's rise and the prison gangs shape talk of who may punish.
- **Bapedi, VhaVenda and Vatsonga villages in Limpopo:** check accusations of witchcraft as a separate route to blame and redress, with Ralushai's cases as a start.
- **Basotho mine workers and their families in the Free State:** check Marashea rules beside village hearings back home.
- **Afrikaners:** check how the war-camp deaths and the TRC are each framed as justice owed, in Afrikaans church and press.
- **Khoe and San and Griqua descendants:** check how the 2002 reburial of Sarah Baartman at Hankey is spoken of as redress.
- **Jewish and Afrikaner lawyers of the political trials (Bram Fischer, Arthur Chaskalson):** check how their memoirs and biographies describe due process under apartheid.
- **Portuguese and Chinese South African shopkeepers:** check whether robbery victims report, and what their associations ask of police.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for judgments, inquiries and crime surveys; mixed for township and village hearings; thin for San communities' own redress. Trust in courts and obedience to law go to Capricorn.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for the 1883 evidence; Setswana alongside Plaatje; Tshivenda and Sepedi for the Ralushai testimony; Afrikaans for camp memory; Kaaps and prison argot for Steinberg's world.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** witchcraft accusations name living people; vigilante and gang sources carry danger; necklacing images; human remains and their return.
- **Held for review:** courts, punishment and redress histories involving South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, omitted from the justice sheet.
