## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Township couples in KwaZulu-Natal and their rural kin:** check how living together before bridewealth is named, and whether the families treat such a pair as a partnership.
- **Dutch Reformed and Afrikaans Pentecostal congregations in the platteland and Pretoria:** check how sermons and courses describe marriage as covenant, and how cohabiting members are spoken of.
- **Same-sex couples in Cape Town and in Gugulethu or Soweto:** check how each describes marrying, including meeting officials who once could refuse them.
- **Batswana and Basotho cattle owners of North West and the Free State:** check whether loaned-cattle ties still bind two households and who may recall the herd.
- **Griqua of Griquatown and Kokstad:** check how the captaincies' alliances are retold at church and in commemorations.
- **Wives in the Eastern Cape whose husbands work in Gauteng:** check how a partnership is kept across distance through phones, money and visits.
- **Co-wives in amaNdebele and amaSwati homesteads in Mpumalanga:** check how each describes seniority and shared work between them.
- **Jewish and Portuguese family firms in Johannesburg:** check how founding partners and in-laws divide shares across generations.
- **Chinese South Africans of Gqeberha and Johannesburg:** check clan and district associations as places where business partners were vouched for.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage law, cohabitation counts and coalition politics; mixed for business partnerships; thin for Khoe partnership forms and for cattle-loan bonds today.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for iqabane's struggle sense; isiZulu for Hunter's field terms; Sesotho for Moshoeshoe sources; Afrikaans for romance fiction and church teaching; Cape Dutch for the Griqua treaty papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** HIV status in intimacy studies; partner violence; same-sex couples in hostile settings.
- **Held for review:** spouse-choice, joint-business and alliance sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not drawn on for partnership.
