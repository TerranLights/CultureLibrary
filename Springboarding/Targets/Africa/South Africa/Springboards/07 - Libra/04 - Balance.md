## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balobedu villages around Modjadjiskloof:** check how the queenship's part in rain and order is spoken of now, in Khilobedu and in Sepedi sources.
- **Healers and households in KwaZulu-Natal:** check which hot, cool, dark and clean categories are used in treatment, and how clinic medicine is fitted beside them.
- **Coloured communities of the Cape Flats and Namaqualand, in their own self-designation:** check whether "in-between" is a phrase people claim, refuse, or turn around.
- **Cape Muslim households:** check how remedies from a doekum and hospital care are weighed against each other.
- **Karoo Afrikaans households:** check which boererate are still kept, and for which ailments.
- **Chinese South Africans in Cyrildene, Johannesburg:** check how herbal shops explain paired warming and cooling foods to buyers.
- **Western Cape voters against Gauteng voters:** check how provincial and national ballots split, and how voters explain a split.
- **Villages bordering Kruger in Mpumalanga and Limpopo:** check how residents weigh elephant and buffalo numbers against crops and cattle.
- **amaNdebele women painters:** check the rules of symmetry they pass to daughters, and where a pattern may break.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional checks, labor law and conservation; mixed for healing systems; thin for San ideas of balance outside rock-art scholarship.
- **Languages needed:** Khilobedu and Sepedi for the Rain Queen sources; isiZulu for Berglund's ritual vocabulary; Afrikaans for boererate studies; isiNdebele for painters' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' and rain-making knowledge is guarded; the royal succession of the Balobedu has been disputed; rock-art sites need permits; ritual animal killing is a live legal dispute.
- **Held for review:** healing-system, moderation and festival-calendar material from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not used for balance.
