# South Africa: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Tshivenda, Xitsonga, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps, and the urban mixed codes Iscamtho and Tsotsitaal; Khoekhoegowab for Khoi and San councils. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New wives in amaXhosa homesteads of the Eastern Cape and in amaZulu families of KwaZulu-Natal:** check how much avoidance speech is still used toward in-laws, in town against village.
- **Afrikaner negotiators of 1990-1994 and their voters:** check how talks with former enemies were explained in Afrikaans papers and at church meetings.
- **The amaZulu royal house and its supporters:** check how the 1994 land-trust bargain is told now, as gain or as concession.
- **VhaVenda and Batswana who lived in Venda and Bophuthatswana:** check how independence ceremonies and homeland envoys are remembered.
- **Vatsonga of Limpopo with kin across the Mozambique border:** check how visits, permits and family business across the line are handled.
- **National Khoi and San Council delegates:** check how talks with government over recognition are reported back to member communities.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check the Board of Deputies' representations to governments from the 1930s onward.
- **Chinese South Africans:** check how community bodies handled Pretoria's 1998 switch of recognition from Taipei to Beijing.
- **Portuguese South Africans:** check how community bodies dealt with the authorities when families arrived from Angola and Mozambique in 1975-1976.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1990s negotiations and foreign policy; mixed for everyday tact and respect speech; thin for San relations with neighbors before colonial records.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for imbongi recordings and Peires's oral sources; isiZulu for hlonipha vocabulary; Afrikaans for the negotiators' home press; Portuguese and Mandarin or Cantonese for community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homeland-era records carry collaborators' names; hlonipha bears on women's status, so ask women first; some negotiators' papers are closed.
- **Held for review:** envoys, interpreters and community-representation material connected with South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not drawn on for the diplomacy sheet.
