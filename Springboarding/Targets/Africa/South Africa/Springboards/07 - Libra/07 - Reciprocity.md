# South Africa: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps; Portuguese, Hebrew and Yiddish, and Cantonese for community records. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Funeral-goers in Soweto and Mamelodi:** check what attending a neighbor's funeral obliges in return, and how after-tears gatherings are paid for.
- **Vatsonga households of Limpopo:** check which marriage and mourning gifts are still given and returned, set against Junod's century-old lists.
- **VhaVenda villages:** check what is given to a chief's household and what is expected back.
- **Afrikaners of the Free State and Northern Cape platteland:** check how the Helpmekaar memory and church collections frame help to one's own and to outsiders.
- **Cape Malay households of the Bo-Kaap and Walmer Estate:** check the route of boeka and Eid plates and the custom for returning the dish.
- **Migrant workers in Gauteng from KwaZulu-Natal and the Eastern Cape:** check what money sent home buys in standing and in claims on a homestead.
- **Jewish South Africans in Johannesburg:** check how Chevra Kadisha giving is funded and who may receive it.
- **Portuguese South Africans on the East Rand:** check club and parish sickness and death funds, and the duty to pay in.
- **Chinese South Africans of Gqeberha and Johannesburg:** check rotating credit and clan loans in early community records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household transfers and savings groups; mixed for wedding and funeral exchanges; thin for San sharing customs recorded inside the nation's present borders.
- **Languages needed:** Xitsonga for Junod's terms; Tshivenda for Stayt's; isiZulu for wedding gifts; Afrikaans for the Helpmekaar papers; Hebrew and Yiddish for Chevra Kadisha minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** household debts and funeral costs are private; grant recipients face stigma; old ethnographies use dated labels.
- **Held for review:** gift-exchange, mutual-aid and remittance sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not drawn on for reciprocity.
