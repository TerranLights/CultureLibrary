## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bapedi households in Sekhukhune:** check when ditaola are thrown before a move, a marriage or a burial, and who has the last word after.
- **Basotho of QwaQwa and the eastern Free State:** check whether village meetings still talk until agreement or close with a vote.
- **Afrikaner voters of Pretoria and the Free State in 1992:** check how the referendum is remembered, as a choice made or a choice forced.
- **Coloured voters of the Western and Northern Cape, in their own self-designation:** check late deciding and party-switching across the elections since 1994.
- **School leavers in Gauteng and Limpopo:** check who picks a course of study: the learner, the family, or the bursary on offer.
- **Families of dying patients in Cape Town and Durban hospitals:** check who signs, and whether elders or ministers are asked first.
- **!Xun and Khwe at Platfontein, Northern Cape:** check how the community trust reaches decisions on land and money.
- **Chinese South Africans:** check whether almanacs are used to choose dates for weddings and shop openings.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check when rabbis are asked to rule on medical choices near the end of life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public decision-making and elections; mixed for household choosing; thin for how Khoe and San communities deliberate, outside trust records.
- **Languages needed:** Sepedi and Setswana for ditaola terms; Sesotho and French for Casalis; isiZulu for Shembe church sources; Afrikaans for the 1960 and 1992 campaigns.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' consultations are private; end-of-life choices involve grieving families; mental-health data needs care.
- **Held for review:** choosing, consultation and family-decision sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, outside the indecision sheet.
