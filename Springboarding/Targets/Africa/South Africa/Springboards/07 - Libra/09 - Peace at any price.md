# South Africa: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps; Sepedi and Xitsonga for Limpopo church hearings. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Basotho and Batswana women in the Free State and North West:** check who quotes the sharp-edge proverb in counseling, church and court, and to whom.
- **amaXhosa men in the Eastern and Western Cape:** check when the saying about tears falling inward is used to keep men quiet about distress, and how men's groups answer it.
- **NG Kerk congregations:** check how Naudé's break is remembered, as betrayal, as prophecy, or as both.
- **English-speaking and Afrikaner men of conscription age in the 1980s:** check what objectors and those who served say about refusing or going along.
- **Cape Muslim congregations:** check how Imam Abdullah Haron's death in detention (1969) is told beside clergy who kept quiet.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check how later generations debate the Board of Deputies' wartime and apartheid-era caution.
- **Township traders in Khayelitsha, Nyanga and Umlazi:** check who pays protection fees and what happens to those who refuse.
- **Khulumani members in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal:** check whether they call the 1990s settlement a peace paid for by victims.
- **Congregants of new charismatic churches in Limpopo and Gauteng:** check what the CRL hearings record about why abuse went unreported.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for partner violence, transitional justice and extortion; mixed for family harmony norms; thin for Khoe and San peacekeeping customs inside the present provinces.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho and Setswana for the proverb's use; isiXhosa for men's sayings; Afrikaans for Naudé and Krog; Sepedi and Xitsonga for Limpopo church testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors of abuse; extortion sources face danger; TRC testimony; living congregants and pastors named in hearings.
- **Held for review:** silence, endurance and appeasement material centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside for the peace-at-any-price sheet.
