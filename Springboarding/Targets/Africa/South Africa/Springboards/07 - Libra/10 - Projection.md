# South Africa: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, Afrikaans, South African English, Kaaps; Portuguese and Chinese for community histories; Khoekhoegowab for ‡Khomani and Nama counter-accounts. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself, and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Soweto households and Bushbuckridge villagers:** check whether misfortune is first put down to a neighbor's envy, to God, or to chance, and how town and village differ.
- **amaXhosa of the Eastern Cape:** check how the cattle-killing is retold, and on whom each telling lays the blame.
- **Residents of Alexandra and Diepsloot:** check whom people name as the cause of joblessness and crime, and how foreign shopkeepers there answer.
- **Afrikaners of different generations:** check how "swart gevaar" and "rooi gevaar" messages are recalled, and whether they are owned or disowned.
- **Jewish South Africans:** check community memory of the Greyshirts and the 1930s immigration laws.
- **Chinese and Portuguese South Africans:** check how campaigns, slurs and jokes once aimed at them are recalled in family accounts.
- **Coloured residents of Cape Town and the Boland, in their own self-designation:** check the jokes told about "Vaalies" and other outsiders, and what they say about the teller.
- **Bapedi, Vatsonga and VhaVenda villages:** check, from court and press records, who is accused of boloi and how kin respond.
- **‡Khomani and Nama writers and storytellers:** check how they answer the colonial images Coetzee describes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for witchcraft, xenophobia and apartheid propaganda; mixed on everyday blame in couples and families; thin for Khoe and San accounts of outsiders in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for cattle-killing oral sources; Sepedi, Xitsonga and Tshivenda for accusation cases; Afrikaans for 1948 and 1980s propaganda; Yiddish for 1930s communal papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations can bring violence on living people; slurs appear only as recorded terms; xenophobic attacks involve victims still at risk.
- **Held for review:** scapegoating, riot and enemy-image material concerning South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept off the projection sheet.
