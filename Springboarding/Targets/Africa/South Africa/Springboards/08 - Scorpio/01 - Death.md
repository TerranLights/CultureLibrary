# South Africa: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, Afrikaans, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab; Portuguese and Hebrew for minority mourning records. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Widows in the Nongoma and Mthatha districts:** check for how many months the black mourning cloth stays on and who calls its removal.
- **Bapedi of Sekhukhune and VhaVenda of Thohoyandou:** compare when a newly dead person is first spoken to as an ancestor, and by whom.
- **Gauteng households with roots in Limpopo or the Eastern Cape:** check whether the body is taken home to be buried, and what that costs a family.
- **Coloured families of the Cape Flats, in their own self-designation:** check how young men killed in gang violence are mourned on walls, shirts and street corners.
- **Khoe and San descendants of the Gamtoos valley and Griqua of Kokstad:** check whether the Baartman reburial is told as an ancestor's homecoming or as state theater.
- **Cape Muslim families:** check whether prayer gatherings fall on fixed days after a death and what grief may look like there.
- **Afrikaner families of the Free State:** check how camp deaths are named in family memory beside the official count.
- **Jewish and Portuguese South Africans:** compare shiva and yahrzeit in Johannesburg with the All Souls visit and the mourning year in Madeiran families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality counts, AIDS-era deaths and memorials; mixed for grief and mourning; thin for suicide and stillbirth in the African languages' own terms. Wills and the deceased's estate go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Ngubane's pollution terms; Sepedi for Mönnig's Sekhukhune material; Afrikaans for camp diaries; Portuguese for parish mourning notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains still held in collections; children's deaths; suicide and HIV status are private and carry stigma.
- **Held for review:** how South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands grieve their dead; any such source waits for the author.
