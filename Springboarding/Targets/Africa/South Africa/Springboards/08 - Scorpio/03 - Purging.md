# South Africa: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Tshivenda, Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana, Afrikaans, South African English and Kaaps; |xam for the healing-dance narratives; Hebrew for mikveh records. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **isiZulu-speaking men of Msinga and the taxi ranks:** check whether men returned from deadly fights still seek cleansing, and from whom.
- **VhaVenda and Bapedi villages of the 1990s Northern Province:** check how relatives of those driven out as witches tell the events today.
- **Former exiles of the Angolan camps:** compare the accounts of detainees called spies with those of their guards and commanders.
- **Residents of Alexandra, Diepsloot and Durban's inner city:** check how the case for and against expelling foreign-born neighbors is made street by street.
- **Afrikaner congregations of the Free State and Western Cape:** check which faults led to censure, and whether readmission was public.
- **Cape Muslim families:** check what fasting and ritual washing are said to clear, as practice rather than doctrine.
- **‡Khomani healers and their families:** check whether the trance dance is still practiced in the Kalahari, and by whom.
- **Jewish and Chinese South Africans:** check mikveh use in Johannesburg; check what removals did to the first Chinese quarter on Commissioner Street.
