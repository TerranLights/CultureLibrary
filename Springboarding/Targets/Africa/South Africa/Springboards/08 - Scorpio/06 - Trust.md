# South Africa: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, Afrikaans, South African English and Kaaps; VOC-era Dutch for the first Cape trade records. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down; loyalty given back, broken trust and its repair.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Basotho households of QwaQwa with members working away:** check who is handed the house, the herd or the bank card, and on what grounds.
- **VhaVenda savers around Thohoyandou after the VBS collapse:** check where people now keep money, and what they say a safe keeper looks like.
- **Afrikaner farm communities with radio farm-watch groups:** check who is admitted to the network and who is kept outside it.
- **Members of prophetic churches in Gauteng and Limpopo:** check how members test whether a prophet's gifts are genuine.
- **Griqua and Nama descendants:** check how early dealings with the Company are recounted in their own histories.
- **Households in rural KwaZulu-Natal hit hard by AIDS:** compare reliance on clinics, healers and churches before and after free treatment.
- **Cape Muslim and Jewish traders of old Cape Town and Johannesburg:** check whether credit between merchants rested on a word, a co-religionist's guarantee or paper.
- **Township spaza customers and Chinese South Africans:** check whose shop allows credit; check the part clan and district associations played in vouching for newcomers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey trust items and scandals; mixed for informal credit; thin for older Sotho-Tswana and Khoe forms of entrusting outside the classic ethnographies. Trust in police, courts and the law is Capricorn's.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho and Setswana for mafisa terms; Tshivenda for VBS testimony; Afrikaans for Bosman's world and farm-watch groups; VOC Dutch for Elphick's sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fraud victims' losses; HIV histories; church members' donations; farm-safety debates are charged.
- **Held for review:** trust, credit and certification sources tied to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not consulted for trust.
