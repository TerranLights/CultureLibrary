# South Africa: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, Afrikaans, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab for Nama rain and drought lore. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or the country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Households of Khayelitsha, Mitchells Plain and the southern suburbs during Day Zero:** check who changed water habits and who was already queueing at standpipes.
- **Families moved to shelters after the 2022 eThekwini floods:** compare help received from the state, churches and neighbors, in their accounts.
- **Free State and Karoo Afrikaners whose grandparents were "armblankes":** check how that poverty is recounted, or left unspoken.
- **Basotho of the eastern Free State and isiZulu speakers of the Thukela valley:** compare how the Difaqane and Mfecane are narrated, as war, famine or migration.
- **Shopkeepers and residents of Pietermaritzburg and Soweto in July 2021:** check how street patrols and looting were explained at the time and since.
- **Vhembe and Mopani households in drought years:** check whether people turn to rainmakers, churches or boreholes, and in which order.
- **Nama of the Richtersveld and Namaqualand:** check drought memories and how stock losses are carried through.
- **Chinese and Jewish South Africans:** check how community bodies organized help for their own and for others under lockdown.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemics, disasters, unrest and household panels; mixed for personal and family crises; thin for Xitsonga and Tshivenda disaster vocabulary and for San drought lore inside today's borders. Emergency powers as written law go to Libra; the state's duty in a disaster as legal culture to Capricorn.
- **Languages needed:** Sesotho for Difaqane memory; isiZulu for the flood and unrest testimony; Afrikaans for the Carnegie volumes; Khoekhoegowab for Hahn's material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in the 2021 unrest are charged along group lines; flood victims are still displaced; personal crisis calls are confidential.
- **Held for review:** unrest, flood and epidemic accounts concerning South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left out of this sheet.
