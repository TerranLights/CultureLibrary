## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Soweto and Alexandra households:** check how people speak of hidden harm and protection, and whether churchgoers and non-churchgoers phrase it differently.
- **isiZulu speakers near Howick and the Midlands:** check how inkanyamba stories are told among residents and to outsiders.
- **amaXhosa of rural Eastern Cape and of Gqeberha:** compare impundulu and uTikoloshe accounts across the rural-urban move.
- **Afrikaners of Karoo towns such as Uniondale:** check how ghost lore and catechism classes sit side by side in the same families.
- **VhaVenda of Vhembe:** compare Stayt's account with what Tshivenda-speaking writers and elders say of their own beliefs.
- **Basotho of QwaQwa and Thaba Nchu:** check whether Casalis-era terms survive in present church and ancestral practice.
- **Chinese South Africans in Gqeberha and Johannesburg:** check which ancestral rites remain in family practice.
- **Urban South Africans who tick "no religion":** check how they describe themselves, given the census categories on offer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Nguni belief records and Christian teaching; mixed for measured folk belief; thin for nonbelievers and for Tshivenda sources. Magic, divination and healing ways belong to Pisces; belief turned into action is section 6 (conviction).
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Callaway's texts; French for Casalis; Tshivenda for checking Stayt; Afrikaans for catechesis.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a witchcraft accusation can endanger the accused; tabloid stories can mock believers; older ethnographies use period labels.
- **Held for review:** belief surveys and folklore sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set apart from this belief sheet.
