# South Africa: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Sabela (prison argot) where cited in print. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herd boys in rural KwaZulu-Natal who stick-fight:** check how older boys correct younger ones and at what point adults step in.
- **Eastern Cape initiates, their families and the clinics that treat failed circumcisions:** check how endurance is framed by each, and where medical rules collide with the lodge's.
- **Afrikaans- and English-speaking men who did national service:** check how each recalls punitive drill and whether they pass any of it on to their sons.
- **Cape Malay choir singers and Klopse members of the Cape Flats:** check who enforces rehearsal attendance and what lapses cost a member.
- **Cape Muslim households in Ramadan:** check at what age children start the full fast and how adults ease them into it.
- **Doppers of the Gereformeerde Kerk around Potchefstroom:** check how a congregation now handles a member's lapse, and whether kerklike tug still shows up in kerkraad minutes.
- **Learners at former Model C schools against township schools in Gauteng:** check who writes the conduct code and how learners contest it.
- **Bapedi of Sekhukhune and VhaVenda of Vhembe:** check what restraint and conduct the koma and domba schools teach, as the people allow it to be told.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corporal punishment and child-protection surveys; mixed on sport and the army; thin and closed on what initiation schools teach about self-control. How punishment is set in statute belongs to Libra.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa and Sepedi for initiation terms; Afrikaans for kerkraad and army slang; Kaaps for Klopse and choir talk; Sabela for prison-gang sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation lodge teaching and deaths; child abuse disclosures; prison gang codes; school hair rulings that touch on race.
- **Held for review:** school and household discipline research centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, outside the discipline sheet.
