## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Maroteng royal house and commoner lineages of Sekhukhune:** check how the rank of a chief's wives decides which son succeeds.
- **amaSwati of Mpumalanga:** check whether rank by clan and closeness to the royal house across the Eswatini border still shapes standing at home.
- **Zulu-speaking hostel residents of the East Rand:** check how beds, rooms and induna posts follow seniority and home district.
- **Domestic workers and employers in Johannesburg's northern suburbs:** check which forms of address each side uses, expects and resents.
- **Descendants of Free State bywoner families and of landowning neighbors:** check which family remembers being looked down on in the 1930s, and how the other recalls the tenants on its farm.
- **Women in Gauteng speaking of skin tone:** check how they say it enters dating, hiring and family talk.
- **Khoi-San revivalist councils of the Western Cape:** check how titles such as paramount chief are claimed, granted and disputed.
- **Cape Town and Johannesburg Jewish families:** check whether old lines between German-Jewish and Lithuanian-Jewish families are still drawn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich, sometimes overwhelming, on race and class; mixed on rank inside chiefly and church orders; thin on how San communities describe rank in their own speech. Race classification is heavy with harm: handle each source with care.
- **Languages needed:** Sepedi for Maroteng records; VOC Dutch for the Cape's rank lists; Afrikaans for Carnegie and ATKV papers; siSwati for clan seniority.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reclassification files name living families; colorism talk can wound; succession ranks in royal houses are disputed in court.
- **Held for review:** standing, class and rank studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, excluded from the hierarchy sheet.
