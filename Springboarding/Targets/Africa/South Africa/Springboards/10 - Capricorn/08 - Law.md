*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural women in KwaZulu-Natal and Eastern Cape areas served by traditional courts:** check which forum they choose for a family dispute, and what they say makes a hearing fair.
- **Sekhukhune and Polokwane households that hired Mapogo a Mathamaga:** check what they say the police failed to give, and when they stopped paying.
- **Cape Flats residents, Muslim and Christian, through the PAGAD years:** check whom neighbors blamed for the bombings and killings of 1996-2000, and what they asked of the police.
- **Soweto and Alexandra elders who lived under 1980s people's courts:** check how they now judge those tribunals and the youths who ran them.
- **Batswana of Moses Kotane and Ramotshere Moiloa villages:** check which quarrels stay at the kgotla and which go to the magistrate.
- **VhaVenda of Vhembe:** check what cases still reach a khoro and what kind of settlement closes them.
- **Afrikaans-speaking farmers of the Free State and Northern Cape:** check when a farm-watch radio group is called before the police, and why.
- **‡Khomani San of the Kalahari:** check how the community decides whether to accept a research or film request under its own code.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional thought and vigilantism studies; mixed on customary courts as lived; thin on Khoekhoegowab and San legal terms. Correctional figures sit with Authority.
- **Languages needed:** Setswana for kgotla hearings; Cape Dutch for the 1812 circuit; Tshivenda for khoro talk; Afrikaans for farm-watch radio chatter; Sabela for prison writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** necklacing and mob killings; PAGAD's violence and its victims; San consent rules apply to the researcher, too.
- **Held for review:** legal-culture, policing and community-court sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, not cited for law.
