## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former District Six residents and their children:** check how they describe the old neighborhood's daily order against the planners' word "slum".
- **Residents of informal settlements in eThekwini:** check how they set rules for plot layout, noise and quarrels in settlements they built.
- **Minibus taxi owners and drivers in Gauteng:** check who draws up route maps and rank queues, and which penalties drivers describe for jumping the line.
- **Households in Soweto and in Sandton during load shedding:** check whose meals, homework and sleep move to fit the schedule, and which homes escape it with inverters and generators.
- **Maidens and izinduna at the Umkhosi Womhlanga, Enyokeni:** check who sets the marching order of regiments of girls before the king, and how stragglers are brought back in.
- **Members of the NG Kerk and of the Uniting Reformed Church:** check how each now explains the 1974 language of a created order.
- **Nama herders of the Richtersveld:** check which elders name the day stock moves between posts, and how a late or early move is spoken of.
- **Vatsonga villages of Limpopo laid out under betterment planning:** check how older residents compare the scattered homesteads of their youth with the planned rows.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on spatial planning, removals and protest; mixed on household tidiness and routine; thin on Khoe and San ideas of cosmic order outside the rock art literature. The Group Areas Act's text is Libra's; here it is order as people lived it.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for the 1974 report and *Noem my Skollie*; Khoekhoegowab for Richtersveld herders; isiZulu for the reed dance; Xitsonga for betterment memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some rock art sites are kept unpublicized for protection; the reed dance's virginity testing is contested; taxi feuds can endanger informants.
- **Held for review:** removals, settlement-order and neighborhood-planning sources on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back from the order sheet.
