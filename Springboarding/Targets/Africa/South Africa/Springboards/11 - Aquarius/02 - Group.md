# South Africa: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab and the San languages for San Council material. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manenberg and Hanover Park, Cape Flats:** check how gang, congregation and sports club each recruit teenage boys, as families there tell it.
- **Kaizer Chiefs and Orlando Pirates followers in Soweto:** check how derby loyalty is handed down within households and streets.
- **Basotho of QwaQwa, eastern Free State:** check what lebollo cohort mates may say publicly about lasting ties to one another, and what stays closed.
- **Bapedi from Sekhukhuneland in East Rand hostels:** check whether men of one chiefdom side together in hostel quarrels, and whom they count as outsiders there.
- **Afrikaans speakers in Pretoria's old civil-service suburbs:** check how Broederbond membership is spoken of now in family memory and memoir.
- **!Xun, Khwe and ≠Khomani within the San Council:** check how three peoples with separate histories decide as one body.
- **Durban and Pietermaritzburg taxi ranks:** check how associations admit drivers and settle feuds between routes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on gangs, regiments and struggle-era youth groups; mixed on supporters' clubs; slight on how San bodies reach group decisions.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for the amakhaya material; Kaaps for gang speech; Afrikaans for Broederbond documents; Sepedi for Sebatakgomo sources; Khoekhoegowab and !Xun for San Council meetings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "impimpi" killings and necklacing are trauma for living families; gang codes are dangerous to probe; initiation-cohort teaching is closed; punishment of whole groups belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** union, sport and political-group sources tied to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept off this group sheet under the standing rule.
