# South Africa: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab and N|uu for language-restoration work. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **URCSA congregations in Belhar and Dutch Reformed congregations of the Boland:** check how each side tells the history of church-reunion talks and of the confession.
- **Eastern Cape communities that send sons to ulwaluko:** check who may propose a change to the lodges, through which body, and how a proposal is answered.
- **Students at UCT, Wits, Stellenbosch and the University of the Free State:** check how each campus framed language-policy and curriculum demands, and what changed.
- **Restitution beneficiaries in Vhembe and Mpumalanga's Lowveld:** check how members of claimant communities judge a restored farm a decade later.
- **Afrikaans speakers of Paarl, Bo-Kaap and the Northern Cape:** check whose Afrikaans each language-reform effort aimed to fix, and who answered with Kaaps.
- **Treatment Action Campaign branches in Khayelitsha and Lusikisiki:** check how members tell the movement's story as reform won from below.
- **N|uu speakers and Khoe revival groups in the Northern and Western Cape:** check what they count as restoring a language, and who teaches it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plentiful on movements, commissions and church reform; patchy on rural reform of custom; sparse on how San communities judge state programs done to them.
- **Languages needed:** Afrikaans for Belhar debates and taalbeweging sources; isiXhosa for the Ntsikana hymn and initiation coverage; Kaaps for recovery testimony; N|uu, with care, for revival teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation is closed knowledge and deaths are raw for families; addiction testimony names living people; the legal side of land restitution and state capture is Libra's and Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** reform-movement and resistance histories centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held out of this reform sheet by the standing rule.
