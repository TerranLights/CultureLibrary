# South Africa: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab for Nama mourning terms. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity), others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Groot Marico and the bushveld of North West:** check how local readers and visitors each hear Bosman's deadpan, and whether it is claimed as a local manner.
- **Eastern Cape families with a son who became itshipa:** check how kin speak of a man who stopped writing, sending money or coming home.
- **Non-voting young adults in Gauteng and the Western Cape:** check the reasons they give, keeping protest and indifference apart.
- **Retreat-goers at Mariannhill and Ixopo:** check which vocabulary of letting go each house teaches, and who comes.
- **Men in Sonke programs in Gauteng and the Eastern Cape:** check how participants describe having been taught not to cry.
- **Chinese South African families in Johannesburg:** check how silence about the apartheid years is handled across three generations.
- **Bapedi and Batswana widows:** check how mourning periods set limits on display, and how strictly they are kept in town and village.
- **Afrikaans farm families in the Karoo:** check how distance between farms shapes visiting, and whether isolation is spoken of as hardship or as choice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** solid for election, household and literary sources; uneven for everyday emotional style; meager for Khoe and San grief customs outside old records.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu, Sesotho and Setswana to read the old mourning rules; isiXhosa to hear how kin name a son lost to the city; Afrikaans for Karoo farm memoirs and the Marico setting.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "cold" and "warm" labels for peoples are stereotypes to record as claims only; widowhood rites are contested by women's groups; responders' trauma is personal; impartiality as a legal duty goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** studies of reserve, mourning and isolation among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, shelved for this detachment sheet under the standing rule.
