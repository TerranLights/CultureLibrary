## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngubane's KwaZulu-Natal and the Balobedu of Limpopo:** check whether the same sorting of misfortune (ancestors, sorcery, chance) appears, and who is entitled to diagnose it in each.
- **Bapedi malopo and VhaVenda malombo groups:** check how each separates healing possession from feared possession, and who the drums are played for.
- **Afrikaans platteland towns of the Karoo and the Free State:** check where boererate, the NG Kerk and the district surgeon each sit when someone falls ill or is troubled at night.
- **Cape Muslim households of Bo-Kaap and the Cape Flats:** check the doekoem's place beside the imam and the clinic, and which kramats are visited for what.
- **≠Khomani in the southern Kalahari and !Xun and Khwe at Platfontein:** check which spirit and healing traditions each community chooses to share, and through whom.
- **Johannesburg's Chinese and Portuguese South Africans:** check which shrines, saints' days and luck practices each keeps, as a contrast to the muthi markets nearby.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Zulu, Xhosa and Sotho-Tswana healing and witchcraft, and on asylum history; thinner on Tsonga, Ndebele and Swati sources in their own words. The tracker records no change to the pre-war lands, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the Pisces sheets are written for the nine present provinces.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Callaway's facing-page texts; Sepedi and Tshivenda for possession-cult terms; Afrikaans for the NALN holdings; Khoekhoegowab and |xam for the being names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiates' and diviners' knowledge is often closed; witchcraft accusation can lead to violence, so naming accused people is out; rock-art sites have access rules.
- **Held for review:** for this domain the standing rule parks the shrines, healers and devotions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
