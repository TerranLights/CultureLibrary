# South Africa: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab and San languages for older lore. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **TRC hearings in the Eastern Cape and in Mpumalanga:** check how interpreters carried isiXhosa or siSwati testimony into English and Afrikaans, and what listeners said they missed.
- **Kaaps writers of the Cape Flats and standard-Afrikaans writers of the Boland:** check who writes whose inner life, and how each readership answered.
- **Vatsonga and VhaVenda patients at Limpopo clinics and patients at Gauteng hospitals:** check how listening is described when nurse and patient share a language and when they do not.
- **amaZulu izangoma in KwaZulu-Natal and amaXhosa amagqirha in Bührmann's work:** check how each tradition says a healer comes to feel a client's trouble, and what that feeling is called.
- **!Xun and Khwe families at Platfontein and Afrikaans Border War veterans:** check how each speaks of understanding people met on the other side of the same war.
- **Jewish South Africans and Afrikaners in museum and memorial work:** check how each community's institutions frame suffering so outsiders can feel with it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on TRC listening and cross-cultural clinical work; mixed on survey measures; thin on Khoe and San empathy words outside the nineteenth-century archive.
- **Languages needed:** isiXhosa for Bührmann's healers and Eastern Cape hearings; Kaaps for Small; Afrikaans for Krog's and Matthee's originals; Xitsonga and Tshivenda for clinic talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** perpetrator interviews and survivors' testimony carry living pain and names; healers' accounts of sensing are often taught in confidence.
- **Held for review:** writing on fellow-feeling across lines by or about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set apart for the author.
