# South Africa: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; N|uu and Khoekhoegowab for language-loss sources. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (working definition proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zionist congregations in KwaZulu-Natal and Apostolic churches in the Free State:** check how members describe filling by the spirit, and who is allowed to enter it during a service.
- **VhaVenda domba participants and Bapedi malopo groups:** check how drumming and the chain dance are said to join a person to others or to loosen her hold on herself.
- **Cape Muslim ratiep troupes of the Cape Flats:** check how performers name the state they enter and who teaches it.
- **≠Khomani families who speak N|uu or Afrikaans:** check how grandparents and grandchildren describe the shift from one language to the other.
- **Former residents of District Six and of Sophiatown:** check how each scattered community tells its own breaking up, and which rituals survived the move.
- **Former Bophuthatswana civil servants in Mahikeng and Transkei residents in Mthatha:** check how each recalls the day the homeland ended.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on removals, Zionist churches and San trance imagery; mixed on homeland endings; thin on dementia and divorce in African-language terms.
- **Languages needed:** Tshivenda for domba songs; isiXhosa and isiZulu for river-people callings; Afrikaans for Marais and AfrikaBurn's Karoo neighbors; N|uu for revival materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domba and other initiation teaching is closed; possession and water callings are told by healers only as they choose; rock-art interpretation is contested among scholars and San descendants.
- **Held for review:** trance, merging and assimilation sources among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, kept out of the dissolution sheet.
