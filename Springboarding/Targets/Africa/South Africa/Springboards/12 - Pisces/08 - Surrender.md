# South Africa: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; archival Dutch for the VOC period. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving one's will or control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of bittereinders and of hensoppers in the Free State and the former Transvaal:** check how families speak of an ancestor who laid down arms, and whether the word is still used as a slur.
- **Mpanza valley families linked to Bhambatha and loyalist families nearby:** check whether 1906 is told as defeat, surrender or betrayal in each.
- **siSwati-speaking healers in Mpumalanga and amaXhosa amagqirha:** check how accepting the calling is described and what refusing it is said to bring.
- **Cape Muslim and Dutch Reformed families at a deathbed:** check which words about God's will are said to the dying and the bereaved in each.
- **Griqua and Nama community historians:** check how they tell the old Khoe defeats beside Elphick's account.
- **Hospice patients' families in Soweto and in Stellenbosch:** check how stopping treatment is phrased in isiZulu, Sesotho, Afrikaans and English.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on war defeats and the 1990s settlement; mixed on end-of-life acceptance; thin on surrender in Tsonga, Venda and Ndebele terms.
- **Languages needed:** High Dutch for the Vereeniging debates and Afrikaans for hensopper memory; siSwati for ngoma accounts; isiXhosa for the hymnal; isiZulu for 1906 remembrance; Dutch for Khoe treaties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "hensopper" and "sellout" still wound; the dying and their families need consent and care; ngoma training is restricted to initiates.
- **Held for review:** resignation, prayer and end-of-life practice among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set outside the surrender sheet.
