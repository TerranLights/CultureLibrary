## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vatsonga tinhlolo readers in Limpopo and Basotho ditaola readers in the Free State:** check how a throw is read and whether the reader claims to feel as well as see.
- **Karoo wiggelaars and the borehole drillers who work beside them:** check how a farmer decides whose sense of the ground to pay for.
- **Afrikaans readers of Siener van Rensburg and Dutch Reformed ministers:** check how each judges a vision, and where the two quarrel in print.
- **≠Khomani trackers in the Kalahari and !Xun and Khwe trackers at Platfontein:** check how each group describes reading a trail once the signs give out.
- **Cape Muslim users of istikhara and isiXhosa-speaking families who tell dreams at breakfast:** check whether guidance is sought by prayer, by dream or by both, and who interprets.
- **Casualty-ward nurses in Gauteng and clinic sisters in rural Eastern Cape:** check how a nurse's feeling that something is wrong is written up and acted on in each setting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on divination ethnography and tracking; mixed on everyday hunches; thin on survey measures of intuitive thinking for any local group.
- **Languages needed:** Xitsonga for Junod's terms; Tshivenda for divining dice; isiXhosa for ukuthwasa accounts; Afrikaans for siener and wiggelaar material; Tsotsitaal for urban talk of hunches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** diviners' readings and training are restricted, and outsiders get partial accounts; prophecy lore can feed political fears; hunches about strangers can carry racial bias, to be studied as such.
- **Held for review:** premonition, omen and guidance practices among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, excluded from the intuition sheet.
