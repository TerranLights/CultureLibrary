# South Africa: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English, Kaaps and Tsotsitaal. **Concept in one line:** what the culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law itself is researched in Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet takes the idea of wrongdoing and the wrongdoer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coloured neighborhoods of the Cape Flats and Soweto's old tsotsi districts:** check how gang members, mothers and neighbors each name the gang (family, threat or employer) in Kaaps and Tsotsitaal.
- **VhaVenda and Bapedi villages in Limpopo:** check whether witchcraft accusations and theft are handled by the same people and with the same words.
- **Stock-farming districts on the Free State and Eastern Cape borders with Lesotho:** check how Basotho, amaXhosa and Afrikaner farmers each name and judge stock theft.
- **Hangberg fishing families and Overberg divers:** check who calls abalone taking a crime, and who calls it a right.
- **Afrikaans readers of Deon Meyer and isiZulu viewers of *Uzalo*:** check which wrongdoers each audience sides with, and why viewers say so.
- **Gauteng mining towns where zama zamas work:** check how residents separate the miners from the gangs around them, and where talk turns into blame of foreigners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on gangs, violent crime and victimization; mixed on wrongs of the powerful as the public sees them; thin on Khoe, San and Ndebele ideas of wrongdoing in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Kaaps for Number slang and *Four Corners*; Tsotsitaal for Glaser's sources; Tshivenda and Sepedi for Ralushai testimony; Afrikaans for Meyer's originals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names of victims, accused witches and suspects must not be repeated; vigilante and xenophobic violence are live dangers; crime talk easily becomes racial or national stereotype, so record such claims and never adopt them.
- **Held for review:** crime, victim and gang studies naming South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, withheld from the crime sheet.
