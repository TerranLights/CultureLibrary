# South Africa: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** South Africa, region Africa. **Language(s) for searching:** isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, siSwati, isiNdebele, South African English and Kaaps; Khoekhoegowab for Khoe lore. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Basotho and Batswana families of the Free State and North West:** check how Difaqane-era dread survives in clan histories and praise, and how it differs between the two.
- **KwaZulu-Natal villages near Howick and Karoo towns near Uniondale:** check how each feared place is told to children and to visitors.
- **Township residents of Alexandra and Mamelodi during 2008:** check how they described fear of neighbors from other countries, and who refused it.
- **Afrikaans farming families in Limpopo and the Free State and Black farm-worker families on the same farms:** check what each fears at night and how each speaks of it.
- **Jewish South Africans and Portuguese and Chinese South African shopkeeping families:** check what each community's own papers and bodies say about safety and threat.
- **Mineworkers' families in Rustenburg and the Free State goldfields:** check how fear of the underground is told at home and kept from children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on fear of crime and political scares; mixed on anxiety measures by language; thin on Khoe and San fear lore outside the Bleek and Lloyd notebooks.
- **Languages needed:** isiZulu for Vilakazi and Howick lore; Sesotho and Setswana for Difaqane memory; Afrikaans for spookstories and the swart-gevaar campaign; Khoekhoegowab for ||Gaunab.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fear of crime and of outsiders feeds racial and xenophobic claims, to be recorded and never adopted; belief in the tokoloshe must not be mocked; trauma survivors need care.
- **Held for review:** fear, threat and safety reporting by or on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left out of the fear sheet.
