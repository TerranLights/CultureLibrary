## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eldest sons, middle sons, the *kenje* and daughters:** each leaves (or stays) on different terms.
- **Aul families in Kyzylorda and Turkistan** against **nuclear households in Astana and Almaty**: whether the decision unit is the person or the extended family.
- **Mangystau and Atyrau families with Adai lineage ties** against **northern Kazakh families in Kostanay**: test whether lineage elders' say over marriage differs by region rather than assuming it.
- **Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians of the Virgin Lands towns**: a Soviet nuclear-family pattern to set beside Kazakh households.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs** with their neighborhood and men's circles.
- **Lutheran and Baptist German congregations of Karaganda and Pavlodar**: congregations that governed themselves in exile.
- **Chechen and Ingush families** of the 1944 deportation: elders' authority in a minority setting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; family ethnography and literature are rich, measured autonomy data sparse and mostly national.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for epics, ethnography and online talk; Russian for surveys; Uyghur and Uzbek for neighborhood forms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride abduction and forced marriage; regional autonomy claims (treated as separatism); the 1979 events. Rules on marriage and self-government belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on choice or self-rule was set aside.
