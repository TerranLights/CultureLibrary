# Kazakhstan: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh, Russian; Uzbek, Uyghur, Tatar, Koryo-mar for the minorities' own new-year and first-day rites; Persian for one founding chronicle. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Kazakhstan, Aktobe and Mangystau (Amal)** against **the south (Nauryz as the main new year)**: two dates for one start.
- **Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians**: the January new year, Orthodox Christmas and the "Old New Year"; and the Virgin Lands as their families' founding story.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs**: Sabantuy as a spring start.
- **Koryo-saram**: the lunar calendar's new year and the first-birthday feast, kept in exile.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs**: Navro'z and Noruz with their own dishes and gatherings.
- **Germans**: Lutheran and Catholic calendars, and emigration in the 1990s as a family's new start abroad.
- **Qandas**: starting a household from nothing on arrival.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for calendar rites and founding history; thin for personal starting over (debt, recovery).
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for rites and blessings; Russian for Soviet and settler sources; Persian (or translations) for the founding chronicle; Korean for the Koryo-saram sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** founding dates are political (rival claims of antiquity); the Virgin Lands story may be told very differently by settler families and by Kazakh families on the same lands.
- **Held for review:** none; no founding or first-rite pointer was held back.
