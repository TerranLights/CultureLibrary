# Kazakhstan: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh, Russian; Ukrainian, Uyghur, Korean and Chechen for those communities' own hero lore. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The west (Atyrau, Oral, Mangystau)**, with Makhambet and Isatai as regional heroes, against **the south and Zhetysu**, where other batyrs' mausoleums stand.
- **Women** across all groups: Tomiris, Moldagulova and Mämetova as public figures, and the endurance of camp and famine survivors.
- **Russians and Ukrainians** in the north and east: the Second World War and the Soviet-Afghan war as family memory.
- **Chechens and Ingush** of the 1944 deportation: honor codes kept in exile.
- **Koryo-saram**: independence-era heroes held in common with Korea.
- **Uyghurs of Zhetysu**: their own folk and rebel heroes, such as Sadir Palvan (*check*).
- **Afghan-war veterans** of every nationality.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epics, war memory and state honors; thin for everyday and moral courage in surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for epics and Makhambet; Russian for war memoir and award records; Korean and Chechen for those communities' sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1986 events; the disputed "Panfilov 28" story; the deaths of January 2022; the 1930s famine.
- **Held for review:** none; no hero or bravery source was set aside.
