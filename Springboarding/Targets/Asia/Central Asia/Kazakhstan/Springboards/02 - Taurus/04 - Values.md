## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The south (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda):** shrine pilgrimage and denser religious practice, as a hypothesis to test.
- **The north and east:** Russian-majority towns and Soviet secular habits.
- **The west (Mangystau, Atyrau):** Adai and other lineages beside oil-town newcomers and new money.
- **Almaty and Astana:** urban professionals and the young.
- **Uzbeks, Uyghurs and Dungan:** Muslim minorities with their own clergy and customs.
- **Russians, Ukrainians, Poles and Germans:** Christian congregations and secular families.
- **Koryo-saram:** family and education priorities after deportation.
- **Tatars:** a Muslim merchant and teaching tradition in Petropavl and Semey.
- Comparison only: Kazakhs in Turkey and in Xinjiang.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in classical and state texts; mixed in survey data; state documents need reading against practice.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for verse, proverbs and sermons; Russian for state texts and scholarship; Uzbek, Uyghur, Korean and German for community bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious freedom and the treatment of minority faiths; labels such as "extremist"; values talk in political speech. Law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on what is prized was withdrawn under the standing rules.
