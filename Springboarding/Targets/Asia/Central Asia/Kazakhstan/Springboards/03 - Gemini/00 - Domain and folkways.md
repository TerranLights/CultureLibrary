## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West (Atyrau, Mangystau, Aktobe, Oral):** how much town life runs in Kazakh, and how oil-town migration mixes speech.
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan, Kyzylorda, Taraz):** Kazakh in town and village talk, with Uzbek as a neighbor tongue; compare with the north.
- **North and east (Petropavl, Kostanay, Pavlodar, Oskemen, Semey):** towns where Russian long filled streets and schools; whether and how a shift is under way.
- **Karaganda and the central mining towns:** deportee and Virgin Lands mixtures (Germans, Ukrainians, Chechens, Poles) in shared courtyards.
- **Almaty and Astana:** an older bilingual city against a capital drawing migrants from every region.
- **Uyghurs, Dungan, Koryo-saram, Tatars, Uzbeks:** whether each kept or lost its language, and how each teaches its children.
- **Qandastar (returnees, formerly *oralman*):** come from Mongolia, China and Uzbekistan with other scripts and accents.
- Diaspora comparison only: Kazakhs of Xinjiang, who still write in the Arabic-based script.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich; language use is one of the most studied topics in the country.
- **Scope:** Kazakhstan's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; the Gemini sheets are written for the core lands of the pre-war republic.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh and Russian for every layer; for the domain's minority side, Uyghur and Uzbek school records, Korean and German community histories, and Dungan oral sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language status is politically charged (the Russian-language question, the pace of Kazakh); slurs for rural or Russified speakers appear in online talk; language law belongs to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** one scholarly pointer set aside under the category "South Asian-heritage scholars writing on the nation's lands".
