# Kazakhstan: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh, whose sibling terms mark age and the speaker's sex (Cyrillic and Latin), and Russian; Uyghur, Uzbek, Korean (Koryo-mar), Chechen and Dungan for those families' own terms. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda):** compare household size and how many siblings and cousins share one home.
- **North and east:** Russian, Ukrainian and urban Kazakh families, to test where only children are more common.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau):** oil-town families where elder siblings may move for work.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs of the south and southeast:** their own sibling terms and seniority customs.
- **Koryo-saram:** Korean family forms adapted over generations in Kazakhstan.
- **Chechens and Ingush:** brothers' roles inside a clan (*тайп*) system formed in exile.
- **Dungan villages:** a Hui family system in a Kazakh setting.
- **Germans:** families divided by emigration, siblings in two countries.
- **Qandastar:** some siblings returned, others stayed in Mongolia or China (comparison only for the diaspora side).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kin terms, customs and statistics; thin for sibling violence, estrangement and adult closeness.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for kin terms, epics and customs; Russian for memoirs, statistics and care records; Uyghur, Korean and Chechen for their own family systems.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** levirate, polygyny and inheritance are matters for Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); children in state care; family separations under repression.
