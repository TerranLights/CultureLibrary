## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda, Zhambyl):** larger households and courtyard houses.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Kokshetau) and east (Oskemen, Semey):** Kazakh, Russian and Ukrainian households side by side.
- **West (Atyrau, Aktau, Oral):** Junior zhuz genealogies; oil-shift work splitting households.
- **Karaganda:** a mining center settled by deportees, with mixed families.
- **Almaty and Astana; remaining steppe auyls:** apartment life against herding households with seasonal quarters.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs:** *mahalla* and courtyard households in the south and southeast.
- **Germans, Poles, Ukrainians:** northern village families, many split by emigration.
- **Tatars, Dungan, Koryo-saram:** urban Tatar families, Korday's Dungan villages, Ushtobe's Korean families.
- **Qandastar:** returnee households from Mongolia, China and Uzbekistan, against the kin they joined.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household statistics, kin terms and yurt ethnography; mixed for family memory and hosting; thin for the home's emotional climate.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for house, kin and genealogy words; Russian for census and Soviet ethnography; the minority languages for each community's own household vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** zhuz and clan origin can touch politics and patronage; family violence and polygyny need care; marriage and inheritance law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled by the author.
- **Held for review:** none; no household or kinship pointer needed setting aside.
