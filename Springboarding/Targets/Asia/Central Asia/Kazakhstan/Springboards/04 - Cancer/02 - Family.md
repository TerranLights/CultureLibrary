## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elder, Middle and Junior zhuz regions:** the south and southeast, center and north, and west, where genealogies and their public role may differ.
- **Rural south against northern towns:** the reach of active kin networks.
- **Almaty and Astana migrants:** kin left in the auyl, kin who follow to the city.
- **Koryo-saram:** surname clans and written genealogies alongside Kazakh oral ones.
- **Russia-Germans and Poles:** families divided across borders by emigration.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the east and north:** smaller kin circles, often mixed.
- **Chechens and Ingush:** lineage-based families formed in deportation.
- **Dungan and Uyghurs of the southeast:** extended families centered on villages.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** long-urban Turkic Muslim families, often intermarried with Kazakhs.
- **Mixed families:** Kazakh-Russian and other marriages, and how children are reckoned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on genealogy, kin terms and marriage customs; mixed on adoption and blended families; thin on estrangement among kin.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for genealogies and in-law terms; Russian for statistics and Soviet-era family studies; Korean, German and Chechen for each people's own lineage records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** zhuz and *ру* belonging is touchy when tied to jobs or politics; mixed marriage and adoption across ethnic lines can be private; marriage and inheritance as law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the family-system and genealogy pointers raised no hold.
