## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families of Ulytau, Mangystau and Zhetysu:** children raised among animals.
- **Almaty and Astana middle class:** tutors, clubs and online parenting advice.
- **Southern villages (Turkistan, Kyzylorda):** many children, older siblings and grandmothers tending the young.
- **Northern towns (Kostanay, Petropavl):** Russian-language childhoods with kindergarten from early on.
- **Western oil towns:** shift-working fathers away for weeks.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs:** cradle feasts, cradles and the mother-in-law's role.
- **Koryo-saram:** first-birthday rites and pressure on schooling.
- **Russia-Germans and Mennonite or Baptist families:** religious upbringing in large rural households.
- **Dungan and Tatars:** Muslim upbringing in villages and in towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore, ethnopedagogy and birth customs; mixed on today's practice; thin on fathers' everyday nurture.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for lullabies, precepts and birth rites; Russian for health surveys, forums and Soviet memoir; Uzbek, Uyghur and Korean for each people's own child rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's health records; physical punishment is a matter for Capricorn (discipline); religious upbringing in unregistered groups.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on raising children was set aside.
