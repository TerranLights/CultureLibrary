## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herding families in remote districts:** dogs, sentries and kin as the first line of guard.
- **Almaty:** earthquake and mudflow risk; gated estates against old courtyards.
- **Northern and eastern Slavic villages:** charms, icons and church blessings of the house.
- **Southern *mahalla* towns (Sairam, Turkistan):** elders and neighbors as protectors.
- **Western oil towns:** company security and families with fathers away on shifts.
- **Uyghur villages of the Ili valley:** household rites and community watch.
- **Chechen and Ingush families:** kin guarding kin, as the community describes it.
- **Koryo-saram and Germans:** families who once had no one to shield them, and the memory of that.
- **Women and children in cities against auyls:** reach of crisis centers and police.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and NGO reports; mixed for household practice; thin for men as victims and for the protectors' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for epics, folk belief and place names; Russian for state reports, crisis-center material and press; Uyghur, Uzbek and Ukrainian for each household rite.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence and child abuse cases; the law on them belongs to Libra and its spirit to Capricorn; honor-driven "protection" of daughters touches Leo and needs care.
