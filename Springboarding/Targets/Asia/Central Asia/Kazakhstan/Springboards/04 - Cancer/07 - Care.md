## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural south and Turkistan:** large households where care stays in the family.
- **Northern and eastern cities:** older populations, emigrated children, elders living alone.
- **Western oil towns:** paid help and absent shift-working sons.
- **Almaty and Astana:** hospices, hired sitters, private clinics.
- **Remote herding districts:** distance from clinics; healers and kin as first carers.
- **Russia-Germans and Russians:** elders left behind by emigration.
- **Koryo-saram:** filial care inside a smaller, urban community.
- **Sairam Uzbeks and Ili-valley Uyghurs:** the *tabib* healer beside clinic and kin.
- **Dungan and Tatars:** village and town Muslim families compared.
- **Families of disabled children and adults:** care across a whole life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state services and folk healing; mixed on family care; thin on carers' own voices and on men who care.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for healing, visiting and in-law vocabulary; Russian for health and social services, statistics and city press; German, Korean, Uzbek and Uyghur for each community's elder care.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health and disability records; abuse in care homes; healer practices some believers hold restricted; who should care by sex is a Libra question.
- **Held for review:** none; tending, nursing and elder-care pointers raised no hold.
