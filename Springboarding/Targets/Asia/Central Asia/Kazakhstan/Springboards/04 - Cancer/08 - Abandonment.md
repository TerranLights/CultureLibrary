## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern villages (Kostanay, North Kazakhstan, Akmola):** depopulation and elders left behind.
- **East (Semey, Abai region):** closed military and test-site towns.
- **Karaganda and mining towns:** unpaid wages and emptied settlements.
- **Southern towns (Turkistan, Shymkent):** parents abroad, children with grandparents.
- **Almaty and Astana:** where most baby-abandonment and child-support cases appear in the press.
- **Russia-Germans and Poles:** deported peoples whose families were broken or left.
- **Russians and Ukrainians:** elders whose children emigrated after 1991.
- **Chechens, Ingush and Koryo-saram:** families separated in deportation.
- **Orphans and care leavers:** young people leaving children's homes at eighteen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on orphans, deportation and emptied places; mixed on child-support and baby abandonment; thin on adults' fear of being left and on estrangement.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for folktales, laments and village press; Russian for child-welfare data, archives and urban press; Polish and German for the deported communities' own memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare and adoption records are closed; famine and deportation are painful; Uyghurs and Kazakhs with relatives detained in Xinjiang (East Turkestan as comparison only) need great care; adoption and support law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on orphans, emptied places or absent parents was held back.
