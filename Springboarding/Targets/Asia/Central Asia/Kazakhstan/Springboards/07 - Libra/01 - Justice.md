# Kazakhstan: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and the 2021 Latin script) and Russian; German, Korean, Chechen, Uzbek and Uyghur for the deported and minority communities' accounts of wrongs and redress. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turkistan and the south:** the Ordabasy memory and village elders who still hear quarrels before any court.
- **Mangystau (Zhanaozen, Aktau):** justice demanded after the 2011 shootings, a separate thread in the west.
- **Almaty:** the 1986 *Желтоқсан* and January 2022 trials, each with its own claim for redress.
- **Akmola and Karaganda:** camp lands where descendants of the repressed seek names, files and graves.
- **Abai region (Semey) and the east:** test-site victims as claimants for corrective justice.
- **Germans, Chechens, Ingush, Koryo-saram, Poles, Meskhetian Turks:** deported peoples whose rehabilitation laws differ in date and reach.
- **Dungan of Korday:** a 2020 clash and its trials, read by the community and the state differently.
- **Uyghurs of Zhetysu:** the Uyghur-language press on courts and policing of their districts.
- **Russian speakers of the north:** proceedings in Russian, and how language of trial shapes access.
- **Qandastar:** returnees meeting a court system they did not grow up with.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for imperial and Soviet legal history and for codes; mixed for court statistics; thin for village elders' settlements outside the record.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for biy oratory and sermons; Russian for codes, statistics and rehabilitation files; German, Korean and Chechen for deportees' testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** torture claims after January 2022; the Zhanaozen dead; victims' families in streamed trials; rehabilitation archives may be partly closed. What people make of the law (trust, obedience) belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside on this concept.
