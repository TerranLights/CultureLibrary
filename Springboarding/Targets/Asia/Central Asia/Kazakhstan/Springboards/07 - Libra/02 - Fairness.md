# Kazakhstan: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and the 2021 Latin script) and Russian; Uzbek and Uyghur for bazaar and irrigation-turn customs in the south and Zhetysu; Korean and German for the farm communities' sharing rules. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West (Atyrau, Mangystau):** fairness at work framed as locals against foreign contractors.
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda):** water turns and land plots in densely farmed districts.
- **North (Kostanay, Petropavl, Pavlodar):** grain villages where late-Soviet leveling talk may linger among older people.
- **Almaty and Astana:** housing queues, mortgages and grant competition at their tightest.
- **Kazakh-medium and Russian-medium school graduates:** whether the national test feels equal across languages.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs of the south and Zhetysu:** bazaar weighing, haggling and market-stall turns.
- **Koryo-saram farm families:** shares within the leased-field gang.
- **Germans of the north and center:** church and colony sharing customs before mass emigration.
- **Dungan and Tatars:** small-trader and urban views of a level field.
- **Qandastar:** returnee benefits seen as fair by some and as favoritism by others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; attitude items exist in international surveys, while customary sharing rules sit in older ethnography and the press.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for feast etiquette and children's lore; Russian for sociology and regulators; Uzbek and Uyghur for water and market disputes in the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** resentment of returnees, migrants and foreign workers can slide into ethnic blame; the 2011 Zhanaozen strike grew from a pay dispute.
- **Held for review:** none; every fair-share and turn-taking pointer stays in the table.
