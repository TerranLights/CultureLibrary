## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Sairam, Kyzylorda):** shrine pilgrimage and Sufi lineages (*қожа*) near the dead.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau, Aktobe):** carved-stone necropolises and Adai descent groups; oil towns beside them.
- **East (Semey, Kurchatov, Oskemen, Zaisan):** test-site villages; Old Believer settlements of the Rudny Altai.
- **North and center (Petropavl, Kostanay, Karaganda):** Orthodox, Lutheran and Mennonite cemeteries; camp burial grounds.
- **Almaty, Astana:** funeral agencies, crematoria and online condolences against village practice.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan of Zhetysu and Zhambyl:** *mazar* tending and Hui-style burial.
- **Koryo-saram, Germans, Poles, Chechens:** deported peoples whose first dead lie in the steppe.
- **Uzbeks of the south; Tatars of the towns:** *mahalla* and imam-led funerals.
- **Qandastar (returnees) from Mongolia and China:** mourning ways kept outside the Soviet record.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for funeral and memorial customs and for the 1930s dead; mixed for inheritance practice and for the commons; thin for sex ways beyond health surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for laments, gravestone texts and village custom; Russian for Soviet and camp records; Uyghur, Korean, German and Tatar for each community's own cemeteries and rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine counts are disputed; test-site health records are partly closed; sexual-minority respondents need protection; human remains (Kenesary Khan's lost skull) are a live issue. Inheritance as law belongs to Libra. No scope file exists yet and the author has not settled the nation's exact lands; the Scorpio sheets are written for the core lands of present Kazakhstan.
- **Held for review:** none; no death-ways or inheritance pointer was set aside.
