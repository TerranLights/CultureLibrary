## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turkistan and Shymkent:** Sufi and Hanafi revival around shrines; Salafi turning set against it.
- **Western oblasts:** *Амал* customs; young men drawn to stricter piety in oil towns.
- **Aral region and Kyzylorda:** fishermen who came back to the water.
- **East (Semey, Kurchatov):** a test-site region trying to be known for something else.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Pavlodar):** Orthodox parish revival; Ukrainian identity reawakened since 2022.
- **Germans:** emigration as rebirth elsewhere; the few who returned.
- **Koryo-saram:** Protestant conversion through Korean missions; language relearned.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** old town mosques reopened; a scholarly class remembered.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan:** schools, theater and press; Dungan villages of Zhambyl.
- **Qandastar from China, Mongolia, Karakalpakstan:** a second start, and its frictions.
- **Almaty and Astana:** personal-growth trainings, life coaching and rehab clinics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official revival and rehabilitation; mixed for conversion and addiction recovery; thin for personal redemption after prison.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for the Alash texts and seasonal custom; Russian for rehabilitation files and treatment services; Korean, Tatar and Ukrainian for those revivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion away from Islam can split families; minority faiths face registration limits; drug-use questions touch criminal law (point to Libra); debates over Tengrism can turn political.
- **Held for review:** none; the revival and conversion pointers break no standing rule.
