## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Deported peoples (Koryo-saram 1937; Germans 1941; Chechens, Ingush, Karachays, Balkars, Meskhetian Turks 1943-44; Poles 1936):** each carries its own memory of being purged from a homeland.
- **Kurds of the south:** deported twice, and the 2007 Mayatas clashes (*check*).
- **Shrine-goers and Salafi-leaning Muslims:** a live quarrel over which practices are impure.
- **East (Oskemen, Ridder, Semey):** metal smelting and test-site fallout; cleanup as a daily topic.
- **Center (Temirtau, Karaganda, Balkhash):** steel and copper towns; who is removed after a mine disaster (Kostenko mine, 2023 *check*).
- **West (Aksai, Atyrau):** gas-field villages; flaring and resettlement.
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan):** *емші* healers and the evil eye beside clinics.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the north:** Orthodox fasting and confession.
- **Uyghurs and Uzbeks:** their own cleansing rites and fasts.
- **Ministries and the old elite in Astana:** post-2022 removals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet purges and deportations; mixed for anti-corruption and pollution; thin for household cleansing rites in written form.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for rites and the terminology debate; Russian for party files and agency reports; French for Castagné; German, Korean and Chechen for deportees' memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** post-2022 elite removals are politically live; healers and exorcism sit in a gray zone with religious authorities; deportation memory can set peoples against each other.
- **Held for review:** none, as no cleansing or purge pointer conflicts with the standing rules.
