## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Koryo-saram:** deported in 1937 as presumed spies for Japan; a whole people called disloyal.
- **Germans:** called a "fifth column" in 1941, labor army; the later choice to leave.
- **Chechens, Ingush, Karachays, Meskhetian Turks:** deported as "traitor peoples"; their own codes on informers.
- **Uyghurs of Almaty and Zharkent:** whether pressure from across the Chinese border shapes talk of informers, as a question to research.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the north:** since 2022, rival charges of disloyalty to one or the other homeland.
- **Qandastar and Russian-speaking Kazakhs:** charges of being *мәңгүрт* or *шала қазақ* ("half-Kazakh") traded both ways.
- **Kazakhs who changed faith:** called betrayers of the ancestors by some kin.
- **The three zhuz:** favoritism charged when a leader from one advances his own.
- **Prison subculture of Karaganda and Pavlodar colonies:** the inmate code on snitching (query «зоновские понятия стукач Казахстан»).
- **Astana and Almaty elites:** post-2022 fallings-out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era denunciation and treason labels; mixed for elite fallings-out; thin for measured infidelity.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for epics, Makhambet and forums on marriage; Russian for court news, memoirs and Party files; Korean and German for deportees' accounts of being branded disloyal; Uyghur for cross-border pressure.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** treason cases after January 2022 are political; the Aliyev and Ablyazov accounts are partisan and some are banned; informer files can name living families.
- **Held for review:** none; the treason and infidelity pointers are all in scope.
