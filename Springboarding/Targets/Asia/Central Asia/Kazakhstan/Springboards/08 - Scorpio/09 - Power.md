## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Töre and qoja lineages:** older nobility and sacred descent; what remains of their standing.
- **The three zhuz:** whose men rise to office, as a question asked in every era.
- **West (Atyrau, Mangystau):** oil wealth, foreign firms and local bosses.
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan):** dense kin networks and trade power.
- **North and east (Pavlodar, Oskemen):** industrial towns run around a plant.
- **Astana and Almaty:** ministries, holding companies and new money.
- **Russians and Ukrainians:** their share of plant and office posts before and after 1991, as a question.
- **Koryo-saram:** the paths by which some rose in farming and business, in their own histories.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs:** influence through trade, set against representation in office.
- **Germans and Tatars:** what standing they held in Soviet workplaces, to be checked.
- **Qandastar:** newcomers with little backing, as a test of *арқа сүйеу*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite politics and the khanate chronicles; mixed for local patronage; thin for measured power distance.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *кие*, epics and satire; Russian for political studies and the press; Chagatai and Persian for the chronicles' originals; Korean and Uzbek for community leaders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming patrons and protectors can endanger sources; the post-2022 transfer of power is politically charged. Authority and rank as such go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the khanate, elite and patronage pointers fit the rules.
