## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
Fischer's freedom-ways question here: what each founding population meant by freedom, and which institutions carried it.
- **Herding Kazakhs of the steppe and the south:** freedom tied to pasture, kin and movement.
- **West (Oral, Atyrau):** Cossack *воля* and the Junior Zhuz's risings, side by side.
- **North and center (Karaganda, Zhezkazgan, Ekibastuz):** former camp and special-settlement zones.
- **East (Bukhtarma, Ridder, Semey):** Old Believers and their flight from the state.
- **Germans, Chechens, Ingush, Koryo-saram, Poles:** freedom as release from special settlement.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan:** freedom as refuge; East Turkestan as comparison only.
- **Women and young people in Almaty and Astana:** dress, speech and assembly.
- **Qandastar:** returnees from China and Mongolia comparing what they left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet confinement and deportation; mixed for steppe history; thin for everyday private freedoms.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *küi* lore and the ethnonym debate; Russian for Cossack, Old Believer and Gulag sources; Uyghur and Dungan for the 19th-c. crossings in their own memory; Chechen and German for release from special settlement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest, religion and women's activism can expose living people; foreign monitors' reports carry their own agendas. Freedom as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** one pointer on a religious-freedom case, filed under the category "South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands" and set aside under the standing rule.
