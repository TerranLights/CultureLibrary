## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent):** Sufi and scriptural authority; dreams and saints as sources.
- **West (Atyrau, Mangystau, Aktobe):** oil-town news and rumor set against official accounts of the 2011 strike.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Pavlodar):** Russian-language and Russian-state media audiences.
- **East (Oskemen, Semey):** test-site families and the official health accounts given to them.
- **Almaty and Astana:** fact-checkers, independent outlets, the academic historians.
- **Russians and Ukrainians:** split audiences for Moscow and Kyiv accounts.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan of Zhetysu:** family memory of crossings and of the land left behind.
- **Koryo-saram and Germans:** community newspapers that kept their own record of deportation.
- **Qandastar:** news habits formed in China, Mongolia or Uzbekistan.
- **Generations:** Soviet-schooled elders against the Instagram and Telegram young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official history and for media; mixed for trust surveys; thin for folk tests of truth beyond oath and dream lore.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for evidentials, oaths and dream books; Russian for media studies and archives; Chagatai for Yasawi's text; Uyghur for the 1962 memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** archive access is partial; disputed history (famine, 1986, 2022) touches living politics; "false information" can carry penalties (law belongs to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none; no truth pointer was set aside.
