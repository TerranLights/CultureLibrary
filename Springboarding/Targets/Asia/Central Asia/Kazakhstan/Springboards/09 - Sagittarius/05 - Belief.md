## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda):** saints, shrines and healers beside the official creed taught in the same towns.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau):** pilgrim belief at underground mosques against scripturalist newcomers.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Kokshetau):** Soviet-trained disbelief and Slavic folk belief.
- **East (Semey, Zaisan, Ridder):** Altai lore of mountains and springs; Old Believer creed.
- **Almaty and Astana:** Tengrist circles, seers for hire, online nonbelievers.
- **Uyghurs, Dungan, Uzbeks:** their own omens, healers and imams.
- **Germans, Ukrainians, Poles:** church creeds and village belief after deportation.
- **Koryo-saram:** household belief beside Protestant conversion.
- **Qandastar:** beliefs kept in China and Mongolia, outside Soviet atheism.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and official creed; mixed for measured belief; thin for nonbelievers and for minority folk belief.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for myth, *қисса* and creed books; Chagatai-Kazakh in Arabic script for the Kazan prints; Russian for atheist propaganda and Slavic folklore; Uyghur, Dungan and Korean for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers and new religious currents may be called "destructive" by the state; nonbelievers may hide their view from family; conspiracy cases touch foreign relations.
