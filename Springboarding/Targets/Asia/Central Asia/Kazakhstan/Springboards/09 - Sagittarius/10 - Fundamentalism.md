# Kazakhstan: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and Latin) and Russian; Arabic for the movements' own texts; Uzbek and Tatar for older reform debates; German and Russian for the strict Protestant congregations. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West (Atyrau, Aktobe, Mangystau, Oral):** rehabilitation centers and press attention, to set against measured data.
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan, Kyzylorda):** shrine Islam and scriptural critics of shrines in the same towns.
- **North and center (Karaganda, Pavlodar, Kostanay):** Baptist and other strict Protestant congregations.
- **East (Oskemen, Ridder):** Old Believer strictness.
- **Almaty and Astana:** online preaching and the Islamic university.
- **Uyghurs, Uzbeks, Chechens, Dungan:** their own imams, brotherhoods and debates; no assumption of strictness.
- **Germans and Russians:** separated Protestant churches.
- **Young converts and returnees:** across faiths, beside those born in.
- **East Turkestan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan:** comparison only, for movements that crossed borders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state policy and press; mixed for scholarship; thin for members' own voices and for measured literalism.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh and Russian for sermons, policy and press; Arabic for the movements' texts; Tatar and Chagatai for the reform debate; German and Russian for the Baptist congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labels like "extremist" and "destructive" carry penalties and stigma; returnees and leavers are vulnerable; banned-group material may be illegal to hold. Religion law and its use go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** one pointer on a transnational missionary movement, filed under the category "South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands" and set aside under the standing rule.
