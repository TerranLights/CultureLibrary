## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Kyzylorda, Shymkent):** holy-lineage and imam authority may weigh beside the akim's.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau):** company managers and oil-field unions as rival authorities to local officials.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Kokshetau):** Russian-speaking towns where Soviet habits of deference to the boss may linger.
- **East (Oskemen, Semey):** test-site memory and distrust of authorities who kept secrets.
- **Astana:** the power vertical seen from the ministries.
- **Almaty:** the protest city with the densest civil society.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan of Zhetysu and Zhambyl:** village elders and imams, and their dealings with state office.
- **Koryo-saram and Germans:** association leaders and pastors as internal authorities.
- **Russians and Cossack descendants of the Ural and Irtysh lines:** an inherited military idiom of command.
- **Qandastar from China and Mongolia:** formed under other states' authorities, now learning Kazakhstan's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state authority and protest; mixed for parental and workplace authority; thin for minorities' internal leadership.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for proverbs, oratory and the aul's religious lines; Russian for police, polling and political science; Korean, Uyghur and German for associations' and churches' own papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest and criticism of officials can bring arrest; interviewees may self-censor. The permission share is scored under Libra's letter of the law, not here.
- **Held for review:** none; no authority pointer needed setting aside.
