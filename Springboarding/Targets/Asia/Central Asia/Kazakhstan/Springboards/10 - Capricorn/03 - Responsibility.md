## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Zhambyl, Shymkent):** large households where siblings carry one another.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau, Aktobe):** oil-field safety culture and environmental blame aimed at firms.
- **North and center (Kostanay, Pavlodar, Karaganda):** industrial accidents and the Soviet habit of the collective answering for the brigade.
- **East (Semey, Kurchatov):** the test-site region's long argument over who answers for harm.
- **Almaty and Astana:** the gig economy shifting risk onto the person.
- **The steppe aul:** herders answering for animals they do not own.
- **Koryo-saram and Germans:** collective blame imposed in 1937 and 1941, and its remembering.
- **Chechens, Ingush and Meskhetian Turks:** other peoples deported as wholes, with their own accounts.
- **Uzbeks of Sairam and Uyghurs of Zhetysu:** family businesses where kin answer for one another's debts.
- **Qandas families:** children who take on adult tasks in a new language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official accountability and historical blame; mixed for family responsibility; thin for the private weighing of fault.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for custom, childhood rites and aul disputes; Russian for courts, decrees and the famine debate; Korean and German for deportees' memory; Uzbek for the south's family firms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine responsibility touches relations with Russia and is politically charged; deportation memory is painful; juvenile files are closed.
- **Held for review:** none; responsibility needed no pointer held back.
