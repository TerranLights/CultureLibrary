## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe auls of the center and west:** horse and herd regimes setting the day.
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan):** prayer times and fasting as daily discipline, and stricter dress codes in some families.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay):** Orthodox fasting and Soviet school habits among Russians and Ukrainians.
- **Karaganda and Temirtau:** mining and steel shifts, and the boxing gyms of industrial towns.
- **Almaty and Astana:** private schools, tutors and fitness culture.
- **Uyghurs of Zhetysu:** the *мәшрәп* as a school of manners.
- **Dungan of Zhambyl:** village and mosque routines, to check from community sources.
- **Koryo-saram:** collective-farm brigade routines, and how families now speak of study and work, from their own accounts.
- **Germans and Mennonites:** church order and plain living.
- **Qandastar from China:** schooling under another state's discipline, now in Kazakh schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school and sport; mixed for home discipline (MICS gives a floor); thin for self-discipline as people describe it.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for horse lore, proverbs and Zhumabayev; Russian for Soviet decrees, cadet and prison reports; Uyghur for *мәшрәп* customs; Church Slavonic terms for Orthodox fasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child beating and prison abuse touch living victims; cadet and prison records may be closed.
