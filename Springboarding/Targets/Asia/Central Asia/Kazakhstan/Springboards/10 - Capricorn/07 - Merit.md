## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Shymkent, Turkistan, Zhambyl):** dense kin networks and strong competition for state grants.
- **West (Atyrau, Aktau):** oil-sector hiring and local-content rules against imported skill.
- **North (Kostanay, Petropavl, Pavlodar):** Russian-language schools and graduates weighing language tests.
- **Center and east (Karaganda, Oskemen):** engineering towns hit hardest by the 1990s departures.
- **Almaty and Astana:** universities, ministries and quasi-state firms where "career model" hiring is tested.
- **Aul schools:** small rural schools and their UNT results.
- **Russians, Germans, Ukrainians:** specialists who stayed and the language bar.
- **Koryo-saram:** professional careers and the Kazakh-language requirement, from community sources.
- **Uzbek- and Uyghur-medium school graduates:** testing in a second or third language.
- **Qandastar:** grant quotas and how their peers view them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for testing and hiring rules; mixed for actual practice; thin for private judgments of who deserves what.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for titles, *тектілік* and *көке* talk; Russian for test data, investigations and 1990s migration; English for publishing rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming nepotism touches powerful families; ethnic hiring gaps are a delicate subject; testing data by ethnicity may be withheld.
- **Held for review:** none; merit raised nothing for the review list.
