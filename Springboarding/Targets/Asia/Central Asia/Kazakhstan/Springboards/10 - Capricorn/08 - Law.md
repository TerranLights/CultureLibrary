*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South (Turkistan, Shymkent, Sairam):** sharia-minded families and holy lineages beside state courts.
- **West (Mangystau, Atyrau):** labor law, oil firms and the memory of Zhanaozen as a test of the law's fairness.
- **North (Petropavl, Kostanay, Pavlodar):** a Soviet-trained legal culture among Russians and Ukrainians.
- **East and center (Oskemen, Karaganda):** industrial towns with old prison camps, where the thieves' code has deep roots.
- **Almaty and Astana:** lawyers, courts and the Law and Order campaign at its center.
- **The steppe aul:** livestock theft and self-help far from the district police.
- **Dungan and Uyghurs:** community elders and imams as a first court.
- **Chechens and Ingush:** clan elders and reconciliation customs kept since exile.
- **Uzbeks of Sairam and Zhetysai:** *mahalla* arbitration and family courts.
- **Qandastar from China:** experience of a very different state law, and how it shapes trust here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official programs and history; mixed for trust and bribery; thin for informal justice in minority villages.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for custom and proverbs; Russian for court surveys, criminal slang and the nihilism debate; Arabic and Chagatai for sharia records; Uzbek, Uyghur and Dungan for community elders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bribery and mob justice implicate living people; criticizing courts can carry risk. The letter of the law and the permission share sit with Libra; the correctional-control share is pointed to under Authority.
- **Held for review:** none; the spirit of the law needed nothing set aside.
