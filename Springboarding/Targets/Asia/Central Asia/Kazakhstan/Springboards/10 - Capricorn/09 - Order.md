## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Astana:** a planned capital where order is a national showcase.
- **Almaty:** a Soviet grid with *арык* channels and sprawling informal outskirts.
- **Shymkent and the Turkistan region:** fast growth and self-built neighborhoods.
- **West (Atyrau, Aktau):** company towns with fenced compounds beside older settlements.
- **North and center (Karaganda, Pavlodar, Petropavl):** shrinking industrial towns and the upkeep of public space.
- **East (Oskemen, Ridder):** polluted mining towns and their own sense of order.
- **The steppe aul:** seasonal moves and herding routines.
- **Uyghurs of Almaty:** neighborhood order kept within the community.
- **Russians and Cossack descendants:** church processions and civic ritual.
- **Dungan villages of Zhambyl:** compact village layouts, from community sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for planning, policing and surveillance; mixed for felt safety; thin for household order as families describe it.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for camp and yurt order and taboos; Russian for police, planning and emergency records; Uyghur for the Almaty districts; Japanese for Kurokawa's own design writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** terrorism and emergency records are partly closed; surveillance raises privacy concerns; accounts of the January 2022 unrest remain politically delicate.
- **Held for review:** none; order raised no standing-rule issue.
