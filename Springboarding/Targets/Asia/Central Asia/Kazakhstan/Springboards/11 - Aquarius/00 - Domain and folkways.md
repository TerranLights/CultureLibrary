## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Steppe auls of the center and Torgai against the irrigated south:** scattered herding hamlets and dense oasis villages set different daily circles.
- **The northern industrial belt (Pavlodar, Petropavl, Kostanay, Ust-Kamenogorsk):** Soviet courtyard and factory communities, mostly Russian-speaking.
- **Oil towns of the west (Atyrau, Aktau, Zhanaozen):** shift-work camps and *жерлес* ties among migrant workers.
- **Almaty and Astana:** new-build districts, apartment chats and clubs, where newcomers from the aul meet the city.
- **Uzbeks of Turkistan and Shymkent:** the *mahalla* as an organized neighborhood.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan of Zhetysu and Kordai:** village councils, *meshrep* and mosque congregations.
- **Koryo-saram, Germans, Chechens, Meskhetian Turks:** deportation-formed communities with their own associations and memory.
- **Russians, Ukrainians, Tatars, Poles:** Orthodox, Catholic and Muslim parishes as civic hubs.
- **Qandastar returnees:** a test of how far "we" extends to co-ethnics raised abroad (Kazakhs of Mongolia, China and Uzbekistan only as comparison).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, hospitality and state "friendship of peoples" programs; mixed for associations and protest; thin for minorities' internal clubs and rural neighboring.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for oral and custom terms; Russian for the Soviet and urban record; Uzbek, Uyghur, Dungan and Korean for minority community life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest, independent associations and interethnic friction are policed topics; the *шала қазақ* label is a slur in some uses. Kazakhstan's post-war territory is not yet settled by the author and there is no scope file; this sign's sheets therefore take today's republic as their working ground.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on community and ideals had to be set aside.
