# Kazakhstan: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and 2021 Latin) and Russian; Uzbek and Uyghur for the southern neighborhoods, Tatar for old merchant parishes, Korean for Koryo-saram villages. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old auls of the south and southeast:** dense kin settlement where neighbor and relative overlap; test whether *көрші* means anything apart from kin.
- **Northern grain towns and Virgin Lands sovkhoz villages (Akmola, Kostanay, North Kazakhstan):** mixed-origin settlements formed by state transfer rather than descent.
- **Western oil and fishing settlements (Atyrau, Kulsary, Aral):** work communities exposed to boom, bust and ecological loss.
- **Almaty and Astana high-rises:** neighbors who never share kin, governed by ОСИ and chats.
- **Uzbek *mahallas* of Turkistan region:** the most formal neighborhood bodies in the nation.
- **Dungan and Uyghur villages of Zhambyl and Almaty regions:** endogamous village life under pressure from outside.
- **Koryo-saram and Chechen settlements born from deportation:** communities whose memory starts in exile.
- **Volga Tatars and Bashkirs of the north:** parish communities that served as Islamic hubs before Soviet rule.
- **Russian Orthodox and Old Believer villages of the east (Altai foothills, Ridder area):** a faith-and-place community with its own discipline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in the press and NGO reports for disasters and ethnic associations; mixed for neighborhood life; thin for small rural minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *жылу*, proverbs and mosque teaching; Russian for housing and monotown files; Uzbek, Uyghur, Dungan and Tatar for their own parish and neighborhood records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Kordai events touch interethnic violence and remain politically charged; work on deportee communities needs survivors' families' consent.
- **Held for review:** none; every community pointer here passed the standing rules.
