## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western oil oblasts (Atyrau, Mangystau):** high wages beside local unemployment; how pay gaps with foreign and northern staff are read.
- **Rural south (Turkistan, Kyzylorda, Zhambyl):** larger families and lower incomes; the region most often named in urban slurs.
- **Almaty and Astana elites:** inherited wealth and *мажор* children; the home of feminist and disability organizing.
- **Northern industrial towns:** Soviet-era pay leveling remembered against present wage gaps.
- **Uzbeks of Turkistan region:** representation in local office as a minority majority in some districts.
- **Uyghurs, Dungan and Kurds:** small peoples' standing in rural districts and land.
- **Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians:** shifting from the old majority to a minority in the professions.
- **Germans and Koryo-saram:** once deported "special settlers," now often urban professionals.
- **Women across these groups, and *қандас* newcomers:** equality debated across lines of sex and origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gender statistics and income data; mixed for ethnic representation; thin for disability and leveling customs.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *айтыс*, *Айқап* and present feminist writing in Kazakh; Russian for Soviet campaigns and statistics; Uzbek and Uyghur for minorities' own view of their standing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence against women and ethnic representation are charged; slurs are recorded as data only; equality before the courts belongs to Libra, the purpose of law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** one equality pointer set aside under the category "South Asian-heritage scholars writing on the nation's lands."
