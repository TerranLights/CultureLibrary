## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Volga Tatar families of Semey, Petropavl and Uralsk:** heirs of the teachers who carried the "new method" schools to the steppe.
- **Uzbeks of Turkistan and Sairam:** the southern Jadid line and its later memory.
- **Northern Virgin Lands towns:** reform from Moscow remembered by Russians, Ukrainians, Belarusians and Kazakh herders alike.
- **Semey and the Abai region:** the test-site campaign's home ground.
- **Astana:** a capital built as a reform in itself; where state reform programs are drafted.
- **Almaty:** civil society and investigative media pressing from below.
- **Western oil towns:** privatization and foreign concessions as lived change.
- **Germans:** emigration after 1989 as one answer to failed reform; those who stayed.
- **Koryo-saram rice farmers of Kyzylorda and Ushtobe:** Soviet agricultural reform seen from the collective farm.
- **Muslim and Orthodox congregations:** custom reform pushed by clergy, and resisted by families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state programs and the 1990s; mixed for pre-1917 reform press; thin for minorities' own reform voices.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh in Arabic script for *Қазақ* and early reformers; Russian for Soviet and state files; Tatar for the Jadid teachers; Uzbek for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reform movements outside the state are watched; returnees from Syria are a security matter; court reform is Libra's, the spirit of law Capricorn's.
