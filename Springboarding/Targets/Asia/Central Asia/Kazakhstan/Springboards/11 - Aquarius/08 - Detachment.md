# Kazakhstan: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and 2021 Latin) and Russian; Chagatai and Tatar for the Sufi manuals read on the steppe, Uzbek and Uyghur for southern mourning ways, Korean for Koryo-saram elders. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms count as calm, objectivity or equanimity, others as coldness, indifference or withdrawal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mangystau and the west:** Sufi retreat sites and pilgrim custom; a regional tradition of seclusion to test.
- **Rural south (Turkistan, Kyzylorda):** dense kin life, where living alone may be rare; check how isolation shows up there.
- **Northern and eastern cities (Ust-Kamenogorsk, Petropavl):** test whether older Russian and Ukrainian districts hold more people living alone.
- **Almaty and Astana:** newcomers' loneliness in tower blocks and the young's civic apathy.
- **Uzbeks and Uyghurs of the south:** mourning and family duties that leave little room for distance.
- **Koryo-saram:** elders' ways of reserve, and the emotional styles of their children's generation.
- **Germans who stayed after the 1990s emigration:** families split by distance, and the isolation of the old.
- **Tatars and Bashkirs:** Sufi reading circles and mosque retreat customs.
- **Herders and shift workers:** solitude imposed by work, against chosen withdrawal.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for apathy surveys and census counts; mixed for literary solitude; thin for display rules and composure customs.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for proverbs, laments and poetry; Chagatai and Tatar for the Sufi manual; Russian for urban talk and surveys; Uzbek and Uyghur for southern mourning.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** care-home and loneliness research touches family honor; religious retreat sites have their own rules of access.
- **Held for review:** none; every detachment pointer cleared the standing rules.
