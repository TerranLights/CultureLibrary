## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The south (Turkistan, Shymkent, Kyzylorda):** dense shrine country where *зиярат* and healers cluster.
- **The west (Mangystau, Atyrau):** cave mosques and necropolises, and Adai lineages with their own saints.
- **The north and Russian-speaking cities:** psychics, Orthodox elders and therapy side by side.
- **The east and Rudny Altai:** Old Believers (*кержаки*) and the Altai's mountain-spirit lore.
- **The central steppe:** scattered herding auls far from clinics.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan of Zhetysu:** their own healers, *mazar* customs and Sufi lines.
- **Koryo-saram:** what survived of Korean spirit rites after the 1937 deportation, and the Protestant turn.
- **Germans, Tatars, Ukrainians:** Lutheran, Mennonite, Muslim and Orthodox folk healing of their own.
- **Qandas returnees from Mongolia and China:** practices kept apart from Soviet secularism (those countries only as comparison).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and Soviet ethnography; mixed for present healing; thin for measured belief and minorities' spirit lore.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for the healers' and mourners' vocabulary; Russian for the psychiatric and Soviet record; Uyghur, Tatar and Korean for each minority's hidden world.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers and Tengrist circles may avoid outsiders; clergy and healers are in open conflict; suicide figures are politically touchy. Kazakhstan's post-war lands are still to be settled by the author and no scope file exists yet, so the twelve Pisces sheets work from today's republic.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on the hidden or the transcendent needed setting aside.
