## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern towns and villages:** mosque-centered alms and *садақа* at shrines.
- **Northern and eastern industrial cities:** Soviet-style collections at work and Orthodox parish charity.
- **Western oil oblasts:** company-funded charity beside poor villages nearby.
- **Almaty and Astana:** NGOs, online appeals and animal-rights groups in Russian and Kazakh.
- **Koryo-saram and Germans:** memory of being received in 1937 and 1941, and their own charities now.
- **Uyghurs and Uzbeks of the south and Zhetysu:** neighborhood and mosque welfare in their own languages.
- **Chechens and Ingush:** debts of gratitude to Kazakh villages kept in family memory.
- **Qandas returnees:** both givers and receivers of aid, often judged as "deserving" or not.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for charity talk, online appeals and state programs; mixed for measured giving; thin for minority charities and rural alms.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *обал*, *садақа* and the tale corpus; Russian for NGO and Orthodox records; Korean, German and Chechen for deportees' memory of mercy received.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-treatment appeals expose sick children's data; the deportation memory is a state-curated theme of harmony, so private accounts may differ; mercy toward offenders goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; every pointer on mercy and pity met the standing rules.
