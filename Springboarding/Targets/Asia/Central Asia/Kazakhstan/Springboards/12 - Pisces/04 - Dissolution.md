## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turkistan and the south:** Sufi lineages and shrine crowds where self-loss in prayer has a history.
- **Northern grain oblasts (Kostanay, North Kazakhstan, Akmola):** villages thinning and closing since the 1990s.
- **Ex-Soviet company towns (Arkalyk, Zhanatas, Kentau):** communities dissolved when the plant or the oblast went.
- **Almaty and Astana:** concerts, clubs and festivals as places to lose oneself.
- **Germans:** the 1990s emigration as the test case of a community coming apart, and those who stayed.
- **Koryo-saram and Tatars:** languages fading into Russian among urban grandchildren.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the east:** the end of a Soviet "we," and what replaced it.
- **Qandas returnees:** absorbed into the Kazakh majority, or kept as a group apart.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the end of the USSR and emigration; mixed for divorce and language shift; thin for trance, dementia and the inner side of self-loss.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *күй*, *зікір* and the household idioms; Russian for Soviet doctrine and statistics; German and Korean for the vanishing communities' own record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** loud *zikr* and religious groups are policed; former members may face danger; the 1991 breakup is read very differently by Russian- and Kazakh-speakers.
- **Held for review:** none; every pointer on coming apart cleared the standing rules.
