## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern towns (Shymkent, Turkistan, Sayram):** walled yards, dense kin and closer watch on modesty.
- **Northern and eastern cities:** Soviet apartment blocks with shared walls and different privacy habits.
- **Border towns (Khorgos, Zharkent, Petropavl, Uralsk, Saryagash):** lives drawn across a frontier.
- **Western oil towns:** shift camps and fenced company compounds beside old settlements.
- **Almaty and Astana:** gated complexes and therapy language of boundaries among the young.
- **Uzbeks of the south:** the walled *hovli* and the *mahalla* as a semi-public space.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan:** communities whose lines with the majority are kept by language, faith and marriage.
- **Russians and Germans of the north:** private garden plots, fences and the dacha as a personal domain.
- **Qandas returnees in new suburbs:** where the line between "locals" and "newcomers" is drawn on the ground.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for borders and land; mixed for household customs; thin for measured personal space and privacy views.
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for threshold and modesty words; Russian for border, housing and press; Uzbek for walled southern houses; Chinese and Uyghur for the Khorgos side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** border disputes and ethnic distance are politically charged; family privacy can hide abuse; land rules and their enforcement belong to Libra and Capricorn.
