# Kazakhstan: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Kazakhstan, region Central Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Kazakh (Cyrillic and 2021 Latin) and Russian; Chechen, German, Polish and Ukrainian for minority devotions; Arabic for prayer terms. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside the institution (faith and religion as institutions are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turkistan and Kyzylorda:** shrine visits, *түнеу* and Sufi memory around Yasawi's region.
- **Mangystau:** cave mosques and necropolises tied to Beket Ata and Shopan Ata.
- **The Kazakh-speaking steppe center:** ancestor rites at home, far from mosques.
- **Northern and eastern Russian-speaking cities:** Orthodox, Old Believer and secular seekers.
- **Akmola and Karaganda:** Catholic Poles and Germans with their shrine.
- **Chechens and Ingush:** Sufi circles from the deportation years.
- **Koryo-saram:** ancestor rites beside Protestant churches.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan:** Islam kept in their own mosques, languages and festivals.
- **Tatars:** urban Muslims with their own reformist past.
- **Young urban seekers:** Tengrist, charismatic or "spiritual but not religious."

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for shrines and home rites; mixed for minority devotions; thin for measured experience (no national survey of spiritual experience known).
- **Languages needed:** Kazakh for *кие*, *түнеу* and home rites; Russian for Orthodox and Soviet files; Chechen, Polish and German for minority devotions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered groups and Sufi circles are watched; healers and new movements may face prosecution; some sites ask visitors not to film.
- **Held for review:** none; no spiritual-practice pointer fell under the standing rules.
