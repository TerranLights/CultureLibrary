## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern oblasts (Turkistan, Zhambyl):** livestock theft, migrant labor abuse and dense kin pressure on offenders' families.
- **Taraz and Almaty:** the remembered criminal groups of the 1990s.
- **Karaganda and the north:** camp heritage and prison speech in everyday Russian.
- **Western oil towns:** where labor protest and oil money both raise the question of who the real wrongdoer is.
- **Astana:** officials' corruption cases as the face of "crimes of the powerful."
- **Chechens, Ingush and Kurds:** groups tagged in crime stereotypes, to be recorded and tested, never adopted.
- **Uzbek and Kyrgyz labor migrants:** victims more often than offenders, a question to measure.
- **Russians and Ukrainians:** carriers of the Soviet criminal-world vocabulary.
- **Uyghurs and Dungan:** whether community elders settle wrongs before they reach the police.
- **Qandas returnees:** newcomers accused or victimized in local disputes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corruption talk, crime news and 1990s memory; mixed for victimization; thin for how wrongdoers themselves see crime.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for prison slang and official statistics; Kazakh for proverbs, sermons and rural cases; Uzbek and Chechen where those communities are named.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crime news can harm named families; ethnic labeling is inflammatory; forced-labor victims need protection; any question of what the law says goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no crime pointer had to be held back.
