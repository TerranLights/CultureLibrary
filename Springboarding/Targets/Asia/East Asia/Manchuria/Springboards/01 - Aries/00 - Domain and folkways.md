# Manchuria: Domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia; a culture-nation of its own, distinct from the Sinian Federation. **Language(s) for searching:** Northeastern Mandarin (Chinese, simplified and traditional characters); Korean (Yanbian usage); Manchu; Mongolian (Khorchin and Harqin speech, traditional script); Russian; Daur, Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe (Nanai) and Xibe for the smaller peoples; Japanese for colonial-era sources. **Concept in one line:** the domain of self and identity: what a self is here, and how it asserts itself; folkway: naming ways (runner-up, sport ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han Northeasterners by migration origin** (Shandong, Hebei, Henan forebears): family naming patterns may keep a home-province trace three or four generations on.
- **Manchu families of old banner descent and those who re-registered as Manchu after 1980:** two routes into one ethnic label, possibly two senses of self.
- **Chaoxianzu of Yanbian and the scattered Korean villages of Heilongjiang and Liaoning:** bilingual naming and a different first-birthday and naming cycle.
- **Khorchin, Harqin, Gorlos and Dorbod Mongols:** steppe-edge naming and lineage memory under strong Han contact.
- **Daur, Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe:** small peoples with clan-based personhood and recent shifts from hunting and fishing.
- **Xibe and Hui of Shenyang, and Russian-descended families of Ergun:** minority identities in city and border settings.
- **Port Dalian, industrial Shenyang and Anshan, and Harbin:** three urban self-images shaped by different foreign pasts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing and colonial-era sources and Han Northeastern folklore; mixed for Korean and Mongol communities; thin for current Hezhe, Oroqen and Evenki self-description. Spoken Manchu survives in very few villages, so Manchu-language sources are mostly archival.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheets are written for the core lands (the three former northeastern provinces and the eastern Inner Mongolian leagues long counted as Manchuria).
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Manchu (reading), Mongolian, Russian, Japanese; the Tungusic and Daur dictionaries for the small peoples.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Manchukuo era; the dispute over Goguryeo and Bohai history (the "Northeast Project", 东北工程); ethnic reclassification; clan shaman rites some families keep private. Popular "Northeasterner" stereotypes (bold, loud) are claims to test, never data.
- **Held for review:** none.
