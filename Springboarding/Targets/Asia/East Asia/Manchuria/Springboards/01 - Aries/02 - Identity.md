# Manchuria: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (Northeastern Mandarin); Korean; Manchu; Mongolian; Russian; Japanese for the colonial era; Evenki, Oroqen, Daur, Hezhe and Xibe terms for self-names. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han families who still name a Shandong or Hebei 祖籍 against those who answer only "Northeasterner":** two different first answers to "who are you?"
- **Manchu speakers' last villages (e.g. Sanjiazi, Fuyu county) against urban Manchus without the language:** identity with and without the tongue.
- **Chaoxianzu in Yanbian against those who have worked in South Korea and returned:** a label tested by the other Korean state (diaspora as comparison only).
- **Khorchin farmers and Hulunbuir herders:** language, livelihood and Mongolness pulled apart.
- **Aoluguya Evenki, Solon Evenki and Oroqen after resettlement:** a hunting identity without the hunt.
- **Russian-descended families of Ergun and the families of Japanese orphans:** mixed descent and recorded nationality.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and literature; mixed for present-day surveys; thin and mostly outsider-written for the Tungusic peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Manchu (archival), Mongolian, Russian, Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification and its politics; claims over Goguryeo, Bohai and Paektu/Changbai; Manchukuo memory; identity talk among North Korean border crossers (danger to informants).
- **Held for review:** none.
