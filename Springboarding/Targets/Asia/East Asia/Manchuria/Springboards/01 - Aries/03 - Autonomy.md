# Manchuria: Autonomy (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former state-farm families of the Heilongjiang reclamation zone (北大荒):** lives once organized end to end by a farm bureau.
- **Only children of 1980s-1990s cities against rural siblings:** parental reach differs with family size.
- **Women of older small-town generations against urban women now:** spouse choice and divorce decisions.
- **Korean villages emptied by labor migration:** elders deciding alone, children deciding abroad.
- **Oroqen and Daur households:** clan authority, state programs and personal choice in tension.
- **Hulunbuir herders against Khorchin farmers:** whether land tenure leaves a family free to move.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and family sociology; mixed in surveys (regional samples small); thin for clan decision-making today.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu archival reading.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and regional self-government is a legal and political subject: its legal side goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); elections and petitions can be sensitive for informants. Clan matters may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
