## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han families whose story starts with the journey north:** origin placed in an arrival, not an ancestral land.
- **Manchu lineages with an origin tied to Changbai and the Hetu Ala homeland:** origin placed in a mountain and a founder.
- **Chaoxianzu:** a border crossing as the family's first page, and Korean first-birthday customs.
- **Khorchin Mongols:** the new year of the white month alongside the Chinese calendar.
- **Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe:** festivals newly founded for peoples whose seasonal round is old.
- **Reclamation-farm and railway-city families (Beidahuang, Harbin):** places that count time from a founding by outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, myths and migration history; mixed for fresh-start statistics by region; thin for Tungusic peoples' own accounts of origins.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Russian, Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin claims (Hongshan, Goguryeo, Bohai, Paektu/Changbai) are politically charged; settler "new land" stories overlay Indigenous presence. Almanac day-choosing is folk practice to describe, not a classifier.
- **Held for review:** none.
