# Manchuria: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian; Hezhe, Oroqen and Daur hero-tale terms through Chinese-language collections. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women fighters and nurses against male soldiers in the official record:** whose courage gets a monument.
- **Greater Khingan foresters and firefighters:** physical courage as a job, not a legend.
- **Coal-town families of Hegang, Jixi and Fuxin:** daily risk, and how widows and sons speak of it.
- **Chaoxianzu:** two states honor the same 1920 fighters in different ways.
- **Khorchin Mongols:** a rebel hero kept alive in song.
- **Hezhe, Oroqen, Daur and Evenki:** hunter-hero ideals from oral epic, now performed as heritage.
- **Old banner lineages:** pride in ancestors' Qing titles against a modern civilian life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in commemoration and literature; mixed for everyday courage; thin for surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Hezhe and Oroqen material is mostly in Chinese transcription.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory (Japan, Korea, the civil war) is shaped by state narratives; independence-army memory is contested between states; some hunting rites may be private to clans.
- **Held for review:** none.
