# Manchuria: The domain, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria (the post-war nation), region East Asia, carrying lands from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern and Jiao-Liao varieties), Korean (Yanbian norm), Manchu (archival), Mongolian (Khorchin), Russian (Harbin records); Nanai (Hezhe), Oroqen, Evenki, Daur and Xibe where sources exist. **Concept in one line:** the domain question: what is worth having, and how is it held? (possessions, money, resources, food, dress and wealth ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han descendants of the "Crossing to Guandong" migrants (闯关东) from Shandong and Hebei:** household thrift and gift customs may carry their source provinces' patterns.
- **Manchu communities in the autonomous counties (for example Xinbin, Xiuyan, Yitong) and urban banner descendants:** stipend-era and clan-held ideas of property could differ from settler ones.
- **Yanbian and scattered Korean (Chaoxianzu) villages:** rice-farming households with heavy labor migration to South Korea; remittance wealth is a separate track.
- **Khorchin, Dörbet and Gorlos Mongols:** herd and pasture wealth against grain wealth.
- **Nanai (Hezhe), Oroqen, Evenki and Daur:** fishing, hunting and reindeer-related economies with their own goods of value.
- **Hui Muslims in Shenyang, Harbin and Qiqihar:** halal food ways and mosque-centered giving; Harbin's Russian-era and Dalian's port heritage add foreign-shaped display.
- **Rust-belt industrial cities against state-farm (Beidahuang) and forest-zone towns:** three distinct pasts of who provided goods.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing court goods, colonial-era surveys and post-1978 household statistics; mixed for Indigenous peoples' own accounts; thin in Manchu as a living language.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu (Möllendorff); Japanese for colonial surveys; Korean; Russian; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial-era sources carry the occupier's framing; some Indigenous and shamanic ritual objects may be restricted by their communities. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; these sheets are written for the core Northeast.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search a library catalog for Yan Yunxiang, *The Flow of Gifts*, and pull its gift-list tables and chapter on wedding payments.
2. Run "东北三宝" + "人参 貂皮 乌拉草" in a Chinese academic database (CNKI) to trace the saying's versions and dates.
3. Download the latest Heilongjiang Statistical Yearbook and locate the household survey tables on durable goods per 100 households.
4. Search the Dalian Library catalog for 満鉄 surveys with 物価 (prices) or 生活 (livelihood) in the title.
5. Open the Hezhe Yimakan nomination file on the UNESCO heritage site for named tellers and texts.
