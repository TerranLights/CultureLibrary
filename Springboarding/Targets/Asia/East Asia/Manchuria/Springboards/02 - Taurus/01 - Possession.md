# Manchuria: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, post-war nation in East Asia (lands from pre-war China). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Korean, Manchu, Mongolian, Russian; Oroqen, Evenki and Nanai terms through their dictionaries. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu clans in rural Xinbin, Qingyuan and Kuandian against city Manchu households:** whether ritual boxes and genealogies are still kept in the house or moved to halls and museums.
- **Oroqen and Evenki families settled since the 1950s:** settlement changed what could be carried and stored.
- **Hezhe (Nanai) fishing villages at Jiejinkou and Bacha:** fish-skin and net gear as personal kit against household goods.
- **Korean villages in Yanbian and around Mudanjiang:** households emptied by migration abroad, with things left in parents' care.
- **Mongol households in Dörbet and Qian Gorlos:** saddles, bowls and silver as personal possessions.
- **Rural Han households of migrant descent:** what great-grandparents brought from Shandong, and whether it survives.
- **Rust-belt apartment dwellers relocated by redevelopment:** what moved into new flats and what was discarded.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in museums, folklore anthologies and fiction, thin in direct studies of present-day attachment to objects.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Japanese for repatriate records; Korean; Manchu for older ritual terms; Russian for émigré memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan ritual boxes may be closed to outsiders; Indigenous objects held by museums may be contested by their communities; repatriate memoirs carry settler grief and the settlers' framing.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for "祖宗板" OR "妈妈口袋" with "满族" to find studies of household ritual objects.
2. Locate the Liaoning volume of 中国民间故事集成 and scan its index for treasure, hoard and lost-object tales.
3. Search the CFPS codebook for its durable-goods module and confirm the Liaoning oversample.
4. Run "鄂伦春 桦皮 器物" in a museum or ICH database for catalogs of carried goods.
5. Search the Maizuru museum's online collection for items brought back from Manchuria.
