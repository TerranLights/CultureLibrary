# Manchuria: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the post-war nation of Manchuria (East Asia), formed from lands of pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Manchu (archival), Japanese (colonial records), Korean, Mongolian, Russian. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of banner households in Liaoning:** their families' land rested on Qing grants rather than purchase or clearing.
- **Descendants of Shandong and Hebei settlers who cleared land in the north:** claims began with clearing and leasing from banners or Mongols.
- **Korean farming villages in Yanbian and the Mudanjiang basin:** tenancy, colonial land companies and later collectivization layered on one community.
- **Khorchin, Gorlos and Dörbet Mongols:** pasture held in common, then opened, leased and contracted.
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhe and Daur:** hunting, fishing and reindeer lands recognized differently by Qing, colonial and later governments.
- **State-farm families and forest-industry workers:** land and housing owned by an enterprise, not by them.
- **Urban flat owners in rust-belt cities against Dalian and Shenyang buyers:** the same deed, very different worth.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing and colonial tenure records and for post-1978 statistics; mixed for herders' and hunters' own accounts of their lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and classical Chinese; Manchu; Japanese; Korean; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial land seizures, land-reform violence and the collapse of Indigenous hunting rights remain painful; property law itself belongs to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit) and is not scouted here.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search a library for Isett (2007) and follow its notes into the Liaoning Provincial Archives' 黑图档 holdings.
2. Run "満洲旧慣調査報告" in the National Diet Library (Japan) digital collections to see which volumes are online.
3. Search CNKI for "蒙地 放垦 科尔沁" to find studies of the opening of Mongol pasture.
4. Pull the Heilongjiang report of the 2016 agricultural census and find the tables on land leased in and out.
5. Search news archives for "鹤岗 房价" (2019) to gather coverage and owner interviews.
