# Manchuria: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, a post-war East Asian nation whose lands come from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (with Northeastern dialect words), Korean, Manchu, Mongolian; Russian and Japanese for minority and historical records. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Laid-off state-factory workers in Shenyang, Fushun, Anshan and Harbin, and their grown children:** two generations whose worth was tied to the same factories.
- **Banner-descended Manchus in Liaoning against Manchus who registered in the 1980s and 1990s:** inherited standing against a recovered one.
- **Yanbian Koreans with family working in South Korea (diaspora as comparison only):** worth judged by kin abroad and neighbors at home.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe after settlement:** how communities ranked "primitive" by outsiders speak of their own worth.
- **Dörbet and Fuxin Mongols:** worth through horsemanship and song against worth through schooling and office.
- **Rural Han farmers on the Songnen and Sanjiang plains:** the country person as comic figure, and the farmer's own pride in grain.
- **Japanese left-behind children and their adoptive Northeastern families:** belonging and worth across two nations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in film, fiction and comedy; mixed in surveys; thin in self-esteem studies sorted by ethnic group.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin with Northeastern dialect; Korean; Mongolian; Manchu for banner-era terms; Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mass layoffs, state ethnic ranking and colonial-era children are painful topics; regional comedy trades on stereotypes and is material to study, never a description of the people.
- **Held for review:** none.
