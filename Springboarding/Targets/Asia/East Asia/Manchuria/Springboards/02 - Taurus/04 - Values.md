# Manchuria: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria (post-war; East Asia), on lands taken over from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, classical Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian; Oroqen, Evenki and Nanai through translated collections. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu clans that still hold household rites and those that do not:** whether ritual duty ranks above other obligations in each.
- **Korean villages in Yanbian:** filial duty under strain when the working generation is abroad.
- **Hui Muslims of Shenyang, Harbin and Qiqihar:** religious law and halal practice as a fixed ranking; separate from neighbors.
- **Protestant congregations, Buddhist temples and Northeastern spirit-medium practice (出马仙):** three different sources of moral teaching to separate.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe hunters and fishers:** obligations to animals and land as values, as these peoples state them.
- **Dörbet, Gorlos and Fuxin Mongols:** hospitality and herding custom against school and office values.
- **Industrial-city families shaped by the work unit, against farm families of the Songnen plain:** collective and household rankings of work and kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in official and historical texts; mixed in surveys; ethnographic depth concentrated in a few villages and peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state values campaigns are a frame to study, not a description of what people hold; some clan rites and genealogies are private; Indigenous taboos may be shared only by permission.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the annals of Shizong in the *Jin shi* (Zhonghua edition) for his edicts on Jurchen customs.
2. Search CNKI for "满族家训" and "辽宁 家谱 家训" for studies that quote and classify clan instructions.
3. Search the EASS data portal for its family module and confirm the China province code.
4. Find the Liaoning volume of 中国谚语集成 and list its sections on conduct and family.
5. Locate a copy of the 1747 Manchu ritual code with a modern Chinese translation.
