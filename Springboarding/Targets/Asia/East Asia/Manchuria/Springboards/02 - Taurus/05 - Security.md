# Manchuria: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, post-war nation, region East Asia; lands drawn from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; English and Japanese for the scholarship and colonial records. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Retired and laid-off state-enterprise workers in Fushun, Anshan, Fuxin and Qiqihar:** pensions and the old guarantees, held or lost.
- **Workers still inside large state firms or public posts:** security kept, for comparison with those who lost it.
- **Farmers of the Sanjiang and Songnen plains:** grain, land contracts and weather as their own security.
- **Yanbian Korean households (diaspora as comparison only):** security bought through family members working abroad.
- **Oroqen and Evenki families after hunting ended:** relief programs and settlement replacing the hunt.
- **Border-trade towns (Suifenhe, Heihe, Hunchun, Dandong):** livelihoods tied to the state of cross-border trade.
- **Hui and Mongol communities:** mosque, kin and herd as separate safety nets.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the 1990s-2000s labor crisis and on official programs; thinner on the inner sense of security and on minority peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Japanese for colonial-era village surveys; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor protests, pension shortfalls and the 2022 lockdowns in Jilin are politically sensitive and may be scrubbed from Chinese-language sites; keep archived copies.
- **Held for review:** none.
