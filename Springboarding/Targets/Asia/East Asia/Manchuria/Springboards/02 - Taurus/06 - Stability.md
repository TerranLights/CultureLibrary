# Manchuria: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria (East Asia), the post-war nation built on lands of pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Russian; Oroqen and Evenki through settlement-era records. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **"Old households" (老户) against later arrivals:** the region's own distinction between first settlers and newcomers.
- **Retirees who winter in Hainan (as comparison only, since they live abroad part of the year):** a mobile old age set against those who stay.
- **Sanjiazi and other Manchu villages against urban Manchus:** language and routine kept against lost.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe:** settled within living memory; continuity measured by a different clock.
- **Forest-zone towns of the Greater and Lesser Khingan:** the halt to commercial logging in natural forests (around 2015 *check*) as a test of what continuity means in a one-industry town.
- **Daqing oil workers' families:** a planned city whose continuity depends on one resource.
- **Dalian and the Liaodong peninsula:** trade-port mobility and Shandong ties across the strait.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in demographic and historical sources; mixed for the felt sense of continuity; good literary and screen material.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu; Korean; Japanese for Manchukuo currency and settlement records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** population decline and lost industry are politically touchy topics; Oroqen settlement histories are often told only from the state's side.
- **Held for review:** none.
