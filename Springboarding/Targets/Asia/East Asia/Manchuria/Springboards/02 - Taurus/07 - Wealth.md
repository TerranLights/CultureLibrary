# Manchuria: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, a post-war nation of East Asia on territory from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (including ginseng-hunters' and merchants' jargon), classical Chinese, Manchu, Mongolian, Korean, Russian. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it. (The left-right spending measure is handled under Ownership.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changbai mountain communities (Fusong, Ji'an, Linjiang):** wild against farmed ginseng as two kinds of wealth, and the lore of the wild root.
- **Dalian and Shenyang business families against rust-belt towns:** where post-reform fortunes concentrated and where they did not.
- **Korean villages and towns of Yanbian:** whether money earned abroad is shown, saved or spent on schooling.
- **Hezhe fishing families on the Amur and Ussuri:** wealth in fish runs and in heritage tourism.
- **Mongol herding families in Dörbet and Qian Gorlos:** herd size as standing.
- **Manchu descendants of banner officials against commoner Manchus:** old rank and old money in one ethnic group.
- **Heilongjiang state-farm households:** large mechanized farms as a different route to prosperity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical tribute and frontier fortunes; mixed for present-day wealth surveys at province level; thin for Indigenous peoples' own measures of wealth.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu for tribute records; Mongolian; Korean; Russian for Harbin trade records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some ginseng-digging lore is kept within families and hunting crews; wealth, corruption and organized crime in the 1990s Northeast are touchy topics and appear mostly in fiction and court reporting.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the *Liao shi* 食货志 in the Zhonghua edition and note how wealth is listed.
2. Search CNKI for "打牲乌拉 东珠 贡品" for studies of the tribute office's yields.
3. Search the national intangible heritage database for "长白山采参习俗" and read its listing.
4. Pull the latest Hurun list and filter by birthplace in the three provinces.
5. Search CNKI for "保家仙 东北 财富" for fieldwork on spirits of household prosperity.
