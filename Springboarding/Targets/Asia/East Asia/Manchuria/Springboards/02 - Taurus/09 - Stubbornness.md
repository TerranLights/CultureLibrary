# Manchuria: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria in East Asia, a post-war nation whose lands come from pre-war China. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and its northern colloquial words, classical Chinese and Manchu for the founding period, Korean, Mongolian (Khorchin song), Russian. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daqing and Anshan industrial families:** the official praise of refusing to quit, set against their own stories.
- **Former sent-down youth who stayed on the farms, and those who returned to cities:** one choice read as steadfast by some and stubborn by others.
- **Khorchin, Gorlos and Fuxin Mongols:** land defense in song and memory.
- **Manchu language activists against Manchus indifferent to the language:** who holds on and who lets go.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** schooling and language firmness inside a bilingual system.
- **Oroqen and Hezhe elders:** keeping or yielding old ways after bans and settlement.
- **Holdout households in redevelopment zones:** firmness about a home under heavy pressure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official heroes and resistance memory; mixed for everyday stubbornness; thin for direct study of how firmness is judged in daily life.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu; Mongolian (song texts); Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor protests and holdout cases are politically sensitive and often removed from Chinese platforms; the stubborn Northeasterner is a stock media figure, to be studied as an image, never taken as a description.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the 1618 Seven Grievances passage in the *Qing Taizu shilu* (any modern punctuated edition) and note its wording.

3. Search the China Labour Bulletin site for "Liaoyang 2002".
4. Search CNKI for "钉子户 东北" and for "知青 扎根 北大荒" studies.
5. Search a Korean database (RISS) for "조선족 민족교육" papers from the last decade.
