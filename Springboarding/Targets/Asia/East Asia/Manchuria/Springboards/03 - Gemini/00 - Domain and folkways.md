# Manchuria: Communication and learning, the domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, the post-war culture-nation (separate from the Sinian Federation), region East Asia; pre-war lands carried: China (written here for the core lands of the old Northeast). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeastern and Jiao-Liao varieties), Korean (Yanbian), Mongolian (Horqin, Barga, Buryat), Manchu (classical and the surviving village speech), Russian; Japanese for colonial-era records. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech ways, learning ways, neighbors and short journeys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Liaodong peninsula (Dalian, Dandong) against the interior:** Jiao-Liao speech and Shandong-origin settlement may set a different talk style from the Northeastern Mandarin heartland.
- **Manchu villages with surviving speech (Sanjiazi, Qiqihar area) against Mandarin-speaking Manchus:** separates what language loss changed from what persisted in address and visiting.
- **Korean communities of Yanbian and scattered Korean villages:** a full second language of schooling, press and church.
- **Horqin, Barga and Buryat Mongols:** distinct dialects and oral genres in the western grasslands.
- **Daur, Evenki (including the reindeer herders of Aoluguya), Oroqen and Hezhen:** small peoples whose learning ways ran through forest, river and oral narrative.
- **Xibe of Liaoning, Hui, and the Enhe Russians:** small communities with their own languages or faith-based learning.
- **Rust-belt work-unit districts (Shenyang's Tiexi) against state-farm settlements of the Great Northern Wilderness (北大荒):** two built settings for neighbors and schooling.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** Harbin Russian émigrés; Northeastern Koreans working in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Northeastern Mandarin and Yanbian Korean; mixed for Mongolian; thin for Manchu as spoken today and for the small Tungusic peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Manchu; Korean; Mongolian in the traditional script; Russian; Japanese for colonial records; Chinese- and Russian-language scholarship for Daur, Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhen.
- **Scope:** Manchuria's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet writes for the old three northeastern provinces and notes the eastern Mongol leagues where relevant.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mother-tongue schooling for Mongols and Koreans is politically charged; border communities facing North Korea; Manchu clan ritual texts and shamans' knowledge may be restricted to the clan; Japanese colonial and Soviet-era materials carry their makers' aims.
- **Held for review:** none.
