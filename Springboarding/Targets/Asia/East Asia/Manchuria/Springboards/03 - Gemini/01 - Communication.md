# Manchuria: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia (a culture-nation of its own, not part of the Sinian Federation). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern and Jiao-Liao), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Russian; Japanese for the colonial era. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the standards a community holds for good communication and its purpose.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harbin against Shenyang and Dalian:** Harbin's Russian-contact past may show in loanwords and address forms the southern cities lack.
- **Jiao-Liao speakers on the Liaodong coast:** directness and teasing may run differently where Shandong-origin speech prevails.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** a full honorific ladder in Korean set beside Mandarin's lighter one, inside the same households.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** how much Mandarin enters Mongolian talk in each area, and which setting calls for which language.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen communities:** older long-distance signals (trail markers, river calls) as the peoples describe them.
- **Deaf communities in the big cities:** a separate language community with its own channels.
- **Rust-belt generations:** laid-off workers of the 1990s against their children on short-video platforms; the medium may change the register.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** Northeasterners working in Russia's Far East or in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Mandarin media and dialect lexicography; mixed for Korean; thin for Manchu conversation today and for Tungusic signaling.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian (traditional script); classical Manchu for archives; Russian and Japanese for contact-era sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** platform censorship shapes what online talk survives; talk with officials and petitions touches politics; colonial contact-language studies carry occupation history; recordings of elders need consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
