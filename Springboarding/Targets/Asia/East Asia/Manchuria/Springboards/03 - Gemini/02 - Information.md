# Manchuria: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia (its own culture-nation, apart from the Sinian Federation). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu (archival), Russian and Japanese (press and records of the contact eras). **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how people gather, pass on, trust, withhold and use it: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Korean readers of Yanbian, Changchun and Harbin:** a second-language press with its own editorial offices, to set beside Mandarin news.
- **Mongol herders of Hulunbuir and Horqin:** distance and seasonal moves may make radio and phones matter differently than in towns.
- **Forestry and state-farm settlements:** closed workplaces that ran their own news channels for decades.
- **Border towns facing Russia and North Korea:** cross-border broadcasts and traders bring news from outside; who hears what is worth separating.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen elders:** knowledge of land, weather and animals held by particular people, shared only as the communities allow.
- **Laid-off rust-belt households of the 1990s:** news of jobs and pensions often travelling by word of mouth; check the channels.
- **Older people in all groups:** a frequent target of health-product sales talks and phone scams; how they sort true from false.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** Harbin Russian émigrés' memoirs, kept apart from the home record.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press archives and gazetteers; mixed for survey data (provincial samples are small); thin for minority-language and forest-people channels.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin (classical Chinese for gazetteers); Manchu for Qing archives; Korean; Mongolian; Russian; Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** censorship and rumor-control campaigns; personnel dossiers (档案) touch privacy and politics; colonial statistics carry their makers' categories; some shamanic and hunting knowledge is not for outsiders. Rules on public information belong to Libra, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. CNKI: 东北 报业史 and 盛京时报 研究 to map the press history.
2. Catalog search at the three provincial libraries for 地方文献 newspaper holdings.
3. CGSS documentation: find the media-trust items and the Liaoning, Jilin and Heilongjiang sample sizes.
4. Read the 《宁古塔纪略》 and 《黑龙江外记》 chapters on customs and post roads.
5. Search 흑룡강신문 and 길림신문 archives for features on how readers get news.
