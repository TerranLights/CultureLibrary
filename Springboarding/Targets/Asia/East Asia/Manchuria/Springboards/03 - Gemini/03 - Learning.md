*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Korean-medium and Mandarin-medium families in Yanbian:** the choice of school language may shape what home learning looks like.
- **Mongols of Tongliao and Hulunbuir:** the medium of instruction and its recent changes, set beside learning on the steppe.
- **Manchu heritage learners:** adults learning their own people's language from books and teachers, rarely from parents.
- **Aoluguya Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhen:** land, forest and river learning against settled schooling; each as the community describes it.
- **Hui families:** mosque-school learning alongside state schools.
- **State-enterprise worker families of Anshan, Shenyang, Changchun and Harbin:** apprenticeship inherited across generations, then cut off.
- **Farm villages of the Songnen and Liao plains:** children learning by working alongside adults in field and kitchen.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** Northeastern Koreans' children schooled in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state schooling statistics and novels; mixed for apprenticeship (oral history needed); thin for Tungusic land-based learning and for Manchu revival outcomes.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian (traditional script); Manchu for Qing primers; Japanese for colonial schooling; English for mission records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-language schooling policy is politically charged and the subject of protest; sent-down-youth memory; some initiation and hunting knowledge is not shared outside the community.
- **Held for review:** none.
