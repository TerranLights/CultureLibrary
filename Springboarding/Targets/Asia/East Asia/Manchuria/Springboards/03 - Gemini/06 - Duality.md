# Manchuria: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia; treated as an independent culture-nation, not as part of the Sinian Federation. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Daur (through Chinese sources), Russian, Japanese. **Concept in one line:** living with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchus:** two names, two scripts, and the question of which to show in which setting.
- **Korean Chinese:** belonging to a nation-state and to a people whose homeland lies across the border.
- **Horqin Mongols in farming villages:** a herding heritage with a farming present, and some households that speak only Mandarin.
- **Daur:** a people historically placed between larger neighbors' categories.
- **Hui:** a Mandarin-speaking people with a separate faith; how the two sit together in daily life.
- **Enhe Russians and mixed families:** two languages and two churches or none, in one household.
- **Families who raised Japanese orphans:** loyalty and love across a wartime enemy line.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** returned orphans in Japan; Korean Chinese in Seoul who are called foreigners there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Manchu-Han history and Korean Chinese identity studies; mixed for border towns; thin for ritual pairs outside Manchu clans.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Manchu for Qing bilingual texts; Korean; Mongolian; Russian; Japanese for orphan and Manchukuo sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collaboration labels from the occupation era; ethnic identity and classification are political; clan ritual knowledge belongs to the clans; questions to Korean Chinese about loyalty may wound.
- **Held for review:** none.
