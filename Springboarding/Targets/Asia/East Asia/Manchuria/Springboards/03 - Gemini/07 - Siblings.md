# Manchuria: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia; a culture-nation with its own row, apart from the Sinian Federation. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian; Evenki, Oroqen and Daur kin terms through Chinese ethnology. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City families of the policy era (Dalian, Shenyang, Harbin):** a generation with few or no siblings, against their parents' large sibling sets.
- **Farm villages:** more siblings and the household-division custom set beside the city pattern.
- **Korean Chinese families:** siblings split between Yanbian, other cities and South Korea; who minds whom.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** the youngest son's tie to the parental hearth.
- **Manchu clans:** kin terms and clan rules that rank brothers.
- **Evenki, Oroqen and Daur:** clan systems in which cousins may stand in a sibling's place; to check community by community.
- **Hui families:** whether religious teaching on inheritance and care sets sibling duties apart from neighbors'.
- **Families with a second or third child after the policy changes:** the shift from only child to elder sibling.
- **Diasporas, for comparison only:** sibling sets spread between the Northeast, Korea and Japan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policy-era demography and only-child studies; mixed for rural inheritance; thin for Tungusic kinship today.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian; classical Chinese for clan rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-policy enforcement and its harms; inheritance disputes (legal side to Libra); left-behind children; clan documents are family-held.
- **Held for review:** none.
