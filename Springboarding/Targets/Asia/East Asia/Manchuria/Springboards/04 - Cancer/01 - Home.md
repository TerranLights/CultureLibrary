# Manchuria: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (Northeastern Mandarin), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Russian; Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe and Daur terms through Chinese-language ethnography; Japanese for settler-era material. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Worker-block residents against village kang-house households:** two different ideas of a home's walls and warmth.
- **Families moved by shantytown redevelopment:** home as before and after.
- **Korean-Chinese of Yanbian:** villages emptied by work abroad; the house kept for a return.
- **Reindeer Evenki of Aoluguya and settled Oroqen:** forest camp against assigned house.
- **Hezhe fishing villages on the Amur, Ussuri and lower Songhua:** river homes at a border.
- **Horqin Mongols:** a shift from herding dwellings to fixed farm houses.
- **Manchu villages in the old banner lands:** houses that keep rite space.
- **Harbin's old districts and border towns** (Heihe, Suifenhe, Dandong, Hunchun): homes shaped by foreign trade and architecture.
- **Russian-descended villages near Ergun:** log houses and a mixed household.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Northeasterners wintering in Hainan, with two homes a year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban housing and literature; mixed for Korean-Chinese and Mongol homes; thin for Hezhe, Daur and Oroqen, where most writing is by outsiders.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Korean; Russian for Harbin; Japanese for settler memory; Mongolian for Horqin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** demolition and resettlement can be politically sensitive, and residents' accounts may be hard to reach. Settler-era sources need care. Property and eviction rules belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
