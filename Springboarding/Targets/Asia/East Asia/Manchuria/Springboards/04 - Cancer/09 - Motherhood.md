*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banner-descended Manchu families:** Qing-era terms of address and widows' standing still remembered.
- **Han families of migrant origin:** mothers left in Shandong or Hebei villages in the migration years, and mothers who came north later.
- **Korean-Chinese mothers:** those who work abroad against those who stay; motherhood across borders.
- **Mongol families of Horqin:** mothers in song and storytelling.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe:** birth moved from camp customs to hospital.
- **Urban worker families:** the single-child mother of the planned-birth era.
- **Rural mothers-in-law and daughters-in-law:** the household's second mother.
- **Hui families:** mosque-linked customs of birth and naming.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese women who married into South Korean families; Northeastern women working as postpartum carers in other Chinese cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography and Qing-era Manchu women; mixed for folk custom; thin for the small peoples' birth practice except in older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** planned-birth history, forced procedures and infertility are painful and political; protect informants. Birth rites of hunting peoples may be private. Marriage and gender roles go to Libra; birth and sex to Scorpio; parental authority to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
