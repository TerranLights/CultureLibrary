# Manchuria: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Russian for Harbin's émigré press. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men,** given the gendered rules for showing feeling that Xiao Hong's generation wrote against; check how these differ now.
- **Generations:** the planned-economy generation, those laid off in the 1990s, and their children on platforms.
- **Korean bilinguals of Yanbian,** who may express differently in Korean and in Chinese.
- **Mongols,** whose song may carry what speech does not.
- **Manchus** choosing whether to show identity, urban and rural.
- **Russian-descended families of Ergun and Harbin.**
- **City against village;** Dalian's port culture against inland towns.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese in Seoul's Daerim-dong; Northeastern migrants in Beijing and Shenzhen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and song; mixed for survey measures (regional cuts uncertain); online data volatile because posts are removed.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian (traditional script), Manchu, Russian, Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression is censored, so silences must be read as evidence of limits, not of temperament; ethnic dress has drawn cross-border quarrels (Korean dress at public events); oral histories of layoffs and protest are sensitive; the image of the blunt, loud Northeasterner is a media trope to test.
- **Held for review:** none.
