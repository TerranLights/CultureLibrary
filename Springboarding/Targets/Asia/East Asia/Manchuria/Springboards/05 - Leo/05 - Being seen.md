# Manchuria: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Japanese for colonial imagery, French for one Qing text in translation. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Older people in emptying towns** against those in cities with family nearby.
- **Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhen,** seen mostly through tourism and others' art; separate how each shows itself.
- **Koreans of Yanbian,** visible in signs and festivals, less so elsewhere.
- **Mongols of Hulunbuir and Khorchin,** whose script and dress mark public space.
- **Chinese-Russian families of Ergun** and Harbin's remnants of other foreign communities.
- **Rural Han villagers,** now performing village life online.
- **Women of the square-dance generation** against younger urban women.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Chaoxianzu as depicted in South Korean films.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for images (colonial, imperial, online); mixed for census and representation; thin for how small peoples themselves judge their visibility.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Japanese, French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mass camera surveillance is a national system, not a local choice, and research on it is restricted; changes to Mongolian- and Korean-language schooling are politically charged; colonial photos are propaganda; vloggers' lives may be staged.
- **Held for review:** none.
