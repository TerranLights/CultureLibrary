# Manchuria: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Oroqen terms via Chinese transcription. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Laid-off state workers and their children;** check whether layoffs fell differently on women and men.
- **Recipients of 低保** against workers in the private sector.
- **Older people** in Korean, Han and Mongol villages, many with absent children.
- **Oroqen and Evenki** after the end of hunting and herding; separate each people.
- **Mongol herders** under grazing bans and resettlement.
- **Manchus,** with household rules of respect from banner times.
- **Families of war victims,** Han, Korean and others.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** retired Northeasterners wintering in Hainan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for layoffs and war crimes; mixed for elder care and welfare; thin for small peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Japanese for Unit 731 records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Unit 731 material is graphic and politically used; layoffs and protests remain sensitive; resettlement policy is officially framed as progress, so herders' and hunters' own views may be hard to publish; respect owed to elders differs by family, never one rule for all.
- **Held for review:** none.
