# Manchuria: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Japanese (war and remolding records). **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han Northeasterners,** for whom war memory and the Cultural Revolution carry different weights by family.
- **Manchus,** whose ties to the Qing and to Manchukuo make the "puppet" label personal.
- **Koreans,** who faced campaigns against "local nationalism" and now face slights at home and abroad.
- **Mongols,** with the purge of the late 1960s.
- **Former residents of the Japanese era:** collaborators, war orphans, foster families.
- **Women and men:** whether shame attaches to sexual reputation for women and to defeat or job loss for men; test, do not assume.
- **Children and teenagers** in school.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese workers in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national humiliation and the Cultural Revolution; mixed for school bullying; thin for everyday teasing, which needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Japanese, Manchu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Mongol purge and Cultural Revolution violence are restricted topics in China; Yang Haiying's work is contested; Song-era accounts of the Jingkang captivity are hostile to the Jurchen; museum narratives are state-shaped.
- **Held for review:** none.
