# Manchuria: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Oroqen and Evenki terms via Chinese transcription. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han villagers** (Yan's Xiajia as a baseline) against city families and young office workers.
- **Koreans of Yanbian,** with savings clubs, event gifts and remittance money from abroad.
- **Mongols,** whose hospitality rules for guests may differ by herding or farming life.
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhen,** with older rules for sharing the catch, now under settlement.
- **Hui of Shenyang and Harbin,** with Islamic almsgiving (*check* local terms).
- **Christians and Buddhists,** giving through congregations and temples.
- **Laid-off and low-income families,** for whom event gifts may weigh heavily.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese workers in South Korea sending money home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich, because Yan's work offers a deep baseline; mixed for measured giving by province; thin for the small peoples' present practice.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, Arabic terms for Hui giving.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** church charity faces state limits; gift money shades into bribery in official settings (a law matter: Libra or Capricorn, not here); avoid painting any people as uniformly generous or tight-fisted, a common regional trope.
- **Held for review:** none.
