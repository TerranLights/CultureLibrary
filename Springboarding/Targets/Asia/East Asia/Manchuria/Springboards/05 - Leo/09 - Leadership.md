# Manchuria: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Jurchen terms through Chinese transcription. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

English keywords: Eight Banners leadership; Jurchen council; Manchu clan heads; Zhang Zuolin; Anshan Constitution; village elections Lishu; Northeast bandits; women in leadership Northeast China; big-brother culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchus,** with clan heads and memories of banner captains.
- **Daur, Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhen,** whose clan heads and hunt leaders followed their own rules.
- **Mongols** of Khorchin and Hulunbuir, with noble lines and herders' chosen leaders.
- **Koreans,** for whom teachers, church elders and village heads may lead.
- **Han villagers** (elected heads, lineage elders, the capable "能人") against factory and office hierarchies.
- **Women:** leadership in families, villages, factories and the women's federation.
- **Workers' gangs and informal "big brothers"** against formal managers.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese community associations in Seoul.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dynastic and warlord leaders; mixed for village and workplace leadership; thin for clan leadership among the small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** formal authority and command belong to Capricorn; Party leadership is a closed topic; the "big brother" image overlaps with organized-crime stereotypes and must be tested, not taken; warlord and bandit stories are romanticized in film.
- **Held for review:** none.
