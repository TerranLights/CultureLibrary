*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu, Han and Mongol children,** who share some games (knucklebones) and differ in others.
- **Daur, Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen,** with games tied to their own work.
- **Koreans,** whose festival games keep a separate calendar.
- **Rural against city children,** and winter against summer play.
- **Older adults** (mahjong, winter swimming, square dance) against young people (screens, skiing).
- **Girls and boys,** where game rules or customs split them.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese children raised in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festival games and court spectacle; mixed for children's everyday play; thin for adult play outside gambling and sport.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mahjong for money touches gambling rules (a law matter, left to Libra or Capricorn), so players may not speak openly about stakes; state sports programs reshape minority games; screen-time rules for minors are national policy.
- **Held for review:** none.
