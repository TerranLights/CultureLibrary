# Manchuria: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian; Daur and Evenki tale terms via Chinese transcription. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchus,** with banner-era codes of loyalty and widowhood.
- **Han descendants of Shandong and Hebei migrants,** whose codes of righteousness are popularly celebrated.
- **Koreans,** with ideas of righteousness and filial honor in their own words.
- **Mongols,** for oath and name.
- **Daur, Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhen,** whose hunter-hero ideals may differ from each other.
- **Soldiers' and martyrs' families** against civilians.
- **Women and men:** what "honor" asks of each, as a question to research.
- **Traders and workers** in markets, factories and border towns.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Korean-Chinese in South Korea, whose honor is at stake under a new gaze.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state honors, Qing widow records and popular drama; mixed for everyday honor; thin for the small peoples' present ideas.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian (classical for the *Secret History*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martyr status is state-defined; An Jung-geun's honoring touches Japan relations; family honor tied to women's conduct must be studied from women's own accounts; violence over insults is a stereotype to test.
- **Held for review:** none.
