# Manchuria: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (standard and Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Oroqen song titles via Chinese. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural and urban Han families,** whose New Year and wedding joys differ.
- **Manchus,** where clan rites still end in shared feasting.
- **Mongols,** whose festival calendar follows the lunar year and the herding season.
- **Koreans,** whose celebrations have their own songs, dances and calendar.
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhen and Daur,** whose festivals now often host visitors.
- **Older people and the young:** what each names as the best of times.
- **Buddhist, Daoist, Christian and Muslim believers,** each with its own feasts.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Northeastern New Year as kept by migrants in southern cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and comedy; mixed for well-being measures; thin for small peoples' own festive life beyond staged events.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian (traditional script for song and toast texts), Manchu for rite texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan rites may be closed to outsiders; staged minority joy in media is a policy product; drinking at celebrations is a health topic to handle without moralizing; happiness rankings are promotional.
- **Held for review:** none.
