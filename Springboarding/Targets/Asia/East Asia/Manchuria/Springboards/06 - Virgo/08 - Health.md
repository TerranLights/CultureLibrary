# Manchuria: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Chaoxianzu Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Japanese and Russian for the plague years and the colonial era. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harbin's multiethnic past** (Russian, Jewish, Chinese) for plague-era health practice.
- **Rural households** who mix hospitals, folk remedies and mediums, against urban families relying on hospitals.
- **Chaoxianzu patients and practitioners** of Korean medicine.
- **Mongol communities** with their own medical tradition and bone-setters.
- **Manchu families** with shamanic lineages, as they describe them.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe** with recent shamanic memory and resettlement health histories.
- **Former work-unit employees** who lost factory hospitals and sanatoria after restructuring.
- **Villages in the endemic-disease belt** against unaffected areas.
- **Hui families** for food rules and health.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemics, endemic diseases and survey data; mixed for folk healing; thin and sensitive for shamanic healing.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Russian and Japanese for 1900-1945; Manchu; Korean (including classical Chinese in Korean texts); Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Unit 731 and wartime experiments; shamanic and mediumistic knowledge, shared only by its holders; mental illness and suicide; official limits on folk religion.
- **Held for review:** none.
