# Manchuria: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian; Japanese and Russian for the war-crimes records. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered (here with the letter of the law only).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Laid-off state-factory workers** in Shenyang's Tiexi, Fushun, Anshan and Daqing: wage and pension claims pushed them into petitions, which may color how justice is sought.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** trials can be held in Korean, and memories of anti-Japanese struggle frame wrongs; check the courts' language practice.
- **Manchu families:** the shift from banner privilege to post-1911 discrimination makes "who got their due" a question with its own history.
- **Khorchin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** grazing-land enclosure and mining disputes put herders before courts on their own terms.
- **Oroqen and Evenki hunters:** hunting bans and forest-protection rules changed what counts as an offense for them; their side first.
- **Victims' families of the occupation and Unit 731, and the Japanese orphans left in Heilongjiang:** historical justice runs through both.
- **Border towns** (Heihe, Suifenhe, Dandong): smuggling and trade cases with Russians and North Koreans test justice across a line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for courts and the war-crimes trials in Mandarin; mixed for the Qing codes (classical Chinese and Manchu); thin for minority customary redress.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian; Russian (Khabarovsk); Japanese (defendants' memoirs).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petitioning and labor protest are politically sensitive; court databases have been trimmed; organized-crime and corruption cases touch living officials; war-crimes material is emotionally and diplomatically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
