# Manchuria: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen through Chinese-language ethnography. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State-farm employees of Beidahuang against collective villagers:** land came by job assignment for one and by household head-count for the other.
- **Older laid-off workers against their children:** the severance settlements of the 1990s and the exam system offer two different pictures of a fair deal.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen families:** hunt and catch shares, their own rules first.
- **Khorchin and Hulunbuir herders:** pasture divided by contract and fence against older common grazing.
- **Yanbian Korean households with members working abroad:** remittances change who is seen as getting more than their share.
- **Manchu descendants of banner-land holders:** memories of allotted land and its loss.
- **Dalian and the coastal Liaodong cities against interior counties:** markets and private firms set different expectations of a fair wage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for attitudes and courts in Mandarin; mixed for village land practice; thin for the small peoples' sharing rules outside the 1950s surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian; written Manchu for banner-land records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** layoff settlements and petitions remain touchy; minority survey volumes reflect their era's class framing.
- **Held for review:** none.
