## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khorchin Mongol and Manchu families with old marriage ties:** partnership as an alliance between peoples, not only between two people.
- **Yanbian Korean families:** transnational marriage and long separations through work abroad strain the couple in their own way.
- **Russians of Ergun (Enhe and nearby villages):** families descended from Chinese-Russian marriages; mixed partnership as everyday life.
- **Changbai digging teams, timber gangs and Chagan Lake crews:** work partnerships with headmen and fixed shares, distinct from farm households.
- **Beidahuang state-farm households:** couples formed under assignment, including sent-down youth who stayed.
- **Rust-belt couples split by migration** to the south or abroad for work.
- **Shandong-origin migrant lineages:** imported matchmaking and wedding customs, to set against long-settled families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage statistics and Qing alliances; mixed for work-crew customs (heritage write-ups); thin for small peoples' couple life outside general ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese for gazetteers and banner statutes; Korean; Mongolian; Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** brides brought across the North Korean border and marriage-broker fraud; older informal arrangements such as 拉帮套 (a second man taken into a poor household) need careful, non-sensational handling *check*; family-planning law history.
- **Held for review:** none.
