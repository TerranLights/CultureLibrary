## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Herders of Hulunbuir and Khorchin:** grazing quotas put a number on balance; their own herding sense may differ.
- **Forestry towns of Daxing'anling and Yichun:** a sudden shift from cutting to protecting; balance felt as loss of work.
- **Aoluguya Evenki reindeer families:** relocated from the forest; what balance with the land meant to them, in their words.
- **Villages inside the tiger-leopard park zone:** Korean and Han farmers weighing livestock losses against protection.
- **Grain farmers of the black-soil plains:** yields against soil, under new legal limits.
- **Users of Korean Sasang and Mongolian medicine:** different body models of balance beside Chinese medicine.
- **Manchu heritage groups:** the banner wings and joint rule as a remembered model of power held in check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land policy and medicine in Mandarin; mixed for minority medical traditions; thin for how small peoples phrase balance with land outside fiction and state records.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian; Manchu for the early Qing material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grassland enclosure and relocation of herders and reindeer families; layoffs after the logging ban; ethnic-autonomy quotas are politically delicate.
- **Held for review:** none.
