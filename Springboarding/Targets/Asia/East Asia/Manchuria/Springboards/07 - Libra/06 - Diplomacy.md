# Manchuria: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian (including Buryat), Russian; Japanese and Latin for older negotiations. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yanbian Koreans:** go-betweens for Seoul and, more quietly, for Pyongyang; their tact works in two directions.
- **Dandong, Heihe and Suifenhe border traders:** daily bargaining with North Korean and Russian partners in mixed languages.
- **Hulunbuir Buryats, Barga and Ölöt Mongols:** cross-border kin in Mongolia and Russia make diplomacy a family matter.
- **Hezhen, Oroqen and Evenki:** a history of tribute and treaty-era officials, told from their side first.
- **Dalian's business circles:** long Japanese ties shape a different negotiating style to test against inland cities.
- **State-enterprise managers against private entrepreneurs:** the banquet against the contract as ways to settle a deal.
- **Families of Japanese war orphans and their adoptive Chinese parents:** reconciliation across a wartime line, in private.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and the Qing–Russian treaties; mixed for trade-fair and border-trade material; thin for small peoples' own protocols.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Manchu; Russian; Korean; Japanese; Latin for Nerchinsk; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** North Korea relations and border trade; competing accounts of 1931–1945; border-settlement history touches nationalist feeling on both sides of the Amur.
- **Held for review:** none.
