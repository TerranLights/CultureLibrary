# Manchuria: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Oroqen and Evenki rites through Chinese-language ethnography. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han villages of Heilongjiang and Jilin:** dense household gift ledgers and rising banquet costs make them the main case to measure.
- **Yanbian Korean villages:** labor exchange and family-event contributions under heavy out-migration; who now returns the help.
- **Hulunbuir and Khorchin Mongols:** hospitality and ovoo offerings tie reciprocity to guests and to the land.
- **Oroqen and Evenki:** obligations to animals and spirits, told on their own terms, beside the state's hunting bans.
- **Manchu families keeping household rites:** offerings that repay the ancestors and protective spirits.
- **City workers in former work units:** gift circles built around the factory that shrank when the factories did.
- **Heihe and Suifenhe residents:** cross-border hospitality and favors with Russian partners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han gift exchange (ethnography, surveys, policy); mixed for Korean and Mongol practice; thin and sensitive for small peoples' rites.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian; Manchu for the ritual code.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some spirit rites are private to families and clans; campaigns against "feudal superstition" shaped what was recorded; banquet bans touch officials' conduct rules.
- **Held for review:** none.
