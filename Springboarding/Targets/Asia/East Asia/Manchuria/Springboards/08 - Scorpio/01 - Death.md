# Manchuria: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Northeastern Mandarin, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Daur, Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, including grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han villagers vs. city workers:** home deathbeds and burial in family fields against hospital deaths and urban cremation quotas.
- **Manchu clans:** whether ancestors called in shamanic rites change how a death is grieved; Jiutai and Sanjiazi as separate cases.
- **Korean Chinese of Yanbian and Changbai:** honorific death speech, the chief mourner's role, and Protestant against Confucian mourning.
- **Mongols of Fuxin, Qian Gorlos and Dorbod:** lamas' part at the deathbed and choices of burial ground.
- **Daur of Meilisi, Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhe:** souls and spirits of the dead in their own terms; settlement's effect on burial.
- **Mining and forestry towns (Fuxin, Hegang, Jixi, the Greater Khingan forest districts):** sudden death at work as its own grief culture.
- **Comparison only (diaspora):** Japanese war orphans' families in Japan, for memory of deaths on these lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality counts and Han village custom; mixed for Manchu and Korean grief; thin for Hezhe and Oroqen beyond older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Manchu; Korean; Mongolian; Japanese for occupation-era deaths.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data; war dead of the occupation and the civil war (the Changchun siege is politically touchy); human remains at Pingfang; shamanic texts some clans keep private.
- **Held for review:** none.
