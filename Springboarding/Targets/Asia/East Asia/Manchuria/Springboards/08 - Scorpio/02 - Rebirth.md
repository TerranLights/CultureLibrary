# Manchuria: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Northeastern Mandarin, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Evenki, Hezhe; Japanese for the Fushun testimonies. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former state-sector workers of Shenyang, Anshan, Fushun and Daqing:** whose "second start" after layoffs was planned for them, and how they talk about it.
- **Manchus who re-registered in the 1980s vs. lineages that never hid:** revival as paperwork, as language study, or as clan ritual.
- **Korean Chinese Protestants and Catholics:** born-again speech in Korean, and the pull of South Korean churches.
- **Mongol Buddhists of Fuxin and Harqin Left Wing:** reincarnation as doctrine and as an institution with named lines.
- **Aoluguya Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe:** state resettlement narrated as renewal, against each people's own account.
- **Han rural converts and Buddhist lay groups:** two competing routes to a new self in the same villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state narratives and industrial renewal; mixed for conversion (much is unpublished or sensitive); thin for small peoples' own revival voices.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Japanese; Mongolian; Manchu for revival materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** thought-reform history; unregistered churches; Falun Gong began in Changchun and is a banned movement, so any research route there needs special care; resettlement of the Evenki and Oroqen is contested ground.
- **Held for review:** none.
