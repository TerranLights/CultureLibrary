# Manchuria: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Northeastern Mandarin, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Japanese and Russian for expulsions of the 1940s and 1950s. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mongols of the eastern counties:** the late-1960s purge as a collective wound, and purification by juniper and fire in household practice.
- **Korean Chinese of Yanbian:** how the Cultural Revolution treated local Korean leaders (*check*); exorcism words in Korean.
- **Han villagers of the land-reform heartland (Songhua basin):** families on both sides of struggle meetings.
- **Harbin's former Russian community and the Japanese repatriated via Huludao:** whole groups removed from the lands; comparison only for their later accounts abroad.
- **State-farm and labor-farm districts (Beidahuang, Xingkai Lake):** places built partly around those sent away.
- **Urban bathhouse-goers vs. rural households:** two everyday routes to bodily cleansing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state campaigns and official discipline; mixed for victims' memory (much in Japanese or unpublished); thin for minority healing rites.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Mongolian; Korean; Japanese; Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Inner Mongolian People's Party purge, the land reform's killings and the Cultural Revolution are politically restricted topics; keepers of healing rites may limit access.
- **Held for review:** none.
