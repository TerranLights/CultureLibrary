# Manchuria: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Northeastern Mandarin, Korean, Manchu, Mongolian; Russian for the Amur events of 1900. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rust-belt cities (Shenyang's Tiexi, Anshan, Fushun, Fuxin, Hegang):** layoffs and shrinking population as a shared trial, met differently by each town.
- **Han farming families of the Songhua and Nen river plains:** flood years and harvest failures.
- **Korean Chinese of Yanbian:** crisis by departure, as work abroad draws people away.
- **Manchus and Han of border counties along the Amur and Ussuri:** frontier catastrophes from 1900 onward.
- **Oroqen and Evenki:** the end of hunting and forced moves, which may be named as a crisis or not, in their own voices.
- **Comparison only (diaspora):** Northeasterners who left for Hainan and other southern cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for layoffs, floods and wartime catastrophe; mixed for personal and family crisis; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Korean; Russian; Japanese for 1945.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor unrest, the 1900 border history and pandemic-era posts may be censored or deleted; mental-health crisis data is sparse.
- **Held for review:** none.
