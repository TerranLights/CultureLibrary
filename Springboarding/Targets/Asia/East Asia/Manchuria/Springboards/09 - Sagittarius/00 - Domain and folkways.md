# Manchuria: Belief and philosophy (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Northeastern (Dongbei) Mandarin; Korean (Yanbian usage); Mongolian (eastern varieties); Manchu (historical texts and today's revival); Xibe, Daur, Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe (Nanai) terms where they exist. **Concept in one line:** the domain question, what is true and where is meaning found: religion, philosophy, higher learning and long journeys, with religious ways and freedom ways as the folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northeastern Han by origin:** Shandong- versus Hebei-descended lineages, old Liaodong settlers versus later arrivals; migrants carried different temple cults.
- **Manchus:** village clan rites (Jiutai, Ningguta, Sanjiazi) versus urban bannermen's Buddhist and Daoist practice.
- **Koreans of Yanbian and Changbai:** Protestant, Catholic and Daejonggyo histories of their own.
- **Mongols** of Fuxin and Harqin (Tibetan Buddhist monastery life) and of the eastern steppe.
- **Xibe** of Shenyang (their family temple); **Daur, Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe**, each with its own shamanic and story traditions.
- **Hui Muslims** in Shenyang, Harbin and the cities.
- **Russians of the Argun border** and Harbin's former Russian, Jewish and Polish communities (past layers).
- **Industrial cities (Shenyang's Tiexi, Anshan, Fushun) versus forest and farm counties:** work-unit secularism against village temple life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Chinese sources are plentiful but framed by state categories; minority and folk practice is thinner, often recorded by outsiders. In this setting Manchuria is its own culture-nation, not part of the Sinian Federation; Chinese-language scholarship is used for its coverage of these lands only. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core Manchurian lands of Northeast China.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Japanese (colonial era), Russian (Harbin émigrés), Mongolian, Manchu (archives).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** groups the state designates 邪教 (xiéjiào); missionary work near the North Korean border; clan spirit-lists and shaman songs (神本) some families keep private; colonial-era sources need critical reading.
- **Held for review:** two English-language studies of Manchukuo-era religion and borderland politics by South Asian-heritage scholars (category only).
