# Manchuria: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (Northeastern Mandarin), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu; Hui Muslim Chinese vocabulary; Evenki, Oroqen and Daur terms where documented. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Han households** keeping a 保家仙 shrine versus urban households without one: where trust in the spirit-household survives the move to cities.
- **Mediums' clients** across generations: young people consulting through apps versus elders visiting in person.
- **Manchu clans** that still hold family rites versus Manchus with none: what is kept when the rite lapses.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** Protestant, Catholic and nonreligious households, and those touched by South Korean churches.
- **Mongols of Fuxin and Harqin** (lamaseries) versus Mongols of the eastern steppe (shamanic revival).
- **Hui** communities in Shenyang, Harbin and Changchun, each with its own mosque history.
- **Evenki, Oroqen, Daur and Hezhe:** spirit faith after settlement, and Christian or Buddhist converts among them.
- **Former state-enterprise workers**, for whom faith in the work unit and the Party may have been the main faith.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; temple, church and mission sources are good, while household and medium practice sits mainly in theses and local press.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Mongolian, Manchu, French and German (mission archives), Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered churches; groups the state designates 邪教, one of which began in Changchun (handle only as a flag); mediums may avoid scrutiny; clan prayer texts may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
