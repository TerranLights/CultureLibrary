# Manchuria: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (with Northeastern folk vocabulary), Korean, Manchu, Mongolian, Russian; Oroqen and Evenki terms where recorded. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Party members and state employees,** bound to profess atheism, versus their own households' practice.
- **Rural Han** with spirit-animal beliefs versus urban professionals who call them 迷信.
- **Manchu families** that keep crow and dog taboos versus those that never heard of them.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** churchgoers, those who keep folk belief and the nonreligious, with different food and household taboos from Han and Manchu neighbors.
- **Khorchin and Barga Mongols** (cairn rites) versus Fuxin Mongols (monastic Buddhism).
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Daur and Hezhe** hunters and fishers, and their children raised in towns.
- **Hui** households, with Islamic belief marked in food and prayer.
- **Russian-descended** families of the Argun border.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folklore collections and ethnographies are rich, while survey data on what people believe today is thin below the national level.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Russian, Mongolian, Manchu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** professing belief can carry costs for Party members and officials; some folk practice is avoided in front of outsiders; groups the state bans are a flag only, not a pointer.
- **Held for review:** none.
