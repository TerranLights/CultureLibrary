# Manchuria: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (with Northeastern colloquial), Korean, Manchu, Mongolian, Russian, Japanese. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Shandong and Hebei migrants:** families that went back and forth versus those that settled for good.
- **Yanbian Koreans:** households with members working in South Korea or Japan versus those who stayed; diaspora accounts as comparison only.
- **Border towns** (Heihe, Suifenhe, Manzhouli, Dandong) versus interior farm counties: daily crossing versus rare travel.
- **Hulunbuir and Khorchin herders** (seasonal moves) and **Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe** (hunting and fishing rounds).
- **Older retirees** who winter in the south versus those who stay through winter.
- **Harbin and Dalian:** cities built by railways and ports, against towns on the forest and farm frontier.
- **Families tied to Japan** through orphans and returnees; **Russian-descended** families near the Argun.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration history and tourism; mixed for present-day personal travel.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Korean, Japanese (settler and orphan sources), Russian (border and émigré sources), Manchu, Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crossings from North Korea and the Yalu border are restricted subjects; Japanese settler and orphan history carries strong feelings on both sides.
- **Held for review:** none.
