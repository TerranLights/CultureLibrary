# Manchuria: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese, Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Russian, Japanese. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu clans:** clan shamans and clan heads, set against the court's standardized rite.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** hereditary banner princes until the 1940s; the question of who replaced them.
- **Daur, Evenki and Oroqen:** hunt leaders and shamans, then forced settlement in the 1950s; authority before and after.
- **Hezhe** of the lower Sungari and Ussuri, for epic models of leadership.
- **Chaoxianzu Koreans:** school, church and elder authority in a Korean-language community.
- **Han migrants' descendants:** whether lineage heads from Shandong and Hebei kept any role after the move north.
- **Beidahuang state farms:** quasi-military command on the farms against civilian villages.
- **Ergun Russians and Harbin's Orthodox community; Hui of Shenyang** with their imams.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on imperial, warlord and colonial authority; mixed on the planned-economy era; thin on small peoples' own views of their leaders.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for official speech and village records; Manchu for the shamanic code and court commands; Japanese for Manchukuo's legitimacy campaigns; Korean for Yanbian community leadership; Mongolian for banner princes; Russian for Harbin's railway and church authorities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Puyi's memoir and 1950s surveys were written under political pressure; shamans' clan rites are shared only as clans allow; dissent toward the pre-war state is a hazardous research subject. The permission-share figure is scored from the law in force, with Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for 满族 萨满 族权 and 鄂伦春 塔坦达 to find studies of clan and hunt-group authority.
2. Pull the Manchu shamanic code's Chinese edition and compare its rites with surviving clan practice in Jilin.
3. Read Duara's Manchukuo chapters, then McCormack's, to set two rival claims to rule side by side.
4. Check the Asian Barometer documentation for province codes covering Liaoning, Jilin and Heilongjiang.
5. Search the Yanbian Daily archive for 권위 and 어르신 to see how leaders and elders are named.
