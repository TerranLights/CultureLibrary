# Manchuria: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese first; Manchu and Japanese for archives; Korean, Mongolian and Russian for the minority communities. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rustbelt factory families of Liaoning:** duties built around the work unit, then cut loose after the layoffs.
- **Oilfield Daqing and state-farm Beidahuang:** places founded on assigned service; second generations to compare with founders.
- **Manchu clans** keeping ancestor rites against families that dropped them.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe:** historic tribute duties to the throne, and hunting or fishing shares owed within the band.
- **Mongols and Daur of Hulunbuir and Horqin:** duties to land, livestock and kin across pastoral seasons.
- **Chaoxianzu Koreans:** family duty stretched across labor migration to South Korea; the homeland households are the unit, the migrants abroad comparison only.
- **Ergun Russians and mixed families:** duties in two kin traditions at once.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state model-worker campaigns and memorial culture; mixed for family caregiving (survey data exist, provincial cuts uncertain); thin for small peoples' sharing duties outside 1950s surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for model campaigns, memoirs and caregiving studies; Manchu for tribute rolls and clan registers; Japanese for orphan records and pioneer-era documents; Korean for Yanbian family and migration duties; Mongolian for *oboo* rites and pastoral obligations; Russian for Ergun family accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war memory and martyr cults are politically managed; Japanese orphans and foster families involve private grief; clan genealogies may be family property, opened only with consent; *oboo* rites are shown only as communities allow.
- **Held for review:** none.
