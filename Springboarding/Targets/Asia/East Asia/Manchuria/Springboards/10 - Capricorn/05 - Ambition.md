# Manchuria: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (including internet slang), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu for the founding chronicles, Russian. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Shandong and Hebei migrants:** family stories of coming north to make good.
- **Rustbelt workers' children** in Shenyang, Anshan and Fushun: ambition after the parents' ladder broke.
- **Dalian and the Liaodong coast:** port trade and foreign firms against the inland north.
- **Manchu families:** the shadow of the idle-banner idiom and its later reversals.
- **Chaoxianzu Koreans:** households built around work abroad and children's education.
- **Mongol families of Horqin and Hulunbuir:** schooling choices and herding against town careers.
- **Evenki, Oroqen, Daur and Hezhe youth:** leaving or staying, and with what hopes.
- **Border-trade towns** (Heihe, Suifenhe, Manzhouli): trading ambition across the Russian border.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration history and recent outmigration; mixed for attitudes (few province-level aspiration surveys); thin for small peoples' own voices on striving.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for census data, slang, livestream culture and migration history; Manchu for the founding chronicle; Korean for Yanbian's labor-migration research; Mongolian for families' schooling choices; Russian for border traders and émigré Harbin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Dalian scandal touches national politics; Mongolian-language schooling is politically sensitive; regional "decline" talk can slide into stereotype and should be tested, not repeated.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the 2020 census migration tables for Liaoning, Jilin and Heilongjiang by age.
2. Search CNKI for 东北 青年 外流 and 东北 考公 to find studies of the two exits from the region's ladders.
3. Read Gottschang and Lary's chapters on migrants' motives, then compare with 闯关东 memoir collections.
4. Search RISS and CNKI for 코리안 드림 연변 and 延边 朝鲜族 出国务工.
5. Locate the 《满洲实录》 facsimile and read its early sections on Nurhaci's rise.
