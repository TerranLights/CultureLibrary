# Manchuria: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese; Manchu for banner rank; Korean (speech levels); Mongolian (noble and commoner); Japanese for the colonial ethnic order; Russian. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of banner families against civilian Han families:** whether the old divide still shapes standing.
- **Cadre, worker and peasant families** of the planned economy; urban against rural household registration.
- **Chaoxianzu Koreans:** speech levels and age rank within the community, and its standing beside the majority.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** noble and commoner lines, and Mongol-Han relations.
- **Daur, Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe:** clan ties, perhaps less marked by rank; to research, not assume.
- **Ergun Russians and mixed families:** where they stood in border society.
- **Dalian, shaped by Russian and Japanese leasehold**, against Harbin's railway hierarchy and the farm country.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing banner and colonial hierarchies; mixed for the socialist work unit; thin for present-day class talk, which is muted in public sources.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for land reform, work-unit and banquet rank; Manchu for banner registers and court terms; Japanese for colonial ethnic rankings; Korean for speech-level studies; Mongolian for noble lineages; Russian for émigré and railway ranks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic ranking under Manchukuo is painful memory; land-reform violence and "class labels" (成分) are politically sensitive; banner descent claims can carry pride or stigma, so ask, do not assign.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the digital 《八旗通志》 for 包衣 and 佐领 to map banner ranks.
2. Search CNKI for 满族 民居 西炕 and 满族 礼俗 座次.
3. Read Rhoads's chapters on the end of banner status, then search CNKI for 旗人 民人 关系 东北.
4. Search RISS for 연변 방언 높임법 to find Yanbian honorific studies.
5. Pull the three provincial yearbooks' urban and rural income tables for the latest year.
