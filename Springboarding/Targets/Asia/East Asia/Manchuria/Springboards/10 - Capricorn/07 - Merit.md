# Manchuria: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese; classical Chinese and Manchu for examination and military-merit records; Japanese for colonial schooling; Korean; Mongolian; Russian. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of former state-factory workers:** posts once passed on by *dingti*, now sought by exam.
- **Manchu families:** banner quota memory against open competition.
- **Chaoxianzu Koreans:** Korean-medium schooling and exam strategies.
- **Mongols and Daur:** earned titles in games beside school-based merit.
- **Evenki and Oroqen:** hunters' earned standing before and after settlement.
- **Graduates of elite universities** (Jilin University, Harbin Institute of Technology) against vocational graduates.
- **Private-business owners in Dalian and Shenyang** against civil servants: two definitions of having earned it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on examinations, model workers and honors; mixed on hiring practice; thin on small peoples' ways of earning standing.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for exam rules, forums and model-worker records; classical Chinese for the Jin history; Manchu for military-merit grants; Japanese for Kenkoku records; Korean for Yanbian admissions; Mongolian for naadam titles; Russian for émigré professional standing in Harbin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic bonus points and Korean- and Mongolian-medium schooling are politically sensitive; corruption cases need care with named people.
- **Held for review:** none.
