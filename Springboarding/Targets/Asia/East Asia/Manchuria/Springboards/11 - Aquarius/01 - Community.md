# Manchuria: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Korean; Mongolian; Manchu (archival); Hui sources in Mandarin; small-people terms through Chinese ethnography. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest or cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rust-belt work-unit districts** (Tiexi in Shenyang, Anshan, Fushun, Benxi) against newer commercial housing estates, where neighbors may be strangers.
- **Planned towns:** Daqing's oilfield settlements; Beidahuang farms; Khingan forestry towns, each built for one employer.
- **Han villages** by settler origin, and by the age of settlement (Liaoning first, northern Heilongjiang last).
- **Korean villages in Yanbian,** strongly affected by working-age migration.
- **Manchu clan villages** in Jilin and Heilongjiang, where clan rites may define the community.
- **Hui neighborhoods** clustered around mosques in the main cities.
- **Mongol villages** of Khorchin, Fuxin and Dorbod; herders of the steppe margin if included.
- **Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhen** settlements created by sedentarization drives; the camp before, the settlement after.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Northeastern neighborhoods formed in Sanya and in Seoul's Daerim-dong.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban community-building and work units (much Chinese policy literature); mixed for villages; thin and mostly secondhand for the small Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin for policy, farm and factory histories; Classical Chinese and Manchu to read clan and Jin settlement communities; Korean for Yanbian village news and memoir; Mongolian for herding-group terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residents' committees double as surveillance; rust-belt layoffs and protests; resettlement of hunting peoples; clan rites closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
