# Manchuria: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Manchu and Classical Chinese (Qing records); Korean; Mongolian; Japanese (colonial administration). **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banner descendants** (Manchu, Han-martial and Mongol banners) against families of civilian settlers: an old legal line between groups, worth tracing today.
- **Manchu clans** in Jilin and Heilongjiang, where hala and mukūn may still organize members.
- **Koreans,** whose group belonging has been claimed by three states.
- **Mongol banners** of Khorchin, Jarud and Fuxin, with their own territorial groups.
- **Daur, Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhen,** each with its own clans, and each grouped from outside by the 1950s classification.
- **Hui,** whose group line runs through faith.
- **Industrial workforces** (Anshan, Fushun, Daqing), where the work group was the main group.
- **Sent-down youth cohorts** of the Beidahuang farms, a generation-group formed by one experience.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Korean Chinese workers in South Korea, sorted by host society into a group of their own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on banners, ethnic categories and wartime organization; mixed on present-day group life; thin on clan organization among the smallest peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu and Classical Chinese for banner and Jurchen group-making; Japanese for the colonial collective-responsibility files; Korean for Korean-Chinese membership debates; Mongolian for banner and clan names; Mandarin for the classification literature and fan media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collaboration and "traitor" labels from the 1930s and 1940s; the 2009 Tonghua Steel unrest and other labor clashes; a new religious movement founded in Changchun in 1992 and banned since 1999 (its own and state sources are both partisan); organized-crime trials.
- **Held for review:** none.
