# Manchuria: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese and Manchu (Qing status law and edicts); Japanese (Manchukuo); Korean; Mongolian. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banner descendants and civilian-settler descendants:** once unequal in law; check whether that memory survives.
- **Rust-belt workers** laid off in the 1990s against those kept in state firms and the new private middle class.
- **Rural and urban residents** under household registration; state-farm employees, who stood between.
- **Men and women** in heavy industry and on state farms, where equal-work campaigns were strongest.
- **Koreans, Mongols and Manchu** under group-specific policies (bonus points, autonomous areas).
- **Hui,** whose equality claims run through faith and diet.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhen,** whose own sharing customs met state redistribution.
- **Dalian and Shenyang** against small northern towns: regional gaps inside the nation.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Korean Chinese ranked as a separate group in South Korea's labor market.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land reform, socialist industry and income gaps; mixed for attitudes; thin for the small peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese and Manchu for Qing status rules; Japanese for colonial ration and wage scales; Korean for speech levels and schooling debates; Mongolian for autonomy-policy writing; Mandarin for land-reform and layoff studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land-reform violence; wartime ethnic ranking; layoff protests; minority preferential policies as a political subject.
- **Held for review:** none.
