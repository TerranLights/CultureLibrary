# Manchuria: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Japanese (colonial-era utopian projects); Korean; Mongolian; Russian (Harbin émigrés); Manchu (founding legends). **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sent-down youth generation** (now elderly) against their children and grandchildren, for the arc from ideal to disillusion.
- **Oilfield and steel families** raised on model-worker ideals, against private-sector newcomers.
- **Koreans,** with an independence-era educational ideal rooted in Longjing and Yanbian.
- **Daur and eastern Mongols,** whose 1920s revolutionaries held their own national vision.
- **Manchu,** whose founding legend became a court ideal; check how today's Manchu associations use it.
- **Harbin's old Russian and Jewish communities,** with émigré ideals of many kinds (monarchist, Zionist, fascist, Soviet).
- **Religious communities** (Buddhist, Daoist, Christian, Muslim) with their own pictures of the good life.
- **Young urban professionals** in Dalian and Shenyang, whose ideals may be career- or consumption-shaped.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Japanese former settlers and their children, who keep their own idealized and mourned image of the region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state ideals and colonial utopian projects; mixed for private ideals; thin for the smaller peoples' own visions.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese for Kenkoku University and the pioneer settlers' own words; Korean for the independence schools and Kim Hak-cheol; Russian for Harbin's émigré programs; Mongolian and Mandarin for Merse's circle; Manchu for the founding legend's original text.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Manchukuo nostalgia and its critics; the Cultural Revolution; state hero cults (questioning them can be restricted); far-right émigré history.
- **Held for review:** none.
