# Manchuria: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Northeastern colloquial especially); Manchu (classical dictionaries); Mongolian; Korean; Japanese and Russian for friendships across peoples. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men and women** in industrial cities: the Shenyang writers' male friendships against women's networks, less studied.
- **Villagers** of Heilongjiang (gift-network studies) against Liaoning villages with older settlement.
- **Koreans,** whose friendship vocabulary and speech levels set their own rules.
- **Mongols,** with the *anda* tradition as a living word.
- **Oroqen and Evenki,** whose trade friendships linked them to outsiders.
- **Han settler families** where sworn brotherhood joined migrants without kin.
- **Older generations** shaped by Sino-Soviet friendship and work-unit comradeship, against young people who meet friends online.
- **Japanese-orphan families,** whose bonds cross a war line.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** returned Japanese orphans in Japan and their contact with Chinese foster families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gift networks and guanxi; mixed for friendship as such (often folded into "connections"); thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mongolian for the *anda* tradition; Manchu for the Qing dictionaries' friendship words; Japanese for orphans' and foster families' testimony; Russian for the friendship-association material; Korean for Yanbian programs; Mandarin for gift-network and sworn-brotherhood studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drinking stereotypes of the region; wartime enmity and the orphan question; friendship and favoritism shading into corruption.
- **Held for review:** none.
