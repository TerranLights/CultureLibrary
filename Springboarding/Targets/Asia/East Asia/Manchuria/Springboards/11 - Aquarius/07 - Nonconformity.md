*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rust-belt youth** (the "children of the laid-off") against young professionals in Dalian, for local styles of rebellion.
- **Rural Han** with spirit-medium traditions, against urban secular residents.
- **Koreans** of Yanbian, whose young people may follow Seoul's culture.
- **Mongols** of Khorchin, with shamanic and healing-dance traditions.
- **Manchu** who revive shaman rites, against those who regard them as superstition.
- **Russian Old Believers** and other religious dissenters, historically.
- **Online creators** from small Northeastern towns, against the cultural authorities that regulate them.
- **LGBTQ residents** of the big cities, mostly unseen in official sources.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Old Believer communities descended from Manchurian settlers abroad, for continuity of dissent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on folk religion and folk art; mixed on subcultures (mostly online and journalistic); thin and restricted on dissidents and sexual minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for the Ningguta exiles' writing; Russian for Old Believer records; Mongolian for Khorchin healing rites; Korean for Yanbian youth culture; Manchu for Qing terms of heterodoxy; Mandarin with Northeastern slang for online styles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political dissent; banned religious groups; LGBTQ life; mediums' private knowledge. Persons named here are public figures; private individuals in fieldwork need protection.
- **Held for review:** none.
