# Manchuria: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese (monastic gazetteers); Mongolian (Buddhist vocabulary); Korean; Manchu; English (a plague doctor's memoir). **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings or from outcomes, in forms that may be prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity) or condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monastic communities** (Han Buddhist, Quanzhen Daoist, Mongol Tibetan-Buddhist), whose detachment is a vocation.
- **Elders left alone** by outmigration in small towns and Korean villages, against elders in Dalian or Shenyang with family nearby.
- **Young people** who opt out ("lie flat") against those who leave to compete elsewhere.
- **Forest workers** (ginseng diggers, loggers, forest rangers) who spend long spells alone.
- **Evenki and Oroqen,** whose withdrawal after resettlement is a distinct, painful case.
- **Medical and scientific workers,** heirs of the plague-fighting tradition, for professional distance.
- **Koreans** and **Mongols,** whose own emotional-style vocabularies differ from Mandarin's.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Yanbian parents working abroad, whose distance from home is the other side of the elders' isolation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for isolation data and monastic history; mixed for emotional style (stereotype-heavy); thin for small peoples' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for temple gazetteers; Mongolian and Tibetan terms for lamasery teaching; Manchu for the Buddhist canon's vocabulary; Korean for Yanbian elders' welfare research; English for Wu Lien-teh; Mandarin for the census, surveys and lying-flat coverage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health and alcohol questions among resettled hunting peoples (handle with the communities' consent); religious life under state control; stereotypes of Northeastern temperament.
- **Held for review:** none.
