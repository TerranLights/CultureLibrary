# Manchuria: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Manchu (shaman texts and folk tales); Korean; Mongolian; Russian for Harbin émigré memory. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Older rust-belt residents** who lived through the layoffs, against their children, who may hope to leave or to rebuild.
- **Shrinking northern towns** (coal and forestry) against Dalian and Shenyang.
- **Korean families** whose hopes run through work in South Korea.
- **Manchu clans** that keep shaman prayers for fortune; Manchu villagers who no longer do.
- **Mongols,** with *khiimori* and other fortune rites.
- **Christian, Buddhist and Daoist believers,** whose hopes reach past this life.
- **Farm families** on the black-soil plain, whose hopes follow the harvest.
- **Daur, Evenki and Oroqen** cultural revivalists, hoping to keep a language alive.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Northeastern retirees in Hainan and Korean Chinese in Seoul, whose hopes left the region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for economic expectations and migration; mixed for religious and folk hope; thin for small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu for the shaman hymns and the Nišan manuscripts; Korean for Yun Dong-ju and the "Korean dream" studies; Mongolian for fortune rites; Russian for émigré memoirs; Mandarin for surveys, lottery data and online discourse.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data; regional decline as a political topic; clan prayer texts that families may not share.
- **Held for review:** none.
