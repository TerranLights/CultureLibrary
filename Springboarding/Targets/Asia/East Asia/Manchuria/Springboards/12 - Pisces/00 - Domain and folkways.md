# Manchuria: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia (pre-war lands carried: part of China). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeast Mandarin), Korean (Yanbian), Mongolian (Horqin, Barga, Buryat), Manchu (heritage and liturgical), Daur, Evenki, Oroqen, Hezhe (Nanai), Xibe; Japanese and Russian for colonial-era and émigré records. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and transcendence: how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape, with magic ways (healing, divination, protection, spirit work) as its folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han of Liaoning against Han of Heilongjiang and Jilin:** older southern settlement against northern frontier and state-farm towns.
- **Descendants of Shandong and Hebei migrants (闯关东):** to separate carried-over custom from local custom.
- **Manchu banner descendants, including Han-army banner (汉军旗) lines:** clan shamanism and the Han-army drum rites (单鼓) form separate traditions.
- **Joseonjok of Yanbian and urban Shenyang:** Korean shaman rites and a strong church presence change the map of the unseen.
- **Horqin Mongols, and Barga and Buryat Mongols of Hulunbuir:** Buddhist and shamanic healing in different balance.
- **Daur, Evenki (including the Aoluguya reindeer herders), Oroqen, Hezhe:** own spirit traditions, recorded mostly by outsiders.
- **Xibe of Shenyang, Hui, and the Russians of Ergun:** each keeps a separate religious life.
- **Diaspora as comparison only:** Harbin's Russian and Jewish émigrés; Joseonjok in South Korea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Qing ritual texts, colonial surveys and folklore; thin for regional surveys of belief or distress.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for nearly everything current; Manchu for liturgy and epics; Korean for Yanbian rites and church life; Mongolian for Horqin and Hulunbuir healing; Japanese for Manchukuo surveys; Russian for Harbin émigré and early ethnographic records.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (its tracker carries "China" as pre-war lands); the sign's sheets are written for the three Northeast provinces and the adjoining eastern steppe. In this setting Manchuria is a culture-nation of its own, separate from the Sinian Federation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan spirit registers and shaman songs may be clan-private; Unit 731, labor camps, the 1990s layoffs and religious crackdowns are politically sensitive; mediums may shun outsiders because of the "superstition" label.
- **Held for review:** none.
