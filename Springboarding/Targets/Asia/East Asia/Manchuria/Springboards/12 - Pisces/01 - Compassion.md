# Manchuria: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeast Mandarin), Korean, Mongolian, Manchu (historical), with Japanese and English for mission and colonial records. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist lay devotees against Protestant congregations in rural Heilongjiang and Jilin:** charity flows through merit in one, through church fellowship in the other.
- **Former state-enterprise workers against private-sector and rural households:** the old work-unit safety net against crowdfunding and kin.
- **Joseonjok of Yanbian:** money sent home from work in South Korea reshapes who helps whom.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** Buddhist almsgiving and herding-family hospitality to travelers in need.
- **Hui communities of Shenyang and Harbin:** mosque-based giving with its own calendar.
- **Evenki, Oroqen and Hezhe:** sharing of game and fish as a separate frame from charity.
- **Diaspora as comparison only:** Harbin's former Jewish and Russian émigré relief societies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Gazetteers and mission records are rich for poor relief; current giving data are national with thin provincial cuts; civil society is small and state-managed.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for gazetteers, platforms and the state model; Manchu to read gosin and jilan in Qing texts; Korean for Yanbian church and family giving; Mongolian for temple and herding hospitality; Japanese for Manchukuo relief records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foreign-funded NGOs are tightly regulated; aid by Yanbian churches to people crossing from North Korea is a sensitive, hidden subject; crowdfunding pages carry private medical details.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up gosin and jilan in the 1708 Manchu dictionary and note their defining phrases.
2. Search the 1934 Fengtian gazetteer for 普济堂 and 育婴堂 to list relief houses and their rules.
3. Search Shuidichou for appeals from 黑龙江, 吉林, 辽宁 and sample how suffering is described.
4. Pull the China Charity Alliance reports and extract the three provinces' giving figures.
5. Search CNKI for 红卍字会 满洲 to find studies of religious relief under Manchukuo.
