# Manchuria: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oil and state-farm towns (Daqing, the Beidahuang farms):** production sacrifice is their founding story.
- **Rust-belt families in Shenyang, Anshan, Fushun:** a parents' generation that gave decades to firms that later shut.
- **Sent-down youth who stayed in Heilongjiang:** the migrants who never went home.
- **Joseonjok:** independence-era martyrs, and parents working in South Korea to pay for children's schooling.
- **Manchu banner descendants:** older ideals of loyal service and widow constancy.
- **Women in multigenerational households:** daughters-in-law as caregivers; what is expected of them against sons.
- **Horqin Mongols and Hulunbuir herders:** sacrifice in herding families during dzud winters and drought.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official memory (museums, models, martyr lists); mixed for family sacrifice; thin for voices that resent sacrifice.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for model campaigns, memoirs and surveys; Manchu for the 1626 records and banner sources; Korean for Joseonjok martyr memory; Japanese for Manchukuo-era deaths and the plague years' records; Mongolian for herders' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martyr narratives are state-curated and contested; organ-donation history in China is sensitive; Korean War memory and Kim Il-sung's Manchurian guerrilla years are politically delicate; criticism of model workers is risky to publish.
- **Held for review:** none.
