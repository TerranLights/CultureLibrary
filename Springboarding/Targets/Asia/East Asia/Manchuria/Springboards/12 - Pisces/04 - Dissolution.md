# Manchuria: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeast Mandarin), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian (Horqin), Xibe, with Japanese and Russian for communities that dissolved in the twentieth century. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu clans with surviving rites (Jilin) against city Manchu who keep only a registered identity:** two very different relations to the old forms.
- **Sanjiazi speakers and new Manchu learners:** language loss and its reversal side by side.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe:** resettled hunters and herders whose ways of life were ended by policy.
- **Xibe of Shenyang:** a people whose language left with the westward branch.
- **Joseonjok:** emigration to South Korea emptying villages and schools in Yanbian.
- **Rust-belt and forestry towns:** the end of the work unit as a whole way of life.
- **Horqin Mongols:** trance and group dance against settled farming life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language loss and Manchu identity; mixed for trance (films and folklore, little fieldwork in other languages); thin for dementia in the region.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu for shaman songs and Sanjiazi speech; Chinese for most scholarship and statistics; Korean for Yanbian depopulation; Mongolian for andai and Horqin rites; Japanese for the settler collapse; Russian for the end of Harbin's émigré life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan rites may be closed to outsiders; resettlement of hunting peoples and assimilation are politically sensitive; the 1945 collapse involves wartime violence against and by settlers.
- **Held for review:** none.
