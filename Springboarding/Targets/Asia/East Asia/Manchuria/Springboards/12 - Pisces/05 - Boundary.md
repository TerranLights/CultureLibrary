# Manchuria: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeast Mandarin), Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Russian (border towns, treaty texts), Japanese (Manchukuo records), Latin (the 1689 treaty). **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han villagers on the shared heated platform against urban apartment dwellers:** family privacy may change with the house.
- **Joseonjok of Yanbian:** a people on both sides of a national line, with their own family and in-law rules.
- **Manchu banner descendants:** old garrison-city separation and the house's sacred west side.
- **Hui:** food and marriage lines that mark the community.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** pasture boundaries, fencing of grasslands and the Mongol-Han farming line.
- **Russians of Ergun and the border towns:** mixed families living on the Chinese-Russian line.
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Hezhe:** hunting and fishing territories redrawn by settlement and reserves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state frontiers and treaties; mixed for house and family space; thin for personal-space or privacy surveys in the region.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu and Latin for the treaty and palisade records; Russian for the Amur frontier and border towns; Korean for the Tumen line and Yanbian family life; Mongolian for pasture and fencing disputes; Japanese for Manchukuo ethnic categories; Chinese throughout.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Gando question, the lost Amur lands and the 1900 Amur killings are politically charged in three countries; the North Korean border is restricted; grassland fencing touches Mongol land disputes.
- **Held for review:** one standard English-language study of Manchukuo's sovereignty and national boundaries, set aside under the standing rule on South Asian scholars.
