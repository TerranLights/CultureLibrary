## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Laid-off workers and their sons:** escapes in the 1990s crisis against those of the online generation.
- **Village households in the winter months:** the idle season against city work rhythms.
- **Retirees who winter in Hainan against those who stay:** escape priced by pension and health.
- **Joseonjok of Yanbian:** a strong pull of work and life in South Korea as an exit.
- **Oroqen and Evenki:** escape and despair after resettlement, studied with care.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** drinking customs at feasts and festivals, against drinking alone.
- **Women:** mahjong, square dancing and livestreaming as escapes that may be judged differently from men's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for drinking and lotteries in national surveys; mixed for drug use (state framing); thin for fantasy, games and reading habits at regional level.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for health surveys, lottery data and fiction; Japanese for the opium monopoly; Korean for Yanbian nightlife and labor migration; Mongolian for feast drinking customs; Manchu only for older words for drink.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug-rehabilitation centers are compulsory and closed; gambling is mostly illegal and hidden; drinking among small peoples is a stigma risk; health data access is controlled.
- **Held for review:** none.
