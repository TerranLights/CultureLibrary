## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han temple-goers against lay Buddhist circles (居士):** vows to efficacious deities against disciplined cultivation.
- **Rural Protestants and Catholics:** prayer and healing experience in small congregations.
- **Party members and the openly secular:** the sacred kept at a distance, and what remains (graves, Changbai, holidays).
- **Manchu clans:** Changbai and ancestral rites as a separate sacred map.
- **Joseonjok:** Christian, Buddhist and folk practice, and Paektu as homeland mountain.
- **Mongols of Horqin and Hulunbuir:** obo, Tibetan Buddhism and sky worship together.
- **Oroqen, Evenki, Daur and Hezhe:** forest, river and mountain spirits.
- **Harbin's Orthodox remnant and the Russians of Ergun:** Orthodox practice on the frontier.
- **Former qigong practitioners:** a generation's spiritual experiment, now partly hidden.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing sacred geography and temples; mixed for surveys (national with thin regional samples); thin and guarded for unregistered practice.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu for the origin chronicle and the Mukden ode; Chinese for temples, surveys and qigong history; Korean for Paektu and Joseonjok church life; Mongolian for obo rites; Russian for Orthodox Harbin; Oroqen and Evenki terms through Chinese-language ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Falun Gong began in Changchun and is banned, so qigong history is guarded; house churches are unregistered; Paektu touches a Korean-Chinese dispute; some forest and clan rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
