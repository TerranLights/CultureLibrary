# Manchuria: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Manchuria, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin Chinese (Northeast Mandarin), Manchu, Japanese, Korean, Mongolian, Russian (1945 records). **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of Manchu nobles and bannermen:** the 1912 abdication as a family memory of yielding.
- **Families of laid-off state workers:** the buyout as a private surrender, against those who protested.
- **Veterans' families on different sides of 1945-1949:** surrender as honor, shame or survival.
- **Lay Buddhists:** letting go at death as a practiced discipline.
- **Christians and Hui Muslims:** yielding to God's will in their own words.
- **Joseonjok:** 팔자 and resignation in Korean idiom, and families split by migration.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** fate and herding losses in drought and blizzard.
- **Evenki and Oroqen:** giving up the forest life under resettlement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and dynastic surrender; mixed for end-of-life practice; thin for everyday letting go.
- **Languages needed:** Manchu for conquest-era submission records; Chinese for the Qing biographies, museums and surveys; Japanese for 1945 memoirs and Fushun confessions; Russian for the Soviet campaign; Korean for 팔자 and Joseonjok resignation; Mongolian for herders' accounts of loss.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Changchun siege, the 1931 non-resistance order and the Fushun "reform" are politically managed; settlers' deaths in 1945 are painful in Japan and China; end-of-life data are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
