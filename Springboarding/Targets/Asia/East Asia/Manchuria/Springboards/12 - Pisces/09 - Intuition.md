*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daur:** the elder-shaman pairing in the ethnography is a frame to test against Daur speakers' own accounts.
- **Hezhe fishers and Oroqen and Evenki hunters:** knowledge of land, water and animals.
- **Ginseng diggers and forest workers of the Changbai uplands:** a closed craft with its own signs.
- **Coal-mining towns:** danger-sense in dangerous work.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** touch-diagnosis and sign-reading in herding life.
- **Joseonjok:** dream-reading in Korean idiom.
- **Urban professionals in Shenyang, Dalian and Harbin:** data-led work against "experience", in medicine, trade and policing.
- **Border traders (Suifenhe, Heihe):** reading strangers across a language line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folklore and ethnography are rich; measured intuition studies rarely name the region; the 1975 prediction is well studied but contested.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for medicine, folklore and psychology; Daur terms through Humphrey and Chinese ethnography; Hezhe through Ling's Chinese text; Mongolian for divination and bone-setting; Korean for dream customs; Manchu for older words of perception.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fortune-telling is officially "superstition", so practitioners may hide; the Haicheng story has political uses; some ginseng-digging lore may be guarded within families.
- **Held for review:** none.
