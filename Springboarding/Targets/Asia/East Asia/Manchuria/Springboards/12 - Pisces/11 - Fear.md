## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han villagers and city dwellers:** animal-spirit dread and ghost talk against urban anxiety.
- **Oroqen, Evenki and Hezhe:** predators, the forest and the river, and their avoidance customs.
- **Manchu families:** lullabies and clan spirits that frighten or protect.
- **Joseonjok:** ghosts and sleep paralysis in Korean idiom; life near a guarded border.
- **Horqin and Hulunbuir Mongols:** blizzard, wolf and drought as named dangers.
- **Forest villages near Hunchun and the Changbai uplands:** fear of tigers returning.
- **Harbin and Fushun families with plague or massacre memories:** inherited collective fear.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemic and wartime terror and for folklore; mixed for disasters; thin for measured anxiety at regional level.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for folklore, press and search data; Manchu for cradle songs and older words of fright; Korean for Yanbian tales; Mongolian for herding dangers; Oroqen and Evenki words through Chinese ethnography; English, Russian and Japanese for the plague years.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2022 lockdowns, the 2005 spill's handling and massacre memory are politically managed; ghost and spirit talk carries the "superstition" label; some avoidance words may be restricted knowledge.
- **Held for review:** none.
