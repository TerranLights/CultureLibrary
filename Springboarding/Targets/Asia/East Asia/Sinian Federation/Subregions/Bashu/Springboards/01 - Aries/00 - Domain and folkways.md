# Bashu: Domain and folkways, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Mandarin; Southwestern Mandarin (Sichuanese of Chengdu and Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming and Guiyang speech); Nuosu Yi, Tibetan (Kham and Amdo), Hmong-Mien (Hmu, A-Hmao, Hmong), Tujia, Dong, Bai, Naxi, Tai Lü and other minority languages. **Concept in one line:** the domain of self and identity: what a self is here, and how it asserts itself (personhood, the body, appearance, naming ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han regional cultures:** the Chengdu plain (Shu), Chongqing and eastern Sichuan (Ba), the Wuhan and Jianghan region of Hubei (Chu heritage), Han settler descendants in Yunnan and Guizhou, including Anshun's Tunpu (屯堡) people.
- **Sichuan Hakka** (Luodai, Dongshan) and the migrant-descended "Huguang" lineages.
- **Yi peoples:** Liangshan Nuosu, and Yunnan's Sani, Lolopo and Nisu, who differ in dress and ritual.
- **Tibetans** of Garzê (Kham), Ngawa (Amdo, Gyalrong) and Diqing; the **Qiang** (Rma) and Baima.
- **Miao (Hmu, A-Hmao, Hmong), Dong, Buyi, Shui and Gelao** of Guizhou; **Tujia** of Enshi and southeast Chongqing.
- **Yunnan's** Bai, Naxi, Na (Mosuo), Dai, Hani, Lisu, Wa, Jingpo, Lahu and Hui.
- **Urban and rural people, and migrant workers** living away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han urban Sichuan, Chongqing and Wuhan (psychology, media, fiction); mixed for Yunnan and Guizhou minorities, where ethnography is strong but survey data thin.
- **Lands:** Bashu's exact lands are still to be settled; the sign's sheets are written for the five core units its tracker lists (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou).
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and written Chinese; Sichuanese and Wuhan speech; Nuosu (Yi script), Tibetan, Hmong-Mien and Tai for the minority material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification is contested by some groups; Tibetan and Liangshan areas can be hard to reach; bimo, Dongba and Tibetan ritual knowledge may be restricted to specialists; old outsider portraits carry colonial framing.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. CNKI: 西南 自我建构 (self-construal) and 民族 身份认同, filtered by Sichuan, Yunnan and Guizhou universities.
2. Read Hsu's *Under the Ancestors' Shadow* and Lin Yaohua's 《凉山夷家》 for two baseline models of a self in lineage and clan.
3. Search the 2020 census tables for each province's ethnic composition to fix the comparison units.
4. Search 字辈 族谱 四川 and 摩梭 成年礼 (the Na coming-of-age rite) for naming and personhood material.
5. Request the Sanxingdui Museum's and the Yunnan Nationalities Museum's (Kunming) catalogs for the body and dress record.
