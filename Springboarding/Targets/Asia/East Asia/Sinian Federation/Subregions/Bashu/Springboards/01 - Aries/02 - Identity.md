*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Southwestern Mandarin varieties; Sichuan Hakka; Nuosu Yi, Tibetan, Qiang, Miao, Tujia, Lisu, Naxi, Bai, Dai. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ba (Chongqing) against Shu (Chengdu plain):** one family or two since 1997.
- **Hubei:** Chu heritage claims, and Wuhan's river-port mix of origins.
- **Settler-descended Han:** Huguang-origin Sichuan lineages, Hakka of Luodai, Tunpu of Anshun, Yunnan Han with Nanjing origin tales.
- **Groups contesting their label:** Chuanqing, Gejia (classed as Miao), Na (Mosuo) classed as Naxi in Yunnan and as Mongol in Sichuan, Baima classed as Tibetan.
- **Religious identities:** Yunnan Hui, Lisu and A-Hmao Christians, Tibetan Catholics of Diqing, Dai Theravada Buddhists.
- **Language-shift cases:** the Mongols of Xingmeng, Tonghai, who speak Kazhuo; Tujia (most no longer speak Tujia), Qiang villages with and without the language.
- **Migrant workers and their children:** identity split between hometown and coast.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ethnic identity (decades of ethnography); mixed for Han regional identity, which is mostly in Chinese-language local studies and online talk.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and local Mandarin varieties; Hakka; Nuosu Yi, Tibetan, Qiang, Lisu, Naxi for self-naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification and recognition claims are political; Tibetan and Hui identity questions can be restricted; ancestry DNA claims need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
