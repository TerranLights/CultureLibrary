## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zigong and the salt towns:** old investor networks against later state ownership.
- **Hankou and Wuhan:** river-port merchants, and the small traders of the reform era.
- **Chongqing's factory belt:** motorcycle and car makers grown from workshops; workers' place in them.
- **Chengdu:** tech startups and the city's leisure image, which may cut against hustle.
- **Yunnan's trading peoples:** Bai of Xizhou, Hui caravan families, Tengchong Han.
- **Kham Tibetans** in the tea trade; **Dong and Miao villages**, where works are started by the community.
- **Women founders and returning migrants:** businesses started with savings from the coast.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business and reform history and for statistics; thinner for everyday initiative at home and at work, which needs ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; local speech for oral histories; Bai, Kam and Tibetan for community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** model-worker stories are state-made and need cross-checking; Wenchuan's volunteer and NGO history touches school collapses, which are politically sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Zelin's *The Merchants of Zigong* for the documented baseline of private enterprise.
2. Pull new-firm and self-employed-household counts from each province's statistical yearbook (2010 to the latest).

4. Search 汉正街 口述史 and 喜洲商帮 for traders' own accounts.
5. Search 功德碑 侗族 风雨桥 for inscriptions recorded by local scholars.
