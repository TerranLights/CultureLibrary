## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chongqing against Chengdu:** a fiery reputation against a smoldering, sulking one; both labels to test.
- **Wuhan:** loud street quarrels as public theater, and the novels that draw on them.
- **Yunnan and Guizhou Han towns:** less studied; check gazetteers and local TV.
- **Liangshan Nuosu:** clan feuds and their mediators.
- **Miao and Dong villages:** elders and songs that settle disputes.
- **Kham Tibetan herders:** grassland feuds and lama mediation.
- **Women and men, workers and bosses:** who may show anger, and at whom (wage disputes, family quarrels).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history, fiction and medicine; mixed for emotion surveys split by province; thin and sensitive for collective and domestic anger.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and Classical Chinese; Sichuanese and Wuhan speech; Nuosu Yi and Tibetan for feud material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protests and riots are censored; domestic violence data is patchy; Tibetan feud and monastery material may be restricted; regional "fiery temper" talk is a stereotype to test, not use.
- **Held for review:** a Buddhist scriptural text of South Asian origin taught on anger and patience in the Tibetan monasteries of the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. CNKI: 郁怒 肝气郁结 临床 四川 OR 湖北, to find how clinicians frame anger.

3. Search 德古 调解 凉山 and 理老 苗族 纠纷 for minority mediation studies.
4. Watch a sample of 《雾都夜话》 episodes and log the triggers and endings of quarrels.
5. Browse the Sichuan Provincial Archives' published Ba County volumes for quarrel and insult cases.
