# Bashu (巴蜀): Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Southwestern Mandarin; Classical Chinese; Nuosu, Tibetan, Naxi, Hmu and Hmong, Kam and Qiang for minority sources. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

English keywords: "Sichuan" or "Yunnan" with "material culture", "heirloom", "grave goods", "earthquake survivors belongings", "collecting", "secondhand market", "possessive construction".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu** households against **Chongqing**'s hill-city flats: how space shapes storage and display.
- **Three Gorges resettled households** (Fengjie, Wushan, Yunyang, Yichang): what moved with them.
- **Out-migrant families** of rural Sichuan, Chongqing and Guizhou: what the absent send and keep, and the belongings of left-behind children.
- **Nuosu (Yi)** of Liangshan: the dead's goods under a cremation tradition *check*.
- **Tibetans** of Garzê and Ngawa: household shrines and offerings to monasteries.
- **Naxi** of Lijiang: sacred manuscripts as owned objects.
- **Miao and Dong** of Qiandongnan: embroidered clothing and silver made for and handed to daughters.
- **Hui** of Yunnan and **Bai** of Dali: household and village objects kept by merchant families.
- Comparison only: Sichuanese migrants in other federation states.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in museums, film and marketplaces; thin in ethnography of everyday belongings for most minorities; possessive grammar likely covered in 《民族语文》 and similar linguistics journals *check*.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (modern and classical, including excavated-text conventions); Southwestern Mandarin slang; Nuosu, Naxi and Tibetan for owners' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2008 school collapses are politically sensitive; sacred Naxi and Tibetan objects; claims over excavated and repatriated objects. Theft and property claims as law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
