# Bashu: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin (Sichuan, Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming, Guiyang); Jianghuai and Gan speech in eastern Hubei; Sichuan Hakka; Nuosu Yi, Tibetan, Qiang, Hmongic, Kam, Bai, Naxi, Tai Lue. **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what is home, and who is family? (household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, family meals; building, family and child-rearing ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shu (Chengdu Plain) against Ba (eastern Sichuan, Chongqing):** the state's own name pairs two old cores, to test apart.
- **Hubei:** Wuhan, the Jianghan Plain villages, and the Tujia and Miao of Enshi.
- **Han settler layers:** Qing-era resettlers of Sichuan; the Hakka near Chengdu; the Tunpu of Anshun, with Ming garrison-colony house ways.
- **Yi:** Nuosu of Liangshan and the Yi of Yunnan and western Guizhou, whose clan systems may differ.
- **Tibetans of Ganzi and Aba:** Khams, Amdo and Gyalrong; herders and farmers apart.
- **Qiang, Tujia, Gelao:** the mountain belts around the Sichuan Basin.
- **Guizhou:** Miao (Hmub, A-Hmao, Hmong), Dong, Buyei and Shui, each with its own house and kin forms.
- **Yunnan:** Bai, Naxi, Mosuo, Hani, Dai, Lisu, Lahu, Wa, Jingpo, Zhuang, Yao and Hui, among others.
- **Split households and transplants:** families divided by coastal work; Third Front factory communities.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Yunnanese Chinese settlements in northern Thailand and Myanmar; Hmong communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han households; mixed for minorities, where much comes from outside ethnographers and 1950s classification surveys. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled: the five provinces in the tracker are a working list.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, with Sichuanese and Wuhan home and kin terms; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Hmongic; Bai and Naxi for specialist work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fieldwork in Tibetan and some Yi areas needs permits; relocation, birth-planning history and ethnic classification are touchy; family and inheritance law belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the 家庭户 type tables (一代户, 二代户, 三代户及以上) from the five 2020 provincial census yearbooks, by prefecture.
2. Read *Huayang guozhi*'s Ba and Shu sections for household and marriage customs, then Hsu (1948) and Lin (1947) for two contrasting pre-1950 baselines.
3. Search CNKI for 四川民居 OR 川西林盘 OR 吊脚楼 OR 三坊一照壁 to map house types by area.
4. In CFPS, compare co-residence with parents across Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan and Guizhou samples.
5. Search D-PLACE for Southwest China societies and note which peoples have no coding at all.
