# Bashu: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Southwestern Mandarin kin vocabulary; Sichuan Hakka; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Hmong and Hmub (Miao); Kam (Dong); Tujia-area Chinese; Bai; Na (Mosuo). **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu Plain against eastern Sichuan and Chongqing:** settler lineages formed after the Qing repopulation, possibly shallower than elsewhere.
- **Hubei:** Jianghan lineage villages against Wuhan's city families; Tujia lineages in Enshi.
- **Hakka of Luodai and Dongshan:** a minority within the Han that kept its own clan halls.
- **Zigong salt families and other merchant towns:** kin as business.
- **Nuosu Yi:** clan reckoning that may cross county and provincial lines.
- **Mosuo of Lugu Lake:** households built on the mother's line.
- **Hmub, A-Hmao and Hmong (Miao):** clans and in-law ties that may differ between eastern and western Guizhou.
- **Dong, Buyei, Shui, Gelao:** village lineage forms in Guizhou.
- **Khams, Amdo and Gyalrong Tibetans:** house-based families, and any brothers-sharing households, to check.
- **Bai and Naxi:** lowland town families in Dali and Lijiang.
- **Third Front families:** workers from other provinces who formed a new kin web in Panzhihua and Guizhou.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Sichuanese and Yunnanese families abroad keeping ties by remittance and calls.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han lineage records and surveys; mixed for minorities, with heavy reliance on a few outside ethnographers and some open disputes (Mosuo, Yi caste and clan).
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for genealogies; Sichuanese kin vocabulary; Nuosu Yi; Hmongic; Tibetan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies may be private; Yi caste history and Mosuo sexual customs draw sensational coverage; marriage, adoption and inheritance law go to Libra; family authority goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search 中国家谱总目 for genealogies compiled in Sichuan and Chongqing counties, and note founding-ancestor origins.
2. Search CNKI for 亲属称谓 四川方言 OR 西南官话 to collect kin terms and how far they stretch.
3. Pull CGSS items on contact and help among kin, by province.
4. Read Harrell (2001) and Shih (2010) side by side for the two most distinct minority family forms.
5. Search 分家 with 四川 OR 湖北 in CNKI for how households split today.
