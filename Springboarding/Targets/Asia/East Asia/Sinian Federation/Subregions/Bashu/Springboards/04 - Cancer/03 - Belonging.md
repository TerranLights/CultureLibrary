# Bashu: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Southwestern Mandarin; the self-names and in-group words of Nuosu Yi, Tibetan, Hmongic, Kam, Bai, Naxi and Na speakers. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sichuan Han by settler origin:** families that trace to Huguang, Guangdong (Hakka), Shaanxi or Jiangxi, each with its own hall and speech.
- **Chengdu people and Chongqing people:** a rivalry worth separating from Sichuan-wide identity.
- **Wuhan and the rest of Hubei:** whether Hubei feels part of a Southwest "us" at all.
- **Rural migrants in Chengdu, Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming and Guiyang:** paper status against felt status.
- **Third Front transplants and their children:** Shanghai, Northeast and other accents in Panzhihua and Guizhou.
- **Yi:** Nuosu of Liangshan against Yunnan and Guizhou Yi, who may not share a self-name.
- **Miao branches:** Hmub, A-Hmao, Hmong and others grouped under one official name.
- **Tibetans of Ganzi and Aba:** Khams, Amdo and Gyalrong speakers, and the seam with Han and Qiang neighbors.
- **Na (Mosuo):** a people classified differently on the two sides of a provincial line.
- **Tunpu, Chuanqing, Hui, Bai, Naxi, Dai:** groups whose official labels and self-names may not match.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Yunnanese Chinese villages in northern Thailand and Myanmar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on migration, hukou and guild halls; mixed on how minority people themselves describe belonging, much filtered through state categories.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Sichuanese slang; Nuosu Yi, Tibetan and Hmongic for self-naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification, unrecognized groups and Tibetan and Hui identity are politically sensitive; the Zhang Xianzhong massacre story behind the repopulation is contested history; citizenship rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
