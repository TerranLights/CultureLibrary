# Bashu: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese and other Southwestern Mandarin child-rearing talk; Nuosu Yi; Tibetan; Hmub and Hmong; Kam; Buyei and Shui for Guizhou craft terms. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Grandparent-raised children in the Sichuan Basin and Chongqing:** parents away, grandparents nurturing; set against city two-parent households.
- **Chengdu, Wuhan and Chongqing middle classes:** intensive schooling and enrichment.
- **Hubei:** Jianghan Plain villages against Enshi Tujia homes.
- **Nuosu Yi:** home language against state preschools.
- **Khams and Amdo Tibetans:** family, monastery and boarding school as competing nurturers.
- **Hmub and Hmong Miao, Dong, Buyei, Shui:** song, craft and work learned at home.
- **Bai and Naxi:** town families with long schooling traditions in Dali and Lijiang.
- **Dai, Hani, Lisu, Wa:** border peoples of southern and western Yunnan, where data is thin.
- **Fathers:** the father's part in daily nurture, to separate from mothers' and grandmothers'.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Sichuanese parents raising children in coastal cities without grandparents nearby.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rural child development and grandparent care (many surveys and NGO reports); mixed for minority child-rearing in the people's own words.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Sichuanese; Nuosu Yi; Tibetan; Hmongic and Kam for song and craft terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding schools and preschool language policy in Tibetan and Yi areas are sensitive; corporal punishment and discipline go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
