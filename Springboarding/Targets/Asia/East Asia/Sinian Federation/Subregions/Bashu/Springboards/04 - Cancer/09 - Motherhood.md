# Bashu: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Wuhan and Sichuanese speech; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Hmong and Hmub; Na (Mosuo); Tujia-area Chinese song texts. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the motherland, holy and mythic mothers, the earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu, Chongqing and Wuhan mothers:** paid work, confinement centers and few children.
- **Village mothers in labor-export counties:** working away, mothering at a distance.
- **Hubei and Sichuan Han in-law households:** a mother's standing toward her son's wife.
- **Tujia of Enshi and southeast Chongqing:** bridal laments and the mother-daughter bond.
- **Na (Mosuo):** mothers as household heads, and the mother's brother's role.
- **Nuosu Yi:** mothers' lines in sacred story and birth customs in Liangshan.
- **Hmub, A-Hmao and Hmong Miao:** the mythic mother and everyday mothering.
- **Khams and Amdo Tibetans:** mothers in herding and farming households, and the Buddhist image of all beings as mothers.
- **Bai, Naxi, Dai, Hani:** Yunnan peoples whose birth customs may differ widely.
- **Single, widowed and stepmothers:** separate their standing from the married birth mother's.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Bashu mothers working in coastal cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fertility, maternal health and postpartum markets; mixed for minority mothers' own voices; myth and song well collected but filtered through heritage reports.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Hmongic; Na through Chinese studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-limit history and its exemptions for minorities, coerced procedures, and Tibetan and Yi birth policy are sensitive; marriage and birth law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
