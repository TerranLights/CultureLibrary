**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (working lands: Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified script); Southwestern Mandarin varieties (Chengdu, Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming, Guiyang speech); Nuosu Yi, Khams and Amdo Tibetan, Hmub (Miao), Kam (Dong), Tujia, Bai, Naxi, Dai and Hani for the minority lands. **Concept in one line:** how people shine, play and take pleasure: creativity, play, pleasure, romance, children as joy, fame, celebration, gambling and sport.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu-plain Sichuanese and Chongqing (Ba) people:** the Cheng–Yu split is the first test of whether "Bashu" pleasure is one thing.
- **Wuhan and the Jianghan plain (Chu heritage); Gan-speaking southeast Hubei:** a different festival history and speech.
- **Tujia and Miao of Enshi and southeast Chongqing:** their own songs, dances and courtship.
- **Nuosu Yi of Liangshan; Yunnan's Yi branches (Sani, Lolopo):** contests and display at the Torch Festival vary by branch.
- **Khams Tibetans (Garzê), Amdo Tibetans and Qiang (Ngawa):** horse festivals, circle dance, monastery calendars.
- **Bai, Naxi and Mosuo (northwest Yunnan); Dai (Xishuangbanna, Dehong); Hani (Honghe); Jingpo, Wa, Lisu:** separate festival and courtship systems.
- **Hmub, Kam, Bouyei, Shui and Gelao (Guizhou); Tunpu garrison Han (Anshun):** village festival economies and staged display for tourism.
- **Hakka of Longquanyi (Chengdu); Hui of Yunnan; Christian Lisu and Miao:** groups whose pleasures may run on other calendars.
- **Migrant workers and the diaspora:** comparison only; the nation's findings apply to them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chengdu, Chongqing and Wuhan (press, history, tourism); mixed for Yunnan and Guizhou minorities (much is tourism-shaped); thin for small peoples. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet covers the five listed provinces.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese and the local Mandarin varieties for oral sources; Nuosu, Tibetan, Hmub, Kam and Dai for songs and festivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mosuo courtship is heavily exoticized; Tibetan festivals and gatherings in Garzê and Ngawa are politically watched; gambling and border casinos are policed; staged "ethnic" shows may not match village practice.
- **Held for review:** none.
