# Bashu: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese, Chongqing and Wuhan speech; Tibetan, Naxi, Hmub (Miao), Nuosu Yi and Tujia for craft traditions. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu, Chongqing, Wuhan:** animation and games, an art academy and graffiti, design and punk; three creative economies to tell apart.
- **Hmub (Miao) of Leishan and Taijiang; Bouyei and Miao batik makers of Danzhai and Anshun:** craft passed through women's lines and now sold to tourists.
- **Bai of Zhoucheng (tie-dye) and Jianshui potters; Rongchang potters in Chongqing:** workshop craft and its new markets.
- **Nuosu Yi lacquerware (Xide, Liangshan); Tujia xilankapu brocade (Enshi, southeast Chongqing); Qiang embroidery (Ngawa):** craft as ethnic marker.
- **Khams Tibetan thangka and metalwork schools of Garzê:** a monastic craft line with its own rules.
- **Naxi of Lijiang:** pictographic art drawn into the tourist market.
- **Rural improvisers and migrant makers:** everyday making-do, not only recognized art.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for heritage crafts and city industries (policy and press heavy); thin on how ordinary people talk about creativity; little on everyday improvising.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese for almost all sources; Tibetan for monastic craft; Naxi and Hmub for makers' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heritage status brings money and rivalry, so lineage claims are contested; Tibetan religious art in Garzê sits under political control; ritual images may be restricted by their owners.
- **Held for review:** none.
