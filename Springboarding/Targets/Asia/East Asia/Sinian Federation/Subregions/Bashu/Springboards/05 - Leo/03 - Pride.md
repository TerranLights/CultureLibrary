# Bashu: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese, Chongqing and Wuhan speech; Nuosu Yi, Hmub (Miao) and Khams Tibetan for epic and lineage sources. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu against Chongqing:** two cities with a rivalry; pride may follow different symbols.
- **Hubei (Wuhan, Jianghan plain):** Chu heritage and 九头鸟 as a label used both ways.
- **Guizhou Han and the Tunpu garrison descendants:** a province long described by outsiders' idioms of poverty and hubris.
- **Yunnan Han and Yunnan Hui:** frontier and Muslim pride may sit apart from the provincial brand.
- **Nuosu Yi:** clan pride and its internal strata (a sensitive topic).
- **Hmub, Kam and Bouyei of Guizhou:** epics and festival display as group pride.
- **Khams and Amdo Tibetans, Qiang:** horse festivals and ancestral stories, under political limits.
- **Huguang-descended families and the Hakka of Longquanyi:** pride in migrant origin.
- **Migrant workers and laid-off factory workers (Wuhan, Chongqing):** where wounded pride may show.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for city and provincial pride (press, sport, museums); mixed for minority pride (filtered through official ethnic policy); thin for personal pride versus modesty in daily talk.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese and Wuhan speech for chants and sayings; Nuosu for genealogy; Hmub for the Yalu Wang epic; Tibetan for Khams sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan and Nuosu group pride touch state ethnic policy; Nuosu caste history is painful; regional stereotypes (九头鸟, 夜郎自大) must be studied as sayings, never adopted.
- **Held for review:** none.
