# Bashu: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Chongqing and Sichuanese speech; Hmub (Miao), Bai and Khams Tibetan for festival and tourism settings. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hmub and Kam villages in tourism (Qiandongnan):** visibility as income and burden.
- **Mosuo of Lugu Lake:** heavily looked at by outsiders; how they manage it.
- **Khams Tibetans of Garzê:** sudden fame and close watching in the same region.
- **Wa and Lisu of the Yunnan border:** long misrepresented in Han accounts; recent self-made media.
- **Rural women creators in Sichuan and Guizhou:** a new path to visibility.
- **Left-behind children and empty-nest elders (Sichuan, Chongqing, Guizhou, Hubei villages):** the unseen at home.
- **Porters, sanitation workers and delivery riders in Chongqing, Wuhan and Chengdu:** unseen city labor.
- **Hui Muslims of Yunnan:** dress and visibility as faith matters.
- **LGBTQ people and people with disabilities:** visibility shaped by family pressure and censorship.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on tourism and the gaze (strong scholarship); rich on online fame; thin on everyday norms of eye contact and greeting.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Hmub and Kam for village voices; Tibetan for Litang; Chongqing speech for porters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance research is restricted; Tibetan visibility is politically managed; left-behind children's deaths in Bijie (2015) are a painful and censored topic; images of minority women raise consent questions.
- **Held for review:** none.
