# Bashu: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese, Chongqing and Wuhan speech for teasing and insult; Nuosu Yi and Yunnan Yi varieties for customary responses to shame. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu, Chongqing and Wuhan city people:** teasing styles and face talk may differ by dialect and class.
- **Guizhou Han:** a province long mocked in idioms; test how people answer it.
- **Nuosu Yi of Liangshan:** customary responses to insult and shame, separate from Han face rules.
- **Yunnan Yi and other upland peoples:** campaign-era humiliation remembered in ritual.
- **Children in rural boarding schools and left-behind children:** bullying without parents nearby.
- **Cultural Revolution generation in Chongqing and Wuhan:** memory of public struggle sessions.
- **Women shamed online and in street confrontations:** sexual reputation as a target.
- **Debtors and the poor:** humiliation through exposure and collection.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school bullying, online shaming and national-humiliation narratives; mixed for minority customs; thin and restricted for Cultural Revolution violence.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese and Wuhan speech for teasing and insults; Nuosu for customary practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cultural Revolution sites and records are restricted; 死给 involves suicide and should be handled with care; national-humiliation material is state-shaped, so read it as narrative, not as data.
- **Held for review:** none.
