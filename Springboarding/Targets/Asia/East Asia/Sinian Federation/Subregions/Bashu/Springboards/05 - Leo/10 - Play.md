# Bashu: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese, Chongqing and Wuhan speech for games and wordplay; Dai, Wa, Lisu, Hani and Tibetan for festival play. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu and Chongqing:** mahjong and card play as city habit; test generations and districts.
- **Wuhan and the Jianghan plain:** river play, a different card and opera culture.
- **Rural children in the Sichuan basin, Guizhou and the Wuling mountains:** self-made games against screen play.
- **Dai of Xishuangbanna and Wa of Cangyuan:** festivals that license play.
- **Lisu of Nujiang and Tengchong; Hani of Honghe:** feats and swings as festival play.
- **Khams Tibetans:** circle dance and horse games.
- **Nuosu Yi:** wrestling and games outside the Torch Festival.
- **Students under exam pressure:** how little time is left to play.
- **Older people in parks:** chess, cards and dance in public space.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mahjong, games and festivals; good folklore anthologies; thin for adult playfulness and joking in daily life.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Sichuanese, Chongqing and Wuhan speech; Dai, Wa, Lisu and Hani for festival play.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mahjong for money crosses into illegal gambling; minors' gaming is tightly regulated; staged festival play may differ from village play.
- **Held for review:** none.
