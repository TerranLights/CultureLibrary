# Bashu: Domain and folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; its lands are carried from Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan and Guizhou. **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin (Chengdu-Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming, Guiyang varieties); Jianghuai Mandarin and Gan in eastern and southeastern Hubei; Sichuan Hakka; Nuosu Yi and other Yi languages; Kham and Amdo Tibetan; Hmong/Miao varieties; Dong, Bouyei, Bai, Naxi, Dai, Hani, Tujia, Qiang. **Concept in one line:** the domain of work and service (work, service, daily routine, health): what is good work and a well-kept life?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Chengdu-plain Shu Han against Chongqing and east-Sichuan Ba Han: irrigated plain against river port and hill farming.
- Wuhan and the Jianghan plain; the Jianghuai- and Gan-speaking counties of east and southeast Hubei.
- Central Yunnan Han (Kunming), the Tengchong frontier towns, Guizhou Han and the Tunpu garrison descendants of Anshun; the Hakka of Luodai.
- Nuosu Yi of Liangshan and the Yunnan and Guizhou Yi branches; Kham and Amdo Tibetans, Gyalrong and Qiang in western Sichuan.
- Tujia of Enshi and southeast Chongqing; Miao of Qiandongnan, the A-Hmao of Weining, Western Hmong of Wenshan; Dong, Bouyei, Shui, Gelao.
- Bai, Naxi and Mosuo of northwest Yunnan; Dai, Hani, Wa, Lahu, Jingpo, Lisu and Derung of the borderlands; Yunnan Hui.
- Three Gorges resettlers and the migrant-labor households whose members work away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Chinese for Han towns and for official surveys; mixed for minority peoples, where the best material is ethnography and heritage collections; thin for oral work lore of smaller groups.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese first; the regional Mandarin varieties for spoken terms; Nuosu Yi, Tibetan, Hmong and Dai for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Liangshan's pre-1956 rank and bonded-labor history; Tibetan areas with limited access; ritual specialists' texts (bimo, Dongba, Shui script) that are not public knowledge; defense sites of the Third Front. Labor law goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); the clock and public order to Capricorn's time and order ways. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled (the five provinces are a working list the author still confirms); the federation as a whole appears only for comparison.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read 《荆楚岁时记》 and 《华阳国志》 for the work calendar and livelihoods, noting places.

4. Read Skinner, Crook and Gilmartin, and Fei and Zhang side by side for the rural week.
5. Search eHRAF for Yi and Miao files under OCM 46 (labor) and 51 (routines).
