# Bashu: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Sichuanese and Wuhan varieties; Kham Tibetan, Nuosu Yi, Hmu Miao, Kam, Tai Lue (Dai). **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu and Chongqing urban Han:** different recent histories of policing campaigns and courts.
- **Hubei, especially Wuhan and the Jianghan Plain:** the oldest excavated legal record in these lands sits here.
- **Nuosu Yi of Liangshan against the Yi of central Yunnan:** clan compensation against a longer history of state courts.
- **Kham Tibetans (Garzê) against Amdo Tibetans (Aba):** monastic and lay arbiters; pastoral against farming disputes.
- **Hmu Miao and Kam of southeast Guizhou:** chanted case reasoning and village compacts.
- **Dai of Xishuangbanna and Dehong:** Theravada codes of the old princedoms.
- **Hui of Yunnan:** mosque-centered dispute settling; the memory of the Panthay (Du Wenxiu) rising and its suppression.
- **Qiang and Gyalrong of the upper Min valley:** small mountain communities between Han and Tibetan systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han legal history (Hubei manuscripts, Qing archives); mixed for living customary law; thin for recent politically charged cases.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for the manuscripts; Tibetan and Nuosu Yi for oral law; Dai script for palm-leaf texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wenchuan accountability, the Chongqing campaign, the Panthay memory, and Tibetan areas are politically sensitive; Tibetan and Yi compensation practices that conflict with criminal law are a live dispute, to be described neutrally.
- **Held for review:** none.
