# Bashu: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Wuhan and Sichuanese varieties; Qiang, Dai (Tai Lue), Hmu Miao, Nuosu Yi, Kham Tibetan. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others what one disowns in oneself, and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Han settlers and Miao of Guizhou:** the classic two-way case of poison accusations.
- **Qiang villages of the upper Min valley:** blame inside small communities.
- **Dai of Xishuangbanna:** accusation and expulsion as an explanation of illness.
- **Nuosu Yi of Liangshan:** misfortune traced to enemies and turned back by ritual.
- **Kham Tibetans:** illness blamed on harmful spirits rather than people *check*.
- **Wuhan and Hubei residents:** a province cast as the source of a crisis.
- **Chengdu against Chongqing:** neighboring cities' jokes about each other.
- **Urban clients of psychotherapy:** where the clinical idea of projection is now in use.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical sources and Qing images of minorities; mixed for accusation practices; thin for clinical data from the region.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (modern and classical); Dai, Qiang and Nuosu Yi through specialists; Tibetan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations harm real people, often women, so names and villages must be protected; the Cultural Revolution, the 2020 epidemic and ethnic images are politically sensitive; a minority people's healing and cursing knowledge belongs to them.
- **Held for review:** none.
