*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin; Tibetan; Qiang; Nuosu Yi; Tai (Lü); Lisu and A-Hmao (Western Hmong). **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and belief that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist laity around Emei and Baoguang Temple against Daoist circles of Qingcheng: two vocabularies of becoming new.
- Catholics of Sichuan, among China's oldest communities, against Protestant converts of the reform era: slow sacramental life against the sudden "came to believe".
- A-Hmao Christians of Weining and Lisu Christians of Nujiang: conversion as a people's history.
- Tibetans of Garzê and Ngawa: rebirth as doctrine, and the politics of recognizing reincarnate teachers.
- Dai of Xishuangbanna and Dehong: temple novitiate as remaking.
- Qiang and Tibetan towns rebuilt after 2008 against Han towns rebuilt after the same quake.
- Three Gorges resettlers in Chongqing and Hubei, some moved far downriver.
- Nuosu Yi youth who left for cities and came back, with or without addiction.
- Laid-off Third Front and state-factory workers in Panzhihua, Shiyan, Mianyang and Chengdu.
- Returning migrant workers starting businesses at home (返乡创业).
