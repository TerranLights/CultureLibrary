## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nuosu Yi clans of Liangshan: feud, mediation and payment, before and after the 1950s.
- Yunnan Yi groups outside Liangshan, whose clan structures differ.
- Kham Tibetans of Garzê: feud and "man-price" among herders.
- Jingpo, Lisu and Wa of the western border: feud traditions and their suppression.
- Miao of eastern Guizhou: memories of the 19th-c. wars and reprisals.
- Hui of Yunnan: massacre memory from the Panthay war and from 1975.
- Han lineages of northeastern Hubei (Macheng and its neighbors): militia and lineage violence.
- Chongqing and Chengdu city youth: "face" and getting even in street and online life.
- Tujia and Han of the Wuling hills: lineage quarrels and ghost revenge in story.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Liangshan feud ethnography and Qing rebellions; mixed for Han lineage feud; thin for quiet and private revenge.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese and classical Chinese; Nuosu Yi; Tibetan; Jingpo.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hui massacres; Tibetan unrest; Wa headhunting history; self-death in Liangshan; the feud framing has been used to stigmatize Yi, so read for variation.
- **Held for review:** none.
