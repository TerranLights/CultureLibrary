# Bashu: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin; Nuosu Yi; Tai Lü; Tibetan (Gyalrong and Khams); Hmub and Kam. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how a culture judges who holds it and how it is used.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nuosu Yi of Liangshan: a ranked clan order until the 1950s; power by birth.
- Yunnan and Guizhou Yi heirs of the Nasu chieftains: a different, longer state relationship.
- Dai of Xishuangbanna and Dehong: Tai principalities and their Buddhist courts.
- Gyalrong and Kham Tibetans: chieftains and monasteries as two powers.
- Tujia of western Hubei and southeastern Chongqing: tusi lineages and their descendants.
- Miao and Dong villages: councils of elders and sworn assemblies (议榔, 款).
- Han gentry, warlord and landlord families of the Chengdu plain.
- Chongqing against Chengdu: rival centers since Chongqing became a separate municipality in 1997.
- Wuhan as a power hub of central China, against rural Hubei.
- Hui communities led by mosques and clerics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for chieftain history, warlords and rural governance; mixed for minority councils; thin and guarded for present elite power.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese and classical Chinese; Nuosu Yi; Tai Lü; Tibetan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current leaders and their networks; the Nuosu ranked order (the term "slave society" is contested and charged); Tibetan monastery power.
- **Held for review:** none.
