## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu plain (Shu) against Chongqing and eastern Sichuan (Ba):** the two halves of the state's name.
- **Hubei's Jianghan plain and Wuhan:** Chu heritage and river trade; Jianghuai and Gan speech in the east.
- **Western Hubei and southeastern Chongqing (Enshi, Xiangxi borders):** Tujia and Miao ritual life.
- **Yunnan and Guizhou Han, including Guizhou's Tunpu 屯堡** (Ming garrison descendants with their own *dixi* masked rites).
- **Liangshan Nuosu against Yunnan Yi groups:** different priesthoods and scripts.
- **Tibetans of Garzê, Ngawa and Dêqên:** Kham and Amdo, monastic and lay, and Bön.
- **Qiang; Naxi and Mosuo; Bai** (village tutelary *benzhu* cults); **Dai** (Theravada); **Hani; Lisu, Lahu and Wa; Miao, Dong, Buyei, Shui, Gelao** in Guizhou; **Hui** across Yunnan and Hubei.
- **Third Front (三线) migrants:** Panzhihua, Liupanshui, Shiyan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for sites, missions and ethnography; thin and filtered for believers' shares by province.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin for oral sources; Tibetan, Nuosu Yi, Naxi and Hmong-Mien languages for the ritual corpora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan monasteries and institutes (Larung Gar, Kirti), Hui mosques under "sinicization," and unregistered Protestant churches (Chengdu's Early Rain Covenant Church) are sensitive and fieldwork-restricted; some Dongba, bimox and shibi rites are specialist knowledge, used only as the communities share it. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled by the author.
- **Held for review:** none.
