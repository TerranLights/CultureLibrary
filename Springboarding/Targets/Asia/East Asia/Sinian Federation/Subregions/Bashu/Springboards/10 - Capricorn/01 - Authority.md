# Bashu: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Sichuan-Chongqing and Hubei speech; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Jingpo, Kam and Hmu terms mostly through Chinese renderings. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu gentry families vs. Chongqing's river-port households:** a literary patriarch vs. a migrant, trading city.
- **Wuhan and the Jianghan plain:** a treaty-port and industrial city where factory and party authority may outweigh lineage.
- **Tujia lands (Enshi, Youyang, Xiushan):** former chieftaincies whose memory of the chief may still shape views of office.
- **Liangshan Nuosu:** authority through clan and stratum rather than village office.
- **Khampa and Amdowa Tibetans; Gyalrong:** chief, lama and state as rival sources of command.
- **Dong and Hmu villages of southeast Guizhou:** elders and compacts.
- **Jingpo and Wa of the Yunnan border:** chiefly lines and their critics.
- **Mosuo of Lugu Lake:** household authority outside the father-son line.
- **Hui of Yunnan:** clergy and community heads.
- **Urban young adults across the five provinces:** where workplace and online authority may differ most from parents'.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han Sichuan in literature and history; mixed for minority chiefly systems, much of it filtered through 1950s reform-era framing.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (modern and classical); Tibetan for Kham sources; Nuosu for Yi texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** monastic and lay authority in Tibetan areas; the state's own authority is a guarded topic for survey questions; some Dong and Miao recitations belong to designated reciters.
- **Held for review:** none.
