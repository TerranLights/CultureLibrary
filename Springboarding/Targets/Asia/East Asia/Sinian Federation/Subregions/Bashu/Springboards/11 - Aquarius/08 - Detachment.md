# Bashu: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; classical Chinese (Han-era thought, Chan records, Daoist texts); Sichuanese; Tibetan (monastic usage); Tujia and Nuosu Yi for funeral customs; present-day internet slang. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance, emotional, personal or social, from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, impartiality, equanimity), others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu and Chongqing urban youth:** lying-flat talk against long work hours.
- **Hubei's Chan and Daoist heartlands (Huangmei, Wudang):** religious detachment as a local inheritance.
- **Sichuan Daoist and Buddhist mountain communities (Qingcheng, Emei):** calm as practice, and as tourism.
- **Kham Tibetan monastics and lay people:** retreat discipline set against lay life.
- **Tujia of western Hubei and southeastern Chongqing:** a funeral style that tests ideas about restraint.
- **Nuosu Yi:** mourning rules and composure in clan settings.
- **Older adults who winter in Panzhihua and Xishuangbanna:** late-life distance from home.
- **Professionals in Chengdu and Wuhan hospitals:** detachment as a job skill.
- **Dai Theravada villages of Xishuangbanna:** temple novices and lay calm as another religious frame.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical religion and the online lying-flat debate; mixed for funeral display rules; thin for Tibetan retreat practice from inside.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Mandarin; Tibetan; Tujia and Nuosu sources (mostly through Chinese).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan monastic sites (Larung Gar among them); lying flat is officially criticized; bystander cases need care.
- **Held for review:** canonical Buddhist scriptures of South Asian origin, as a category, set aside under the standing rule.
