# Bashu: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), one of the Sinian Federation's member states, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Qing documentary and sectarian Chinese; Sichuanese; A-Hmao (Pollard script) and Lisu (Fraser script) Christian writing; Tibetan; Nuosu Yi; Tai Lü. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hill settlers of the Sichuan-Hubei-Shaanxi border (Daba Mountains):** where the 1796 rising began; test whether millenarian talk persists there.
- **A-Hmao (Big Flowery Miao) of northwest Guizhou and northeast Yunnan:** Christian hope tied to schooling.
- **Lisu, Nu and Derung of the Nujiang:** church-centered hope in remote valleys.
- **Rural girls and their families in Lijiang and Liangshan:** education as a route out, as families and teachers describe it.
- **Qiang and Han survivors in Beichuan and Wenchuan:** hope after disaster.
- **Science-fiction fans in Chengdu:** the future as a shared hobby.
- **Tujia and Han villagers of Wuling (Dejiang, Youyang):** vow rituals.
- **Nuosu Yi and Kham Tibetans:** festival wishes and luck customs.
- **Returning migrants and village influencers:** hope placed in the countryside.
- **Wuhan residents after 2020:** how hope was voiced after the lockdown, by district and age.
