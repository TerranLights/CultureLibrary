# Bashu: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; working lands Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Southwestern Mandarin (Sichuan-Chongqing, Wuhan, Kunming, Guiyang); eastern Hubei Jianghuai speech; Sichuan Hakka; Nuosu (Yi), Tibetan, Qiang, Naxi, Bai, Dai, Hmong/Hmu, Kam, Bouyei, Sui, Tujia. **Concept in one line:** how the culture meets suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape; with the magic ways folkway (healing, luck, divination, harm and hidden forces).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu Plain Han** against **Chongqing and the Three Gorges**: reservoir displacement may reshape the unseen.
- **Jianghan and eastern Hubei**: the Chu soul-calling inheritance.
- **Nuosu Yi of Liangshan** against **Chuxiong-Honghe Yi**: priestly writing against mostly oral ritual.
- **Tibetans of Garzê, Ngawa, Diqing** and **Gyalrong**: monastic Buddhism and Bon beside village rites.
- **Qiang** (Wenchuan, Mao County): shibi priests, rebuilt after 2008.
- **Miao/Hmong, Kam, Bouyei, Sui** in Guizhou: separate soul and ancestor systems.
- **Tujia** of Enshi and southeastern Chongqing: tima rites.
- **Naxi, Mosuo, Bai, Dai, Hani, Lisu, Wa** in Yunnan: dongba, benzhu cults, Theravada temples, Christian villages.
- **Tunpu Han** of Anshun and **Sichuan Hakka**: carried-over ritual of migrant and garrison descendants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han ritual, Nuo, Naxi and Yi texts; thin for smaller Yunnan peoples; surveys rarely split below province.
- **Lands:** the member state's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the five listed provinces.
- **Languages needed:** classical and modern Chinese, dialect forms, Tibetan, Nuosu, Naxi, Hmong; much minority material is in Chinese translation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** campaigns against "feudal superstition" color older sources; Tibetan religious matters are politically sensitive; gu-poison accusations against Miao women are stigmatizing, studied as accusation only; some priestly texts are restricted by their keepers.
- **Held for review:** none.
