*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese, Sichuan-Chongqing and Wuhan speech; Tibetan; Hmong; Tujia (mostly through Chinese). **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu and Chongqing youth:** counseling vocabulary taken up online; may differ from parents' older idioms.
- **Wuhan:** a separate urban speech and literature of ordinary lives; a city that went through a long lockdown.
- **Tujia women of Enshi and Youyang:** lament singing as shared feeling.
- **Nuosu of Liangshan:** clan obligations shape whose grief one shares.
- **Qiang and Tibetan quake survivors:** received outside sympathy and aid workers in 2008 and 2013.
- **Hmong of Zhaotong and Bijie:** liver-based idioms of feeling.
- **Rural elders with migrant children:** empathy at a distance, by phone and video.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Psychology studies on students are plentiful; studies of minority peoples' own idioms are few and mostly in Chinese.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Sichuanese and Wuhan speech, Tibetan, Hmong; Tujia and Nuosu material through local transcriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wuhan's lockdown memory; post-quake politics; minors in the bullying and left-behind studies.
- **Held for review:** none.
