# Bashu: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bashu (巴蜀), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Yunnan, Guizhou). **Language(s) for searching:** classical and modern Chinese; Sichuanese; Tibetan; Hui Chinese Islamic vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chengdu Han:** an idiom of happy defeat as part of local self-mockery; test whether it is pride or irony.
- **Kham and Amdo Tibetans:** prostration and refuge as daily surrender.
- **Hui of Yunnan:** surrender to God's decree in Islamic terms.
- **Elderly lay Buddhists, mostly women:** chanting toward a good death.
- **Families of the 1949 "uprising" officers:** surrender under another name.
- **Beichuan and Wenchuan survivors:** acceptance asked of them, and grief kept apart.
- **Gyalrong communities:** memory of the chieftains' end.
- **Patients and families in hospice pilots:** stopping treatment against the duty to try everything.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for classical idioms and official memory; thin for how ordinary people decide to let go; hospice data new.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese, modern Chinese, Sichuanese, Tibetan, Arabic-derived Hui vocabulary, French for the MEP archive.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1949 handovers and later purges; post-quake grievances; Tibetan pilgrimage under travel limits; "fatalism" must not be written as a people's trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
