# Gan: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Gan, Hakka, Hui and Wu varieties of the province; She speech. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nanchang apartment households against Gan River villages:** storage space, new goods and resale differ sharply between them.
- **Gannan Hakka households:** reburial and bone jars set their own rules for the possessions of the dead.
- **Jingdezhen potters and dealers:** objects there move between keepsake, stock and investment.
- **Wuyuan and the Huizhou-linked northeast:** heritage houses as possessions that outsiders want.
- **Resettled households (reservoir and lakeshore):** a test case of what people carry when they cannot keep everything.
- **She communities:** embroidered dress and silver may be held and handed on differently *check*.
- **Migrant-worker families:** possessions split between a coastal dormitory and a house at home.
- **Buddhist and Daoist clergy (Baizhang, Longhushan 龙虎山):** having little as a rule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for collecting, porcelain and burials; thin for everyday belongings, which need interviews or home inventories.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese, classical Chinese for monastic and Song sources, Gan and Hakka for household vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2018 coffin seizures; reburial and grave goods (family consent); the sale of old houses and carvings (possible illegal trade).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for "江西 嫁妆" and "赣南 客家 嫁妆" to find dowry studies with item lists.
2. Search the open web for "江西 殡葬改革 寿材" and save the reports, noting dates and counties.
3. Find the *Baizhang qinggui* section on a deceased monk's goods (亡僧, 唱衣) and read it in the original.
4. Search CNKI for "人情簿" or "礼单" with "江西" to find studies of gift ledgers.
5. Search "婺源 古宅 木雕 倒卖" for reports on the sale of house carvings.
