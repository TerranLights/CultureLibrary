# Gan: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Putonghua; Gan (Nanchang and county forms); Hakka of Gannan and the northwest; Huizhou, Wu and Mandarin varieties in border areas; She speech; classical Chinese for older letters. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the ideas held about what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nanchang city speakers vs. rural Chang-Du villages: urban Putonghua use may reshape tone and directness differently *check*.
- Ji'an/Luling and Linchuan, with their old letter-writing elites, vs. counties without such a lettered past.
- Gannan Hakka villages, where sung exchange and market talk may carry news differently.
- Wuyuan and Fuliang (Huizhou speech), whose merchant letters left a separate written record.
- Guangfeng-Yushan Wu speakers, cut off in speech from Nanchang.
- Jiujiang river-port talk, with its Mandarin, vs. its Gan hinterland.
- She villagers, likely bilingual: how they move between speech worlds.
- Elders who speak only dialect vs. grandchildren schooled in Putonghua: talk across a generation gap.
- Deaf Jiangxi people, whose signing may differ from the national standard *check*. Jiangxi speakers on the coast, as comparison only: how far home speech travels with them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical letters and national politeness research; mixed for Gan pragmatics; thin for everyday recorded conversation, She speech and Deaf communities.
- **Languages needed:** Putonghua, classical Chinese, Gan and Hakka (listening, for recordings).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dialect broadcasting is shaped by language policy; studies of left-behind children touch family distress; online platforms are moderated.
- **Held for review:** none.
