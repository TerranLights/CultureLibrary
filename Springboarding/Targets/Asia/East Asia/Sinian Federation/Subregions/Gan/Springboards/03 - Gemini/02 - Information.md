# Gan: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Putonghua (simplified; traditional for pre-1950 print); classical Chinese for notices and printed books; Gan and Hakka for spoken news and lore; Huizhou and Wu varieties in the east. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how it is gathered, passed on, trusted, withheld and used: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nanchang and Jiujiang, with dense papers and broadband, vs. mountain counties of Gannan and the Luoxiao range, where loudspeakers may still carry notices *check*.
- Poyang Lake fishing and farming communities, whose knowledge of water may run parallel to official gauges.
- Gannan Hakka market towns, where market days may still serve as news exchanges.
- Jinxi and Linchuan, with an old book trade, vs. counties without one.
- Jingdezhen ceramic workshops: trade news, prices and craft know-how in a single-industry town.
- Wuyuan and other tourist counties: information made for outsiders vs. information among residents.
- She villages: which languages their news arrives in.
- Older people reliant on broadcast and word of mouth vs. young people on short-video apps.
- Migrant workers and their home villages: news that flows in both directions; the migrants' own news sources away from home count as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the official press and printing history; mixed for rumor and village chat studies; thin for oral news, She communities and fishers' lore.
- **Languages needed:** Putonghua, classical Chinese, Gan and Hakka for oral material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** media and online speech are tightly managed; archives restrict access; studies of rumor touch political control. Privacy and censorship rules as law belong in Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
