# Gan: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Putonghua; classical Chinese for lineage and guild records; Gan and Hakka for kin and favor talk; Huizhou speech in Wuyuan; She speech. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lineage-dense Gan villages of Ji'an and Fuzhou vs. Nanchang's housing estates, where ties may run through work and school instead.
- Gannan Hakka, with links to a worldwide Hakka network.
- Jingdezhen's craft workers, many descended from migrants of Duchang and Huizhou: ties by place of origin inside one town.
- Jiujiang and De'an, home ground of the Yimen Chen memory.
- Poyang counties as the remembered starting point of out-migration.
- Wuyuan's Huizhou-speaking merchant lineages, oriented toward Anhui.
- She families: ties with Han neighbors through marriage and market.
- Migrant workers' households split between Jiangxi and the coast; elders left behind. Hometown associations of Jiangxi people in Guangdong or Zhejiang, as comparison only: ties seen from the far end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage and guild history and for national guanxi studies; mixed for migration networks; thin for She ties and for loneliness measured at the county level.
- **Languages needed:** Putonghua, classical Chinese (genealogies, guild steles), Gan and Hakka.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** connections shade into corruption, a politically charged topic; lineage revival has at times been discouraged; family ledgers are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
