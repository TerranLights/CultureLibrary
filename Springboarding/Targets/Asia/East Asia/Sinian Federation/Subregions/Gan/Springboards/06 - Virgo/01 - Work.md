# Gan: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Gan; Hakka; Huizhou (Wuyuan); Wu (Shangrao); Classical Chinese. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jingdezhen porcelain workers:** kiln hands, throwers and painters with guild histories; compare workshop hierarchy with farm work.
- **Pingxiang and Anyuan miners; Dayu tungsten miners:** heavy industry in two different speech areas.
- **Poyang Lake fishing households:** a trade now ending under the fishing ban.
- **Gannan Hakka villages:** hill rice, tea and later navel-orange 赣南脐橙 orchards; check, never assume, claims about Hakka women's field work.
- **Nanchang's factory and office workers:** aviation and automobile plants (*check*) against the service economy.
- **Ji'an, Jinggangshan and Ruijin:** red-tourism guides and staff, whose work is heritage itself.
- **Wuyuan's tea growers and Huizhou merchant descendants:** a different commercial tradition.
- **Fuzhou/Linchuan:** a center of scholar families; how book learning ranked against manual work.
- **She communities:** hill farming and their own crafts, little recorded.
- **Out-migrants in Guangdong, Zhejiang and Fujian:** comparison only, since the findings for Gan apply to them too.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history and statistics; mixed for dialect and folk sources.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese, Classical Chinese; Gan and Hakka for oral sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** revolutionary-era labor history is officially curated (read Anyuan memory critically, as Perry does); camps and cadre-school labor touch restricted archives; mine safety records may be thin.
- **Held for review:** none.
