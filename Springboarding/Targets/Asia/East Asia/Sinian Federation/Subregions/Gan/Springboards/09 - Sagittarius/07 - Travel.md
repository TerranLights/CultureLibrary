# Gan: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣, a member state of the Sinian Federation (lands: Jiangxi 江西), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese; Gan 赣语; Hakka 客家话; Huizhou speech (Wuyuan); Wu speech (Shangrao, Yushan); Mandarin of Jiujiang. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gan valley and Ji'an:** origin counties of the merchants and the westward migrants.
- **Poyang Lake and Jiujiang:** waterborne travel and treaty-port contact with foreigners.
- **Gannan Hakka:** a people whose name and histories are built on migration.
- **Northwest hill Hakka (Tonggu, Xiushui):** later in-migrants who came in from the south.
- **Wuyuan:** Huizhou merchant tradition of leaving young and returning rich; now a tourist magnet.
- **Shangrao and Sanqingshan:** pilgrims and hikers on a Daoist mountain.
- **Labor-sending counties (Yudu, Xingguo, Poyang, Duchang):** the yearly round of leaving and return.
- **Nanchang and Ganzhou:** destination cities for rural migrants inside the province.
- **She communities:** their own migration narratives into Jiangxi.
- **Comparison only:** Jiangxi workers in Guangdong and Zhejiang, and the old Jiangxi merchant settlements in the southwest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historic routes, merchants and migration; rich for official tourism data; mixed for migrants' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Gan, Hakka and Huizhou speech for oral histories; English for customs and mission records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** migrant registration and left-behind families need care; Hakka origin claims are debated among scholars, so mark them as accounts, not facts.
- **Held for review:** none.
