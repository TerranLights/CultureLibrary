# Gan: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and written Chinese (including classical); Gan 赣语 (Nanchang and Ji'an speech especially); Hakka 客家话 of Gannan and the northwest; Huizhou 徽语 (Wuyuan); She 畲 speech. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and what members are expected to do together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native Gan lineages and Hakka settlers** in the northwest and south: the historic native-guest line; check whether it still marks groups.
- **Pingxiang miners:** a workforce that became a political group; compare with peasant groups nearby.
- **Jingdezhen trade blocs:** groups by origin within one town, including Huizhou-origin workers.
- **Jinggangshan and the Luoxiao highlands:** bands, lineages and parties overlapping.
- **Wuyuan:** a county whose bloc identity faced toward Huizhou.
- **Religious groups:** Longhushan Daoists, Buddhist monastic communities, Christian congregations; each draws its own boundary.
- **She communities:** a group named by the state, set against how members name themselves.
- **Jiangxi migrant workers in other provinces:** hometown blocs formed abroad; comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revolutionary, guild and lineage groups; mixed for secret societies; thin for present-day voluntary groups.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and classical Chinese; Gan and Hakka for oral histories and slang.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** secret societies, feuds and religious groups touch state security; revolutionary group histories are officially curated; group collective-punishment cases may be closed in archives.
- **Held for review:** none.
