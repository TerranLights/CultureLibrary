# Gan: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Gan 赣 (Jiangxi 江西), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and written Chinese (classical for legends and temple texts); Gan 赣语 (Nanchang, Fuzhou, Ji'an speech for blessings and New Year formulas); Hakka 客家话 (Gannan lantern and son-birth customs); Huizhou 徽语 and Wu 吴语 (northeast border counties). **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan Hakka:** son-lantern customs, siting traditions and Soviet-era memory; hope tied to descendants and to official revival plans.
- **Poyang Lake and Gan River lowlands:** flood-prone places where hope turns on water.
- **Nanchang and Jiujiang cities:** jobs, housing and children's schooling as the main hopes; compare with villages.
- **Lushan and Jiujiang:** Peach Blossom and Pure Land traditions as local heritage.
- **Jingdezhen:** a declining-then-reviving craft city; hope in the trade's future.
- **Migrant households** in all subregions: hope sent home as money and built as houses; workers abroad are comparison only.
- **She communities:** blessings and festivals of their own.
- **Young people:** online despair ("lying flat") set against official dreams.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious, literary and revolutionary hope; mixed for present-day personal expectations; thin for She and Wu- and Huizhou-speaking communities.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Mandarin; Gan and Hakka for blessings, festival formulas and interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official futures are policy language; despair and rural suicide are sensitive topics; siting and temple divination may be labeled "superstition" in official sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
