# Hakka: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (Meixian, Huiyang, Tingzhou and Gannan varieties), Standard Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong), English. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men:** field labor gave Hakka women public roles; separate women's song and speech from men's hall speech.
- **Hong Kong New Territories villagers:** a minority beside Punti lineages, with formal bodies for voicing claims.
- **Pearl River delta Hakka** (Chixi, Taishan border areas): claims shaped by the feuds.
- **Meixian, Xingning and Wuhua:** neighboring counties with different reputations; test local rivalry talk.
- **Western Fujian tulou lineages:** claims made as a lineage, not as a person.
- **Southern Jiangxi "new" settlers** against older residents: latecomers pressing for room.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** a minority among other Chinese speakers; whether speech is kept quiet or kept loud.
- **She communities** near Hakka villages: assertion of a separate people within mixed speech.
- **Cantonese, Teochew and Hokkien neighbors:** each has its own words for a pushy outsider.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Hakka language-rights movement; overseas Hakka associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for collective and historical claims, thin for personal everyday assertion.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, Cantonese, classical Chinese for gazetteers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "hard neck" talk is a stock trope; treat it as a claim to study. Feud memories still sting between Hakka and Cantonese families.
- **Held for review:** none.
