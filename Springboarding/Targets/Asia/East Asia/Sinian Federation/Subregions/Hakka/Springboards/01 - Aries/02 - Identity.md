# Hakka: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, Cantonese, classical Chinese, English. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou Hakka:** home of the "Hakka capital" claim; check whether their self-image becomes the template for all.
- **Changting and Ninghua:** rival homeland claims (Tingzhou as "Hakka seat", Shibi as ancestral ground).
- **Gannan "old" and "new" Hakka:** two layers who may name themselves differently.
- **Semi-Hakka of Jiexi and Fengshun:** an identity split between two languages.
- **Sichuan Hakka (Luodai, Longquanyi):** identity kept through guild halls and home speech after resettlement.
- **Guangxi and Hunan Hakka:** local names for their speech; whether "Hakka" was learned from outside.
- **Hainan Hakka:** small scattered settlements; thin data expected.
- **Hong Kong New Territories:** Hakka identity layered with "indigenous villager" status.
- **She people:** a recognized ethnic group whose history overlaps Hakka origins.
- **Cantonese, Hokkien, Teochew and Gan neighbors:** the outside names and jokes that Hakka identity answers.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's state-backed Hakka identity; overseas Hakka associations and their congresses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the origin debate alone has a large literature in Chinese.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese, classical Chinese, Hakka varieties, Cantonese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** descent claims bear on Han status and on She identity; genetic studies are easily misused; leaders' ethnic origins are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
