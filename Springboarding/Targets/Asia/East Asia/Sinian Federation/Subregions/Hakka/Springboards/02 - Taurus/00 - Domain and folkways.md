# Hakka: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话; Meixian as the reference variety, plus Huiyang, Hailu, Tingzhou and Gannan varieties), Standard Chinese, Cantonese for Hong Kong material. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how is it held: possessions, money, resources, values, self-worth, with the food, dress and wealth ways around them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou core** (Meixian, Xingning, Wuhua, Dabu, Jiaoling, Pingyuan): emigrant-heavy, remittance-shaped households.
- **Dongjiang and Hailu Hakka** (Huiyang, Heyuan, Haifeng-Lufeng): closer to Cantonese commerce and coast.
- **Western Fujian** (Changting, Yongding, Shanghang, Liancheng, Ninghua with Shibi 石壁): tulou economy, paper and tobacco trades *check*.
- **Gannan** (Ningdu, Ruijin, Xingguo, Longnan): inland, rice and hill farming, fortified houses.
- **Outlying settlements:** Guangxi (Bobai, Luchuan, Hezhou), Sichuan (Dongshan around Luodai 洛帶), Hunan, Hainan, Hong Kong's New Territories: each from a different migration.
- **Hakka living as a minority** among Hokkien (Zhao'an 詔安) and Teochew (Raoping 饒平) speakers.
- **She 畲 communities** sharing hills and speech with Hakka.
- **Hakka Christians** (Basel Mission and Tsung Tsin churches) against temple-going households: tithes against temple gifts.
- Taiwan's Hakka and the overseas diaspora: comparison only, to see which household goods and dishes travel with migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Meizhou, the tulou and Hong Kong; mixed for Gannan; thin for the Hunan, Hainan and Guangxi settlements.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese for most scholarship; Hakka for songs, dictionaries and fieldwork (several varieties, not one); Cantonese for Hong Kong; English and German for Basel Mission records.
- **Scope:** no scope file has been written for this sub-unit, and the tracker's list of communities awaits the author's confirmation, so its exact extent is still to be settled; these sheets cover the heartland and the listed outlying communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform, the Punti–Hakka wars and Hong Kong village land claims are live topics; inheritance and property law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none for the domain sheet.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the customs (禮俗) and produce (物產) chapters of the Guangxu 《嘉應州志》, noting every named good, gift and market.
2. Search CNKI for 客家 物質文化 and 客家 墟市, then sort the results by Meizhou, Longyan and Ganzhou.
3. Pull the table of contents of each volume of 《客家傳統社會叢書》 and list which villages record markets and lineage estates.
4. Check the China Hakka Museum's and Sam Tung Uk's published exhibit lists side by side.
5. Find the Meizhou statistical yearbook's household-spending tables for the latest five years.
