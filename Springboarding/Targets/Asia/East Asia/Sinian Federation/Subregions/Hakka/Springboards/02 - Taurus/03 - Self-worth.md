# Hakka: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), a people-based sub-unit of the Sinian Federation spread over several member states; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话) with its regional varieties; Standard Chinese; Cantonese where Hong Kong villagers are concerned. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is taken to rest on.

## 2. Where to look first

| 徐旭曾 Xu Xuzeng, 《豐湖雜記》 (1808) | Short early-Qing text on who the Hakka are | One of the first written claims of Hakka standing and descent, made under pressure from neighbors; the pre-modern root of group worth | library; reprinted in Hakka source books | *check* |

| Sow-Theng Leong, *Migration and Ethnicity in Chinese History* (1997) | Historical study of Hakka and their neighbors | How conflict over land and markets hardened a sense of group worth and grievance | library | *check* |

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou** with its scholar and emigrant traditions, against **Gannan** hill counties with fewer degree-holders *check*.
- **Hakka living among Cantonese** (Huiyang, Hong Kong) or **among Teochew and Hokkien** (Raoping, Zhao'an), where the neighbor's view of them shaped how they speak of themselves.
- **New Territories Hakka villagers** with indigenous-inhabitant status, against Hong Kong's urban Hakka who no longer speak the language.
- **Sichuan's "Tu Guangdong" (土廣東) Hakka:** worth after three centuries as a minority speech.
- **She 畲 people:** the line drawn between She and Hakka, and who claimed higher standing.
- **Women, sons and daughters-in-law:** whose worth is counted in the genealogy and whose is not.
- **Returnees and stay-at-homes** in emigrant villages.
- Taiwan's Hakka rights movement and diaspora associations: comparison only, as other settings where group pride was organized.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on group identity and pride; thin on personal self-esteem measured in Hakka communities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Hakka for songs and interviews; classical Chinese for Qing texts; Cantonese for the New Territories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims about who is or is not Han; the place of Hakka in revolutionary history (Mary Erbaugh's 1992 *China Quarterly* article *check*) is politically charged; women's past roles (child brides, 等郎妹) need care. Avoid treating praise of "Hakka spirit" as a finding.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside on self-worth.
