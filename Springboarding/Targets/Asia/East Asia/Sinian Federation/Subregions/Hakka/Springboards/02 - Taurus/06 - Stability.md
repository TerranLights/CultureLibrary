# Hakka: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) as a people living across several Sinian Federation member states, with no lands of their own as a unit; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Hakka (客家話 / 客家话), heartland and settler varieties; Cantonese for Hong Kong villages. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Heartland lineages** that claim centuries in one valley, against **settler branches** in Sichuan, Guangxi, Hunan and Hainan who moved within the last three centuries.
- **Tulou and weilongwu families** against those now in town flats.
- **Meizhou emigrant villages** and **New Territories villages** emptied by emigration, against hill villages that stayed full until recent decades *check*.
- **Gannan relocated households** under poverty-relief schemes.
- **She 畲 communities:** a separate story of origin in the same hills.
- **Hakka minorities in Hokkien and Teochew counties,** where language shift tests continuity.
- **Elders and grandchildren** left in the village while parents work in the Pearl River Delta *check*.
- Taiwan's Hakka and overseas communities: comparison only, for how roots are kept far from the heartland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on migration history and origin debates; mixed on present-day mobility; thin on routine and daily life.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for genealogies; Hakka for songs and interviews; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin claims (northern descent against local formation) are debated within the community; relocation schemes and village clearances are politically delicate.
- **Held for review:** none for continuity and settlement.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Luo's 《客家源流考》 and Xie's 《客家形成發展史綱》 side by side; list where they disagree on settlement and continuity.
2. CNKI: 石壁 客家祖地 祭祖; note the start year and who attends.
3. CNKI: 承啟樓 居民 and 土樓 空心化 (emptying).
4. Search CMDS documentation for prefecture-of-origin variables covering Meizhou, Ganzhou and Longyan.
5. Search the Lai Chi Wo project's reports for oral histories of former villagers.
