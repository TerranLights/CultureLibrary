# Hakka: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people of the Sinian Federation, a sub-unit without a territory of its own; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话) first, since the key term is Hakka; Standard Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou,** where 硬頸 rhetoric is most public, against **Gannan and western Fujian,** where other labels may serve *check*.
- **Former Soviet-base counties** (Ruijin, Yudu, Xingguo), whose official memory is of holding out.
- **Sichuan Hakka (Luodai, Longquanyi)** and **Guangxi Hakka:** keeping speech and custom far from the heartland.
- **Zhao'an and Raoping Hakka:** small minorities among Min speakers; firmness or adjustment.
- **New Territories villagers:** 1899 memory and present land disputes.
- **She 畲 communities:** their own tales of refusal under dynastic rule *check*.
- **Elders who resist funeral reform** against younger relatives who accept it.
- Taiwan's Hakka movement, which took up 硬頸 as a banner: comparison only, for how the term spread.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the 硬頸 label and on revolutionary holdouts; mixed on everyday quarrels and apology; thin on measured obstinacy.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka is essential for the core term and folktales; Standard Chinese; Cantonese and English for 1899 sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 硬頸 is a stereotype risk: record who says it and why, never assume it; revolutionary history and funeral reform are politically charged; demolition disputes and land claims touch Libra.
- **Held for review:** none on firmness or holdouts.

## 5. First queries
1. Look up 硬頸 and 拗 in the 《客家話通用詞典》 and in one Meixian and one Gannan dictionary; note every sense.

3. Search 文天祥 客家 勤王 for legends and genealogical claims by county.
4. Search 贛州 殯葬改革 and 梅州 殯葬改革 for reports since 2010.
5. Find 崔榮昌's survey and list the Sichuan localities where Hakka is still spoken.
