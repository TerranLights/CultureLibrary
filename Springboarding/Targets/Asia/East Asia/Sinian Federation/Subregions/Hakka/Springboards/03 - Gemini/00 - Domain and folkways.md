**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话) in its Meixian, Tingzhou, Gannan, Huiyang and western varieties; written Chinese (traditional and simplified); Cantonese and Hokkien for the neighbors' view; English. **Concept in one line:** how do people talk, learn and connect close to home?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meixian / Meizhou heartland:** the variety most often used as the reference, and the densest scholarship.
- **Western Fujian (Changting, Yongding, Shanghang, Liancheng, Ninghua):** Tingzhou speech, tulou living, and the Shibi (石壁) "ancestral place" tradition give a separate local story.
- **Southern Jiangxi (Ganzhou, Xingguo, Ningdu):** Gannan Hakka sits closest to Gan speech; useful for where the line blurs.
- **Northern and eastern Guangdong (Heyuan, Shaoguan, Huizhou, Hailufeng, Jiexi):** border zones with Cantonese and Teochew speakers.
- **Guangxi (Bobai, Luchuan, Hezhou):** later migrants living among Zhuang and Cantonese.
- **Sichuan (Luodai, Longquanyi) and Hunan (Liuyang):** speech islands from Qing migration, under pressure from the local majority.
- **Hainan and Hong Kong's New Territories:** small communities; Hong Kong adds English and Cantonese schooling.
- **She (畲族) communities:** a people whose speech is close to Hakka; separate, not merged.
- Taiwan's Hakka and the overseas diaspora: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Meixian and western Fujian; mixed for Gannan; thin for Guangxi, Sichuan, Hunan and Hainan communities, where the dialect recordings may be the main route.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese (both scripts), Hakka varieties, Cantonese, some Hokkien; English and German for mission archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the community list in the tracker header is a working list still to be confirmed by the author, so the exact set of Hakka communities is unsettled. Origin claims (northern Han descent against local mixing with She and others) are contested; the Mandarin-promotion policy and dialect decline are politically touchy topics.
- **Held for review:** none.
