# Hakka: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, written Chinese (traditional and simplified), Cantonese for Hong Kong, English and German for mission records. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meixian against Xingning and Wuhua:** neighboring Meizhou accents, to test how far talk norms vary inside one prefecture.
- **Tingzhou (Changting, Liancheng) and Yongding:** western Fujian speech with Min neighbors close by.
- **Gannan (Xingguo, Ningdu, Yudu):** the sung-dialogue tradition and the line with Gan speech.
- **Shenzhen, Huizhou and Dongguan Hakka:** communities where Cantonese and Mandarin press hardest on home speech.
- **Hong Kong New Territories:** Christian and non-Christian Hakka villages; English and Cantonese schooling.
- **Guangxi (Bobai, Luchuan, Hezhou) and Hainan:** Hakka among Zhuang, Cantonese and Hainanese speakers.
- **Sichuan (Luodai) and Hunan (Liuyang):** Hakka speech islands; bilingual daily life.
- **Women and men; returned-migrant (歸僑) households:** who sang, wrote letters or translated for the family.
- **She (畲族) villages:** separate speech community near the Hakka heartland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mountain songs, mission records and qiaopi; mixed for everyday conversation norms; thin for Guangxi, Hainan and Hunan.
- **Languages needed:** spoken Hakka (Meixian and Tingzhou at least), Cantonese, mission German and English, Pha̍k-fa-sṳ reading.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** qiaopi and family letters hold private family matters; mission records reflect outsiders' agendas, so read critically; Mandarin promotion versus home language is politically touchy.
- **Held for review:** none.
