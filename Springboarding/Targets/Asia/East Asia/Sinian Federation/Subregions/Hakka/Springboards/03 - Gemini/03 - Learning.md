# Hakka: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese (classical and modern, both scripts), Hakka varieties (including their literary reading pronunciations), English for mission and diaspora studies. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution sits under education, Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meixian and Dabu lineages with long exam records:** the case most often cited; test it against poorer hill villages.
- **Western Fujian (Changting, Liancheng, Yongding):** tulou communities and nearby book-printing villages.
- **Southern Jiangxi:** Gannan villages, with a later revolution-era school history.
- **Shenzhen, Huizhou and Heyuan:** fast urban change; Hakka children schooled in Mandarin and Cantonese.
- **Hong Kong New Territories:** village schools, mission schools and English-medium learning.
- **Guangxi (Bobai, Hezhou), Sichuan (Luodai) and Hunan (Liuyang):** migrant communities with their own school histories.
- **Hainan Hakka:** little known; start with county records.
- **Girls and boys; Christian and non-Christian families:** separate tracks in schooling and crafts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exam history and mission schools; mixed for children's everyday learning; thin outside the heartland.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for gazetteers and genealogies; Hakka with its literary readings; English and German for mission records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims of Hakka scholarly distinction are a popular theme; treat them as a hypothesis to test, not a finding; school-language policy is sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.
