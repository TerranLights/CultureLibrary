# Hakka: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (children's speech, rhymes and sayings vary by dialect), modern written Chinese, classical Chinese for household maxims, Cantonese for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou emigrant and commuter families:** children raised by grandparents while parents work far away; feeding and schooling in their hands.
- **Rural southern Jiangxi:** high out-migration; Hakka and Gan villages side by side, useful to see what upbringing differs across the language line.
- **Western Fujian tulou villages:** child-minding inside a shared building, where many adults are near.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou urban Hakka:** city schooling and intensive tutoring; how much Hakka is still spoken to children.
- **New Territories Hakka:** village schools of the past, emigrant parents abroad, grandparents at home.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** where parents may no longer pass the language on.
- **Christian Hakka:** Sunday schools and mission schools as a second source of nurture.
- **She (畲族) families:** shared hill settings with different song and rite traditions for children.
- **Cantonese and Hokkien neighbors:** for contrast. Taiwan's Hakka immersion kindergartens: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; folk rhymes and genealogical rules are plentiful, measured parenting data is national, not Hakka.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka for rhymes and baby talk, whose wording is lost in standard-Chinese transcriptions; classical Chinese for maxims; Cantonese for Hong Kong oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** left-behind children and son-preference rites are touchy; data on minors needs care.
- **Held for review:** none for nurture.
