# Hakka: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (sickroom, remedy and kin-duty words), modern written Chinese for surveys and welfare studies, classical Chinese for gazetteer biographies, Cantonese for Hong Kong services. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou emigrant families:** care sent as remittances from abroad or from Shenzhen; old parents at home with hired help or none.
- **Gannan rural counties:** heavy out-migration; mutual-aid homes and 五保 care; Hakka next to Gan villages for contrast.
- **Western Fujian tulou villages:** elders cared for inside a shared building, now often half empty.
- **New Territories Hakka:** ancestral trusts, church services and the Hong Kong welfare system side by side.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou urban Hakka:** paid carers and care homes in a city where many residents are newcomers.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** care customs away from the heartland; whether secondary burial and filial songs survive there.
- **She (畲族):** healing and death-care customs in shared hill districts.
- **Cantonese, Teochew and Hokkien neighbors:** secondary burial and herb use are not Hakka alone, so contrast is needed. Taiwan's Hakka elder-care studies: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; national aging surveys are strong but do not code Hakka; ritual care of the dead is well described, daily care of the living less so.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka for remedy, nursing and kin-duty words and for interviewing elders; classical Chinese for exemplar biographies; Cantonese for Hong Kong services.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** neglect of elders and extreme filial acts are touchy; bone-gathering is handled by specialists and may not be shown to outsiders. Health-system law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none at this stage.
