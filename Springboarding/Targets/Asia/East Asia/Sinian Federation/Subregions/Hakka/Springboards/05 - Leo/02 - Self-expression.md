# Hakka: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家话), including Sichuan's 土广东话 and Guangxi's 涯话 / 新民话; Standard written Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men:** laments and field songs are largely women's channels; men's outlets need separate looking.
- **Meizhou heartland against Hakka in Cantonese-majority cities:** open speech in the home district, code-switching outside.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** long-settled islands where Hakka speech is kept indoors; compare when it is shown.
- **Southern Jiangxi:** song tied to revolution and to state framing.
- **New Territories villages:** lament and dress under colonial and later urban change.
- **Western Fujian tulou communities:** speech within one large shared building, where privacy is scarce.
- **She people and semi-Hakka border speakers:** two-way code choice.
- *Comparison only:* Taiwan's Hakka language movements; overseas Hakka associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; laments and songs are well recorded, everyday emotional display and personal style are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka (song texts and laments rely on dialect wording), written Chinese, Cantonese, English for Hong Kong ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** revolutionary-era and political material is officially framed; read with care. Laments are mourning speech; families may not want them recorded.
- **Held for review:** none.
