# Hakka: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家话) and its dialects; Standard written Chinese; Cantonese where Hakka live among Cantonese speakers. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sons and daughters:** the lantern rite and genealogies show one, not the other; compare how daughters are made visible.
- **Hakka in Cantonese and Hokkien cities:** the "invisible Hakka" question.
- **Emptied villages against county towns:** who stays in view after migration.
- **Tulou and weilongwu residents against people in modern flats:** communal view against private living.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** small islands seen mostly through tourism or not at all.
- **New Territories villagers:** visible as indigenous villagers, less so as Hakka.
- **She people:** often unseen inside Hakka districts.
- *Comparison only:* Taiwan's Hakka public broadcasting; overseas Hakka associations' visibility work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; birth rites and folk customs are documented, everyday attention, greeting and gaze norms are thin; no official counts.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese; Hakka for greeting and naming terms; Cantonese for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** son-preference practices touch on gender and birth policy; left-behind families are vulnerable subjects.
- **Held for review:** none.
