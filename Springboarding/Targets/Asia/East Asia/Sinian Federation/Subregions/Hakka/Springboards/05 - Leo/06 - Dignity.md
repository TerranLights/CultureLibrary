# Hakka: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家话) and its dialects; Standard written Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong care and labor sources. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women and men:** women's worth tied to labor and endurance; men's to learning and lineage standing.
- **Child brides and adopted daughters:** a separate status within the same households.
- **Factory migrants and stay-at-home villagers:** dignity at work against dignity at home.
- **Miners in Gannan and laborers elsewhere:** dangerous work, its standing and its risks.
- **The old in emptied villages:** care without children nearby.
- **New Territories villagers displaced by development:** dignity under relocation.
- **Guangxi and Sichuan Hakka:** conduct norms shaped by different neighbors.
- **She people:** treatment of a recognized minority by Hakka neighbors.
- *Comparison only:* Taiwan's Hakka; overseas Hakka laborers' histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; prescriptive texts (family rules, couplets, proverbs) are plentiful; experience of indignity, especially women's, is thin and scattered.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for genealogies; modern written Chinese; Hakka for proverbs and spoken measures; Cantonese for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child brides and labor injuries are painful subjects; genealogies are often held privately by lineages.
- **Held for review:** none.
