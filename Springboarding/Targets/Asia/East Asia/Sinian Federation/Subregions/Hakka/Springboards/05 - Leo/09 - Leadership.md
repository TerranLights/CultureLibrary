# Hakka: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家话) and its dialects; Standard written Chinese; Cantonese and English for New Territories institutions. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lineage seniors against degree-holders and wealthy returnees:** three different routes to standing in the same village.
- **Revolutionary districts (Gannan, western Fujian) against non-revolutionary Hakka districts:** different models of a leader.
- **Guangxi Hakka and the Taiping legacy:** charismatic and religious leadership.
- **New Territories villagers:** leaders inside a colonial and later special-region rural council.
- **Sichuan migrants:** guild leaders in place of lineage heads.
- **Women:** wives of absent migrants, village women's leaders, women in revolutionary memory.
- **She villages:** their own headmen among Hakka neighbors.
- *Comparison only:* Taiwan's Hakka political leaders; Hakka leaders abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on famous leaders and lineage offices; thin on everyday informal leadership and how followers choose.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese; Hakka for headman and decision vocabulary; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** revolutionary leaders are officially narrated; claims of Hakka origin for some figures are disputed. Formal authority and office belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Faure 1986 for how village leaders were chosen in the eastern New Territories.
2. Search CNKI for 客家 族长 选立 and 客家 宗族 管理 to find lineage-office studies.
3. Search for 新乡贤 梅州 and 新乡贤 赣州 for present-day village leadership.
4. Search for 番客婶 to locate studies of women managing households.
5. Visit or read the exhibition texts of the Gutian and Ruijin memorial museums.
