# Hakka: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家) people and communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家话) and its dialects; Standard written Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong festival play. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boys and girls:** dragon, lion and unicorn troupes are mostly male; girls' games and rhymes need separate collection.
- **Children of left-behind households against children living with parents in cities:** where and with whom they play.
- **Meizhou, western Fujian and Gannan:** each district's festival play differs (fire dragons, lanterns, processions).
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** play shaped by neighbors' festivals.
- **New Territories villages:** festival troupes kept alive by village associations.
- **Elders and young adults:** cards and chess against phones and online games.
- **She children in shared villages:** rhymes and games in She speech.
- *Comparison only:* Taiwan's Hakka children's programming; overseas Hakka lion-dance troupes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; festival play is well documented through heritage listings; children's everyday play and adult games are thin and scattered in local papers.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka is essential for rhymes and riddles (wordplay depends on dialect sounds); written Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong troupes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling studies touch on illegal activity; some martial-arts troupes guard their forms.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search library catalogs for 客家童谣 and collect versions of 月光光 from at least three districts.
2. Search CNKI for 客家 传统体育 游戏 and list games with rules described.
3. Pull heritage dossiers for the Fengshun fire dragon and the Sai Kung unicorn dance.
4. Search for 客家 令子 to find riddle collections.
5. Search local news for 老人活动中心 梅州 to see what elders play.
