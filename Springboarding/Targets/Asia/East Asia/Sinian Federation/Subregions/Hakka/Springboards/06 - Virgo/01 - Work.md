# Hakka: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties (Meixian, Changting, Ningdu and others), Standard Chinese, Cantonese where Hong Kong villages are concerned. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Meizhou's emigrant counties against Xingning and Wuhua: remittance households against counties known for building trades.
- Western Fujian: printing (Sibao), paper, river boats and tobacco set its work apart from Meizhou's.
- Southern Jiangxi: hill farming, tea and later tungsten; Soviet-era reorganization of labor.
- Northwestern Jiangxi's "shack people" (棚民) settlers: hill industries that drew Hakka labor.
- Guangxi's Thistle Mountain (紫荊山) area: charcoal burners and miners in Taiping origins.
- Sichuan's Dongshan Hakka: farming migrants beside Sichuanese speakers.
- Hong Kong's New Territories: quarrying, farming, then emigration and wage work in the city.
- The She people and the Cantonese and Teochew lowlands: who did which work at the ethnic boundary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history and local surveys; work statistics exist only by place, never by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese (classical for gazetteers), Hakka for songs and terms, Cantonese for Hong Kong oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet-era and Land Reform labor history; mining accidents; women's labor framed as an ethnic badge (avoid making it one voice).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *Report from Xunwu* and list every trade and wage it names.
2. Search CNKI for 客家婦女 勞動 and 客家 手工業 to build a bibliography by county.
3. Search the 僑批 holdings for letters from Meizhou senders that discuss jobs and wages.
4. Pull the labor and industry sections of the new county gazetteers of 長汀, 永定 and 尋烏.
5. Search Hong Kong archives for 茶果嶺 and 打石 oral histories.
