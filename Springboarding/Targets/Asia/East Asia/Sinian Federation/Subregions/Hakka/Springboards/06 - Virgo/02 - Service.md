# Hakka: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (heartland and Hong Kong varieties), Standard Chinese; Cantonese and German for some institutional records. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or a god), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Meizhou's emigrant villages: service flows from abroad (remittances, donations) more than in hill counties without emigrants.
- Western Fujian's tulou lineages: duties rotated inside a single building.
- Southern Jiangxi: Soviet-era mobilization and martyr memory shape the language of service.
- Christian Hakka communities (Basel Mission, Catholic, Hong Kong's Shung Him Tong) against temple-based villages.
- Hong Kong New Territories villages, where festival and lineage roles persist beside city jobs.
- Guangxi and Sichuan Hakka, where native-place halls and lineage service may differ after migration.
- Cantonese and Teochew neighbors, whose lineage and temple service is the closest comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Lineage, mission and philanthropy records are rich; everyday service (to elders, to customers) is thin and needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Hakka; Cantonese; German for the Basel Mission.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service and martyrdom narratives; family service by adopted daughters-in-law; household labor is easily turned into a single "Hakka woman" image (avoid).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for 客家 茶亭 施茶 and 客家 公益 傳統.
2. Search the Shanghai Library genealogy catalog for 梅縣 and 永定 lineages; read their rules on rotating duties.
3. Search the Basel Mission archive catalog for "Hakka" with "Gehilfe" or "Bibelfrau".
4. Look up the Tsung Tsin Association's anniversary volumes in Hong Kong libraries.
5. Search CNKI for 香花 佛事 梅州 and read any fieldwork on funeral specialists.
