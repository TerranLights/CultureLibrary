# Hakka: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (for illness names, remedies and healers' speech), Standard and classical Chinese, Cantonese; German and English for mission medicine. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Meizhou: mission medicine, herbal soups and a longevity-branded county.
- Western Fujian: the Gospel Hospital and Red Army medicine; temple healing cults such as Dingguang.
- Southern Jiangxi: hill fevers in older records; Soviet-era clinics; barefoot doctors.
- Huizhou, Heyuan and eastern Shenzhen: factory health and fast urban change in Hakka villages.
- Guangxi and Hunan Hakka: covered by national health surveys, so they offer a statistical frame.
- Sichuan's Dongshan Hakka and Hong Kong's New Territories: remedies kept or changed under other medical systems.
- Cantonese soup and herbal-tea culture and the She people's healers, which overlap and need separating.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Folk and mission material is rich; health statistics exist only by place, and Hakka is never a survey variable.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka varieties for illness names and rites; Standard and classical Chinese; Cantonese; German and English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge may be guarded; folk healing sits in a gray zone of regulation; postpartum taboos touch women's private lives; longevity branding mixes data with promotion.
- **Held for review:** none.
