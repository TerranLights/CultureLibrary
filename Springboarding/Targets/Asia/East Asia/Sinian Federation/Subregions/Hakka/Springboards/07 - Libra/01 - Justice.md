# Hakka: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話), Mandarin, Literary Chinese for Qing case records, Cantonese and English for Hong Kong courts. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan and western Fujian** (Ruijin, Xunwu, Changting): heartland of the 1929–34 land revolution, so its memory of redistributive justice differs from other Hakka zones.
- **Meizhou counties**: emigrant remittance economy and opera traditions; justice may be pursued more through lineage halls and courts than through campaigns.
- **Western Pearl River Delta Hakka**: inheritors of the feud with the Punti and of the Qing settlement imposed on it.
- **Hong Kong New Territories villages**: the only Hakka communities whose wrongs are judged under common law alongside protected custom.
- **Guangxi Hakka** around the Taiping homeland: a different relation to rebellion and its punishment.
- **Sichuan and Hunan Hakka**: settlers whose quarrels with other migrant groups went before frontier magistrates.
- **The She (畲) people**: neighbors whose disputes with Hakka lineages may appear in the same case files.
- **Hakka Christians** (Basel Mission congregations): church discipline as a second court.
- Taiwan's Hakka only as comparison: a separate history of rebellions and redress.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing cases, revolutionary records and Hong Kong judgments; mixed for present-day courts (database access has narrowed); thin for surveys of justice attitudes that identify Hakka respondents.
- **Languages needed:** Literary Chinese and Qing legal style; Hakka for tales, songs and opera; Mandarin; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform, campaign killings and revolutionary tribunals are politically sensitive and some archives are closed; the Taiping and the Hakka-Punti wars carry rival memories; court-database access is restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search published selections of 刑科題本 for cases from 嘉應州, 汀州府 and 贛州府; note the kinds of wrong.
2. Read the Xunwu report with Thompson's introduction, then list Hakka grievances it names to test in other counties.
3. HKLII: search "tso" and "tong" with "New Territories Ordinance" for village justice under written law.
4. In the folk-literature volumes for 梅縣 and 永定, find tales of judges and revenge.
5. Search CNKI for 客家 族規 懲罰 to locate studies of lineage penalties.
