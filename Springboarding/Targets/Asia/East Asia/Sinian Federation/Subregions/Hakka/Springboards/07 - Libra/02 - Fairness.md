# Hakka: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, Mandarin, Literary Chinese for household papers, Cantonese for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yongding round-house villages**: shared houses with many households may have formal rules for equal rooms and turns.
- **Meizhou enclosed-house (圍龍屋) lineages**: rank by branch and generation may cut across equal shares.
- **Gannan counties**: square fortified houses (圍屋) and a different landholding history after the 1930s.
- **Hong Kong New Territories**: the only Hakka communities where daughters' shares were changed by statute.
- **Hakka in the Chaoshan border zone (Fengshun, Jiexi)**: markets and quotas shared with Teochew neighbors.
- **Sichuan Hakka around Luodai**: shares settled among migrants from many provinces.
- **Guangxi Hakka**: land shared or contested with Zhuang villages.
- **Women and men within each community**: household and field work split, as each describes it.
- Overseas Hakka rotating-credit clubs only as comparison, for how the same device worked abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for division deeds and house layouts; mixed for market and credit practice; thin for attitude surveys that name the Hakka.
- **Languages needed:** Literary Chinese with local land-measure terms; Hakka for rhymes and songs; Mandarin; Cantonese for Hong Kong debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** daughters' and widows' shares; village male-line rights in Hong Kong; family papers held by descendants.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: 分家文書 or 鬮書 with 閩西, 長汀, 贛南; list who has published Hakka-area deeds.
2. Read the UNESCO Fujian Tulou file and note each house's room count and shared spaces.
3. Search 客籍學額 in the Qing Veritable Records (清實錄) database and in Guangdong gazetteers.
4. HKLII: the Kwok Cheuk Kin judgments, then the 1994 ordinance's debates in the Legislative Council record.
5. Search CNKI for 客家 標會 and 客家 墟市 for credit and market studies.
