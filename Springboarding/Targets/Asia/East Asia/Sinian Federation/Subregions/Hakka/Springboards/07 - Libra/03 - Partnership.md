# Hakka: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, Mandarin, Literary Chinese for contracts and letters, Cantonese for Hong Kong associations. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou emigrant counties** (Meixian, Jiaoling, Dabu): many marriages lived across oceans, so partnership leans on letters and remittances.
- **Liancheng and the Sibao printing villages**: a lineage-based business partnership model.
- **Yongding**: tobacco merchants' partnerships beside round-house kin.
- **Gannan**: couples and work partnerships reshaped first by the Soviet years.
- **Western Fujian She-Hakka border**: intermarriage between peoples.
- **Hong Kong New Territories**: association-based alliances and church weddings in Hakka villages.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka**: marriage and business ties with non-Hakka settlers.
- **Urban Shenzhen and Huizhou Hakka**: couples in factory and city work, away from lineage halls.
- Overseas Hakka trading firms only as comparison, for partnerships run from abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for letters, contracts and trade histories; mixed for couple life inside the household; thin for surveys of partnership attitudes among Hakka specifically.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka (letters may mix Hakka words into written Chinese), Literary Chinese, Mandarin, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abandoned wives and second families abroad; private letters still held by families; She identity politics.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Brokaw's chapters on household division and trading routes, and list the partnership forms named.
2. Search 僑批 梅州 夫妻 for published letter selections and studies of spouses.

4. Visit or contact the Tsung Tsin Association for its history volumes and member rolls.
5. Pull marital-status tables from the 2020 census for Meixian, Yongding and Ningdu.
