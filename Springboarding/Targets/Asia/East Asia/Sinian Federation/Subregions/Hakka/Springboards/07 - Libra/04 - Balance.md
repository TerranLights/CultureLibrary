# Hakka: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan (Xingguo, Ningdu, Xunwu)**: homeland of the geomancer lineages and source of the Dongjiang, so balance with the land carries both a craft and a water politics.
- **Meizhou**: enclosed-house layouts with pond and mound; herbal soups and wine-cooked postpartum food.
- **Western Fujian**: eroded hills of Changting and the round houses of Yongding.
- **Hakka in Jiexi and the Chaoshan border**: the shared deity and a meeting of Hakka and Teochew food and medicine.
- **Dongjiang Hakka in Heyuan and Huizhou**: counties that hold the reservoir for the delta.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan Hakka**: factory and city hours against older rhythms.
- **Sichuan Hakka (Luodai)**: a spicy regional diet beside Hakka home cooking, a test case for body-balance ideas.
- **Hong Kong New Territories Hakka**: geomancy disputes over development near villages and graves.
- Taiwan Hakka pounded-tea revival only as comparison, for how a balance food becomes a heritage product.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for geomancy, architecture and land restoration; mixed for folk medicine (much is oral and held by healers); thin for time-use data that separate Hakka households.
- **Languages needed:** Literary Chinese for geomantic texts; Hakka for healers and songs; Mandarin; Cantonese for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** geomancy is officially discouraged in some settings and practitioners may be wary; herbal claims need medical caution; grave-siting quarrels.
- **Held for review:** none.
