# Hakka: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, Mandarin, Literary Chinese for examination and official records, Cantonese and English for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou's examination counties** (Meixian, Jiaoling): local histories stress examination success (to check), so blind selection is a live theme.
- **Single-surname villages in western Fujian**: officials and voters may share a lineage, so impartiality is hard to separate from kinship.
- **Multi-surname villages in Gannan**: rival lineages watch each other, which may push toward even-handed rules.
- **Hong Kong New Territories**: indigenous and non-indigenous residents under one written electoral rule, with an anti-graft agency watching.
- **Hakka minorities in Guangxi, Sichuan and Hunan**: their experience of being judged by officials from the majority groups around them.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan**: Hakka residents among migrants, in a city with formal hiring rules.
- **She communities**: whether officials and temple bodies treat them alike with Hakka neighbors.
- Taiwan's Hakka-majority townships only as comparison, for how a different electoral system handles kin voting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for examination lists and Hong Kong anti-graft records; mixed for village-election studies; thin for direct measures of favoritism among Hakka.
- **Languages needed:** Literary Chinese for gazetteers and regulations; Mandarin for official notices; Hakka for proverbs and tales; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption cases are politically framed; village-election studies may be restricted; naming individuals in favoritism cases needs care.
- **Held for review:** none.
