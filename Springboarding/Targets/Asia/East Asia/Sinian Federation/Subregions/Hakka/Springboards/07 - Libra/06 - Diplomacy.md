# Hakka: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, Mandarin, Literary Chinese for Ming and Qing records, Cantonese, Teochew and Hokkien for the neighbors Hakka speakers deal with. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan (Chongyi, Longnan, Anyuan)**: the counties where Ming pacification and compacts were tried first, a test case for later relations with the state.
- **Meizhou**: home of diplomats and officials who represented the state abroad.
- **Huiyang and Shenzhen**: Hakka among Cantonese speakers, where switching languages may be part of everyday tact.
- **Hakka on the Teochew border (Fengshun, Jiexi, Dabu)**: everyday dealings across a language line.
- **Western Fujian (Changting, Ninghua, Liancheng)**: water and market agreements between lineages in narrow valleys.
- **Hong Kong New Territories**: a body that negotiates formally with government.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka**: whether speakers keep Hakka at home and the majority tongue outside, and how they manage the two.
- **She communities**: relations with Hakka neighbors and the state since the Ming.
- Taiwan's Hakka and the overseas associations only as comparison, for envoys of identity who negotiate from outside the heartland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Ming and Qing official writing and for modern diplomats' biographies; mixed for everyday tact (mostly in folklore and linguistics); thin for surveys of conflict style among Hakka.
- **Languages needed:** Literary Chinese; Hakka; Mandarin; Cantonese; some Teochew or Hokkien for border communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** civil-war and party histories are politically framed; Ming "pacification" involved violence against upland peoples and must not be read only as diplomacy; family marriage talks are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
