# Hakka: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話) in its local varieties; Standard Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong records. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, including grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou households served by 香花 monks, and western Fujian households served by Daoist or ritual masters:** two liturgies that may frame the dead differently.
- **Emigrant-linked counties (Dabu, Meixian, Xingning):** families whose dead lie abroad or came home as bones; distance shapes mourning.
- **Southern Jiangxi (Ruijin, Yudu, Ningdu):** where war dead and martyr cults sit beside lineage rites.
- **Hong Kong New Territories villages:** hillside burial still permitted for indigenous villagers, against tight urban cremation elsewhere.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** migrants who left ancestral graves behind; check whether and how ties to distant graves are kept.
- **Christian Hakka:** whether converts keep tablets, laments and bone-gathering, and in what form.
- **Neighbors as contrast:** Cantonese and Teochew households who also gather bones; the She people of the same hills.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan Hakka mourning practice and diaspora funeral associations in Southeast Asia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on laments, liturgies and ancestor rites; mixed on dying itself, end-of-life care and suicide, where prefecture data does not separate Hakka from neighbors.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka for laments and interviews (lament texts are dense and local); classical Chinese for liturgies and gazetteers; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine and political deaths of the 1950s–70s; forced cremation and coffin seizures under funeral reform (search 江西 殡葬改革 棺材 with care); some liturgies are passed only to initiated ritual specialists.
- **Held for review:** none.
