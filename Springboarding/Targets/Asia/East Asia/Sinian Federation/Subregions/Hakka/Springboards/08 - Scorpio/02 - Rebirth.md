# Hakka: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話) and Standard Chinese; Hakka in church romanization (Pha̍k-fa-sṳ) for mission-era texts; Cantonese for Hong Kong villages. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Protestant Hakka (Basel Mission heirs and Presbyterian congregations) and Catholic Hakka of the Meizhou diocese:** two church routes to a new life, possibly told differently.
- **Guangxi Hakka around Guiping:** where the Taiping began; how local memory now handles a rebirth movement that ended in war.
- **Western Fujian (Ninghua, Changting):** root-seeking pilgrimage and its hosts.
- **Southern Jiangxi (Ruijin, Yudu):** revolutionary heritage as a remaking story told by the state.
- **Hong Kong New Territories villages:** emptied by emigration, now partly revived.
- **Sichuan (Luodai) and Hunan Hakka:** revival far from the heartland, under pressure from surrounding speech.
- **Temple and ancestral-hall households:** reincarnation and fate-changing rites rather than conversion.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Hakka language-revival movement, and diaspora associations that fund heartland revival.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century conversion and for state-led revival; thin for personal recovery stories (addiction, prison) told by Hakka speakers, which are not filed as Hakka anywhere.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Hakka romanized church texts; Hakka speech for tales and interviews; English and German for mission archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Taiping and revolutionary remakings are politically framed; religious conversion is a sensitive topic on the mainland; drug-rehabilitation records are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
