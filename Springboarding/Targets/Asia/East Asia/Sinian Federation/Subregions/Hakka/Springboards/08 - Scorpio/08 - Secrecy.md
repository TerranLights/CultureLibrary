# Hakka: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話), including song idiom; Standard Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong, where Hakka is often kept out of public talk; classical Chinese for society documents. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is one of its facets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Fujian–Guangdong border counties (Yunxiao, Pinghe, Zhao'an, Raoping):** the Triad homeland legends and mixed Hakka–Hokkien speech.
- **Guangxi Hakka near Zijingshan:** hidden worship before rebellion.
- **Hong Kong urban Hakka and New Territories villagers:** concealment of the language in the city against open village use; triad contact.
- **Meizhou core:** identity shown openly as heritage; useful against the places where it was hidden.
- **Western Fujian and Gannan ritual households:** restricted initiation knowledge.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou:** migrant cities where Hakka origins may go unspoken.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** "Tu Guangdong" speakers who used Hakka at home and Sichuanese outside; check whether that worked as secrecy.
- **The She:** their own guarded rites and the history of being classed with or apart from Hakka.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's "invisible Hakka" debate and diaspora brotherhoods such as Borneo's kongsi.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on secret societies and origin controversies; mixed on coded speech and craft secrets; thin on household secrets and privacy, which few studies treat directly.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Hakka for songs, argots and interviews; classical Chinese for society documents; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual masters' initiation knowledge is restricted, so record only what practitioners choose to share; triad material touches living networks; origin and genetics debates are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
