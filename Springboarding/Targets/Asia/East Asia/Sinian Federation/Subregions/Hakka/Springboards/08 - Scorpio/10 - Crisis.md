# Hakka: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities (non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話) and Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for Qing disaster and relief records; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Jiangxi and western Fujian base areas:** the crisis of 1934–38 and its reprisals.
- **Meizhou emigrant counties:** crisis felt through broken remittances and absent men.
- **Pingyuan, Meixian and other flood-hit Meizhou valleys:** recent disaster against older flood memory.
- **Hakka settlers in the former evacuated coastal zone and Hong Kong:** communities created by one crisis and later emptied by another.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** descendants of migrants who came after war and depopulation; check how that origin crisis is told.
- **Guangxi Hakka:** the Taiping war and its aftermath as the defining crisis.
- **Huizhou and Shenzhen:** factory closures and economic shocks in boom cities.
- **The She and Gan-speaking neighbors:** the same disasters seen from other communities.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan Hakka disaster memory (for example the 1999 earthquake) and diaspora responses to Southeast Asian crises.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing disasters, migration crises and the 1930s wars; mixed for famines (sensitive) and recent floods (news-heavy); thin for personal crises such as breakdown or bankruptcy.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for gazetteers and granary records; Standard Chinese; Hakka for songs and oral histories; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1959–61 famine and civil-war reprisals are restricted topics on the mainland; disaster blame is censored; survivors' accounts need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
