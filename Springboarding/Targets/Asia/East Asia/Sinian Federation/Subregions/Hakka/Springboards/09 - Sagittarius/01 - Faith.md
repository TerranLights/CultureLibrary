# Hakka: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties; Standard Chinese; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular trust in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou Buddhist and folk practice vs. Minxi's Dingguang cult:** two native holy figures; whether trust attaches to a person, a temple or a place.
- **Hakka Protestants (Basel-descended) vs. Hakka Catholics (Maryknoll, earlier the Paris Foreign Missions *check*):** two vocabularies and two histories of keeping faith under pressure.
- **New Territories walled villages:** faith tied to lineage, land and *jiao*; separate indigenous-villager rights holders from later residents.
- **Guangxi Hakka:** the Taiping memory makes faith a political question; check local attitudes rather than assume them.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** faith carried by migrants and rebuilt around guild halls; a test of what travels.
- **The She (畲族) in the same hills:** their ancestor cult (the Panhu story) must be kept apart from Hakka devotion.
- **Cantonese and Teochew neighbors:** shared gods such as the Three Mountain Kings make the contrast sharp.
- **Taiwan's Hakka (comparison only):** a parallel stream where the Yimin cult grew around faith in the war dead; useful to see what the mainland cores lack or share.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on missions and Hong Kong; patchy on mainland folk practice, often written up as heritage tourism; thin on secular faith (progress, science, the state).
- **Languages needed:** Hakka local varieties for vows and prayers; church romanization for older Protestant material; English and German for mission archives; Cantonese for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered house churches and underground Catholic communities; the purge of foreign missions; temple revival sometimes reported as "superstition". Protect informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
