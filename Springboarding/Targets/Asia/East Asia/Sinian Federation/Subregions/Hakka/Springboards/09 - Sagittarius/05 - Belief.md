# Hakka: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties (Liancheng and Changting speech differ sharply from Meixian); Standard Chinese; Cantonese for Hong Kong; classical Chinese for Song-to-Qing gazetteers. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou vs. Liancheng and Changting:** different speech and different festival forms; belief vocabularies may not match.
- **Gannan's geomancy lineages:** belief as a profession; compare clients and practitioners.
- **Hakka Christians (Hong Kong, Meizhou, Huiyang):** beliefs taught by catechism, held beside or against ancestor rites.
- **New Territories villages:** colonial-era recording of fengshui claims makes belief part of the written record.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** whether house cosmology and reburial customs traveled.
- **The She (畲族):** a neighboring belief system with its own ancestral legend; check overlap without merging.
- **Hokkien and Teochew neighbors:** spirit-medium and procession customs to contrast with Hakka ritual masters.
- **Taiwan's Hakka (comparison only):** its earth-god (伯公) shrines are densely documented, a check on which heartland beliefs went unrecorded rather than unheld.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and ethnography; mixed in measurement, since surveys rarely ask about geomancy or soul-calling, and the "superstition" label may depress answers.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka local varieties (Minxi speech especially); Standard and classical Chinese; Cantonese; English and German for mission texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** practitioners may hide their work from officials; some ritual texts are kept within a master's line and not for outsiders; graves and reburial are private family matters.
- **Held for review:** none.
