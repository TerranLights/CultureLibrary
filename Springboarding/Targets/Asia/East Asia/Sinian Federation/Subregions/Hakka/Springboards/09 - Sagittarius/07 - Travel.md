# Hakka: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties (heartland, Sichuan and Hainan forms); Standard Chinese; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong; Malay and Indonesian loanwords in returnees' speech *check*. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou and Dabu (major emigrant districts) vs. Gannan (less overseas emigration *check*):** the journey abroad may weigh very differently in each.
- **Minxi tulou communities:** a heritage-tourism economy; travelers coming in rather than villagers going out.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** a completed migration three centuries old; compare how they tell arrival with how the heartland tells departure.
- **Hainan and returnee communities:** the journey home from abroad as a second uprooting.
- **New Territories Hakka:** emigration within living memory under a different state.
- **Women who stayed behind:** "living widows" of emigrants; their songs and letters are the other side of 過番.
- **Cantonese and Teochew emigrant districts:** neighbors with parallel remittance networks (Teochew qiaopi above all), useful to see what is shared.
- **Overseas diaspora (comparison only):** the far end of the same letters; their side of the qiaopi exchange completes the heartland's half.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on emigration, remittances and roots tourism; mixed on internal labor migration's meaning; thin on Hunan Hakka travel history.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka varieties (letters often mix dialect and literary Chinese); Standard Chinese; English for colonial and UK sources; Malay or Indonesian for returnees' terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family remittance letters are private; returnee experiences in the 1960s may be painful; border and household-registration rules go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
