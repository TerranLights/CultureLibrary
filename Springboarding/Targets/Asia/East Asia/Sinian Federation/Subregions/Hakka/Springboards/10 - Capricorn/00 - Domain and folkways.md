# Hakka: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; written for Hakka communities wherever they live inside the federation, not for a territory. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话) in its Meixian, Huiyang, Tingzhou, Gannan, Guangxi and Sichuan varieties; standard written Chinese (Mandarin); Cantonese for Hong Kong and Pearl River delta material. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed (career, status, reputation, age, time, rank, order and power ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou core (Meixian, Xingning, Wuhua, Dabu, Pingyuan, Jiaoling):** the standard dialect and the densest lineage-and-emigrant villages; likely the reference case for the domain.
- **Western Fujian (Changting, Ninghua, Shanghang, Yongding, Liancheng):** tulou and the Shibi ancestral-site cult give different forms of shared command.
- **Southern Jiangxi (Ganzhou counties):** older settlers against Qing-era arrivals, and a Soviet-era past that rewrote local authority.
- **East River Hakka (Huizhou, Heyuan, Shenzhen) and northern Guangdong (Shaoguan):** urban growth and neighboring non-Hakka speech.
- **Guangxi, Hunan, Sichuan (Chengdu's Longquanyi) and Hainan settlements:** enclaves among other majorities, where duty and status may follow a minority's logic.
- **Hong Kong New Territories Hakka villages:** a colonial and post-1997 legal status that ties rank to descent.
- **She people (畲族):** many speak a Hakka-like language; worth separating where the two share villages.
- **Taiwan's Hakka and the overseas diaspora (comparison only):** separate legal and political settings show which parts of the domain travel and which belong to the mainland homeland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in Chinese for lineages, customs and migration; thin in surveys, which rarely record Hakka identity. No scope file exists; the communities are still a working list for the author to confirm, and the sheets follow the tracker header's list.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and classical Chinese for gazetteers and genealogies; Hakka for songs and interviews; Cantonese for Hong Kong; German and English for mission archives; Japanese for part of the scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Luo's northern-pedigree thesis is politically charged; Soviet-era sources follow official lines; claims that given leaders were Hakka are contested.
- **Held for review:** none.
