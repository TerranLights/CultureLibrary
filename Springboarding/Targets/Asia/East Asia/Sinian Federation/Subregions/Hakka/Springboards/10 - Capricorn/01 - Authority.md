# Hakka: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (Meixian standard plus regional varieties), standard written Chinese, Cantonese for Hong Kong. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan and Minxi Soviet-era counties (Ruijin, Xingguo, Changting, Shanghang):** lineage authority was broken and rebuilt; a test of where accepted authority now sits.
- **Yongding and Nanjing tulou belts:** Hakka and neighboring Hokkien round-house lineages; compare how one building's decisions are made.
- **Meizhou emigrant villages:** whether remitting kin abroad gained a say at home.
- **Guiping and the Thistle Mountain area in Guangxi:** the Taiping heartland's Hakka, where religious authority once overrode lineage.
- **Hong Kong NT Hakka villages beside Punti (Cantonese) lineages:** statutory and customary authority meet.
- **Catholic and Protestant Hakka congregations:** clergy as a rival to the ancestral hall.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** the Liudui 六堆 militia federation of the Pingtung Hakka, a self-organized command structure under Qing rule.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** the Lanfang kongsi 蘭芳公司 of western Borneo, whose chosen heads show authority built away from the lineage village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for lineage and state history, thin for survey measures by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Ming orders and genealogies; Mandarin; Hakka for temple and village fieldwork; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet-era and Taiping history is officially framed; lineage power in elections is a sensitive topic for officials; Hong Kong rural politics is contentious.
- **Held for review:** none.
