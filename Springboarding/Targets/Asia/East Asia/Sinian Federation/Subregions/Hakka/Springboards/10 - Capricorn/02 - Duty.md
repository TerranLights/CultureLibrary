# Hakka: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard written Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong). **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou and Dabu emigrant households:** remittance duty and the "overseas wife" who stayed; the richest written record.
- **Hakka Christian families (Basel-origin and Catholic):** how duties to the dead were kept, changed or refused.
- **Western Fujian lineages around the Shibi cult:** a duty owed to an origin place, not only to a grave.
- **Gannan Soviet-era counties:** state service set beside lineage duty; families of the fallen.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi enclaves:** whether duties to ancestors in Guangdong were kept across long distances.
- **Women in Hakka households:** the labor and care duties that fell on them, studied apart from men's lineage duties.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** the 義民 (righteous militia) cult of the Hsinchu Hakka, a duty to the war dead turned into a yearly festival.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** the senders' side of the remittance letters, for what was owed seen from abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for remittance letters, rites and lineage texts; mixed for women's own voices; thin for survey data by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** classical and written Chinese for letters and genealogies; Hakka for songs and fieldwork; German and English for mission archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-bride customs are painful family history; martyr records are official; family letters may raise privacy questions where descendants still live.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library and museum catalogs: 侨批 梅州, then 客家 侨批 家书.
2. CNKI: 客家 二次葬 OR 捡金, and 客家 蒸尝 OR 尝产.
3. Read Constable (1994) and Lutz and Lutz (1998).
4. Find 客家 家训 in genealogies held by the Meizhou or Ganzhou library.
5. Download CLHLS and pull its Guangdong, Fujian and Jiangxi counties.
