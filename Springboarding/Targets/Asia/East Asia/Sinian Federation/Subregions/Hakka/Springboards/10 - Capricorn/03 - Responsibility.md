# Hakka: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard written Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong). **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tulou communities of Yongding and neighboring counties:** responsibility shared inside one building, a sharp case.
- **Gannan mining and geomancy counties (Xingguo, Longnan, Xunwu):** blame put on fate, on site, or on outsiders.
- **Meizhou emigrant households:** women left answerable for farm, elders and children.
- **Hong Kong NT villages:** village heads and trust managers answering to members under colonial and later rules.
- **Urban Hakka in Shenzhen, Huizhou and Chengdu:** whether answerability shifts from lineage to employer and state.
- **Guangxi and Hunan enclaves:** small communities that may answer to non-Hakka majorities.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** the Meinong anti-dam campaign of the 1990s, a Hakka town claiming responsibility for its river.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** shared-liability partnerships of Hakka tin miners in Malaya, for how risk and blame were pooled abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; good for steles, deeds and village studies; thin for survey data by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for steles and deeds; written Chinese; Hakka for village interviews; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mining pollution and official blame are politically sensitive; lineage sanctions can touch living families.
- **Held for review:** none.
