# Hakka: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard written Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong and Shenzhen martial-arts material). **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Fujian and Gannan base areas:** where Party-army discipline was first imposed on villages; memory and official story to separate.
- **Huiyang, Shenzhen (Longgang) and Hong Kong NT:** martial-arts and qilin-dance training as a village discipline.
- **Meizhou exam-school towns:** school discipline tied to exam ambition.
- **Christian Hakka:** church discipline beside lineage discipline.
- **Women and men:** different disciplines of labor and conduct, to study separately.
- **Sichuan and Hunan enclaves:** whether home-training customs held among non-Hakka neighbors.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** the 硬頸 language as used in Taiwan Hakka identity politics, to see how a word about discipline was made into a banner.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Basel-church Hakka settlers in North Borneo, where the church's discipline was carried into a new colony.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; strong for Party, church and martial-arts sources; thin for measures of home discipline by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** written and classical Chinese; Hakka for sayings and interviews; Cantonese for Hong Kong martial-arts lineages; German for Basel records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Party history is officially framed; corporal punishment is a family-privacy matter; some martial lineages keep training methods private.
- **Held for review:** none.
