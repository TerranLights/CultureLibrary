# Hakka: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard written Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong business and emigration sources). **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou and Dabu emigrant counties:** the oldest overseas-ambition path; check how it is told now that remittances matter less.
- **Gannan labor-export counties:** ambition through factory migration, not overseas trade.
- **Western Fujian (Yongding, Shanghang):** merchant and army routes side by side.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou urban Hakka:** sudden wealth from land, a different relation to striving.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** descendants of earlier settlers who went west for land; whether the migration story still frames ambition.
- **Hong Kong NT villages:** emigration to Europe and the ding-right house as goals.
- **Women:** whether daughters' ambitions are spoken of in the same terms as sons'.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** start-up and cultural grants for young Hakka from Taiwan's Hakka Affairs Council, an official push on ambition to compare with mainland campaigns.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** the Tiger Balm fortune of the Aw family, with roots in Yongding, for how overseas success is claimed back home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for biographies and migration history; mixed for survey measures; thin for women's own accounts of ambition.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese; Hakka for songs and interviews; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** magnate biographies are often promotional; army and Soviet-era histories are official; labor-migration family stories touch on left-behind children.
- **Held for review:** none.
