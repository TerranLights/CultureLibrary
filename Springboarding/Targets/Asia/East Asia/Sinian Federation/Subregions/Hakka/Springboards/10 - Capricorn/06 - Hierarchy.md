# Hakka: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard written Chinese, Cantonese (Hong Kong), with She and neighboring Min and Yue terms where ranks cross group lines. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan "old" and "new" Hakka:** earlier settlers and Qing-era arrivals from Guangdong and Fujian ranked against each other inside one people.
- **Meizhou core against peripheral Hakka speakers:** whose accent and customs count as standard.
- **Pearl River delta Hakka after the 1850s-60s fighting (Chixi, Taishan):** a minority placed below Cantonese neighbors.
- **Hong Kong NT:** indigenous against non-indigenous residents, and men against women under the ding rules.
- **She communities:** ranked beside and below Hakka in local records.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** one group among many Qing settler streams; whether Hakka stood low or high there.
- **Women and men:** daughters, wives and widows in the lineage order, studied apart.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** Hakka as a smaller group beside a Hoklo majority, a mirror for ranking among Han subgroups.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Hakka shopkeepers in Jamaica and Tahiti, placed as a middle rank in colonial orders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage and architecture; mixed for language attitudes; thin and restricted for land-reform files.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for genealogies and Qing records; Mandarin; Hakka for kin terms and interviews; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class labels and land reform; the Han-or-not dispute; gender exclusion from lineage rites; Hong Kong ding rights.
- **Held for review:** none.
