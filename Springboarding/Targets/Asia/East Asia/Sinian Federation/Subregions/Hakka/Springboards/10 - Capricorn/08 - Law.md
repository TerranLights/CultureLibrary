# Hakka: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard and classical Chinese, Cantonese and English (Hong Kong). **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them; under Capricorn, the spirit of the law only: what law is for, whose it is, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong NT Hakka villages:** the one place where Hakka custom is written into statute; the sharpest test of law's purpose.
- **Western Fujian and Gannan hill counties:** long histories of custom, smuggling and revolutionary law in the same places.
- **Meizhou funeral-reform counties:** custom against administrative rules on the dead.
- **Shenzhen, Huizhou and Dongguan former villages:** collective land custom meeting city law.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** whether native-place guilds and lineage rules once handled what courts did not.
- **Women:** inheritance and lineage membership under custom and under statute.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** the Qing Danshui-Xinzhu court archive (淡新檔案), with Hakka litigants, used by Philip Huang, for how hill settlers used Qing courts.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** the Lanfang kongsi's own rules in Borneo, law made by Hakka settlers without an imperial state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Ming-Qing contract history; mixed for present-day mainland practice; court databases have been thinned.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for deeds and notices; written Chinese; Hakka for songs and interviews; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** funeral-reform enforcement and court access are sensitive on the mainland; Hong Kong ding rights are a heated political issue; the letter of the law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
