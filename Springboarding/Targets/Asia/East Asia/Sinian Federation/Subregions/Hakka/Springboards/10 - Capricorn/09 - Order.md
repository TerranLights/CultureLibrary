# Hakka: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka, standard and classical Chinese, Cantonese and English (Hong Kong). **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pearl River delta Hakka (Chixi, Taishan, Heshan):** memory of the feud years and their effect on present order.
- **Gannan fortified-house counties (Longnan, Anyuan, Dingnan):** order held by walls, against order held by the state.
- **Jinggangshan and the Jiangxi-Hunan border:** Hakka and native settlers and the revolution's order.
- **Hong Kong NT:** night watch, fengshui groves and colonial police in one setting.
- **Urban Meizhou, Ganzhou, Longyan and Shenzhen:** campaign order in streets new to many residents.
- **Guangxi Hakka near Guiping:** a district where rebellion once overturned order.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** settler towns where order was kept by guilds among mixed migrants.
- **Taiwan (comparison only):** Qing-era armed fights between Hakka and Hoklo settlers, for how ethnic militia kept order in a frontier.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** the Larut Wars among Chinese tin miners in Perak, where Hakka and Cantonese societies fought over mining order.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and architecture; mixed for present-day order; crime data are not kept by Hakka identity.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for gazetteers and Ming records; Mandarin; Hakka for interviews and songs; Cantonese and English for Hong Kong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic feuds and lineage violence are touchy for living communities; Soviet-era purges in the base areas are officially framed; security statistics are selective.
- **Held for review:** none.
