# Hakka: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people (客家), a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話 / 客家话; Meixian 梅縣 as reference variety, plus Tingzhou, Gannan, Huiyang and Sichuan varieties), written Chinese in both scripts, Cantonese for Hong Kong, English. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, groups, hopes, ideals toward humanity, and the social ways of settlement, migration, association and hospitality.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou (Meixian, Xingning, Wuhua, Dabu, Jiaoling):** reference speech and densest records; is its lineage-and-enclosure pattern the rule or one case?
- **Western Fujian (Changting, Ninghua's Shibi 石壁, Shanghang, Yongding, Liancheng):** tulou living and the Shibi origin legend shape community differently.
- **Southern Jiangxi (Ningdu, Xingguo, Ruijin, Longnan, Xunwu):** walled houses and a revolutionary past with its own records on groups and hopes.
- **Huizhou, Heyuan, Shaoguan, Shenzhen's Longgang:** Hakka in mixed, urbanizing settings beside Cantonese speakers.
- **Guangxi (Bobai, Luchuan), Sichuan, Hunan, Hainan:** later migrant communities living as minorities.
- **Hong Kong's New Territories (Sha Tau Kok, Lai Chi Wo, Sai Kung):** rural Hakka inside a Cantonese city.
- **Neighbors:** Cantonese (Punti), Hokkien and Teochew, Gan, and the She 畲族, many speaking a Hakka-like tongue; the boundary itself is a domain subject.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Hakka and the overseas diaspora, where Hakka run their own associations as minorities; shows which group forms travel.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Chinese on lineages, migration, houses and songs; thin on surveys, since the census and CGSS, CFPS and the World Values Survey's China waves do not separate Hakka from other Han. Heartland counties can serve as proxies.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese in both scripts; several Hakka varieties for oral sources; Cantonese; English; German for Basel Mission records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the origin debate (Central Plains descent against mixing with the She); the Punti-Hakka violence; land reform and later campaigns; contested claims that famous leaders were Hakka. Name variation, never one "Hakka spirit".
- **Scope:** the tracker header's list of Hakka communities is a working list still to be settled by the author; these sheets write for the heartland and the listed outlying communities.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for 客家 with 宗族, 會館 or 鄉約, filtered by Meizhou, Longyan and Ganzhou.
2. Read the 風俗 chapter of the Guangxu 嘉應州志 beside that of the 汀州府志, noting differences in markets, festivals and guest customs.
3. Read Constable's introduction to *Guest People* with Luo's 客家研究導論, to see how the group's definition shifted between 1933 and the 1990s.
4. Search eHRAF for "Hakka" and list what is coded under community and interpersonal relations.
5. Pull the Hakka sheets of the *Language Atlas of China* to place each community among its neighbors.
