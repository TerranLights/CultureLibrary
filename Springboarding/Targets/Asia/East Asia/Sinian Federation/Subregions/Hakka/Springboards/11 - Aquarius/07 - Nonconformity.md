# Hakka: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話), written Chinese (Ming and Qing official prose included), Cantonese, English. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gannan and the Tingzhou border:** the old outlaw highlands; nonconformity as a state label for whole districts.
- **Guangxi's Taiping country:** heterodoxy that became a war; how the memory is handled now.
- **Wuhua, Xingning and the Lilong area:** Christian converts living among non-Christian kin.
- **Meizhou city:** modernist writers and artists; a center that tolerated or resisted them.
- **Shenzhen and Hong Kong youth:** urban styles and language choices far from village norms.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** a speech island called 土廣東, "local Cantonese", by neighbors; difference kept or dropped.
- **The She 畲族:** a people whose dress and customs marked them out within shared hills.
- **Neighbors:** Cantonese and Hokkien attitudes to the same dress, religion and gender roles, to see whether Hakka difference was real or a label.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Hakka protest musicians and diaspora Hakka in Christian or secular movements, to see where Hakka difference took other forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for heterodoxy, rebellion and converts; thin for everyday eccentricity and youth culture.
- **Languages needed:** classical and modern written Chinese; Hakka for slang and songs; Cantonese; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious practice and "deviant sect" labels are politically sensitive; Cultural Revolution persecution; gender and sexual nonconformity is little documented and may expose people; avoid the stubborn-Hakka stereotype.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Lutz and Lutz for converts' own accounts, listing the villages and the reactions they record.
2. Search CNKI for 硬頸 AND 客家 and sort by date to see when the word became a slogan.
3. Read 曾國藩's proclamation beside Hamberg's *The Visions of Hung-Siu-Tshuen* (1854), to see departure named from both sides.
4. Search county gazetteers for 教案 in Jiaying and Huizhou prefectures.
5. Search Douyin and Bilibili for 客家話 說唱 and note the accounts' home counties.
