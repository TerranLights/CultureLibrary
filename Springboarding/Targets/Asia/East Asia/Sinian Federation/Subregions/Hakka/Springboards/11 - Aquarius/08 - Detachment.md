# Hakka: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話), written Chinese (classical for monastic and recluse sources), English. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, impartiality, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ningdu and Gannan:** a written tradition of scholarly withdrawal set in Hakka hills.
- **Meixian:** monastic models (陰那山) and the 香花 ritual tradition.
- **Western Fujian (Wuping, Changting):** a renouncer whose cult became community property.
- **Emptied heartland villages:** elders living alone while the young work in cities.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou:** urban Hakka where anonymity, not withdrawal, may set the distance.
- **Hong Kong's New Territories:** half-empty villages after emigration; the elders who stayed.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** a community long parted from its homeland; how it speaks of the distance.
- **Neighbors:** Cantonese and Hokkien vegetarian halls and death customs, to see which forms are regional.
- **Comparison only:** diaspora Hakka cut off from home for generations, and Taiwan's Hakka, for how distance from an ancestral land is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: good for monastic, recluse and death-ritual sources; thin for everyday emotional reserve, which needs fieldwork and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; modern written Chinese; Hakka for laments and songs; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grave and bone customs are private and sometimes contested by funeral policy; religious sites and practice; mental health and loneliness among the elderly; impartiality as a legal duty belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
