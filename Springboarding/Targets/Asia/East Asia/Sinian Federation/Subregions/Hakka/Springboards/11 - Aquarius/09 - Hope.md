# Hakka: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka people and their communities, a non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (客家話), written Chinese (letters, genealogies, temple texts), Cantonese for Hong Kong, English. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou and Dabu emigrant districts:** hope tied to remittances and the return of those abroad.
- **Gannan:** poorer hill counties where official revival plans meet local hopes.
- **Western Fujian:** tulou villages now hoping for tourism income.
- **Sichuan and Guangxi Hakka:** descendants of families who left in hope of land; what they now hope for.
- **Shenzhen and Huizhou:** native villagers made rich by land sales; their hopes differ from the hill counties'.
- **Hong Kong's New Territories:** lantern rites for sons in villages kept alive by returnees.
- **Neighbors:** Cantonese and Teochew remittance districts with the same letter tradition, to see which hopes were Hakka in idiom and which were regional.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Hakka writers' longing for the heartland, and diaspora Hakka whose hopes the remittance letters record from the other end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for remittance letters, rites and genealogies; thin for measured optimism, since surveys do not separate Hakka.
- **Languages needed:** written Chinese (letters mix classical forms and local speech); Hakka for rhymes and rites; Cantonese for Hong Kong; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** son preference in the lantern rites; private letters held by families; temple practice may be officially discouraged; hope tied to emigration touches family breakups.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the qiaopi collections' catalogs for letters sent to Meixian, Dabu and Xingning, and read a sample for stated hopes.
2. Collect Hakka versions of 月光光 from county folk-song volumes and compare the wishes in each.
3. CNKI: 客家 AND (上燈 OR 添丁) AND 元宵, then 天穿日.
4. Search FamilySearch for Sichuan Hakka genealogies (Longquanyi and the other Dongshan districts) and read their migration prefaces.
5. Search Meizhou and Ganzhou government sites for 鄉村振興 plans and set them against interview studies of villagers.
