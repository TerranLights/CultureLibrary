# Hakka: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka (Meixian, Tingzhou, Gannan and Huiyang varieties), Standard Chinese, Cantonese (New Territories). **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meixian and Dabu:** remittance-rich districts where giving came from abroad; compare with poorer inland villages.
- **Xingning and Wuhua:** large labor-migrant counties; check whether help flows differently there.
- **Western Fujian tulou lineages (Yongding, Nanjing county):** one building, shared stores; test whether relief stayed inside the wall.
- **Gannan:** poor uplands and former Soviet base counties, where state pensions to martyrs' families (烈屬) sit beside lineage help.
- **Guangxi and Sichuan Hakka:** migrant settlers whose guild halls and lineages may have replaced the home-county safety net.
- **New Territories Hakka:** church welfare and village trusts side by side.
- **She villages and Teochew neighbors:** the Chaoshan benevolent-hall (善堂) tradition next door is a contrast to test, not assume.
- **Comparison only:** Southeast Asian Hakka associations (會館), whose burial and relief funds for poor members show what the home model looked like once transplanted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Lineage and remittance records are rich; giving statistics are provincial or county, never Hakka as such.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka for pity words and tales; Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for gazetteers; German and English for mission files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "charity" was politically loaded after 1949, so older informants may frame aid as state work; begging and poverty are sore topics.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on compassion was set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Oxfeld's chapters on obligation and conscience, listing the Hakka terms she records for kindness and pity.
2. CNKI: 客家 蒸嘗 救濟, then 梅州 僑批 贍養, sorting results by county.
3. Pull the 義行 sections of the 興寧 and 長汀 gazetteers and tabulate acts of relief by type.
4. Find the Meixian and Yongding 民間故事 county volumes and mark every tale of a tested or rewarded kindness.
5. Search 廣東扶貧濟困日 梅州 for county giving figures across years.
