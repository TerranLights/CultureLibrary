# Hakka: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, Cantonese (New Territories and the old Punti front), Teochew and Hokkien for border zones, classical Chinese. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou:** weilongwu households and a Hakka-majority setting where "outsider" means something different.
- **Yongding and Nanjing county (Fujian):** round and square tulou; boundaries within a building shared by one lineage.
- **Gannan:** fortified houses and a disputed language line with Gan.
- **Chaoshan border:** the Hakka-Teochew line, crossed daily in markets.
- **Old Punti front (Taishan, Enping, Chixi):** where the line once meant war.
- **Sha Tau Kok and the New Territories:** a state border through Hakka land; the indigenous-inhabitant line.
- **Guangxi and Sichuan Hakka:** guild halls named for the home province, marking origin as a line among settlers.
- **Hunan and Hainan pockets:** small enclaves; check how they mark themselves.
- **She and Yao peoples:** the lines Hakka draw with non-Han neighbors, and theirs with Hakka.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's Liudui (六堆), a Hakka militia federation that defined a Hakka territory against Hoklo neighbors; useful for asking whether any mainland area ever organized its line that way.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on architecture and ethnic lines; mixed on household and bodily boundaries; thin on measured privacy or personal space.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka, Standard Chinese, Cantonese, Teochew, classical Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names for neighbors can be slurs; New Territories indigenous status is politically charged; purity customs around women need careful framing.
- **Held for review:** none; no boundary pointer was set aside.
