# Hakka: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, classical Chinese, Cantonese (New Territories), and the Basel romanized Hakka of church texts. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion (the institutions themselves are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou:** monk-saint cults, ancestral halls and mountain temples.
- **Western Fujian:** Dingguang and Tingzhou cults; the Ting River rites; Shibi as origin site.
- **Gannan:** old Buddhist sites and village cults on the Gan border; revolutionary memorials as a newer sacred register.
- **Chaoshan border:** the Three Mountain Kings, worshipped across the Hakka-Teochew line.
- **Guangxi Hakka:** Taiping memory beside village temples and Zhuang neighbors' cults.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** migrant saints in guild halls.
- **Hunan and Hainan pockets:** check for distinct gods or a blend with neighbors'.
- **New Territories Hakka:** Christian villages and qilin-dancing villages, sometimes near neighbors.
- **She people:** their ancestor-dog myth (盤瓠) and rites, distinct from Hakka practice.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan Hakka's 義民 temples and Southeast Asian Hakka's 大伯公 temples, both showing how the earth-god and the honored dead become a community's center away from the heartland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for temples and cults; mixed for personal experience; thin for survey data by dialect group.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka (ritual texts often mix Hakka and literary readings), Standard Chinese, classical Chinese, Cantonese, German and English for mission texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some rites are kept by ritual masters and not shown to outsiders; folk religion and house churches face official limits; diaspora-funded "root" events carry politics.
