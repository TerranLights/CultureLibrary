# Hakka: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, classical Chinese (lineage instructions), Cantonese (New Territories). **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou:** home of the 等郎妹 song tradition and of the 硬頸 label; test both.
- **Heyuan (Xinfengjiang area):** resettled villages, a case of mass giving-up of land.
- **Gannan and western Fujian:** memory of the 1934 defeat; lineages split by it.
- **Guangxi Hakka:** Taiping defeat memory; check how descendants speak of it.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** migrants who once yielded home for new land; their sayings on fate.
- **Hunan and Hainan pockets:** check for distinct death or fate customs.
- **New Territories Hakka:** land given up to reservoirs and new towns; hospital deaths replacing home deaths.
- **She people:** their own death and fate customs, studied apart.
- **Comparison only:** the Lanfang Republic (蘭芳公司), a Hakka mining polity in West Kalimantan ended by Dutch forces in the 1880s; how its end is remembered shows a Hakka community's memory of yielding power abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on songs, lineage texts and resettlement; mixed on deathbed practice; thin on measured fatalism, which outsiders have often claimed and must not be assumed.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka for songs of resignation and deathbed words; Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for lineage instructions; Cantonese for New Territories resettlement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1934 surrender and "self-renewal" are politically delicate; reservoir resettlement grievances persist; child-bride customs shame some families.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on surrender was set aside.
