# Hakka: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hakka (客家), non-territorial sub-unit of the Sinian Federation, region Asia / East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Hakka varieties, Standard Chinese, classical Chinese (gazetteer calamity records), Cantonese (New Territories), German for folk-tale catalogs. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Meizhou (Pingyuan, Jiaoling, Dabu):** floods and landslides in steep valleys; shame talk in a dense lineage world.
- **Gannan:** tiger lore and the fears of the 1930s war years.
- **Western Fujian:** tulou memory of raids; the seventh-month customs of Yongding and Changting.
- **Chaoshan border:** earthquake memory shared with Teochew neighbors.
- **Guangxi Hakka:** fears tied to Taiping-era violence and later settlement among other peoples.
- **Sichuan Hakka:** an origin story built on an earlier massacre.
- **Hainan pocket:** typhoon fear in a coastal setting; check for a distinct vocabulary.
- **Hunan pocket:** check for distinct bogeymen.
- **New Territories Hakka:** ghost lore, fengshui fears and urban crime fear side by side.
- **She people:** their own tales of tigers and spirits, kept separate.
- **Comparison only:** Hakka in West Kalimantan, many driven from the interior in 1967; the fear passed down there is a contrast for how a community remembers expulsion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on tales and calamity records; mixed on present-day anxiety; thin on Hakka-specific measures.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka, Standard Chinese, classical Chinese, Cantonese, German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fears tied to political campaigns are restricted ground; soul-calling and charms are labeled superstition officially; disaster deaths can be politically sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none; no fear pointer was set aside.
