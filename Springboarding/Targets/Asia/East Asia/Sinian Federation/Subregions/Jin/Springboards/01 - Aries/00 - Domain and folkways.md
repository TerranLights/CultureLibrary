# Jin 晋: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, a member state of the Sinian Federation (its lands: Shanxi 山西), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Mandarin (Putonghua 普通话); the Jin dialects 晋语 of central, northern, western and southeastern Shanxi; the Fenhe 汾河 cluster of Zhongyuan Mandarin 中原官话 in the south; Classical Chinese for older sources. **Concept in one line:** the domain of self and identity: what a self is here (person, body, appearance, name) and how it asserts itself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinbei 晋北** (Datong, Shuozhou, Xinzhou): the old frontier by the Great Wall, with Mongol contact and a pass-crossing past.
- **Jinzhong basin 晋中** (Taiyuan, Pingyao, Qi, Taigu): merchant towns whose family compounds displayed a household self.
- **Jinnan / Hedong 晋南 河东** (Linfen, Yuncheng): Zhongyuan Mandarin speakers, old great clans and the Yao-Shun legends.
- **Shangdang 上党** (Changzhi, Jincheng): the Taihang highlands, a wartime base area with its own opera and dialect.
- **Lüliang and the Yellow River counties 吕梁 / 河曲**: loess villages and songs of leaving home.
- **Coal towns** (Yangquan, Datong, Jincheng mining districts): worker identities and migrants from other provinces.
- **Hui Muslims** (Taiyuan, Datong, Changzhi): a minority self kept through mosque and food.
- **Catholic villages** around Taiyuan and elsewhere: a religious identity several centuries deep.
- **Manchu and Mongol descendants**, few and scattered; **Wutai Shan 五台山** monastics, both Han and Tibetan Buddhist.
- Cut across all by city and village, generation and gender.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for dialect, lineage, merchants and opera; thin for province-level psychology of the self.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; the Jin dialects and Fenhe Mandarin; Classical Chinese; some Arabic- and Persian-derived Hui terms and Tibetan or Mongolian for Wutai Shan, *check*.
