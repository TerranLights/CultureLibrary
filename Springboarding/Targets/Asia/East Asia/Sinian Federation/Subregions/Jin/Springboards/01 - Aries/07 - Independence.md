## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinzhong merchant towns**: thrift and family-firm self-sufficiency as heritage.
- **Taiyuan**: state-enterprise welfare against private-sector self-reliance.
- **Shangdang** (Pingshun, Changzhi): collective self-reliance models from the 1950s on.
- **Lüliang and Xinzhou hills**: left-behind elders, migrant remittances, relocated households.
- **Hedong (Yuncheng, Linfen)**: fruit and grain farming households that can feed themselves.
- **Coal towns**: dependence on one employer and what follows when mines close.
- **Hui households**: trade and food businesses and mutual help through the mosque.
- **Catholic parishes**: church charity as interdependence.
- **Wutai Shan**: monastic self-support against donations.
- **Women**: earning power and the move from household to wage work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in official poverty and welfare materials and in survey data; mixed in ethnography of households; thin in older personal accounts outside merchant and scholar elites.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese for Fu Shan and family instructions; dialect for village interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** poverty statistics and campaign claims; collective-era coercion behind "self-reliance" models; disaster response criticism.
- **Held for review:** none.
