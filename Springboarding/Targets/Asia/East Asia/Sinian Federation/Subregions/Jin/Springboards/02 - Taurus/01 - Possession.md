*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, Sinian Federation member state (Shanxi 山西), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Jin 晋语 dialects (Bingzhou, Lüliang, Shangdang, Wutai, Da-Bao subgroups); Fenhe 汾河 Zhongyuan Mandarin in Linfen and Yuncheng; Classical Chinese; Hui religious vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinzhong merchant towns (Pingyao, Qixian, Taigu):** heirlooms and compound furnishings survive in numbers; a test of keeping against selling off.
- **Lüliang and the northwest cave-dwelling villages:** few goods, long use, much mending; useful to see possession at the low end.
- **Jinbei border counties (Hequ, Pianguan, Youyu):** households split by migration; goods left with wives and parents.
- **Jinnan and Hedong (Linfen, Yuncheng):** a wheat-and-cotton economy with a different dialect; dowry textiles may differ in kind.
- **Shangdang (Changzhi, Jincheng):** fortified villages and their storerooms; what was hidden in war years.
- **Coal-city households:** sudden new goods after the boom, and the rush to sell them after closures.
- **Hui families:** funeral practice that limits grave goods; compare what passes to kin.
- **Buddhist monastics of Wutai Shan:** renunciation as a set rule, beside lay donors.
- **Catholic villages:** devotional objects as family possessions.
- *Comparison only:* Zouxikou descendants in Inner Mongolia, who kept Shanxi things across the move.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich on elite objects and antiques; thin on ordinary belongings, which need surveys, memoirs and fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; dialect grammars for possessive forms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the export of village antiques and looted grave goods is a legal and ethical issue (pointer only; law goes to Libra); Hui funeral practice should be described as the communities state it.
- **Held for review:** none.
