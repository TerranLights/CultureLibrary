## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinnan and Hedong:** hit hardest in the 1870s famine; the stele record is densest here.
- **Jinzhong merchant towns:** security bought (escorts, codes, walls) rather than stored.
- **Qinshui and Yangcheng gentry villages:** security built in stone against late-Ming and Qing raids.
- **Datong and the Great Wall belt:** frontier security and later coal; two different fears.
- **Miners and their families (Datong, Yangquan, Lüliang, Linfen):** job security against bodily risk; state mines against small private mines.
- **Rural elders in the hollowed-out villages of Lüliang and Xinzhou:** pensions versus children far away.
- **Coal-subsidence households:** security of housing itself.
- **Hui families:** religious reliance alongside community aid.
- **Taiyuan public-sector workers:** the "iron rice bowl" and its erosion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for famine and merchant history; mixed for present household security beyond official bulletins.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for steles and gazetteers; Standard Chinese; dialect for oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mine accidents and their under-reporting; petitions over subsidence and pensions; famine cannibalism accounts appear in sources and need care. National-security and policing matters stay out of scope.
- **Held for review:** none.
