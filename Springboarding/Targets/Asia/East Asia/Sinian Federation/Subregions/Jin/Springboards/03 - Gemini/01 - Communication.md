# Jin: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, Sinian Federation member state (pre-war Shanxi 山西), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Jin 晋语 subgroups (Bingzhou, Lüliang, Shangdang, Wutai, Da-Bao); southern Zhongyuan Mandarin (Fenhe subgroup); the Hui register of mosque learning; Chinese Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Taiyuan city talk against Taiyuan-basin villages:** the capital's mix of Standard Chinese and Bingzhou speech; check whether town speakers switch differently.
- **Datong and the northern frontier counties:** Da-Bao speech, sung and spoken traditions shared with the Inner Mongolian side.
- **Lüliang hill villages (Linxian, Liulin, Lishi):** scattered settlements where talk across ravines and at fairs mattered.
- **Changzhi and Jincheng:** Shangdang speech with its own storytelling trades.
- **Linfen and Yuncheng:** Mandarin rather than Jin; whether the north-south speech line is also a line in manners of talk.
- **Hui speakers:** a religious register beside local Chinese.
- **Coal-town in-migrants (Yangquan, Datong mining districts):** talk across dialects in work-unit settings.
- **Deaf signers, the old and the young, women and men:** separate norms to check, not assume.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialect description and performance genres; thin for recorded everyday conversation; turn-taking and politeness studies of Jin speakers may be few.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese; listening knowledge of Jin subgroups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public speech and broadcasting are state-run; records of political speech and loudspeaker content may be restricted; tread carefully with religious registers.
- **Held for review:** none.
