## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinbei 晋北** (Datong 大同, Shuozhou 朔州): steppe-edge heritage, errentai 二人台, coal towns.
- **Xinzhou and Wutai 五台**: wrestling, mountain pilgrimage, Hequ 河曲 folk song.
- **Jinzhong basin 晋中** (Pingyao, Qixian, Taigu, Jiexiu): merchant towns, compound display, Jinju heartland.
- **Taiyuan 太原**: provincial capital, urban leisure and professional sport.
- **Lüliang 吕梁** and the western loess: uplands with their own songs and paper-cut.
- **Shangdang 上党** (Changzhi, Jincheng): Taihang villages, sai festivals, Shangdang opera.
- **Hedong 河东** (Linfen, Yuncheng): southern Mandarin speech, Puju, drum troupes, Guandi temple fairs.
- **Hui communities** (Changzhi, Taiyuan, Datong): festivals and celebrations outside the Han calendar.
- **Catholic villages** (e.g., near Taiyuan) and Buddhist monastic Wutai: festival life set by a religious calendar.
- **Comparison only:** Shanxi-descended communities beyond its borders (the 走西口 migrants in Inner Mongolia; merchant guild halls elsewhere; the Hongdong 大槐树 root-seeking network).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for opera, festivals and folk art (strong heritage and scholarly literature); mixed for surveys at province level; thin for minorities and for dialect-specific vocabulary.
- **Lands:** the tracker lists only Shanxi; the member state's exact lands are still to be confirmed by the author in Stage 0.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese (classical Chinese for gazetteers and diaries); Jin dialect dictionaries; Jingtang terms for Hui life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land-reform and Cultural Revolution material (Long Bow, Dazhai) is politically charged; media images of "coal bosses" and their display are a stereotype to test, not a pointer to repeat.
- **Held for review:** none.
