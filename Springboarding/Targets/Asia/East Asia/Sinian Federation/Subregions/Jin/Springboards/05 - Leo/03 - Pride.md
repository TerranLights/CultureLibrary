## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hedong and Linfen basin**: claims to the sage kings and earliest states; pride in antiquity.
- **Jinzhong merchant towns** (Pingyao, Qixian, Taigu): merchant-heritage pride, now tied to tourism.
- **Datong and Jinbei**: pride as an old frontier and imperial capital (Northern Wei, Liao, Jin); also coal.
- **Coal and steel towns** (Yangquan, Shuozhou, parts of Taiyuan): pride in labor; wounded pride after closures.
- **Lüliang and Taihang old base areas**: revolutionary pride, official and lived.
- **Xinzhou / Daixian**: frontier-general and pass-defense pride (Yanmen 雁门).
- **Hui communities**: minority pride and its limits.
- **Rural and urban**: villagers and city dwellers judge "showing off" differently; worth separating.
- **Comparison only:** Shanxi-descended people elsewhere (the 走西口 descendants in Inner Mongolia; Hongdong-ancestry lineages across north China).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in history, opera and heritage branding; mixed for survey data; thin for minority pride.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; dialect for nicknames and mockery.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride is a political subject; official heritage narratives and revolutionary memorials need critical reading; regional mockery online repeats stereotypes, which must be studied as claims only.
- **Held for review:** none.
