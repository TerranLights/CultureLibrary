## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinzhong merchant towns**: owner and manager roles, lineage leadership.
- **Taiyuan**: provincial politics, warlord-era memory, Tang founding lore.
- **Taihang and Shangdang**: wartime cadres, model-village leaders, association heads in older festivals.
- **Hequ and the northwest**: village voting experiments.
- **Datong**: city leadership by a single strong mayor; mining enterprise bosses.
- **Coal counties**: private mine owners against state firm managers.
- **Wutai**: monastic leadership across ethnic lines.
- **Hui communities**: elders and clerics.
- **Women**: Wenshui memory, labor-model leaders, today's village heads.
- **Comparison only:** Shanxi merchant leaders who ran guild halls in other provinces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for merchant history and modern politics; mixed for village leadership; thin for minority and women's informal leadership.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Jingtang terms; Mongolian or Tibetan only for Wutai's Qing-era clergy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** party leadership, anti-corruption cases and village elections are politically sensitive; Yan Xishan and wartime figures are contested; formal command and its rules go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
