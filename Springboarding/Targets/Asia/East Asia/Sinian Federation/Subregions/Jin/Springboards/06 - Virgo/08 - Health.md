# Jin: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋 (Shanxi 山西), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Jin 晋语 dialects; southern Central Plains Mandarin; classical Chinese for medical texts; Hui and Tibetan Buddhist terms for small communities. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coal counties** (Datong, Yangquan, Jincheng, Gujiao): occupational disease and mine-town hospitals.
- **Datong basin villages:** illness tied to water and land.
- **Lüliang uplands:** birth health and thin rural services.
- **Taiyuan and Linfen:** city air pollution and big hospitals.
- **Changzhi and Hunyuan herb areas:** herb growers and traders.
- **Wutai Shan:** monastic health practice and pilgrims seeking cures, Chinese and Tibetan.
- **Hui families:** halal diet, Hui medicine and ritual washing.
- **Catholic villages:** church-run clinics and sisters' nursing, past and present.
- **Elders left in villages:** care at a distance from migrant children.
- **Diaspora, for comparison only:** Shanxi herb merchants' old networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chinese medicine, environmental and occupational health; mixed for folk healing; thin for Hui and Tibetan Buddhist health practice.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Jin dialect for illness words and home remedies; some Arabic-derived Hui terms; Tibetan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pneumoconiosis claims and compensation; pollution data; birth defects (stigma); epidemic records. Health law and compensation courts belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

4. Pull causes of death and doctors per head by city from the Shanxi health yearbook.
5. Search 山西 药王庙 庙会 and 叫魂 for folk healing accounts by county.
