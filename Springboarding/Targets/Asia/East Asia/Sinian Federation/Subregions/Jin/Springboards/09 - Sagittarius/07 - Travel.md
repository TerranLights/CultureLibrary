# Jin 晋: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, the Sinian Federation member state on the lands of Shanxi, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Jin dialects of the Yellow River and Datong districts for migration songs; Literary Chinese and Japanese (Kanbun) for early travel diaries; Hui usage for the Hajj. **Concept in one line:** going far from home (long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration) and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yellow River counties (Hequ, Baode, Pianguan) and Youyu:** the western-pass migration and its songs of farewell.
- **Jinzhong merchant towns:** long postings away from home as a trade career.
- **Hongdong and Linfen:** the place people come back to, rather than leave from.
- **Wutai and Hengshan villages:** hosts to pilgrims and tourists.
- **Pingyao, Datong and the temple counties of the south:** tourism and its effect on residents.
- **Coal towns:** arrivals of migrant miners and departures after closures.
- **Hui congregations:** the Hajj as the farthest journey.
- **Catholic villages:** pilgrimage inside the province.
- **Comparison only:** western-pass descendants in Inner Mongolia; Shanxi-ancestry lineages elsewhere who visit Hongdong.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; travel diaries, songs, merchant history, tourism statistics.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Literary Chinese, Japanese, Mongolian and Russian for the tea trade's far end *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan and Mongol pilgrimage is politically sensitive; out-migration from poor counties overlaps with poverty politics.
- **Held for review:** none.
