# Jin: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, a member state of the Sinian Federation (its lands: Shanxi 山西), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (Putonghua); Jin 晋语 dialects (Bingzhou, Lüliang, Shangdang, Wutai, Da-Bao subgroups); Zhongyuan Mandarin of the Fen River valley in the south; Classical Chinese for older sources; Mongolian and Tibetan terms for Wutai Shan; Hui Muslim religious vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** the domain question: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed? (career, status, reputation, authority, responsibility; age, time, rank, order and power ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jinbei 晋北** (Datong, Shuozhou, Xinzhou): frontier garrisons, Mongol contact, Wutai Shan; military rank ways.
- **Jinzhong 晋中** (Taiyuan basin: Pingyao, Qixian, Taigu, Jiexiu): merchant households and shop hierarchies.
- **Lüliang 吕梁** and western loess hills: cave-dwelling villages, wartime base areas, poverty programs.
- **Shangdang 上党** (Changzhi, Jincheng): Taihang uplands, collective-era model villages, a separate Jin subgroup.
- **Jinnan 晋南 / Hedong 河东** (Linfen, Yuncheng): Zhongyuan Mandarin speakers, older official and scholar families.
- **Coal and industrial towns** (Yangquan, Datong, Jincheng): state-firm work units and private mine owners.
- **Hui Muslims** (Taiyuan, Datong, Changzhi *check*): mosque authority beside state authority.
- **Catholic and Protestant villages**: church hierarchy beside lineage and state.
- **Wutai Shan clergy**: Han and Tibetan-Mongolian Buddhist ranks.
- **Manchu and Mongol descendants**: small; banner-garrison and border-trade histories.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Shanxi merchant communities in Inner Mongolia, Beijing and beyond.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern Jin, the merchant era and the revolutionary period; mixed for today; thin for the small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Jin dialects for oral sources; some Mongolian and Tibetan for Wutai; Arabic and Persian loanwords in Hui usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent anti-corruption purges, mine disasters, forced-labor cases and the Cultural Revolution; post-1949 archives are often closed; religious communities need care.
- **Lands:** Jin's lands (Shanxi, per the tracker header) are a working list the author still confirms in Stage 0.
- **Held for review:** none.
