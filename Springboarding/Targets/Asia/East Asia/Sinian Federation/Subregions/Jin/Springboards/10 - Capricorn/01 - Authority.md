## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shangdang and Taihang villages:** early base areas, where wartime authority was rebuilt first; set beside places under Yan Xishan until 1949.
- **Lüliang and the northwest:** the Jinsui base and the poorest counties; authority arriving as relief and campaigns.
- **Jinzhong merchant towns:** owners' and managers' authority in shops and compounds, inherited by private firms.
- **Datong and the north:** princely, garrison and later state-mine authority; a frontier with Mongol ties.
- **Southern Hedong:** older gentry and degree-holder families; a different dialect and kin layout to test.
- **Coal-mine districts:** private mine owners as local power holders beside the state.
- **Catholic villages:** bishop, priest and lay leaders, split between registered and unregistered churches.
- **Hui congregations:** religious and lay leaders inside the state's religious system.
- **Wutai Shan monasteries:** Han and Tibetan-Mongolian lines under one mountain.
- **Generations:** collective-era elders, reform-era parents, and young migrants who left the villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land reform, base areas and village elections; mixed for workplaces; thin for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Jin dialects for oral history; Tibetan and Mongolian for Wutai records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petitioning, purges of officials and unregistered churches; interviews must protect people; Party records are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
