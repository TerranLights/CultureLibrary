# Jin: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋 (Shanxi 山西), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Mandarin; Classical Chinese (Warring States, Tang and Song sources); Jin 晋语 and Fenhe Mandarin for oral history; Hui Islamic terms; English for missionary and treaty-era sources. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hedong (Yuncheng) literati tradition:** the province's classical reformers and their critics.
- **Datong and the north:** reforms imposed from distant capitals, Northern Wei to the coal era.
- **Jinzhong gentry and merchant towns:** late-Qing reformers with money and schools.
- **Taiyuan:** university, government and the press, where reform programs were written.
- **Coal counties:** those who lived through consolidation, transformation and cleanup.
- **Christian communities in Linfen and Hongdong:** reform of the person through conversion.
- **Hui congregations:** reform of religious custom inside Islam, *check* which schools are present.
- **Collective-era model villages (Xiyang, Pingshun):** how reform looks from the places it dethroned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical and late-Qing reform and for official reform programs; mixed for how ordinary people judged reforms.
- **Languages needed:** Classical and Mandarin Chinese; English for mission sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** anti-corruption cases, coal-sector reform and pollution data; official accounts dominate. Law reform as such goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the 胡服骑射 debate in 《战国策》 and Sima Guang's anti-New Policies memorials, to frame the classical poles.
2. Read Thompson (1995), then search CNKI for 山西 争矿运动 and 山西村政.
3. Find the history office of Shanxi University for its 1902 founding records.
4. Search provincial sites for 资源型经济转型 试验区 评估 and Shanxi Daily for 政治生态.
5. Read 《新星》 and look for reader responses from 1985.
