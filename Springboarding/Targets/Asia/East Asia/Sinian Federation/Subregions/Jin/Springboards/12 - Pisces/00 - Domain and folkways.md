## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Shanxi (Datong, Shuozhou, Xinzhou):** frontier history, Wutaishan and Yungang, Jin Da-Bao and Wutai dialect areas; pilgrimage and steppe contact.
- **Central Shanxi (Taiyuan, Jinzhong, Pingyao, Jiexiu):** the Bingzhou dialect core and the merchant heartland; city-god and lineage temples.
- **Lüliang and the western hills along the Yellow River:** poorer, Lüliang dialects, close ties to Shaanbei across the river.
- **Southeast, Shangdang (Changzhi, Jincheng):** the densest village sai tradition; separate Shangdang dialects.
- **South (Linfen, Yuncheng):** Zhongyuan Mandarin speakers; Yao and Guan Yu cult sites; Puju opera.
- **Hui Muslims** (Taiyuan, Datong, Changzhi); **Catholic villages**; **small Mongol and Manchu descendant communities**; **coal-mining towns** (Yangquan, Datong, Jincheng) as an occupational culture; **urban against village**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for temples, ritual drama and Catholic history; mixed for spirit mediums and folk healing (much of it is unofficial); survey data mostly national, with thin Shanxi cuts.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Jin dialect knowledge for fieldwork and gazetteers; Classical Chinese for steles and ritual manuscripts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mediums and "superstition" can be legally and politically sensitive to discuss; ghost marriage involves reported corpse thefts (handle with care); the Boxer killings of 1900 and wartime camps carry heavy memory.
