# Jin: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Jin 晋 member state (Shanxi 山西) of the Sinian Federation; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese (Daoist and Buddhist texts, dynastic histories); the Jin dialect subgroups and southern Zhongyuan Mandarin, themselves a subject here (dialect shift); Hui Chinese usage. **Concept in one line** (proposed): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Datong and the north:** the Northern Wei and later frontier peoples; Da-Bao Jin speakers; coal-city restructuring.
- **Central basin (Taiyuan, Jinzhong):** urban Bingzhou speakers shifting to Standard Chinese; Manchu descendants; merchant lineages dispersed.
- **Lüliang and the Yellow River counties:** village relocation and hollow villages.
- **Shangdang (Changzhi, Jincheng):** the strongest village temple life, so a test of whether communal rites still hold.
- **Southern Shanxi (Linfen, Yuncheng):** Zhongyuan Mandarin; drum troupes as merging crowds.
- **Hui Muslims** (assimilation pressure and death customs); **Buddhist monastics and lay meditators**; **Catholic villages**; **the old with dementia and their carers**; **young migrants** who leave dialect and village behind.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; strong for dialect documentation and Northern Wei history; thin for first-person accounts of mystical self-loss; relocation data official and partial.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Jin dialect expertise for language-shift work; Tibetan for Wutaishan ritual texts *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** assimilation of non-Han peoples is politically charged; relocation and urban demolition touch on grievances; dementia carries stigma in family settings.
- **Held for review:** none.
