## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wutaishan area (Xinzhou):** Han Chinese Buddhist 青庙 and Tibetan Buddhist 黄庙 temples side by side; Mongol and Tibetan pilgrims as visitors.
- **Northern Shanxi (Datong, Hunyuan):** Yungang heritage and Hengshan Daoism.
- **Central basin (Taiyuan, Jinzhong):** urban lay Buddhists, qigong practitioners in parks, Catholic parishes.
- **Southern Shanxi (Yuncheng, Linfen):** Guandi, Houtu and Yao cults among Zhongyuan Mandarin speakers.
- **Shangdang (Changzhi, Jincheng):** village deity worship and vows at local shrines.
- **Hui Muslims; Catholics** (rural villages and city parishes); **Protestant house groups** (handled carefully); **secular and nonreligious residents**; **young urban seekers** drawn to meditation and sutra copying.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Wutaishan and temple history; mixed for lived practice today; national surveys rarely give Shanxi-level detail.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese, Buddhist Chinese; Tibetan and Mongolian for Wutaishan's Tibetan Buddhist sources; Jin dialect for oral devotion.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered religious groups and banned qigong-derived movements are politically restricted; do not collect identifying details of members; Tibetan Buddhist topics are sensitive. Doctrine and religious institutions belong to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** none.
