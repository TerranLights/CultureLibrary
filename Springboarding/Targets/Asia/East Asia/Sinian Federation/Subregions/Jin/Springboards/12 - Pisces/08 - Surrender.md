# Jin: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋, the Shanxi 山西 member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese (Daoist texts, Song and Ming-Qing biographies); Jin dialect and southern Zhongyuan Mandarin for oral sayings of resignation; Hui Islamic Chinese; Japanese for the 1945-49 holdouts. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern frontier counties (Daixian, Shuozhou, Datong):** Yang family legend and frontier defeat memory.
- **Taiyuan:** siege memory, Fu Shan's legacy, urban hospice and Buddhist chanting groups.
- **Lüliang uplands:** long poverty and the literature of resignation.
- **Coal regions (Datong, Yangquan, Jincheng):** miners' illness and acceptance.
- **Southern Shanxi (Linfen, Yuncheng):** Zhongyuan Mandarin speakers; Guandi loyalty ideals as a counterweight to yielding.
- **Hui Muslims** (predestination teaching), **Buddhists** (letting go), **Catholics** (God's will in suffering), **Daoist practitioners**; **old and young** (renming against 躺平-era talk); **men and women** separately on giving in at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical surrender and refusal; mixed for everyday resignation and deathbed practice; thin for provincial hospice data.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Japanese for the post-1945 holdouts; Jin dialect for proverbs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** civil-war memory differs sharply between mainland and Taiwan sources; occupation-era topics are charged; "fatalism" must not be written as a trait of the people.
- **Held for review:** none.
