# Jin: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Jin 晋 (lands of Shanxi 山西), a member state of the Sinian Federation; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Jin dialect child-scare words and ghost vocabulary across its subgroups (Bingzhou, Lüliang, Shangdang, Wutai, Da-Bao); southern Zhongyuan Mandarin; Classical Chinese for exorcism texts and disaster steles; Hui religious usage. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coal regions (Datong, Gujiao, Yangquan, Jincheng):** fear of explosions, floods and subsidence; miners' wives.
- **Fen valley (Taiyuan, Linfen, Yuncheng):** pollution fear and quake risk; Zhongyuan Mandarin speech in the south.
- **Southern ritual villages (Quwo, Shangdang):** exorcistic drama and plague-god rites.
- **Loess uplands (Lüliang, northwest Shanxi):** wolves, ghosts and child-scare figures in rural tales.
- **Northern frontier (Datong, Shuozhou):** war memory and occupation terror.
- **Hui Muslims** (reverent fear of God, fear of hostility); **Catholics** (memories of 1900); **children and the old** (different fears); **women** (fear of trafficking and violence); **urban residents** with online legends against rural tellers of older tales.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster and folk-tale material; mixed for measured anxiety at provincial scale; ghost-related media is limited by national rules on horror content.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Jin dialect for folk tales and child-scare words; Classical Chinese for ritual texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disaster blame, pollution and occupation memory touch on official responsibility; political fear (campaign-era persecution) is a restricted subject; protect informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
