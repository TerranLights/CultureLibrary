*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhou, Zhangzhou and Xiamen people:** older rivalry between Quanzhou and Zhangzhou speech and origin lore; ask whether it still divides.
- **Fuzhou people and the Fuqing–Changle emigrant belt:** a strong district identity tied to emigration.
- **Puxian speakers:** the old label 文献名邦 and Mazu's home island are offered as Putian's identity; test who actually claims them.
- **Northern Fujian uplands:** heirs to the Minyue heartland and to Zhu Xi's teaching places.
- **She, Hui and boat-dwelling communities in Fujian:** identities partly defined against the landed Han majority.
- **Hainanese Han of Wenchang, Qionghai and Haikou:** returnee and emigrant ties shape the answer to "who are you?"
- **Danzhou, Lingao, Cun, Jun and Mai speakers:** small Han or Han-classified groups with their own speech.
- **Li branches and Hainan Miao:** official labels set beside the branch or village self-names.
- **Utsul of Sanya:** Muslim, Chamic-speaking, and counted as Hui.
- **Post-1988 incomers and returnee farms:** identity by arrival.
- **Hakka of western Fujian:** separate sub-unit; note only the Hakka–Minnan boundary zone.
- **Comparison only:** Hokkien, Fuzhou, Henghua and Hainanese associations in Southeast Asia; Taiwan; Chaoshan and Leizhou.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage and origin claims; rich in Chinese for returnees; thin for Utsul, Cun and Lingao.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Southern Min, Fuzhou and Hainanese for interviews; Hlai; Tsat; English for Chamic linguistics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification and self-identification are politically sensitive; "Min-Tai" shared-identity discourse is official policy toward Taiwan, so read it as such; religious identity of the Hui and Utsul.
- **Held for review:** none.
