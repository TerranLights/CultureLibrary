## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Fuzhou and its counties:** a festival calendar with an opening rite of its own (拗九).
- **Putian and Xianyou:** a second New Year tied to a remembered disaster.
- **Quanzhou and Zhangzhou:** home of a known almanac-making tradition; ask how far people still choose days to begin things.
- **Inland Minbei towns:** temple-founding and incense-division links to the coast.
- **Hainanese Han in the north and east:** earthquake memory and returnee starts.
- **Fishing communities in Tanmen and Sanya:** each voyage is a beginning with its own rites.
- **Li of the Limu Mountain area:** an origin legend tied to a place.
- **She of Ningde:** a spring festival distinct from Han New Year.
- **Utsul of Sanya:** ask whether their own origin story begins overseas, and how it differs from scholars' accounts.
- **Returnees on overseas Chinese farms:** forced new starts within living memory.
- **Comparison only:** temples in Southeast Asia founded by incense division from Fujian; Taiwan's temple networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festival calendars and temple founding; mixed for birth customs today; thin for Li and Utsul origin material in English.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Southern Min and Fuzhou for rites and almanacs; Puxian; Hlai; Tsat.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fishermen's route books and voyage rites touch on South China Sea claims, so keep the folklore apart from the politics; origin legends of minorities can be used to rank peoples; returnee resettlement involved expulsion and loss.
- **Held for review:** none.
