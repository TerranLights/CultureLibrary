## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minnan-speaking Xiamen** (high mixing with Putonghua through migration) against **rural Quanzhou and Zhangzhou** (where dialect may still carry daily talk): a split by place and age.
- **Fuzhou and Minnan speakers**: mutually unintelligible, so Putonghua may serve as a bridge inside the member state; test this.
- **Puxian** speakers squeezed between both blocs.
- **Hui'an coastal women** (Chongwu and nearby): gendered peer groups may shape who speaks to whom.
- **Li and Hainan Miao**, who may use Hainanese or Mandarin as contact languages with Han neighbors.
- **Utsat of Sanya**, a three-language daily life.
- **Lingao (Be) and Danzhou speakers**: non-Min speech islands on Hainan.
- **Returned overseas Chinese** (Xinglong and Fujian farms), where Indonesian, Malay or Vietnamese may be heard at home.
- **Internal migrant workers**, for whom Putonghua is the only shared tongue.
- **Hakka of western Fujian**, briefly (a separate sub-unit): mixed Hakka–Minnan towns near Longyan show how talk crosses that language line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Minnan code-switching and Putonghua promotion; mixed for Fuzhou; thin for Puxian, inland Min, Hlai and Be talk norms.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Minnan in Tâi-lô or POJ; Foochow Romanized; Hainanese; Hlai and Tsat materials; English for Thurgood and missionary sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dialect loss and Putonghua promotion are political; cross-Strait broadcasting is propaganda and needs framing; Taiwan's Hokkien, Kinmen and Matsu are comparison only.
- **Held for review:** none.
