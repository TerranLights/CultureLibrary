## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xiamen and Fuzhou** city readers, with dense media, against **mountain counties** of Ningde, Nanping and Sanming and **central Hainan**: gaps in access and in whom people trust.
- **Coastal fishing communities** (Tanmen, Chongwu, Pingtan): weather and sea information as livelihood.
- **Danjia boat people**, who may rely on word of mouth and radio more than print.
- **Li and Hainan Miao villages** in Wuzhishan, Qiongzhong and Baoting: knowledge kept by elders and by Li-language programs.
- **Utsat and Quanzhou Hui**: religious information networks reaching abroad.
- **Returned overseas Chinese**, who may keep news ties to Southeast Asia.
- **Internal migrants** in Jinjiang and Xiamen, with group chats organized by home province.
- **Older and younger** speakers of the same dialect: print and radio against short video.
- **Hakka of western Fujian**, briefly (a separate sub-unit): news may reach upland Hakka villages by other channels than the Min coast's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for printing history and the modern press; mixed for village channels; thin for Li, Be and Tsat information habits.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Minnan for song booklets; Fuzhou for vernacular writing; English for customs reports; Hlai and Tsat for fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** press controls and closed archives; the Genglubu and other South China Sea material are used in territorial claims; Kinmen belongs to the Taiwan target (comparison only); propaganda sites need careful framing.
- **Held for review:** none.
