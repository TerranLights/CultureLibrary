## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Putian and Quanzhou lineage villages**, with long exam-going reputations, against **upland Ningde and Nanping** villages: does the lineage school leave a trace?
- **Craft towns**: Dehua (porcelain), Hui'an (stone), Fuzhou (lacquer, Shoushan stone), Anxi and Wuyishan (tea): trade learning, gendered.
- **Hui'an women**, who may learn heavy outdoor work alongside men's crafts.
- **Li weavers and Li villages** in Baisha, Ledong and Wuzhishan: textile learning as female knowledge.
- **Hainan Miao** in Qiongzhong and Baoting, learning in Kim Mun, Hainanese and Mandarin at once.
- **Danjia boat children**, whose schooling and work learning may differ from shore children.
- **Utsat children**: mosque and state school side by side.
- **Returned overseas Chinese farms**: Southeast Asian ways of raising children carried back.
- **Migrant children in Xiamen and Jinjiang** and **left-behind children** in sending counties.
- **Hakka of western Fujian**, briefly (a separate sub-unit): compare craft and school learning only at the Min–Hakka edge.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Zhu Xi's learning, lineage education and craft heritage; mixed for early childhood at home; thin for Li, Miao, Danjia and Utsat child learning.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese (primers, academy records); Standard Chinese; Minnan and Fuzhou for rhymes; Hlai and Kim Mun for fieldwork; Arabic script for mosque teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heritage-bearer lists are state-chosen and may leave out uncertified masters; studies of left-behind children can stigmatize sending counties; religious instruction of minors is politically sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.
