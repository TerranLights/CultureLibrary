*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Qiaoxiang families** in Jinjiang, Shishi, Fuqing and Wenchang: two homes, two languages, two loyalties.
- **Quanzhou and Zhangzhou** Minnan speakers: an inner pair within one language.
- **Putian and Quanzhou villages** with old two-camp factions, against areas without them.
- **Quanzhou Hui** (Chendai, Baiqi): Muslim descent and Han practice.
- **Utsat** of Sanya: a Chamic language and a Hui classification.
- **She families with Han in-laws** in Ningde and Fu'an.
- **Li in towns**, switching between Hlai, Hainanese and Mandarin.
- **Danjia families moved ashore**: boat life and land life within one lifetime.
- **Returnee farm households**, Southeast Asian and Fujianese or Hainanese at once.
- **Hakka of western Fujian**, briefly (a separate sub-unit): families in Longyan and Zhangping who move between Hakka and Min identities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emigrant households, faction history and Min linguistic strata; mixed for Hui identity; thin for She and Li views on paired worlds.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Minnan and Puxian; Japanese (Kuwabara); English; Tsat and Hlai for fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification of the Utsat and Chendai Hui is contested; two-household marriages touch living families; Xiamen–Kinmen ties and Kinmen itself belong to the Taiwan target (comparison only); faction histories may revive old grudges.
- **Held for review:** none.
