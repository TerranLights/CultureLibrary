*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tanka and coastal fishing families:** the boat as shelter, and harbors in storms.
- **Xiamen, Fuzhou and Haikou:** state relief stations and city emergency plans.
- **Migrant factory workers in Jinjiang and Shishi:** dormitory and rented-room shelter.
- **Returnees and refugees on farms** in Fujian and Hainan: decades of "temporary" settlement.
- **Upland Min villages:** shelter from floods and landslides.
- **The Li:** field huts and village refuges; branches to separate.
- **Buddhist and Christian communities:** religious shelter beside the state's.
- **Winter rough sleepers** in Sanya and Haikou.
- **The She and the Utsul:** taking in strangers by their own customs, to check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on typhoon shelter and refugee history; mixed on state relief stations; thin on rough sleeping and on Li and She practice.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Hokkien and Pe̍h-ōe-jī; Hainanese; Hlai; Vietnamese for refugee memories; English for treaty-port records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** detention-like history of older vagrant shelters; refugee status; migrant housing; religious groups.
- **Held for review:** none.
