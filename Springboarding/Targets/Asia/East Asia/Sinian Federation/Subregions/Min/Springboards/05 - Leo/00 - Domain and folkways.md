## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Min, three ways:** Quanzhou (Nanyin, marionettes), Zhangzhou (glove puppets, woodblock prints), Xiamen (port city); different festive repertoires.
- **Fuzhou and Mindong:** Min opera and Fuzhou storytelling in a separate speech world.
- **Puxian (Putian, Xianyou):** long Lantern season and Puxian opera; Mazu's home district.
- **Northern and Central Min (Nanping, Sanming):** inland, least studied.
- **Hui'an coastal women:** display through distinctive dress.
- **Hui of Quanzhou (Chendai Ding, Baiqi Guo):** festivity outside the temple cycle.
- **She of Ningde, Fuding, Xiapu:** song duels and phoenix-style dress.
- **Tanka boat people (Minjiang estuary; Sanya, Lingshui):** water festivity, past exclusion.
- **Hainanese of Haikou, Wenchang, Qionghai:** Qiong opera, 军坡 and 公期 village festivals.
- **Hlai subgroups, Kim Mun Miao, Lingao and Danzhou speakers, Utsat Hui of Sanya:** each a separate unit for Hainan.
- **Hainan newcomers:** winter retirees, Free Trade Port arrivals.
- Hakka communities of western Fujian and Hainan: named only; a separate sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Southern Min performance and Putian ritual; mixed for Fuzhou; thin for Hlai, Kim Mun, Lingao and Utsat, where state folklore lists may be the main written record.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; Fujian and Hainan are a working list. Kinmen and Matsu (Fujian islands administered from Taiwan) are outside it unless the author decides otherwise.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan (a separate target), Chaoshan and Leizhou (Min-speaking, in the Yue member state), and Southeast Asian Hokkien, Hainanese, Fuzhou and Henghua diasporas.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Hokkien romanization (POJ or Tâi-lô); Fuzhou dictionaries; Hlai and She material mostly via Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Li facial tattooing; god processions once labeled superstition; underground lotteries are illegal; Utsat religious life.
- **Held for review:** none.
