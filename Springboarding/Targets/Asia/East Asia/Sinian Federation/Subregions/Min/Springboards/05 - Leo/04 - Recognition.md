## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Putian:** Mazu's titles and a temple-donor culture.
- **Quanzhou and Zhangzhou:** inscriptions, arches and examination records.
- **Fuzhou:** scholar-official and scientist reputations.
- **Hui'an women:** labor honored by the state, little credited otherwise.
- **She:** recognized as a people in the 1950s.
- **Lingao speakers and Kim Mun Miao:** classification cases to read with care.
- **Utsat Hui of Sanya:** a Chamic-speaking group counted as Hui.
- **Tanka:** a status lifted by edict, a stigma that lasted.
- **Hlai subgroups:** recognition through ICH titles and tourism.
- **Hainanese revolutionaries (Qionghai, Wenchang):** honors and memory.
- **Returned overseas Chinese (归侨) in Fujian and Hainan farms:** status as a recognized category.
- Hakka in western Fujian: honors lists cover them too, but they are researched in the separate Hakka sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inscriptions and state honors; mixed for everyday praise; thin for how She, Hlai and Lingao speakers themselves view classification.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for steles and edicts; Standard Chinese; Southern Min and Hainanese for oral praise words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification is politically sensitive; revolutionary honors carry official framing; ask before naming living honorees.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's honors; diaspora clan-hall donor boards in Singapore and Penang.
- **Held for review:** none.
