## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhou-Jinjiang industrial belt:** family firms and migrant labor, where fair pay and fair shares inside a family business meet.
- **Zhangzhou farm counties:** land shares, water and the old drawing of lots among brothers.
- **Fuqing and Changle:** remittance households, where money from abroad must be divided among kin at home.
- **Putian:** dense lineage and temple rotations.
- **Minbei rice and tea valleys (Wuyi, Jian'ou):** shares in tea-hill income; little studied.
- **Hui'an coast:** fishing shares and the distinct place of married women.
- **She villages:** whether daughters' shares differ from Han neighbors.
- **Li of Wuzhishan, Baoting and Qiongzhong:** collective harvest rules from the 合亩 tradition.
- **Hainanese Han of Wenchang and Haikou:** tourism money and fairness to outsiders.
- **Sanya's Danjia fishers and Utsul:** small communities with their own catch and trade customs.
- **Seasonal northern residents in Sanya:** the newest party to arguments about prices and queues.
- **Hakka of western Fujian (a separate sub-unit):** named only to mark where Min estate-division customs end and Hakka ones begin.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's household-division records; Chaoshan family firms; Southeast Asian Hokkien firms and their partnership splits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for property division and lineage estates (Ming-Qing documents, much scholarship); mixed for present-day attitudes; thin for the Li and She in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Classical and modern Chinese for documents; Hokkien and Fuzhou for village fieldwork; Hlai and She with native speakers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** married-out daughters' cases involve living families and courts; migrant-worker disputes; tourist-price scandals touch local livelihoods.
- **Held for review:** none.
