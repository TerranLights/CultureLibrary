## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhou and Xiamen:** street herbal medicine and the red-brick house.
- **Zhangzhou plain:** a farming calendar of feasts and fasts.
- **Putian:** the weir and its temple as civic memory of balance with water.
- **Fuzhou:** a soup-and-tonic cuisine whose names for heating and cooling may differ from Minnan.
- **Wuyi and Jian'ou (Minbei):** tea craft and Zhu Xi's academies.
- **Sanming and Yong'an (Minzhong):** mountain foods and healers; little recorded.
- **She of Ningde:** their own herbal medicine.
- **Hui-descended lineages of Quanzhou:** dietary rules and the balance they keep between two inheritances.
- **Hainanese Han of Haikou and Wenchang:** cooling foods in a hot climate.
- **Li and Miao inside the rainforest park:** land, forest and livelihood.
- **Danjia fishers of Sanya and Lingshui:** sea calendars and boat life.
- **Hakka of Longyan (a separate sub-unit):** their own building and siting traditions; note only where they borrow from Min practice.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's herbal-tea shops and the same winter-tonic custom; Chaoshan gongfu tea; Hokkien herbal halls overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, medicine and building; mixed for ecology and work time; thin for She and Li healing in their own terms, and for the rainforest people's view.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Zhu Xi and siting manuals; Mandarin; Hokkien, Fuzhou and Hainanese for markets and kitchens; Hlai and She with healers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge may be guarded; resettlement from the rainforest park; sex-ratio data touches family-planning history.
- **Held for review:** none.
