## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Fuzhou:** host of tribute envoys and seat of provincial protocol.
- **Changle:** Zheng He's harbor and today's emigrant town.
- **Quanzhou:** a port long used to foreign merchants and faiths.
- **Xiamen and Gulangyu:** treaty port, international enclave, cross-strait venue.
- **Putian and Meizhou:** temple protocol for pilgrims from abroad.
- **Anxi and the tea towns:** deal-making at the tea table.
- **Zhangzhou:** its old port of Yuegang, opened to private overseas trade in the 1560s.
- **She communities:** how elders speak for the village to officials.
- **Hui-descended lineages:** links with Muslim visitors and the foreign past of Quanzhou.
- **Li of the interior:** headmen as go-betweens, and memory of the Qing campaigns.
- **Hainanese Han of Qionghai and Wenchang:** Boao's hosts, with strong emigrant ties.
- **Utsul of Sanya:** a community with ties across the South China Sea.
- **Hakka of western Fujian (a separate sub-unit):** only as neighbors with whom Min towns bargained over hill land and markets.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan (the other side of cross-strait talks); Ryukyu and Okinawa; overseas Fuzhou and Hainanese associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for maritime and tribute history; good for present-day forums; thin for Li and She go-betweens in their own words, and for everyday politeness research in the Min languages.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Hokkien, Fuzhou and Hainanese for everyday tact; Japanese and Ryukyuan sources for comparison; Hlai and She with native speakers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cross-strait relations; South China Sea fishing and maritime claims; Qing campaigns against the Li.
- **Held for review:** none.
