## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhou and Zhangzhou lineage villages:** feuds ended by written terms; who signed and who lost out.
- **Xiamen:** a city that wrote mediation into local law.
- **Fuzhou and Mindong:** emigrant families where quarrels may be kept quiet to protect remittances.
- **Putian:** temple processions as occasions when old quarrels flare or are smoothed.
- **Hui'an coast:** outsiders' portraits of its women as enduring, to test against the women's own accounts.
- **Minbei and Minzhong:** mountain counties with few records.
- **She of Ningde:** elders' settlements and village harmony.
- **Hui-descended lineages:** keeping peace across a religious line with Han neighbors.
- **Li of Baisha and the interior:** the 1943 rising and older reconciliation customs.
- **Hainanese Han:** village-god committees as peacekeepers.
- **Danjia and Utsul communities:** small groups whose relations with larger neighbors may have depended on giving way.
- **Hakka of western Fujian (a separate sub-unit):** only where feuds with Min-speaking neighbors closed with written terms.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwan's lineage feuds and settlements; Chaoshan; Southeast Asian Hokkien clan associations' internal arbitration.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing feud settlements and mediation law; mixed for present-day silence in families; thin for Li and She settlement in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Hokkien for opera and village speech; Fuzhou and Hainanese for interviews; Hlai and She with native speakers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence and family secrets; complaints and petitioning are politically sensitive; the Li uprising's memory; survivors must be approached with care.
- **Held for review:** none.
