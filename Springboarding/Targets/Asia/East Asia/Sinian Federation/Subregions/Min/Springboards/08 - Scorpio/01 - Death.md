## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhou against Zhangzhou Minnan:** different funeral music, lament forms and reburial habits to check.
- **Fuzhou and Fuqing (Eastern Min):** their own language for death and a distinct sequence of the sevens.
- **Putian–Xianyou (Puxian):** temple-centered rites for the dead in a dense ritual landscape.
- **Inland Minbei and Minzhong:** less documented; Neo-Confucian funeral models may weigh more.
- **Quanzhou Hui lineages:** Islamic burial memory beside Han ancestral rites.
- **She of eastern Fujian:** their own ritual specialists for the dead.
- **Hainanese coast (Wenchang, Qionghai):** emigrant families whose dead lie abroad.
- **Li, Kim Mun and Lingao-speaking communities:** non-Han death practice under cremation pressure.
- **Utsul of Sanya:** Muslim burial on Hainan.
- **Tanka fishing families:** deaths at sea and bodies not recovered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Minnan rites (19th-century ethnography, temple life, video); mixed for Fuzhou; thin for Li, Lingao and Utsul grief in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Hokkien and Fuzhou for euphemisms and laments; classical Chinese for epitaphs; Hlai.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide; Cultural Revolution and 1950s campaign deaths; occupation-era atrocities; some rites for the dead are performed only for the family and its ritual specialists.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwanese Hokkien rites (for example, Emily Martin Ahern's work, *check*); Chaoshan funeral customs; diaspora funerals in Southeast Asia.
- **Held for review:** none.
