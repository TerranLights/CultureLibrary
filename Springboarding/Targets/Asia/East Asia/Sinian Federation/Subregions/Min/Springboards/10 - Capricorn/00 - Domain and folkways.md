## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Min (Xiamen, Quanzhou, Zhangzhou):** dense lineages, temple networks and emigrant money.
- **Eastern Min (Fuzhou, Fuqing, Changle, Ningde):** official and naval-school families of the old capital against recent mass emigration.
- **Puxian (Putian, Xianyou):** examination history and village ritual alliances.
- **Northern and Central Min (Nanping, Jian'ou, Sanming):** the inland, upland Fujian of Zhu Xi's academies.
- **Hainanese Han (Haikou, Wenchang, Qionghai):** island frontier with its own emigrant base.
- **Li (Hlai):** headmen and work groups set against imposed county administration.
- **She (northeast Fujian):** lineage rank carried by ritual objects.
- **Quanzhou Hui lineages (Chendai Ding, Baiqi Guo):** Muslim-descended lineages under Han lineage forms.
- **Smaller groups:** Lingao, Danzhou, Cun and Jun speakers; the Utsat of Sanya; Hainan's Kim Mun-speaking Miao; boat people of the Min River and Sanya.
- **Hakka of western Fujian (Longyan, Yongding):** a separate sub-unit; worth one comparison because walled communal houses put lineage authority under one roof.
- Comparison only: Taiwan (separate target), Kinmen and Matsu, Chaoshan and Leizhou (Yue), Southeast Asian Hokkien, Hainanese, Fuzhou and Henghua communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage, gazetteers and temples; mixed for Li and She voices; thin for the smaller Hainan groups. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (Fujian and Hainan as a working list for the author to confirm).
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Hokkien, Fuzhou, Puxian and Hainanese for fieldwork and opera; Hlai and She material mostly through Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Party-state authority, the Taiwan Strait and the South China Sea are restricted subjects; the classification of the Lingao, Utsat and Hainan Miao is contested; some lineage and She ritual material stays within families.
