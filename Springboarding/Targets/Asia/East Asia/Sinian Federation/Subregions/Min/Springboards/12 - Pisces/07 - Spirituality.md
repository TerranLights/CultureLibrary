## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Putian and Meizhou:** a pilgrimage hub, with locals and visiting pilgrims to separate.
- **Quanzhou:** many faiths in one city; music clubs with their own patron.
- **Xiamen:** a lay Buddhist revival and early Protestant congregations.
- **Fu'an and the Mindong uplands:** Catholic villages of long standing.
- **Fuzhou:** Gushan's Yongquan Temple 涌泉寺 and Protestant churches.
- **Wuyishan and Fuding:** mountains as sacred places, now also tourist sites.
- **She:** ancestral scrolls, and how far rites turn on them (to check).
- **Quanzhou Hui and Sanya's Utsat:** two Muslim-descended communities, one Han-speaking, one not.
- **Li of Wuzhishan and Qiongzhong:** sacred mountains and ancestor spirits.
- **Hainan Miao:** Panhu 盘瓠 ancestor devotion (to check).
- **Hainanese Han villages:** Lady Xian processions as devotion outside temples.
- **Hakka of western Fujian (separate sub-unit):** Dingguang Gufo 定光古佛 at Wuping, a Buddhist saint shared across the Min-Hakka line (to check).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Mazu, Buddhism and Catholic history; mixed for lay household practice; thin and filtered for Li, Miao and She.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Hokkien and Fuzhou (incl. romanized church texts); Hainanese; She; Hlai; Latin and Spanish for Dominican archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered churches and sects; closed Li and She rites (respect, do not research); religion surveys in China are sparse and politically shaped.
- **Held for review:** none.
