*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese, with Guanzhong, Shaanbei (Jin), Lanyin and Xining speech; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Salar and the Mongolic languages for the small peoples. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong villages against Xi'an offices**: the bluntness label may fit one and not the other.
- **Shaanbei**: folk-song and drum traditions that make loud public display ordinary.
- **Shaannan**: its Sichuan- and Hubei-leaning speech may soften requests differently.
- **Lanzhou and the Hexi Corridor**: industrial towns where workplace voice may follow the state enterprise.
- **Ningxia and Linxia Hui**: religious order and gender shape who speaks in the mosque and the market.
- **Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an**: trading peoples; haggling roles may separate them from neighbors.
- **Amdo Tibetans**: lay herders against monastic debate culture; Golok against Rebkong.
- **Women and men, young and old**: in every group, separately.
- **Tu**: wedding song exchanges between the two families *check*, a formal space for retort.
- **Kazakhs and Mongols of Haixi and Aksay**: steppe verbal arts.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Dungan communities of Kyrgyzstan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han complaint culture and media; mixed for the Hui; thin and mostly ethnographic for the Kazakhs, Tu and small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese and its dialect writing; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Arabic and Persian religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petitioning (信访) and protest are politically sensitive and records are partly closed; assertion by Tibetans and Hui toward the state is a sensitive topic and must not be inferred from thin sources; the "blunt Shaanxi person" label is a stereotype to test.
- **Held for review:** none.
