# Qin: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh and Mongolian for clan terms; Salar, Tu, Dongxiang, Bao'an and Yugur through the scholarship on them. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong, Shaanbei and Shaannan**: three answers to "which Shaanxi?", each tied to dialect and landscape.
- **Eastern Gansu against the Hexi Corridor**: an old Qin homeland against a garrison and Silk Road corridor.
- **Hui by order**: Jahriyya, Khufiyya, Qadiriyya and Yihewani adherents may name themselves first by path, not by ethnicity.
- **Amdo Tibetans by area and life**: Rebkong farmers, Golok herders, Labrang townspeople; **Yushu (Kham)** separately.
- **Tibetan-speaking Mongols of Henan County** against the Mongols of Haixi: one registered status, two languages.
- **Tu of Huzhu against Minhe**: two self-names and two languages.
- **Salar, Bao'an, Dongxiang, Yugur**: small peoples whose identity rests on contested origins.
- **Kazakhs of Aksay**: moved from Xinjiang in the 20th century; clan identity under a new home.
- **Han settlers and their children** in Qinghai and Gansu: "where are you from?" has two answers.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** the Dungan, whose self-names and Shaanxi or Gansu origins are a mirror for Hui identity at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han regional identity and Hui orders; mixed for Amdo; thin but present for each small people.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; some Arabic for Hui religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and religious identity in Tibetan and Hui areas, language policy in Tibetan schooling, and Sufi order rivalries are sensitive; identity claims must not be inferred for a whole people from one author.
- **Held for review:** none.
