## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong grain farmers** against **Xi'an salaried workers**: household division and old-age support may follow different rules.
- **Shaanbei**: the Yan'an self-reliance story at home, and new energy wealth beside it.
- **Longzhong, Dingxi and south Ningxia**: dry lands where water and aid shaped dependence.
- **Shaannan**: wetter mountain farming and out-migration; a different provisioning base.
- **Hexi oasis farmers** and **state-enterprise towns**: irrigation and the old "iron rice bowl."
- **Hui**: alms and mosque funds as a way of depending on one another.
- **Amdo herders** against **monastery residents**: household production against lay patronage.
- **Kazakhs and Mongols**: herd self-sufficiency and neighborly help.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an, Yugur**: small farming and herding communities, little studied on this concept.
- **Disabled people and elders in every group**: where help is a right, a duty or a shame.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** remittance-receiving households in Linxia and Hualong, whose income comes from kin elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policy and poverty programs (mostly official voice); mixed for household studies; thin for pastoral and small-people perspectives.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; some Arabic and Persian for Hui terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence in the political sense is highly sensitive for Tibetan areas and must stay a matter of record only; poverty discourse can stigmatize whole areas; monastic finance is a sensitive policy area.
- **Held for review:** none.
