## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong**: a long exam and scholar tradition; compare its families' schooling contests with other regions.
- **Shaanbei**: newer energy wealth may shift contests toward houses and cars.
- **Shaannan**: smaller cities, quieter rankings; test against Xi'an.
- **Dry central Gansu** (Huining, Dingxi): exam striving as a route out of poverty.
- **Ningxia and Qinghai** against Shaanxi: different exam quotas draw exam migrants; compare how locals see the contest.
- **Hui**: business rivalry and religious contests side by side.
- **Amdo Tibetans**: horse racing and archery between villages and tribes; monastic rank in its own system.
- **Mongols and Kazakhs**: steppe sport as a public stage for young men and, in kyz kuu, young women.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an, Yugur**: their own games, often displayed by the state.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Linxia and Ningxia students abroad in Arabic-speaking countries, for how the exam contest is avoided or replaced.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exams and sport events; mixed for village rivalry; thin for envy and its sanctions among minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Arabic for recitation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exam quotas by ethnicity and province are politically charged; religious contests depend on shifting rules; state showcases of minority games need the communities' own views alongside.
- **Held for review:** none.
