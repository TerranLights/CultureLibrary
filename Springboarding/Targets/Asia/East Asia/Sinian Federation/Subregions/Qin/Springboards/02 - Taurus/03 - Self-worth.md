# Qin: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Northwest dialect idiom; Amdo Tibetan (with its honorific register); Hui religious vocabulary; Kazakh, Mongolian, Tu, Salar. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is taken to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shaanbei village youth:** a well-studied local story of worth through schooling and leaving.
- **Guanzhong clan villages:** face tied to lineage and the family hall.
- **Longzhong and Dingxi exam counties:** worth staked on the college entrance exam.
- **Xihaigu and resettled villagers in Minning:** standing after moving from poverty.
- **Hui in Xi'an, Linxia, Yinchuan:** communal dignity and piety as grounds of standing; compare orders.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** the honorific register and monastic rank; herders against townspeople.
- **Kazakhs of Aksay, Mongols of Haixi:** lineage knowledge and honor words in small peoples.
- **Tu of Huzhu and Minhe:** festival roles and household standing.
- **Migrant workers and scrap collectors in Xi'an and Lanzhou:** worth claimed in a city that looks past them.
- **Laid-off Third Front and state-enterprise workers (Baoji, Tianshui, Yumen):** worth after the iron rice bowl broke.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in fiction and psychology papers for Han Shaanxi and Gansu; mixed for Hui; thin for Kazakh, Mongol, Tu and Salar self-regard.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Sima Qian), Amdo Tibetan including honorifics, Kazakh, Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insults to Hui and Tibetan communities and protests over them; suicide and depression data; ethnic hierarchy in schooling. Do not generalize one group's pride or shame into a trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
