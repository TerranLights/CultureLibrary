*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin, a Sinian Federation member state (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Northwest Mandarin and Jin speech; Amdo Tibetan; Hui register; Mongolic and Turkic minority languages. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local sense of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han**: the reputation for blunt, loud talk is a stereotype to test with recorded conversation, not to repeat.
- **Shaanbei**: sung address (*xintianyou*) as a channel for what is hard to say outright.
- **Shaannan**: Southwestern Mandarin and Sichuan-leaning speech norms.
- **Lanzhou and the Hexi Corridor**: Lanyin speech beside the Putonghua of Third Front migrants; two norms in one city.
- **Ningxia and Linxia Hui**: the jingtang register and Arabic greetings as markers of talk within the mosque community.
- **Hehuang valley** (Xining, Minhe, Ledu): daily code-switching between Chinese, Tibetan and Tu.
- **Amdo nomads and farmers**: oratory at gatherings and wedding speech, likely different by livelihood.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang and Bao'an**: small peoples who often talk to neighbors in Hezhou-type Chinese.
- **Yugur**: two languages, Turkic and Mongolic, in one people; Chinese as the bridge.
- **Haixi Mongols and Aksay Kazakhs**: languages from the steppe, cut off from their main speech areas.
- **Deaf communities in Xi'an and Lanzhou**: regional sign varieties (*check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on dialect description and language use in Chinese; thin on recorded conversation (turn-taking, pauses) in any of these languages; minority oratory mostly in small English and Chinese collections.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, Amdo Tibetan, and help with Salar, Tu and Oirat Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the place of Tibetan and Arabic in public communication is politically charged; interviews on it need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
