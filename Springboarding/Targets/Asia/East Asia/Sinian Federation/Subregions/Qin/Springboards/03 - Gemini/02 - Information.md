## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xi'an**: many universities and media outlets; the densest information market in the member state.
- **Lanzhou**: a provincial publishing center (*Duzhe*) and Third Front hub.
- **Third Front towns** (Baiyin, Jinchang, Yumen, Shaanxi's hill factories): histories of restricted information and later opening.
- **Rural Longdong, Longzhong and Xihaigu**: weaker access and stronger reliance on word of mouth and village loudspeakers (*check*).
- **Hui communities**: mosque networks and private periodicals as parallel channels, differing by Sufi order and Yihewani.
- **Amdo farmers, Amdo nomads and Khams speakers in Yushu**: radio, motorcycles, monasteries and phones as news routes, by livelihood.
- **Haixi Mongols, Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an, Yugur**: whether any news exists in their own languages, or only in Chinese and Tibetan.
- **Aksay Kazakhs**: links to Kazakh-language media from Xinjiang and beyond.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in official statistics and state press; mixed in survey data (one strong Gansu panel); thin for minority-language information and for word-of-mouth channels.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, classical Chinese, Tibetan (Old Tibetan for Dunhuang), Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** censorship, internal circulation and outside broadcasts in Tibetan and Chinese are sensitive; Third Front and weapons history touches state secrets. Some Tibetan and Hui knowledge is restricted by teacher or order; respect those limits.
- **Held for review:** none.
