## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong**: the long academy tradition and dense schooling; test how far book learning sets status.
- **Shaanbei**: crafts such as paper-cutting and song taught at home, and the Yan'an legacy in adult learning.
- **Shaannan**: hill farming and out-migration; many children raised by grandparents.
- **Longdong, Longzhong and the Hexi Corridor**: dry farming, oasis towns and different migration patterns.
- **Ningxia's north and Xihaigu**: relocated villages and the schools that came with them.
- **Hui of Linxia, Ningxia and Xi'an**: mosque study beside state school, by order and gender.
- **Amdo farmers and nomads**: monastic, school and herding paths; Khams speakers in Yushu as a third case.
- **Tu of Huzhu and Minhe**: craft learning and language loss to test.
- **Salar, Dongxiang and Bao'an**: Muslim Mongolic and Turkic peoples where mosque and state learning meet; Bao'an knife-making as a trade (*check*).
- **Yugur, Haixi Mongols and Aksay Kazakhs**: small populations and the problem of schooling in their own languages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han schooling and rural-development studies; mixed for crafts (heritage listings, thin ethnography); thin and politically filtered for mosque and monastic learning.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (with classical), Amdo Tibetan, Arabic and Persian for Hui texts, plus Tu, Mongolian and Kazakh help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-language schooling, boarding schools for minority children and religious study by minors are politically charged; some Tibetan teachings pass only from teacher to initiate.
- **Held for review:** none.
