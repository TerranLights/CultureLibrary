## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lintong and Guanzhong villages**: people who live on top of buried sites and whose fields turn up finds.
- **Xi'an's urban public**: museums, science centers and visitors; a mass audience for the past.
- **Dunhuang and the Hexi Corridor**: long contact with travelers and expeditions, and how outsiders' curiosity is remembered.
- **Haixi, Jiuquan and Lenghu**: space, sky and desert as new frontiers for visitors and locals.
- **Rural Longdong and Xihaigu**: what questions are asked of strangers and returning migrants.
- **Hui**: curiosity about the Muslim world through study and pilgrimage, against suspicion of the new; varies by order.
- **Amdo Tibetans**: monastic learning against 1980s modernizing writers; nomads and farmers apart.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an and Yugur**: small peoples who are often the objects of tourist and research curiosity; their own limits on what may be asked.
- **Haixi Mongols and Aksay Kazakhs**: riddle and question games in their own languages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exploration history, archaeology and museums; thin for everyday curiosity and nosiness; minority riddle and question lore sits in folklore collections.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (with classical), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foreign removal of Dunhuang manuscripts is a live grievance; crime cases involve victims' families; some Tibetan and Hui knowledge is not open to every asker.
- **Held for review:** none.
