*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin, a Sinian Federation member state (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (modern and classical), Guanzhong and Shaanbei colloquial; Hui usage; Amdo Tibetan; Salar; Mongolian; Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han**: household division among brothers and seniority in dense villages.
- **Shaanbei Han**: out-migration and the brother-sister words of folk song.
- **Urban Han in Xi'an and Lanzhou**: the largest share of only children; cousins in the place of siblings (*check*).
- **Ningxia and Gansu Hui**: larger families in some areas, faith brotherhood, Sufi orders.
- **Salar**: named kin groups that set who counts as a brother.
- **Dongxiang and Bao'an**: Muslim Mongolic peoples with their own kin terms.
- **Amdo farmers and nomads**: *pha spun* groups and tent households; whether practices differ by livelihood.
- **Tu**: kin terms shaped by three neighboring languages.
- **Haixi Mongols, Kazakhs and Yugur**: elder-younger ranking in steppe kinship terms.
- **Relocated families from Xihaigu**: brothers settled in different places.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han household division and elder care; mixed for Hui; thin for small peoples' kin terms outside Chinese ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (with classical), Amdo Tibetan, Salar, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-limit policy, sex ratios and minority fertility are politically sensitive; sworn-brotherhood societies carry a criminal label in official sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
