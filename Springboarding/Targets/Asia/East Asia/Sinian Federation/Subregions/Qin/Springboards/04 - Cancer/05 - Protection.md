# Qin: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and northern colloquial forms; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Hui religious vocabulary; Tu and Salar through local scholarship. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong villages:** door gods and walled compounds against modern gated estates in Xi'an.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** cave homes and earthquake risk; paper-cut protective figures.
- **Hexi Corridor and Minqin:** guarding farmland from sand and drought.
- **Ningxia and southern Gansu Hui:** whether martial-art schools and the mosque community act as guardians.
- **Amdo farming villages:** village deities, and who performs the festival duties.
- **Amdo and Yushu herders:** dogs, night watch and camp placement against wolves, theft and weather.
- **Tu, Salar and Bao'an:** ask which guardian rites each keeps; little is in English.
- **Kazakhs and Mongols:** amulets and guardian spirits in pastoral households.
- **Urban families:** apps, cameras and anti-abduction worry.
- **Rangers and conservation workers:** state and local guards of land and wildlife in Qinghai.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk art and disaster history; mixed on felt safety (official polls only); thin on Tu, Salar and Bao'an protective practice.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Ming texts), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** village deity cults and Hui self-defense history are politically sensitive; Tibetan protector deities have restricted rites; civilian arms are a legal question (Libra and Capricorn). Some protective rites are not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
