# Qin: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (with classical Chinese for Han to Qing records); Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Hui religious loanwords. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xi'an:** refugee-built quarters and modern aid stations in one city.
- **Guanzhong farm villages:** hosts of seasonal harvesters from Gansu.
- **Longdong and central Gansu:** the home places of the harvesters and of famine flight.
- **Hexi Corridor:** relay stations of the Silk Road; dugouts of later settlers.
- **Ningxia and Xihaigu:** resettled villages and Xihaigu told as a religious refuge.
- **Linxia and Xining Hui:** mosque lodging for travelers and students.
- **Amdo and Yushu Tibetans:** monasteries, pilgrims and tents as places of refuge.
- **Mongols and Kazakhs:** yurt hospitality and winter livestock shelter.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang and Bao'an villages:** survivors of the 2023 Jishishan earthquake, and who took them in.
- **Urban homeless people and migrant workers:** construction-site sheds, aid stations and night shelters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in historical gazetteers and disaster reporting; mixed on present-day homelessness (official counts only); thin on minority hospitality customs.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Han slips and gazetteers), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh; Arabic and Persian terms for Hui usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Jiabiangou and the Great Famine; Jahriyya history; the state's handling of vagrants; disaster-recovery reporting may be one-sided.
- **Held for review:** none.
