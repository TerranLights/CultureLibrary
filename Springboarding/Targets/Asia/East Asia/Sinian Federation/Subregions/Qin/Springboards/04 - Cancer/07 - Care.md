# Qin: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and colloquial care vocabulary; Amdo Tibetan, including the vocabulary of Tibetan medicine; Hui charitable terms; Mongolian; Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xi'an and Lanzhou families:** paid aides and nursing homes against home care by kin.
- **Rural Guanzhong and Longdong:** daughters-in-law, sons and daughters: who does the bodily work.
- **Shaannan and Xihaigu:** ask who is left to care when the young leave for work.
- **Hui communities:** mosque alms, Hui medicine and same-faith care for the dying.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** Tibetan doctors, monks and family as separate carers.
- **Tu and Salar villages:** healers and the care of elders, mostly undocumented in English.
- **Dongxiang and Bao'an:** how far care depends on reaching a clinic from mountain townships.
- **Mongol and Kazakh herders:** care at a distance across pastures; care of animals as daily work.
- **Former Third Front and state-factory families:** pensions and work-unit clinics against market care.
- **Childless elders:** the 五保 system as the state's stand-in for family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on elder care in surveys and policy research; mixed on Tibetan and Hui healing (much in Chinese and Tibetan only); thin on small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Sun Simiao), Amdo Tibetan (medical terms), Arabic and Persian loans for Hui practice, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan monastic medicine under state control; religious charity is regulated; some healing rites are private.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read 大医精诚 in a modern annotated edition.
2. Search CNKI for 农村 养老 照料 with 甘肃 or 陕西, and for 五保 敬老院 青海.
3. Check CLHLS province lists and pull caregiver variables.
4. Search 乜贴 回族 for studies of Hui giving.
5. Find a recorded Shaanxi production of 墙头记 and note what the stage mocks.
