# Qin: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (with classical Chinese for early poetry and legend); Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Hui and other minority communities through Chinese-language local sources. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong:** the waiting-wife legend as a local byword; check how it is invoked today.
- **Shaanbei:** departure songs and labor migration, heard from the side of those who stayed.
- **Shaannan and Longdong hill villages:** check for hollowed villages and elders living alone.
- **Hexi and Qaidam oil and mining towns:** whole communities left when the work ended.
- **Ningxia's industrial north:** Shizuishan and the coal economy.
- **Hui families:** mosque and kin care for orphans and the abandoned; check practice by place.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** orphans, monasteries as refuges, and boarding-school separations as felt loss.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang and Bao'an:** how small, close communities treat a member who leaves.
- **Kazakh and Mongol herders:** whether clans take in the orphaned and kinless.
- **Third Front families:** workers sent to remote factories; ask whether they feel left when their children move away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folk legend and on migration; mixed on infant abandonment (sensitive and officially thin); thin on minority customs for orphans.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Su Hui), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** infant abandonment and birth-planning history; boarding-school separation of Tibetan children; decline of state-backed towns. Survivors' and adoptees' stories need consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
