# Qin: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and northern dialect mother terms; classical Chinese for myth and ritual; Amdo Tibetan (including Buddhist vocabulary); Mongolian; Kazakh; Hui usage; Salar and Tu terms through local scholars. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** how the tie between a mother-in-law and a daughter-in-law is spoken of in village households.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** women's folk arts as a mother's and grandmother's voice.
- **Xi'an and Lanzhou urban mothers:** working mothers and one-child or two-child families.
- **Hui mothers:** whether faith is taught at home or through women's schools, by place.
- **Amdo Tibetan herding and farming mothers:** birth at home or clinic; Buddhist mother imagery.
- **Tu and Salar:** wedding and birth customs that set a mother's place, recorded mainly in Chinese.
- **Dongxiang and Bao'an:** family size and maternal health in mountain townships; ask the women themselves.
- **Mongols and Kazakhs:** songs and rites honoring mothers in pastoral families.
- **Migrant mothers:** mothers who work in the cities, and who raises their children meanwhile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on maternal health statistics and folk deities; mixed on lived motherhood (Han fieldwork); thin on Tu, Salar, Bao'an and Yugur mothers' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for the Odes), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-planning history and its different rules for minorities; Tibetan reproductive health; women's religious schooling under state limits. Childbirth customs may be private to women.
- **Held for review:** none.
