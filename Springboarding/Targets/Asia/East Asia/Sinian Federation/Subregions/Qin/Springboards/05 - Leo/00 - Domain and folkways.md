## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong** (Xi'an, Baoji): Qinqiang heartland; city against village festivity.
- **Shaanbei** (Yan'an, Yulin): Jin-speaking; yangge, waist drums, xintianyou.
- **Shaannan** (Hanzhong, Ankang): south of the Qinling, nearer Sichuan forms.
- **Longdong and Longzhong** (Qingyang, Tianshui, Lanzhou): shadow plays and shehuo.
- **Hexi Corridor** (Wuwei to Dunhuang): oasis towns, xianxiao balladry, heritage tourism.
- **Hehuang** (Xining, Haidong, Linxia): the hua'er belt, shared by several peoples.
- **Hui** (Ningxia, Linxia): views on music and festivity differ by Islamic school.
- **Dongxiang, Bao'an, Salar, Tu (Monguor)**: small peoples with their own languages and festivals.
- **Amdo and Yushu Tibetans**: monastery festivals, horse racing, love songs.
- **Mongols, Kazakhs (Aksay), Yugur (Sunan)**: pastoral games and song contests.
- **Third Front industrial towns** (Baiyin, Jinchang): migrant-built leisure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk performance and festivals (decades of collecting, much heritage listing); mixed for attitudes, since surveys rarely report the four provinces apart; thin in English for Salar, Bao'an and Yugur.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (including dialect dictionaries), Tibetan, Mongolian; Kazakh, and specialist help for Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an and Yugur.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan religious gatherings are politically watched; Muslim views on music and dance are contested; heritage listing can turn a practice into a show, so separate staged from lived forms. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet covers the four listed provinces.
- **Held for review:** none.
