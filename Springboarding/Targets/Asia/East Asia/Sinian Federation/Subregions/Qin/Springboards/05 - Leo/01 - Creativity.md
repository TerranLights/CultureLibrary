## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** fine art and literature centered on Xi'an; academies and film.
- **Shaanbei Han:** paper-cutting and embroidery from household practice.
- **Longdong and Longnan:** shadow troupes and rural craft in poorer counties.
- **Hexi oasis towns:** heritage industry around Silk Road art, copying of murals as a skill.
- **Hui and Bao'an:** ornament without figures; brick carving, metal craft.
- **Tu and Rebkong Tibetans:** monastery-linked painting lineages.
- **Golog and Yushu pastoralists:** craft tied to mobile life (tents, saddles, silverwork).
- **Kazakhs and Mongols:** felt and embroidery, wood and leather work.
- **Urban youth in Xi'an and Lanzhou:** design, games and online making.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for heritage crafts, thanks to heritage listing; thinner for everyday improvising and for attitudes toward originality.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Tibetan for Regong painters' own manuals and interviews; Mongolian and Kazakh for pastoral crafts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred images and monastery art have religious rules on who may make them; heritage designation shapes what makers say to researchers.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search CNKI for 热贡艺术 传承 创新 and list fieldwork on Wutun painters' training and family lines.
2. Find histories of Huxian peasant painting (户县农民画 历史) and separate the 1970s political phase from the market phase.
3. Pull the Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia and Qinghai provincial heritage-bearer lists and count crafts by people and county.
4. Search for studies of Ku Shulan and Ansai paper-cut (库淑兰 剪纸 研究) to see how a single maker is credited.
5. Read the 《考工记》 in a modern annotated edition for its terms of craft and skill.
