# Qin: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; lands Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and the Guanzhong, Lanzhou and Xining dialects; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Arabic and Persian loanwords in Hui religious speech. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong:** imperial-past pride, centered on Xi'an.
- **Shaanbei:** revolutionary heritage pride against its rural poverty.
- **Shaannan:** a southern identity that may not share Guanzhong's capital story.
- **Gansu's Longzhong and Longdong:** a province often portrayed as poor; pride and its wounds.
- **Hexi Corridor:** Silk Road pride, Dunhuang and Wuwei as claims to world standing.
- **Ningxia Hui and Han:** Western Xia heritage, Hui identity, migrant farmers moved from the dry south.
- **Qinghai Han, Hui and Salar in Xining and Xunhua:** trading towns and their reputations.
- **Amdo and Yushu Tibetans:** pride in language, epic and monastery, under political limits.
- **Tu, Mongols, Kazakhs, Yugur:** small peoples whose pride is tied to language survival.
- **Third Front families:** migrants from eastern cities, often with a different sense of home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Heritage branding and media are rich; survey data on pride by province is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; some Arabic and Persian for Hui teaching texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic pride among Tibetans and Muslims can be framed by the state as separatism; LGBTQ organizing is restricted; separate official from lived pride.
- **Held for review:** none.
