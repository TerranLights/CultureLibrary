## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong scholar families:** a long memory of exam degrees and academy learning.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong villages:** honor boards, admission banquets and model-worker pride in poor counties.
- **Work-unit cities** (Lanzhou, Baiyin, Xi'an's industrial districts): recognition through the danwei.
- **Hui of Linxia and Ningxia:** recognition by mosque, pilgrimage and Sufi order, beside state honors.
- **Dongxiang, Salar, Bao'an:** small peoples recognized as nationalities since the 1950s.
- **Tu and Yugur:** recognition of language and identity as distinct peoples.
- **Amdo and Yushu Tibetans:** monastic titles and the reincarnation question.
- **Mongols and Kazakhs:** contest titles in pastoral sport and song.
- **Women:** whose work is named on boards and in gazetteers, and whose is left out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state honors and gazetteers; mixed for everyday recognition; thin for workplace data by province.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for gazetteers; Chinese; Tibetan; some Arabic and Persian for Sufi texts; Mongolian and Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reincarnation is among the most sensitive topics in Tibetan areas; Jiabiangou and other past campaigns are partly censored; Sufi shrines have faced demolition.
- **Held for review:** none.
