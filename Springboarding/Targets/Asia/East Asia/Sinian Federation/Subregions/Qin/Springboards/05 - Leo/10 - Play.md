## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong city and village children:** urban playgrounds against courtyard and street play.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** grid games, knucklebones, play on the loess.
- **Shaannan:** wetter, hilly south; river and mountain play.
- **Lanzhou and Xining adults:** riverbank tea, cards and picnics.
- **Hexi oasis towns:** desert-edge play and tourist entertainments.
- **Hui, Dongxiang, Salar, Bao'an:** wooden ball, foot-fighting, and Islamic views on games of chance.
- **Tu:** festival swings and circle dances.
- **Amdo and Yushu Tibetans:** dice, horse games, summer picnics.
- **Kazakhs, Mongols, Yugur:** knucklebones and horseback games.
- **Left-behind children:** play without parents present.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festival and minority games (state records); thin for everyday adult play and children's own lore in recent decades.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese and Classical Chinese; Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; specialists for Tu and Salar terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling is illegal outside state lotteries, so games played for money are underreported; monastic rules on games may be private.
- **Held for review:** none.
