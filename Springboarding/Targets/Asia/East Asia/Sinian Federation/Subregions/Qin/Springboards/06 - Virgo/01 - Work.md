# Qin: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese with Guanzhong, Lanyin and Shaanbei Jin vocabulary; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Salar, Monguor, Dongxiang, Bonan and Yugur where sources exist. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong farmers and Xi'an's urban workforce:** grain-belt tradition against a large city of factories, universities and tourism.
- **Shaanbei:** dry-farm loess households against new coal, oil and gas employment.
- **Shaannan:** paddy, tea and forest work, closer in rhythm to the Sichuan basin.
- **Longdong and central Gansu:** among the heaviest sources of out-migrant labor; leaving home as the normal path.
- **Hexi Corridor:** oasis farming, state farms, Jiuquan steel and Jinchang nickel.
- **Ningxia plain vs. Xihaigu:** irrigated farming and industry vs. resettlement and migrant work.
- **Hui (Linxia, Ningxia, Xining):** trade, transport, restaurants and leather; commerce held in religious frames.
- **Salar, Dongxiang and Bao'an:** small Muslim peoples with distinct trades (Bao'an knife-making is one to check).
- **Amdo Tibetans:** herding, fungus digging, monastic economies, thangka painting.
- **Tu (Huzhu), Yugur (Sunan), Mongols (Henan, Haixi), Kazakhs (Aksay):** mixed farming and herding, each with its own division of tasks.
- **Women's unpaid work:** across all groups; and left-behind elders and children in migrant-sending villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official employment data and Han literature; mixed for Hui and Tibetan livelihoods (mostly Chinese-language and Western ethnography); thin for Bonan, Yugur and Kazakh work vocabulary.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, classical Chinese, Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian and Turkic sources for small peoples.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official figures on poverty-alleviation labor transfers and resettlement are contested; Tibetan and Muslim livelihoods are politically sensitive; labor law and contracts go to Libra, and duty and discipline to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
