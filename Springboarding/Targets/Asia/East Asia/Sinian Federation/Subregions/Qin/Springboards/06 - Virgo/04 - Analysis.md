## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong and Xi'an:** dense universities, defense and aerospace research; the most visible analytic professions.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** wheat and millet dry farming; a test of the wheat-zone thinking-style claim.
- **Shaannan:** rice-growing valleys, the within-province contrast that the rice-wheat frame predicts.
- **Lanzhou and the Hexi Corridor:** petrochemical, nuclear and space-related industry; engineers who came from elsewhere.
- **Ningxia:** irrigation engineering on the plain vs. dry-land farming in the south.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** monastic logic and calculation; herders' practical observation; secular Tibetan-medium schooling.
- **Hui and Salar:** mosque learning in Arabic and Persian texts; trade reckoning.
- **Tu, Yugur, Mongols, Kazakhs:** small communities with their own herding and weather knowledge, studied little in their own languages.
- **Gender:** who in a family keeps accounts and works things out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chinese history of science and university histories; mixed for minority reasoning traditions; thin for everyday reckoning in Monguor, Salar or Kazakh.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; literary Tibetan (logic texts are technical); Arabic and Persian; Mongolian and Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** defense and nuclear research sites are restricted; monastic curricula are under state oversight; the rice-wheat thesis is debated and must not be read as a fixed trait of any people.
- **Held for review:** none.
