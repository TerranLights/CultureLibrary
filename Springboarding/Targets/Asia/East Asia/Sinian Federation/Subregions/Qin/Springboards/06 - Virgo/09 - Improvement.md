## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong:** heirs to the oldest state-reform tradition; peri-urban villages swallowed by Xi'an's growth.
- **Shaanbei:** loess farmers under Grain for Green; coal money remaking towns.
- **Shaannan:** relocated mountain households.
- **Longdong and Longzhong:** dry-farm poverty, out-migration and education as the route up.
- **Hexi Corridor:** desert-control villages (Gulang, Minqin) and oasis water fights.
- **Ningxia:** Xihaigu resettlement to the irrigated north; Hui families' schooling and trade.
- **Linxia Hui and Dongxiang:** private religious-and-Arabic schooling; trade-led rise.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders and monks:** resettlement, the Lamrim path, monastery-run schools.
- **Tu, Yugur, Salar, Bao'an:** language renewal and small-people development projects.
- **Mongols and Kazakhs** of Haixi, Henan and Aksay: grassland policy and herding change.
- **Urban middle class** in Xi'an and Lanzhou: study, fitness and house buying as betterment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state development programs (mostly official); mixed for independent studies of resettlement; thin for minority-language accounts of their own renewal.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese and classical Chinese; Tibetan; Monguor, Yugur, Salar; Mongolian, Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** resettlement, poverty statistics and grassland policy in Tibetan and Muslim areas are politically sensitive; independent fieldwork may be blocked; give the voices of the moved first. Reform law goes to Libra; state authority to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
