# Qin: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and Classical Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; Hui usage of Arabic and Persian terms. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** the densest court record and the judge-play stage; compare the opera's justice with the archive's.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong Han:** mass trials and mediation in the Border Region, then reform-era courts.
- **Ningxia Hui (Xihaigu against the Yinchuan plain) and Linxia Hui:** sect memory of punishment, and the ahong's role in wrongs.
- **Salar of Xunhua:** the 1781 rising and its suppression as a justice memory *check*.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders (Golog, Yushu, Huangnan)** against **Amdo farmers (Hualong, Ledu)**: blood price is reported mainly among herders.
- **Mongols of Henan county and Haixi:** a Mongol-Tibetan borderland with its own compensation customs.
- **Aksay Kazakhs:** the *qūn* tradition carried from Xinjiang.
- **Tu (Huzhu, Minhe) and Dongxiang:** small communities, likely settled in villages and through clergy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical and Border Region law; mixed for blood-price practice; thin for Tu, Dongxiang, Bao'an, Yugur and Aksay Kazakhs.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; Tangut via Chinese translation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Qing-era Hui and Salar risings, the Jahriyya, Tibetan customary law against the state, petitioning (上访) and death-penalty data are politically sensitive; the judgment database has been pared. Approach blood-price research through published scholarship before any fieldwork.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: Hulsewé's translation of the Shuihudi slips, then the Book of Lord Shang (Pines), noting the punishment scale.
2. CNKI: "赔命价 青海 OR 甘南" and "番例 青海", sorted by citations.
3. Shaanxi Provincial Archives catalog: "陕甘宁边区高等法院"; list the case types held.
4. China Judgments Online: search "赔命价" and "调解 寺院" filtered to Qinghai and Gansu courts.
5. Watch *The Story of Qiu Ju*, and gather Yisushe Bao Gong recordings, logging the vocabulary of 说法, 冤 and 青天.
