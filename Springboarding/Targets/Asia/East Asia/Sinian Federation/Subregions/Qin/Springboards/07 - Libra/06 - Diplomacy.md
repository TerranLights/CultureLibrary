## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** the capital's long tradition of receiving envoys; city elites as hosts.
- **Shaannan Han:** Zhang Qian's home ground; a borderland toward Sichuan and Hubei.
- **Hexi Corridor Han:** frontier stations and oasis towns where envoys passed.
- **Hehuang Han and Hui of Xining and Linxia:** brokers between Tibetan, Mongol and Chinese worlds.
- **Ningxia Hui:** Arabic-language links and their official framing.
- **Amdo Tibetans (Labrang, Rebkong, Golog):** monasteries and tribes as negotiators with states.
- **Mongols of Qinghai:** princely leagues, banners and Qing terms.
- **Tu, Salar, Bao'an, Dongxiang:** small peoples in the middle of contact zones; mixed-language speakers of Wutun and Tangwang.
- **Aksay Kazakhs and Sunan Yugur:** herders who arrived or stayed through settlements with stronger neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical and Qing sources; mixed for brokers; thin for everyday tact among the small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian (classical script for the 1640 code); Kazakh; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tang-Tibet and Sakya-Mongol history carries rival sovereignty readings; give each side's own sources. Ekvall wrote as a missionary; read critically.
- **Held for review:** none.
