# Qin: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and Classical Chinese; Hui usage of Arabic and Persian terms; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; Tu through its dictionaries. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** the compact tradition and dense gift ledgers.
- **Shaanbei Han:** labor exchange as custom and as policy.
- **Longdong and Hexi Han:** village *shehuo* visits and gift spending in poorer counties.
- **Shaannan Han:** a separate gift and banquet calendar, to be checked.
- **Hui of Ningxia, Linxia and Xi'an:** giving under religious rules.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** returns owed to deities, monasteries and tribes; herders against farmers.
- **Tu of Minhe and Huzhu:** Nadun hosting and Rebkong rites.
- **Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an:** Muslim minorities with their own exchange customs.
- **Aksay Kazakhs, Haixi Mongols and Sunan Yugur:** guest-right among herders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Dunhuang, the compact and Border Region policy; mixed for gift ledgers; thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Bao'an practice.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Arabic and Persian loanwords; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh; Mongolian; Tu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gift caps are part of official campaigns; read them as written rules, not as findings on practice. Rites to local deities may have parts outsiders may not record.
- **Held for review:** none.
