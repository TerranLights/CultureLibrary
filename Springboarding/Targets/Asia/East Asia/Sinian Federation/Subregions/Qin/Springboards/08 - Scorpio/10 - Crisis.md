*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Chinese (Guanzhong and Gansu dialect words for famine and disaster; classical Chinese), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian; Bao'an, Dongxiang and Salar for survivors' accounts. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** the 1556 earthquake and the 1928-1930 famine in Wei plain memory.
- **Longzhong and Longdong Han:** Tongwei, Dingxi and neighboring counties named in histories of the 1959-1961 famine.
- **Hexi Han:** drought and sand at Minqin; outmigration.
- **Henan-origin Xi'an people:** a community traced to the 1942 flight (*check*).
- **Ningxia Hui:** the 1920 Haiyuan quake and drought in Xihaigu.
- **Bao'an, Dongxiang, Salar:** the 2023 Jishishan quake.
- **Gannan Tibetans:** the 2010 Zhouqu disaster; herders' winter-storm losses (雪灾).
- **Yushu and Golog Tibetans:** earthquake and snow-disaster memory on the plateau.
- **Industrial workers of Lanzhou and Baiyin:** economic crisis rather than natural.
- **Urban Xi'an residents:** the 2021-2022 lockdown.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for earthquakes, the Great Famine (outside the mainland) and the Xi'an lockdown; mixed for the 1928-1930 famine; thin for herders' snow disasters.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (dialect famine terms, classical for Ming records), Amdo Tibetan, Henan dialect for Daobei; minority languages for Jishishan survivors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1959-1961 famine, lockdown failures and Tibetan disaster politics are guarded; survivors of recent disasters need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
