# Qin: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; lands Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Northwest dialect wording; classical Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Salar; Hui Islamic vocabulary; Mongolian, Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han**: the Chang'an departure story; a destination for domestic tourists.
- **Shaanbei**: coal-town wealth and outmigration; road trips to Inner Mongolia.
- **Shaannan**: rural outmigration to Xi'an and the south.
- **Hexi Corridor**: Silk Road heritage towns living on tourism.
- **Longdong and Tianshui**: migrant-sending farm districts.
- **Hui** of Linxia, Hualong and Ningxia: traders, noodle migrants and hajjis.
- **Salar**: travel in their origin story and in present trade.
- **Dongxiang** and **Bao'an**: trade and wage migration; Bao'an knife-making towns.
- **Amdo Tibetans**: pilgrims and herders' seasonal moves; hosts to tourists in Xiahe and Rebkong.
- **Mongols** of Haixi (with a seventeenth-century arrival from the north and west), **Kazakhs** of Aksay (twentieth-century arrival), **Yugur** of Sunan.
- **Tu**: hosts in tourism villages of Huzhu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Silk Road heritage, tourism and labor migration; mixed for hajj and Tibetan pilgrimage; thin for Yugur, Mongol and Kazakh travel lore.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical for Han and Tang sources), Amdo Tibetan, Salar, Mongolian, Kazakh; Arabic for hajj material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** travel by Tibetans and Muslims (passports, hajj quotas, movement to Lhasa) is policed and sensitive; protect informants. No law research here.
- **Held for review:** none.
