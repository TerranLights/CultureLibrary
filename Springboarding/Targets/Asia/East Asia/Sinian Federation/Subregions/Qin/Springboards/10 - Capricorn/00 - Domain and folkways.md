## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong** (Xi'an, Wei valley): old imperial core with clan and compact traditions.
- **Shaanbei** (Yan'an, Yulin): Jin-speaking uplands, a revolutionary base; a different story of who leads.
- **Shaannan** (Hanzhong, Ankang, Shangluo): Southwestern Mandarin, migrant mountain settlement.
- **Longdong, Longzhong and the Hexi Corridor**: dry-farming Gansu against garrison-born oasis towns.
- **Ningxia's irrigated north and upland south (Xihaigu)**.
- **Hui**, by Sufi order or school (Khufiyya, Jahriyya, Qadiriyya, Yihewani, Salafiyya): each has its own line of leaders.
- **Amdo Tibetans**: herders, farmers and monastery towns (Rebkong, Labrang, Kumbum).
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an**: Hehuang peoples with their own clan and religious offices.
- **Yugur, Mongols (Henan, Haixi, Subei), Kazakhs (Aksay)**: pastoral minorities with separate rank traditions.
- **State-farm and migrant Han** in Qinghai and the Hexi oases: authority shaped by the work unit.
- The federation only briefly: compare with other member states, not an assumed single "Sinian" norm.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Guanzhong, Yan'an history and Han-written sources; mixed for Hui and Amdo; thin for Yugur, Bao'an, Kazakh and Subei Mongols. National surveys carry few Qinghai and Ningxia cases.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical and modern), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian, Kazakh; the small Turkic and Mongolic languages mostly through Chinese-language ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state authority, ethnic policy, religious leadership and monasteries are politically sensitive; the Northwest Muslim rebellions and 20th-century violence are contested memory. Some Sufi lineage knowledge stays within the order.
- **Scope:** Qin's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sheet is written for the four provinces listed.
- **Held for review:** none.
