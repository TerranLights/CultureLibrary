# Qin: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and the Shaanxi, Gansu and Ningxia Mandarin and Jin dialects; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Monguor, Salar and Dongxiang through Chinese-language studies; Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong clan villages and Xi'an's work units:** lineage elders against bureau and enterprise leaders.
- **Shaanbei:** authority rooted in revolutionary memory, temple committees and village cadres; worth separating from the Wei valley.
- **Shaannan:** mountain counties settled by migrants, with weaker lineages; a test of who fills the gap.
- **Hui of Linxia, Ningxia and Xi'an:** authority split among Sufi masters, mosque imams and reformist schools, so record order by order.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders and monastery towns:** reincarnate teachers, tribal headmen and township officials side by side.
- **Tu of Huzhu and Minhe:** a remembered chieftain system against present village offices.
- **Salar of Xunhua and Dongxiang of Linxia:** clan and mosque offices among Turkic- and Mongolic-speaking Muslims; little studied, so keep separate.
- **Mongols of Henan and Haixi, Kazakhs of Aksay:** banner and elder traditions against county government.
- **Hexi state farms and mining towns** (Jinchang, Jiayuguan): enterprise authority over settler families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Yan'an-era authority and for Labrang and Xi'an Hui; mixed for Guanzhong lineages; thin for Salar, Bao'an, Yugur and Aksay Kazakh authority.
- **Languages needed:** classical and modern Chinese; Amdo Tibetan for monastery and tribal records; Arabic and Persian terms used in Hui religious life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** questions about obedience to the state, reincarnate lineages and Sufi leadership are politically sensitive; informants may not speak freely. Some Sufi and monastic knowledge is given only to initiates.
- **Held for review:** none.
