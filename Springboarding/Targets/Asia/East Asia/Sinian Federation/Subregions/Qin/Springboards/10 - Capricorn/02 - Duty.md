# Qin: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and local Mandarin and Jin speech; the Hui religious vocabulary (Arabic and Persian loans); Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Tu and Salar through Chinese-language studies. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong lineage villages:** duties set by compact and ancestral hall; compare with migrant-built Shaannan, where kin are fewer.
- **Shaanbei loess villages:** duty shaped by poverty, out-migration and revolutionary campaigns.
- **Longzhong dry farmers and Ningxia's Xihaigu:** households where remittances carry family duty; separate from irrigated plains.
- **Hui of Linxia, Ningxia and Xi'an:** religious obligations differ by Sufi order and by reformist school, so compare them, not one Hui pattern.
- **Salar and Dongxiang:** Muslim peoples with their own clan obligations; compare with neighboring Hui.
- **Tu of Huzhu and Minhe:** village ritual rosters; Minhe and Huzhu may differ.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders and farmers:** duty to lamas, mountain deities and the tribe; herders and farming villages separately.
- **Mongols of Haixi and Henan, Kazakhs of Aksay, Yugur of Sunan:** hospitality and kin obligations in pastoral life.
- **Third Front and state-farm families:** duty to the work unit and the nation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han family obligation and Yan'an campaigns; mixed for Hui religious duty (Chinese sources plenty, own voices fewer); thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Bao'an.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for compacts and slips; Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Arabic and Persian terms used in Hui life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Third Front, sent-down youth and campaign histories touch party narratives; some village rites in Rebkong are closed to outsiders or to women; religious giving is politically watched. Military service records are restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
