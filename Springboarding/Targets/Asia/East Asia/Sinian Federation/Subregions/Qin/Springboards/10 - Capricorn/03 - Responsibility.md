## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong villages and Xi'an offices:** lineage answerability against bureaucratic accountability chains.
- **Shaanbei and Ningdong coal towns:** mine safety makes blame after accidents a live public question.
- **Shaannan's mountain townships:** small local governments facing petitions and disaster risk.
- **Hexi oases and Minqin:** responsibility for water and sand, shared by farmers and the state.
- **Xihaigu and Longzhong:** labor migration leaves children and elders in charge at home.
- **Hui of Linxia and Ningxia:** religious answerability and community settlement, varying by order and school.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders:** tribal settlement of harm and karmic language; herders and farmers may differ.
- **Salar, Bao'an and Dongxiang of the Jishishan area:** a recent disaster zone; small peoples whose own terms are little recorded.
- **Mongols of Haixi and Kazakhs of Aksay:** pastoral responsibility for herds and pasture under contracts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official accountability and disaster reports; mixed for customary settlement; thin for Salar, Bao'an, Mongol and Kazakh terms.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; modern Chinese and slang; Amdo Tibetan; Arabic and Persian terms in Hui religious life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official accountability, petitions and disaster blame are sensitive; customary blood-price settlement sits uneasily with state law, so informants may be cautious.
- **Held for review:** none.
