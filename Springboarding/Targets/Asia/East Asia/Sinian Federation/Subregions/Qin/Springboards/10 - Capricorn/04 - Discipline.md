## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong families and Xi'an schools:** strict urban schooling and older Confucian norms of self-command.
- **Huining, Dingxi and other Longzhong counties:** exam discipline as a poverty-escape strategy.
- **Shaanbei drum and yangge villages:** group discipline in performance, separate from school discipline.
- **Hexi Corridor:** labor-camp memory and state farms; compare memory and present practice.
- **Hui of Linxia, Ningxia and Xi'an:** mosque-school regimes and fasting, varying by order and school.
- **Amdo Tibetan monks and lay herders:** monastic rule against lay life; Rebkong craft training separately.
- **Tu of Huzhu and Minhe:** Buddhist and folk practice among a Mongolic people; compare with Amdo neighbors.
- **Salar and Dongxiang:** Muslim discipline in tight clan villages; little recorded in English.
- **Kazakhs of Aksay and Mongols of Haixi:** herding and horse training as discipline of the body.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han schooling and Yan'an history; mixed for monastic and mosque discipline; thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Bao'an practice.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese; Amdo Tibetan and classical Tibetan for charters; Arabic and Persian terms in Hui education.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Rectification, labor camps and reform through labor are politically sensitive; Gao Hua's book is not sold on the mainland. Religious schooling of minors and fasting by students and officials are restricted and watched. Monastic internal rules may not be shared with outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
