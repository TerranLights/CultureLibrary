## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xi'an's urban middle class:** exam and credential competition at its sharpest.
- **Guanzhong farm counties:** a long record of exam degree holders; does that memory shape present choices?
- **Shaanbei and Shaannan:** exam-poor in the imperial record; whether a different route (army, migration) stood in for merit.
- **Longzhong and Xihaigu:** exam schooling as the poor's ladder, and resentment of exam migrants.
- **Hexi oil and mining towns:** honor by output in state enterprises; separate from farm counties.
- **Hui of Linxia, Ningxia and Xi'an:** religious learning against secular schooling; each order may judge merit differently.
- **Salar and Dongxiang:** small peoples who benefit from minority preferences, a live debate to record from both sides.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** monastic degree, Tibetan-medium schooling, and contest honors as separate ladders.
- **Kazakhs, Mongols and Yugur:** skill contests in pastoral life against state schooling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exams old and new; mixed for monastic and mosque merit; thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Bao'an contests.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Qin statutes and exam lists; Chinese; Amdo and classical Tibetan; Arabic and Persian terms in Hui schooling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority bonus points and regional quotas are contested and politically delicate; record all sides. Monastic degree exams are regulated by the state.
- **Held for review:** none.
- **Term note:** Buddhist "merit" (བསོད་ནམས་, 功德) is a different idea; keep it apart from this concept.
