# Qin: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** classical and Standard Chinese; Hui religious-legal terms; Amdo and classical Tibetan; Mongolian (including Oirat legal history); Kazakh. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them; under Capricorn, the *spirit* only: what law is for, whose it is, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it (the letter of the law goes to Libra).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong:** home of Qin and Han law and of the Qiu Ju story; state law closest at hand.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** Border Region mediation as a living memory.
- **Shaannan:** mountain counties far from courts; private settlement worth testing.
- **Hexi Corridor:** oasis towns and a labor-camp past; what law meant to the sent and their guards.
- **Ningxia and Linxia Hui:** state, sharia and order or school all in play; record each order.
- **Salar and Dongxiang:** Muslim clan settlement; little studied in English.
- **Amdo Tibetan herders (Golog, Gannan) and farmers (Rebkong):** customary and religious law beside courts; herders and farmers may differ.
- **Qinghai Mongols:** an Oirat legal heritage now mostly remembered.
- **Kazakhs of Aksay:** elders' customary law under a county court.
- **Tu and Yugur:** small peoples between Tibetan, Mongol and Han legal worlds.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for early imperial law and Border Region justice; mixed for Islamic and Tibetan customary law; thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Salar legal culture.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Chinese; classical and Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian and Oirat sources; Kazakh; Arabic for Hui legal texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** customary and religious law sit uneasily with state law, and studies may be shaped by policy; courts, petitions and religious courts are politically sensitive. This sheet covers the spirit of the law only; the letter goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
