# Qin: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** classical and Standard Chinese, Shaanxi and Gansu dialect words; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Hui, Tu and Salar sources mostly through Chinese. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xi'an:** a planned grid, walled center and heavy campaigning for civic order.
- **Guanzhong villages:** courtyard and lineage order; compare preserved villages with rebuilt ones.
- **Shaanbei:** temple associations and lantern rites as local order-keepers.
- **Shaannan:** mountain settlement with looser village plans; flood and landslide disruption.
- **Longdong and the Hexi oases:** *shehuo* towns and garrison-born grids.
- **Ningxia's Yinchuan plain and Xihaigu:** resettlement towns built to a plan against older villages.
- **Hui:** neighborhood and mosque order in Xi'an, Linxia and Ningxia; memory of the 19th-century uprising differs from Han memory.
- **Amdo Tibetans:** order with the land's powers; herders and farmers separately.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an:** small peoples in the Hehuang borderland; village order in mixed areas.
- **Mongols, Kazakhs and Yugur:** dwelling and camp order in pastoral life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical cosmology, Xi'an urban order and Shaanbei popular religion; mixed for Amdo ritual; thin for Kazakh, Yugur and Bao'an.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stability maintenance, grid surveillance, lockdowns and past Hui-Han and Tibetan unrest are politically sensitive; memories of the 1860s uprising are contested between communities. Some mountain-deity rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
