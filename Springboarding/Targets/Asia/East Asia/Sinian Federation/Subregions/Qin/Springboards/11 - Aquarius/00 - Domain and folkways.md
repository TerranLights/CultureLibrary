## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han** (Xi'an, Baoji, Weinan): plain villages with clan halls against the danwei compounds of the city.
- **Shaanbei Han** (Yan'an, Yulin; Jin-group speech): cave-dwelling villages, temple fairs, the revolutionary base area.
- **Shaannan Han** (Hanzhong, Ankang, Shangluo): mountain settlement and Sichuan- and Hubei-linked migrant origins.
- **Longdong and Longzhong Han** (Qingyang, Pingliang, Tianshui, Dingxi): dry-farming villages and their gathering customs.
- **Hexi Corridor** (Wuwei to Dunhuang): garrison- and migrant-built towns, oases.
- **Hui** in Ningxia, Linxia and Xining: congregations split by Sufi order and school.
- **Amdo Tibetans** (Gannan, Huangnan, Hainan, Golog) and **Kham Tibetans** of Yushu: pastoral camps against farming villages and monasteries.
- **Tu, Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an**: smaller peoples with their own clan and village units.
- **Mongols** (Haixi, Henan county, Subei) and **Kazakhs** (Aksay), **Yugur** (Sunan): herding camps.
- **Third Front and oil, nuclear and mining towns** (Jinchang, Yumen, the former Base 221 in Haiyan): planned communities of migrants.
- *Comparison only:* the Dungan of Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan, descended from Shaanxi and Gansu Hui.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Guanzhong Han and the Hui; mixed for Amdo; thin and scattered for Yugur, Bao'an, Kazakh and Mongol groups. Qin's exact lands are still to be settled by the author (working list: Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai).
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (classical and modern), Amdo Tibetan, Mongolian (classical script), Kazakh (Arabic script), Arabic and Persian loans in Hui speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tibetan and Hui religious organizations, Sufi-order disputes, the 1958 campaigns in Amdo, labor camps, and official "ethnic unity" (民族团结) framing all need careful sourcing; some archives are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
