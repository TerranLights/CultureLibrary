# Qin: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and regional Mandarin; Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Yugur; Hui religious terms. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han villages**: lineage halls and village troupes as the groups people name first; compare with **Xi'an danwei** loyalties.
- **Shaanbei**: the revolutionary base, where collective organization was first tried; separate the area's older temple and kin groups.
- **Hui** by order: Khufiyya, Jahriyya, Qadiriyya, Ikhwan, Xidaotang and others; loyalties differ sharply.
- **Salar** (Xunhua): the *gong* and kin units; and the **Dongxiang** and **Bao'an**, whose order loyalties overlap with the Hui.
- **Amdo Tibetan tribes** (Golog, Rebkong, Labrang's estates): tribal membership and monastic affiliation.
- **Tu** (Huzhu, Minhe): clans and village temples.
- **Qinghai Mongols** (banners) and **Aksay Kazakhs** (clans): kin-based herding groups.
- **Yugur** (Sunan): Turkic- and Mongolic-speaking divisions of one people.
- *Comparison only:* the Dungan of Central Asia, whose Shaanxi and Gansu origin groups still mark villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hui orders and the Yan'an period; mixed for Amdo tribes; thin for Yugur, Kazakh and Bao'an groups.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese (including Qing documents); Amdo Tibetan; Mongolian; Kazakh; Arabic and Persian for order names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sectarian conflict among Muslim orders (some violent in the past); Tibetan tribal and monastic loyalties; the Rectification and later campaigns; collective blame of whole peoples after uprisings. Collective punishment as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
