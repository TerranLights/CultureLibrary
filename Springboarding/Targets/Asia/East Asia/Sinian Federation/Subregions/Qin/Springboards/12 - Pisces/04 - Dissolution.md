# Qin: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard and classical Chinese (Daoist and Buddhist registers); Hui Sufi vocabulary; Amdo Tibetan; Eastern and Western Yugur, Tu, Bao'an and Tangut sources for language loss. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quanzhen and Zhengyi Daoists:** monastic self-cultivation against lay ritual.
- **Han Buddhists in Xi'an and Hexi:** temple deaths and lay chanting groups.
- **Hui by order:** Jahriyya and other Sufi orders against Yihewani and Salafi-leaning reformers.
- **Amdo and Kham Tibetans:** monastic and lay deaths, pastoral and farming.
- **Yugur, Tu, Bao'an and Mongols:** language shift and absorption into larger groups.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong villages:** hollowing out through migration.
- **Resettled communities** (Sanmenxia, Liujiaxia, Xihaigu): bonds broken and remade.
- **Urban families with a member who has dementia:** home care against care homes.
- **Young urban couples:** divorce and marriage change in Xi'an and Lanzhou.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Daoist sites and Dunhuang art; mixed on Sufi ritual; thin on measured experiences of self-loss.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese (Daoist canon), Amdo Tibetan, Hui Sufi vocabulary; Yugur and Tu for language-loss testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sufi ritual and Tibetan death teachings are partly restricted; assimilation of minorities is a political topic (official "fusion" language against communities' own words); resettlement can be coerced.
- **Held for review:** none.
