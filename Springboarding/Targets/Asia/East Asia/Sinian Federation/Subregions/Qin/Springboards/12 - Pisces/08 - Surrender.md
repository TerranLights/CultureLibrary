*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Qin (秦), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard and classical Chinese; Guanzhong and Gansu speech; Hui Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Kazakh and Mongolian for fate words; Russian and Dungan sources for the 1870s exodus. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guanzhong Han:** Laozi's homeland claim; Buddhist deathbed practice.
- **Shaanbei and Longdong:** dryland farmers facing drought; proverb traditions.
- **Shaannan:** a southern-leaning dialect area with its own fate sayings.
- **Hexi:** oasis farmers, desertification and moving away.
- **Hui of Ningxia and Linxia:** trust in God and death rites; the uprising's memory.
- **Salar, Dongxiang, Bao'an:** their own words for God's decree.
- **Amdo herders and monastics; Kham Tibetans:** refuge, loss of herds, ordination.
- **Tu and Yugur:** mixed Buddhist and older rites at death.
- **Qinghai Mongols and Aksay Kazakhs:** fate words in pastoral loss.
- **Urban families:** hospice choices, living wills, letting go of things.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Laozi and the uprising's history; mixed on death rites; thin on hospice and recovery in the region.
- **Languages needed:** classical and Standard Chinese; Amdo Tibetan; Arabic loans; Russian and Dungan for the exodus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hui uprising memory and abduction of women are charged; Tibetan ordination of minors is regulated; outsiders' labels of "fatalism" for any people are to be recorded as claims, not adopted.
