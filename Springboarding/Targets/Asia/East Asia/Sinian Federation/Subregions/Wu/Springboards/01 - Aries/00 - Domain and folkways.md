# Wu: Domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; lands carried: Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified; traditional for older print); Wu Chinese (吴语) in its many varieties; Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin; Huizhou Chinese (徽语); Gan and Southern Min pockets; She speech (畲话). **Concept in one line:** the domain of self and identity: what a self is here and how it asserts itself, through personhood, the body, appearance and naming.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai natives, "new Shanghainese" (新上海人) and migrants**: residence and dialect.
- **Southern against central and northern Jiangsu (Yangzhou, Yancheng, Xuzhou, Lianyungang)**: Wu against Mandarin, rice against wheat.
- **Zhejiang's north, east coast (Ningbo, Zhoushan), south (Wenzhou, Taizhou) and interior (Jinhua, Quzhou, Lishui)**: distinct Wu varieties.
- **Southern (Huizhou), central (Hefei, Anqing) and northern Anhui (Fuyang, Bozhou, Huaibei)**: three dialect zones, three lineage histories.
- **She people of Lishui and Wenzhou**: the main non-Han Indigenous people.
- **Hui Muslims of Nanjing, Shanghai and Anhui**: a second, religious name.
- **Island and water communities (Chongming, Zhoushan, former Fuchun River boat people)**: separate histories.
- **Diaspora (comparison only)**: Shanghainese in Hong Kong and Taiwan; Wenzhou and Qingtian people in Europe.
