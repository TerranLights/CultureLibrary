## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai-born with Shanghai *hukou* against long-term residents without it**: identity drawn by paperwork against by life.
- **Subei-origin families in Shanghai**: the classic case of a stigma label, worth tracing to the present.
- **Wu-speaking southern Jiangsu, Jianghuai central Jiangsu, Zhongyuan northern Jiangsu (Xuzhou, Suqian)**: one provincial unit, three self-pictures.
- **Huizhou, central Anhui (Hefei, Anqing) and the Huaibei plain**: whether "Anhui person" exists as an identity at all.
- **Zhejiang's coast (Ningbo, Zhoushan, Taizhou) against its hills (Quzhou, Lishui) and Wenzhou's south (Southern Min-speaking Cangnan and Pingyang)**.
- **She (Lishui, Jingning, Wenzhou), Hui (Nanjing, Shanghai, Anhui) and descendants of banner garrisons**: the non-Han peoples of the lands.
- **Christian Wenzhou and Buddhist Putuo and Jiuhua pilgrimage towns**: religious identity in place.
- **Diaspora (comparison only)**: Qingtian and Wenzhou people in Europe; Shanghainese in Hong Kong.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai (history, press); mixed for Jiangsu and Zhejiang regions; thin for She, *duomin* descendants and boat communities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Shanghainese and Ningbo Wu; Huizhou Chinese; She.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious identity (Wenzhou church disputes, Hui Muslims); stigma labels (苏北人, 堕民) need careful handling with living people; ethnic classification is official and political.
- **Held for review:** none.
