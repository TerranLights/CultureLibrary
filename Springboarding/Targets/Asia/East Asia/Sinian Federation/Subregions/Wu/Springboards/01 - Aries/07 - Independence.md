## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai single-child adults and their parents**: dependence that runs both ways, with housing at its center.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou family firms**: independence from the state, dependence on kin.
- **Southern Jiangsu collective villages**: security from the collective rather than the household.
- **Rural northern Anhui and northern Jiangsu households**: reliance on migrant members' earnings.
- **Elders in Shanghai lanes (弄堂) against elders in emptied hill villages of Lishui and southern Anhui**.
- **Disabled workers (blind masseurs, Nanjing and Shanghai)**: earning one's own living.
- **She upland farmers; Hui traders in Nanjing and Hefei; Zhoushan fishing households**: three different provisioning bases.
- **Diaspora (comparison only)**: Wenzhou and Qingtian families abroad whose earnings support kin at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai statistics and literature; mixed for household dependence elsewhere; thin for She provisioning.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Wenzhou Wu for informal-finance oral sources; She.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional independence and 1911-era "独立" are sensitive in present-day political terms; informal finance overlaps with law (Libra or Capricorn); welfare-dependence talk can stigmatize migrants.
- **Held for review:** none.
