## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai alley-house and high-rise households:** floor space limits what a family can keep.
- **Old collecting families of Ningbo, Suzhou and Changshu:** heirloom libraries and art kept or dispersed.
- **Huizhou mountain villages:** objects held by a lineage hall, not a household.
- **Wenzhou and Yiwu trading families:** the line between stock for sale and things for home.
- **Zhoushan and coastal fishing families:** boats and gear as the main belongings, and goods lost at sea.
- **Northern Anhui and northern Jiangsu migrant workers:** few belongings, carried between city and village.
- **The She:** silver and dress as family holdings.
- **Hui Muslims of Nanjing and Hefei:** no paper goods or grave goods at burial, against Han practice.
- **Generations:** parents of the "three that turn" against children who resell on Xianyu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Jiangnan collecting and Shanghai daily life; mixed for rural households; thin for the She and the islands.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Written Chinese; Classical Chinese; Wu dialect dictionaries (Shanghainese, Suzhou, Ningbo) for everyday words for things.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** property seized in land reform and the Cultural Revolution and later returned or not; families' private papers; funeral customs.
- **Held for review:** none.
