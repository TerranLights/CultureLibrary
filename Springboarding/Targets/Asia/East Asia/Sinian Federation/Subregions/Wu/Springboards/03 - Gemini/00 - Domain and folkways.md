## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Taihu Wu core** (Shanghai, Suzhou, Wuxi, Changzhou, Jiaxing, Huzhou, Hangzhou, Shaoxing, Ningbo): the best-documented speech and neighborhood forms; separate city from canal town.
- **Southern Wu** (Taizhou, Wenzhou, Jinhua, Quzhou, Lishui): mutually hard-to-follow varieties, so talk and schooling may run differently.
- **Huizhou** (Shexian, Xiuning, Jixi): its own speech group and a deep lineage literacy record.
- **Jianghuai Mandarin belt** (Nanjing, Yangzhou, Yancheng, Hefei, Anqing): Mandarin-speaking lands; the old Jiangnan/Subei divide.
- **Northern Jiangsu and Anhui** (Xuzhou, Lianyungang, Huaibei, Fuyang, Bozhou): Zhongyuan Mandarin speech, a different rural pattern.
- **Southwest Anhui Gan speakers** (around Susong, Yuexi *check*): a third Chinese language group in the lands.
- **She** of Lishui and Wenzhou; **Hui Muslims** in Nanjing and Anhui towns; **Southern Min and Manhua (蛮话) enclaves** in Cangnan and Pingyang.
- **Islanders and boat people:** Chongming, Zhoushan, the Jiuxing fishing families (九姓渔民) of the Xin'an River *check*.
- **Newcomers:** migrants from elsewhere in the federation, and "new Shanghainese" (新上海人).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai, Suzhou and Hangzhou; mixed for northern Jiangsu and Anhui; thin for She and the small enclaves. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the four provinces in the tracker header.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese reading (simplified and traditional); listening skill in at least Shanghainese and one southern Wu variety; dialect dictionaries for the rest. Pre-1949 sources are in classical or semi-classical Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dialect policy and Putonghua promotion are political topics; minority and religious material should come through the communities' own bodies.
