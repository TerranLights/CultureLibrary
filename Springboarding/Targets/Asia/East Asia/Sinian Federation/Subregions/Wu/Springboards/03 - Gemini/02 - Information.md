## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai press city against inland Anhui counties:** print and broadband reached them on very different schedules.
- **Huizhou merchant lineages:** built written information systems (route books, account books, letters) for long-distance trade.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou private-business towns:** market information moved through kin and native-place networks; compare with official channels.
- **Zhoushan and coastal fishing communities:** weather and sea information, from lore to radio forecasts, is a matter of life and death.
- **Hui Muslims:** mosque sermons and community notices as a separate channel.
- **She villages:** what is kept in ancestral scrolls and sung history, and who may see it.
- **Elderly residents:** the health-code years showed who could not get through data-based systems.
- **Migrant workers in the delta's factory towns:** news by phone and hometown groups rather than local media.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai's press and Jiangnan book history; mixed for rural Anhui and Zhejiang hill counties; thin for She and Hui channels.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; traditional characters and classical Chinese for archives and gazetteers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** censorship, surveillance and data-privacy topics are politically sensitive; archives restrict many post-1949 files; online material can vanish. Rights to information as law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
