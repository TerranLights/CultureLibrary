## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jiangnan scholar families** (Suzhou, Changzhou, Hangzhou): learning held as a family inheritance; the best-documented case, so do not let it stand for all.
- **Huizhou merchant lineages:** trade and Confucian study were combined (the phrase 贾而好儒 *check*), so learning for profit and for status sat in one family.
- **Rural northern Anhui and Subei:** labor out-migration leaves many children with grandparents; who teaches them day to day.
- **Migrant families in Shanghai and Suzhou:** children's access to city schools and to after-school help.
- **Craft towns** (Yixing, Dongyang woodcarving, Zhenhu embroidery): skill passed by hand, outside school.
- **Hui families:** mosque learning beside state school.
- **She villages:** song and ritual learning alongside schooling.
- **Fishing and island families** (Zhoushan, Chongming): learning the sea by going out on it.
- **Girls and boys:** documented differences in home tutoring and in what each was expected to learn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite Jiangnan learning and for Shanghai test data; mixed for crafts; thin for She and for rural Anhui home learning.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for primers and Qing sources; Wu for rhymes and craft terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the tutoring crackdown and education policy are politically live; testing results are curated. Schooling as an institution belongs to Sagittarius.
