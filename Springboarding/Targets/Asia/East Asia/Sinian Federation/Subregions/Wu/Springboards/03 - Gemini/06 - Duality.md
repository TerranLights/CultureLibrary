*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for Buddhist, Neo-Confucian and Eastern Han sources; Shanghainese for city sayings; Hui Chinese for Hui-Confucian terms. **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived with: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Puxi and Pudong residents:** the river split the city into an old and a new half; a lived local pair.
- **Jiangnan and Subei people:** the internal Han divide, with status attached; separate it from simple geography.
- **South and north Anhui:** Huizhou and the Huai plain are often paired as different worlds; check how each side speaks of the other.
- **Wenzhou Christians:** members of a large Protestant community who also live as local entrepreneurs and kin; dual loyalties are a research topic there (Nanlai Cao's work *check*).
- **Hui Muslims:** the Hui-Confucian tradition and today's dual religious and civic identity.
- **She speakers:** many use She at home and Chinese outside; how they name the switch.
- **Yue opera performers and fans:** an art where gender doubling is the standard.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai's hybrid culture and the Subei question; mixed for folk religion; thin for She.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for the Han, Buddhist and Neo-Confucian sources; Shanghainese for city sayings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious dual loyalty (Christians and Muslims) and the treatment of migrants are politically sensitive; the Subei label is a slur in some uses, so quote it only as a research term.
- **Held for review:** none.
