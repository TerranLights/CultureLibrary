**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Wu kin terms (Shanghai, Suzhou, Ningbo, Wenzhou); classical Chinese for genealogies and family-division deeds; She and Hui kin usage. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban Shanghai, Suzhou and Hangzhou:** the earliest and deepest drop to one child; cousins took the place of siblings.
- **Rural northern Jiangsu and Anhui:** larger sibling sets lasted longer; older siblings raised younger ones when parents migrated.
- **Huizhou lineages:** branches (房) traced to brothers; sibling rank written into the landscape of ancestral halls.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou families:** brother-run firms; sibling loyalty tested by money.
- **Zhoushan and Chongming fishing families:** brothers crewing one boat; shared risk.
- **Hui families:** Islamic rules on shares between sons and daughters set different expectations (as law, see Libra).
- **She families:** their own kin terms and the mother's brother's role at weddings.
- **Factory sisterhoods and sworn bonds:** chosen siblings among migrant workers then and now.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage records, Huizhou deeds and the one-child era; mixed for present-day adult sibling relations; thin for She and Hui sibling customs.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for genealogies and deeds; Wu kin terms in several varieties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-policy history (forced measures, unregistered children) is sensitive; gang brotherhoods touch crime. Inheritance rules as law go to Libra, the spirit of family law to Capricorn.
