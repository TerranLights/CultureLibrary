## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai lane-house residents and tower-block residents:** shared kitchens and taps against closed doors; whether talk about neighbors moved online.
- **Canal and water towns** (Zhouzhuang, Tongli, Xitang): small, dense places where washing steps and teahouses served as news posts.
- **Small Anhui and Subei county towns:** "everyone knows everyone"; compare the weight of reputation with that in the big cities.
- **Shanghai's entertainment world:** a century of star gossip, from the tabloids to fan accounts.
- **Wenzhou church congregations and Hui mosque communities:** religious teaching on the tongue, and close networks where reputation travels fast.
- **She villages:** song as a channel for teasing and public comment; query 畲族 山歌 讽刺 (thin).
- **Migrant workers in factory dormitories:** gossip among strangers far from home.
- **Retirees and neighborhood volunteers:** the "watchful elder" role, joked about and relied on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Republican Shanghai and the 1768 case; mixed for present-day village talk; thin for She and Hui.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Shanghainese for street words and comedy; classical Chinese for Qing archives and Ming jokes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "rumor" is also a legal and political label in present-day China; the official line between rumor and news must not be taken as given. Slander as an offense belongs to Libra and Pisces.
- **Held for review:** none.
