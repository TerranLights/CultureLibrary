# Wu: Home and family, domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; lands Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu and Anhui. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (普通话; simplified characters, traditional for pre-modern texts) and Classical Chinese; the Wu varieties (Shanghainese, Suzhou, Ningbo, Hangzhou, Wenzhou, Jinhua, Chuqu, and Xuanzhou Wu in Anhui); Jianghuai Mandarin (Nanjing, Yangzhou, Hefei); Zhongyuan Mandarin (Xuzhou, northern Anhui); Hui Chinese (Huizhou); the She language (畲话). **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what home is, and who counts as family; with Cancer's folkways of building, family and child-rearing.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Taihu Wu heartland** (Shanghai, Suzhou, Wuxi, Changzhou, Jiaxing, Huzhou, Hangzhou): water-town and silk households beside the metropolis.
- **Ningbo and Shaoxing:** merchant lineages and walled family compounds (台门).
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou:** separate Wu varieties; family firms; emigrant households (diaspora as comparison only).
- **Jinhua, Quzhou, Lishui:** mountain lineage villages and the She.
- **Huizhou (southern Anhui):** lineage and merchant society, its own dialect group.
- **Jianghuai belt** (Nanjing, Yangzhou, Hefei, Huai'an): Mandarin-speaking, a distinct family vocabulary.
- **Xuzhou and Huaibei:** Zhongyuan Mandarin, flood plain, heavy out-migration.
- **Subei migrants and their descendants in Shanghai:** a housing and status history of their own.
- **Hui Muslims** (Nanjing, Hefei, Shouxian, Hangzhou): household religion and food rules at home.
- **She:** Lishui, southern Wenzhou, Ningguo in Anhui.
- **Boat-dwelling fishing families** of Taihu and the Fuchun River: homes afloat until resettlement.
- **Islanders** (Zhoushan, Chongming) and **Christian families** (Wenzhou; Shanghai's Xujiahui).
- **Recent migrants** from other provinces in Wu's cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (census, panel surveys, archives, genealogies, local gazetteers 地方志); thinner for the She, boat families and Wu speech, which is mostly oral.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; several Wu varieties (not mutually intelligible); Hui Chinese; She.
- **Lands:** Wu's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets cover the four provinces listed. Federation-wide patterns belong in the Sinian Federation's own sheets.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** household registration (户口), the birth-limit decades, house churches, and demolition and relocation disputes. Anything legal goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read 《江村经济》 alongside one Kaixiangong restudy (search CNKI 中国知网 for "开弦弓 再研究"); list the household and kin words.
2. Download the 2020 census table "家庭户类别" (family household types) from each of the four provincial statistics bureaus.
3. Search the Shanghai Library catalog for "宗谱" plus one county (绍兴, 歙县, 无锡) and compare what each register counts as family.
4. Search CNKI for "徽州 分家书" and "江南 家庭结构 明清".
5. Look up 屋里, 娘舅 and 过房 in a Shanghainese dictionary, then ask speakers what each means today.
