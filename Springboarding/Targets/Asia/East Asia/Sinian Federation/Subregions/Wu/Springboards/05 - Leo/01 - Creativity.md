## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou, Wuxi and Changshu:** fine embroidery (the Zhenhu embroidery villages), gardens and orthodox painting; workshop lineages.
- **Yangzhou:** lacquer, jade and the eccentric painters; patronage by salt merchants.
- **Huizhou (southern Anhui):** brick, wood and stone carving (徽州三雕), ink-making, lineage craft secrets.
- **Southern Zhejiang:** Dongyang woodcarving, Yueqing boxwood, Longquan celadon and swords, Qingtian stone; makers who often moved abroad.
- **Shanghai:** commercial art (calendar posters, the 海派 painters) and today's design and game studios.
- **Northern Jiangsu and Anhui plains:** paper-cutting and New Year prints; thinner records.
- **She people:** costume, ribbon weaving, songs made for occasions.
- **Hui Muslims:** calligraphy and mosque decoration.
- **Factory workers** in Kunshan and Suzhou electronics plants: making to order against designing.
- **Wenzhou and Yiwu traders:** copying, adapting and branding.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite painting, gardens and named crafts; mixed for modern art; thin for everyday improvising and She crafts.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for art theory; Mandarin; Wu for workshop talk; Japanese and English for Ming-Qing art history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** contemporary art can be closed or censored; craft families may guard recipes and refuse to share; disputes over who "owns" a regional craft name.
- **Held for review:** none.
