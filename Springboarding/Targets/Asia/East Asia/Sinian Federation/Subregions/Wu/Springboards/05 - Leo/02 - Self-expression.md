# Wu: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Classical Chinese, Shanghainese and other Wu varieties, Nanjing and Hefei Mandarin, She speech. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai lane-house generations against high-rise youth:** old neighborhood talk norms against online expression.
- **Suzhou and Wuxi:** the "soft Wu speech" (吴侬软语) image, a stereotype to test against local accounts.
- **Nanjing and Yangzhou:** Jianghuai Mandarin speakers and the "big radish" self-image.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou:** southern Wu speech and church testimony traditions among Protestants.
- **Northern Jiangsu and northern Anhui:** plains cultures with louder festival forms (flower-drum lantern dance), less studied.
- **Huizhou:** lineage villages where restraint was codified in family rules.
- **Women** (Yue opera fans and performers; 1930s magazine readers) and **LGBT communities** in Shanghai and Hangzhou.
- **She people:** song as conversation. **Hui Muslims:** modest dress and religious self-marking.
- **Migrant workers:** expression in a second dialect, at work and online.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai's print culture and elite writing; mixed for present-day attitudes; thin for She and rural Anhui.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Shanghainese and Suzhou Wu for literature and interviews; English and Japanese secondary work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression (1986, 1989, the 2022 Shanghai vigils) is censored, and informants may be at risk; LGBT expression; religious testimony in Zhejiang.
- **Held for review:** none.
