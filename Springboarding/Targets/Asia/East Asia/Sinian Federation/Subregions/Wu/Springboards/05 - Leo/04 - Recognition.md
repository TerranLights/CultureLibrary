## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou, Changzhou and Wuxi:** dense exam-success traditions; recognition through degrees and scholarship.
- **Huizhou:** merchant wealth turned into lineage honors and arches.
- **Ningbo and Shaoxing:** merchants and clerks (the Shaoxing private secretaries, 绍兴师爷), credited behind the scenes.
- **Shanghai:** entertainment awards and corporate recognition; old honor boards in mills and workshops.
- **Northern Jiangsu and northern Anhui:** fewer pre-modern honors; recognition sought through army, Party or migration.
- **Women writers and workers:** credit given late or under a husband's name.
- **She people:** recognition as a nationality, and village titles of their own.
- **Hui Muslims:** religious titles and community standing.
- **Rehabilitated families** after 1978; **migrant workers** whose labor is rarely credited.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exam history and state honors; mixed for everyday workplace credit; thin for She titles.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for degree lists and genealogies; Mandarin; Shanghainese for interviews; English and Japanese secondary works.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rehabilitation cases touch political history; ethnic classification is state-defined; some planned-era files need permission.
- **Held for review:** none.
