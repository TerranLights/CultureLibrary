# Wu: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Classical Chinese, Shanghainese, Shaoxing and Ningbo Wu, Hui Muslim usage. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Shanghai families against recent arrivals:** decency marked by neatness, speech and address.
- **Shaoxing and Ningbo:** descendants of the 堕民 and of the households that hired them.
- **Huizhou lineage villages:** masters and hereditary servants.
- **River and lake boat people** (the Xin'an and Fuchun, Taihu fishers).
- **Northern Anhui and northern Jiangsu:** famine memory, migration and the dignity of labor.
- **Factory workers** in Kunshan, Suzhou and Wuxi; **domestic and care workers** in Shanghai and Hangzhou.
- **The very old** in Shanghai's inner districts and in emptied villages of Anhui and southern Zhejiang: care at home against care homes.
- **Hui Muslims:** burial rites and their protection under funeral reform.
- **She people:** dignity in a minority's own customs.
- **Disabled people:** community programs in Shanghai and elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for philosophy and literature; mixed for eldercare and hospice; thin for outcast-group descendants, who may not wish to be identified.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Shaoxing, Ningbo and Shanghai Wu for oral history; English and Japanese secondary works.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outcast and servant ancestry is stigmatizing; comfort-women testimony needs care; the Anqing case and factory disasters are politically sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.
