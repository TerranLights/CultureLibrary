## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou and Changzhou lineages:** endowments and charitable estates.
- **Huizhou merchants:** donations at home while trading far away.
- **Ningbo and Shaoxing:** native-place associations; giving through home-town networks.
- **Wenzhou:** rotating credit, church and temple building, giving among traders.
- **Shanghai:** young professionals (bill-splitting) against older lane-house neighbors (food shared over walls).
- **Yangzhou:** a patronage city's memory.
- **Northern Anhui and northern Jiangsu villages:** gift money at weddings and funerals as a burden.
- **Hui Muslims:** alms and Eid giving.
- **She people:** exchanged labor and festival food.
- **Buddhist lay groups:** release-of-life and temple donations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern and Republican charity; mixed for current giving data; thin for informal giving and minority practice.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Japanese (Fuma); Ningbo and Wenzhou Wu for interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "stingy" stereotypes about Shanghai people are a trap to test, not repeat; church giving in Zhejiang is sensitive; wealthy donors' motives are politically charged after 2021.
- **Held for review:** none.
