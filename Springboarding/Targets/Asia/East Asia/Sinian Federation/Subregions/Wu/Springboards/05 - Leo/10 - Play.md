## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai lane children of the 1950s to 1980s against today's scheduled children:** street games against tutoring.
- **Ningbo and Hangzhou:** mahjong tables, Go clubs, cricket fighters.
- **Huai'an, Yancheng and northern Jiangsu:** guandan and storytelling; plains festival play.
- **Huizhou and southern Anhui:** fish-lantern and dragon processions in lineage villages.
- **Southern Zhejiang (Jinhua, Lishui):** bench dragons, toy makers, mountain games.
- **Office workers and officials:** card games as bonding.
- **Elderly in parks:** chess, cards and singing groups.
- **She people:** their own games and song contests.
- **Hui Muslims:** where the line on gambling and cards falls.
- **Young gamers and esports players** in Shanghai and Hangzhou.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Go, games history and urban amusements; mixed for children's free play; thin for She and village games.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Mandarin; Shanghainese and Ningbo Wu for rhymes and games; English for game studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling games are illegal for money outside state lotteries; minors' gaming limits are politically framed; informants may play down betting.
- **Held for review:** none.
