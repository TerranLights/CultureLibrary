## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai state-sector and mill families**: the danwei generation against their laid-off and gig-working children.
- **Taihu silk and rice villages**: household labor split by sex and season.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou workshop families**: self-employment and family hours.
- **Yiwu and Jinhua traders**: market work as a whole-family enterprise.
- **Huizhou merchant lineages**: boys sent out young to apprentice; work far from home.
- **Huaibei and central Anhui migrants**: work as departure; the Fengyang famine memory.
- **Huainan and Ma'anshan coal and steel workers**: heavy industry and danger at work.
- **Zhoushan and Ningbo fishers; Yancheng salt workers**: seasonal and hereditary trades.
- **She hill farmers**: tea and terraces in their own language and song.
- **Hui Muslim butchers, cooks and traders**: trades shaped by halal rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai and the delta (labor history is a large field); mixed for Anhui; thin for She, boat peoples and Huaibei villages.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Wu for oral histories; Japanese for Jiangnan economic history; English for labor history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** strikes, labor camps (Shanghai's farms inside Anhui, *check*) and the Fengyang famine are restricted topics; worker protest posts are deleted; migrants may fear speaking.
- **Held for review:** none.
