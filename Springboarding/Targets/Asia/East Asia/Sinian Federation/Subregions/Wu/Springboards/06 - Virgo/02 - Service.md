## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yangzhou and north Jiangsu**: service trades exported to Shanghai and the stigma or pride that came with them.
- **Wuwei and central Anhui**: generations of women working as domestic help in other cities.
- **Shanghai lane households**: employers of 娘姨 and 阿姨; the view from the served side.
- **Hangzhou and Suzhou tourism workers**: hospitality as the city's trade.
- **Nanjing**: an old capital of court and official service.
- **Huizhou lineages**: service owed inside the clan; ancestral-hall duties.
- **She villages**: hospitality and elder care in their own customs.
- **Hui Muslim restaurant families**: serving non-Muslim customers within halal rules.
- **Southern Anhui**: military-service memory of the New Fourth Army region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai domestic service and Jiangnan charity; mixed for public service; thin for She and Hui service customs.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Japanese (Fuma and others); Wu for older servant terms; Manchu only for bondservant records (*check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic workers' complaints and labor disputes; "Subei" stigma; military history is politically framed; petition records are restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.

1. Search CNKI for "娘姨 上海" and "无为 保姆" to trace domestic service across a century.

5. Look up the Shanghai Volunteers Association's annual reports for volunteer counts and categories.
