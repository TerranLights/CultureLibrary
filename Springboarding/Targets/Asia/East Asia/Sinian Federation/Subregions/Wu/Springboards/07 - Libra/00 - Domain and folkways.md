# Wu: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; lands carried: Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui. **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese (simplified; traditional for pre-1950 sources); Wu varieties (Shanghainese, Suzhou, Ningbo, Shaoxing, Wenzhou and others); Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin; Huizhou Chinese; She; Southern Min pockets in southern Zhejiang. **Concept in one line:** partnership, marriage, contracts, open enemies, balance and justice: what is a fair bargain and a good partnership?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai old families, Subei migrants and recent arrivals (新上海人):** separate marriage markets and paths to settling a quarrel, with an old line of prejudice inside the city.
- **Sunan Wu (Suzhou, Wuxi, Changzhou) against Subei (Xuzhou, Huai'an, Yancheng):** Jiangsu's north-south seam.
- **Nanjing and Yangzhou (Jianghuai Mandarin):** a former capital and a salt-merchant city.
- **Northern Zhejiang Wu (Hangzhou, Jiaxing, Huzhou, Shaoxing, Ningbo-Zhoushan islanders), southern Zhejiang (Wenzhou, Taizhou; Southern Min speakers in Cangnan and Pingyang), inland Zhejiang (Jinhua, Yiwu, Quzhou, Lishui):** compared area by area.
- **She people (Jingning She Autonomous County and other Lishui and Anhui She villages):** the region's main non-Han people, with their own marriage songs.
- **Hui Muslims (Nanjing, Hefei and northern Anhui towns):** Islamic marriage contracts beside civil registration.
- **Huizhou (She, Yi, Xiuning counties; Hui Chinese):** lineage villages with the densest contract archive.
- **Central Anhui (Hefei, Anqing) and northern Anhui (Fuyang, Bozhou, Huaibei):** Mandarin-speaking, more rural areas whose marriage payments may differ.
- **Wenzhou Protestants, Shanghai Catholics:** church weddings and church mediation.
- Diaspora (Wenzhou and Qingtian emigrants in Europe; Shanghainese in Hong Kong): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for Huizhou, Shanghai and Zhejiang; thin for the She and for Hui Muslim marriage practice.
- **Languages needed:** Modern and Classical Chinese; Wu dialect reading; Japanese for much Huizhou and Jiangnan scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some post-1949 files are closed; divorce records involve living people. The exact lands of Wu are still to be settled. The federation's post-war law is not yet written; pre-war law is the baseline.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Anhui University Huizhou studies center catalog and the facsimile set for 婚书 (marriage contracts) and 合同 (agreements); note the formulas for witnesses (凭中).
2. Pull CFPS microdata for the four provinces on bride price, marital status and housework; compare with the provincial yearbooks' marriage and divorce counts.
3. Search 吃讲茶 and 老娘舅 in Shanghai local gazetteers (上海通志) and newspapers to trace who settled quarrels and where.
4. Search 吴越同舟 and 吴越春秋 for how the old rivalry is used today in Suzhou and Shaoxing civic writing.
