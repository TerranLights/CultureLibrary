# Wu: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese, modern and classical; Wu varieties (Shanghainese, Shaoxing, Hangzhou and others); Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin of Anhui and northern Jiangsu; Huizhou Chinese; She. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai:** treaty-port memory and international finance in its courts; residents' and migrants' routes to redress.
- **Zhuji and Shaoxing:** home of a mediation model and of the old legal-secretary trade.
- **Wenzhou:** debt disputes from private lending after the 2011 credit crisis, in and outside court.
- **Huizhou lineage villages:** ancestral-hall rules (族规) and punishments beside the county court.
- **Hefei and central Anhui:** home ground of the cult of the judge Bao Zheng.
- **Northern Anhui and Xuzhou:** a history of banditry and the Nian rising; self-help justice and its memory.
- **Nanjing:** war memory and historical justice as public ritual.
- **She villages (Lishui, Jingning) and Hui Muslim communities:** customary and mosque-centered hearing of wrongs beside state courts, as they share it.
- **Migrant workers across the delta:** wage arrears (讨薪) and labor arbitration.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern and Republican court records and for current court publicity; thin and uneven for prison, petitioning and sentencing data.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Song and Qing texts; modern Chinese; Japanese and English for war-crimes trial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petitioning (信访), detention and political cases are sensitive and may be closed; death-penalty counts are not published nationally; war memory carries strong feeling. Treat each with care and cite only open sources.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the 原法 essay and the 以理杀人 passage side by side, then search CNKI for 江南 法律文化 to find modern work that uses them.
2. Search the Longquan archive selections for 冤 and 上诉 (appeal) to see how litigants framed injustice.
3. Search 杨乃武 小白菜 档案 for the trial records and the opera and TV versions.
4. Download the latest work reports of the Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu and Anhui high courts and list the terms each uses for justice.
5. Search 会审公廨 档案 in the Shanghai Municipal Archives catalog.
