# Wu: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese; Shanghainese and other Wu varieties for everyday speech; Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin; Huizhou Chinese; She. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai household-registration holders and newcomers:** how the registration line shapes what counts as fair access.
- **Families in the four exam systems:** Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu and Anhui set different hurdles for the same goal.
- **Coastal Zhejiang and its mountain counties (Lishui, Quzhou):** the gap the common-prosperity program names.
- **Sunan and Subei:** an income seam inside Jiangsu.
- **Anhui out-migrants working in delta cities:** domestic and factory workers who see fairness from the outside in.
- **Wenzhou and Yiwu traders:** market fairness, fixed price against bargaining, among small sellers.
- **She and Hui students:** minority bonus points in exams and the arguments over them.
- **Huizhou lineage families:** inheritance by lot and its memory.
- **Rural and urban pension holders:** different benefit scales as a live fairness question.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official rules, exam policy and survey data; mixed for everyday practice; thin for She and Hui viewpoints in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** modern Chinese; Classical Chinese for deeds and memorials; Wu dialect reading for everyday terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** household registration, exam bonuses for minorities and income gaps are politically charged; regional slurs about stinginess or shrewdness must be studied as stereotypes, never repeated as findings.
- **Held for review:** none.
