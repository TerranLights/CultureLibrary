# Wu: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese; Shanghainese, Ningbo, Wenzhou and other Wu varieties; Jianghuai Mandarin (Hefei, Nanjing); Huizhou Chinese; She. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Huizhou merchant lineages:** kin partnerships tied to the ancestral hall.
- **Ningbo-Shaoxing networks in Shanghai:** partnership by native place.
- **Wenzhou and Yiwu family firms:** couples and kin as business partners.
- **Hangzhou's tech sector:** co-founders and partner systems.
- **Shanghai couples:** late marriage, cohabitation and the 搭子 trend among the young; compare with older couples who call each other 老伴.
- **Anqing and the Huangmei opera country:** the rural married pair as a working unit.
- **Zhoushan and Taizhou fishing crews:** partners who share a boat and a catch.
- **She communities:** courtship by antiphonal song and marriage rules, as they share them.
- **Hui Muslims in Nanjing and Anhui:** marriage within the faith and business partnerships in halal trades.
- **Subei and northern Anhui migrants:** couples separated by migration and left-behind spouses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for merchant history, marriage statistics and literature; mixed for current business partnership; thin for She and Hui partnership in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** modern and Classical Chinese; Wu dialect reading for opera and Pingtan texts; Japanese for some merchant-history scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** underworld history; corporate governance of large firms can be politically sensitive; couples separated by migration involve vulnerable people.
- **Held for review:** none.
