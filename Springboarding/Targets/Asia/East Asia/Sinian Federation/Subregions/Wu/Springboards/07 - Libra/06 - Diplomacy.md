# Wu: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese, modern and classical; Shanghainese and other Wu varieties for everyday tact; Jianghuai Mandarin (Hefei, Huai'an, Nanjing); Huizhou Chinese; She; English and Arabic for Yiwu's trade diplomacy. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai:** treaty-port and international diplomacy, and everyday tact between Shanghainese and newcomers.
- **Hefei and Huai'an:** home cities of two famous negotiators, remembered differently.
- **Hangzhou and Wuzhen:** cities that host summits.
- **Yiwu:** street-level diplomacy with foreign buyers, including Arab and African traders.
- **Wenzhou:** merchant networks negotiating as outsiders elsewhere.
- **Ningbo and Zhoushan:** port go-betweens and their descendants.
- **Nanjing:** the Republican capital's memory of foreign affairs, and of treaties under force.
- **She communities:** relations with Han neighbors and officials.
- **Hui Muslims:** mosque leaders as go-betweens with officials and with Muslim visitors from abroad.
- **Families:** go-betweens in marriage talks and in-law diplomacy, compared between city and village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and treaty-port history; mixed for everyday tact; thin for minority viewpoints.
- **Languages needed:** modern and Classical Chinese; English for treaty-port and diplomatic sources; Arabic or French for some Yiwu traders' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current foreign-policy topics are sensitive; Li Hongzhang and wartime collaborators are contested; some Republican foreign-affairs files are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
