# Wu: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** written Chinese, modern and classical; Shanghainese, Wenzhou and other Wu varieties; Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin; Huizhou Chinese; She; Hui Muslim usage. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou lineage families:** welfare funded by the descent group.
- **Wenzhou and Taizhou entrepreneurs:** rotating credit and its risks.
- **Ningbo and Shaoxing natives in Shanghai:** guild-based mutual aid and its memory.
- **Shanghai alley neighborhoods against new high-rise estates:** the fate of neighborly exchange.
- **Qingtian and Wenzhou home villages:** households living on money sent from abroad (the senders are diaspora, comparison only).
- **Rural Anhui and Subei:** gift money at Spring Festival when migrants come home.
- **She villages:** labor exchange and hospitality, as they describe them.
- **Hui Muslims:** alms and gifts at Eid and in the mosque community.
- **Buddhist pilgrims and Wenzhou Protestants:** vows to deities against church giving.
- **Elderly urban residents:** time banks and children's care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage history, guild history and gift-spending data; mixed for current neighbor exchange; thin for She and Hui exchange in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** modern and Classical Chinese; Wu dialect reading; English and Japanese for some scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informal finance collapses involve fraud cases; gifts to officials shade into bribery (see Impartiality); household gift books are private and need consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
