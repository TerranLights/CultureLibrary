# Wu: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (classical Chinese for older texts); Wu varieties, above all Shanghainese and the Shaoxing-Ningbo speech; Jianghuai Mandarin; Hui Muslim religious terms; She. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai hospital deaths against home deaths in the Lake Tai and Ningbo countryside:** where the dying are kept, and who sits with them.
- **Shaoxing and the Ningbo-Shaoxing lands:** a living theater of ghosts and underworld messengers.
- **Southwest Anhui (Anqing, Tongcheng; Gan- and Jianghuai-speaking):** where burial and cremation policy collided openly.
- **Nanjing:** the city where public mourning for mass death is concentrated.
- **Xuzhou and the Huaibei plain:** Central Plains speech and northern burial habits.
- **Hui Muslims (Nanjing, Hefei, Shouxian):** their own words for dying and their own rite.
- **She people (Lishui, Jingning):** a non-Han understanding of the dead.
- **Zhoushan and coastal fishers:** loss at sea as a separate kind of death.
- **Wenzhou Christians and lay Buddhists:** different teachings on what follows death.
- **Diaspora (Wenzhou and Qingtian families in Europe):** comparison only; whether the dead or their ashes are brought home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai and Nanjing; mixed in the Lake Tai and Shaoxing lands; thin for She, Zhoushan and rural Anhui.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Wu varieties for oral accounts; Hui religious terms of Arabic and Persian origin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine deaths and Cultural Revolution deaths are politically constrained; the 2014 Anqing case has removed reports; Nanjing Massacre figures are disputed internationally.
- **Held for review:** none.
