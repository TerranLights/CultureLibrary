# Wu: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for Ming and Qing cases; Wu varieties; the Gan and Jianghuai speech of southern and central Anhui (for nuo villages); Hui Muslim ritual terms. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, mind, group, institution or place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai's cadres, intellectuals and factory rebels:** the city where 20th-century purges were densest.
- **Huzhou, Tongxiang and Tongcheng literati:** the Qing purges of writing fell hard on them.
- **Nanjing:** purges inside regimes (Ming, Taiping, Republic) based in the same city.
- **Southern Anhui nuo villages (Guichi, Qimen):** purging done by mask and procession.
- **Descendants of the boat people and the "fallen people":** the long tail of being cast out.
- **Hui Muslims:** a body-purity rule separate from Han funeral pollution.
- **Lake districts once hit by snail fever (Qingpu, Jiaxing, the Anhui river plain):** a cleanup campaign remembered in farm villages.
- **Wenzhou Christians:** church discipline and expulsion inside congregations.
- **Diaspora:** comparison only; émigré memoirs of the purges, read against accounts at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Ming-Qing and Republican purges; mixed for the Mao years (archives partly closed); thin for village cleansing rites outside the best-known nuo sites.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; local speech in nuo villages; Wu varieties for oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mao-era campaigns, Lin Zhao and present anti-corruption cases are politically restricted; descendants of outcast groups may not wish to be identified.
- **Held for review:** none.
