*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou and Huizhou scholarly traditions:** two methods of proof, two regional pride stories.
- **Shanghai's educated readership vs. rural Anhui and Subei:** news sources, rumor paths, family messaging groups.
- **Generations in Shanghai:** those who remember the Cultural Revolution's public "truths" and those raised online.
- **Nanjing:** a city whose identity carries a contested history.
- **Buddhist, Christian and Muslim communities:** revealed truth beside state truth.
- **The She and Hui:** oral and written records of their own past, and how outsiders treat them.
- **Wenzhou and Yiwu traders:** truth in deals, labels and counterfeits (see honesty).
- **Scientists and engineers** in Hefei (University of Science and Technology of China) and Shanghai's research institutes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Qing scholarship, the Republican press and the Nanjing Massacre; mixed for public trust in news (few surveys by province); thin for She oral tradition in print.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Wu varieties for rumor and oral history; She; Japanese and English for wartime records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official versions of recent history; censorship; the Nanjing Massacre is a live diplomatic issue. Courts and evidence rules go to Libra.
