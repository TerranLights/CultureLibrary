*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese; Wu Chinese varieties (Shanghainese, Ningbo, Wenzhou, Qingtian speech); Jianghuai and Zhongyuan Mandarin (Subei and Anhui migrants); Hui Chinese (Huizhou merchants). **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Huizhou and Ningbo merchant lineages:** generations raised to leave; compare the two regions' customs of departure.
- **Wenzhou and Qingtian emigrant counties:** travel abroad as a family strategy.
- **Anhui and Subei migrants in Shanghai and southern Jiangsu:** travel as work, and the welcome or contempt they meet.
- **Shanghai's returned sent-down youth:** travel that was ordered, not chosen.
- **Shanghai's young middle class:** leisure and study abroad as status.
- **Grand Canal and Taihu boat people:** a life on the water.
- **Hui Muslims:** the Hajj and their own migration stories.
- **The She:** their ancestors' migration from the southern mountains into Zhejiang, as the She tell it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tourism statistics, Ming-Qing merchants and Republican Shanghai; good census data; thinner for migrant workers' own words and for the She.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Wu varieties, with Wenzhou and Qingtian speech for emigrant villages; Jianghuai Mandarin for Subei migrants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prejudice against Subei and Anhui people; the sent-down youth campaign; foreign traders' residence status. Residence and border rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
