*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; working list of lands: Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified; traditional for pre-1950 print), Wu Chinese (Shanghai, Suzhou, Ningbo, Shaoxing, Wenzhou varieties), Jianghuai Mandarin (Nanjing, Yangzhou, Hefei), Hui Chinese (Huizhou), She, and the mosque-school Chinese of the Hui Muslims. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed, with career, status, reputation, and the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai:** old Shanghainese families against "new Shanghai people" (新上海人): does standing turn on residence status as much as wealth?
- **Southern Jiangsu Wu (Suzhou, Wuxi, Changzhou):** gentry heartland, for lineage and examination authority.
- **Jianghuai and northern Jiangsu (Nanjing, Yangzhou, Yancheng, Nantong; Xuzhou):** another speech zone; a north-south status divide to test.
- **Northern and eastern Zhejiang (Hangzhou, Ningbo, Shaoxing, Zhoushan) against southern Zhejiang (Wenzhou, Taizhou, Lishui; Min speakers in Cangnan):** merchant and clan authority in different settings.
- **Huizhou, the Anhui Yangzi belt (Anqing, Wuhu), Hefei, Huaibei:** lineage villages against a flood-prone northern plain.
- **She communities** (Lishui, Jingning): their own clan and ritual offices.
- **Hui Muslims** (Nanjing, Huainan, Bengbu): congregational authority.
- **Manchu descendants** of the Hangzhou, Nanjing and Zhenjiang garrisons; **Catholics** of Shanghai; **Protestants** of Wenzhou; monks of Putuoshan and Jiuhuashan; **island and boat people**; **migrant workers**.
