## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Jiangsu collective villages against Wenzhou and Taizhou family firms:** a party secretary's authority set against an owner-patriarch's.
- **Shanghai** (a former concession city with layered foreign and Chinese rule) against **Nanjing** (a former capital of governments): different memories of who held power locally.
- **Huizhou lineage villages against Huaibei:** clan heads against plains society with weaker lineages, to test.
- **She communities:** ritual masters and clan elders as distinct sources of command.
- **Hui Muslims:** congregational offices beside state religious bureaus.
- **Catholic Shanghai and Protestant Wenzhou:** church leaders as rival authorities.
- **Zhoushan and Taizhou fishing crews:** a skipper's command at sea.
- **Migrant workers:** facing urban managers and residence officials from outside their own networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern Jiangnan and Republican Shanghai; mixed for present-day attitudes (sensitive survey items); thin for She and fishing crews.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Ming-Qing texts; Wu dialect reading for Shanghai fiction and talk; Standard Chinese for surveys.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political authority, church-state relations and the 2014-2016 Zhejiang church-cross removals are sensitive; protect informants. Present each religious community's own account first.
