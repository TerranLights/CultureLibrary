# Wu: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese, Classical Chinese, Wu Chinese, Jianghuai Mandarin, She, Hui mosque Chinese. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou and Shanghai families** (winter-solstice ancestor offerings) against **Huizhou lineages** (hall rites): household against lineage-level duties to the dead.
- **Anhui sending counties against Shanghai receiving districts:** the same filial duty seen from the village and from the city.
- **Only-child families in Shanghai** (the "4-2-1" household): one child owing four elders.
- **Hui Muslims:** religious duties alongside family ones.
- **Manchu descendants:** memories of hereditary service.
- **Wenzhou and Qingtian emigrant households:** duties across borders (comparison with emigrants abroad only).
- **She communities:** mourning and ancestral duties in their own forms.
- **Zhoushan fishing families:** duties to kin lost at sea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for drama, family instructions and Ming-Qing administration; mixed for survey data on filial duty by province; thin for She and Hui practice in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for drama and instructions; Wu dialect for Shanghai and Suzhou ritual sayings; Hui mosque vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2022 Shanghai lockdown volunteering is politically sensitive; military recruitment data may be restricted; the Fang Xiaoru story involves mass execution of kin.
- **Held for review:** none.
