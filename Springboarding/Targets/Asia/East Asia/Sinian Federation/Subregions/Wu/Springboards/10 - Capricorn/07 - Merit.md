*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou, Changzhou and Wuxi:** a strong examination heritage (a local pride to test, not assume).
- **Huizhou:** merchant lineages that funded schooling for degrees.
- **Shanghai natives against migrants:** points-based membership and local admission quotas.
- **Anhui and northern Jiangsu students:** competing in large exam pools for Shanghai and Nanjing universities.
- **She students:** bonus points and their withdrawal.
- **Hui Muslims:** religious schooling and credentials within the community.
- **State-sector job seekers against private-sector founders** (Hangzhou, Wenzhou): exams against markets as judges of merit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for examination history and gaokao policy; mixed for attitude surveys by province; thin for She and Hui views.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for exam records and fiction; Standard Chinese for policy; Wu dialect only for local talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** admission quotas and minority bonus points are contested; exam-cheating cases involve real students; residence rules shift often.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. In CBDB, query jinshi by prefecture for Suzhou, Changzhou, Huizhou and Shaoxing, Ming and Qing.
2. Read Elman's chapter on quotas and list the regional rules.
3. On the Shanghai human resources bureau site, download the current 居住证积分 scoring table.
4. Search CNKI for "新高考 浙江 公平 争议" and "少数民族加分 浙江 畲族".
5. Read the Fan Jin chapters of 《儒林外史》 and note the words for earned and unearned standing.
