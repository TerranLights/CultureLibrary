# Wu: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu (吴), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese; Wu Chinese (Shanghainese, Wenzhou, Wuxi); Jianghuai Mandarin (Nantong, Nanjing); Central Plains Mandarin (Fengyang); Hui Chinese (Huizhou); She speech. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Wenzhou and Taizhou private-enterprise towns against the Sunan collective towns: which reform path each claims as its own.
- Fengyang and the Huaibei countryside against Shanghai: reform from the field and reform from the planning office.
- Huizhou scholars (Hu Shi, Tao Xingzhi) against Shanghai's press reformers: gradual reform in different hands.
- Lake Tai villagers and fishermen: who bore the costs of industrial reform.
- She villages, where customs campaigns (移风易俗) reached weddings and rites.
- Hui communities in Nanjing and Shanghai, with their own modernizing educators *check*.
- Laid-off state-factory workers in Shanghai, Wuxi and Hefei (下岗) after the 1990s reforms: reform's losers, in their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for reform history and official reform narratives; mixed for attitudes; thin for those who lost under reforms, outside oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese; Wenzhou dialect for local oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official reform stories dominate; activists, labor unrest and the 1950s remolding campaigns need non-official sources too. Reform through law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
