## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai's core against its rural fringe** (Chongming, Jinshan): city mediums and hospitals against village temples.
- **The Taihu heartland** (Suzhou, Wuxi, Huzhou, Jiaxing): Wutong and silkworm rites.
- **Shaoxing and Ningbo:** ghost opera, paper money, nearby Buddhist mountains.
- **Wenzhou:** its own speech, dense temples and many Protestants.
- **Northern Jiangsu and northern Anhui:** Mandarin-speaking flood plains with different hard times.
- **Huizhou:** lineage ritual, geomancy and Mulian opera.
- **The She** (Lishui, Jingning; Ningguo in Anhui): ancestor ritual and healers, as they describe them.
- **Hui Muslims** (Nanjing, Hefei, the Huai towns): mosque life and death customs.
- **Boat and island peoples** (Zhoushan; the Jiuxing 九姓 boat families): sea gods and water's dangers.
- **Inland migrants in the cities:** their healers move with them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Shanghai and the Taihu cities; mixed for Wenzhou and Huizhou; thin for the She, boat peoples and northern Anhui villages.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Wu varieties for oral work (each city differs); northern Mandarin; She speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 迷信 label makes healers and mediums wary; psychiatric records are closed; famine years and campaigns against religion are in living memory. Federation-wide frames (religious registration, health statistics) shape what is recorded.
