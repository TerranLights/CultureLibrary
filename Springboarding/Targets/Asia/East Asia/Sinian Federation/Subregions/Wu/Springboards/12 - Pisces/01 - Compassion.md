## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suzhou lineage giving against Shanghai's benevolent halls:** kin charity against city charity for strangers.
- **Hangzhou and Ningbo lay Buddhists:** animal release and temple feeding make compassion a merit practice.
- **Wenzhou Christians against Wenzhou temple associations:** two ways of caring for the old and sick in one city.
- **Huizhou merchant families:** money earned far away paid for granaries, bridges and relief at home.
- **Northern Anhui and northern Jiangsu:** long cast as recipients of pity; compare their own mutual aid.
- **Hui Muslims:** alms, Ramadan and burial help inside the community.
- **Shanghai locals and migrants (外地人):** who is seen as deserving in a city of newcomers.
- **Urban pet rescuers in Shanghai and Hangzhou:** compassion for stray cats and dogs as a new public cause.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on historical charity (Jiangnan is the best-studied region for it); rich online for crowdfunding; thin for village mutual aid in the north.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Wu speech for the everyday pity words; Hui religious terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nanjing Massacre is a state memorial theme; grassroots charities have registration limits; private sickness on crowdfunding pages calls for care in quoting.
- **Held for review:** none.
