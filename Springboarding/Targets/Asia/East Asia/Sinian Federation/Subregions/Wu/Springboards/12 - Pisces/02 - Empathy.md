## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shanghai natives and migrants from Subei and Anhui:** the classic line across which fellow-feeling was withheld.
- **Domestic workers and their employers:** reading another for a living against being read.
- **Wu-speaking cities against Mandarin-speaking northern Jiangsu and Anhui:** check how directness and indirectness differ; variation to measure, not a trait to assume.
- **Yue and Huangmei opera audiences:** largely women; identification through stage roles.
- **Doctors and patients in big-city hospitals:** where failed empathy turns to conflict.
- **Deaf people using Shanghai's sign language:** how hearing neighbors try, or fail, to understand them.
- **Old people left in Anhui villages and their children in the cities:** empathy at a distance, by phone and video.
- **The She and Hui:** how Han neighbors grasp, or misread, their customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for students and online talk; mixed for performance traditions; thin for villages and minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Suzhou speech for pingtan; Shanghainese for radio and slang; Shanghai sign language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nanjing memorial is a state theme; violence against doctors is reported in a political frame.
- **Held for review:** none.
