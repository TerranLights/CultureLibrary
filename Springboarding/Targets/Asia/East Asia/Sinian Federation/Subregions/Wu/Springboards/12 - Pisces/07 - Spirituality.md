## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pure Land lay devotees around Suzhou and Shanghai:** recitation and vegetarian circles.
- **Daoist priests who live at home (火居道士) in Jiangnan villages:** ritual for hire, against monastic Maoshan.
- **Jiangnan Catholics, especially old boat-dwelling families:** a centuries-old local church.
- **Wenzhou Protestants:** dense congregations, often led by business people.
- **Hui Muslims of Nanjing and Anhui:** a written Chinese Islamic tradition.
- **The She:** ancestor scrolls and initiation, as they share them.
- **Silk-farming households of Huzhou and Jiaxing:** a sacred calendar tied to work.
- **Zhoushan and Xiangshan fishermen:** sea gods and offerings.
- **Young urban temple-goers:** devotion mixed with jokes and online posting.
- **The large unaffiliated majority:** ancestors, incense and luck without membership.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buddhism and Catholicism history; mixed for Daoist and folk practice; thin for the She and village priests.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Wu speech for village ritual; Hui terms with Arabic and Persian roots.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religion is under federation-wide registration; the Zhejiang cross removals (2014-2016) are sensitive; some She and Daoist rites are kept from outsiders, respected and not pursued.
- **Held for review:** translated scriptures of South Asian origin recited in local Buddhist practice (category only).
