# Wu (吴): Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Wu, Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia (Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard and classical Chinese; Shanghainese and other Wu varieties; Jianghuai Mandarin; Hui religious vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pure Land families against hospital deaths in Shanghai:** two scripts for the end.
- **Hui Muslims:** predestination and leave-taking at death.
- **Catholics of Shanghai and Jiangnan:** resignation to God's will in a local church.
- **Elders who lived through famine and campaigns, against the young:** 认命 against 躺平, as each generation uses them.
- **Nanjing and Yangzhou memories of 1645:** surrender in Nanjing, resistance and massacre in Yangzhou.
- **Coastal Zhejiang villagers:** leaving or staying before typhoons.
- **Rural Anhui elders far from hospitals:** accepting illness without treatment, as they describe it.
- **Recovery groups in Shanghai:** mostly expatriate at first; compare Chinese-language meetings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and the 1645 and 1864 defeats; mixed for hospice; thin for village deaths and Hui practice.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Wu speech for elders' talk of fate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life choice touches law (see Libra); war memory of defeat is politically framed; famine-era resignation is a sensitive memory.
- **Held for review:** none.
