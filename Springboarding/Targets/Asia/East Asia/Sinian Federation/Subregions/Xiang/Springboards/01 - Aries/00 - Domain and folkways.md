**Target:** Xiang 湘 (pre-war Hunan 湖南), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified; traditional for older titles); Xiang 湘语 (New Xiang of Changsha; Old Xiang of Loudi-Shaoyang; Chen-Xu, Yong-Quan and Hengzhou subgroups); Gan, Southwestern Mandarin, Hakka, Xiangnan Tuhua 湘南土话 and Waxiang 瓦乡话; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao (Qo Xiong), Dong (Kam), Yao (Mien and others). **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming, and sport as a statement of the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha-Zhuzhou-Xiangtan:** the urban, media-made self that dominates writing about "Hunanese".
- **Loudi-Shaoyang uplands, Meishan 梅山 counties (Xinhua, Anhua):** a ritual tradition with its own ideas of soul and body.
- **Hengyang and Nanyue 南岳:** pilgrimage shapes life-stage rites.
- **Dongting plain (Yueyang, Changde, Yiyang):** lake life, Mandarin-leaning speech.
- **Eastern counties (Liuyang, Pingjiang, Liling):** Gan-speaking, with Hakka pockets.
- **South (Chenzhou, Yongzhou):** Tuhua villages, Hakka, Jiangyong Nüshu.
- **Xiangxi and Zhangjiajie:** Tujia and Miao registration, dress and naming, with mixed families.
- **Huaihua:** Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang; Waxiang speakers registered under different ethnicities.
- **Yao:** Jianghua, Jiangyong, Rucheng; Huayao 花瑶 of Longhui and Xupu.
- **Bai of Sangzhi, Hui of Changde and Shaoyang, Uyghurs of Taoyuan:** small groups with distinct origin stories.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Hunanese in Guangdong.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Han elite self (letters, diaries, gazetteers); mixed for minorities; thin for rural first-person voices outside Nüshu.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese, Xiang dialect reading; Tujia and Xiangxi Miao for fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification; Qing records of Miao revolts are one-sided; the Taoyuan Uyghur community needs care.
- **Lands:** this member state's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; the sheets are written for Hunan.
- **Held for review:** none.
