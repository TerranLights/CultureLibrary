## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha urban youth:** media and service-sector speech, where talk-show norms of speaking up meet older courtesy.
- **Xiangxiang and Shuangfeng gentry families:** the Zeng household model of yielding and pressing, as remembered locally.
- **Liuyang and Pingjiang (Gan-speaking):** a different dialect stock, so different formulas for refusal.
- **Tujia of Longshan, Yongshun and Baojing:** ritual license to complain at weddings.
- **Miao of Huayuan, Fenghuang and Jishou:** sung assertion in courtship; contrast with Han wedding speech.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** antiphonal singing and village assemblies as spaces for a voice.
- **Women of Jiangyong:** Nüshu texts as private complaint written down.
- **Migrant workers back from the coast:** wage disputes abroad may change speech at home.
- **Hui traders of Changde:** market bargaining norms shaped by a trading minority.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Hunanese workers in Guangdong factories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite letters and modern media; mixed for minority speech; thin for measured conversation data in Xiang dialects.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese, Changsha Xiang (listening), classical Chinese for Zeng and Yuelu; Tujia and Miao for fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 维权 and petitioning 上访 are politically sensitive; protest records may be closed; Mao's 1927 Hunan peasant report is a contested political text if used.
