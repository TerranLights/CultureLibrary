## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha:** the city whose speech and media are taken as "Hunanese"; test whether rural people accept that.
- **Xiangtan and Shaoshan:** a revolutionary-heritage identity, with pilgrimage tourism.
- **Loudi, Shaoyang and Meishan uplands:** Old Xiang speakers with a ritual inheritance of their own.
- **Shaodong merchants:** an identity built on trade abroad.
- **Yueyang and Dongting Lake:** a lake people, with Yueyang Tower as an emblem.
- **Changde and Zhangjiajie:** Mandarin-leaning speech; Wuling 武陵 and Peach Blossom Spring 桃花源 as place myths.
- **Chenzhou and Yongzhou:** Tuhua villages and Guangdong ties.
- **Hakka of Rucheng, Guidong and Liuyang:** a second Han identity with its own migration story.
- **Tujia, Miao, Dong and Yao:** each with its own origin stories and its relation to the state's categories.
- **Waxiang, Bai, Hui and Uyghur communities:** small groups where classification is the question.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Hunanese in Guangdong, and Hunan native-place associations elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite and provincial identity; mixed for minority self-accounts; reclassification data exist but may be uneven.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Tujia, Miao and Yao for self-names and origin songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification, Miao frontier history, and the Uyghur community in Taoyuan; outsider nicknames can wound.
- **Held for review:** none.
