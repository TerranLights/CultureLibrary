## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha and Xiangtan:** revived school rites, shop openings and New Year media.
- **Old Xiang uplands and the Meishan counties:** almanac and ritual-specialist traditions for beginnings.
- **Dongting Lake counties:** fishing-season and flood-rebuilding starts.
- **Southern Hunan (Yanling, Ningyuan, Daoxian):** ancestral-sage sites and the oldest archaeological firsts.
- **Hakka villages of Rucheng and Guidong:** their own migration-origin stories and house rites.
- **Tujia of Yongshun, Longshan and Sangzhi:** an early New Year and Tima origin songs.
- **Miao of Huayuan, Fenghuang and Chengbu:** Nuo and flood-origin rites; new-rice festivals.
- **Dong of Tongdao, Xinhuang and Jingzhou:** a New Year on their own calendar.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Jiangyong:** vows to King Pan.
- **Waxiang speakers along the Yuan River:** their own lore of where they came from.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Hunanese abroad celebrating the Lunar New Year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gazetteers, archaeology and festivals; mixed for minority origin songs (recorded, but in uneven editions); thin for measured "fresh start" attitudes.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese (Chu manuscript, gazetteers); Tujia and Miao ritual language; Kam and Mien for Dong and Yao calendars.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual specialists may restrict some texts; the Chu silk manuscript's removal abroad is a contested heritage question.
- **Held for review:** none.
