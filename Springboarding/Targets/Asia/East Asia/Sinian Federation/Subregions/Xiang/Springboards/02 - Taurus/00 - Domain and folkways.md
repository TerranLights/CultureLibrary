# Xiang (湘): Possessions and value, the domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, a member state of the Sinian Federation (the lands of Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified and traditional script); Xiang 湘语 (New Xiang of Changsha, Old Xiang of Loudi and Shaoyang); Gan 赣语 of the eastern counties; Southwestern Mandarin of the northwest and far south; Hakka 客家话; Xiangnan Tuhua 湘南土话; Waxiang 瓦乡话; Tujia (Bizika); Western Hunan Miao (Xong); Dong (Kam); Mien and other Yao speech; the Nüshu 女书 script. **Concept in one line:** possessions and value: what is worth having, and how it is held (money, goods, resources, food, dress, wealth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chang-Zhu-Tan 长株潭 core** (Changsha, Zhuzhou, Xiangtan; New Xiang): the urban consumer center, to set against the countryside.
- **Dongting Lake plain** (Yueyang, Yiyang, Changde): lake fishing, polders and rice; Changde speaks Southwestern Mandarin.
- **Central hills, Loudi and Shaoyang** (Old Xiang; Meishan 梅山 hunting culture; Baoqing 宝庆 merchants): a separate trading tradition.
- **Hengyang and the Hengshan 衡山 pilgrimage area**: temple economy and pilgrim spending.
- **Eastern Gan-speaking belt** (Liuyang, Liling, Pingjiang, Chaling): craft industries such as fireworks and porcelain.
- **Southern Hunan** (Chenzhou, Yongzhou): Tuhua speakers, Hakka of Rucheng and Guidong, Yao of Jianghua and Jiangyong.
- **West Hunan** (Xiangxi prefecture, Zhangjiajie, Huaihua): Tujia, Miao, Dong, Waxiang speakers, Bai of Sangzhi; the Hongjiang merchant world.
- **Hui and Uyghur communities** (Changde's Taoyuan, Shaoyang): halal food and trade networks.
- **Hunanese outside the member state** (Guangdong labor migrants, Taiwan): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, excavated texts and Han elite writing; mixed for the minorities (ICH listings, 1950s surveys); thin for Waxiang and Tuhua speakers.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese, classical Chinese for slips and letters, Xiang dialect dictionaries; Tujia, Miao and Dong materials largely mediated through Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform and the Cultural Revolution as framings of wealth; ethnic minority materials written by outsiders; the member state's exact lands (listed as Hunan) are still to be confirmed by the author.
- **Held for review:** none.
