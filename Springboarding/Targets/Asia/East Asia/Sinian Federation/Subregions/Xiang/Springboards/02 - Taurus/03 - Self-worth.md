# Xiang (湘): Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, Sinian Federation member state (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha Xiang and Old Xiang speech for praise and insult words; Southwestern Mandarin of the west; Tujia, Miao and Dong sources mostly in Chinese. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha students and white-collar workers**: worth through exams, jobs and consumption.
- **Old Xiang sending counties** (Shaoyang, Loudi, Xinhua): migrant parents abroad in Guangdong, children at home; worth through remittances.
- **Laid-off workers of the Zhuzhou-Xiangtan industrial belt**: worth after the loss of a state job.
- **Women of the Nüshu and Tujia wedding-song areas**: genres in which women speak of their own standing.
- **Tujia**: a people recognized only in 1957; collective worth and recognition.
- **Miao of Xiangxi**: insider against outsider accounts of standing after centuries of frontier conflict.
- **Waxiang speakers** (Yuanling, Luxi, Guzhang): an identity registered under other names; check how they describe their own standing.
- **Hui and Uyghur of Taoyuan and Changde**: religious grounds of worth.
- **Elders in villages emptied by migration**: worth in old age without nearby children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; much psychology research uses national scales and student samples; good literary and media record; thin for minority self-accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese for Zeng's diaries; dialect dictionaries for praise and insult words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and mental illness; ethnic hierarchy and frontier history; provincial-pride texts can slide into stereotype (name variation, never one Hunanese voice).
- **Held for review:** none.
