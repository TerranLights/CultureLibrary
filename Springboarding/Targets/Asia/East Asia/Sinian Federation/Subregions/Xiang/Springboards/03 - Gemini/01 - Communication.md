# Xiang: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha and Loudi Xiang; Southwestern Mandarin of the northwest; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Kam (Dong). **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts locally as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha urban speakers against Old Xiang rural speakers (Loudi, Shuangfeng, Shaoyang):** test whether talk styles differ with dialect and town size.
- **Southwestern Mandarin speakers of Changde and Zhangjiajie:** a communication style shaped by another Chinese language within one state.
- **Hengyang and Yongzhou speakers:** the southern Xiang subgroups, often overlooked.
- **Xiangxi Miao (Huayuan, Fenghuang) and Chengbu Miao:** formal reasoning speech and song as channels.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang:** assembly oratory under the 款 tradition.
- **Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan:** ritual speech by the tima, in a language few still speak daily.
- **Yao of Jianghua:** Mien-speaking households and their switching to Chinese.
- **Deaf Hunanese:** a language community that crosses every dialect boundary.
- **Generations:** dialect-dominant elders against Putonghua-schooled grandchildren.
- **Returning migrant workers from Guangdong:** speech habits brought home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Changsha dialect description; mixed for pragmatics (much is national-level); thin for Deaf communication and for Waxiang and Bai speakers.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Xiang dialect writing conventions (homophone characters vary); IPA for Tujia, Miao and Kam sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual speech (tima chants, Miao reasoning speech) may be restricted to specialists or occasions; ask communities before recording. Mocking of accents online can shade into regional stereotype; treat it as data about attitudes only.
- **Held for review:** none.
