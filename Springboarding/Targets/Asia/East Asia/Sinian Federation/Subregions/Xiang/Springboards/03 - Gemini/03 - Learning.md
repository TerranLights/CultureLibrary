## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha middle-class families:** tutoring, after-school classes and parental coaching.
- **Rural Shaoyang, Loudi and Huaihua villages with many migrant parents:** grandparent-led learning.
- **Craft towns (Liling, Liuyang, Shaping):** learning a trade inside the family or workshop.
- **Hengyang and Yongzhou:** the home districts of Wang Fuzhi and Zhou Dunyi; how local memory of them enters teaching.
- **Xiangxi Miao (Fenghuang, Huayuan):** silversmith lineages, song learning, ritual apprenticeship.
- **Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan:** brocade learned by girls; language learned or lost at home.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Ningyuan; Huayao of Longhui:** initiation and craft teaching that differ by group.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** song and drama learned in village groups.
- **Jiangyong women and girls:** script learned from kin, now from classes.
- **Hakka and Tuhua speakers of the south:** whether children learn the home variety at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Huxiang thinkers and reform-era schools; mixed for crafts (heritage listings are official and promotional); thin for learning in Bai, Hui and Waxiang households.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; dialect for rhymes and craft words; Mien, Miao and Tujia terms for initiation and crafts (IPA sources).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation knowledge may not be open to outsiders; describe only what the community permits. Left-behind children are a vulnerable group; work from published studies.
- **Held for review:** none.
