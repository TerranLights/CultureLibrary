# Xiang: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese; Xiang and Southwestern Mandarin speech; Miao, Tujia and Mien (Yao) song and tale vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha students and young professionals:** museums, science centers, online explainers.
- **Rural central Hunan (Xiangtan, Shaoyang, Loudi):** curiosity about the outside world through migration.
- **Yongzhou and Daoxian:** a southern district with its own classical travel-writing heritage.
- **Dongting Lake communities:** birding, fishing knowledge and water lore as fields of curiosity.
- **Xiangxi Miao and Tujia:** both objects of outside curiosity and holders of their own question songs; separate the two.
- **Zhangjiajie Tujia and Bai of Sangzhi:** tourism brought strangers' questions to their doors.
- **Yao of Jianghua and the Huayao of Longhui:** Panhu tales and their versions; the rules on what may be asked.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** questions in courtship singing.
- **Returnees from abroad and from coastal cities:** "opened eyes" against stay-at-home neighbors.
- **Elders against children:** whether a child's questions are welcomed or hushed, to be studied household by household.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical travel and reform-era writing; mixed for present-day attitudes (national surveys, few provincial cuts); thin for minority question genres outside heritage files.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for the early texts; Standard Chinese; Miao and Tujia song texts often in Chinese transcription with IPA.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outsider exoticism about Xiangxi (gu, corpse-driving) can stigmatize Miao and Tujia communities; treat it as a record of outsiders' attitudes. Panhu tales and Yao charters may be sacred; quote only what is published.
- **Held for review:** none.
