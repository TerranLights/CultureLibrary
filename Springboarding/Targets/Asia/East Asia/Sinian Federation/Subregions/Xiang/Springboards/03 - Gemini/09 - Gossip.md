# Xiang: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha and other Xiang dialect words for idle and malicious talk; Southwestern Mandarin of the northwest; Miao and Tujia terms where accusation gossip is studied. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) is included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha neighborhoods and housing compounds:** dialect gossip, online owner groups, celebrity talk.
- **Market towns of southern and western Hunan (Wangcun, Fenghuang, Hongjiang):** small-town reputation in tight streets.
- **Miluo and the Dongting plain:** village talk as recorded in fiction.
- **Shaoyang and Loudi counties:** active county forums and migrant ties back home.
- **Xiangxi Miao villages:** accusation gossip and its stigma; sung mockery.
- **Tujia villages of Yongshun and Longshan:** fire-pit talk and reputation in small hamlets.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** drum-tower gatherings and teasing songs.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Huayao of Longhui:** close-knit mountain communities.
- **Hui of Changde:** talk within a minority faith community.
- **Women and men:** test whether talk differs, rather than assume; men's talk is often given another name.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical rumor (Qing and Republican Changsha) and for fiction; mixed for online gossip (fast-changing, often deleted); thin for minority communities outside the gu-accusation literature.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Changsha and Southwestern Mandarin slang (vulgar terms included); classical and late-Qing Chinese for tracts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rumor control is an official function in the Sinian Federation, and some rumor cases are politically sensitive; denunciation during political campaigns is sensitive history. Gu accusations still stigmatize living families: never name individuals. Treat "long-tongued woman" stereotypes as a claim to test.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: Cohen (1963) on Zhou Han; then search 周汉 反洋教 揭帖 for the tracts themselves.
2. Library: 芙蓉镇 and 马桥词典; index scenes and entries that turn on talk about others.
3. CNKI: 湘西 蛊 污名 and 火塘 闲谈 土家族, for accusation and everyday gossip.
4. Open web: browse two county Tieba forums for a month; log rumor threads and refutations.
5. piyao.org.cn: search 湖南 and 长沙 for refuted rumors by year.
