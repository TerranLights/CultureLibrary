**Target:** Xiang 湘, the Hunan 湖南 member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and Classical Chinese (genealogies); Xiang kin vocabularies (Changsha, Loudi, Shuangfeng, Hengyang); Gan, Hakka and Southwestern Mandarin kin words; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Kam and Yao terms where published. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lou-Shao and Hengyang lineage villages:** strong written lineages, to test how far kin duties still reach.
- **Changsha and other cities:** small families, with kin kept close through phones and festivals.
- **Hakka and Gan counties of the east and southeast:** lineages with other origin stories and hall traditions.
- **Tujia of Xiangxi and Zhangjiajie:** chieftain lineages, founding ancestors and later Han-style genealogies.
- **Xiangxi Miao:** surname groups and ancestor rites distinct from Han lineage forms.
- **Dong of the southwest:** lineage branches inside village leagues.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Jiangyong:** clan charters and the twelve surnames.
- **Bai of Sangzhi and Hui of Changde and Shaoyang:** minority lineages with migration stories from far away.
- **Uyghurs of Taoyuan:** a single-surname community.
- Hunanese families split by migration to coastal cities: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han genealogies and survey data; mixed for minority lineages, where Han-style registers may hide older forms; thin for Waxiang and Tuhua-speaking families.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for genealogies; Xiang dialect kin terms; Tujia, Miao, Kam and Yao for kin words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the one-child era and hidden births; lineage revival and its politics; ancestral rites kept within the family; Uyghur and Hui identity.
- **Held for review:** none.
