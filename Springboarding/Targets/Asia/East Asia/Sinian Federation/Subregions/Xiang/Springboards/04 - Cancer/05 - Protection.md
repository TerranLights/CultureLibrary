## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dongting Lake polder villages:** protection against water, collective and state-led.
- **Lou-Shao and Meishan hill country:** ritual protection of households and hunters.
- **Changsha and other cities:** locks, cameras, insurance and the state as protector.
- **Xiangxi Miao around Fenghuang and Huayuan:** a people once walled off as a danger; their own protective rites.
- **Tujia of Longshan, Yongshun and Zhangjiajie:** ritual specialists and household guardians.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang:** the grandmother guardian and village fire watch in wooden villages.
- **Yao of Jianghua:** protective rites tied to initiation and ancestors.
- **Hakka and Gan counties of the east:** lineage defense and fortified building traditions.
- **Hui of Changde and Shaoyang:** protection within a faith community.
- Hunanese migrant families in factory towns: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for militia history, floods and disasters; mixed for ritual protection; thin and scattered for the smaller Yao and Waxiang communities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Xiang and Southwestern Mandarin ritual vocabularies; Tujia, Miao, Kam and Yao for ritual words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations of sorcery made against Miao women in western Hunan (stigma; handle with care and do not repeat as fact); ritual texts kept by specialists; domestic violence; the frontier wall's history of conquest.
- **Held for review:** none.
