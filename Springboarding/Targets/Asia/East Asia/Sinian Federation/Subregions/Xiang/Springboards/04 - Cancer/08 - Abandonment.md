# Xiang 湘: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, Hunan 湖南 within the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese (Han rhapsodies, Qing charity and gazetteer records); Xiang dialect words for leaving and being left; Southwestern Mandarin of the west; Tujia, Miao, Kam and Yao tale and lament terms. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Out-migration counties of Shaoyang, Loudi, Hengyang, Yongzhou and Huaihua:** whole villages of children, wives and elders left behind.
- **Changsha:** divorce, elders living alone and urban loneliness.
- **Southern Hunan (Hengyang, Shaoyang):** the counties named in the reported adoption cases, to test against neighbors.
- **Old industrial towns (Lengshuijiang, Lianyuan):** places left by industry.
- **Tujia and Miao of Xiangxi:** emptied mountain villages and relocation.
- **Dong of the southwest:** village solidarity tested by out-migration.
- **Yao of Jianghua:** upland villages losing their young.
- **Hakka and Gan counties of the east:** lineage support for orphans and widows.
- **Waxiang and Tuhua communities:** language loss as a form of being left behind, to test.
- Hunanese adoptees raised abroad: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for left-behind children and elders; mixed for historical foundling records; thin and sensitive for infant abandonment in the family-planning era.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Xiang dialects; Tujia, Miao, Kam and Yao for tales and laments.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family-planning enforcement and infant trafficking; female infanticide in history; adoptees' and birth families' privacy; political figures' family histories.
- **Held for review:** none.
