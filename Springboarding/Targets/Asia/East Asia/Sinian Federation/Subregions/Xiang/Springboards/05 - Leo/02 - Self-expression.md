# Xiang: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (pre-war Hunan), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha Xiang; Tuhua and Southwestern Mandarin of the south and west; Tujia, Dong, Miao and Yao song vocabularies; the Nüshu script of Jiangyong. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha's young urban residents:** livehouses, dialect comedy and online posting; expression in a media hub.
- **Lou-Shao rural counties:** test the reputation for blunt speech against what people actually say in public.
- **Xiangnan Tuhua speakers (Chenzhou, Yongzhou):** switching between Tuhua and Southwestern Mandarin, and what each register lets them say.
- **Jiangyong women:** Nüshu as a private channel, now turned into heritage.
- **Tujia of Longshan and Yongshun:** brides' laments and the hand-waving dance as shared expression.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** voice in choir and in duet; personal song inside collective form.
- **Miao of Xiangxi and Huayao of Longhui:** festival dress and song; display tied to clan and age.
- **Hui of Changde and Shaoyang:** dress and diet as religious expression in Han-majority towns.
- **Returned migrant workers:** styles and speech brought back from coastal cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and Nüshu scholarship; mixed for everyday speech; thin for minority individual voice outside heritage framings.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Changsha Xiang; Nüshu; Tujia, Dong and Miao for songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political speech is restricted and sources may be self-censored; Nüshu texts were often burned with their owners, so surviving corpora are partial.
- **Held for review:** none.
