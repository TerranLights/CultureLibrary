# Xiang: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (pre-war Hunan), one of the Sinian Federation's nine member states, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Xiang (Changsha, Xiangxiang and Lou-Shao forms); Southwestern Mandarin of Changde and the west; Tujia, Miao, Dong, Yao and Bai self-names and praise songs. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xiangxiang 湘乡 and Shuangfeng:** Xiang Army home counties; family pride in ancestors who served.
- **Changsha and Xiangtan:** academy, revolutionary and media pride concentrated in the capital region.
- **Hengyang:** Wang Fuzhi's home; a second center of scholarly pride.
- **Lou-Shao counties:** the 霸蛮 label is often attached here; test it against local accounts.
- **Liuyang and the Gan-speaking east:** songs and fireworks as place pride; Gan speakers within a Xiang-named state.
- **Tujia of Longshan, Yongshun and Sangzhi:** pride rebuilt after recognition as a distinct people in the 1950s.
- **Miao of Xiangxi and Chengbu:** silver, song and stars; pride under a tourism spotlight.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Yao of Jianghua:** choir song and the Panwang festival as group emblems.
- **Bai of Sangzhi and Uyghurs of Taoyuan 桃源:** pride in distant origins carried by small communities.
- **Hunanese in Taiwan and Guangdong (comparison only):** how pride in the home province is kept abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite and literary pride (Platt, Zeng, Wang Fuzhi); mixed for ordinary people; thin for small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** classical and modern Chinese; Xiang dialects; Tujia and Miao for self-names and songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mao-related sites and Xiang Army memory are politically charged; claims of a "Hunan character" slide into stereotype and must be tested, not repeated.
- **Held for review:** none.
