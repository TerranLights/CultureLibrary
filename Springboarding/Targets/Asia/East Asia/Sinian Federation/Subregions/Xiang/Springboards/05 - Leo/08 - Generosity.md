*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (pre-war Hunan), a member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Xiang and Southwestern Mandarin terms for treating and banquets; Dong, Miao, Tujia and Yao for feasts and hospitality rites. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha's middle class:** formal donation, volunteer campaigns, splitting the bill.
- **Lou-Shao and Hengyang villages:** gift-money burdens at frequent banquets.
- **Old trade towns (Xiangtan, Hongjiang 洪江):** merchant sponsorship and guild relief.
- **Wangcheng:** Lei Feng's birthplace; local volunteering under a national model.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang:** village-wide feasts and gate-wine for guests.
- **Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan:** house-raising feasts and shared labor.
- **Yao of Jianghua:** festival hosting and ancestor feasts.
- **Hui of Changde and Shaoyang:** almsgiving and mosque-based relief.
- **Sending villages of out-migrants:** remittances as giving across distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on banquet gift economies and official charity; mixed for lineage charity (much is undigitized); thin for minority feasting outside tourism.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Xiang for village talk; Dong, Tujia, Miao and Yao for ritual terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gift-giving shades into bribery in official settings; campaigns against banquets politicize ordinary giving; private ledgers name families and need consent.
- **Held for review:** none.
