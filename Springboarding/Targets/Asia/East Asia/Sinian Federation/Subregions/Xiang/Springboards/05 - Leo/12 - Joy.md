# Xiang: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (pre-war Hunan), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** classical and Standard Chinese; Changsha and Changde speech; Southwestern Mandarin of the northwest; Tujia, Bai, Miao and Dong for festival and work songs. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha:** joy sold as media and city brand; nightlife; measured well-being.
- **Yueyang and the lake shore:** the tower's joy-and-care ethic; boat-festival crowds.
- **Changde:** comic ballad singing in Southwestern Mandarin.
- **Xiangtan and Hengyang:** pilgrimage joy at Mount Heng.
- **Sangzhi's Bai and Tujia:** folk songs as shared merriment.
- **Miao of Huayuan:** the autumn festival as a seasonal peak.
- **Tujia of Longshan and Yongshun:** an early new year and its own calendar of gladness.
- **Dong of Tongdao:** new-year songs and choir gatherings.
- **Older rural residents:** funerals of the long-lived as "joyous" events.
- **People in psychiatric care:** what joy and its absence mean to them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey well-being and folk performance; mixed for festivals (tourist framing); thin for minority-language emotion words.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Changde and Changsha speech; Tujia, Bai, Miao and Dong for song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** happiness rankings are promotional; festive funerals have drawn official criticism; mental-health data need care with privacy.
- **Held for review:** none.
