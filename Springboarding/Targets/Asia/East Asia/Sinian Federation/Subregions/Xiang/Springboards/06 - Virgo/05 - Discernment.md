## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha's young urban adults:** dating screens, food and drink trends, scam exposure.
- **Older Han villagers of the Lou–Shao and Hengyang hills:** matchmakers, geomancers and day-selection in decisions.
- **Xiangxi Tujia communities (Longshan, Yongshun, Baojing):** the Tima's diagnostic role.
- **Xiangxi Miao communities (Huayuan, Fenghuang, Jishou):** the Badai, and markets as places of judgment.
- **Dong and Miao of Huaihua:** cattle markets and the judging of animals.
- **Anhua and Yueyang tea districts:** professional tasting and grading.
- **Liuyang and Changsha dealers:** stone, porcelain and antiques.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Bai of Sangzhi:** whose counsel is sought, to be found.
- **Rural elders targeted by fraud:** which schemes reach them, and whose advice they weigh.
- **Waxiang speakers:** test whether their own words for shrewd and naive differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on marriage customs and fraud; mixed on taste; thin on minority ideas of judgment.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Xiang slang; Tujia and Miao for ritual vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Xiangxi ritual specialists should be approached as communities present them, not as "superstition"; claims about gu poison (蛊) have long stigmatized Miao women and must be treated as slander, not data; rumor rebuttal is state framing.
- **Held for review:** none.
