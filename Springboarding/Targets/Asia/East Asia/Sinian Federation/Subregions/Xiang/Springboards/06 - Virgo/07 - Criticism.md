*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha media workers and audiences:** professional critique and online roasting meet here.
- **Miluo and Yueyang:** the remonstrance legend as local identity.
- **Southern Hunan (Daoxian, Yongzhou):** a place where political criticism once turned violent; memory and silence.
- **Older Han villagers:** scolding, teasing and the face rules of family criticism.
- **Tujia women of Yongshun and Longshan:** whether ritual lament gives room for complaint that ordinary speech would not.
- **Jiangyong women's Nüshu circles:** how letters between sworn sisters voice grievance, if they do.
- **Miao and Dong singers:** teasing and rebuke in antiphonal song.
- **State-sector workers in Zhuzhou and Xiangtan:** memories of workplace criticism meetings.
- **Hui of Changde:** criticism inside mosque communities, to be found.
- **Students:** teacher feedback, red-pen marking and class rankings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on classical and reform-era criticism; mixed on everyday family criticism; thin on minority teasing traditions outside song.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Changsha speech; Tujia, Miao and Dong for song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cultural Revolution dissent and killings, land-reform "speaking bitterness" and present-day political criticism are restricted inside China; some sources sit only in overseas libraries; protect people's identities in online material.
- **Held for review:** none.
