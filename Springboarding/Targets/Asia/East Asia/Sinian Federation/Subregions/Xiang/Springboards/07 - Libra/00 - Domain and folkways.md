# Xiang: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, a member state of the Sinian Federation (lands: Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified and traditional script; classical Chinese for older texts); Xiang 湘语 (New Xiang of Changsha, Old Xiang of Loudi-Shaoyang); Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Dong and Yao (Mien) terms where known. **Concept in one line:** what a fair bargain and a good partnership are: marriage, contracts, rivals, settling disputes, gender ways, and the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha-Zhuzhou-Xiangtan core:** New Xiang urban life; courts and media cluster here.
- **Dongting Lake plain (Yueyang, Yiyang, Changde):** polder communities with their own water bargains.
- **Lou-Shao Old Xiang heartland (Loudi, Shaoyang, Shuangfeng):** Meishan 梅山 ritual tradition, a lead to test for oaths and settlements.
- **Xiangnan (Hengyang, Chenzhou, Yongzhou):** Tuhua 土话 speakers; a different marriage-exchange web.
- **Eastern Gan- and Hakka-speaking counties (Liuyang, Liling, Pingjiang, Rucheng):** separate dialect and lineage worlds.
- **Tujia of Yongshun, Longshan, Baojing and Zhangjiajie:** tusi 土司 rule until the 1720s-30s reforms.
- **Xiangxi Miao (Fenghuang, Huayuan, Jishou) and southwestern Miao (Chengbu, Suining, Mayang):** arbitration and marriage fairs differ by area.
- **Dong of Tongdao, Xinhuang, Zhijiang and Jingzhou:** kuan alliances.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Jiangyong:** sworn-sibling and uxorilocal forms.
- **Bai of Sangzhi; Hui of Changde and Shaoyang; Uyghur of Taoyuan; Waxiang 瓦乡 speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang:** small communities with distinct marriage rules *check*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han written law, clan records and media; mixed for Tujia, Miao, Dong and Yao, where much is held in county offices or oral form.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; classical Chinese and Qin-Han script; Xiang (both subgroups), Gan, Southwestern Mandarin; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Dong and Mien vocabularies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** court-record access shrank recently; ethnic classification and Qing frontier violence are politically charged; bride-price campaigns are state-driven, so official sources argue a side. Nüshu is heavily commercialized.
- **Lands:** this member state's lands (Hunan) are a working list still to be confirmed by the author at Stage 0.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Hunan Library and CNKI 中国知网 for "湖南 族规 调解" and "民商事习惯 湖南".
2. Pull Hunan entries from the 1930 civil-customs survey, then Shi Qigui's chapters on marriage and arbitration.
3. Query China Judgments Online for Hunan courts, case type 离婚纠纷 and 合同纠纷, to map what reaches court.
4. Search "侗款 通道" and "女书 老同" for the Dong and Jiangyong partnership forms.
5. Read the Mawangdui 《经法》 in a collected edition for the classical baseline on law and balance.
