## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha:** provincial offices, media and exam centers; the densest networks of connection.
- **Hengyang:** the 2013 bribery case's home ground; Hengzhou Xiang speakers.
- **Chenzhou and Xiangnan mining towns:** Tuhua speakers; resource money and collusion cases.
- **Anhua and Xinhua uplands:** tea economy and Meishan villages; ties of place and lineage.
- **Dongting Lake counties:** lineage blocs in large polder villages.
- **Dong villages of Tongdao and Suining:** elder-arbiter traditions.
- **Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan and Miao of Huayuan and Fenghuang:** cadre quotas and the history of outsider officials after chieftain rule ended.
- **Yao of Jianghua:** autonomous-county appointment rules.
- **Hui of Changde:** community leaders as arbiters inside and outside the mosque *check*.
- **Gan-speaking Pingjiang and Liuyang:** strong lineage networks to test against the Xiang core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official anticorruption material and imperial exam history; mixed for everyday favoritism, which is mostly in ethnographies and news.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Qing administrative Chinese; Xiang and Gan dialects for village fieldwork; Dong for elders' titles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption and election cases are politically sensitive; official sources frame them as resolved; interviewees on favoritism may be at risk.
