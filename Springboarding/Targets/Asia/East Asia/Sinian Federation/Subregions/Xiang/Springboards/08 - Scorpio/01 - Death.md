# Xiang: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, a member state of the Sinian Federation (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Xiang 湘语 (New and Old Xiang); Southwestern Mandarin, Gan, Hakka, Tuhua and Waxiang inside Hunan; Tujia, Xong (Miao), Kam (Dong), Iu Mien (Yao). **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, including grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha and Xiangtan:** funeral-home deaths and cremation; hospice beginning in teaching hospitals.
- **Old Xiang counties (Shaoyang, Loudi, Shuangfeng):** household ritual masters and statuettes; long wakes.
- **Dongting Lake plain:** drowning and flood deaths in memory; fishing households' rites *check*.
- **Xiangxi Tujia and Miao:** separate soul beliefs and funeral music; the corpse-driving legend's home ground.
- **Waxiang speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang:** a distinct Han-classified group with its own customs *check*.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang:** village-level mourning around drum towers *check*.
- **Yao of Jianghua and Jiangyong:** Daoist-influenced Yao funerals; Tuhua-speaking Han neighbors.
- **Hui and Uyghur households:** Islamic burial within a day.
- **Christians and Buddhists (Nanyue Hengshan 南岳衡山 pilgrims):** rival accounts of what follows death.
- Hunanese working away from home: comparison only, for bodies sent back to home counties and wakes kept by phone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical and archaeological sources and ritual ethnography; mixed for mortality statistics by county; thin for grief as felt.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Chu and Han texts; Xiang dialects for wake songs; Tujia, Xong and Mien for minority rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine and political deaths of 1950–1976 are restricted; rural suicide is sensitive; ritual masters' texts may be kept from outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
