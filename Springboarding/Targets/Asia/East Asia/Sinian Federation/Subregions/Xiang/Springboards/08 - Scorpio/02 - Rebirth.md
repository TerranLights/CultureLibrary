# Xiang: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, a member state of the Sinian Federation (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Xiang 湘语; Southwestern Mandarin, Gan, Hakka, Tuhua and Waxiang varieties in Hunan; Tujia, Xong (Miao), Kam (Dong), Iu Mien (Yao). **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha intelligentsia and reform tradition:** "new people" rhetoric; secular remaking.
- **Hengshan and Buddhist households across the Xiang valley:** merit and rebirth vocabulary.
- **Shaoyang–Loudi household Daoist masters:** ordination and ritual names *check*.
- **Yao of Jianghua, Jiangyong and Ningyuan:** 度戒 as a coming of age.
- **Tujia of Longshan, Yongshun and Baojing:** heritage revival of dance and language.
- **Miao of Fenghuang and Huayuan:** festival revival alongside tourism.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang:** village festivals and song revival *check*.
- **Dongting Lake resettled households:** rebuilding after flood.
- **Christian converts and Muslim communities:** conversion against kin expectations.
- Hunanese who resettled far from home: comparison only, for starting over as migrants rather than inside the province.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chan texts, the reform era and festival revival; mixed for minority rites; thin for personal recovery stories.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Chan records; Mien and Tujia for ritual terms; Xiang dialects for village testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** thought reform, labor camps and unregistered churches are restricted topics; drug rehabilitation records are closed; Yao ordination texts belong to their masters.
- **Held for review:** none.
