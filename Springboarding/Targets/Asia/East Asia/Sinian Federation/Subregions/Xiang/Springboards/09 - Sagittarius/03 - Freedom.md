# Xiang: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), a Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese, including late-Qing reform prose; Xiang and Gan varieties for oral sources; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao and the Nüshu 女书 script of Jiangyong for women's and minority voices. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha students and press** (1897-1920s) against **rural Xiang counties** of the 1927 peasant movement and 1950s land reform: liberation as ideas, against liberation as land.
- **Tujia** of the former Tusi domains (Yongshun, Longshan, Baojing): collective self-rule lost in the 1720s.
- **Miao** of the old frontier (Fenghuang, Huayuan, Jishou, Mayang): walls, military colonies and risings.
- **Women of Jiangyong** (Nüshu) and **Tujia brides**: freedom within marriage customs.
- **Dong** villages of Tongdao and Jingzhou, and **Yao** of Jianghua: frontier autonomy in different forms.
- **Liuyang and the Gan-speaking east:** Tan Sitong's town; later dissent.
- **Migrants** out of Shaoyang, Yongzhou and Loudi: movement and the hukou.
- **Hui** of Changde; **Hakka** of Rucheng; **Waxiang** of Yuanling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1897-1927 Changsha print; mixed for minority history (Qing records outnumber Tujia and Miao voices); thin and risky for present-day speech.
- **Languages needed:** late-Qing and Republican Chinese; Nüshu (specialist reading); Tujia and Miao song texts in Chinese transcription.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1989 cases, censorship and political imprisonment are restricted topics inside the PRC; poverty-alleviation sites are showcases, so seek independent voices. Law (the 1922 provincial charter; hukou rules) goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
