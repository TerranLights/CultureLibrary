# Xiang: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), Sinian Federation member state, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (simplified; classical for Chan and Qing texts); Xiang sayings; Mien and other minority terms for ordination and training, through specialists. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Xiangzhong counties (Xiangxiang, Shuangfeng, Loudi):** the Xiang Army's home ground, where Zeng-style regimen is promoted as local heritage, a claim to test.
- **Changsha's elite-school families against rural boarding pupils:** two very different study regimes.
- **Monastics at Nanyue and Guishan:** discipline as a whole way of life.
- **Yao of Jianghua and the Longhui Huayao:** discipline through ordination ordeals.
- **Hui of Changde:** fasting and prayer as personal discipline, set beside school and work rhythms.
- **Meishan martial-arts and opera lineages:** discipline passed master to pupil.
- **Party members and officials:** a separate, codified discipline with its own sanctions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gentry, military and Party texts; mixed for family practice, which needs surveys and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Chan and Qing material; Mien for Yao rites; Xiang speech for family sayings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Party discipline is politically sensitive; corporal punishment involves children; ordination rites are shared only as communities allow.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Zeng Guofan's diaries for his daily rules; list each rule and when he says he broke it.
2. Search CNKI for 沩山警策 and 南岳 清规 to gather studies of Hunan monastic discipline.
3. Search 湖南 体罚 儿童 调查 and 湖南 中学 管理 寄宿 for family and school studies.
4. Browse the Hunan discipline-inspection website for the categories of violations it lists.
5. Search 度戒 瑶族 江华 for ethnographies of the rite.
