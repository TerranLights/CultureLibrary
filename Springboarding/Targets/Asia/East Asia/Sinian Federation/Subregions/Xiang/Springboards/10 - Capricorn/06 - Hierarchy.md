## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Single-lineage villages (Yueyang's Zhangguying, Hakka villages of Rucheng):** seniority written into buildings and names.
- **Provincial and county cadres:** a codified rank ladder that also orders social life outside work.
- **Xiangxi Miao and Tujia:** a history of being ranked by the state, which may shape how rank is spoken of now.
- **Waxiang speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang:** standing tied to how they are classified.
- **Lowland and mountain Yao of Jianghua and Ningyuan:** a rank distinction drawn by the old registers, to test against current self-terms.
- **Rural-registered workers in Changsha:** ranked by residence papers inside the provincial capital.
- **Former merchant towns (Hongjiang, Jinshi):** commercial rank beside gentry and official rank.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lineage and official rank; mixed for minority internal ranks; class-label records are uneven by county.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Qing and genealogy texts; Changsha and Hakka speech; Mien, Miao, Dong and Waxiang for internal terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Qing frontier categories are offensive and must be quoted only as history; class-label histories touch on persecution; ethnic classification is a political topic; cadre rank data can be sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.
