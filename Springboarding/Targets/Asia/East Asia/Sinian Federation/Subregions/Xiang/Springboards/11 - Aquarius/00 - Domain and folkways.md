## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chang-Zhu-Tan 长株潭 (Changsha, Zhuzhou, Xiangtan):** urban associational life, workplace (单位) communities.
- **Dongting Lake country (Yueyang, Yiyang, Changde):** polder and fishing communities organized around dikes.
- **Central Hunan (Loudi, Shaoyang, Xiangxiang):** Old Xiang speakers, the Meishan 梅山 uplands, Xiang Army home counties.
- **Gan-speaking east (Liuyang, Pingjiang, Liling) and the south (Hengyang, Chenzhou, Yongzhou):** separate dialect communities, Tuhua and Hakka enclaves.
- **Xiangxi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture (Jishou, Fenghuang, Yongshun, Longshan):** Tujia and Miao village and festival bonds.
- **Huaihua:** Dong and Miao counties (Tongdao, Xinhuang, Zhijiang, Jingzhou, Mayang); Waxiang speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang.
- **Zhangjiajie (Sangzhi):** Bai and Tujia communities.
- **Jianghua and Jiangyong (Yongzhou):** Yao communities.
- **Hui and the small Uyghur-descended community of Taoyuan County:** Muslim congregations as communities, *check*.
- **Comparison only:** Hunanese in Guangdong and abroad, and the federation's other member states.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite and revolutionary history, mixed for everyday sociability, thin for minority associations outside heritage writing.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Xiang varieties for oral sources; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Dong and Mien for field terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** associations, protest and revolutionary memory are politically managed; official sources present one voice. Minority festivals are often staged for tourism; separate staged and lived forms. The member state's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for Hunan.
- **Held for review:** none.
