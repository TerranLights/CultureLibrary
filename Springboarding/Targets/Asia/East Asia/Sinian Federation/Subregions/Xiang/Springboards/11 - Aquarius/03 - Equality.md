# Xiang: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese, Classical and Song-era documentary Chinese; Changsha and Old Xiang speech; Southwestern Mandarin in the west; Tuhua of the south; Nüshu 女书 texts of Jiangyong; Tujia and Xiangxi Miao. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha and Chang-Zhu-Tan against the rural west and south:** the sharpest income and service gaps inside the state.
- **Liuyang and Xiangxiang:** the home counties of reformers and their opponents, for the elite debate on rank.
- **Jiangyong and Daoxian women's communities:** Nüshu as a record of unequal standing.
- **Xiangxi Tujia and Miao (Yongshun, Longshan, Huayuan, Fenghuang):** ethnic recognition and poverty relief.
- **Waxiang speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang:** a group whose classification was long disputed, *check*.
- **Labor-sending counties (Shaoyang, Loudi, Yongzhou):** hukou, migrant parents and children left behind.
- **Dongting Lake fishers and farmers:** a leveling rising in the 12th century and land reform in the 20th.
- **Hui of Changde and Shaoyang; Yao of Jianghua; Dong of Tongdao:** minority standing in mostly Han counties.
- **Comparison only:** Hunanese migrant workers in Guangdong.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite and revolutionary texts on equality; mixed for gender and income data; thin for minority voices outside official recognition stories.
- **Languages needed:** Standard, Classical and Song documentary Chinese; Xiang for oral histories; Nüshu reading; Tujia and Xiangxi Miao for field terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform and its violence, poverty statistics and ethnic classification are officially managed; show-piece villages present one voice. Nüshu is now heritage and tourism; separate revived from inherited use. Gender norms vary by county and generation: no single voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
