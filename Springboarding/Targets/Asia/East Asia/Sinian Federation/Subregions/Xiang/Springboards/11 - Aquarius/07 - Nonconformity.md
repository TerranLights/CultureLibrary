# Xiang: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), a member state of the Sinian Federation, East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese (art criticism, Qing tracts); Changsha Xiang and the Old Xiang of the Meishan uplands; Southwestern Mandarin of Xiangxi; Xiangxi Miao and Tujia for ritual terms. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far that departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Changsha's young urban scenes (Jiefang West Road, campus districts):** chosen difference in style, music and sexuality.
- **Xiangtan and Fenghuang artists:** the eccentric artist as a local honor.
- **Yongzhou (Lingling):** a Tang eccentric's home, for the older form.
- **Meishan uplands (Xinhua, Anhua, Lengshuijiang):** a ritual tradition long called heterodox.
- **Xiangxi Miao women (Huayuan, Fenghuang, Jishou):** difference imposed by accusation.
- **Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan:** ritual specialists and how officials ranked them.
- **Christians of Changsha, Hengyang and Changde; Hui congregations:** religious minorities and their standing.
- **1968 Changsha student dissenters:** principled dissent and its price.
- **Dong of Tongdao and Yao of Jianghua:** custom that outsiders call strange, and how insiders answer, *check*.
- **Comparison only:** Hunanese artists and dissidents abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for artists and elite eccentrics; mixed for youth subcultures (online, fast-changing); thin for minority and religious nonconformity outside official or outsider writing.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Xiang and Old Xiang for oral sources; Xiangxi Miao and Tujia for ritual terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political dissent, unregistered religion and LGBTQ organizing are restricted, and groups may close or disappear; protect informants. *Gu* and "bandit" labels are stigmatizing outsider claims; record them as claims. Some Meishan and Miao ritual knowledge is restricted by its keepers.
- **Held for review:** none.
