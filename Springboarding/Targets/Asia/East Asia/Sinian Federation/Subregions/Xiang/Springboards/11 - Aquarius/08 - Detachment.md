# Xiang: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘 (Hunan 湖南), member state of the Sinian Federation; East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Classical Chinese, including Chan and Daoist texts and Han silk manuscripts; Xiang speech of Changsha, Hengyang and Changde; Southwestern Mandarin of Xiangxi; Tujia and Xiangxi Miao for lament and funeral song. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms count as calm or impartial, others as coldness or withdrawal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nanyue and Hengyang:** monks, Daoists, pilgrims and retreat-goers; religious detachment as a practice.
- **Ningxiang and Shimen:** Chan temple counties outside Hengshan, for local variation.
- **Changsha's young professionals:** *foxi* and lying-flat talk, and the demand to strive.
- **Yongzhou (Lingling):** exile and solitude as a literary heritage.
- **Tujia of Yongshun, Longshan and Sangzhi:** laments and funeral songs, for display set against reserve.
- **Xiangxi Miao and Huaihua Dong:** funeral and mourning customs, still to be mapped.
- **Empty-nest elders in labor-sending counties (Shaoyang, Loudi, Yiyang):** isolation not chosen.
- **Dongting Lake fishing households:** working alone on the water, *check*.
- **Hui of Changde:** mourning rules of a Muslim community.
- **Comparison only:** Hunanese monks and teachers elsewhere in the federation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buddhist and Daoist texts and sites; mixed for elders' isolation (national surveys, thin provincial breakdowns); thin for everyday emotional reserve by county or people.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese (Chan, Daoist and Han manuscript styles); Standard Chinese; Xiang for oral sources; Tujia and Xiangxi Miao for laments.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lying flat is politically criticized and posts are removed; mental-health sources need care with personal data. Temple and retreat life is partly commercial; separate practice from marketing. Funeral customs are under reform campaigns, so practice may be hidden.
- **Held for review:** none.
