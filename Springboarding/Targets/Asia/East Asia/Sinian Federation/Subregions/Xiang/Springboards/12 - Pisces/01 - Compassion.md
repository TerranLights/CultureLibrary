# Xiang: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, Sinian Federation member state (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha and Lou-Shao Xiang; Southwestern Mandarin of the northwest; Tujia, Xiangxi Miao, Kam and Mien terms where communities publish them. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Changsha's urban middle class against villages of the Xiang and Zi valleys: different channels of help (app crowdfunding, the work unit, kin and neighbors).
- Buddhist lay followers around Nanyue Hengshan and the Changsha temples: merit and animal release as motives.
- Christian congregations in Changsha and Yueyang: church relief, and the memory of missionary hospitals.
- Hui communities of Changde and Shaoyang: alms and mosque-based help on their own terms.
- Tujia and Miao villages of Xiangxi, Dong villages of Tongdao and Xinhuang, Yao of Jianghua: collective help at funerals, fires and building.
- Migrant-sending counties (Shaoyang, Loudi, Yongzhou): whether compassion travels by phone and remittance.
- Hunanese communities elsewhere: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; charity history and disaster news are rich, provincial numbers on everyday giving are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and classical Chinese; Changsha Xiang and Southwestern Mandarin for the pity words; minority languages for village mutual aid.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** poverty and disaster reporting can be shaped by official framing; mercy toward offenders belongs to Libra and Capricorn (law), not here.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *Renxue*'s opening sections on 仁 and 通 and list the terms Tan Sitong uses for fellow-feeling.
2. Search digitized Qing Changsha and Xiangtan gazetteers for 善堂 and 育婴堂 and note founders, dates and rules of admission.
3. Search CNKI for 湖南 善堂 慈善 and 作孽 方言 to find historians and dialectologists.
4. Pull Hunan Charity Federation annual reports for five recent years and list causes by amount.
5. Search newspaper archives for 2008 郴州 冰灾 互助 and compare praised helpers with blamed officials.
