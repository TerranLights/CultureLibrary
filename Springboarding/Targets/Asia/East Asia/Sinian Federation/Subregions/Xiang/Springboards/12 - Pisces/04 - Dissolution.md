# Xiang: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, Sinian Federation member state (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard and classical Chinese (Chan and Neo-Confucian texts); Xiang; Northern and Southern Tujia, whose shift to Chinese is itself a dissolution question; Waxiang; Xiangxi Miao. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Buddhist lay circles around Hengshan and Ningxiang: no-self as practiced, not only read.
- Tujia of Longshan and Yongshun: crowd dance, a fading language and the memory of a dissolved chiefdom.
- Waxiang speakers of Yuanling, Luxi and Guzhang: an identity between categories.
- Yuan and Zi valley families moved for reservoirs: places dissolved under water.
- Changsha's young adults: club, fandom and online crowds as modern merging.
- Rural elders of Shaoyang and Loudi with children away: dementia met by kin or by no one.
- Dong of Tongdao and Yao of Jianghua: collective song and assimilation pressures.
- Children of Hunanese migrants raised in other provinces: comparison only, for dialect and identity fading across one generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chan and Neo-Confucian texts and heritage dances; mixed for language shift; thin for lived self-loss outside ritual and nightlife.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Standard Chinese; Tujia and Waxiang for shift studies; Xiang for wake songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** assimilation and resettlement are politically sensitive; cremation enforcement has caused local protest; group-merging religious movements may be banned and hard to study.
- **Held for review:** none.
