## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Miao of Fenghuang and Huayuan, and Han descendants of the frontier colonies: the two sides of the old wall.
- Tujia of Yongshun and Longshan: a former chiefdom's line against the empire.
- Dong of Tongdao and Xinhuang: villages that ritualize the gate.
- Lineage villages of Yueyang and the Xiang valley: kin walls and halls.
- Changsha's gated housing estates: privacy, property management and neighbors.
- Dongting Lake polder communities: water as the moving boundary.
- Gan-speaking east (Liuyang, Liling) and Hakka south (Rucheng): dialect lines as social lines.
- Yao of Jiangyong and Jianghua; the Hui of Changde: community edges kept by custom and faith.
- Hunanese neighborhoods in Shenzhen and Dongguan: comparison only, for in-group lines redrawn among fellow provincials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Miao frontier (Qing records, recent histories); mixed for lineage space; thin for personal and emotional boundaries outside psychology papers.
- **Languages needed:** Qing documentary Chinese; Standard Chinese; Miao and Tujia for frontier memory; Kam for gate songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "raw" and "cooked" are imperial labels; frontier history touches ethnic grievance; menstrual and postpartum taboos are private and gendered.
- **Held for review:** none.
