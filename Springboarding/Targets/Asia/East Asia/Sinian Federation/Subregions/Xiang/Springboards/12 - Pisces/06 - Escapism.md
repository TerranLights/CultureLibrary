# Xiang: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Xiang 湘, Sinian Federation member state (Hunan 湖南), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese; Changsha Xiang (slang of eating, drinking and card play); Southwestern Mandarin of Changde and Zhangjiajie; Mien and the Pingdi Yao varieties for the Qianjiadong lore; Tujia and Miao for drinking customs. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Changsha's young workers: nightlife, nostalgia halls, games and phones as escape from long hours.
- Xiangtan and central Hunan: areca chewing as daily habit, by class and age.
- Rural Shaoyang, Loudi and Yongzhou: card rooms and drinking where many working-age people are away.
- Tujia and Miao villages of Xiangxi: ceremonial drinking set apart from heavy drinking, as communities describe it.
- Yao of Jiangyong and Jianghua: a homeland legend as a different kind of refuge.
- Dongting Lake fishing towns: drink and gambling in seasonal idle time.
- Tourist towns (Fenghuang, Zhangjiajie) and their residents, who host others' escape.
- Hunanese restaurant and nightlife workers on the coast: comparison only, for escapes exported as a trade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tourism and entertainment industries; mixed for drink and tobacco (cohort data exist); thin and politically filtered for drugs and gambling.
- **Languages needed:** Standard Chinese; Changsha Xiang slang; Mien for Qianjiadong; Tujia and Miao for drinking customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling and drug use are illegal and under-reported; areca advertising has been restricted, so industry sources may be guarded; internet-addiction camps elsewhere in China make the topic charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
