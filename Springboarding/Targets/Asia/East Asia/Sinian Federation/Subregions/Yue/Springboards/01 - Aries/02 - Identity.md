## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guangfu Cantonese with Zhuji Lane genealogies:** identity by claimed northern descent.
- **Taishanese and the other Siyi counties:** a home-county identity kept through emigration.
- **Hong Kong people of different arrival waves** (pre-war, 1949, 1970s, post-1997): "local" versus "new".
- **New Territories lineage villagers and the Heung Yee Kuk 鄉議局:** identity as an organized status group (rights questions go to Libra).
- **Tanka and Danjia boat people:** once a caste-like label, now a heritage category.
- **Macanese, Macau Chinese and Macau's Fujianese:** three answers to "who is from Macau".
- **Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** Han but not Cantonese inside Guangdong.
- **Zhuang subgroups (Nong, Dai/Tai, northern Zhuang):** varied self-names under one state category.
- **Yao, Miao, Dong, Mulao, Maonan, Jing and She:** separate charters and origin stories.
- **Hakka (optional):** the Punti-Hakka wars of 1855-1867 in the Siyi counties hardened both labels, so Cantonese identity there was defined in that conflict.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong, Macau and delta lineages; mixed for Guangxi Han; growing but state-framed for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Taishanese, Standard Written Chinese, Teochew, Zhuang, Mien, Portuguese, English; Classical Chinese for genealogies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hong Kong and Macau identity is politically charged; language activism in Guangzhou is sensitive; minority identity is shaped by state classification; some Yao charters are held by ritual specialists.
- **Held for review:** identity pointers concerning South Asian-heritage communities in Hong Kong (category only; set aside under the standing rule).
