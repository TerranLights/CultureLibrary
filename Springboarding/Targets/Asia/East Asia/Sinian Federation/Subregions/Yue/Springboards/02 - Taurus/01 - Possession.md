*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong subdivided-flat and cage-home tenants against owner households:** possessions shaped by floor space.
- **New Territories lineage villagers:** ancestral tablets and hall objects held by the lineage, not one person.
- **Guangzhou and delta Cantonese:** the core case for funerary handling of a dead person's things.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** separate funeral and household-division customs to check, not assume.
- **Danjia boat families:** what fits aboard, and what is kept ashore.
- **Zhuang:** secondary burial and bone jars as their own handling of the dead.
- **Yao (Mien, Pai Yao) and Miao, Dong:** sacred scrolls held by priests; women's silver as personal property.
- **Macanese:** Catholic keepsakes and Portuguese-Chinese family heirlooms.
- **Dormitory migrant workers in Dongguan and Shenzhen:** a life's goods kept in a bag or under a bunk.
- **Hong Kong and Macau elderly living alone:** what old people keep, and who clears it after death.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (press, NGO and clinical studies); mixed for Guangdong; thin and in Chinese for Guangxi's peoples, mostly through folklore and museum studies.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese, Zhuang, Iu Mien, Portuguese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yao ritual scrolls are sacred and restricted to ordained men; their sale is a live grievance. Bone jars and graves concern lineages' own dead. Hoarding cases involve private households.
- **Held for review:** none.
