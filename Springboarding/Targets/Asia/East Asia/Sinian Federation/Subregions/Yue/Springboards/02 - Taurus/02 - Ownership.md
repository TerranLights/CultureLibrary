## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong flat buyers and renters:** all land held on government lease, so "owning" means a timed lease; check how owners feel this.
- **New Territories indigenous villagers (Punti lineages):** a male heir's claim to a house site, inherited by descent.
- **Guangzhou and delta villagers turned shareholders:** collective land converted to dividends.
- **Shenzhen urban-village landlords and their migrant tenants:** owners without full title and renters without a claim.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** lineage and temple property as group holdings; check how shares are held.
- **Zhuang:** chieftaincy tenure in the past, collective forest and paddy now.
- **Yao and Miao hill villages:** shared slopes and forest; who may clear and plant.
- **Danjia and Jing fishing families:** sea and boat rights against land rights.
- **Macanese and Macau's Chinese:** colonial-era land grants and today's housing lotteries.
- **Hakka (optional):** the Hakka-Punti clashes of 1854-1867 in the Siyi counties were fought over who held land, so they show contested first claims.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong land politics; good for Guangdong reform history; mixed for Guangxi tenure, mostly in Chinese.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese (deeds), Zhuang, Teochew, Portuguese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform violence and urban-village demolitions are sensitive in the mainland; New Territories claims are politically charged. Land law, leases and the Small House Policy as written belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
