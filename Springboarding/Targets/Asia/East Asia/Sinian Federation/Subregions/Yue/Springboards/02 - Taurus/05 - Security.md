## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong salaried middle class against grassroots workers:** MPF accounts against having no buffer at all.
- **Hong Kong civil servants:** the "iron rice bowl" as a real class of worker.
- **Guangzhou and delta Cantonese families:** savings, flats and gold as household buffers.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan migrant workers:** insurance they cannot carry between provinces.
- **Macau residents:** security as a share of gaming revenue; compare Chinese and Macanese households.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** kin and lineage as the first safety net; check how far it reaches.
- **Western Guangdong and Leizhou farmers:** typhoon and drought exposure.
- **Guangxi river towns (Wuzhou, Liuzhou):** flood as the defining risk.
- **Zhuang, Yao, Miao and Dong villages:** harvest rites, granaries and hill farming against hunger.
- **Danjia and Jing fishers:** security at sea, by boat and by festival.
- **Hakka (optional):** enclosed and fortified houses (圍龍屋, 四角樓) in northern and eastern Guangdong are a different, much-studied answer to insecurity, useful as a contrast to Cantonese walled villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau (official pension and welfare data, crisis press); mixed for Guangdong; thin for Guangxi villages outside folklore and flood records.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese and Standard Written Chinese for press and policy; Classical Chinese for granary records; Zhuang for harvest rites; Teochew; Portuguese and English for Macau and Hong Kong official data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national security, policing and emigration after 2020 are politically charged; famine years under the 1959-1961 crisis are sensitive in the mainland.
- **Held for review:** none.
