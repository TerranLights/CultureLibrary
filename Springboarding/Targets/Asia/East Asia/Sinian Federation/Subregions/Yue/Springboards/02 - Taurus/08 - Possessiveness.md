## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong couples in small flats:** control over phones, money and friends under crowding.
- **Guangzhou and delta families:** older mui tsai and concubinage history set against present-day couples.
- **Chaoshan Teochew families:** male-line hold on firms, recipes and ancestral property.
- **New Territories lineages:** guarding of village land against outsiders and against daughters' claims.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan migrant couples:** long separations and the jealousy they bring.
- **Zhuang:** song-courtship customs; check how claims on a partner are made and broken.
- **Dong and Miao of Sanjiang and Rongshui:** group courting against private guarding.
- **Macau and Hong Kong domestic workers:** employers' claims on workers' time and phones.
- **Border-town residents (Sheung Shui, Tuen Mun):** turf over local shops.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (press, surveys, social work); good for Guangdong history; thin for Guangxi's minorities outside courtship ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese with slang, Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese, Teochew, Zhuang, Kam, Portuguese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence data need care and privacy; localist protests are politically sensitive; inheritance quarrels involve living families. Inheritance law itself belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
