## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** Cantonese mixed with English words in one sentence; who mixes, who avoids it, and how it is judged.
- **Guangzhou and the delta:** switching between Cantonese and Putonghua by setting (office, home, market).
- **Macau:** Chinese, Portuguese and Macanese speech in the same offices; translation as daily routine.
- **Siyi emigrant villages:** families whose talk ran through letters, then calls, then video, across oceans.
- **Western Guangdong and Leizhou:** Leizhou songs 雷州歌 as sung exchange in a Min-speaking area.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** tea-table talk and song-books read aloud.
- **Zhuang:** answering in song between villages and sexes; Zhuang-language media against Putonghua schooling.
- **Yao, Dong and Miao:** letter-songs, drum-tower talk and sung dialogue, each to be studied separately.
- **Jing of Dongxing:** Vietnamese-related speech kept beside Cantonese and Putonghua.
- **Deaf people across the four lands:** two signing traditions meeting at the boundary.
- **Putonghua-speaking migrants:** factory dormitories where Putonghua is the shared tongue.
- **Older and younger speakers:** voice notes on WeChat and WhatsApp against typed messages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (corpora, pragmatics, media); good for Guangzhou; thin for Guangxi's minority-language talk outside song collections.
- **Languages needed:** spoken and written Cantonese (including Hong Kong code-mixed forms), Standard Written and Classical Chinese, Zhuang, Mien, Teochew, Portuguese, English, Hong Kong Sign Language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public speech in Hong Kong and the mainland is politically constrained; recent media closures limit archives. Speech law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
