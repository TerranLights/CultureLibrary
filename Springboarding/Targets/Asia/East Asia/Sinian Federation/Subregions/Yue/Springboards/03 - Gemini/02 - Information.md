## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** news habits split by age and politics; the shift since 2020 in which outlets exist.
- **Guangzhou:** the Southern Media Group as a hub of reporting, and its limits.
- **Shenzhen:** a city of platform workers whose own firms build the feeds others read.
- **Macau:** a small press in Chinese, Portuguese (*Ponto Final*, *Hoje Macau*) and English; who reads which.
- **Rural Guangdong and Guangxi:** loudspeakers, village groups on WeChat and word of mouth.
- **Zhuang, Yao and Dong villages:** ritual specialists' manuscripts as stores of knowledge with rules on who may read them.
- **Chaoshan:** merchant news networks and lineage halls as places where word is posted.
- **Danjia and other fishing communities:** sea and weather knowledge held by working families.
- **Elders across the four lands:** targets of scams; also the oldest keepers of local knowledge.
- **Hakka (optional):** village notices in New Territories Hakka walled villages; a separate sub-unit, contrast only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (surveys, digitized press); mixed for Guangdong; thin for Guangxi outside official media; Macau's Portuguese press is small but archived.
- **Languages needed:** written Cantonese and Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese for Qing notices, Portuguese, English; Zhuang and Yao for ritual knowledge.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** press freedom and censorship are live and dangerous subjects; some archives have been removed online. Yao and Zhuang ritual manuscripts may be restricted to the initiated; ask before using. Information law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
