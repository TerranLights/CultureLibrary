*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** museum and hobby culture beside a tabloid appetite for scandal; who is praised as curious, who is called 多事.
- **Guangzhou:** the old port's long contact with foreigners and foreign things.
- **Shenzhen:** a young city of engineers and makers; tinkering as a career.
- **Macau:** a Portuguese-Chinese crossroads; what each community wanted to know of the other.
- **Siyi emigrant counties:** returned emigrants and the foreign things they brought home, objects of village curiosity.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** questions asked at the tea table, and what is left unasked in a merchant family.
- **Rural Guangxi Han:** Pinghua and Baihua villages' questions to strangers and visitors.
- **Zhuang:** riddling songs; curiosity voiced in public play.
- **Yao, Miao and Dong:** knowledge closed to outsiders; tourists' curiosity in "ethnic villages".
- **Jing of the Beibu Gulf coast:** fishers' knowledge of the sea and its creatures.
- **Young people across the four lands:** online rabbit holes against elders' face-to-face questions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau museums and hobbies; good for classical Lingnan natural history; thin for curiosity norms in Guangxi villages.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for 異物 and 博物 writing; Cantonese; Standard Written Chinese; Zhuang; Teochew; Portuguese; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some questions (politics, income, family history of emigration or flight) are touchy in all four lands; ethnic tourism raises consent questions; ritual knowledge of minority peoples may not be open to askers.
- **Held for review:** none.
