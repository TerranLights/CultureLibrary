## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong postwar families:** many children, eldest daughters at work, brothers at school; set against today's one- and two-child households.
- **Guangzhou and the delta:** one-child cohorts now raising their own two children.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** family size and rank by birth, to measure rather than assume.
- **Siyi:** brothers sent abroad one after another; village households run by those left behind.
- **Western Guangdong and Leizhou:** rural families with several sons and the division of land among them.
- **Guangxi Han towns and villages:** Baihua and Pinghua families under looser birth limits.
- **Zhuang:** the maternal uncle's role; whether sibling terms mark age more than sex.
- **Yao and Dong:** brothers-in-law and maternal uncles at weddings and feasts.
- **Danjia:** brothers and sisters working the same boat.
- **Macau:** Macanese families with relatives across several countries.
- **Migrant families in Shenzhen and Dongguan:** older siblings left home with grandparents, younger ones in the city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (ethnography, press, family services); good for policy-shaped family size in Guangdong and Guangxi; thin for minority sibling customs beyond kinship studies.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Standard Written and Classical Chinese, Zhuang, Teochew, Portuguese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth-policy history is sensitive; son preference and sisters' sacrifice can be painful in oral history. Inheritance between siblings as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
