## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Single-surname lineage villages (the Tang 鄧, Man, Liu, Hau and Pang of the New Territories; delta villages):** the family that reaches through the hall, against multi-surname villages.
- **Guangzhou and Hong Kong urban households:** the nuclear flat family, cut off from the hall.
- **Macanese families:** Portuguese and Chinese descent mixed, with godparents and church records.
- **Siyi and Zhongshan emigrant families:** kin split over continents for generations (diaspora households as comparison only).
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** large families and strong kin networks worth testing apart from the delta.
- **Zhuang villages:** brides who stay with their own kin for a time after marriage.
- **Yao (Mien, Bunu, Lakkja):** adoption and in-marrying sons-in-law.
- **Dong and Miao 房族 (lineage branches) of Sanjiang and Rongshui:** kin groups organized around village halls.
- **Jing and Danjia:** families bound by the sea and the boat.
- **Hui of Guangzhou and Guangxi:** marriage within the faith; families tied to a mosque.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan migrants:** couples apart from children and parents; family by phone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for New Territories and delta lineage ethnography (in English and Chinese); mixed for mainland urban families; thin for Guangxi minorities outside Chinese-language journals.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Standard Written and Classical Chinese, Zhuang (for marriage-residence customs), Teochew (for Chaoshan kin terms), Portuguese (for Macanese church registers), English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies may be private to the lineage; family planning and inheritance law go to Libra and Capricorn; women's exclusion from lineage estates is a live dispute in the New Territories.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
