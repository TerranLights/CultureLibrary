## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New Territories walled-village lineages:** protection organized by the lineage, with walls, watchmen and guns.
- **Hong Kong and Macau city dwellers:** protection handed to police, charities and the state.
- **Guangzhou and the delta cities:** window grilles, guards and gated estates against older open lanes.
- **Leizhou peninsula (Leizhou Min speakers):** stone guardians and their rites.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** household and lineage gods asked for protection in their own words.
- **Zhuang villages:** ritual specialists who guard households against harm.
- **Dong of Sanjiang:** a grandmother deity as the village's guardian.
- **Yao (Mien, Bunu) and Miao:** protection rites tied to their own ordination and spirit lore.
- **Danjia boat families:** keeping small children safe on the water.
- **Migrant families in Shenzhen and Dongguan:** fear of abduction and the watched child.
- **Southeast Asian domestic workers in Hong Kong:** workers who need protection and also guard the children in their care.
- **Elders living alone:** who watches over them, and who is blamed when no one did.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong charities and child-protection NGOs; mixed for mainland trafficking and village defense; thin for minority protective rites, mostly in Chinese ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese, Standard Written and Classical Chinese, Leizhou Min, Teochew, Dong, Portuguese, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse and trafficking cases involve real children; triad rackets and policing are sensitive; some protective rites are private. Child-protection law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
