## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guangzhou and the delta counties against Hong Kong:** temple-fair leisure against a commercial pop and film industry.
- **Macau's Chinese, Macanese and Portuguese residents:** three festival calendars, and a city whose leisure economy is gaming.
- **Siyi emigrant counties:** celebrations paid for from abroad.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** their own theater, songs and procession seasons, outside Cantonese opera.
- **Western Guangdong and Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** village festival display away from the delta.
- **Zhuang (northern and southern), Yao (Mien, Bunu, Lakkja), Dong, Miao, Mulao, Maonan, Jing:** song, dance and courtship gatherings that differ from Han forms, and from each other.
- **Danjia boat people and the Hui:** water-borne songs; celebrations without drink or betting.
- **Hakka (optional):** where Hakka mountain songs meet Zhuang and Cantonese singing in mixed villages; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau (surveys, industry records, press); mixed for Guangdong's cities; thin and mainly in Chinese for Guangxi's smaller peoples. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled;** the Leo set is written for Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau as a working list, which the author still confirms.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for pop, film and fair sources; Classical Chinese for notebooks; Zhuang and Sawndip for songbooks; Teochew for the 1566 play; Portuguese for Macau listings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Yao and Zhuang ritual songs are for initiates; betting law goes to Libra and Capricorn; protest songs and art since 2019 can be risky to collect.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
