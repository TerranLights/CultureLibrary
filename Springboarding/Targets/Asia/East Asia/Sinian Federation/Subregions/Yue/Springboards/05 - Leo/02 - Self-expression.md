## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong against Guangzhou and Shenzhen:** different public rules on dress, speech and protest.
- **Cantonese against English speakers in Hong Kong:** the language-switch question raised by Chen and Bond.
- **Macau's Macanese and Portuguese residents:** Catholic festival life and a different stage culture.
- **Chaoshan:** Yingge troupes and Teochew opera as men's and women's channels.
- **Siyi counties:** expression shaped by letters and videos sent abroad.
- **Zhuang, Dong, Yao and Miao singers:** song duels, pipa songs and festival dress as a people's way of speaking up.
- **Jing of Dongxing:** Vietnamese-related songs in a Chinese border town.
- **LGBTQ people in Hong Kong and in the delta's cities:** visibility differs sharply.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong pop culture and press; mixed for the mainland, where online expression is moderated; thin for small peoples outside festival coverage.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for lyrics, press and interviews; Classical Chinese for 《粵風》; Kam for pipa-song texts; Teochew for Yingge troupe talk; Portuguese for Macau fringe programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression since 2019 is legally risky to collect in Hong Kong (law to Libra and Capricorn); 《粵風》 uses old slurs for the Zhuang and Yao; LGBTQ informants need privacy.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
