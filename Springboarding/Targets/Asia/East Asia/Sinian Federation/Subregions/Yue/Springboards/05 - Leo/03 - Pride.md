*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guangzhou against Hong Kong:** an old provincial capital and a city that grew rich apart from it; rival claims to be the center of Cantonese culture.
- **Shunde and Chaozhou cooks:** food prides that rival Guangzhou's.
- **Shenzhen migrants:** pride in a city most were not born in.
- **Chaoshan:** a regional pride that sets itself apart from the Cantonese majority.
- **Siyi:** pride in emigrant success, and in villages built with money from abroad.
- **Macau's Macanese:** pride in a creole people and their cooking.
- **Zhuang of Donglan and the Youjiang valley:** revolutionary memory as a source of standing.
- **Yao of Jinxiu and Gongcheng, Jing of Dongxing:** peoples whose festival performances are their public face.
- **Guilin against Nanning:** a scenic boast against a capital's administrative standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Chinese for regional and food pride; mixed for psychological research outside Hong Kong; thin for how small peoples speak of their own pride outside official settings.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for sayings and press; Classical Chinese for the Tang and Song sources; Teochew for Chaoshan identity talk; Portuguese for Macanese societies; Zhuang for hero stories told at home.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional and Hong Kong pride can read as political since 2019; anthem conduct is regulated by law (Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
