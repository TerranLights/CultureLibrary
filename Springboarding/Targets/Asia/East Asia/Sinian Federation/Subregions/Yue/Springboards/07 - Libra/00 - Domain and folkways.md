## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta Cantonese (Guangzhou, Foshan, Shunde):** lineage-based marriage and business bonds in the old core.
- **Hong Kong's city against its New Territories indigenous villagers (原居民):** common law, with a customary regime kept for the villages.
- **Macau's Chinese and Macanese:** Portuguese civil law; Catholic and Chinese weddings side by side.
- **Siyi (Taishan, Kaiping):** marriages arranged across oceans.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** their own feud histories and wedding forms.
- **Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua speakers:** older Han settlers in a mostly non-Han province.
- **Zhuang, Yao (Mien, Bunu), Dong, Miao, Mulao, Maonan, Jing:** their own courting, marriage and quarrel-settling customs, and how far state law bends to them.
- **Danjia boat people and the Hui:** water people once kept from marrying on land; Muslim marriage beside civil registration.
- **Hakka (optional):** the 1855-1867 feud is one story with two sides, so the Hakka telling checks the Punti one; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the written law (three legal systems, all published) and Hong Kong case law; mixed for mainland judgments (the database has been thinned); thin, mostly in Chinese, for Guangxi minorities' own customs. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled;** the Libra set is written for Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau as a working list, which the author still confirms.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for courtship and settlement words; Classical Chinese for the Qing code; English for Hong Kong statutes; Portuguese for Macau's codes; Danjia speech for the laments; Miao, Yao and Dong for local marriage rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hong Kong courts and politics since 2020; mainland judgment access is restricted; some minority marriage rites are private to the families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
