## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong, Macau and the mainland provinces:** three criminal codes, three ideas of trial (jury, judge-led civil-law, people's court) and of the death penalty, within one member state.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** the land of Lan Dingyuan's cases, with its own record of lineage self-help.
- **Guangxi's counties hit in 1967-1968 (Wuxuan, Binyang and others) *check*:** where historical justice is still an open account.
- **Zhuang, Jing, Yao, Dong, Maonan, Mulao:** village elders and customary penalties against the state's courts.
- **Hong Kong's young protesters and their families since 2019:** a generation's first meeting with criminal courts.
- **Danjia and other water people:** once under separate rules of status, now under ordinary law.
- **Macau's Macanese:** lawyers and judges carrying Portuguese legal training into the Chinese-language era.
- **Hakka (optional):** where Hakka and Punti village leaders in the New Territories took disputes to the colonial magistrate rather than to elders, a direct test of who was trusted to judge; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau law and judgments; mixed for the mainland (codes open, case counts official, independent data thin); thin and sensitive for the Guangxi Cultural Revolution record.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Lan's casebook; English and Chinese for Hong Kong statutes and judgments; Portuguese for Macau's codes; Teochew and Jing for village accounts of wrongs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national-security trials; the 1967-1968 killings; death-penalty data on the mainland is not public.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
