# Yue (粤): Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), a member state of the Sinian Federation (its own lands and peoples), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Taishanese, Standard Written Chinese (simplified and traditional), Classical Chinese, Standard Zhuang, Mulao and Iu Mien (through Chinese-language studies), Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong, Macau and the mainland as legal partners:** arrangements on judgments and trade that may or may not run both ways.
- **Siyi (Taishan, Kaiping, Enping, Xinhui):** families held together by remittance and reply.
- **Delta Cantonese households against Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** wedding and funeral gift customs and amounts.
- **Zhuang, Mulao, Yao, Maonan, Dong:** village labor exchange and its repayment.
- **Temple-goers in Hong Kong and Guangzhou:** vows to gods, and what counts as repaying them.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** a separate tradition of remittance letters and of gifts at festivals.
- **Macau's Chinese and Macanese:** gift customs at Catholic and Chinese feasts side by side.
- **Hakka (optional):** where Hakka emigrant villages near the Siyi counties kept their own remittance channels, a check on whether reply customs were shared or separate; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the cross-boundary legal texts and remittance archives; mixed for gift customs (press and folklore, few careful studies); thin for minority labor exchange outside Chinese-language ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Qu Dajun; English and Chinese for the Hong Kong ordinances; Portuguese for Macau's maintenance duty; Taishanese for remittance-family memories; Zhuang and Mulao for village help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gift money shades into bribery (Libra's impartiality sheet); the legal side of family duty is written, but how families keep it is a question to research.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
