## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong and Macau in 1966-1967:** two colonial governments facing the same pressure, one yielding in writing and one not.
- **Mainland courts and villages against Hong Kong's courts:** settlement steered by the Party against settlement steered by court rules.
- **Wives and daughters-in-law in Cantonese, Teochew and Guangxi Han households:** the expected cost of family harmony, as women describe it.
- **Zhuang, Yao, Dong, Miao, Maonan:** customary payment-and-feast settlements beside state mediation.
- **Delta merchants and shopkeepers:** harmony as a business rule.
- **Danjia and other coastal communities:** the people who lived with the pirates and with the amnesty that followed.
- **Villagers facing land takings across Guangdong:** yielding to end a dispute with the state.
- **Hakka (optional):** where Hakka villages in Sai Kung were moved for the Plover Cove and High Island reservoirs (1960s-1970s) *check*, a case of accepting the state's terms to end a dispute over land; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong statutes and court rules; mixed for mainland mediation (official numbers, few independent studies); thin for customary settlements and for women's own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for 啞忍 and family talk; Classical Chinese for the 1810 amnesty documents; Portuguese for Macau's 1967 records; Zhuang, Kam and Mien for customary settlements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence; land disputes; the 1966-1967 events and "silent majority" politics; Fengqiao-style mediation touches state control.
