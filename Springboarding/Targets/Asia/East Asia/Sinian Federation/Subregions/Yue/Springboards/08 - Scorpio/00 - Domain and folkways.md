## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta lineage villages and Hong Kong's walled villages:** trusts and halls tie the dead to the estate.
- **Hong Kong and Guangzhou high-rise households:** niches, sea scattering and small flats change where the dead are kept.
- **Siyi (Taishan, Kaiping) emigrant villages:** bones returned from abroad and estates run from overseas.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** their own merit rites, ghost-feeding and lineage property.
- **Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** older Han settler customs among non-Han neighbors.
- **Zhuang, Yao (Iu Mien, Bunu), Dong, Miao, Mulao, Maonan, Jing:** their own funerals, soul ideas and inheritance rules, heard from their ritual keepers.
- **Macau's Macanese and Chinese Catholics:** church burial beside Chinese rites.
- **Hakka (optional):** in Hakka-Cantonese mixed counties, the shared hillsides hold graves of both, so grave-site and land disputes between them are a local test of the domain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau; mixed for Guangdong (CNKI, gazetteers, press); thinner for Guangxi minorities, mostly in Chinese-language state surveys. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled;** this sign is written for Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau as a working list the author still confirms.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for the softening words and screen dialogue; Classical Chinese for ritual manuals and genealogies; Zhuang and Iu Mien for funeral and soul terms; Teochew for merit rites; Portuguese for Macau's church and cemetery registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Yao and Zhuang ritual texts are kept by their masters and shared only by consent; sex work involves living people; power ways belong mainly to Capricorn, law to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
