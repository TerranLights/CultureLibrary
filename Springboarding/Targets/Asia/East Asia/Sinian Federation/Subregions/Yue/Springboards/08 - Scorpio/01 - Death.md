# Yue (粤): Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), a member state of the Sinian Federation (its own lands and peoples), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese, Standard Zhuang, Iu Mien, Teochew, Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong and Macau:** hospital deaths, scarce burial space, ghost lore around flats.
- **New Territories walled villages and Weitou speakers:** lament songs and hillside graves of an older village order.
- **Delta and western Guangdong Cantonese (Zhaoqing, Yangjiang):** Taoist and Buddhist funerals, and secondary burial where it survives.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** their own funeral rites, hospice uptake and ideas of an untimely death.
- **Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** Han funeral ways shaped by long life among non-Han neighbors.
- **Zhuang, Yao (Iu Mien, Bunu), Mulao, Maonan, Dong, Miao, Jing:** death songs, soul guidance and mourning periods in their own words.
- **Macanese Catholics:** All Souls' Day, requiem masses and Portuguese mourning dress.
- **Hakka (optional):** the women's funeral laments recorded in New Territories Hakka villages sit beside Weitou ones, a close comparison for the lament pointer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (suicide research, statistics, press, film); mixed for Guangdong; thin and Chinese-mediated for Guangxi minorities' own ideas of the dead.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for the softened and joking death words; Classical Chinese for epitaphs and the Han report; Iu Mien and Classical Chinese together for Yao manuscripts; Zhuang and Mulao for laments; Portuguese for Macanese mourning.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data must be handled with the safe-reporting guidance the research center gives; some Yao funeral texts are for initiated masters only; leprosy survivors are living people. Medical aid in dying and wills as law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
