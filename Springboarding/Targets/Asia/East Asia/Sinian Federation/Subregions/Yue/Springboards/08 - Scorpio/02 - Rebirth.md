# Yue (粤): Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), a member state of the Sinian Federation (its own lands and peoples), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese, Classical Chinese, Iu Mien and other Yao languages, Standard Zhuang, Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong evangelical and Pentecostal networks:** sudden conversion and testimony.
- **Macau's Catholic parishes:** slow initiation through the sacraments.
- **Delta lineage villages and Guangzhou's urban villages:** rebirth as rebuilding halls, genealogies and whole villages.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** Buddhist merit, rebirth ideas and charitable halls to check separately.
- **Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** drug routes from the border and the recovery work around them.
- **Yao (Iu Mien, Bunu, Lajia), Zhuang, Dong, Miao, Jing:** initiation as a ritual death; soul and rebirth teachings as their ritual keepers state them.
- **Former Danjia boat families:** a whole way of life left behind on settling ashore.
- **Hakka (optional):** the Taiping movement that drew on Liang Fa's tracts was led by Hakka in Guangxi, so the Hakka reading of that conversion story is needed beside the Cantonese.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong recovery, church and film sources; mixed for Guangdong; thin and Chinese-mediated for Yao and Zhuang teachings on the soul's return.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Ge Hong and Liang Fa; Cantonese for testimony and recovery-house talk; Iu Mien for ordination terms; English for Pullinger and the HKU research; Portuguese for Macau's parish practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parts of Yao ordination are for initiates only; drug-rehab residents and ex-prisoners are vulnerable people; unregistered churches on the mainland are a restricted subject.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
