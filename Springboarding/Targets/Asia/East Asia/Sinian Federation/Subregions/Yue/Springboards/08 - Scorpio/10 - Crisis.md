# Yue (粤): Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong's market city:** crises of money (1998, 2008) and of panic (2020).
- **Macau's gaming workforce:** one-industry exposure.
- **Delta factory towns (Dongguan, Shenzhen, Foshan):** export shocks felt by migrants.
- **Siyi emigrant villages:** crises that come when money from abroad stops.
- **Chaoshan Teochew and Leizhou Min speakers:** typhoon coasts, with relief halls and overseas kin.
- **Guangxi Baihua and Pinghua towns:** river floods on the Xi.
- **Zhuang, Yao, Miao, Dong, Jing:** drought in the karst, flood myths, and coastal storms for the Jing.
- **Danjia fishing families:** typhoons met on the water.
- **Hakka (optional):** the 1943 famine also struck Hakka villages inland of Siyi *check*, so their records show whether the remittance cut-off was the cause there too.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau (press, hotlines, research centers); mixed for Guangdong (gazetteers, CNKI); thin for Guangxi minority accounts beyond official relief reports.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for gazetteers; Taishanese for the 1943 famine; Teochew for 1922; Zhuang and Yao for myths and drought memory; Portuguese for Macau's 2020 press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine deaths and child-selling in 1943 are painful family histories; crisis-line data must be handled with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
