## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** temples, churches and faith-run schools beside many who call themselves non-religious.
- **Macau:** Catholic institutions of four centuries beside Chinese temples; Macanese and Chinese Catholics may differ.
- **Guangzhou and the delta:** popular religion rebuilt after the 1980s next to registered Buddhism, Taoism, Islam and Christianity.
- **Siyi emigrant counties:** temples, churches and schools funded from abroad.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** dense village-deity cults and charitable halls (善堂).
- **Leizhou Min speakers:** their own deities and festival styles on the peninsula.
- **Guangxi Han (Baihua, Pinghua, Guiliu speakers):** settler temples among non-Han neighbors.
- **Zhuang:** Mo chanting and Buluotuo; how much is lived and how much revived.
- **Yao (Iu Mien, Bunu), Dong, Miao, Mulao, Maonan, Jing:** each with its own specialists and origin stories, to be heard from their own keepers.
- **Hakka (optional):** the Basel Mission worked mainly in Hakka counties, so Protestant congregations there grew from a different mission than the delta's; their church history is a separate strand.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau; mixed for Guangdong (registered faiths are counted, popular religion less so); thin for Guangxi minorities outside state surveys and heritage files. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled;** this sign is written for Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau as a working list the author still confirms.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for temple speech and radio; Classical Chinese for Ming philosophy; Zhuang and Iu Mien for chant texts; Teochew for village cults; Portuguese and Latin for Macau's church archive.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered churches, Falun Gong and groups labeled 邪教 are politically charged on the mainland; some Yao and Zhuang ritual books are held by priests and shown only with consent. Law goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
