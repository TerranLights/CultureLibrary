# Yue (粤): Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), Sinian Federation member state; its own lands (Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong, Macau) and peoples; region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese (simplified, traditional), Classical Chinese, Standard Zhuang, Iu Mien, Teochew, Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong temple-goers and Christians:** temple vows against conversion and "going cold".
- **Guangzhou and Foshan neighborhoods:** popular faith rebuilt after 1978, with temples reopened by residents.
- **Siyi villages:** gods and churches kept up by remittances; faith at a distance.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** halls where worship and relief work are one practice.
- **Leizhou Min speakers:** stone-dog guardians and their own village cults, as a separate set of faiths.
- **Danjia boat families:** sea gods and boat shrines, before and after moving ashore.
- **Zhuang villages of western Guangxi:** Mo priests and the revived Buluotuo cult; generational gaps to check.
- **Yao, Miao and Dong:** vow rites and ritual masters, each on its own terms.
- **Jing of Dongxing:** the Ha festival 哈節 and its sea deities, near the border.
- **Macau Macanese:** Catholic devotion at home and in the streets.
- **Guangzhou's Hui:** a minority faith kept inside a Han city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau congregations and temples; mixed for Guangdong popular faith; thin and mostly in Chinese-language heritage files for Guangxi minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese to hear vows and testimonies; Classical Chinese for temple steles; Zhuang and Iu Mien for chant and vow texts; Portuguese for Macau's devotions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered churches and banned groups on the mainland; Yao and Zhuang ritual books shown only with the keeper's consent; testimony videos name living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
