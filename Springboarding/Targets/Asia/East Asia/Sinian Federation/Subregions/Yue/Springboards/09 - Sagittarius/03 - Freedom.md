*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong residents across generations:** escapees who came for freedom from want, and grandchildren who spoke of political freedom.
- **Macau:** a quieter path to 1999; what Macau residents mean by freedom may differ.
- **Guangzhou and Shenzhen:** a press that pushed limits; migrants without local residence.
- **Danjia boat families:** freedom from debased status, and later from life on the water.
- **Descendants of mui tsai and domestic servants:** servitude remembered in families, or silenced.
- **Yao (Iu Mien, Bunu):** the charter as their own idea of free movement through the hills.
- **Zhuang of Donglan and Baise:** liberation as their revolutionary memory.
- **Jing of the Beibu Gulf coast:** a border people's freedom to fish and to cross.
- **Hakka (optional):** the Taiping promise of release from the old order was a Hakka-led movement in Guangxi, so its legacy is told differently there than in Cantonese counties.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (press, polls, memoirs) and the reform-era press of Guangzhou; mixed for Macau; thin for Yao and Jing ideas of freedom outside heritage files.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for slang of escape and release; Classical Chinese for Qing edicts and Yao charters; Iu Mien for the charter's oral telling; Portuguese for Macau's colonial files.
