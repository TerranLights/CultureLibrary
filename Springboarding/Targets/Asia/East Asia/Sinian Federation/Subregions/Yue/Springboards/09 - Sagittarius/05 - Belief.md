## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** many who claim no religion yet keep ghost, number and word beliefs; how the two fit.
- **Guangzhou:** a city campaigned against "superstition" for a century; what survived and what returned.
- **Shenzhen newcomers:** beliefs brought from every province into a new city.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** village gods held to guard the whole settlement, with lineage ranking in the rites.
- **Leizhou Min speakers:** a thunder deity of their own, unlike the delta's gods.
- **Western Guangdong Cantonese (Maoming, Zhanjiang):** belief in the deified Lady Xian 冼夫人.
- **Zhuang:** ghost and spirit beliefs voiced in Zhuang, beside the school's materialism.
- **Dong:** Sama as a people's own deity.
- **Mulao, Maonan and Jing:** small peoples' own festivals and spirits, heard from their keepers.
- **Macau Catholics and Macanese families:** church doctrine beside Chinese household belief.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (surveys, films, radio); mixed for Guangdong (campaign history, press); thin for Dong, Mulao and Maonan beyond heritage files.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for omen and ghost vocabulary; Classical Chinese for Song and Qing accounts; Kam and Zhuang for deity names; Leizhou Min for the thunder cult; Portuguese for Macau's church archive.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "superstition" and "cult" labels carry state force on the mainland; Dong and Zhuang altar rites may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
