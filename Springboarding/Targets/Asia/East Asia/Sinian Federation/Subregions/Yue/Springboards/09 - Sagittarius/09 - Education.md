## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong elite-school and grassroots families:** tutoring, school places and the DSE as two experiences.
- **Macau:** a mostly private and church-run school system, with Portuguese, Chinese and English streams.
- **Guangzhou and Shenzhen:** strong universities and migrant children shut out of local exams.
- **Northern and western Guangdong (Shaoguan, Zhanjiang, Maoming):** first-generation students and long distances to good schools.
- **Siyi emigrant villages:** schools founded with overseas money, and study abroad as a family plan.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** merchant families' choices between business and higher study.
- **Zhuang:** schooling in Chinese, Zhuang classes, and what the university route asks of them.
- **Yao, Miao and Dong of the Guangxi hills:** boarding schools and the cost of leaving the village to study.
- **Danjia families:** late entry to schooling after moving ashore.
- **Hakka (optional):** Meizhou's schooling history belongs to the Hakka sub-unit; mixed Hakka-Cantonese counties, where both send children to the same schools, are the place to compare the two.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong and Macau (exam bodies, histories, press); good for Guangdong (exam data, CNKI); thin for Guangxi minorities beyond state reports.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for school-race slang; Classical Chinese for examination records and academy rules; Zhuang for minority schooling; Teochew for merchant memoirs; Portuguese for Macau's university and school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** curriculum change and the teachers' union's end are political in Hong Kong; minority boarding schools are a delicate topic. Early learning goes to Gemini; academic freedom is in Freedom.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
