## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong and Macau:** colonial-era civil services kept in place, against the mainland's party-led ranks.
- **Guangzhou and the delta:** old merchant status against the reform-era rich.
- **Shenzhen and Dongguan migrants:** status set by household registration as much as by job.
- **Siyi emigrant counties:** standing bought with money sent home from abroad.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** lineage seniority and merchant reputation in their own dialect area.
- **Leizhou and western Guangdong:** Min and Cantonese towns where Lady Xian's cult marks local authority *check*.
- **Guangxi Han (Baihua, Pinghua, Guiliu speakers):** settler towns ranked among non-Han neighbors.
- **Zhuang:** chieftain families and village elders before 1950, and Party and state posts since.
- **Yao, Dong, Miao, Maonan, Mulao, Jing:** elders' councils, kin heads and alliances, each in its own form.
- **Danjia boat people:** a once low-ranked category.
- **Macanese:** a small community that long staffed Macau's administration.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong; mixed for Guangdong (national panels, official reports); thin for Guangxi minorities. **The nation's exact lands are still to be settled;** this sign is written for Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau as a working list the author still confirms.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for workplace and family speech; Classical Chinese for Ming-Qing rank terms; Teochew for merchant circles; Zhuang and other minority languages for elders' titles; Portuguese for Macau's administration.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hong Kong's 2021 electoral changes and mainland political data; 1950s class labels shape the minority surveys, to be read as of their time.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
