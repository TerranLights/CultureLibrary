## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** police authority and its public before and after 2019; a civil service trained to obey rules over persons.
- **Macau:** Portuguese officials, Chinese guild and neighborhood leaders, and the 1966 turning point in who held real say.
- **Delta villages and towns:** cadres who are also lineage members, and the line between those two claims.
- **Lufeng and the Hailufeng Min area:** a case where village consent was withdrawn openly.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** lineage elders' voice beside party and township officials.
- **Western Guangdong:** Lady Xian's cult as authority in local memory.
- **Guangxi Han towns:** county magistrates' seats ringed by chieftain lands until the Qing.
- **Zhuang:** hereditary chieftains until their abolition, then appointed officials.
- **Yao, Dong, Miao, Jing:** elders, headmen and drum-tower meetings, each with its own route to standing.
- **Catholics and Protestants in Hong Kong and Macau:** church authority set against the state's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong polling and oversight; mixed for Guangdong (case studies, official sources); thin and filtered for Guangxi chieftain history outside Chinese-language field reports.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for the Lady Xian biography; Cantonese for police and household address; Hailufeng Min for Wukan villagers' own words; Portuguese for the Leal Senado records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2019 protests, Wukan, and church-state questions are politically charged; the permission and correctional-control shares are scored later from the law in force (Libra).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
