## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** rank by residency, occupation and housing; colonial racial ranks remembered in places.
- **Macau:** Portuguese-born, Macanese and Chinese ranks before 1999, and non-resident workers since.
- **Guangzhou and Shenzhen:** registered residents against newcomers.
- **Delta lineages:** senior and junior branches; heirs of main wives and concubines.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** senior-branch precedence in rites and property.
- **Danjia boat people:** a former "mean" status and its after-effects.
- **Leizhou and western Guangdong:** rural counties set below the delta in the provincial order.
- **Guangxi Han towns:** settlers ranked above or beside non-Han hill peoples.
- **Zhuang:** chieftains, headmen, soldiers and tenants under the old domains.
- **Yao:** landholding and landless groups in Dayaoshan; **Dong, Miao, Maonan, Jing:** each with its own elders and ranks.
- **Hakka (optional):** where New Territories Hakka and Punti villages shared the 1972 Small House rights of "indigenous villagers", a check on how a colonial rank cut across both; otherwise its own sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong law, surveys and colonial records; mixed for mainland registration studies; thin and filtered for Yao and Zhuang ranks outside 1950s fieldwork and later studies.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for genealogies and status law; Cantonese for kitchen and opera trade ranks; Teochew for branch precedence; Yao and Zhuang for hill and domain ranks; Portuguese for Macau status terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** triad material; bonded-servant histories; residency rules for migrant workers; class labels from the 1950s shape the minority sources.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
