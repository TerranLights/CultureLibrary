## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong:** colonial and post-colonial public order; village rites of peace in the New Territories.
- **Macau:** order in a dense gaming city watched by cameras.
- **Guangzhou and Shenzhen:** civility campaigns and street enforcement against hawkers and migrants.
- **Siyi emigrant counties:** watchtower villages built for order in bandit years.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** temple-led processions that order the year *check*.
- **Leizhou Min speakers:** rites to drive out harm and restore order.
- **Guangxi Han cities:** civility drives in Nanning and Liuzhou.
- **Zhuang and Jing border villages:** frontier patrols and border markets.
- **Dong:** nightly fire watch in wooden villages; **Yao, Miao, Maonan, Mulao:** village rules that keep the peace, each in its own form.
- **Danjia fishers:** harbor order among boats.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong records and surveys; mixed for mainland campaigns (official scores open, residents' views thin); thin for Dong and border-village practice outside field reports.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Qing registers; Cantonese for watchmen's calls and street speech; Leizhou Min for exorcistic rites; Zhuang and Jing for border patrols; Dong for the fire watch; Portuguese for Macau policing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2016 Mong Kok unrest and surveillance are politically charged; some village rites limit who may take part or watch.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
