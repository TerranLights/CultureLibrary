# Yue (粤): Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), Sinian Federation member state (Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong, Macau), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese (simplified and traditional), Classical Chinese, Teochew, Standard Zhuang, Dong and Miao (Chinese renderings first), Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), who recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New Territories lineage villages and Hong Kong public-estate tenants:** a community by descent against one assigned by housing.
- **Old Guangzhou and Foshan streets against Shenzhen compounds:** neighbors of a lifetime against neighbors of a group chat.
- **Macau's Catholic parishes and its Chinese residents' associations:** two parallel community structures in one small city.
- **Western Guangdong (Maoming, Zhanjiang, Leizhou):** Cantonese and Leizhou Min villages with their own feast calendars.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** dense lineage villages with members abroad.
- **Siyi emigrant villages:** communities partly emptied by emigration.
- **Zhuang villages:** bronze drums, song and shared field work to check as village ties, district by district.
- **Dong, Miao, Yao, Mulao and Maonan:** each with its own feast, gathering and alliance forms.
- **Jing fishing villages of Dongxing:** a sea community at the border.
- **Danjia fishing families (Aberdeen, Yangjiang):** a work community long kept apart on the water.
- **Factory-dormitory workers in Dongguan and Foshan:** communities formed by shift and province of origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong (NGO, government and press); mixed for Guangdong cities; thin and mostly Chinese-language for Guangxi minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for estate and street talk; Classical Chinese for league and gentry records; Teochew for village boards; Zhuang, Dong and Miao for festival names and songs; Portuguese for Macau's parishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** quarantine records and compound chats touch privacy; mainland residents' committees are party-linked, so official accounts are one side only.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
