## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong Cantonese:** large NGO and telethon sector; arguments about who "deserves" welfare.
- **Guangzhou and the delta:** old merchant charity halls, now beside state and Party-run charity.
- **Macau's Chinese residents and Macanese:** Catholic charity and Chinese benevolent associations side by side.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** charity halls with their own saints, and gifts sent home by emigrants.
- **Northern and western Guangdong (Shaoguan, Zhanjiang):** poorer areas on the receiving side of help.
- **Guangxi Han in Yulin and Wuzhou:** where animal-welfare outsiders meet local food customs.
- **Zhuang, Yao, Dong, Miao, Mulao, Maonan:** villages named in poverty programs; their own customs of pity and help to check.
- **Hong Kong's newcomers from the mainland:** often the subject of pity or blame in welfare debates.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong charity, press and psychology; mixed for Guangdong (official charity reports lead); thin for Guangxi minorities' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Su Shi and the Qing halls; Cantonese for sayings and telethon talk; Zhuang for folktales; Portuguese for Macau's wartime relief.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** street sleepers and refugees are living people; the Yulin dispute is heated on both sides; poverty-program publicity is official and one-sided. Welfare law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
