## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong Cantonese:** temple, spirit-writing hall, church and mindfulness class side by side.
- **Macau's Chinese residents and Macanese Catholics:** shared streets, separate devotions.
- **Guangzhou and the delta villages:** temple revival since the 1980s beside state limits.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** dense village deity cults and processions.
- **Leizhou Min speakers:** their own thunder and stone-dog lore.
- **She of Chaozhou, Huizhou and Shanwei:** a small people's origin story and scrolls.
- **Zhuang of Hechi and Baise:** frog and ancestor rites.
- **Yao (Iu Mien of Jinxiu and Hezhou; Pai Yao of Liannan):** Taoist-derived ordination and painted pantheons.
- **Yi, Gelao and Shui of western and northern Guangxi:** small rites, seen through Chinese reports *check*.
- **Hui of Guangzhou:** Islamic devotion in the old city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong practice and Daoist halls; mixed for Guangdong popular religion; thin for She, Yi and other small peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Chan sayings and spirit-writing texts; Cantonese for hall and hiking talk; Teochew for village cults; Zhuang, Iu Mien and Yi for village rites; Japanese for Shiga's work; Portuguese for Macau.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yao paintings and ordination rites are not for outsiders to handle; sacred objects in museums raise return questions; mainland limits on religious activity affect fieldwork.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
