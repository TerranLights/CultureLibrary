*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong Cantonese:** large crime-entertainment market; second chances argued in public.
- **Macau:** a casino economy with its own picture of the gangster.
- **Delta factory cities (Dongguan, Shenzhen):** migrant workers as both suspects and victims.
- **Leizhou Min and western Guangdong (Zhanjiang, Maoming):** smuggling and fraud as local trades.
- **Hailufeng and Chaoshan:** strong lineages that can shield or shame wrongdoers.
- **Guangxi Han and Zhuang of the Shiwandashan and the Vietnam border:** banditry memory and smuggling routes.
- **Yao and Miao villages:** their own ideas of wrong and repair, kept apart from state crime (customary rules go to Capricorn).
- **Hakka (optional):** some Red Turban and bandit bands of the 1850s recruited across Hakka and Cantonese lines, so the label "bandit" fell on both.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong press and crime culture; mixed for Guangdong (official reporting); thin and politically shaped for Guangxi's banditry memory.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Qing bandit records; Cantonese for street words and true-crime retellings; Leizhou Min for Zhanjiang; Zhuang for border villages; Portuguese for Macau.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims and families of named cases are living people; the 1850s and 1950s killings are touchy; executions are a political subject. All law questions go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
