# Yue (粤): Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Yue (粤), Sinian Federation member state (Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong, Macau), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Cantonese, Standard Written Chinese (both scripts), Classical Chinese, Teochew, Leizhou Min, Standard Zhuang, Jing (a Vietnamese variety), Portuguese, English. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hong Kong Cantonese:** crime-news fears, social anxiety, fear sold as entertainment.
- **Leizhou Min speakers of the peninsula:** the koro outbreak and its aftermath.
- **Chaoshan Teochew:** river and sea fears in a coastal plain.
- **Delta villages and Guangzhou:** drowning lore, epidemics, rumor in dense cities.
- **Northeastern Guangdong (Heyuan):** a reservoir quake and the fear of dams.
- **Zhuang, Jing and Han border villages (Napo, Pingxiang, Dongxing):** landmine fields and war memory.
- **Mainland newcomers in Hong Kong:** feared as outsiders and fearing labels.
- **Macau residents:** typhoons and a small city's sense of exposure.
- **Hakka (optional):** much of the land around the Xinfengjiang reservoir is Hakka country, so the quake's memory there sits partly in another sub-unit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hong Kong press and psychology; mixed for Guangdong epidemics and disasters; thin for border villages and small peoples' own fear words.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese for Han Yu; Cantonese for bogeymen and social fear; Leizhou Min for the koro villages; Zhuang and Jing for border fields; Portuguese for Macau.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** koro studies carry an outsider's label, to be read critically; landmine victims are living people; the "Big Circle" label is a stereotype to record, not adopt.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only, under the standing rule.
