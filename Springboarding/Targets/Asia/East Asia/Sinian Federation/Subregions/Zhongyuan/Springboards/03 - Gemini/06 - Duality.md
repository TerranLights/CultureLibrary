# Zhongyuan: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), Sinian Federation member state (Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan), region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese (modern and classical); Beijing colloquial; Manchu (bilingual Qing documents); Hui usage; Korean; French and English for Tianjin's concession history. **Concept in one line:** how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manchu descendants** in Beijing and the Chengde counties: one identity for forms, another at home, or neither.
- **Hui communities:** Muslim and Chinese, and how the pairing is phrased in Kaifeng, Cangzhou and Niujie.
- **Migrants with rural hukou** in Beijing and Tianjin: compare the city self against the village self on visits home.
- **Tianjin's former concession districts** against the old walled city.
- **Mixed-ethnicity families** choosing a child's 民族.
- **Bidialectal speakers** in Jiaodong, rural Henan and Jin-speaking Hebei: dialect at home, Putonghua at work.
- **Korean residents and Korean Chinese** (朝鲜族) in Beijing.
- **Party members with religious families,** handled with care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical thought and Manchu-Han history; mixed for present-day dual identities; thin for twins and double lives.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese; Manchu for Qing documents; French for concession records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification, hukou and religious identity are political subjects; "two-faced" (两面人) is also an official term for disloyal cadres, so search results mix folk and political senses.
- **Held for review:** none.
