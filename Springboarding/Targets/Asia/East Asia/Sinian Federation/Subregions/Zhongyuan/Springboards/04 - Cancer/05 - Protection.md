## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mount Tai and the Shandong interior** (stone tablets, goddess pilgrimages) against **Beijing and Tianjin temple associations**: one goddess, different ways of asking for protection.
- **Cangzhou and Mengcun:** Han and Hui martial villages where guarding is a learned skill.
- **Henan and Shandong villages in old Red Spear areas** (*check*), for memory of armed village self-defense.
- **Yellow River and Huai river districts** (Henan floodplain; Zhengzhou; Zhumadian, site of the 1975 dam failure): protection from water.
- **Tangshan and other quake-memory towns in Hebei.**
- **Manchu households** in Chengde and Fengning (child-guarding rites); **Hui families** (prayer and mosque as protection).
- **Women in rural Hebei and Henan** against **urban Beijing women:** whom each can turn to for protection at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk religion and late-imperial self-defense; mixed for felt safety (official surveys only); thin and guarded for violence at home and for neighborhood surveillance.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical and opera Chinese; Manchu for ritual texts; Japanese wartime material for Red Spear reports (*check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child abduction, violence at home, disaster death tolls and neighborhood informant networks are sensitive; some reporting is censored. Policing and criminal law go to Libra; obedience to authority to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
