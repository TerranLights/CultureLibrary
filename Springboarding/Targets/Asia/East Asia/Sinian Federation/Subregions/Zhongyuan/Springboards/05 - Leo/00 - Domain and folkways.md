## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Beijing, including banner (Manchu) descendants, against new migrants:** different leisure worlds in one city.
- **Tianjin:** a port city with its own comic and craft traditions and treaty-port past.
- **Hebei plains (Yan-Zhao) against the Bashang plateau** (Zhangjiakou, Chengde; Jin-speaking areas): farming against grassland and Manchu and Mongol autonomous counties.
- **Shandong: Jiaodong peninsula** (Jiaoliao Mandarin, sea and fishing) against the **west and southwest** (Heze, Jining) and the **Qufu-Zou** heartland.
- **Henan by part:** the Jin-speaking north, Luoyang in the west, Kaifeng-Shangqiu in the east, Nanyang-Xinyang in the south.
- **Hui Muslims** (Cangzhou, Mengcun and Dachang counties, Beijing's Niujie, Kaifeng, Zhengzhou, Jining): distinct festivals, food and martial-arts lines.
- **Christians:** Hebei Catholic villages and Henan Protestants, whose festive calendars differ.
- **Korean communities** in Beijing and the Shandong coast, whose leisure ties to Korea; **diasporas** (Shandong migrants to the northeast and Korea; Henan migrant workers in the cities), who carry home pastimes elsewhere.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Beijing and for sport, opera and heritage; mixed for rural Henan and Hebei counties; thin for minority leisure outside official heritage lists.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese; dialect knowledge for Tianjin, Jiaodong and Henan sources; Manchu and Mongolian for small heritage material; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling and some religious festivals are politically sensitive; Christian gatherings and Hui practice need care; heritage lists are curated and should not stand for what people actually do.
- **Scope:** the member state's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sheet uses the five provinces as a working list.
- **Held for review:** none.
