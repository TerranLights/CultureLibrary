# Zhongyuan: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), Sinian Federation member state, East Asia (Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan). **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin and its northern dialects (Beijing, Tianjin, Shandong, Henan speech); Classical Chinese; Manchu for banner-era texts; Korean. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Beijingers against new arrivals:** a city idiom of banter against the speech of migrants from many provinces.
- **Tianjin:** its own comic and plain-spoken talk traditions, worth separating from Beijing.
- **Hebei cities and countryside:** Shijiazhuang's music scene against village opera and yangge.
- **Shandong:** the Qufu-Zou Confucian heartland against Jiaodong's port cities (Qingdao, Yantai) and the inland west.
- **Henan by area:** Zhengzhou's young urban scene against the opera-singing countryside; the Jin-speaking north.
- **Hui Muslims:** dress, caps and headscarves as visible expression of faith.
- **Manchu descendants** in Beijing and Chengde: a revival of dress and language as statements.
- **Korean communities** (Beijing's Wangjing, the Shandong coast): language and style between two cultures.
- **Generations:** those raised before and after the 1980s, and today's online youth.
- **LGBTQ people,** whose expression is shaped by both family and public limits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music, literature and urban youth; mixed for rural everyday expression; thin for minorities outside Hui studies.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and dialect knowledge; Classical Chinese; Manchu for some banner texts; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political expression and sexual identity are sensitive; sources on censorship may be blocked inside the country, and informants may be at risk, so anonymize; any legal side of speech belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Great Preface and *Yueji* in a bilingual edition, then the *Analects* speech passages beside them.
2. Search CNKI for "表达抑制 大学生" and filter for samples from Beijing, Henan or Shandong universities.
3. Read Gladney's Niujie chapters and list the expressive markers he names.
4. Search "子弟书" in the National Library of China catalog.
5. Compare Beijing and Shijiazhuang music-scene coverage in music magazines and online reviews.
