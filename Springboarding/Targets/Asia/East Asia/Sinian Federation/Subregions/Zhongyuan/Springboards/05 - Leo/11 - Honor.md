## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hebei (Yan-Zhao):** a self-image of knightly honor; Cangzhou and Mengcun martial traditions.
- **Tianjin:** dockside and martial honor (Huo Yuanjia); sworn-brother networks.
- **Shandong:** western Shandong's *Water Margin* country and martial villages; the Confucian heartland; Yimeng's wartime memory.
- **Henan:** loyalty cults (Yue Fei, Guan Yu) and women's honor in Mulan and in opera.
- **Beijing:** state honors and burial against old guild and banner codes.
- **Men and women:** masculine honor (爷们儿, 义气) against women's honor (chastity in older records; achievement now), to test rather than assume.
- **Hui Muslims:** honor tied to faith, purity and martial lineage.
- **Manchu and Mongol communities:** banner and clan honor in memory.
- **Korean communities,** with their own codes of loyalty (의리); **veterans and soldiers' families,** for whom honor is tied to service.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical, martial and commemorative sources; mixed for everyday honor and face; thin for present-day honor among minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese; English for missionary sources; Manchu and Mongolian for small heritage material; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official heroes are protected from questioning, so contested accounts may be hard to publish; honor-based control of women is sensitive and must be studied in women's own words; revenge belongs to Scorpio, legal matters to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
