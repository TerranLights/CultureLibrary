## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Beijing banner and palace-service descendants** against **new migrant service workers**: two histories of serving in the capital.
- **Civil servants and Party cadres** against **private hospitality staff**: public service against service for pay.
- **Tianjin's old restaurant and teahouse trades**, with their own forms of address.
- **Shandong hosts** at banquets and lineage events around Qufu, against Jiaodong's port towns.
- **Henan and Hebei villagers** who send domestic workers and guards to the cities; their own view of the work.
- **Hui congregations** (women's mosques in Henan; Beijing's Niujie): religious service roles.
- **Catholic parishes** of central Hebei; **Buddhist temple volunteers** (义工).
- **Manchu, Mongol and Korean** communities' own service and hospitality forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exemplars, archives and ethnography; mixed for everyday customer service outside Beijing.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Classical Chinese; Manchu for palace records; Arabic and Persian loans in Hui usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official exemplars are propaganda and must be read as such; domestic workers' treatment and neighborhood informant volunteering are sensitive; military service is restricted; civil-service law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read 《为人民服务》 in full and trace the Xinhua Gate inscription's history.
2. Search 时传祥 纪念 and 焦裕禄 纪念馆 for memorial literature and school materials.

4. Library: Yan Hairong and Jaschok-Shui together, for domestic and religious service.
5. Search the First Historical Archives catalog for 内务府 + 包衣.
