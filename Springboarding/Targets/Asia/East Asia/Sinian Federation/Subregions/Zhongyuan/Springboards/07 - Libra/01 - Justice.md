# Zhongyuan: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (simplified script; classical Chinese for older texts); regional speech of Beijing, Tianjin, Jilu, Jiao-Liao and Henan; Hui mosque usage; Manchu, Mongolian and Korean for smaller groups. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kaifeng and eastern Henan**, where the Judge Bao cult has its home sites, against **Beijing**, seat of central courts and the target of petitioners.
- **Western Shandong** (Liangshan, Heze, Liaocheng), with its outlaw and Boxer memory, against the **Jiaodong** coast.
- **Tianjin**, with a concession past and old street-tough codes, against **rural Hebei**.
- **Hui** communities (Niujie, Cangzhou, Mengcun, Zhoukou): imams and elders in settling wrongs, as they describe it.
- **Catholic villages** of central Hebei and **Henan Protestants**: divine justice in church teaching.
- **Manchu and Mongol** counties around Chengde and Weichang, where banner law and Mongol customary settlement once applied: a history to trace.
- **Rural migrants** in Beijing and Tianjin, who press wage and injury claims far from home, and the families who send them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-modern and Qing justice and for the Judge Bao tradition; mixed for present-day practice, since court data access has narrowed.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and classical Chinese; Manchu for some Qing papers; Mongolian and Korean for small groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petitioners, intercepted petitions, rights lawyers and wrongful-conviction advocacy are politically sensitive; protect interviewees.
- **Held for review:** none.
