# Zhongyuan: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia; Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (simplified; classical Chinese for Zhou and Warring States texts); Beijing and Shandong polite speech; Manchu (Qing records), Mongolian and Korean. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Beijing officials and diplomats** against **ordinary Beijingers**, whose courtesy forms differ from state protocol.
- **Tianjin**, shaped by concession-era dealings, against **Shandong's** banquet-centered hospitality.
- **Jiaodong** ports (Qingdao, Yantai, Weihai) with Korean and Japanese business ties, against inland **Henan**.
- **Chengde's Manchu and Mongol** heritage of court diplomacy.
- **Hui** communities, which mediate between mosque, state and Muslim visitors from abroad, as they describe it.
- **Korean** residents of Wangjing and Qingdao, and their chambers of commerce.
- **Village go-betweens** against **urban professionals** who negotiate by contract.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for classical and Qing diplomacy and for Beijing protocol; mixed for everyday tact; thin for minority envoys' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and classical Chinese; Manchu for Qing records; Korean, Japanese and Mongolian for counterpart sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foreign-ministry archives are largely closed; present foreign policy is politically framed.
- **Held for review:** none.
