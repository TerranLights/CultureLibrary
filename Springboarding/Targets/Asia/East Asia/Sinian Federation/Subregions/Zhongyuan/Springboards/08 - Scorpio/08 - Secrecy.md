## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of officials and old Beijing compound residents:** insider knowledge and what is never said in public.
- **Old performers and traders of Tianqiao and Tianjin:** argot and trade secrets as livelihood.
- **Villages with sectarian pasts in Hebei, Shandong and Henan:** inherited secrecy around banned faiths.
- **Kaifeng Jewish descendants:** disclosure of origin as a choice with costs.
- **Sexual minorities in Beijing against those in county towns of Henan and Hebei:** the closet in a capital and in a small place.
- **Henan families touched by AIDS:** illness kept from neighbors.
- **Catholics in Hebei:** split between open and unregistered communities, with different needs for secrecy.
- **Korean Chinese in Wangjing and Qingdao:** switching languages to keep talk private.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history (state secrecy, sects, palace papers); mixed for privacy and family secrets; thin for minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Manchu for palace papers; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state secrets, banned faiths, sexual minorities, unregistered churches and the AIDS epidemic are guarded topics; Yiguandao and other banned groups must be studied through published sources only.
