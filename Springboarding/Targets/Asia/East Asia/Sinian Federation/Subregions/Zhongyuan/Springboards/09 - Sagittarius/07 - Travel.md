*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jiaodong and western Shandong:** northeast migration, sea routes, Korea links; two different departure patterns.
- **Hebei's Bashang and Zhangjiakou:** caravan and pastoral routes north.
- **Henan:** famine flight in memory, labor migration now; the leaver as provider.
- **Beijing:** the destination for officials, students, pilgrims and tourists; hosts more than it sends.
- **Tianjin:** a port city with ferries, cruise terminals and treaty-era foreigners.
- **Hui traders and Hajj pilgrims:** religious travel and long-distance commerce.
- **Weichang and Bashang Mongols:** seasonal moving as a way of life, distinct from tourism.
- **Korean residents of Qingdao, Yantai and Beijing:** cross-border lives.
- **Left-behind families:** travel seen from those who stay.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration history and present-day mobility statistics; mixed for travelers' own narratives; thin for minority movement.
- **Languages needed:** modern and classical Chinese; Japanese and Korean for outsider diaries in the original; Mongolian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hukou and migrant treatment; famine history; exit controls on passports for some groups. Rules of entry and residence as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Gottschang and Lary, then search CNKI for 闯关东 口述 to find oral histories.
2. Pull the latest 农民工监测调查报告 tables on workers by province of origin.
3. Download Baidu Migration flows for a Chunyun period into and out of Zhengzhou and Jinan.
4. Read Ennin and Ch'oe Pu on Shandong as two outsider baselines.
5. Search 淄博烧烤 coverage and Mafengwo notes to see how hosts and visitors describe each other.
