# Zhongyuan: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), in the Sinian Federation (East Asia); member-state lands Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong and Henan. **Language(s) for searching:** Standard Chinese and classical Chinese; Hui sectarian names (Arabic-derived); Korean for Korean-language congregations. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hui Gedimu, Yihewani and Salafiyya congregations (Cangzhou, Zhengzhou, Niujie):** the clearest scripture-versus-custom dispute in these lands.
- **Henan registered and house-church Protestants:** literal Bible reading may differ widely between them.
- **Followers of banned new movements:** a group that recruited in Henan villages; members' own accounts are scarce.
- **Confucian revivalists in Qufu and Zoucheng:** restoration of the classics, and debates on how literal it is.
- **Hebei and Tianjin Catholics:** memory of being targets of 1900, and of their own church's traditionalists.
- **Northwest Shandong Boxer counties:** how villages remember a movement born among them.
- **The Red Guard generation in Beijing:** ideological purity, in retrospect.
- **Online cultural purists:** "原教旨" used as an insult in arguments about tradition.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the historical sects and the Boxers; restricted and partisan for banned groups today (state sources and defectors both need reading against each other); thin for Hui reform in these provinces.
- **Languages needed:** classical and modern Chinese; Arabic for Hui reform texts; English for diaspora scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** high. Banned groups, Hui reform and Salafiyya under current policy, the Cultural Revolution. Never imply a people or faith is fundamentalist; record who uses the word about whom. Protect informants.
- **Held for review:** none.
