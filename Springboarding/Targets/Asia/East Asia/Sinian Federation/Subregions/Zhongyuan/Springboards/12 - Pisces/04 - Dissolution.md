# Zhongyuan: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), a Han member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan). **Language(s) for searching:** Classical and Standard Chinese; Manchu and Mongolian for assimilation questions; Hebrew and Chinese for the Kaifeng Jewish records. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (definition proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daoist practitioners on the Jiaodong coast and Chan circles in central Hebei:** two contemplative routes to losing the self's edges.
- **Charismatic Protestant congregations in Henan:** ecstatic worship in a group.
- **Hutong residents moved out of central Beijing, Shandong farmers moved by village mergers, and the Baiyangdian villages around Xiong'an:** three forms of community ending.
- **Yellow River flood and dam-relocation communities in Henan:** dissolution by water.
- **Manchu and Mongol descendants in Beijing and the Chengde autonomous counties; Kaifeng's Jewish-descended families:** identity fading and, in places, revived.
- **Square dancers and festival crowds:** age and class in collective merging.
- **Families caring for relatives with dementia:** the self coming apart at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on classical mysticism and on demolition; mixed on contemporary ecstatic practice; divorce counts by province exist in the civil-affairs yearbooks.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Chinese, modern Chinese, Manchu, some Hebrew for Kaifeng inscriptions, Japanese for some Daoist scholarship.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** qigong groups later banned, unregistered churches, forced relocation and grave-flattening campaigns; Kaifeng Jewish identity is politically delicate.
- **Held for review:** none.
