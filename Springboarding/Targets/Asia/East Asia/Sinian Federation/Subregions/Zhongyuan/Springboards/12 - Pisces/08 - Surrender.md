# Zhongyuan: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Zhongyuan (中原), a Han member state of the Sinian Federation, region East Asia (Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Henan). **Language(s) for searching:** Standard and Classical Chinese; Beijing colloquial; Hui usage; Manchu and Mongolian. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Elderly villagers in Henan and Hebei and young urban "Buddha-style" professionals:** acceptance from hardship against acceptance as a pose of detachment.
- **Families of the dying in Beijing hospitals:** who decides to let go, and in whose name.
- **Tangshan survivors:** loss without choice, and its later telling.
- **Hui Muslims, Hebei Catholics and Buddhist lay devotees:** three vocabularies of yielding to God or to karma.
- **Manchu descendants and families of the soldiers in Beiping in 1949:** memories of a regime's yielding.
- **Park chess and Go players:** conceding as a daily, public act.
- **Disabled people's own writing and associations:** acceptance against fighting for access.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and classical thought; mixed on end-of-life decisions (hospital data rarely public); thin on recovery fellowships.
- **Languages needed:** Classical and modern Chinese, Manchu (abdication-era documents), English and Chinese for Macartney sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide as "giving up" (Lao She's death, 1966) needs care; wartime surrender is politically framed; end-of-life choices are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
