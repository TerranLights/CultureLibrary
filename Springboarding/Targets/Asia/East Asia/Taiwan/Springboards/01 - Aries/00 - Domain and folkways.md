# Taiwan: Domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi, Tâi-lô romanization), Taiwan Hakka (Sixian and Hailu), the Formosan Indigenous languages; Eastern Min for Matsu; Japanese and Dutch for older records. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain, self and identity: what a self is here and how it asserts itself (the self, the body, appearance, beginnings, initiative), with naming as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo majority**, north (Taipei basin) against south (Tainan, Kaohsiung): metropolitan and temple-town self-presentation may differ.
- **Hakka:** the northern belt (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli) against southern Liudui (Pingtung, Meinong); lineage halls shape naming.
- **Waishengren** and descendants, especially the 眷村 generation: a self tied to a lost province.
- **Indigenous peoples, beyond Amis and Atayal:** Paiwan and Rukai (ranked houses and inherited names), Bunun, Tsou, Puyuma, Saisiyat, Truku and Seediq (tattoo and Gaya), Tao on Orchid Island (teknonymy), Kavalan, Thao, Sakizaya, Hla'alua, Kanakanavu.
- **Pingpu peoples** (Siraya, Kaxabu, Pazeh, Taokas and others): identity reclaimed after long absorption.
- **New immigrants (新住民)** and their children (新二代): two naming traditions in one family.
- **Offshore islanders:** Penghu, Kinmen, Matsu, each with its own speech and wartime history.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese Americans and Taiwanese in Japan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Mandarin-language psychology and surveys; mixed for Hakka; thin and scattered for single Indigenous and Pingpu peoples. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled (for example, whether Kinmen and Matsu belong), so these sheets write for the main island and its nearest islands.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taigi, Hakka, Japanese (1895–1945), Dutch (17th-century records), the Indigenous languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Taiwanese and Chinese identity is politically charged; facial tattoo and older rites were bound to headhunting and carry restricted knowledge; the policy side of Indigenous name restoration belongs to Libra or Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
