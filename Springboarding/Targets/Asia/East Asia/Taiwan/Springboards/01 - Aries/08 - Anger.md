# Taiwan: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taigi (much everyday anger talk), Hakka, Puyuma and other Indigenous languages; Classical Chinese for medical classics. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo,** north and south: how anger talk differs between Taigi and Mandarin registers in the same family.
- **Hakka:** whether anger talk differs in Hakka-speaking homes and townships.
- **Waishengren families:** anger and silence after 1949 losses.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Puyuma and Amis protest leaders; Atayal and Paiwan ways of settling disputes inside villages; drinking and anger as an imposed stereotype to examine, not repeat.
- **Families of 228 victims:** when and how their anger was voiced, before and after 1987.
- **Men and women:** who may shout, and how women's anger is labeled.
- **Young people online** against older talk-show audiences.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** overseas Taiwanese groups who organized protests abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest, violence and online anger; mixed for child-rearing; thin for Indigenous dispute customs in their own terms.
