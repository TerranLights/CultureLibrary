# Taiwan: The domain, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (華語, Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (臺語, Tâi-lô romanization), Hakka (客語, Sixian and Hailu varieties), the Formosan Indigenous languages; Japanese and Dutch for colonial-era records. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how is it held: possessions, money, resources, food ways, dress ways and wealth ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien-speaking majority**, separating the old ports (Tainan, Lukang, Dadaocheng in Taipei) from farm towns of the Chianan plain: merchant against farming ideas of worth.
- **Hakka** of Taoyuan-Hsinchu-Miaoli, of Liudui (六堆) in Pingtung and Kaohsiung, and of Hualien-Taitung: separate settlement histories, separate foods.
- **Mainlander families** (外省人) and the former military dependents' villages (眷村): arrived with little land; different inheritance and food stories.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Paiwan and Rukai (ranked houses and heirlooms), Amis (age grades; house through the female line *check*), Atayal, Truku and Seediq (gaga/gaya norms), Bunun, Tsou, Saisiyat, Thao, Puyuma, Kavalan, Sakizaya, Hla'alua, Kanakanavu, and the Tao of Lanyu (boats, taro fields, flying fish).
- **Plains Indigenous groups** (平埔族群: Siraya, Taivoan, Makatao, Ketagalan, Kaxabu and others): land loss under Qing contracts.
- **Penghu islanders:** stone fish weirs (石滬) held in shares.
- **New immigrants** (新住民) from Vietnam and the Philippines, and Southeast Asian migrant workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Statistics, colonial surveys and Academia Sinica archives are deep; Indigenous peoples' own accounts are fewer and should lead over colonial records.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin (Traditional), Hokkien (Tâi-lô and Han characters), Hakka, Formosan languages for terms; Japanese for 1895-1945; Dutch and classical Chinese for earlier periods.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heirloom beads, jars and ritual objects of ranked Paiwan and Rukai houses; Tao ritual knowledge about fish and boats; ask the communities what may be shown. Colonial surveys carry the colonizer's categories.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (whether Penghu, Kinmen, Matsu and the outlying islands belong to it); the other sheets of this sign are written for the main island and its close islands.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. SRDA: search 消費 and 價值觀 across TSCS waves; list every module that asks about money, goods or life goals.
2. DGBAS: download the latest 家庭收支調查 tables by county and the CPI weights; note the oldest year available.
3. Institute of Ethnology library: find the Chinese translation of 番族慣習調查報告書 and note each people's volume on property.
4. Taiwan Diaries Database: search 價錢 and 買 in 灌園先生日記 for a first list of prices and goods.
5. Shung Ye Museum and Council of Indigenous Peoples: ask which collections hold Paiwan and Rukai heirlooms and how the houses want them described.
