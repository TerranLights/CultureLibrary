# Taiwan: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paiwan and Rukai ranked houses** against the more egalitarian Bunun and Tsou: heirlooms tied to rank versus goods spread among kin.
- **Tao of Lanyu:** boats, taro terraces and fishing gear as household things, and rules around a dead person's goods, as the Tao share them.
- **Amis coastal and valley villages:** age-grade gear and the house held through women *check*.
- **Atayal, Truku and Seediq:** woven cloth and weaving tools as a woman's possessions.
- **Mainlander families:** things carried in 1949 (gold, documents, photographs) as the core of family memory.
- **Hokkien and Hakka farm families:** ancestor tablets and the old 三合院 courtyard house's contents.
- **New immigrants from Vietnam and the Philippines:** parcels sent home; what crosses the sea each way.
- **Elderly scavengers (拾荒者) and the homeless around Taipei Main Station:** having almost nothing, and keeping it safe.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Literature, museums and news stories are rich; no national household-inventory study is known to the agent (search for one).
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for older people's words for things; Paiwan, Rukai and Tao for heirloom names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heirlooms of ranked houses and Tao ritual objects; ask before naming or photographing. Disaster losses and the belongings of the dead are painful; approach through survivors' groups.
- **Held for review:** none.
