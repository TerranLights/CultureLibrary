# Taiwan: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Exam generations:** those who sat the joint entrance exam before 2002 against later cohorts under multiple entry routes.
- **Hokkien speakers punished for their language at school** in the 1950s-1980s, against their grandchildren.
- **Hakka** of the north and of Liudui: language loss and pride.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Atayal, Truku and Seediq (earned marks), Rukai and Paiwan (honors within ranked houses), Bunun and Puyuma (rites for boys), Amis (age grades), Tao, Tsou, Saisiyat and the smaller peoples; urban Indigenous families in New Taipei and Taoyuan.
- **Mainlander veterans (榮民)** who aged without families: worth after a soldier's life.
- **New immigrants and their children (新二代).**
- **Disabled people:** the blind massage trade (視障按摩) as a protected livelihood and its pride.
- **Men in industrial towns of Kaohsiung and Taichung** after factories moved overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in psychology and survey work; fewer studies in the peoples' own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien and Hakka for everyday shame and pride words; Formosan languages for honors and rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data need care; facial tattoos and honor rights belong to their peoples' protocols; slurs for Indigenous peoples and migrant spouses should be described, not quoted.
- **Held for review:** none.
