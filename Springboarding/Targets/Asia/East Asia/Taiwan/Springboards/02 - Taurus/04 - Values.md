## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** those schooled under martial-law civics against those under the 2019 curriculum.
- **Religious communities:** folk-religion temple networks, Buddhist groups (Tzu Chi, Fo Guang Shan, Dharma Drum), I-Kuan Tao, Presbyterian and Catholic churches (strong among Indigenous peoples), and the small Muslim community.
- **Hokkien and Hakka lineages:** hall names and family precepts differ by origin (Zhangzhou, Quanzhou, Hakka counties).
- **Mainlander families:** values taught as "Chinese culture" in 1949-1987, and how their grandchildren see them.
- **Indigenous peoples,** each on its own terms: Atayal, Truku and Seediq norms; Bunun prohibitions; Amis age-grade duties; Paiwan and Rukai rank and honor; Tao island rules; Tsou, Saisiyat and Thao ceremonies.
- **North against south:** party and value maps often split along this line; test with TEDS.
- **New immigrants:** values their children are taught at home and at school.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Several repeated surveys sample Taiwan; textbooks are archived.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese for primers; Hokkien and Hakka for sayings; Formosan languages for norms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** debates on marriage and on national identity are polarized; present both sides in their own words. Some ceremonial knowledge in Indigenous norms is not public.
- **Held for review:** none.
