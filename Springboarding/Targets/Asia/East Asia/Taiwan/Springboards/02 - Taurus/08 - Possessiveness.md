# Taiwan: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages; Vietnamese and Tagalog for migrant spouses and workers. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it. Violence and protection orders as written in law go to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien and Hakka patrilineal families** (daughters "married out", sons kept close) against **Amis and Kavalan households** with women at the center of the house *check*: whose children are "one's own".
- **Paiwan and Rukai** inheritance by first-born regardless of sex *check*, against Han division among sons: who may keep the house whole.
- **Mainlander families** split by 1949: second wives and lost first families.
- **Migrant spouses** from Vietnam and the Philippines: in-law control and isolation, as the women describe it.
- **Urban young couples** with tracking apps against older generations' courtship.
- **Temple and neighborhood networks** in Tainan, Kaohsiung and the Banqiao-Sanchong belt: turf.
- **Craft workshops** (woodcarving in Sanyi, tea in Lugu, metal shops in Taichung): masters and apprentices.

## 4. Flags
