# Taiwan: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages; classical Chinese for Qing verse; Japanese for colonial bathing culture. **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien south and north:** Tainan's sweeter cooking and old snack shops against Taipei's.
- **Hakka** of the northwest hills and of Liudui: preserved vegetables, tea and Hakka bayin.
- **Mainlander military-village cooking** (beef noodle soup, dumplings, regional cuisines brought in 1949).
- **Indigenous peoples:** Amis wild greens and polyphony; Bunun millet songs; Paiwan and Rukai millet foods; Tao flying fish; Atayal and Truku preserved meats and weaving textures; Puyuma and Atayal springs.
- **Yunnanese and Thai-Burmese community of Longgang, Zhongli** and the **Muslim community** (halal food).
- **Southeast Asian food streets:** Taichung's ASEAN Square and the Taoyuan station area.
- **Buddhist, I-Kuan Tao and Adventist abstainers** against banquet and drinking cultures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Food writing, film, nutrition data and tourism records abound; touch and personal-space norms are thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for taste and texture words; Hakka; Formosan languages for song and food terms; Japanese for bathing history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** betel-nut sellers and hostess trades risk stigma; some Indigenous songs are ceremonial and not for casual use; Amis recording rights are a live dispute.
- **Held for review:** none.
