# Taiwan: The domain: communication and learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Han characters plus Tâi-lô or Pe̍h-ōe-jī), Hakka (Sixian, Hailu and other varieties), the Formosan (Indigenous) languages, Taiwan Sign Language; Japanese and Dutch for older archives. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech ways, learning ways, neighbors and short journeys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo Taiwanese, north against south:** Taipei speech against Tainan and Kaohsiung, with Lukang and Yilan accents as known local outliers.
- **Hakka of three zones:** Taoyuan-Hsinchu-Miaoli, the Liudui 六堆 settlements of Pingtung and Meinong, and the east-coast Hakka of Hualien and Taitung.
- **Mainlander families and the former military dependents' villages 眷村:** home dialects other than Mandarin, and a different schooling memory.
- **The sixteen recognized Indigenous peoples:** mountain peoples (Atayal, Bunun, Tsou, Paiwan, Rukai) against east-coast Amis and Puyuma, and the Tao of Orchid Island.
- **Plains Indigenous peoples:** Siraya around Tainan, Kavalan, Kaxabu and Pazeh near Puli, with language revival under way.
- **New immigrants and migrant workers:** Vietnamese, Filipino and Thai speakers, and workers from Java.
- **The Deaf community:** Taiwan Sign Language as its own speech community.
- **Island communities:** Penghu, and Kinmen and Matsu if carried (Matsu speaks Eastern Min).
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese communities in North America and Japan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Mandarin and Hokkien; mixed for Hakka; thin and uneven for individual Indigenous peoples and for new-immigrant languages.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for Taiwan and its nearby islands.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Japanese (1895 to 1945), Dutch and Latin (1624 to 1662), and the Indigenous languages for community materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memories of school punishment for speaking home languages; martial-law-era records; Indigenous oral knowledge to be used as each people shares it; Plains Indigenous recognition is a live political question.
- **Law:** language legislation (for example the 2019 national-languages act) goes to Libra for its letter and Capricorn for its spirit.
- **Held for review:** none.
