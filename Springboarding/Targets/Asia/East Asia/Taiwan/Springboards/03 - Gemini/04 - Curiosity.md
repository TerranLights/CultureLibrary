# Taiwan: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tao on Orchid Island:** long studied by outsiders, a case for how being looked at shapes the answer to curious visitors.
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** carved ancestral house posts and noble houses drew collectors; what may be asked about them.
- **Seediq and Truku:** history studied heavily since the Wushe uprising of 1930, with community voices on who asks and why.
- **Hsinchu engineers and science-park families:** a technical culture of tinkering, to test against other regions.
- **Rural townships in Yunlin and Chiayi against Taipei:** whether the norms on asking about neighbors' affairs differ with town size.
- **Hakka in Miaoli and Hsinchu:** local exploring and hometown-history projects.
- **Mainlander veterans' families:** curiosity about lost home provinces, and reunions after travel opened in 1987.
- **New immigrant spouses:** being the object of questions, and their own questions about Taiwan.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** returnees from North America and their children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science education and outsiders' records; thin for everyday norms of asking.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin and Hokkien for media and forums; Japanese for the colonial anthropologists; classical Chinese for Qing travel writing; Paiwan, Tao, Seediq and other Indigenous languages for community views on being studied.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial-era collections of Indigenous objects and remains; knowledge restricted by Indigenous peoples; personal questions as a source of family tension.
- **Held for review:** none.
