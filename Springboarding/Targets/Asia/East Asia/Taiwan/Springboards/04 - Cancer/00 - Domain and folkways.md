# Taiwan: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Tâi-gí, in Tâi-lô or POJ romanization), Hakka, the sixteen recognized Indigenous languages, Japanese (colonial-era sources). **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what home is and who is family, across household forms, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo (Hokkien) Taiwanese:** separate the old southern towns (Tainan, Lukang) from the northern metropolis; house and lineage practice likely differ.
- **Hakka:** the northern belt (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli) versus the southern Liudui 六堆 area (Pingtung, Meinong); different dialects and compound forms.
- **Mainland-origin families (外省人) and the juancun:** a home founded in displacement after 1949.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Amis, Paiwan, Rukai, Atayal, Seediq, Truku, Bunun, Saisiyat, Tao of Lanyu, Tsou, Puyuma, Thao, Kavalan, Sakizaya, Hla'alua and Kanakanavu, each on its own terms; descent, house and settlement systems differ from people to people and should never be pooled.
- **Plains Indigenous (Pingpu) peoples:** Siraya, Ketagalan, Taokas, Pazeh and others; their revival associations are the first voices to seek.
- **Outlying islands:** Kinmen, Matsu (Eastern Min speakers) and Penghu.
- **New immigrants (新住民):** marriage migrants from Vietnam, mainland China and Southeast Asia, and live-in migrant caregivers from the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand and Java.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Strong official statistics, a deep ethnographic record on Han families, colonial surveys; Indigenous sources are thinner in their own voices and must be sought from the peoples' own bodies.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, Japanese for colonial records, Indigenous languages for the peoples' own terms; classical Chinese for Qing documents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous house and clan knowledge (named houses, sacred rooms) belongs to the peoples; colonial and Han-written accounts need critical reading. Family law (inheritance, adoption) goes to Libra and Capricorn, not here. The nation's exact lands (whether Penghu, Kinmen, Matsu, Lanyu and Green Island are carried) are still to be settled; this run writes for the main island and its near islands.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. On DGBAS, open the 2020 census results and search 「家庭型態」 and 「住宅」 for household-type tables by county.
2. In the Academia Sinica Institute of Taiwan History database, search 「鬮書」 and 「分家」.
3. Search library catalogs for "Cohen House United House Divided" and the Institute of Ethnology bulletin for 「家屋」 with an Indigenous people's name.
4. Search the Council of Indigenous Peoples' online dictionaries for the word for "house" in each language.
5. Search 「入厝 習俗」 and 「地基主」 in National Central Library periodical indexes.
