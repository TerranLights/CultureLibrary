# Taiwan: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages; Japanese for colonial registers; classical Chinese for genealogies. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo lineage villages of the southwest:** single-surname settlements, a place to test patrilineage claims.
- **Northern Hakka and Liudui Hakka:** ancestral halls and trusts to compare between two dialect areas.
- **Mainland-origin families:** kin left across the strait; family rebuilt from a single migrant, and reunion after 1987.
- **Amis and Puyuma:** kin systems to read in the peoples' own terms.
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** house and rank systems to check; compare commoner and chiefly houses.
- **Saisiyat, Bunun, Tsou:** clan and surname systems with ritual duties.
- **Atayal, Seediq, Truku:** the gaga group alongside the household.
- **Cross-border families:** Vietnamese, mainland Chinese, Thai and Filipino spouses and their children.
- **Same-sex couples and chosen families**, urban and rural.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Han kinship in Taiwan is among the most studied anywhere; panels and registers are deep. Indigenous kin systems need the peoples' own publications, not only older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, Japanese, classical Chinese, Indigenous languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** household registers hold private data and need permission; adoption and minor-marriage histories touch living families; adoption and family law go to Libra and Capricorn; clan and house knowledge belongs to each Indigenous people.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Register with the PSFD and open its codebook for 「代間移轉」 and 「同住」 items.
2. Search SRDA for 台灣社會變遷基本調查 「家庭」 modules and note the years.
3. Search library catalogs for "Wolf Huang Marriage and Adoption" and for 伊慶春.
4. Query the Ministry of Education dictionaries for 「阿舅」, 「姑丈」 and 「親家」 in Taiwanese and Hakka.
5. Search 「宗親會」 with a common surname (陳, 林) to see how associations describe kin.
