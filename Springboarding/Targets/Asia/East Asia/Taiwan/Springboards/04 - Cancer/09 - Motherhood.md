# Taiwan: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages, Vietnamese; Japanese for colonial-era midwifery. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo households in the south:** the husband's mother and the postpartum month, to compare with northern urban practice.
- **Hakka families:** a strong pioneer-mother image in literature, to test against families' own accounts.
- **Mainland-origin families:** mothers left on the mainland in 1949, and mothers who raised children in the juancun.
- **Amis, Puyuma and other Indigenous peoples:** each people's own account of women's household standing and mothering.
- **Vietnamese and other marriage-migrant mothers:** mothering across two languages and under in-law scrutiny.
- **Urban professional mothers in Taipei and Hsinchu:** a place to study paid postpartum care and the timing of first births.
- **Single and same-sex mothers.**
- **Religious communities:** Mazu devotees, Buddhist and Christian mothers' groups, each with its own model mother.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Fertility, employment and postpartum data are strong; the mother figure in folk religion is well documented; Indigenous mothering needs the peoples' own sources.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, Indigenous languages, Vietnamese, Japanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** infertility, postpartum depression and maternal death; surrogacy and parental-leave law go to Libra and Capricorn; some women's rites may not be open to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
