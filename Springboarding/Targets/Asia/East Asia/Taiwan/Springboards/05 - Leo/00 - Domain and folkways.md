# Taiwan: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi, Tâi-lô romanization), Taiwanese Hakka, the recognized Indigenous languages; Japanese and Classical Chinese for older records. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure (creativity, play, pleasure, romance, children, sport ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien majority, north against south:** Taipei and Keelung against Tainan, Lukang and Kaohsiung; the temple-festival density differs.
- **Hakka, two clusters:** Taoyuan, Hsinchu and Miaoli against the Liudui (六堆) area of Pingtung and Meinong; separate festival calendars and song forms.
- **Post-1949 mainlander families and the veterans' villages (眷村):** different leisure forms (opera, crosstalk, mahjong customs).
- **Indigenous peoples by social structure:** Paiwan and Rukai ranked houses; Amis and Puyuma age-grades; Bunun, Tsou, Atayal, Truku and Seediq; the Tao of Orchid Island; each marks festivity differently.
- **Plains Indigenous (Pingpu) peoples:** Siraya, Kavalan, Taivoan, Makatao, Pazeh and others, whose festivals are being revived.
- **Outlying islands:** Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu (Mindong-speaking), each with its own festival forms.
- **New immigrants and migrant workers from Southeast Asia:** their own holidays held in Taiwanese public space.
- **Generations:** those raised under martial law against those raised after 1987.
- **Taiwanese diaspora (North America, Japan):** comparison only; the nation's findings apply to it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban Han leisure and temple festivals; mixed for Indigenous peoples (much is outsider-written); thin for Pingpu revival, Matsu and migrant workers.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien and Hakka; Japanese for 1895–1945 records; Classical Chinese for Qing sources; Indigenous languages for oral material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parts of some Indigenous ceremonies (the Saisiyat pas-ta'ai among them) carry taboos on outsiders; headhunting history; betel-nut sellers and funeral dancers draw moral judgment; illegal gambling ties to crime.
- **Territory:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for Taiwan island and the outlying islands.
- **Held for review:** none.
