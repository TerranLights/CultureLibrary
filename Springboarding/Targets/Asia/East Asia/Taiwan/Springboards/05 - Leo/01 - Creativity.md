# Taiwan: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Paiwan, Atayal, Tao and others); Japanese for 1895–1945. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old craft towns of Quanzhou heritage:** Lukang and Tainan, with lantern, tin and temple-carving lineages.
- **Hakka craft areas:** Meinong's oil-paper umbrellas, Sanyi's woodcarving in Miaoli; whether Hakka makers frame skill differently.
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** carving and beadwork tied to ranked houses; a test case for who owns a design.
- **Atayal, Truku and Seediq:** weaving and its revival; check how makers relate it to facial-tattoo motifs.
- **Tao of Orchid Island:** plank-boat (tatala) building and carving, as the Tao share it.
- **Hsinchu and the science parks:** engineering invention, a separate creative world from the arts.
- **Taipei and Kaohsiung art districts:** gallery and design scenes against rural crafts.
- **Kinmen and Penghu:** stone carving (Kinmen's wind-lion figures, 風獅爺) and island building crafts.
- **New-immigrant and migrant writers and artists:** Southeast Asian makers' own work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on policy, industry and art history; mixed on crafts; thin on everyday improvising and on Pingpu makers.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien (craft jargon), Hakka, Japanese (colonial salon records), Paiwan, Atayal and Tao terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** design rights of Paiwan and Rukai houses and Atayal facial-tattoo motifs, as the peoples state them; commercial copying of Indigenous patterns.
- **Held for review:** none.
