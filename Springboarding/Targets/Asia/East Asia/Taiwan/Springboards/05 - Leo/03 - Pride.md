# Taiwan: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Bunun, Paiwan, Rukai, Puyuma and others). **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien south against north:** Tainan and Kaohsiung hometown pride set against Taipei's.
- **Hakka in Liudui and in the northern hills:** two forms of Hakka pride to check separately.
- **Post-1949 mainlander families and veterans' villages:** pride in service and in a lost homeland.
- **Bunun, Paiwan, Rukai, Puyuma and Amis:** each has its own marks and settings for pride, by rank, age-grade or deed.
- **Tao of Orchid Island:** pride in boat and sea work, as the Tao describe it.
- **Pingpu groups seeking recognition:** pride as reclaiming.
- **Kinmen and Matsu:** front-line memory and pride distinct from Taiwan island.
- **Science-park engineers against farmers and fishers:** pride in different kinds of work.
- **Southeast Asian new immigrants and their children:** pride in two heritages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national and ethnic pride in surveys and press; mixed on personal pride and boasting; thin on Pingpu and island communities.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Bunun, Paiwan and Rukai terms; Japanese for colonial-era baseball sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride is entangled with cross-strait politics; present it by group and survey item, never as one voice. Indigenous adornment rules belong to the peoples.
- **Held for review:** none.
