# Taiwan: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous and Pingpu terms (Siraya revival vocabulary), Southeast Asian languages for migrant communities. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pingpu peoples (Siraya, Taivoan, Makatao, Kavalan, Ketagalan descendants):** the clearest case of long invisibility and return.
- **Recognized Indigenous peoples by place:** urban Indigenous migrants in New Taipei and Taoyuan against home villages in Taitung, Hualien and the mountains.
- **Tao of Orchid Island:** visibility through tourism and through protest.
- **Hakka:** often described as an "invisible" group in mixed cities (*check* the phrase's use).
- **Southeast Asian migrant workers and new immigrants:** visible in stations and parks, unseen in homes and factories.
- **Elders living alone, especially in rural counties.**
- **Disabled people and the unhoused (Wanhua, Taipei).**
- **Kinmen and Matsu:** small islands far from the national gaze.
- **Online youth against offline older generations:** different stages for being seen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on media and online attention; mixed on census categories; thin on how Pingpu communities and migrant workers themselves describe being seen.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Siraya revival terms; Vietnamese, Thai and Tagalog for migrant voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance files name living people; migrant workers' visibility can carry risk to their jobs; Indigenous ceremonies may limit photography.
- **Held for review:** none.
