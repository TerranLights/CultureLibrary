# Taiwan: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Tao and others), Classical Chinese; Vietnamese, Thai and Tagalog for migrant workers' own accounts. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of 228 and White Terror victims:** Hokkien, mainlander and Indigenous victims, each with different paths to a restored name.
- **Tao of Orchid Island:** dignity tied to land and the nuclear-waste site.
- **Other Indigenous peoples:** name restoration and urban Indigenous workers in construction and fishing.
- **Hakka and Hokkien rural elders:** care in old age at home against in institutions.
- **Post-1949 veterans (榮民) in veterans' homes:** dignity in solitary old age.
- **Southeast Asian migrant caregivers, factory workers and fishers.**
- **Former Losheng residents and disabled people.**
- **The unhoused around Wanhua and Taipei Main Station.**
- **Kinmen and Matsu:** former front-line civilians under military rule until the 1990s.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on transitional justice and NGO reports; mixed on elder care; thin on how migrant workers describe dignity in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, Classical Chinese; Tao terms; Japanese for colonial-era sources on comfort women; migrant workers' languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony (comfort women, political prisoners); patients' privacy; workers' identities. Rights law and court rulings go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
