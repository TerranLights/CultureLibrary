# Taiwan: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Seediq, Tsou and others); Japanese for 1895–1945. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien and Hakka families:** shaming in child-rearing; whether practice differs by class and generation.
- **Post-1949 mainlander families:** their own stories of loss and lowered status after 1949.
- **Tsou, Seediq, Atayal and other peoples:** colonial and postwar slights, in each people's own telling.
- **Pingpu descendants:** whether and why families kept their origins quiet, as their oral histories tell it.
- **Conscripts and veterans:** military hazing across eras.
- **LGBTQ youth:** school humiliation and its changing response.
- **New immigrants from Southeast Asia and their children:** slurs and jokes at school.
- **Kinmen and Matsu:** life under military administration, and how islanders describe it.
- **Online and offline generations:** pile-ons against older forms of gossip and village ridicule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on bullying, online shaming and colonial history; mixed on family shaming; thin on humiliation inside Indigenous and Pingpu communities as they describe it.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Japanese for colonial records; Seediq and Tsou terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide cases, victims' families and slurs; quote slurs only for context. Law on insult and defamation goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
