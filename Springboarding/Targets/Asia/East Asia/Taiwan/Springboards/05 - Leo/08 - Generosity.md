# Taiwan: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien temple communities:** Ghost Month and pilgrimage giving; north (Keelung) against south (Tainan, Pingtung's Donggang).
- **Hakka villages in Hsinchu, Taoyuan and Liudui:** festival rotation and pooled giving.
- **Buddhist lay movements against folk-temple donors:** different reasons and channels for giving.
- **Christian churches, including Presbyterian churches in Indigenous villages:** tithes and church-led relief.
- **Bunun, Tsou, Paiwan, Amis and Tao:** sharing of meat, labor and fish, each to be asked separately.
- **Post-1949 mainlander families:** veterans' associations and native-place (同鄉會) giving.
- **Urban professionals against rural farmers:** giving through apps against giving in kind.
- **Southeast Asian new immigrants and migrant workers:** remittances home, and mosque and church giving in Taiwan.
- **Taiwanese diaspora donors (North America):** comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on organized charity, Buddhist movements and temple festivals; mixed on household giving; thin on informal sharing and on migrant remittances.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Indigenous terms; Vietnamese and Tagalog for migrant giving.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** donor privacy; charity scandals; the 神豬 contest is contested on animal-welfare grounds. Fundraising law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
