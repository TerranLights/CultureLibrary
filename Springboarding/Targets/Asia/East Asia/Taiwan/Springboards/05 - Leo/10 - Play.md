# Taiwan: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Paiwan, Bunun, Amis and others). **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien and Hakka children's lore:** separate rhymes and riddle traditions, by county.
- **Post-1949 mainlander families:** thirteen-tile mahjong, chess and crosstalk against Taiwanese forms.
- **Paiwan, Rukai, Bunun, Amis, Puyuma and Tao:** each people's games and ceremonial play.
- **Pingpu revival groups:** games revived with ceremonies.
- **Elders:** chess and cards in temple plazas and parks.
- **Urban youth:** gaming, esports and claw machines.
- **Rural children:** outdoor play and farm work.
- **Southeast Asian migrant workers:** Sunday games and sports in parks.
- **Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu:** island children's games and festivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on gaming, festivals and folklore collections; mixed on time use; thin on Indigenous and migrant play as the players describe it.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Japanese for colonial folklore; Indigenous terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling and gaming disorder; ceremonial play may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.
