# Taiwan: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages (Amis and others), Mindong (Matsu). **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hokkien and Hakka families:** blessing verse, wedding and New Year joys; Hakka festivals in Miaoli (the lantern-dragon 火旁龍 *check*).
- **Post-1949 mainlander families:** crosstalk, opera and their own festival customs.
- **Amis, Puyuma, Rukai, Paiwan, Bunun and Tao:** harvest, fishing-season and church joys, each to be asked separately.
- **Pingpu revival communities:** night ceremonies and their festive parts.
- **Matsu:** the Lantern Festival 擺暝 celebrations in Mindong-speaking villages.
- **Penghu and Kinmen:** island Lantern Festival customs (Penghu's turtle-cake offerings, 乞龜).
- **Young adults:** small joys, concerts and travel; the 小確幸 debate.
- **Elders:** learning centers, temple plazas and grandchildren.
- **Southeast Asian new immigrants and migrant workers:** their own New Year, Eid and Christmas gatherings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on well-being surveys and festivals; mixed on everyday joy; thin on how Indigenous and island communities name joy in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka; Amis and other Indigenous terms; Mindong for Matsu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some festival parts are closed to outsiders; mental-health data needs care.
- **Held for review:** none.
