# Taiwan: Domain and folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Tâi-gí), Hakka (Sixian and Hailu varieties), the Formosan Indigenous languages, Japanese for the colonial record. **Concept in one line:** what good work and a well-kept life are (daily round, kept house, chores, craft, rest, health).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo Taiwanese:** Tainan and the south, Lukang, the Taipei basin, Yilan; farm, shop and factory lineages differ.
- **Hakka:** the northern belt (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli), southern Liudui (Meinong, Pingtung) and the later eastern Hakka; farming histories differ.
- **Mainlander families (外省人):** veterans' villages (眷村) and the retired soldiers (榮民) who built the Central Cross-Island Highway.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Amis (coastal, age-set labor), Tao of Lanyu (ocean round), Bunun (high-mountain millet), Atayal, Seediq and Truku (weaving and hunting), Paiwan and Rukai (chiefly houses), Tsou, Saisiyat, Puyuma, Thao, Kavalan, Sakizaya, Hla'alua and Kanakanavu, each separately.
- **Plains Indigenous (Pingpu) peoples:** Siraya, Makatao, Taokas, Pazeh-Kaxabu and others, many reviving their own practices.
- **Islanders:** Penghu (fishing), Kinmen, and Matsu (Eastern Min speakers).
- **New immigrants (新住民) and migrant workers (移工):** spouses from Vietnam and the Chinese mainland; caregivers, factory hands and fishing crews from Vietnam, the Philippines, Thailand and 印尼 (Yinni).
- **Science-park engineers against farm, fishing and small-shop households:** different shapes of the working day.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for statistics, labor history and literature; mixed for Indigenous seasonal rounds beyond the larger peoples; thin for Pingpu peoples and the outer islands. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for Taiwan proper with Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu noted.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, Japanese (1895 to 1945 records), the Indigenous languages, and Dutch and Spanish for the seventeenth-century record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous ritual knowledge tied to the work year is restricted to members or its season (for example the Saisiyat pasta'ay); colonial forced labor; migrant and distant-water fishing crews' conditions. Labor law goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Get the TSCS work-orientation wave and read its items on the place of work in life.
2. Search DGBAS for 時間運用調查 and 人力運用調查; note the years and unpaid-work items.
3. In the Taiwan Diaries Database, search 作息 and 工人 in a landlord's and a teacher's diary.
4. Look up 拍拚, 骨力 and 歇睏 in the MOE Hokkien dictionary, and the Amis and Bunun words for work in the ILRDF dictionary.
5. Search Airiti and the national theses system (臺灣博碩士論文知識加值系統) for 尾牙 and 換工.
