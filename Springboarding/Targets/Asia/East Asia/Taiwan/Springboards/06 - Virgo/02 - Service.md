## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist lay volunteers** (Tzu Chi, Fo Guang Shan, Dharma Drum) against temple-procession volunteers of popular religion: two forms of religious service.
- **Presbyterian and Catholic congregations in Indigenous villages:** church service roles beside customary duties.
- **Paiwan and Rukai** (chiefly houses), **Amis** (age grades serving elders) and **Atayal and Seediq** (gaga obligations): different structures for serving the community.
- **Hakka villages:** ancestral-hall and lineage duties.
- **Mainlander military families:** state service as a family tradition.
- **Employers of live-in caregivers** against the caregivers themselves (Vietnamese, Filipino, 印尼 Yinni nationals): served and serving inside one home; workers as comparison.
- **Night-market and convenience-store workers** against hotel and airline staff: informal against trained service.
- **Matsu and Kinmen:** long garrison history shaping military service and local trades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for religious volunteering and migrant care work; mixed for customer service; thin for Indigenous service obligations outside Paiwan and Rukai.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, Japanese, Paiwan, Rukai, Amis, and the migrants' languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime service under Japan; abuse of live-in workers; bond servitude in family memory. The rules of military service and migrant contracts go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
