# Taiwan: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages; Japanese for the colonial-era vocabulary; classical Chinese for craft texts. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Students in Taipei's star schools** against students in rural and Indigenous townships: different exam stakes and supports.
- **Semiconductor workforce (Hsinchu, Tainan, Taichung)** against **traditional craft families** of Lukang, Tainan and Sanyi (Hakka woodcarving town): industrial against hand standards.
- **Temple artisans (Hoklo lineages)** against **Indigenous makers:** Paiwan beads, Atayal and Seediq weaving, Tao boats, each with its own sense of correct form.
- **Older people who use 日本精神 as praise** against younger generations: a generational and political divide to examine.
- **Brides and bridal-photo studios** against couples who refuse the full package.
- **Military academies and basic training:** inspection culture, often linked with Mainlander military families.
- **New immigrant spouses:** the standards placed on them in household and child-rearing, as comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for academic pressure, quality management and bridal industry; mixed for craft standards; thin for Indigenous makers' own accounts of correct form and allowed variation.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, Japanese, Paiwan, Atayal, classical Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** youth suicide and eating disorders (handle with care; no statistics in the sheet); the "Japanese spirit" phrase is politically charged; some Indigenous patterns carry rights restricted to certain houses or families.
- **Held for review:** none.
