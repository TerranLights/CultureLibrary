# Taiwan: The domain, partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, the Formosan (Indigenous) languages; Japanese and Classical Chinese for older records. **Concept in one line:** partnership and justice: what a fair bargain and a good partnership are, across marriage, contracts, rivals, dispute settlement, civility, marriage ways and gender ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo (Holo) majority:** split Quanzhou-descended from Zhangzhou-descended towns, since betrothal customs and dialect differ.
- **Hakka:** northern (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli), southern Liudui (Pingtung, Meinong), eastern (Hualien, Taitung), and Hokkien-speaking Hakka descendants in Changhua and Yunlin.
- **Mainlander families and former military villages (眷村):** different kin networks and marriage histories after 1949.
- **The 16 recognized Indigenous peoples:** descent and marriage rules diverge sharply (Amis and Puyuma house-centered residence, Paiwan and Rukai ranked houses, Atayal, Seediq and Truku *gaga/gaya*, Tao island society).
- **Pingpu (plains) peoples:** Siraya, Taivoan, Makatao, Kaxabu and others, with records mostly in outsiders' hands.
- **New immigrants:** marriage migrants (Vietnamese, mainland Chinese, Indonesian) and migrant workers, who meet the domain through cross-border marriage and labor contracts.
- **Island and religious groups:** Penghu; the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan; Buddhist and I-Kuan Tao networks.
- **Taiwanese communities in North America and Japan:** comparison only, for marriage and gender ways carried overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, statistics and Han family studies; mixed for Indigenous peoples (colonial ethnography heavy, their own publications growing); thin for Pingpu peoples and migrant workers.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien and Hakka (romanizations and Han characters); Formosan languages; Japanese and Classical Chinese for archives; Dutch for the VOC era.
- **Scope:** Taiwan's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sign's sheets are written for the main island and its nearby islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous customary law and ceremonies (Saisiyat *pasta'ay*, Atayal *gaga*) belong to their peoples; authoritarian-era files carry personal data; same-sex-marriage and cross-strait questions are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
