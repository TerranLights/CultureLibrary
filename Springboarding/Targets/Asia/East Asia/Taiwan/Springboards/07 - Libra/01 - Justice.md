*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families by origin (Hoklo, Hakka, Mainlander):** memory of the 228 Incident and the White Terror may differ by family history; test it, never assume it.
- **Hakka of the north:** the Yimin (義民) cult at Xinpu honors militia of the 1786 uprising, a contested memory of righteous force.
- **Seediq, Truku and Atayal:** answers to a breach of *gaya/gaga* versus state courts.
- **Bunun, Paiwan and other hunting peoples:** hunting prosecutions are the main live meeting of the two orders.
- **Pingpu peoples:** historical-justice claims without full recognition.
- **Kinmen and Matsu:** military administration lasted into the 1990s, a separate experience of rule.
- **Migrant workers:** labor complaints and workers who leave their employers.
- **Presbyterian Church in Taiwan:** its human-rights statements of the 1970s.
- **Victims' families and death-penalty opponents.**
- **Overseas activists of the martial-law era:** comparison only, for redress pursued from outside the island.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; judgments, constitutional rulings and NGO case files are online, and redress has a large literature.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin for law and NGOs; Hokkien for opera and temple texts; Japanese for colonial court records; Seediq, Atayal, Bunun terms for customary answers to wrongs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims' and accused persons' identities; White Terror files under archive rules; Seediq memory of 1930 belongs to descendant communities.
- **Held for review:** none.
