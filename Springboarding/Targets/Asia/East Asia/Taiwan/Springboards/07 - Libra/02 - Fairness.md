## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daughters and sons in Hoklo and Hakka families:** inheritance and ancestral-estate membership, where law and custom may part.
- **Bunun hunters and Tao boat crews:** rules for dividing meat and catch, each as the people describes it.
- **Amis age-set villages:** labor and food shares organized by age grade.
- **Public-sector retirees and private-sector workers:** opposite sides of the pension argument.
- **Rural, east-coast and outlying-island students (Penghu, Taitung, Lanyu):** admission routes built around them.
- **Migrant caregivers and factory workers:** different pay rules from locals.
- **Young renters and older property owners:** housing as a fairness question between generations.
- **Taiwanese families abroad:** comparison only, for inheritance shares divided under other legal systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, admissions and pension debates; mixed for inheritance practice (filings exist, motives need interviews); thin for Indigenous sharing rules outside each people's own publications.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien and Hakka for family inheritance talk; Bunun, Tao and Amis for sharing rules; Classical Chinese for deeds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family inheritance disputes are private; hunting-share rules may carry restricted knowledge.
- **Held for review:** none.
