# Taiwan: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan languages; Classical Chinese for the classics. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Keelung's surname associations:** a rotating, contractual form of shared obligation.
- **Northern Hakka villages:** the Yimin festival rotates its hosting among villages around Xinpu.
- **Amis villages:** labor exchange by turns and age grade.
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** tribute to chiefly houses and what returns from them.
- **Tao of Lanyu:** shares given at boat and house launchings, as the Tao describe them.
- **Urban apartment dwellers and rural villages:** gift-ledger customs may thin out in cities; test it.
- **Families employing migrant caregivers:** care for elders bought rather than returned in kind.
- **Marriage migrants and migrant workers:** remittance obligations.
- **Buddhist, Christian and I-Kuan Tao givers:** different ideas of the gift that asks nothing.
- **Taiwanese abroad:** comparison only, for red-envelope and gift-ledger customs kept or dropped overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 人情 psychology, temple life and aging surveys; mixed for household gift ledgers (private, need interviews); thin for Indigenous exchange in its own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for temple and banquet life; Hakka for village festivals; Amis, Paiwan and Tao.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gift ledgers and red-envelope amounts are private family records; chiefly tribute among Paiwan and Rukai is the houses' own matter.
- **Held for review:** none.
