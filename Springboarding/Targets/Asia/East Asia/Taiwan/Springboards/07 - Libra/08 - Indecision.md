# Taiwan: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan languages; Classical Chinese for idioms. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of patients:** Hoklo, Hakka and Mainlander households may differ in who signs medical decisions; test it.
- **Indigenous tribal meetings:** Paiwan, Atayal, Amis and Tao communities each have consent processes over land, with different speed and voting rules.
- **Atayal, Seediq and Truku:** omen birds and dreams consulted before hunts or journeys, in each people's account.
- **Temple-goers and secular households:** decisions handed to deities or almanacs, or not.
- **Christians:** prayer and church counsel as their own way of deciding.
- **Students and young workers:** university and job choices, and slang of choice fatigue.
- **Voters by camp and the unaligned:** the late deciders.
- **Taipei and the rest of the island:** speed of public decisions on housing and transport.
- **Emigrants and returnees:** comparison only, for how the choice to leave or come back is weighed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for election and referendum data, the nuclear-plant record and medical-decision law; mixed for everyday indecision (slang, forums, a few psychology studies); thin for Indigenous decision customs outside community sources.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for temple practice; Hakka; Atayal and Paiwan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the status-quo series sits inside the sovereignty question, so read it with care and never as a verdict on a people; end-of-life records are private; omen practices may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
