# Taiwan: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan languages; Japanese for colonial-era politics; Vietnamese and Bahasa Indonesia for migrants' testimony. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women in Hoklo, Hakka, Mainlander and Indigenous families:** reporting of abuse may differ by family form and place; test it.
- **Marriage migrants:** residence tied to a spouse can make silence costly to break.
- **Migrant workers:** broker debts and fear of losing the job.
- **Hakka:** the stiff-necked ideal as a self-description, set against harmony sayings.
- **Families of 228 and White Terror victims:** silence across generations.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses and other conscientious objectors:** refusal rather than accommodation.
- **Atayal and other peoples with reconciliation rites.**
- **Young and older cohorts:** the #MeToo wave as a generational test.
- **LGBTQ people within their families:** silence kept to keep the household calm.
- **Taiwanese abroad:** comparison only, for silence and speaking out about family matters at a distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for violence statistics, mediation and the 2023 testimonies; mixed for workplace silence; thin for Indigenous reconciliation in its own voice.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for opera and proverbs; Hakka; Atayal; Vietnamese and Bahasa Indonesia for migrants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' identities; sexual-assault cases; cross-strait policy talk is charged and must be read in each side's words; Atayal rite details belong to the community.
- **Held for review:** none.
