# Taiwan: Domain and folkways, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (華語, Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (台語, Tâi-lô/POJ), Hakka (客語), the Formosan Indigenous languages; Japanese for colonial-era records. **Concept in one line:** how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden: death ways, sex ways, shared resources, inheritance and secrets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo (Hokkien) north and south:** Taipei-Keelung and Tainan-Lukang rites differ in scale and in the role of Taoist priests.
- **Hakka of the northwest (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli) and Liudui (六堆, Pingtung-Kaohsiung):** separate reburial, grave and lineage customs.
- **Mainlanders (外省人) and military-village (眷村) families:** graves far from ancestral places; veterans who died without kin.
- **The sixteen recognized Indigenous peoples,** taken one by one: Amis, Atayal, Paiwan, Bunun, Puyuma, Rukai, Tsou, Saisiyat, Tao, Thao, Kavalan, Truku, Sakizaya, Seediq, Hla'alua, Kanakanavu; death, rank and inheritance differ sharply among them.
- **Pingpu (平埔) peoples,** Siraya above all: revived ancestral rites outside official recognition.
- **Outlying islands:** Kinmen, Matsu (Eastern Min speakers), Penghu and Lanyu, with their own grave and festival customs.
- **New immigrants (新住民)** from Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines and Thailand, and migrant workers: whose rites go home and whose stay.
- **Religious lines:** folk-religion households, Buddhist movements, Christians, I-Kuan Tao and Muslims: each buries and mourns by its own book.
- **Comparison only:** the Taiwanese diaspora in North America and Japan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for Han death ways, ritual and transitional justice; mixed for household money and inheritance (survey-based); uneven for the smaller Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien and Hakka for ritual terms; Japanese for colonial sources; Indigenous languages through their own dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous ancestral rites and some ritual songs are restricted by their keepers; White Terror files name victims and informers; sexual-violence material needs care.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for Taiwan proper and its outlying islands.
- **Law:** inheritance and marriage law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
