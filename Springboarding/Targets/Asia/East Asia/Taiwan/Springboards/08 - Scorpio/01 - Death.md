# Taiwan: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Folk-religion Hoklo households:** the line between ancestor and ghost governs grief.
- **Hakka families:** reburial customs change the length and shape of mourning.
- **Buddhist lay movements (Tzu Chi, Fo Guang Shan, Dharma Drum):** chanting for the dying and body donation.
- **Christian households, Presbyterian and Catholic, many of them Indigenous:** funerals without ancestral tablets.
- **Tao of Lanyu:** strong avoidance of the dead.
- **Bunun, Paiwan, Atayal, Seediq and Truku:** where the soul goes differs by people, and so does the path across.
- **Puyuma:** whether the year-end hunting rite (mangayaw *check*) closes the bereaved families' mourning; a communal end to grief to test.
- **Mainlander veterans and their children:** graves far from home and kinless deaths.
- **Southeast Asian spouses and migrant workers:** deaths far from home and bodies repatriated.
- **Urban and rural elderly:** dying in hospital in Taipei against at home in the countryside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on hospice, euthanasia debate and folk ghost lore; mixed on grief itself; thin for the smaller Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for village sources; Indigenous languages for terms of the soul.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide (follow the center's media guidelines); 嬰靈 touches abortion and guilt; Indigenous death rites are not to be pried into.
- **Held for review:** none.
