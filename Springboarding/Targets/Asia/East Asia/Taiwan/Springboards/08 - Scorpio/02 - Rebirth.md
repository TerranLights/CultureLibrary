# Taiwan: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain Indigenous peoples with mass Christian conversion** (Truku, Atayal, Bunun, Paiwan): conversion is a group memory as well as a personal one.
- **Amis and Puyuma:** age-grade and men's-house initiation as rebirth into adulthood.
- **Pingpu peoples (Siraya, Taivoan, Makatao, Kavalan):** rebirth as recovered names and revived ceremonies.
- **Tainan and other old ports:** the sixteen-year rite.
- **Buddhist lay communities:** rebirth as a doctrine of many lives, not one turn.
- **Evangelical and charismatic churches in cities:** sudden conversion and testimony.
- **Released prisoners and people in drug recovery:** redemption weighed by employers and kin.
- **Central Taiwan communities hit in 1999** and **southern villages hit by Typhoon Morakot (2009):** rebuilt and relocated towns.
- **Comparison only:** Taiwanese American churches, where many first-generation converts came to faith.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on rites of passage, church histories and revival movements; mixed on recovery outcomes; thin on reincarnation belief beyond survey items.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for temple rites; Indigenous languages, and Dutch-era Siraya texts for revival.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion histories touch loss of earlier rites; ask the people concerned before reading revival as success or failure; tattoo revival belongs to its own communities.
- **Held for review:** none.
