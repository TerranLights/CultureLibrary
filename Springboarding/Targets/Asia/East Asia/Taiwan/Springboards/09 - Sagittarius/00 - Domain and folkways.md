## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo south and north:** Tainan and Lukang temple towns against Taipei, for temple age and network.
- **Hakka:** Taoyuan-Hsinchu-Miaoli and the Liudui villages of Pingtung, where the earth god and the Yimin cult give a separate religious map.
- **Mainlander families and veterans' villages (眷村):** the 1949 arrivals brought other patron gods, sects and schools of thought.
- **The sixteen recognized Indigenous peoples,** compared people by people (Amis, Atayal, Paiwan, Bunun, Truku, Tao and the smaller ones), since church affiliation and ancestral practice differ.
- **Pingpu (plains) peoples** such as Siraya, Kavalan and Pazeh, whose revival movements restate older practice.
- **The east coast (Hualien, Taitung)** against the west, for later settlement.
- **Outlying islands, if carried:** Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu, each with its own temple, lineage and migration history.
- **Newer residents:** Southeast Asian spouses and migrant workers, with their mosques, churches and temples.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Han temple religion, Buddhism, Christianity and surveys; mixed for Indigenous practice (much written by outsiders); thin for small immigrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Taiwanese Hokkien in romanization; Hakka; Formosan languages; Japanese; seventeenth-century Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous ceremonies (for example the Saisiyat paSta'ay) carry taboos on recording and outsider conduct; the peoples' own accounts come first. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled by the author, so the outlying islands are included here only as candidates. Law-related questions go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
