# Taiwan: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien (including Kinmen speech), Hakka, Formosan Indigenous languages, Japanese (colonial tourism), Matsu's Eastern Min. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kinmen families** with Southeast Asian emigration histories, and **Matsu** islanders with Fuzhou ties.
- **Penghu islanders** who moved to Kaohsiung for work.
- **Hakka** who resettled in the east.
- **Pingpu peoples** whose migrations are part of their revival.
- **Tao and Amis,** for sea journeys and their own origin accounts.
- **Mainlander families and veterans** of the 1949 retreat.
- **Rural young people** moving to Taipei, against those who return home (返鄉青年).
- **Southeast Asian migrant workers and spouses** as travelers into Taiwan.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese businesspeople in mainland China and Taiwanese Americans, whose journeys can be set beside the home findings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pilgrimage, tourism statistics and exile literature; mixed for small-island emigration; Indigenous migration accounts should come from the peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Taiwanese and Kinmen Hokkien; Hakka; Matsu's Eastern Min; Tao, Amis and Pingpu revival languages; Japanese; Southeast Asian languages for migrant writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cross-strait travel is politically charged; migrant workers' conditions and runaways need care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read 《裨海紀遊》 and list the places and peoples it describes.
2. Search the Baishatun temple's site and press for route maps and pilgrims' accounts.
3. Search news archives for 開放出國觀光 1979 and 開放大陸探親 1987.
4. Search Kinmen County cultural bureau publications for 落番 and 僑批.
5. Collect the migrant literature award's winning entries.
