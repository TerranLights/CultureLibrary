# Taiwan: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (華語, traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (臺灣台語, Tâi-gí, in Tâi-lô or POJ romanization), Hakka (客語), and the languages of the sixteen recognized Indigenous peoples; Japanese for colonial-era sources. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty, with career, status, reputation, age, time, rank, order and power as its areas: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo (Minnan) north and south:** Taipei's civil-service and corporate world against the temple towns of Tainan, Lukang and the southwest, where local authority may run through other channels.
- **Hakka:** the northern belt (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli) and the Liudui settlements of Pingtung and Kaohsiung; separate histories of militia service and lineage organization.
- **Mainlanders (外省人) and military-dependent villages (眷村):** a community formed by the state and the army.
- **Indigenous peoples, by social form:** ranked houses (Paiwan, Rukai), age-set societies (Amis, Puyuma), the gaga/gaya norms of the Atayal, Truku and Sediq, and the Tao of Orchid Island; each needs its own reading.
- **Plains Indigenous peoples (Siraya, Kavalan, Ketagalan and others):** histories of erased and reclaimed standing.
- **Penghu and the other outlying islands:** fishing-village authority and emigration patterns apart from the main island.
- **New immigrants (新住民) and Southeast Asian migrant workers:** spouses and contract laborers who meet Taiwanese authority from below.
- **Generations:** those schooled under martial law against those schooled after 1987 and after the 2014 student movement.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese Americans and Taiwanese in Japan, to see which of the domain's habits travel with emigrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for the Han majority; mixed for Hakka; thin for several smaller Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin first; Hokkien and Hakka for folk sources; Japanese for 1895-1945 records; Indigenous languages with community partners.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law and White Terror records; cross-Strait identity labels; some Indigenous ritual knowledge (Saisiyat pas-ta'ai, for example) is not for outsiders to publish.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (for example, which outlying islands are carried); the sheets are written for the main island and Penghu as core lands.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. In SRDA, search the TSCS module list for 家庭, 工作, 階層 and 政治文化 and note the years of each module.
2. In the 臺灣文獻叢刊 full text, search 諸羅縣志 and 鳳山縣志 for 風俗 and read the sections on elders, servants and festivals.
3. In the MOE Hokkien dictionary, look up 頭家, 序大 and 本分 and list their example sentences.
4. In the National Central Library's periodicals index, search 本土心理學 權威 and 家長式 for the core Taiwanese scholarship.
5. Search Tainan city-government pages for 做十六歲 開隆宮 for the rite's documented history.
