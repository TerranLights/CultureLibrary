# Taiwan: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages; Japanese for 1895-1945. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Martial-law cohorts against post-1987 cohorts:** schooling under a party-state and growing up after it may give different answers on when to obey.
- **Mainlander and juancun families:** ties to the army and the old state, and how those ties changed after 2000.
- **Hoklo temple communities in the southwest:** authority voiced through mediums, temple committees and gods' processions.
- **Hakka districts:** local authority through lineage and militia-temple traditions; worth testing against Hoklo neighbors.
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** hereditary chiefly houses living alongside elected township and village offices.
- **Atayal, Truku and Sediq:** authority tied to ancestral norms and elders rather than birth rank, *check*.
- **Amis and Puyuma:** age-set leaders and men's-house authority.
- **Tao (Orchid Island):** a community's resistance to state projects, notably the nuclear-waste site.
- **Christian minorities:** Presbyterian and Catholic churches, strong in several Indigenous communities.
- **New immigrants and migrant workers:** employer and broker authority over contract workers.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese Americans active in overseas opposition during martial law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich; good survey series, strong management and political-science literatures, and a fresh transitional-justice record.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for temple and folk material; Japanese for colonial police and administration records; Indigenous languages for tribal-assembly practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political-victim files carry privacy limits; Indigenous chiefly matters may be contested between houses; cross-Strait politics colors many sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
