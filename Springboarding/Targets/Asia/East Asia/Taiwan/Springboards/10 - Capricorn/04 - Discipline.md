*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages; Japanese for colonial schooling. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban middle-class parents against rural and working-class parents:** cram schools and tutoring against farm and family-shop labor as the training ground.
- **Students of the hair-rule era against post-2005 students:** a clear before-and-after line for school discipline.
- **Conscript cohorts:** two-year, four-month and one-year service men, each with a different experience of the army.
- **Buddhist, I-Kuan Tao and folk-temple practitioners:** distinct forms of self-denial.
- **Puyuma, Amis and Paiwan:** youth training through men's houses or age sets, each as the people describe it.
- **Bunun and other Indigenous athletes:** sports as a path, and the coaching styles they met.
- **Hakka households:** whether upbringing and schooling routines in Hakka districts differ from those of Hoklo neighbors; to be tested, not assumed.
- **Mainlander military families:** army-style household routines, *check*.
- **Factory workers and migrant workers:** dormitory rules and production quotas.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese emigrant parents and their children's school systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for schools and the army (policy records, NGO surveys, a large literature); mixed for household discipline; thin and community-held for Indigenous training rites.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for folk troupes and home speech; Japanese for colonial schools; Puyuma and other Indigenous languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child abuse and military deaths; Indigenous rites with parts closed to outsiders or to women; rehabilitation records.
- **Held for review:** none.
