# Taiwan: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Owners of small and medium firms against salaried tech workers:** two separate ladders, one through ownership and one through firms like TSMC.
- **Exam-era and post-2001 students:** different ways of measuring a young person's worth.
- **Young adults after the 2008 wage stagnation:** whether the 小確幸 debate marks a real change, *check*.
- **Hoklo merchant towns and Hakka farming districts:** different family strategies for rising, to be tested.
- **Mainlander families:** civil service and the military as traditional ladders, and how their weight changed.
- **Indigenous youth:** sports, music, the army and city labor as routes; each people's own view of leaving the village.
- **Women:** ambition at work against family expectations, and change by cohort.
- **New immigrants and their children:** striving as newcomers, and how schools receive them.
- **Penghu and other outlying islands:** whether rising has meant moving to Kaohsiung or Taipei, and how those who stay describe their own goals.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese emigrants to North America after the 1960s, many through graduate study.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich; exam, wage and mobility data are good, and the debate about youth ambition is loud in the press.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for songs and small-business talk; Hakka for Hakka literature; Indigenous languages for village views of leaving.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** affirmative-action debates can be heated; generational labels (草莓族) are contested and should be cited as labels, not findings.
- **Held for review:** none.
