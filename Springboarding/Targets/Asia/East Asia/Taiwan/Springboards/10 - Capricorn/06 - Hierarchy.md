# Taiwan: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages; Japanese for colonial ethnography. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paiwan and Rukai:** inherited ranks of chiefly and commoner houses, as each people describes them.
- **Amis, Puyuma and Tao:** age-based or more level orders; worth setting against the ranked peoples.
- **Plains Indigenous peoples:** a history of being placed below both Han and recognized mountain peoples.
- **Mainlanders against Taiwan-born families:** a reversal of standing over the decades after 1987, *check*.
- **Hoklo and Hakka:** the relative standing of the two in the past and present, to be tested.
- **Taipei against the south and east:** the 重北輕南 complaint, and what Hualien and Taitung add.
- **Employers and migrant domestic workers:** rank inside one household.
- **Seniors and juniors in the army, school sports and universities:** rank by year of entry, and where it turns into hazing.
- **New-immigrant spouses:** standing within husbands' families.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese American professionals and their own status ladders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for class, income and ethnic categories; mixed for workplace rank; Indigenous ranked systems are well studied but their present meaning is each people's to state.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; Hokkien for class words; Japanese for colonial ethnography; Paiwan and Rukai for rank terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and provincial labels remain politically charged; Indigenous rank disputes between houses; migrant workers' conditions.
- **Held for review:** none.
