# Taiwan: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, Indigenous languages; Japanese and classical Chinese for older records. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law only; the letter belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo temple communities:** oaths before gods and City God processions as a parallel justice.
- **Hakka districts:** mediation through lineage and temple leaders, to be tested.
- **Mainlander and military families:** military courts and their reform after 2013.
- **Atayal, Truku and Sediq:** gaga and gaya as living norms beside state law.
- **Bunun and Paiwan hunters and chiefly houses:** clashes over hunting, land and forest rules.
- **Tao:** state law and the island's own customs over land and sea.
- **Plains Indigenous peoples:** the fight for legal recognition itself.
- **Migrant fishermen and workers:** law as met by people with few rights and many brokers.
- **Generations:** those who lived under martial law and those who know only the democracy.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese businesspeople navigating other legal systems.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich; exceptional archives for the Qing and colonial eras, strong legal history and active reform debate.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin; classical Chinese for Qing records; Japanese for colonial law; Hokkien for oaths and proverbs; Indigenous languages for customary norms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** death-penalty and wrongful-conviction cases; some Indigenous customary rules are not for outsiders to set down; gang codes need care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the 淡新檔案 database for 調處 and 稟 to see how petitioners framed their claims.
2. Find 王泰升's survey of Taiwanese legal history and list its chapters on legal consciousness.
3. Pull Ministry of Justice yearly mediation figures for township committees and compare them with civil filings.
4. Search news archives for 斬雞頭 發誓 to collect cases where politicians or litigants swore before gods.
5. Search Judicial Yuan pages for 國民法官 評議 for early evaluations of lay-judge trials.
