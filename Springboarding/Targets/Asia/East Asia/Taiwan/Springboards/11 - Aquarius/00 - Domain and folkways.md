# Taiwan: The domain, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi, Tâi-lô romanization), Hakka (Sixian and Hailu), the Austronesian languages of the recognized Indigenous peoples. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for: friends, groups, associations, humanitarian ideals, and the customs of settling, gathering and joining.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo (閩南) Han:** separate the old Quanzhou- and Zhangzhou-origin settlements (Lukang, Tainan, the Yilan plain), whose temple alliances differ.
- **Hakka:** the northern belt (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli) against the southern Liudui (六堆) of Kaohsiung and Pingtung, named for a Qing-era militia alliance *check*.
- **Postwar mainlanders (外省人) and the military dependents' villages (眷村):** a different settlement history.
- **Indigenous peoples:** not only Amis and Atayal; the Tao of Lanyu (boat-crew groups *check*), Puyuma and Tsou (men's houses), Paiwan and Rukai (ranked houses), Saisiyat, Thao, Kavalan, Truku, Seediq, Sakizaya, Bunun, Hla'alua, Kanakanavu, and urban Indigenous neighborhoods.
- **Plains Indigenous (平埔) peoples, e.g. Siraya and Kaxabu:** revival associations with their own stakes.
- **New immigrants (新住民) and migrant workers:** Vietnamese, Indonesian, Filipino and Thai communities.
- **Penghu islanders; the east coast (Hualien, Taitung) against the western plain; Taipei against small towns.**
- **Religious communities:** folk-temple, Buddhist, Presbyterian and Catholic congregations.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese associations abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich (surveys, ethnography, a large civic press); thinner for smaller Indigenous peoples and recent immigrants.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for Taiwan's core lands.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin (Traditional), Taigi, Hakka, Japanese (colonial records), Indigenous languages; Vietnamese and Indonesian for immigrant media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial and Qing records describe Indigenous peoples in contemptuous terms; read them critically and put each people's own account first. Some Indigenous rites (e.g. the Saisiyat pasta'ay) have restricted parts. Cross-strait identity labels are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
