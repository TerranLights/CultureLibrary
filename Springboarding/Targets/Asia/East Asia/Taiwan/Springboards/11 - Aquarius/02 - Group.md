*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo and Hakka:** different histories of militia, lineage trust (祭祀公業) and temple alliance.
- **Mainlander families and the juancun youth gangs of the 1960s-80s:** a group form tied to one settlement type.
- **Indigenous peoples with men's houses (Tsou, Puyuma) and age-sets (Amis), against ranked-house peoples (Paiwan, Rukai) and the Atayal and Seediq gaga groups.**
- **Kavalan, Thao and Saisiyat:** small peoples whose group lines may sit differently.
- **Blue and green partisans,** and younger voters who reject both labels.
- **Migrant workers in factory dormitories,** grouped by employer and nationality.
- **Students:** school clubs (社團), senior-junior hierarchies (學長姐) and class units.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on factions, unions, identity polling and indigenous psychology; mixed on gangs (mostly journalism); thin on migrant workers' own groups.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Taigi (troupe and gang slang), Hakka, Japanese (colonial police records), Tsou, Puyuma, Paiwan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gang research carries risk; prefer published sources. Identity labels across the strait are charged; report each camp in its own words. Men's-house rites may bar women or outsiders from parts; respect each people's rules. Collective-liability history touches the law: point to Capricorn for its spirit.
- **Held for review:** none.
