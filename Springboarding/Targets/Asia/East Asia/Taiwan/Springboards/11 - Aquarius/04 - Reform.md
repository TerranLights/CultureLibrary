# Taiwan: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin, Taigi, Hakka, Japanese (colonial era), Tao and other Indigenous languages. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Colonial-era reformers:** the gentry wing (Wufeng Lin family) against labor and farmers' unions (台灣農民組合); different routes to change.
- **Mainlander liberals and Taiwanese dangwai:** two reform lineages that met and quarreled.
- **Hakka farmers (Meinong) and Hoklo townspeople (Lukang):** local reform from below.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Tao on Lanyu, Atayal and Truku land claimants, Amis urban activists; reforms done to them and by them, each in its own voice.
- **Religious reformers:** the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan; Buddhist "humanistic" movements.
- **Teachers, parents and students** in education reform; **farmers** in land and rural reform.
- **Migrant workers' advocates,** a newer reform front.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1920s and 1980s-90s movements; good archives; mixed for everyday personal reform.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Japanese (colonial records and the Minpao's Japanese pages), Taigi (speeches, songs), Hakka, Tao, Atayal.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law victims' files and family records; the 228 and White Terror histories remain politically divided. Land reform's winners and losers tell it differently. Reforms of law and constitution belong to Libra; only movements and programs here.
- **Held for review:** none.
