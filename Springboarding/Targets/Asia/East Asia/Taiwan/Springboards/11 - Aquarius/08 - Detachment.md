# Taiwan: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional), Classical Chinese, Taigi (Tâi-lô), Hakka, Tao and other Indigenous languages; caregivers' languages (Tagalog, Indonesian, Vietnamese). **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings or from outcomes, in forms that may be praised (calm, objectivity, equanimity) or blamed (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buddhist lay practitioners against folk-religion households:** letting go taught in one, loud public mourning expected in the other.
- **Northern and southern Hoklo funerals:** expected display may differ by county; test it.
- **Hakka families (Miaoli, Meinong):** their own mourning customs and words for taking things lightly.
- **Tao of Lanyu:** distance from the dead in their own terms; compare Amis, Bunun and Paiwan mourning, each separately.
- **Mainlander veterans in veterans' homes (榮民之家):** distance from kin imposed by history.
- **Men and women:** the composure asked of conscripts and of carers; ask, don't assume.
- **Generations:** elders who lived through martial-law silence against young "edge people" online.
- **Migrant caregivers and their employers:** a tie of daily intimacy kept at a set distance.
- **Professions that train distance:** doctors, hospice nurses, journalists, funeral directors (禮儀師).
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** Taiwanese families abroad; the findings for Taiwan apply to them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in religious teaching, literature, film and isolation statistics; thin on measured display rules or ideal affect for Taiwan, and on most Indigenous peoples' own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Classical Chinese, Taigi (funeral and everyday speech), Hakka, Tao; caregivers' languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, lonely deaths and withdrawal need care and current reporting guidelines; Tao death customs are sensitive and may be restricted: follow Tao sources and consent. Buddhist teaching is one voice among many, not how everyone feels. End-of-life law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
