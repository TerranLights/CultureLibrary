# Taiwan: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (Traditional), Classical Chinese, Taigi, Hakka, Paiwan and other Indigenous languages; Vietnamese, Indonesian and Tagalog for migrants' writing. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young adults on low starting pay against parents of the boom years:** different horizons; test, not assume.
- **Hsinchu science-park households against southern farming and fishing towns:** hope tied to different futures.
- **Mainlander veterans and their children:** a hope of return closed off, then partly met.
- **Hakka of Meinong and the Taoyuan-Miaoli belt:** ancestral longing against staying put.
- **Indigenous peoples:** Paiwan rites for the coming years; Amis in city construction work; Bunun and Truku hopes for language and land, each in its own voice.
- **New immigrant mothers and their children (新二代):** hopes placed in schooling.
- **Migrant workers:** hope carried as savings and remittances home.
- **Temple worshippers, Buddhists and Christians:** where hope is placed, and how it is asked for.
- **Political camps:** whether hope rises and falls with who governs.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** students and families who emigrated from the 1960s on, for what drew them; the nation's findings cover them too.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: polls, literature, song, temple life and movement records; thinner on Indigenous peoples' own words for hope and on migrant workers.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Classical Chinese, Taigi (songs, temples), Hakka, Paiwan and other Indigenous languages, Japanese (colonial-era songs), Southeast Asian languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parts of the maljeveq and other rites may be closed to outsiders; follow each people's rules. Suicide and despair need care. Hopes for the island's political future are deeply divided; give each camp its own sources.
- **Held for review:** none.
