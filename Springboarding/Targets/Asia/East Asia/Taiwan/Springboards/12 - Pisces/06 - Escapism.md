# Taiwan: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Tâi-lô), Hakka, the Indigenous Formosan languages, Japanese (colonial records). **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Students and young workers** (the "world-weary" generation) against **retirees** whose escapes run through temples, mahjong and KTV.
- **Manual workers, drivers and fishermen,** among whom betel nut and drink as work aids may differ from office workers.
- **Indigenous peoples,** separately and in their own terms: ceremonial betel and millet wine are not escape; drinking is a stereotype-laden topic, so start from community health programs and churches.
- **Hakka towns** and **Hoklo towns,** for gambling and temple-lottery links.
- **Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu** (if inside the nation's lands), which voted on casinos.
- **Migrant workers** on their day off, and **conscripts** in their camps.
- **Gamers and fandoms** against the rural young with fewer outlets.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and media; mixed for qualitative accounts of why people escape.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, Japanese; Indigenous languages for community programs; migrant workers' languages for their own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction and Indigenous drinking stereotypes; illegal gambling and drugs (law to Libra and Capricorn); suicide linked to debt.
- **Held for review:** none.
