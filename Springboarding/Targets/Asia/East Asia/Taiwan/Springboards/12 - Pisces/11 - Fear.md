# Taiwan: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Taiwan, region East Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Mandarin (traditional characters), Taiwanese Hokkien (Tâi-lô), Hakka, Amis and other Indigenous Formosan languages, classical Chinese, Japanese. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hoklo coastal fishing towns** (plague-god rites) against **inland farming towns** and **northern cities** (ghost lore and apartment fears).
- **Hakka districts,** with their own fright cures and bogey tales.
- **Indigenous peoples,** separately: Amis legends, Tao fear of the dead, Saisiyat rites for the ta'ay, mountain peoples' dangers of forest and hunt.
- **Survivors and families of the White Terror** against those with no such memory.
- **Earthquake zones** (central Taiwan after 1999; Hualien after recent quakes) against less shaken areas.
- **Generations:** children raised on drills and horror films; elders who lived through air raids and martial law.
- **Kinmen and Matsu,** if inside the nation's lands, with memories of shelling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster, horror and ghost lore; mixed for measured anxiety by region.
- **Languages needed:** Mandarin, Hokkien, Hakka, classical Chinese, Japanese, Indigenous languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous fear-related rites are not for outsiders; political-terror testimony; cross-strait war fear is a live political subject, to be recorded across camps without one voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
