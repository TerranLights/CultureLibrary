# Patani: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic for teaching on renunciation. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malay | *milik*; Jawi مليك | belonging to, owned by | everyday and formal |
| Patani Malay | *punyo* (Malay *punya*); Thai script ปูโญะ | "'s", marker of whose a thing is | Patani form and spelling *check* |
| Malay | *pusaka*; Jawi ڤوساک | heirloom, thing handed down | also inherited land; *check* Patani use |
| Malay | *pinjam* ڤينجم; *curi* چوري | borrow; steal | pair with Thai ยืม, ขโมย |
| Malay | *saka*; Jawi ساک | a spirit "inherited" with a family | the second sense of possession; sensitive |
| Malay | *buah tangan*, *ole-ole* | gifts brought home from travel | Hajj returns especially |

| Arabic / Malay | *zuhud* زهد; *qana'ah* قناعة | renunciation; contentment with little | Jawi *kitab* vocabulary |

English keywords: Patani heirlooms, Malay Muslim material culture Pattani, Patani Qur'an manuscripts, belongings of the dead southern Thailand, Urak Lawoi possessions, pawnshops Pattani, spirit possession Malay healing.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Students in *pondok* huts and students in town dormitories:** check what a student is expected to own, and what is shared among hut neighbors.
- **Fishing households of Yaring and Panare against orchard households of Bannang Sata and Rueso:** check which tools are kept in the family and which are lent.
- **Buddhist Thai families of Tak Bai and Mae Lan:** check what goes with the dead at cremation and what is given to the temple.
- **Thai Chinese families of Pattani town and Betong:** check heirloom keeping across generations that moved between Chinese, Thai and Malay schooling.
- **Malay-speaking Muslims of Saba Yoi and Na Thawi:** check whether *pusaka* objects and their stories survive where Thai has become the home language.
- **Sam-Sam households in Satun and Songkhla:** check which words they use for heirlooms and gifts.
- **The Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe and Ko Adang, and the Kensiw of Than To:** check, in their own accounts, what moves with a household and what is left behind.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's *pusaka* traditions; belongings carried by Patani workers to Malaysia and back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: manuscripts and museum objects are well catalogued; everyday belongings, lending and loss need interviews and household inventories.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic for manuscripts and *zuhud* teaching; Thai for theses and press; Patani Malay for heirloom stories told aloud.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the cannon and museum holdings are tied to disputed history; spirit possession and *saka* are private and sometimes stigmatized; theft reports may name people.
- **Held for review:** objects and keepsakes of South Asian-heritage families in the region's towns (category only).
