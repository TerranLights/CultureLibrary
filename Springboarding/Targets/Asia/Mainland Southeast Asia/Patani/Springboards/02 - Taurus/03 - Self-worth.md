# Patani: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic for the vocabulary of pride and humility. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families with a *babo*, *imam* or hajji in them and families without:** check whether religious titles count for more than income in village standing.
- **Students fluent in Patani Malay only and students fluent in Thai:** check how each describes being judged at school and in town offices.
- **Older men and women in Yaring, Mayo and Rangae:** check how standing shifts with age, widowhood and grandchildren.
- **Returnees from Malaysia and those who never left:** check whether money earned abroad buys standing or questions.
- **Buddhist Thai families of Khok Pho and Sungai Padi:** check what ordination, merit-making and civil-service jobs give a family.
- **Thai Chinese of Yala's market streets and Betong:** check whether association office or business success counts more.
- **Thai-speaking Muslims of Satun and La-ngu, and the Sam-Sam:** check how they speak of standing between Malay and Thai worlds.
- **The Maniq and Kensiw:** check, through their own representatives, how outsiders' names and photographs affect them.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's honor vocabulary; Thai national ideas of face; Patani students in Middle Eastern universities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: theses with self-esteem scales exist, while the vocabulary of *maruah* and *malu* in Patani speech is barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for address terms and shame talk; Arabic and Jawi for *tasawwuf* terms; Thai for psychology theses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dignity and humiliation are central words in the conflict's discourse; quote no party as the region; mental-health data need consent and anonymity.
- **Held for review:** standing and honor among South Asian-heritage Muslim families of the region's towns (category only).
