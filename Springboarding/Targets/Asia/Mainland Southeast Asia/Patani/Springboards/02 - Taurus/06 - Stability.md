# Patani: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Chinese for town histories. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old villages of Yaring and Nong Chik against settlers' villages of the land-settlement schemes:** check how each tells the time since founding and what binds it to the land.
- **Buddhist Thai families with long roots in the region and those who arrived as settlers:** check which call the region home and why.
- **Pattani town's older Chinese families and Betong's twentieth-century arrivals:** check how each marks continuity (shrines, shops, graves).
- ***Pondok* students who move from teacher to teacher:** check whether wandering for learning is read as a stage before settling.
- **Fishing households who beach boats in the monsoon and orchard households with a steady year:** check how each describes a good year's rhythm.
- **Young adults who work in Malaysia for years and those who stay:** check what staying and returning mean to each.
- **Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims and Songkhla's Malay districts:** check how old families there tell continuity across language change.
- **The Urak Lawoi and the Maniq:** check what "settled" means to peoples with mobile histories, through their own accounts.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's village continuity; Thailand's rural-to-urban drift; Patani families settled in Mecca for generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: census and price data are open; continuity of routine and attachment to the home village need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Jawi for local histories; Thai for census, prices and theses; Patani Malay for village founding tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** settlement schemes and population movement are tied to disputed narratives of the region; give each source's framing and never one side's as fact.
- **Held for review:** histories of long-settled South Asian-heritage families in the region's market towns (category only).
