## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Swiftlet-house owners of the towns and fishing families of the same coast:** check how new fortunes are talked about by those who have them and those who do not.
- **Descendants of the old *orang kaya* and noble houses and families of newer wealth:** check whether lineage or money carries more weight at weddings and in mosque affairs.
- **Rubber and durian growers of Yala's hills and rice farmers of the Pattani plain:** check how boom years and bust years changed what households bought.
- **Thai Chinese gold-shop and trading families of Yala and Pattani:** check how wealth is shown or kept quiet at Lunar New Year and in donations.
- **Buddhist Thai households:** check how wealth is turned into merit and temple building.
- **Malay-speaking districts of Songkhla near Hat Yai and Satun's coast:** check how nearness to a commercial city or to tourism shapes ideas of the well-off.
- **The Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe and the Maniq of the Banthat range:** check who gains from tourism and forest trade, and how each people reckons plenty.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's merchant classes; national media framing of the south's poverty; Patani restaurant owners in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for poverty statistics and trade history; thin for how people judge the rich and the stingy, which needs local-language interviews.
- **Languages needed:** classical Chinese for Ming sources; Jawi and Arabic for *kitab*; Thai for statistics and theses; Patani Malay for folk tales and wedding talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** poverty labels can stigmatize the region; present figures by district, not as a people's trait; talk of how a fortune was made can carry accusations against named people; never repeat one as fact.
- **Held for review:** fortunes of South Asian-heritage textile traders in the region's towns (category only).
