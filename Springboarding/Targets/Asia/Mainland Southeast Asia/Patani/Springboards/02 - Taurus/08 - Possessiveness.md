# Patani: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic for ethical terms. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Newly married couples in Sungai Kolok and couples in Narathiwat's interior villages:** check how a border town's mobility changes jealousy talk.
- **Households with co-wives and monogamous households in the same district:** check how each speaks of fairness and guarding, without assuming either.
- **Mothers and grown sons who work away:** check how parents describe holding on to a child who earns abroad.
- **Small-scale fishers of Panare and Saiburi and boat owners from outside the bay:** check how each side states its claim to the water.
- **Orchard families of Yala's hills and seasonal pickers:** check what fruit may be taken freely and what is guarded.
- **Buddhist Thai and Thai Chinese families of Yala city:** check how jealousy and stinginess are named in their speech and stories.
- **The Sam-Sam and Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims:** check which words for jealousy and guarding they use.
- **The Maniq and the Kensiw:** check how sharing and holding back are handled, through each people's own accounts.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's family and gender debates; Thai national campaigns on violence at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed: case data and theses exist, but jealousy and stinginess in daily life need careful interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for songs, tales and household talk; Thai for case data and theses; Arabic and Jawi for *ghirah* and *bakhil* teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gender, polygyny and the guarding of women are easily stereotyped; record each person's own account and never generalize to a community; abuse data need strict privacy.
- **Held for review:** courtship and marriage studies among South Asian-heritage Muslim families of the region (category only).
