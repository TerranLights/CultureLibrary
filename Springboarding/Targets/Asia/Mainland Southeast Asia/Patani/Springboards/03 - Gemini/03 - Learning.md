## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Children who memorize the Qur'an full time and children in ordinary day schools:** check how each household arranges the day around the child's study.
- **Fishing families of Panare and orchard families of Bannang Sata:** check at what age children are trusted with a net or a tapping knife.
- **Adults in Thai literacy classes and adults who read only Jawi:** check what each wants to be able to read.
- **Buddhist Thai boys of Pattani's temples:** check what a short ordination teaches compared with school.
- **Pupils of Chinese descent in Yala and Malay pupils in the same Mandarin class:** check what each family hopes the language will bring.
- **Sam-Sam families and Malay-speaking families of Saba Yoi:** check which language is chosen for a child's first religious lessons.
- **The Maniq and the Urak Lawoi:** check, through their own teachers and elders, what is learned in the forest or at sea that school does not teach.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's *tahfiz* boom; Thai national literacy campaigns; Patani pupils schooled in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: school and religious learning are studied; learning by pitching in and craft transmission sit in a few theses.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Jawi for grammar and recitation texts; Thai for theses and assessments; Patani Malay for interviews with parents and craftsmen; Mandarin for the Chinese programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious schools have been watched as security concerns; describe them through their own records; children's data need consent.
- **Held for review:** home reading circles of a South Asian-founded preaching movement as a route of adult learning (category only).
