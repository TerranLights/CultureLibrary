# Patani: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; the towns' Chinese varieties. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families still in old wooden stilt houses and families who rebuilt in concrete:** check what each says was gained or lost with the new house.
- **Households on the eroding shore of Panare and Saiburi and households of Yala's planned streets:** check how each speaks of a house that may not last.
- **Tenants of public-housing blocks and families on inherited plots:** check whether renting is described as having a home at all.
- **Buddhist Thai households of Khok Pho and Mae Lan:** check which rites mark a new house as home, and who is invited.
- **Chinese families of Yala's ring roads and Betong's hillside lanes:** check where shop ends and home begins inside one building.
- **Malay families of Saba Yoi and Thai-speaking Muslims on Satun's coast:** check whether house forms and threshold customs follow Patani or Southern Thai patterns.
- **The Maniq in state-built houses and the Urak Lawoi beside Ko Lipe's resorts:** check, through their own spokespeople, which place each calls home.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan and Terengganu house forms; houses Patani families own in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: building studies and housing data exist; what makes a house feel like home needs interviews and photographs inside homes.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for house-part words and carpenters' talk; Thai for architecture theses and housing data; early modern English for Hamilton; Chinese for shophouse inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homes searched or burned in the conflict are painful ground; enter homes only with consent; property, tenancy and eviction rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no dwelling or house-building pointer needed setting aside.
