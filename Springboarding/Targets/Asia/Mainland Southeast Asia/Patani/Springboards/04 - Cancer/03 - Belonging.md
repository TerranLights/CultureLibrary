# Patani: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; Chinese; Burmese and Khmer for the port's newcomers. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families on one mosque's register for generations and households who moved into the parish:** check how long before newcomers are asked to serve or to speak.
- **Returnees from Cairo, Medina or Amman and graduates schooled at home:** check what each must show to be accepted back.
- **Myanmar and Cambodian boat crews in Pattani port:** check, through their own accounts and labor groups, where they may gather and pray.
- **Buddhist Thai families rooted in the region for generations:** check whether, and by whom, they are called *khon phuenthi*.
- **Chinese devotees of Yala's pillar shrine and Pattani town's oldest Chinese families:** check what makes a Chinese family "of the town".
- **Malay speakers of Chana and Saba Yoi:** check whether they place themselves with Patani, with Songkhla, or with both.
- **The Sam-Sam and Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims:** check whose gatherings they are invited to, and whose they host.
- **The Maniq and the Urak Lawoi:** check, through their representatives, who decides who belongs to a band or an island village.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's Thai-speaking Buddhists; Patani communities in Malaysia and Mecca.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: political belonging is heavily studied; everyday acceptance of newcomers and returnees is thin and needs interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Portuguese, or the English edition, for Pires; Thai for registers and theses; Patani Malay for district pages; Burmese and Khmer for the port's workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who belongs to which people or nation is the core dispute of the conflict; never state who "really" belongs; citizenship and membership rules go to Libra; migrant workers' status is fragile, so protect their identities.
- **Held for review:** the weekly gatherings of a South Asian-founded preaching movement, as a circle of belonging beside the village mosque (category only).
