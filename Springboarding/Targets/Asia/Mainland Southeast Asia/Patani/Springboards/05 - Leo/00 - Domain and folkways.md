# Patani: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic; Hokkien and Hakka; Urak Lawoi. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure (courtship, children as joy, celebration and display, games of chance, sport and contest, festive dress).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pattani Bay fishing villages and the inland rice plain of Saiburi and Panare:** check whether the year's festive peaks follow the monsoon fishing break or the harvest.
- **Yala's hill orchards and Betong:** check whether durian and longkong seasons bring their own fairs and leisure.
- **Malay-speaking Chana, Thepha and Saba Yoi and the core three provinces:** check whether *dikir hulu*, *rong ngeng* and the Southern Thai *nora* share stages there.
- **Sam-Sam villages of Satun and Songkhla:** check which festive forms they keep, Malay, Thai or both.
- **Buddhist Thai villages around Nong Chik and Tak Bai:** check their temple fairs and merit festivals as their own occasions, apart from the Malay calendar.
- **Chinese households of Yala, Betong and Pattani town:** check which shrine festivals they fund and stage.
- **Maniq bands of the Banthat range and Hala-Bala:** check, through their representatives, what they count as play or celebration.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's performance scene; Thailand's national festival calendar; Patani workers' social life in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: genres and festivals are catalogued in Thai; leisure, courtship and pleasure as lived need Patani Malay fieldwork.
- **Lands:** which lands the post-war nation holds is undecided, so every Leo sheet speaks to the heartland: the three provinces plus the Malay-speaking districts of Songkhla and Satun.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for performance texts and festive talk; Thai for state catalogs; Jawi and Arabic for the music rulings; Chinese for shrine programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether music and stage shows are permitted is a live religious debate, and festivals have been attacked in the conflict; record every side and label no group.
- **Held for review:** popular films and music from South Asia watched in the region (category only), set aside from the leisure pointers.
