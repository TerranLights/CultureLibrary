# Patani: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic; Chinese. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of *ulama* lineages in Pattani and Narathiwat, and families without them:** check what each is proud of when introducing its members.
- **Fishing families of the Pattani Bay coast and rubber and fruit growers of Yala's hills:** check which skills and harvests are spoken of with pride.
- **Malay speakers of Chana and Thepha, and Thai-speaking Muslims of La-ngu:** check whether they speak of pride in being Malay, in being Muslim, or in their district.
- **Sam-Sam communities:** check what they hold up as theirs when neither Malay nor Thai labels fit.
- **Buddhist Thai farming families around Khok Pho and Sai Buri's temple villages:** check whether a son's ordination, a good harvest or a school result is what parents boast of.
- **Chinese families of Betong and Pattani town's old quarter:** check how pride in a family business or school result is shown to kin.
- **The Maniq:** check, only through their representatives, whether they want pride in their way of life spoken of publicly at all.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantanese pride in their state; Patani-descended associations in Malaysia; national pride as measured for Thailand.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: group pride is entangled with the conflict and written about a great deal; personal pride and arrogance in daily life are thin and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for tales and dictionaries; Jawi and Arabic for *takabbur* teaching; Thai for theses, slogans and surveys; Chinese for family and association pride.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pride in a Malay or Patani identity can be read politically; quote nobody without consent and never rank communities by pride; LGBTQ residents' safety comes first.
- **Held for review:** nothing on boasting, humility or group pride was put to one side for this sheet.
