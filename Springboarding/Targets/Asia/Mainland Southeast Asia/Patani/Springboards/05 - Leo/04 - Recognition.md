# Patani: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; classical and modern Chinese. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Graduates of Middle Eastern universities and of Thai universities:** check whose qualifications open which doors locally, and who is called *ustaz*.
- **Old noble lines (Tengku, Nik, Wan) and families of returned pilgrims:** check which kind of standing is acknowledged first at a gathering.
- **Fishers of Saiburi and the Narathiwat coast, and town officials in Yala:** check whose work is thanked in public and whose goes unmentioned.
- **Women religious teachers (*ustazah*) and male *tok guru*:** check how each is credited in school and village notices.
- **Sam-Sam villages and Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims:** check whether a respected elder there is greeted with a Malay title, a Thai one, or both.
- **Buddhist Thai residents of Yala and Narathiwat:** check which local honors (temple, school, state) they seek and receive.
- **The Urak Lawoi and the Maniq:** check what being "recognized" by the state has meant to them, in their own accounts.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's state honors; Patani graduates whose degrees are recognized in Malaysia but not in Thailand.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: formal honors and degree rules are well recorded; everyday credit and thanks need observation in Patani Malay.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay to hear how thanks and titles are spoken; Arabic and Jawi to read *ijazah* chains; Thai for award and degree lists; classical Chinese for the scroll's text.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** acknowledgment of the conflict's victims is contested; handle remedy and apology records with care and name no group as deserving or undeserving.
- **Held for review:** no source on credit, titles or honors had to be withheld under the standing rules.
