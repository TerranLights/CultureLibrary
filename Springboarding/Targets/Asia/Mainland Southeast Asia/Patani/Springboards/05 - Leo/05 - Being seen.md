# Patani: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; English for early photographs and international reports. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young women in Pattani's university district and women in the inland villages of Raman and Bannang Sata:** check who may be seen in public, where, and in whose company.
- **Men of the coastal villages of Panare and the hill villages of Yala:** check how checkpoints and patrols shape where they go and when.
- **Muslims of Satun and the Songkhla districts, outside the core security zone:** check whether being watched is felt differently there.
- **The Sam-Sam:** check whether they feel noticed as a community at all, by Malay or Thai neighbors and by the state.
- **Buddhist Thai residents and monks:** check how they balance visibility and caution.
- **Chinese shop families of Yala and Sungai Kolok:** check how a shop's public front is kept, and when it is shut.
- **The Maniq and the Urak Lawoi:** check, in their own terms, how they deal with visitors' cameras and officials' records.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan, where the same faith is a majority in a federal state; Patani workers in Malaysia, who may be unseen as undocumented labor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: surveillance and disappearance are documented by rights groups; everyday norms of gaze and greeting are thin and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for folk-illness terms and talk about gazes; Thai for rights reports and theses; English for UN and Cambridge records; Arabic and Jawi for the gaze chapters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance, disappearance and the monarchy are sensitive topics; protect informants, quote no one without consent, and attribute claims to their sources.
- **Held for review:** nothing about witness, gaze or visibility was withheld from this sheet.
