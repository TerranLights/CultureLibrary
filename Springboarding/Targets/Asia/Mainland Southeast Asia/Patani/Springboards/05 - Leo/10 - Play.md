*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Children of Pattani town's apartment blocks and of the river villages of Nong Chik:** check where each may play unwatched, and from what age.
- **Boys and girls in Narathiwat's coastal villages:** check which games each may play, and when girls stop playing outdoors.
- **Pupils of *pondok* and of state boarding schools:** check how free time is set and what is played in it.
- **Teenagers of Saba Yoi's Malay-speaking villages and of Satun's Thai-speaking Muslim ones:** check whose comedy clips each shares, and in what language the jokes land.
- **Buddhist Thai children of Yala's temple schools:** check which games come from Thai schooling and which from Malay neighbors.
- **Hakka and Cantonese families of Betong:** check which games pass from grandparents and which are new.
- **The Maniq and the Urak Lawoi:** check, by their own account, which kinds of play adults join and which belong to children.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's kite and top-spinning scene; Patani teenagers in Malaysian towns where their parents work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin: children's and adults' play is little studied apart from listings of folk games; observation and interviews are needed.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for game names, riddles and comedy; Thai for school and press material; Chinese varieties for mahjong rooms; Urak Lawoi for Ko Lipe's games.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** games of chance and some music-and-dance play are disputed on religious grounds; record views without judging; photograph children only with parents' consent.
- **Held for review:** riddles, toys, pitches and jokes all passed the standing rules; nothing on play is held.

## 5. First queries
1. Look for riddle collections under "ปริศนาคำทาย มลายู", then for village toys under "ของเล่นพื้นบ้าน ปัตตานี", in ThaiLIS and the Pattani campus library.
2. Ask the Pattani and Narathiwat museums which *congkak* boards they hold.
3. Collect a month of futsal tournament posters from district Facebook pages.
4. Search YouTube and TikTok for "lawak Patani" and "ตลก มลายู" and list the top channels.
5. Observe one *pondok* and one state school at free time and note what is played.
