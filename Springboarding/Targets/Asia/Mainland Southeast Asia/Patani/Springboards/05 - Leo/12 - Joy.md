# Patani: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic for devotional texts; Chinese; Urak Lawoi. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of Yaring's fishing coast and of Raman's inland rubber villages:** check what each names as a good day, and whether the sea's or the tree's season shapes it.
- **Young adults of Pattani town and elders of the same households:** check whether joy is described as fun and excitement or as calm and gratitude.
- **Followers of Sufi circles and of reform-minded teachers:** check how each speaks of joy in worship and of festive Maulid gatherings.
- **Thai-speaking Muslims of Satun and Malay-speaking Muslims of Saba Yoi:** check whether *suka*, *gembira* or Thai *khwam suk* is the word that comes first.
- **Buddhist Thai villages of Pattani:** check which temple festivals and family events they name as their happiest.
- **Chinese households of Betong:** check whether the New Year, weddings or business openings bring the largest shared gladness.
- **The Maniq:** check, through their own representatives, how gladness is shown in a camp and whether outsiders have ever recorded it fairly.
- **Comparison only:** the *kompang* and *berzanji* repertoires heard across the Kelantan border; how Patani workers in Malaysian towns keep Hari Raya away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin: happiness is measured nationally but rarely for the region by community; festive forms are reachable through theses and videos.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for *suko* and song lyrics; Arabic for the Barzanji text; Thai for theses and surveys; Chinese for wedding customs; Urak Lawoi for Ko Lipe's songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Maulid feasts, Sufi gatherings and music are debated among Muslim teachers; describe each practice and its critics without taking sides. Grief belongs to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** the gladness, festival and well-being sources above passed the standing rules; this sheet withholds nothing.
