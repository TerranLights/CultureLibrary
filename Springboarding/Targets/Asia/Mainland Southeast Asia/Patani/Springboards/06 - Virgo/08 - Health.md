## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families who call a *bomoh*, families who choose *ruqyah*, and families who refuse both:** check how each explains its choice for the same illness.
- **Women and men of Yaring and Panare fishing households:** check who decides when to go to hospital, and who nurses the sick at home.
- **Hill villages of Sukhirin, Bannang Sata and Than To near forest edges:** check how malaria and distance from hospitals shape care.
- **Thai Chinese households of Yala:** check when Chinese medicine is chosen first and when the hospital is.
- **Buddhist Thai villagers of Mayo and Sai Buri:** check whether temple healing and Thai traditional clinics hold a place apart from Malay practice.
- **Maniq bands in the Banthat and Hala-Bala forests:** check, through their representatives, how mobile clinics and ID-card rules affect their care.
- **Comparison only:** Kelantan's *ruqyah* and cupping clinics; Patani patients who cross to Malaysian hospitals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health statistics and services; thin for local theories of illness, which sit in Jawi books and in practice.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic for the treatise and chapbooks; Patani Malay for healers and patients; Thai for surveys and hospital data; Hokkien and Teochew for medicine halls; Maniq through interpreters the bands choose.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge may be guarded; vaccine and healing choices are argued over; trauma care needs consent. Label no community as resistant to medicine.
- **Held for review:** none: every healing, survey and clinic pointer here could stand as written.
