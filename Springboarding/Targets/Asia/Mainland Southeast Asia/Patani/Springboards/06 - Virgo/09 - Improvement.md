# Patani: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; Hokkien, Cantonese and Hakka. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Peat-swamp villages near Pikun Thong and the rice villages that gained drained land:** check how each tells the story of the reclamation.
- **Townspeople of Nong Chik and of Sungai Kolok under the model-cities plan:** check which changes each town calls improvement and which it calls loss.
- **Young people posting *hijrah* stories and their elders in the same villages:** check whether "becoming better" means the same thing to both.
- **Graduates of reformed Islamic schools and *pondok* that kept the old curriculum:** check how each side describes the other's idea of progress.
- **Cantonese and Hakka families of Betong:** check whether tourism growth is spoken of as betterment for the town or only for some.
- **Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe:** check how the island's development is weighed by the community itself.
- **Sam-Sam villages of Satun:** check which models of a better life they look to, Malay or Thai.
- **Comparison only:** Malaysia's development programs across the Golok; national Thai development rhetoric.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state plans and programs; thin for how ordinary people judge whether a change made life better, which needs interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic for the devotional classic; Patani Malay for *hijrah* talk and house-building; Thai for plans and grants; Cantonese, Hakka and Hokkien for association records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** development and reform projects are tied to the conflict and to land; present each project in its planners' and its critics' words, and portray no group as backward or progressive.
- **Held for review:** nothing was set aside on improvement; the reform, planning and self-help pointers above meet the standing rules.

1. Find the latest Southern Border Provinces development plan and list its stated goals and indicators.

5. Request school-completion counts by province from the Non-Formal Education offices of Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat.
