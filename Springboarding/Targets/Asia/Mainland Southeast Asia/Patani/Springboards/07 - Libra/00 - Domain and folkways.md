# Patani: Domain and folkways (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic (the law books); Hokkien and Hakka of the towns; Urak Lawoi; the Maniq (Mani) languages. **Concept in one line:** the domain of partnership and justice: what a fair bargain and a good partnership are (marriage, contracts, open enemies, the settling of quarrels, gender ways), plus the letter of the law as written and applied.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim households under the 1946 Act and Buddhist Thai households of Mueang Pattani and Betong under the civil code:** check how a marriage or estate is settled when the written law differs by religion.
- **Coastal Yaring and Bacho against inland Bannang Sata and Si Sakhon:** check whether the one asked to settle a quarrel (*tok imam*, headman, elder) differs between sea and rubber villages.
- **Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims:** check whether Islamic-law forms run in Thai there follow those run in Malay in Narathiwat.
- **Sam-Sam families of Songkhla:** check how mixed Buddhist-Muslim descent shapes which law and which rites a couple chooses.
- **Maniq bands of the Banthat range:** check how standing before the law has been written for them, in their representatives' account.
- **Outside Patani, for contrast:** Kelantan's Syariah courts and family enactment; Thailand's civil code outside the four provinces; Patani families married in Mecca.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on statute and court counts; mixed on custom; thin on the Urak Lawoi and Maniq.
- **Lands:** Patani's post-war borders are not yet settled; this Libra set writes for Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat and the Malay-speaking districts of Songkhla and Satun.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for statutes and court data; Jawi Malay and Arabic for the *kitab* and the Islamic-law rulings; Patani Malay to hear how weddings and quarrels are talked about.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** security laws and the conflict reach into this domain; cite the documents and never characterize any community, movement or party to it.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside for the domain overview; the law, wedding and association pointers raised no standing-rule issue.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the 1946 Act from krisdika.go.th and list each subject it hands to the Dato Yuttitham.
2. Search the Office of the Judiciary statistics for "ศาลจังหวัดปัตตานี" and note family and civil case counts by year.
3. Ask a Pattani *pondok* teacher which chapters of *Matla' al-Badrayn* are still taught on sale and marriage.
4. Search YouTube for "majlis kahwin Patani" and "งานแต่ง ปัตตานี" and log the rite order in ten videos.
5. Search Thai press for "บัตรประชาชน มานิ" to trace the Maniq registration records.
