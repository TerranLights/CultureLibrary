# Patani: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*; Jawi, Thai and Latin spellings); standard Malay; Thai; Arabic loanwords of the funeral prayers; Hokkien and Cantonese in the towns; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and what death means are understood; grief and mourning sit here, funeral practice in the domain sheet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families bereaved by the conflict and families who lost kin to illness or age:** check whether the words, rites and public mourning differ by the manner of death.
- **Muslim households of Pattani town and of hill villages in Raman and Si Sakhon:** check who washes and watches the dead, and whether hospital deaths change who sits with the dying.
- **Buddhist Thai of Mueang Narathiwat and Sai Buri:** check the length of mourning and the merit-days kept when the family is a minority in the district.
- **Hokkien and Cantonese families of Yala and Betong:** check whether merit rites and death anniversaries are kept at home, at shrines or by associations.
- **Muslims of La-ngu and Mueang Satun, who mostly speak Thai:** check which language the graveside *talqin* and the condolence visit use there.
- **Maniq bands:** check, through their own spokespeople only, how a death in a band is handled and spoken of.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's *kenduri arwah* calendar; Buddhist mourning in Songkhla's Thai districts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on counts (suicide, conflict deaths); mixed on grief (mostly theses); thin on ghosts and the returning dead, except online.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay for *talqin* and prayers; Patani Malay to hear mourning talk; Thai for health records and theses; Cantonese and Hokkien for the towns' rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the word *syahid* and the naming of conflict dead carry political weight; quote sources only, never take a side or describe any group. Bereaved families need consent and care.
- **Held for review:** the grave, grief and ghost pointers all clear the standing rules; this sheet sets no death source aside.
