# Patani: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*: Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings); standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic purity terms; Hokkien and Hakka of the shrines; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line** (proposed): getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Neighbors of different faiths in the mixed lanes of Pattani town and Saiburi:** check how dogs, pork and shared utensils are handled across a common wall, in each family's own telling.
- **Sam-Sam kin groups along the Satun-Songkhla line:** check whether pork, dogs and the washing of utensils are argued over inside mixed kin groups.
- ***Bomoh*-using families of the hill districts and reform-minded households of Mueang Yala:** check which cleansing rites each side keeps, drops or criticizes.
- **Chinese shrine families of Betong and Yala:** check who takes the nine-day fast in full, who in part, and whether Muslim and Buddhist neighbors take part.
- **Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe:** check, through their own speakers, how the boat-floating rite is said to carry ill luck away.
- **Maniq camps:** check through their chosen representatives whether smoke, herbs or moving camp are spoken of as cleansing.
- **For contrast:** purity teaching in Kelantan's state schools; Nine Emperor Gods practice in Phuket.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on purity rules and audits; mixed on cleansing rites; thin on venting and on the island and forest peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for the purity chapters; Patani Malay for *bomoh* talk and the *luahan* pages; Thai for audits, press and clinics; Hokkien for the fast's rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "cleansing" language near the conflict (raids, purges, displacement) is charged; cite reports and do not characterize any community. Healers may keep their washing formulas secret.
- **Held for review:** self-reform tours urging village men to quit vice, run by a missionary body of South Asian origin (category only); kept out of the cleansing pointers.
