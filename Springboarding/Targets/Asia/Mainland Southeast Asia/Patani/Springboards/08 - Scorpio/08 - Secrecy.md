# Patani: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*), written in Jawi or in Thai or Latin letters; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic Sufi vocabulary; Hokkien and Cantonese; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how what may, must or must not be kept hidden is judged; privacy is one facet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of Mueang Narathiwat and of villages in Cho-airong:** check what kinds of trouble (illness, debt, a divorce) are kept from neighbors, and who is told first.
- ***Pondok* teachers of Sufi lineages and reform-minded teachers in Yala:** check what each says may be taught openly and what only to chosen pupils.
- **Buddhist Thai men of Khok Pho and Mae Lan who carry *khatha* or tattoos:** check what may be shown, and to whom.
- **Chinese families of Betong:** check which family matters stay inside the house or the clan association, from memoirs and interviews.
- **Sam-Sam households of Satun:** check whether a mixed lineage is spoken of openly or kept quiet, and in which language.
- **Maniq and Urak Lawoi:** check, only within limits set by their own spokespeople, what knowledge they keep from outsiders; record the limit, never the content.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's Sufi circles; privacy items in Bangkok samples of the ETDA survey.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin by nature; strongest in theses on healing and health, weakest on family secrets; state secrecy is documented only where it leaked.
- **Languages needed:** the technical Arabic of Sufi symbolism, as glossed in Jawi, to read Dawud's treatise; the Patani Malay of preachers for talk of *aib*; Thai for clinic studies, ETDA tables and border reporting; Hokkien and Cantonese to collect sayings about family shame.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restricted knowledge of healers, Sufi circles and the forest and sea peoples must not be published, only its existence noted; armed groups' secrecy is out of scope; sexuality and HIV status need strict anonymity.
- **Held for review:** for secrecy, the Sufi, healer, clinic and border sources all stand; no hidden-knowledge pointer is parked.

## 5. First queries
1. Locate a copy of *al-Manhal al-Safi* (Pattani or Kuala Lumpur printing, or a manuscript catalog) and list the terms it says to keep from beginners.
2. Search YouTube for "tutup aib ceramah" with "Patani" or "Yala" and note where each speaker draws the line on hiding a wrong.
3. Pull every ThaiLIS record matching "การปกปิด" or "การเปิดเผยตัวตน" for the far south and group them by the thing concealed (illness, identity, debt, rites).
4. Download the latest ETDA internet-user survey and check for a southern or provincial cut on privacy items.
5. Look for secret-society (อั้งยี่) cases from the southern tin districts in histories of Chinese in Siam, then trace their archival references.
