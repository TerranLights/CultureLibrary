# Patani: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in its three scripts (Jawi, Thai, Latin); standard Malay; Thai; Arabic prayer formulas against calamity; Chinese of the towns; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riverbank villages of the lower Kolok and Saiburi and hill villages of Than To:** check whether flood and landslide years are remembered, prayed against and planned for alike.
- **Tapping households in Bannang Sata and fishing households in Panare:** check how each got through its sector's crash, and whom it borrowed from.
- **Families with a worker in Malaysia in 2020 and families without:** check how the border closure changed income, debt and household roles.
- **Satun's coastal villages and the Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe:** check how the 2004 waves are recalled, and whether old warning lore figures in the telling.
- **Chinese shopkeepers of Yala town and Buddhist farmers of Khok Pho:** check which crises each names first, and where each turns for help.
- **Maniq camps in the Banthat forest:** check, only through representatives, how floods, haze or outbreaks reached them.
- **Across the border and beyond, only to compare:** the 2014 Kelantan flood relief; Hat Yai's flood of 2010 and its city warning system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on floods, prices and relief schemes; mixed on older epidemics; thin on personal crises outside theses.
- **Languages needed:** Patani Malay for flood memories and *tolak bala* prayers; Jawi for the prayer texts; Thai for archives, hazard maps and press; Urak Lawoi through community interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the conflict is itself framed as a standing crisis; keep to cited sources and never characterize any community, movement or agency. Disaster survivors' grief needs consent.
- **Held for review:** this sheet withholds nothing: the flood, price, epidemic and pandemic pointers all fall within the standing rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask the National Archives reading room for Monthon Pattani public-health returns on cholera and smallpox (อหิวาตกโรค, ไข้ทรพิษ), 1906-1932, and note outbreak years.
2. Collect *doa tolak bala* prayer sheets from two mosques in Narathiwat and compare their Jawi texts.
3. Visit the 1941 landing memorial in Pattani town and record the inscription and the names honored.
4. Download PCD haze readings for Yala for September 2015 and September 2019 and match them to school-closure notices.
5. Search Thai press for "ยางราคาตก" with "ปัตตานี" or "ยะลา" for 2013-2016 and note each protest's demands.
