# Patani: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) as written in Jawi, in Thai script and in Latin; standard Malay; Thai; the Arabic of devotion; Hokkien and Teochew at the shrines; Maniq; Urak Lawoi. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pattani Bay fishing villages (Yaring, Panare) and rubber-tapping households of Yala's hills (Than To, Bannang Sata):** check whether fasting, night prayer and vows are kept differently when the working day follows the tide or the tapping hour.
- **Households where one spouse converted at marriage (Nong Chik, Sai Buri, Mueang Yala):** check how each family arranges the change of faith with both sets of kin, in their own words.
- **Buddhist Thai teachers, officials and shopkeepers living in the region:** check which amulets, vows and merit-making they turn to under threat, against Buddhists of Phatthalung or Trang.
- **Chinese households of Yala, Pattani town and Sungai Kolok:** check who consults a medium, for what troubles, and whether the young still do.
- **Congregations of Satun where the *khutbah* runs in Thai:** check what faith words are used when Malay is not the mosque's language.
- **Maniq groups living along the Banthat range:** check outside religious teaching and their response only through spokespeople they choose.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's state-run religious classes; Patani workers in Malaysian towns keeping Ramadan away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: abundant devotional *kitab* and sermons; very little measured religiosity at province level; Buddhist and Chinese devotion in the region scarcely studied.
- **Languages needed:** spoken Patani Malay to follow the evening *kuliah*, and Jawi for the book being read; Thai for amulet magazines and the converts' theses; Hokkien or Teochew to hear a medium.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leaving Islam is perilous to discuss, and converts' privacy must be kept; no group's faith is to be graded or characterized.
- **Held for review:** none for faith: the sources on converts, mediums, amulets and widows' coping all stay in.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the conversion episode of the *Hikayat Patani* (Teeuw and Wyatt) and list who heals, who persuades and what the raja is said to promise.
2. Query ThaiLIS for "มุอัลลัฟ" with "ปัตตานี", "ยะลา" and "นราธิวาส" and group the theses by how the conversion came about.
3. Sit in on an evening *kuliah* in a Yaring or Panare mosque and note the *kitab*, the chapter and the questions asked afterward.
4. Download the WVS Thailand wave 7 file and count respondents from the five southern border provinces before reading any faith item.
5. Ask the keeper of a Yala Chinese shrine when the medium sits, and who comes to ask.
