# Patani: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*; Jawi for the creed books, Thai and Latin letters for speech); standard Malay; Thai; Arabic theological terms; Hokkien for the shrine committees; Kensiw and Maniq; Urak Lawoi. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and the warrant asked for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families following older-style teachers and those following reform-minded ones, in the same Saiburi or Nong Chik subdistrict:** check which practices for the dead each keeps or refuses, as each explains it.
- **Rice villages of the Saiburi plain and rubber settlements of Narathiwat's Sukhirin and Chanae:** check whether field and forest taboos persist where livelihoods differ.
- **Temple-village Buddhists of Khok Pho and the Buddhist families of Pattani's town wards:** compare which guardian-spirit rites survive in each, and whether a monk or a lay specialist performs them.
- **Datuk Gong keepers in the border towns:** ask why a Malay-styled spirit is honored by Chinese traders, and whether Malay neighbors take any part.
- **The Kensiw village in Than To:** compare what elders and the young say they hold true now that a school and a mosque or temple are close by, in interviews they approve.
- **Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe:** check, with their consent, how sea and island spirits are spoken of beside Islam or Buddhism.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's state rulings against superstition; Patani families in Mecca who teach their children the creed abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on creed texts and sermons; mixed on folk belief (a few surveys and collections); thin on Kensiw, Maniq and Urak Lawoi belief in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic theological terms with their Jawi explanations; Patani Malay for sermon debates and taboos; Thai for the occult-belief theses; Hokkien for Datuk Gong rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** charges of *syirik* or deviance can harm the accused; record the debate, never take a side. Forest and sea peoples' spirit knowledge may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** for the creed sources, the doctrinal readers circulated by a missionary body of South Asian origin (category only); they are not among the pointers.
