## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young activists of Chana and Thepha and their elders on the same coast:** check how each generation chooses between petition, protest and quiet refusal.
- **Teachers who speak publicly after attacks and those who stay silent, in Yala and Narathiwat:** check which reasons each gives, from published interviews only.
- **Chinese devotees of Yala's vegetarian halls and their non-vowing kin:** check how a lifelong vow is kept inside a mixed household.
- **Women who veil their faces and their sisters or classmates who do not, in Pattani and Yala:** check how each describes the decision, without ranking piety.
- **Muslim shopkeepers of Pattani town and Sungai Kolok during the boycott calls:** check how a stand was taken or declined when livelihoods were at stake.
- **Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe and Maniq of the Banthat range:** check, through their chosen representatives, what they have refused or insisted on in dealings with outsiders.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's student and *ulama* bodies; Patani students' groups in Malaysian universities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: good on public stands (statements, petitions, press); thin on private conviction; the conflict makes the line between conviction and partisanship hard to draw.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the 1630s letters; Jawi for Haji Sulong's prose and the *istiqamah* chapters; Thai for the petitions, theses and party press; Chinese for vegetarian-hall booklets.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** never present any armed group's stance as conviction or as its absence; statements by named people can endanger them. Protect sources; characterize no movement or community.
- **Held for review:** the vows of forty days and more given to preaching tours by members of a revival network founded in South Asia (category only); not used among the steadfastness pointers.
