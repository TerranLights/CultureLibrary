## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Graduates of the old *pondok* of Bendang Daya and Dalo and graduates of Fatoni University:** check how each describes the knowledge that counts and the titles it earns.
- **Families in Pattani town and in Rueso's hill villages:** check how far children study, who pays, and which degrees are hoped for.
- **Students choosing between Cairo, Medina and Malaysian or Thai campuses:** check the reasons given and how each route is judged on return.
- **Buddhist novices in *pariyattitham* classes and Buddhist pupils in state schools of Yala:** check where monastic and secular learning lead.
- **Chinese families of Betong:** check whether a Chinese-language degree abroad or a Thai university is preferred, and why.
- **Ko Lipe's Urak Lawoi school-leavers and Maniq pupils at Satun's schools:** ask, with consent, how far schooling goes, who goes on to college, and what blocks the rest.
- **For contrast:** Kelantan's *pondok* and its state Islamic college; Patani graduates who stay to work in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** for *pondok*, Islamic schools and universities the record is deep; attainment and admissions figures are partial; on Chinese study abroad, monastic degrees and the forest and sea peoples it is sparse.
- **Languages needed:** classical Arabic and Jawi for the *pondok* syllabus; Thai for statistics, admissions and theses; Malaysian Malay for the Malaysian route; Mandarin for the China and Taiwan scholarships.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Islamic schools have been treated as security concerns; cite studies, never characterize a school or its teachers.
- **Held for review:** on higher learning, the alumni of South Asian seminaries who now teach in a few of the region's *pondok* (category only); not among the study-abroad rows.
