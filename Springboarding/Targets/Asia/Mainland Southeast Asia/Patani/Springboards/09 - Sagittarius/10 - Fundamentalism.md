# Patani: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*), with Jawi for polemic and Thai or Latin letters online; standard Malay; Thai; the Arabic of creed disputes; Thai Buddhist terms; English for mission literature; Maniq and Kensiw. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Two congregations of one village after a split, for example in Saiburi or Rueso:** check what each says the other got wrong, in its own words, and how marriages and funerals cross the line.
- **Graduates of Saudi universities and of Al-Azhar teaching in the region:** check which texts each assigns and which practices each permits.
- **Women in Salafi-leaning and in traditional households of Yala town:** check dress, schooling and music rules as the women state them.
- **Monks and lay Buddhists of Pattani's temples:** check views on state religion and on armed protection of temples, from published interviews.
- **Chinese and Christian families of Betong and Yala:** check whether they meet mission or strict-faith pressure from any side, and how they describe it.
- **The Kensiw village at Than To and Maniq camps near Satun:** let their representatives say which preachers or missionaries have come, and what was asked of them.
- **Outside Patani, as foils only:** the Kelantan quarrels over *tahlil* and the Twenty Attributes; the reading habits of Patani students in Medina.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed and lopsided: much on security and "radicalization", little on how those in strict currents describe themselves; Buddhist and Christian strict currents in the region are barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic for the dispute terms; Jawi and Patani Malay for sermons and polemic; Thai for theses, press and campaign records; English for mission profiles and Jerryson.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the most sensitive concept in this sign. No community, movement, school or group is to be characterized; labels such as "Wahhabi" are objects of study, not descriptions. Never link a congregation to violence without a cited court or scholarly source; protect informants.
- **Held for review:** a lay revival movement founded in South Asia, often named by outsiders in this debate (category only), excluded from the reform and strict-practice pointers.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask a Pattani Jawi bookshop for the works ascribed to Dawud al-Fatani that warn against heterodox Sufi readings and copy their chapter titles.
2. Collect five talks by Ismail Lutfi Japakiya and five by traditionalist teachers on *bid'ah* and list the evidence each cites.
3. Read Wattana Sugunnasil's 2006 article and note which other scholars responded to it.
4. Search ThaiLIS for "มัสยิด" with "คณะเก่า" and "คณะใหม่" and map the cases by district.
5. Gather press from the 2007 and 2016 constitution drafts on making Buddhism the state religion and note any statements from southern temples.
