# Patani: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*), written in Jawi, Thai letters and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; *kitab* Arabic; the towns' Chinese varieties; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys and what is owed, with career, status and reputation, and the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The old royal-town districts (Yaring, Kru Se, Mueang Pattani) against the interior rubber districts (Raman, Bannang Sata, Rueso):** check, place by place, which titles carry weight (*Tengku*, *Haji*, *Tok Guru*, *kamnan*).
- **Officials posted from other regions against Buddhist families settled for generations (Nong Chik, Khok Pho, Mae Lan):** check how each speaks of office and of local seniors.
- **Shophouse families of Pattani town, Yala and Betong:** check whether a child's career is settled by the family trade, a school examination or a state post.
- **Chana, Thepha, Saba Yoi and Na Thawi in Songkhla, and the Sam-Sam of Satun:** check whether state and religious command sit in the same hands where the home language is Thai or mixed.
- **Coast fishing villages against hill orchards of Yala and Sukhirin:** check who sets the working day.
- **Ko Lipe's Urak Lawoi and the forest Maniq:** check whom each obeys inside the group and toward park and district officials, as their spokespeople put it.
- **Outside Patani, as foils only:** Kelantan's sultanate; Bangkok's ministries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on offices and on the old rajas, thin on rank, age and time as lived in villages.
- **Lands:** with no territory yet fixed (the tracker lists only pre-war Thailand), every Capricorn sheet aims at the three provinces and the Malay-speaking districts of Songkhla and Satun; the caveat is not repeated in the concept sheets.
- **Languages needed:** Thai to read election returns and office lists; Jawi Malay to read the rajas' history; Chinese characters for town association minutes; interpreters whom Maniq and Urak Lawoi choose.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Article 112 of the Criminal Code bounds what can be asked or printed about royal authority; command in the conflict zone is contested, so cite sources and characterize no community, movement or group.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing about offices, elders or the border's two clocks needed holding back.
