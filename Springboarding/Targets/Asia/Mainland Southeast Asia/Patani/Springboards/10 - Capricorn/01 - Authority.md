# Patani: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai script and Rumi; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic; Chinese of the towns; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages where the *imam* and the headman are kin or one person, against villages where they are rivals:** check whose word closes a quarrel.
- **Pupils of state schools against those of private Islamic schools and *pondok*:** check how each teaches obedience to the teacher and when a pupil may object.
- **Temple communities of Yala town and Nong Chik:** check whether the abbot or the district officer is turned to first.
- **Shophouse households of Betong and Yala:** check when a grandparent's word is final in a family firm and when it is not.
- **Thai-speaking Muslims of Satun and Songkhla's Chana and Thepha:** check whether the Chularajmontri, the provincial committee or the local *imam* is cited as the authority.
- **Hala-Bala and Banthat Maniq; Ko Lipe's Urak Lawoi:** check whose permission each seeks to use forest or sea.
- **Beyond the nation, for contrast:** the Kelantan mufti's office under its sultan; mosques of Bangkok's old Muslim quarters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** state policy and *ulama* writing are plentiful; obedience at home and in the village is barely written down.
- **Axis:** the permission share and the correctional-control share are scored from the law in force, so their reading belongs to Libra; here only how people feel about asking leave and being ruled.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi with some Arabic for 4:59 and its gloss; Thai for NSC texts; spoken Patani Malay for what villagers call those above them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal authority can be discussed only within lèse-majesté limits; authority in the conflict is disputed, so point to sources and describe no community or movement.
- **Held for review:** one category only: the chain of command inside a lay preaching body that began in South Asia and tours the region's mosques, kept out of the religious-authority rows.
