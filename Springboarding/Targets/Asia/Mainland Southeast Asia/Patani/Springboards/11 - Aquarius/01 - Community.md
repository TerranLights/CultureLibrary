# Patani: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*), in Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic terms of the mosque; Hokkien and Hakka of the towns; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **River-mouth hamlets of Sai Buri against hill villages of Bannang Sata and Than To:** check whether membership is reckoned by mosque, by kin or by the village's official boundary.
- **Households still living around temples in Mae Lan and Sai Buri:** check what each owes the temple and the village, and who keeps giving once neighbors have moved away.
- **Hakka and Hokkien families of Yala and Betong:** check which cemetery, hall and street circle each belongs to, and whether newcomers from Bangkok are counted in.
- **Na Thawi and Saba Yoi, where Malay and Thai speech meet:** check whether a family names its mosque, its headman's village or its kin as "our place".
- **Ko Lipe's Urak Lawoi and the Maniq of the Banthat range:** learn from their own spokespeople who counts as a member and how outsiders become one.
- **Set beside Patani, not inside it:** a Kelantan *kampung* parish; the Patani quarter around Kampung Baru's food stalls in Kuala Lumpur.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; good for funds, festivals and agency files; thin on how members feel the bond, and on hamlets emptied since 2004.
- **Languages needed:** spoken Patani Malay to follow the Asyura cooking and parish talk; Jawi to read the neighbor chapters; Thai for mosque registers and CODI files; Chinese characters for cemetery-association rolls.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** departures from villages can be read as blame; report numbers only from cited sources, and characterize no community, movement or group. Fund rolls are personal records.
- **Held for review:** no membership or neighbor pointer had to be held back for this concept.
