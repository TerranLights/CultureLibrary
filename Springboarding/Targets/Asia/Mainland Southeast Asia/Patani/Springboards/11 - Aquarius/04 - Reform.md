# Patani: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings; standard Malay; Thai; Arabic of the reform debates; Mandarin and Hokkien of the Chinese schools; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Graduates of the 1930s-1950s madrasah generation and of the old *pondok*:** check, through their writings and pupils, how each camp spoke of changing religious schooling.
- **Rubber holders on reform plots in Yala against tapping families without title in Narathiwat:** check whether land reform is described as gain, burden or broken promise.
- **Alumni of Yala's and Betong's Chinese schools across generations:** check how each age group tells the closures and the revival.
- **Buddhist civil servants posted from Bangkok and local Muslim officials:** check how each speaks of decentralization proposals, from published seminars only.
- **Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe and Maniq of the Banthat hills:** check which outside improvement schemes they accepted, refused or reshaped, in their spokespeople's words.
- **Women's networks of Pattani and young *hijrah* preachers of Yala:** check which changes each asks for and from whom.
- **Kelantan, as a foil only:** its *Kaum Muda* history and state-run religious reforms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; good on top-down reforms, school histories and the 1975 protest; thin on how villagers judged reforms done to them, and thin for Maniq.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Jawi for Wan Ahmad's print work; Thai for the monthon files, land records and self-government papers; Mandarin for school histories; spoken Patani Malay for elders' memories of the 1940s.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1975 protest and self-government proposals are tied to the conflict; cite sources and characterize no community, movement or group. Royal development projects fall under Article 112 limits. Legal reform texts belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** reform programs of a missionary movement and of a scholarly reform current, both founded in South Asia, active among Patani Muslims (category only).
