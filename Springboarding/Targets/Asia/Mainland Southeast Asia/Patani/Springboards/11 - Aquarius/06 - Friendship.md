# Patani: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*) in Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings; standard Malay; Thai and Southern Thai; Arabic of the *adab* manuals; Hokkien and Hakka of the towns; Urak Lawoi; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men of Pattani town's tea shops and men of upland Bannang Sata:** check where each meets friends when there is no tea shop nearby, and at what hours.
- **Teenagers in Yala's mixed secondary schools and in Yarang's single-faith ones:** check whether a best friend of another faith is named at all, and how it began.
- **Buddhist and Muslim households linked by *kloe* in Saba Yoi and Thepha:** check whether the bond survived the years since 2004, as they tell it.
- **Hokkien shop families of Pattani's old quarter and Hakka families of Betong:** check whether friends are drawn from the clan, the school or the trade.
- **Urak Lawoi fishers and their buyers; Maniq and nearby villagers:** check, through the small peoples' spokespeople, which ties they call friendship and which they call dependence.
- **Kelantan, as a foil only:** friendship networks of Patani workers in Kota Bharu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; tea-shop life and *kloe* ties are visible but thinly written; numbers on friends exist only nationally; women's friendship is barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi to read al-Palimbani's companionship chapters; Southern Thai to hear *kloe* talk; Thai for school and patronage theses; Patani Malay to follow comedy clips; Hokkien or Hakka for sworn-brother groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cross-faith friendship stories are used by many sides in the conflict; cite participants' own words and characterize no community, movement or group. Patronage studies may name living people.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the bond-friend, tea-shop, alumni and *taukay* leads raised no South Asian or legal question.
