# Patani: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*; Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings); standard Malay; Thai; Sufi Arabic; the towns' Chinese varieties; Maniq (Kensiw) and Urak Lawoi through their speakers. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pondok students in *zikir* circles against reform-minded teachers in Yala:** check which practices of self-loss each permits and where each draws the edge.
- **Old noble lines of Yaring, Sai Buri and Nong Chik:** check how they tell the end of their raja houses, and what titles or heirlooms they keep.
- **Thai-speaking Muslims of Mueang Satun and Malay speakers of Khuan Don:** check how each family describes the language its grandparents spoke.
- **Three generations of a Chinese family in Pattani town:** check which dialect, surname forms and rites faded or held, and when.
- **Converts on marriage in Mueang Pattani and Betong:** check what each kept of the old family's rites and what was let go.
- **Kensiw speakers of Than To and Urak Lawoi of Ko Lipe:** check, through their own leaders, how they speak of what is being lost and what is held.
- **For contrast only:** Malay-identity debates in Kelantan; Patani-born families settled in Malaysia whose children speak only standard Malay.
