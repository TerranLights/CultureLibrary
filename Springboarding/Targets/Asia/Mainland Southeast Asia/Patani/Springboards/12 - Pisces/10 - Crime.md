# Patani: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Patani, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Patani Malay (*Baso Yawi*; Jawi, Thai-script and Latin spellings); standard Malay; the Arabic of sin lists; Thai; Chinese of the towns. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. The law itself is researched only in Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet cites them and does not repeat them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mosque congregations of Bacho and Rueso, and Buddhist villages of Mae Lan:** check which acts each condemns most sharply (drug dealing, theft, adultery, violence), from studies that asked them.
- **Town youth of Yala and Sungai Kolok against village youth of Raman:** check how teachers and parents in each place name a "bad" boy and what they blame.
- **Readers of Malay-language and of Thai-language news:** check whether the same incident is framed as crime, as war or as injustice, and by whom.
- **Chinese shop owners of Yala and Betong:** check which wrongs they report to police, which to associations, and which to no one.
- **Families of victims on every side, as they speak in published testimony:** check what each calls the wrong done to them, and whom they hold to blame.
- **Forest and island minorities (Maniq near Than To, Urak Lawoi on Ko Lipe):** check, through their advocates, which wrongs done to them over land, wages or trade get called crimes.
- **For contrast only:** Kelantan's mosque talks on *dosa* and *jenayah*; Patani workers in Malaysia, on how they are treated by police there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conflict violence; mixed on everyday crime and stigma; thin on minorities' experience and on wrongs kept inside the home.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi with *kitab* Arabic to rank the sins; Patani Malay to compare *Wartani* with spoken verdicts; Thai for media studies and reintegration theses; Chinese for shop owners' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the labels "bandit", "insurgent" and "fighter" are partisan; quote their users and never adopt one. Cite rights reports as sources, name no suspects, and characterize no community, movement or group. Penal law, courts and sentencing go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; the sin lists, tales, rights reports and shaming pages all stand under the rules.

4. Search ThaiLIS for "ผู้พ้นโทษ" and "ผู้ค้ายาเสพติด" with the three provinces' names and read the community-view sections.
5. Log a month of CCTV-theft posts from Yala and Pattani pages and code the punishments commenters call for.
