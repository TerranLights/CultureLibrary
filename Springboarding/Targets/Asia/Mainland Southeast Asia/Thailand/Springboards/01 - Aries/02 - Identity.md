## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and Central Plains:** the standard against which "upcountry" is defined; compare urban and rural answers to "where are you from."
- **Isan:** shift from "Lao" to "Isan" as a regional self-name; migrants in Bangkok.
- **Lanna:** revived script, dress and the "Lanna" brand.
- **Southern Thai:** Buddhist and Muslim Thai-speakers (Satun, Songkhla, Trang): does one regional self-name cover both?
- **Thai Chinese:** generation and dialect group (Teochew, Hakka, Hainanese, Hokkien of Phuket) may split answers.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** a Khmer heritage inside Thai citizenship.
- **Mon of Koh Kret, Phra Pradaeng, Sangkhlaburi:** an old minority with visible festivals.
- **Karen, Hmong, Akha, Lisu, Lahu, Mien:** citizenship shapes ethnic labels.
- **Phu Thai, Tai Yai, Lao Phuan, Lao Song:** smaller Tai identities.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** stateless and citizen sea people.
- **Patani** (comparison only; separate culture-nation, a contrast case for where the line of "Thai-ness" is drawn in school and census categories). **Thai communities in Los Angeles and Europe** (comparison only, for how second-generation self-labels shift).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Thainess debates and Isan and Thai Chinese identity; thin for Mon, Phu Thai and sea peoples' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for state documents; Chinese for clan-association records; Karen and Northern Khmer for oral material; old Thai for the inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the monarchy as a pillar of national identity is legally protected; the label "Lao" can wound; statelessness.
- **Held for review:** scholarship on Thai identity by South Asian-heritage authors; texts in the original learned languages behind Thai chronicles (category only).
