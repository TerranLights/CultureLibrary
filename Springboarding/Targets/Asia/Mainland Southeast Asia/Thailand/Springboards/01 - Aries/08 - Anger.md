# Thailand: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan; Kham Mueang for spirit cults; Southern Thai; Hmong; Akha. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains village vs. Bangkok:** surface calm and its breaks; anger in traffic and offices.
- **Isan:** how political grievance since the 2000s is voiced in Isan speech and song.
- **Lanna:** whether family discord is still read through angered lineage spirits.
- **Southern Thai:** feud and reconciliation customs, against stereotypes in Bangkok media.
- **Thai Chinese:** family and business quarrels, and temple rites of apology.
- **Akha, Hmong, Lahu, Karen:** dispute settlement by elders and ritual specialists.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais:** cursing and counter-magic beliefs to check.
- **Women and men:** who may show anger, and how women's anger is pictured on TV.
- **Monks and temple-raised men:** how monastic training handles temper, as a separate case.
- **Patani** (comparison only; separate culture-nation, where grievance and conflict are central). **Thai diaspora** (comparison only).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on politics and mental-health guidance, thin on family-level and minority anger outside violence records.
- **Languages needed:** Thai to read DMH guides and lakhon criticism; Isan for protest speech; Akha for dispute rites; Kham Mueang for spirit dialogue.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest anger aimed at the monarchy is prosecuted; violence records are confidential; the 2010 crackdown remains contested.
- **Held for review:** classical commentaries on anger in their original learned languages that sermons cite (category only).
