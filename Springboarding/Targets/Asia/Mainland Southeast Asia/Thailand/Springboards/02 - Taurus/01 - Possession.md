# Thailand: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang for village lending and funeral customs; Hmong, Karen and Moken for heirlooms and carried goods; Teochew-derived terms for Thai Chinese funeral offerings. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok condo dwellers vs. Central Plains farm households:** small rooms and decluttering talk against barns, rice stores and tools.
- **Isan villages:** temple lending, kin sharing of tools, and the goods migrants bring back.
- **Lanna:** keepsakes in household spirit shrines; what passes to daughters with the house.
- **Southern Thai fishing and rubber households** (Krabi, Trang, Nakhon Si Thammarat): boats and rubber trees as working possessions; Muslim and Buddhist funeral handling of a dead person's goods.
- **Thai Chinese:** paper offerings for the dead; ancestor tablets as kept objects.
- **Hmong and Mien:** silver, embroidery and camp keepsakes.
- **Karen and Lua:** heirloom drums, gongs and beads.
- **Mon of Ko Kret and Sangkhlaburi:** pottery and temple objects.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** boat-held goods and few stores on land.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation; compare only for how Malay Muslim households divide a deceased person's belongings). **Thai migrants abroad** (compare only for what is carried out and sent back in parcels).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on funeral customs and religious teaching about attachment; mixed on everyday belongings; thin on hoarding and on Moken goods.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Isan for forest-monk talks; Teochew for paper-offering shops; Hmong and Sgaw Karen for the stories that travel with heirlooms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antiquities taken from temples and sites (the Ban Chiang loss of artifacts abroad); grief around the dead person's things; Karen heirlooms may carry restricted ritual use.
- **Held for review:** canonical monastic texts in their own learned language on what a monk may own (category only).
