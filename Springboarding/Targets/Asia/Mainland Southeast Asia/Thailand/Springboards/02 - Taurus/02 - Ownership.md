# Thailand: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang for village land and spirit owners; Sgaw and Pwo Karen, Hmong and Urak Lawoi for customary claims; Teochew for family-firm records. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** condo and shophouse owners, renters, and squatter communities on public land.
- **Central Plains rice farmers:** tenancy and land sold to developers around the capital.
- **Isan:** land-reform certificates and land pledged for debt.
- **Lanna villages:** irrigation groups, guardian spirits and community forests.
- **Southern Thai rubber and fruit smallholders** (Surat Thani, Trang, Nakhon Si Thammarat): plantation holdings; Muslim Thai-speakers of Satun and Krabi.
- **Thai Chinese:** shophouse property and family-firm shares.
- **Karen, Lua, Hmong, Lisu and Akha:** customary fields inside forest reserves.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of the Cambodian border:** land along a contested line.
- **Urak Lawoi and Moken:** beach villages without paper claims.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation; compare only for waqf land and mosque holdings set beside Buddhist temple land). **Thai owners of homes abroad** (compare only for how property bought overseas is talked about at home).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on land tenure and titling; mixed on everyday ideas of who owns what; thin on sea peoples' own terms. Land law itself goes to Libra; the purpose of land law and obedience to it go to Capricorn.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for land records and NGO reports; Kham Mueang for guardian-spirit rites; Karen and Urak Lawoi for claims told by the claimants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal property (legal limits on comment); forest-reserve evictions and prosecutions of villagers; foreign ownership as a political flashpoint.
- **Held for review:** commentarial texts in the monastic canon's language on temple property (category only).
