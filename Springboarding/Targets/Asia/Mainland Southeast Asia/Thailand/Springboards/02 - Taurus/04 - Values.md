# Thailand: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan for phaya sayings; Southern Thai for phleng bok; Kham Mueang and Tham Lanna script for teaching texts; Hmong for wedding and funeral chant; Teochew for clan-association materials. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok professionals vs. Central Plains villages:** career and consumer choices against merit, kin and land.
- **Isan:** phaya teaching and migrant households' priorities.
- **Lanna:** spirit-cult duties alongside temple values.
- **Southern Thai Buddhists and Muslims** (Songkhla, Satun, Krabi): shared village life with differing religious rules.
- **Thai Chinese:** how filial and commercial priorities are taught in the same household.
- **Mon of Sangkhlaburi and Ko Kret:** temple education in their own language.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** ancestral spirit rites and Buddhist practice together.
- **Hmong, Mien, Akha and Karen:** clan or village codes, and Christian converts among them.
- **Reform movements** (Santi Asoke; forest monasteries; Dhammakaya followers): rival value orders within Buddhism.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation; compare only for how Islamic moral teaching and Thai state values meet in schools). **Thai temples abroad** (compare only for which values diaspora congregations choose to teach children).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in didactic literature and state lists; mixed on lived priorities; thin on value surveys split by minority.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Tham Lanna for teaching texts; Isan for phaya; Hmong for chant; Teochew for clan sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the nation-religion-king triad and the monarchy (legal limits); the Dhammakaya dispute; the 2014 list's link to military rule.
- **Held for review:** maxim collections in classical learned languages from which some Thai maxims derive (category only).
