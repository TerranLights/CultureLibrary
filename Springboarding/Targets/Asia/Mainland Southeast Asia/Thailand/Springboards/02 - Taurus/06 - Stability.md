*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok renters and condo buyers vs. Central Plains rice villages:** frequent moves against houses held for generations.
- **Isan:** circular migration and grandparent care as a test of continuity.
- **Lanna:** lineage spirits and house succession through women.
- **Southern Thai towns** (Songkhla, Phuket, Nakhon Si Thammarat): old quarters and long-settled Buddhist and Muslim families.
- **Thai Chinese shophouse families:** firms passed to the third and fourth generation.
- **Lao Song, Phu Thai and Tai Yuan resettled communities:** customs kept after forced moves.
- **Mon of Ko Kret and Phra Pradaeng:** language held since Ayutthaya-era migrations.
- **Karen, Lua, Hmong and Akha:** how long village sites have been held, and why they moved, in each people's own histories.
- **Urak Lawoi and Moken:** settled against mobile sea lives.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation; compare only for how decades of unrest shape household and village continuity). **Thai émigré families** (compare only for how a house kept in Thailand anchors those abroad).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political history and migration panels; mixed on household succession; thin on sea-people settlement history in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for panels and histories; Tai Dam for ancestor-house rites; Urak Lawoi for island testimony; Teochew for Chinatown oral history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coups and the monarchy's role (legal limits on comment); evictions in old quarters.
- **Held for review:** monastic calendar texts kept in a learned liturgical language (category only).
