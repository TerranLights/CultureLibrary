# Thailand: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Kham Mueang for the old merchant class; Isan for remittance houses and luck cults; Southern Thai for swiftlet and rubber fortunes; Teochew and Hokkien-derived Thai Chinese terms; Hmong and Akha for stored silver. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok's old aristocratic families vs. new tycoon families:** titles and land against corporate fortunes.
- **Central Plains rice and sugar landholders:** rural wealth near the capital.
- **Lanna:** old merchant families and today's tourism money.
- **Isan:** remittance houses, migrant wives' new wealth, and whether neighbors answer it with envy.
- **Southern Thais of Surat Thani, Trang and Phuket:** rubber, tin-era and swiftlet money; Muslim and Buddhist traders.
- **Thai Chinese:** clan and dialect-group networks (Teochew, Hakka, Hainanese, Hokkien).
- **Hmong, Akha, Mien and Lisu:** silver, livestock and cash crops as stores of wealth.
- **Mon and Khmer-speaking Thais:** temple giving as public standing.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** debt ties to middlemen in sea trades, as reported; to be checked.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation; compare only for zakat and mosque endowments as ways of handling surplus). **Thai communities abroad** (compare only for how money made overseas changes a family's rank at home).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on inequality and tycoons; mixed on wealth beliefs; thin on minority stores of wealth. Taxes and estate law go to Libra.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for policy and media; Kham Mueang for northern merchant history; Isan for migrant-wife ethnography; Teochew for clan records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal wealth (legal limits on comment); monk wealth and temple finance scandals; envy-related violence.
- **Held for review:** classical treatises in a learned scriptural language on the householder's wealth (category only).
