## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** the standard's home ground, where the register ladder is most audible.
- **Lanna:** Kham Mueang talk and a Tham-script reading tradition of its own.
- **Isan:** Lao-speaking households schooled in Central Thai.
- **Southern Thai Buddhists and Muslims** (Nakhon Si Thammarat, Songkhla, Satun, Phuket): Pak Tai speech and southern stage forms.
- **Western hill country** (Kanchanaburi, Tak, Mae Hong Son): Karen, Mon and Shan speech across a border.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy** of Surin and Sisaket: three-language villages.
- **Thai Chinese:** Teochew, Hokkien and Hakka family speech; Chinese-school history.
- **Hmong, Akha, Lahu, Lisu, Mien:** villages where state school arrived late.
- **Moken, Moklen, Urak Lawoi:** Andaman sea peoples, learning mostly oral.
- **Deaf communities:** Thai Sign Language and Ban Khor's village sign.
- **Patani** (a separate culture-nation): compare only for Pattani Malay at home against Thai in school, the sharpest language-of-learning contrast on the border. **Thai communities abroad:** compare only for weekend Thai classes and temple schools that keep the language for children born overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on language, schooling and media; mixed on neighbor customs; thin on Moken and highland learning outside state schools. Thailand's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; these Gemini sheets are written for the core Thai lands.
- **Languages needed:** Central Thai throughout; Kham Mueang and Tham Lanna script for northern verse; Isan for mo lam; Northern Khmer, Karen and Hmong for village speech; Teochew for Chinese-school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the royal register and talk about the monarchy (legal limits on comment); stateless highlanders' access to school; Deaf education records involving minors.
- **Held for review:** temple-school chanting in a classical scripture language (category only).
