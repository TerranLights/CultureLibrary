**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Southern Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Hmong and S'gaw Karen for sung speech; Thai Chinese press language; Thai Sign Language; Burmese, Khmer and Lao as spoken by migrant workers. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what good communication is held to be, and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok offices and Central Plains villages:** register switching at work against kin-term talk at home.
- **Isan migrants in Bangkok:** switching between Isan and Central Thai by listener.
- **Southern Thais:** fast, blunt-sounding Pak Tai and its "copper" Central Thai for outsiders.
- **Lanna speakers:** soft particles and the north's own polite forms.
- **Thai Chinese families:** three generations, three languages at one table.
- **Hmong and Karen villages:** sung verse as a channel for what talk avoids.
- **Burmese, Mon and Shan migrant workers:** clinics, factories and children as interpreters.
- **Deaf Thais:** sign, writing and the hearing world.
- **Monks and laity:** the separate vocabulary for speaking to and about monks.
- **Patani** (separate culture-nation): compare only for how Bangkok's radio, officials and signs speak to Malay-speaking listeners, and where messages fail. **Thai workers abroad** (Israel, Taiwan, South Korea): compare only for daily video calls home and what the distance does to family talk.
