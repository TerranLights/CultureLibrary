## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok tutored teens vs. Central Plains farm children:** exam learning against learning by helping.
- **Isan villages:** temple routes, migrant parents, and grandparents as teachers.
- **Lanna:** ex-monk titles, weaving and woodcarving taught at home.
- **Southern Thai Muslims of Satun and Songkhla:** state school by day, Islamic class at weekends.
- **Karen, Hmong, Akha, Lahu:** Border Patrol Police schools against learning in fields and households.
- **Mon of Sangkhlaburi and Ko Kret:** a script kept by monks.
- **Thai Chinese:** Mandarin's return after decades of restriction.
- **Children of Myanmar migrants:** learning centers and the border.
- **Performing-arts lineages:** khon, dance and piphat musicians in teacher lines.
- **Patani** (separate culture-nation): compare only for pondok schooling and its tense meeting with the Thai curriculum, against Satun's quieter mix. **Thais trained for work abroad:** compare only for cooks and masseurs certified at home (the Wat Pho massage school among them) before taking jobs overseas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on schooling, testing and policy; mixed on learning by watching (old village studies); thin on how Moken parents and the forest Maniq teach their young.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Kham Mueang and Isan for monastic titles; Karen, Hmong and Mon for village teaching; Burmese for Mae Sot; Southern Thai for Satun.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stateless and migrant children's records; Karen land disputes over rotational farming.
- **Held for review:** classical scripture-language exams in the monastic schools (category only).
