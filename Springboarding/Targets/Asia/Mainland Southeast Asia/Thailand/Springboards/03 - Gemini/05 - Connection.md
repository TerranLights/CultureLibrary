# Thailand: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan for sworn friendship and string-tying rites; Southern Thai for the kloe bond; Kham Mueang for spirit-kin groups; Hmong for clans; Kuy for the elephant villages; Urak Lawoi; Teochew for clan and tomb customs. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected (or not).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok office workers vs. Central Plains kin:** alumni and work networks against village kin webs.
- **Isan:** sworn friends, migrant chains to the city, and homecoming.
- **Lanna:** spirit-kin groups, and through which line of descent each is reckoned.
- **Southern Thai Buddhists and Muslims:** kloe bonds and shared markets.
- **Thai Chinese:** clan, surname and dialect associations; the family tomb.
- **Hmong, Mien, Akha:** clan ties reaching across borders.
- **Kuy and Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin:** bonds with elephants and with Cambodian kin.
- **Urak Lawoi, Moken and Moklen:** boat and island ties.
- **Elders living alone:** loneliness where children have moved away.
- **Patani** (separate culture-nation): compare only for whether cross-faith friendships and market ties hold under long conflict, a test case beside the Southern Thai kloe. **Thai spouses and workers abroad:** compare only for the village networks that send, support and receive them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on patronage and migration; mixed on ritual friendship; thin on sea peoples' ties and on loneliness by minority.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan for siao rites; Southern Thai for kloe; Kham Mueang for spirit groups; Hmong for clans; Kuy and Urak Lawoi with local help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patronage inside the military and police; family placement in state jobs; spirit-group rites some families keep private.
- **Held for review:** none; every tie-making rite listed is the nation's own.
