# Thailand: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai, with the regional going-home words of Lanna, Isan and the south; Karen, Mon, Akha, Hmong and Moken; Teochew-derived household terms. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan villagers and Isan workers in Bangkok:** a house up-country and a rented room in the city.
- **Chiang Mai, Lamphun, Nan:** houses kept for the family while the children live away.
- **Bangkok's condo and townhouse dwellers:** home without land, often rented.
- **Central Plains canal villages:** stilt houses and houseboats, and the move inland.
- **Pak Tai Buddhist and Muslim families** (Songkhla, Satun, Trang, Nakhon Si Thammarat): home in rubber and fishing country.
- **Karen of the western forests:** home as territory, under park and forest law.
- **Mon of Sangkhlaburi and Ratchaburi:** home places rebuilt after floods and migration.
- **Akha, Lisu, Lahu and Hmong highland villages:** home bounded by gate, altar or clan.
- **Thai Chinese:** shophouse homes and ancestral shrines.
- **Moken, Moklen and Urak Lawoi:** sea and shore, boat and stilt house.
- **Patani, as an outside case** (separate culture-nation): Malay Muslim villages where home sits under long security operations, a sharp contrast to the Pak Tai provinces next door. **Thais living abroad, as an outside case:** how "back home" is spoken of by workers in Taiwan or Israel who keep a house building in the home village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on housing markets and migration songs; mixed on how people talk of home in daily life; thin on Akha and Moken accounts in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang words for going home; Karen and Mon for displacement accounts; Akha and Moken through interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** evictions from parks and forests are contested and policed; Bang Kloi involves a death in custody of a Karen activist; ownership disputes go to Taurus and law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the house rites named are local Thai, Lanna, Isan and Chinese forms.
