## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Khon mueang of the north:** a strong regional self-name beside Thai citizenship.
- **Isan migrants:** "Isan", "Lao" or "Thai" as labels, chosen by setting.
- **Pak Tai southerners:** southern pride outside Patani; Buddhists and Muslims of Songkhla, Satun, Trang.
- **Thai Chinese:** from newcomer to core, across three or four generations.
- **Highland peoples** (Akha, Lahu, Lisu, Hmong, Mien): citizenship as the gate to belonging.
- **Karen and Mon along the western border:** cross-border kin and paper status.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** three-way identities in the lower northeast.
- **Viet Thais of the Mekong provinces:** belonging granted late.
- **Muslims of Bangkok:** Cham, Malay and Persian-descended communities in the capital.
- **Moken, Moklen, Urak Lawoi:** sea peoples and their papers.
- **Patani, set beside Thailand only** (its own culture-nation): Malay Muslim identity where "Thai Muslim" and "Malay" are contested labels, a counter-case for the limits of Thai-ness. **Thais abroad, set beside Thailand only:** children of Thai mothers raised in Europe who visit a village where they are treated as half-outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national identity and citizenship; mixed on regional belonging; thin on Moklen and Viet Thai voices.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang self-labels; Northern Khmer; Vietnamese; Akha, Lahu and Hmong for citizenship accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** citizenship status and the monarchy's place in Thai-ness are delicate; stateless people may fear exposure; nationality law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the classical text named is a Thai rendering of a Mon work.
