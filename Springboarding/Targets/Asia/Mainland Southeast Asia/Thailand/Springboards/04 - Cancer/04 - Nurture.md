## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok's salaried families:** tutoring, one or two children, expert advice.
- **Isan villages:** lullabies, teaching verse and children raised by grandparents while parents work away.
- **The Lanna north:** northern lullabies and grandmothers' roles in compound households.
- **The Pak Tai south:** rong ruea lullabies; Buddhist and Muslim families of Songkhla, Satun and Phatthalung.
- **Thai Chinese:** a-ma and a-kong as daily carers; school ambition.
- **Hmong and Mien:** newborn soul rites and clan teaching.
- **Karen and Lua:** children learning in the rotational farm.
- **Mon of Ratchaburi and Samut Sakhon:** language passed or lost between generations.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of Surin:** lullabies in Khmer and Kuy.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** sea skills taught from early childhood.
- **Patani, a case apart** (separate culture-nation): Malay Muslim child-rearing shaped by Qur'an study at home and the tadika, useful against Satun's Thai-speaking Muslim families. **Thai parents overseas, a case apart:** Thai mothers in Europe deciding which bedtime songs and words to keep.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on official child-development policy and parenting media; mixed on regional practice; thin on Mien, Kuy and Moklen upbringing in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan, Kham Mueang and Pak Tai for lullabies and teaching verse; Hmong for newborn rites; Teochew household terms; Moken through interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** research with children needs consent; corporal punishment and neglect touch on child-protection law (Libra); some Hmong and Karen soul rites are family-only.
- **Held for review:** none; the didactic verse named is Isan, Lao and Thai.
