## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rough sleepers in the capital:** around Hua Lamphong, Sanam Luang and Rajadamnoen.
- **Isan and Lanna farm households:** field huts and kin taken in after a crop failure.
- **Pak Tai coast** (Phang Nga, Krabi, Phuket, Satun): tsunami and storm shelter, and Buddhist and Muslim villages' rebuilding.
- **Karen of Tak and Mae Hong Son:** border camps, and the hill villages that sheltered kin.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Sa Kaeo, Surin and Sisaket:** border villages beside the camps of 1979.
- **Mon of Pak Kret and Phra Pradaeng:** descendants of refugees given land.
- **Hmong:** camp histories and later resettlement.
- **Moken:** monsoon shelter ashore, and post-tsunami villages.
- **Thai Chinese:** shrine foundations that bury and shelter the friendless.
- **Migrant workers from Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos** (inside Thailand, not citizens): dormitory and shed housing.
- **Patani, only as a foil** (separate culture-nation): villagers displaced by insurgency and mosques used as refuge, against the disaster-driven sheltering of the Andaman coast. **Thais abroad, only as a foil:** temples overseas that lodge stranded Thai workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on camps and disasters; mixed on urban homelessness; thin on how kin take in kin, and on field-hut life in writing.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan, Kham Mueang and Pak Tai hut vocabulary; Karen and Burmese for camps and dormitories; Khmer for 1979 memories; Mon.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugees and undocumented workers may face deportation; women's and children's shelters keep locations confidential; asylum and immigration law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the shelter customs named are Thai, Mon and minority practice.
