# Thailand: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai (health-system and folk-medicine vocabularies); Kham Mueang for northern healers; Northern Khmer for Surin healing rites; Isan; Teochew for benevolent hospitals; Burmese and Shan for migrant carers. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan:** grandparents cared for by whichever child stays; herbal healers.
- **Lanna:** mo mueang and the youngest daughter's household.
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** hired Burmese and Lao carers, private nursing homes, hospital bedside duty.
- **Trang, Satun, Songkhla and Nakhon Si Thammarat:** mosque and temple roles in caring for the sick, among Pak Tai Buddhists and Muslims.
- **Thai Chinese:** benevolent hospitals, a-ma at home, and grandchildren's duty.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of Surin:** healing rites led by women elders.
- **Karen and Hmong:** shamans and herbalists beside rural clinics.
- **Mon:** temple-centered care in Sangkhlaburi.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** island healers and distance from hospitals.
- **Migrant carers** (Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia; inside Thailand, not citizens).
- **Patani, an external foil** (separate culture-nation): Islamic duties to the sick and the work of Malay village midwives (bidan), useful against care in Satun's Thai-speaking Muslim homes. **Thai carers in Japan and Israel, an external foil:** Thais paid to look after foreign elders while their own parents are tended by kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the health system and elder care policy; mixed on family care as lived; thin on Khmer, Karen and Moken healing in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; healers' vocabulary in Kham Mueang and Isan; Northern Khmer for klaemo; Burmese and Shan for migrant carers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patients' privacy; HIV stigma around hospices; some healing rites are restricted to initiates; the royal patronage of medical volunteers limits open critique.
- **Held for review:** none; the medicine treatise named is a Thai court text.

1. Read the *Phra Osot Phra Narai* treatise in a modern Thai edition and note passages on tending the sick.

5. Search Pantip and news for reactions to *หลานม่า* and log what viewers say about caring for grandparents.
