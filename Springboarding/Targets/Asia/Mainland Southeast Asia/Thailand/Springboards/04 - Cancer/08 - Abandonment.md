*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan:** grandparent-and-grandchild households, and the charge of akatanyu leveled at children who stay away.
- **Lanna north:** highland children in Chiang Mai homes; elders left as the young move to cities.
- **Bangkok:** street children, unclaimed bodies, and babies left at hospitals.
- **Central Plains provinces:** state homes at Pak Kret and Bang Khae, and the families who visit or do not.
- **Pak Tai** (Phuket, Phang Nga, Krabi, Songkhla): children orphaned by the 2004 tsunami, Buddhist and Muslim.
- **Thai Chinese:** charity burial of the kinless; filial duty to the dead.
- **Akha, Lahu and Karen:** children placed in homes, and parents' reasons.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin and Buriram:** villages emptied by migration to Bangkok and abroad.
- **Mon:** elders left in Sangkhlaburi as the young work in factories.
- **People with leprosy and HIV:** abandonment by family and its later repair.
- **Patani, as a counter-case** (separate culture-nation): orphans of the insurgency raised by kin and Islamic schools, against the state homes used elsewhere. **Thai workers abroad, as a counter-case:** parents in Korea or Israel whose absence the village debates as sacrifice or neglect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on welfare institutions and news; mixed on left-behind families; thin on how abandoned elders and highland children speak of it themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang family talk; Akha, Lahu and Karen with interpreters; Teochew for foundation records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child records are confidential; orphanage tourism and child trafficking overlap; abortion-related cases appear in baby-abandonment news; questions of family law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the abandonment tale named is a Thai folktale cycle.
