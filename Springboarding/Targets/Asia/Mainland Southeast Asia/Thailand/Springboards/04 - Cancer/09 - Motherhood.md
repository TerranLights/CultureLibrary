*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** working mothers, small families, and Mother's Day rites in schools.
- **Isan:** young mothers who migrate and leave babies with grandmothers; the Kong Khao Noi legend at home.
- **Lanna:** mothers in daughters' compound houses; northern teen-birth rates.
- **Pak Tai, Buddhist and Muslim** (Satun, Trang, Krabi, Songkhla): mothers' roles in fishing and rubber households.
- **Thai Chinese:** Guanyin devotion and first-month customs.
- **Hmong and Mien:** postpartum confinement and many children.
- **Karen, Akha and Lahu:** home birth beside the clinic; stateless mothers' access to care.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** mothers' rites in Khmer and Kuy.
- **Mon of Ratchaburi and Samut Sakhon:** mothers keeping Mon at home.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** island mothers far from hospitals.
- **Patani, for comparison only** (its own culture-nation): Malay Muslim mothers widowed by the conflict, and the Malay-language address they use with children, beside Thai-speaking Muslim mothers in Satun. **Thai mothers abroad, for comparison only:** mothers married to foreigners who raise children between a Thai and a foreign household.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on mother figures, health statistics and Mother's Day; mixed on everyday mothering; thin on Kuy, Moken and Mien mothers in their own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including Ayutthaya court verse; Isan for the Yasothon tale; Hmong; Northern Khmer; Karen and Akha through interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mother's Day is tied to the queen mother (comment is legally limited); abortion and teen pregnancy are delicate; the 2010 deaths are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** the scripture-language source of the Mahachat (category only); the Thai court verse itself is the pointer.
