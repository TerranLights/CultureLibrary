## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** stadiums, concerts and nightlife; where fame is made.
- **Isan:** mo lam stages and courting yards; festivity tied to the rice year.
- **Lanna north:** lantern festivals, courting songs, a strong crafts scene.
- **Pak Tai south** (Songkhla, Nakhon Si Thammarat, Phatthalung, Trang, Satun, Phuket): bullfights, bird contests, nora; Buddhist and Muslim festivity side by side.
- **The west** (Kanchanaburi, Ratchaburi, Tak): Karen, Mon and Lao Song festivals.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of Surin, Buriram and Sisaket:** festivals built on elephants and Khmer ruins.
- **Thai Chinese:** shrine fairs, opera, society pages.
- **Highland peoples** (Lisu, Hmong, Akha, Lahu, Mien): New Year as the season of courtship and dress.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** boat-floating rites as the big gatherings.
- **Patani, as a comparison case** (a culture-nation of its own): Malay Muslim festivity under Islamic limits on alcohol and gambling (to check), beside Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims at Pak Tai fairs. **Thai communities abroad, as a comparison case:** which festivals and sports clubs are rebuilt overseas, which dropped.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on festivals, sport and entertainment; mixed on courtship and private pleasure; thin on highland and sea peoples' own accounts. No scope file exists yet and the author has not fixed post-war Thailand's lands, so the Leo sheets are written for the present heartland; the other sheets do not repeat this.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including King Chulalongkorn's prose; Isan and Kham Mueang for courtship verse; Pak Tai for southern contests; Lisu for festival speech, with a local interpreter.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal ceremonies and patronage limit open comment; gambling and nightlife touch illegal trades; children in contests need care.
- **Held for review:** none; the ceremonial calendar named is a Thai king's own text.
