## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lanna:** sala woodcarvers (Ban Tawai) working to order, against art-school artists in Chiang Mai.
- **Isan:** mural painters, candle carvers and mudmee weavers, where invention is collective.
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** court crafts, art schools and the design trade.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** niello smiths, puppet carvers and Phatthalung reed (krachut) weavers.
- **The west:** Lao Song and Karen weavers of Ratchaburi, Phetchaburi and Kanchanaburi.
- **Phu Thai and Tai Lue:** weaving lines named after villages.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin:** silk patterns (pha hol) tied to Khmer heritage.
- **Thai Chinese:** goldsmiths and shrine artisans who keep Chinese styles.
- **Hmong and Mien:** embroidery and silverwork, and how designs move between clans.
- **Moken:** boatbuilding as practical invention.
- **Patani, as a foil only** (separate culture-nation): painted kolae fishing boats, a decorative tradition built on Malay motifs, beside Pak Tai's niello and puppets. **Thai artists based abroad, as a foil only:** how a maker's "Thai" style is judged once it leaves the country.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on court arts, craft promotion and contemporary art; mixed on everyday improvising; thin on minority makers' own ideas of what is new.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for Fine Arts and CEA material; Kham Mueang for sala lore; Isan, sometimes in Tham script, for mural captions; Sgaw Karen for weavers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** film censorship; crafts under royal patronage are hard to critique; brands using minority designs without consent.
- **Held for review:** the textile and jewelry trades of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
