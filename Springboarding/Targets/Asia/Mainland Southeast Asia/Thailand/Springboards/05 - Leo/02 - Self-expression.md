# Thailand: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai, including youth and online slang; Isan; Kham Mueang; Pak Tai; Hmong; Sgaw Karen; Teochew for opera. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok students:** hair and uniform rules, protest art, secret accounts.
- **Central Plains villages:** choi and other sung repartee as licensed frankness.
- **Isan:** speaking Isan on screen after decades of Central Thai at school.
- **Lanna:** northern speech and dress chosen on purpose by younger Chiang Mai people.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** Southern Thai on stage, radio and online; Satun's Muslim students and their dress.
- **Karen and Lahu:** harp and gourd-pipe songs that carry what is not said in talk.
- **Hmong and Akha:** instruments and courtship songs; a new generation on video platforms.
- **Thai Chinese:** opera audiences aging; younger Thai Chinese expressing heritage through food and fashion instead.
- **Kathoey and other LGBT Thais,** in each region.
- **Mon of Pathum Thani and Ratchaburi:** Mon dress and dance worn at festivals as a statement.
- **Patani, kept to one side** (separate culture-nation): the hijab dispute at a state kindergarten in Pattani (2018), a sharp case of dress as expression meeting school rules, beside practice in Satun's schools. **Thai students overseas, kept to one side:** which parts of themselves they choose to show at campus Thai nights.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on youth, protest and media; mixed on emotional display at home; thin on minority artists' views in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai youth slang for online speech; old court verse for *Kamsuan*; Isan and Pak Tai for comedy and radio; Hmong and Sgaw Karen for song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** speech touching the monarchy is legally risky, for subjects and for researchers; minors in protests; anonymous accounts must not be unmasked.
- **Held for review:** none; the poem named is Thai.
