# Thailand: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Isan and Kham Mueang (regional self-names); Pak Tai; Northern Khmer; Sgaw Karen; Hmong; Teochew. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lanna:** the khon mueang name and the princes' past, held against Bangkok.
- **Isan:** pride in Isan speech, food and music, and how it has shifted with migration and media.
- **Central Plains:** Central Thai speech and customs set as the national standard, and the pride that rides on that.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** Nakhon Si Thammarat's ancient-city pride; Muslims of Satun and Trang as Thai-speaking southerners.
- **The west and Kanchanaburi:** Mon and Karen pride along the border.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Buriram and Surin:** Khmer temple heritage claimed as their own.
- **Thai Chinese:** when ancestry is shown or kept quiet, by generation.
- **Hmong, Akha, Lisu:** pageants and dress as public pride.
- **Lao Song and Phuan:** descendants of people moved from Laos, and their pride in an origin elsewhere.
- **LGBT Thais:** pride as a movement, with each region's own story.
- **Patani, a contrasting case only** (its own culture-nation): Malay and Islamic pride standing apart from Thai national symbols, which tests how far the state's pride reaches. **Thai communities abroad, a contrasting case only:** pride in Thai food and boxing gyms abroad as the face of the nation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national symbols, sport and regional media; mixed on personal pride and arrogance in daily life; thin on minority pride in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; the old northern script and Isan for the epic; Northern Khmer for Buriram heritage talk; Mon and Sgaw Karen for self-naming; Hmong for pageant speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride is tied to the monarchy and the army, so critique is risky; LGBT events have faced attack; the stolen-heritage cases touch diplomacy.
- **Held for review:** none; the epic named is Tai.
