# Thailand: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Isan, Kham Mueang and Pak Tai verbs of looking; Shan for Mae Hong Son rites; Mlabri and Hmong through interpreters; Northern Khmer for census language. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** influencers, live-sellers and the unseen night workforce in the same streets.
- **Lanna:** walking streets and lantern nights as stages; Shan rites in Mae Hong Son.
- **Isan:** regional faces on national TV, and how they are cast.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** Southern Thai voices on national media; Satun's Muslims on screen.
- **Central Plains towns:** Loy Krathong queens and school contests.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** how often they appear in national images at all, a count to make.
- **Mlabri, Maniq and Moken:** small peoples seen mostly through outsiders' cameras.
- **Karen, Hmong, Akha, Lahu:** tourist images against their own posts.
- **Thai Chinese:** how far they are seen as Chinese, in business, politics and on screen.
- **Kathoey and transgender Thais:** visibility in entertainment set against offices and schools.
- **Patani, as an outside case only** (separate culture-nation): national coverage that may run mostly through security reporting (to check), which sharpens the question of who controls a group's image. **Thais abroad, as an outside case only:** Thai cooks and masseurs as the public face of the country, and whether their own lives are ever shown.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on media, contests and online use; mixed on everyday gaze and greeting; thin on how small peoples want to be seen.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Shan for Poi Sang Long; Mlabri and Hmong with interpreters; Isan and Pak Tai to hear people describe how they are portrayed.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** photographs of minors and of highland peoples taken without consent; transgender students' privacy; images of the royal family are tightly controlled.
- **Held for review:** none; the court text named is Thai.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *Tamrap Thao Si Chulalak* in a modern edition and note the scenes of display.
2. Search the National Archives and press archives for "นางสาวสยาม งานฉลองรัฐธรรมนูญ" and list the contests by year.
3. Search YouTube for "ปอยส่างลอง" and log the roles of parents, sponsors and onlookers.
4. Pull the census language tables and map the minority languages by province.
5. Search ThaiJO for "ภาพตัวแทน" with "อีสาน", "คนใต้" and "ชาติพันธุ์" and list the studies.
