# Thailand: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai, including political and Buddhist vocabularies; Isan for village and migrant voices; Northern Khmer and Kuy for Surin; Burmese and Shan for migrant workers; Karen and Hmong for highland voices. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan and the north:** rural voters answering the old "poor, ignorant, sick" (โง่ จน เจ็บ) label.
- **Bangkok's poor:** homeless networks, street vendors and the blind lottery sellers.
- **Central Plains factories and ports:** Thai and migrant workers side by side.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** fishing crews of Songkhla and the Andaman ports.
- **The west:** Karen and Mon villagers dealing with officials and park rangers.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of Surin:** elephant keepers and their standing.
- **Highland peoples** (Hmong, Akha, Lahu, Lisu, Mien): the "chao khao" label and its weight.
- **Thai Chinese:** how the coolie past is told in family histories.
- **Sex workers and LGBT Thais:** dignity claimed through organization.
- **Elders across regions:** clubs, temples and home care.
- **Patani, as a parallel only** (separate culture-nation): Malay Muslims' complaints of treatment at checkpoints and in detention, a dignity question under security rule. **Thai workers abroad, as a parallel only:** labor in Israel, Korea and Taiwan, where Thais are the migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on rights groups, labor reports and political talk; mixed on dignity in care; thin on how small peoples frame their own worth.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including 1950s Marxist prose; Isan and Kham Mueang for village interviews; Burmese and Shan for migrant workers; Kuy and Northern Khmer for Surin keepers; Karen and Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sex work is illegal; migrant workers risk their jobs by speaking; political talk about class can touch the monarchy; elephant use draws animal-welfare quarrels.
- **Held for review:** none; the writings named are Thai.
