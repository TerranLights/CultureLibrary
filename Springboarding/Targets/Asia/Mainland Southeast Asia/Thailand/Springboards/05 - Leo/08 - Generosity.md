*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai (merit and charity vocabularies); Kham Mueang; Isan; Southern Thai, including Muslim usage in Satun and Songkhla; Hokkien and Teochew for Chinese alms festivals; Mon. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan:** migrants who pay for temple halls in home villages; the Udon monk's campaign.
- **Lanna:** salak by lot; giving that hides the giver's choice.
- **Central Plains:** Mahachat sermons and Mon river alms.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** Muslim feasts in Satun and Songkhla beside Buddhist temple giving in Nakhon Si Thammarat.
- **Thai Chinese:** ghost-festival alms, benevolent associations and shrine sponsorship.
- **Mon:** river alms and temple patronage.
- **Karen and Hmong:** sharing within villages and clans, to be described from inside.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin:** giving at the Sen Don Ta ancestor festival.
- **Moken:** sharing of the catch and of aid after the 2004 tsunami.
- **Celebrity and online fundraisers:** the argument over what giving should cover and what the state should fund.
- **Patani, as another frame only** (separate culture-nation): giving at Islamic feasts and to pondok schools, beside the kin niao feasts of Satun's Thai speakers. **Thai communities abroad, as another frame only:** donations sent home to build temple halls, and the standing they buy in the village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on merit-making rites, charities and fundraising news; mixed on everyday giving among kin; thin on highland and sea peoples' sharing in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including sermon verse; Kham Mueang for salak verses; Southern Thai and some Malay terms for Satun feasts; Hokkien for Phuket festivals; Mon for river alms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal charity is hard to discuss openly; temple finances and scandals; donors' privacy; aid after disasters.
- **Held for review:** none; the sermon named is a Thai verse retelling, used here in its Thai form only.

1. Attend or watch a Thet Mahachat recording and note the episodes chanted and the audience's offerings.

5. Search YouTube for "ทิ้งกระจาด ภูเก็ต" and "กินเหนียว สตูล" and note who gives, who receives, and what is said.
