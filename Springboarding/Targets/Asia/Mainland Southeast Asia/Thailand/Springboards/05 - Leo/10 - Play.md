## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** kites, makruk, game cafés and esports.
- **Central Plains towns:** folk games kept alive in schools and fairs.
- **Isan:** games at Songkran and harvest; play in mo lam stage banter.
- **Lanna:** northern children's games and festival contests.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** bird contests and bullfights; Satun's Muslim families and which games they allow.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** Songkran dance games and elephant shows.
- **Mon of Phra Pradaeng:** Songkran games kept as Mon custom.
- **Hmong, Akha, Lisu, Lahu:** New Year ball, top and swing games.
- **Thai Chinese:** mahjong and New Year card play.
- **Moken:** children's sea play and boat games, to be described from inside.
- **Patani, a cross-check only** (separate culture-nation): Malay moon kites (wau) flown in the far south *check*, beside Bangkok's chula and pakpao: the same toy under other rules and meanings. **Thai families abroad, a cross-check only:** which childhood games parents teach children raised outside Thailand.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on folk-game collections, comedy and media; mixed on adult play and gaming; thin on highland and sea children's play described by themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and old Sukhothai script; French for La Loubère; Isan and Northern Khmer for game rhymes; Pak Tai for bird-contest talk; Akha and Hmong with local help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gambling at wakes and contests is illegal in principle; children's online gaming and its harms; the dating of the inscription is a touchy academic and national question.
- **Held for review:** none; the inscription and games named are Thai.
