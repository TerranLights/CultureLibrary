## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains:** gangster films, Guan Yu shrines and Bangkok's street codes.
- **Lanna:** phit phi fines and the honor of the house spirits; Uttaradit's sword hero.
- **Isan:** Ya Mo's statue in Korat beside Lao memory of Anouvong; spirit fines.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** the Phuket heroines; Buddhist and Muslim codes of the word given in Songkhla and Satun.
- **The east:** Trat's naval memory and Chanthaburi's veterans.
- **Thai Chinese:** sworn brotherhood, Guan Yu and the rotating savings group.
- **Hmong:** clan name and marriage negotiation.
- **Karen and Lahu:** elders' oaths and truth-telling at disputes, to be described from inside.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin:** border-war veterans of the 2008 to 2011 clashes.
- **Military families** in garrison towns (Lopburi, Kanchanaburi).
- **Patani, a separate test case only** (its own culture-nation): how honor is spoken of during a long insurgency, in Malay and religious terms, a test of the national frame of honor heard in the other regions. **Thai veterans and soldiers abroad, a separate test case only:** Thai troops in Korea (1950 to 1953) and Vietnam, and how their honor is told at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on war heroes, memorials and martial arts; mixed on everyday promise-keeping and money; thin on honor in highland and sea peoples' own terms.
- **Languages needed:** old Thai for the oath poem; Kham Mueang and Isan for spirit fines; Lao for the Anouvong memory; Pak Tai for Phuket's heroines; Mandarin and Teochew for shrines; Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor tied to the monarchy and army is legally and socially guarded; the Anouvong story touches relations with Laos; spirit fines and women's conduct need care; gang codes.
- **Held for review:** none; the oath poem named is Thai.
