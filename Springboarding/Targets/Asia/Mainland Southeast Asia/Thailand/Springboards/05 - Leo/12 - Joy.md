## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Isan:** mo lam nights, the word muan for a good party, and music at wakes.
- **Lanna:** lantern nights and quieter temple festivals.
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** Songkran in the streets, concerts and malls.
- **Pak Tai, outside Patani:** Chak Phra contests and southern concerts; Muslim feasts in Satun and Trang.
- **The west:** Karen New Year and Mon festivals of Kanchanaburi and Ratchaburi.
- **Thai Chinese:** Paknampho, Yaowarat and Phuket festivals.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** Songkran dances and elephant festivals in Surin.
- **Hmong, Lisu, Akha:** New Year as the high point of the year.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** joy at boat-floating rites, to be described in their own words.
- **Elders and the young:** whether joy is shown the same way across ages.
- **Patani, a point of contrast only** (separate culture-nation): Hari Raya joy, set by the Islamic calendar and kept apart from Songkran water play. **Thais abroad, a point of contrast only:** Songkran and Loy Krathong staged in foreign cities, and what delight they keep or lose.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on festivals, concerts and happiness surveys; mixed on everyday joy at home; thin on highland and sea peoples' own words for gladness.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including Ayutthaya verse; Isan and Kham Mueang for party talk; Pak Tai for festival commentary; Hokkien for Phuket and Paknampho; Sgaw Karen, Hmong and Northern Khmer with local help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Songkran deaths and sexual harassment at festivals; alcohol; joy tied to royal celebrations is hard to discuss freely.
- **Held for review:** none; the verse and sermon words named are Thai.
