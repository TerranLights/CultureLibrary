*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Isan; Kham Mueang; Southern Thai (Pak Tai); Teochew; Sgaw Karen, Hmong, Northern Khmer. **Concept in one line:** what good work and a well-kept life are: work, service, daily routine and health, with work ways as the folkway (time ways and order ways border it from Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains and Bangkok:** paddy giving way to office and factory; the clock-bound workday's first home.
- **Lanna north:** its own court crafts and shared irrigation (*muang fai*) labor.
- **Isan (Lao Isan, Phu Thai):** the Hit Sip Song round beside seasonal migration to Bangkok.
- **Pak Tai south** (Nakhon Si Thammarat, Songkhla, Trang, Satun, Phuket, Krabi): predawn rubber tapping and the tides shape another day.
- **Western valleys:** Karen swidden fields, Mon potters and boatmen, Lao Song weavers of Phetchaburi.
- **Northern Khmer and Kuy villages of the lower northeast:** silk and elephant work handed down in families.
- **Thai Chinese:** the shophouse family where home and workplace are one building.
- **Highland peoples** (Hmong, Akha, Lahu, Lisu, Mien, Lua): swidden giving way to cash crops and wages.
- **Moken, Moklen and Urak Lawoi:** a working year set by sea and monsoon.
- **Migrant workers from Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos:** most of the labor in some sectors; sample them apart.
- **Patani, for contrast only** (a culture-nation of its own): rubber and fishing villages whose day is framed by five daily prayers, against Satun's Thai-speaking Muslims inside Thailand. **Thai workers abroad, for contrast only:** farmhands in Israel and the undocumented *phi noi* in South Korea show what work means when it is done to send money home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on labor statistics and farm life; mixed on household routine; thin on sea peoples' and Mlabri work in their own words. No scope file exists yet and the author has not fixed post-war Thailand's lands, so the Virgo sheets are written for the present core lands; the other sheets do not repeat this.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and seventeenth-century French for the court era; Isan for the monthly customs; Sgaw Karen for swidden terms; Teochew for clan and trade records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal ceremonies of the farm year limit open comment; migrant and child labor; labor law itself goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** monastic discipline rules in the canonical scripture language, as a category; Thai-language manuals for monks stay usable.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the NSO time use tables; compare regions for paid, unpaid and sleep time.

4. Find the daily-activities Ratthaniyom in the Royal Gazette archive and press responses of 1941-1942.
5. Query the Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre database for "อาชีพ" crossed with Moken, Urak Lawoi and Mlabri.
