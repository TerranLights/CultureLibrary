# Thailand: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** salaried office work, factory estates and the paddy side by side.
- **Lanna north:** shared weir duty and tobacco, longan and handicraft villages.
- **Isan:** the work that leaves (construction, factories, taxis) and the farms left to the old.
- **Pak Tai south:** rubber and oil-palm smallholders; Buddhist and Muslim hands on one crew.
- **The west and the border** (Kanchanaburi, Tak, Mae Sot): factory zones staffed from Myanmar beside Karen farms.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** hereditary skills with elephants and silk looms.
- **Thai Chinese:** family firms, where work and kin duty share one ledger.
- **Hmong, Akha, Lahu and Mien uplands:** from opium and swidden to cabbage, coffee and day wages.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** diving and boat work now framed by park rules and tourism.
- **Migrant workers** (Burmese, Mon, Shan, Cambodian, Lao): much of the fishing and building labor; record their own terms.
- **Patani, as a contrast** (its own culture-nation): men from the Malay Muslim provinces cooking in Malaysia's tom yam restaurants, a separate route out of village work. **Thai workers overseas, as a contrast:** cooks and masseuses abroad whose national skill is itself the job.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on formal employment and labor history; mixed on unpaid and exchange labor; thin on how Moken, Mlabri and stateless highlanders name and judge work.
- **Languages needed:** Thai for surveys; Isan and Kham Mueang for village labor terms; Burmese and Mon to hear migrant crews; Old Thai script reading for the inscription.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced labor on fishing boats and its press coverage; raids on undocumented workers; royal texts limit open critique.
- **Held for review:** none; the jataka retelling named is a Thai royal work in Thai.
