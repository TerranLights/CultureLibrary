## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok court and official families:** service to the palace and ministries as a lineage's career.
- **Lanna north:** merit-labor for monks and temples as a northern counter-tradition to Bangkok's.
- **Isan:** village volunteers and temple committees; young people serving in Bangkok homes and shops.
- **Pak Tai south:** Thai-speaking Muslim and Buddhist congregations, each with its own unpaid offices.
- **Thai Chinese:** volunteer body-recovery crews and shrine committees, service framed as merit for the dead.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** monks as development leaders in poor provinces.
- **Mon and Karen of the western border:** temples and clinics serving refugees and cross-border kin.
- **Hmong, Lahu and Akha uplands:** clan duties and new state volunteer posts held by highlanders.
- **Moken:** how outside relief workers served them after the 2004 tsunami, as they tell it.
- **Migrant domestic and hotel workers:** the people doing much paid service now; separate them in any sample.
- **Patani, for contrast** (its own culture-nation): imams and pondok teachers serving under Islamic duty, with the state's rangers posted among them. **Thai temples abroad, for contrast:** lay volunteers keeping kitchens and Sunday classes going without a village behind them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on volunteering programs and civil service; mixed on domestic service; thin on highland and sea peoples' own words for serving.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, with court register for *Si Phaendin*; Kham Mueang for Kruba Siwichai's legends; Mon for Sangkhlaburi; Teochew for foundation histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** service to the monarchy and state volunteer schemes limit open comment; the 1976 violence; abuse of conscripts and domestic workers.
- **Held for review:** none; every pointer is Thai-made or about Thailand.
