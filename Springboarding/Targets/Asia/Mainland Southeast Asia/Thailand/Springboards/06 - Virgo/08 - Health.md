## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** private hospitals, medical tourism and gyms beside temple massage.
- **Central Plains:** herbal-medicine networks around Prachinburi and folk healers in rice villages.
- **Lanna north:** *mo mueang* and manuscript medicine.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Thai:** spirit-healing rites and diet-linked illness.
- **Pak Tai south:** Buddhist and Muslim healers in Songkhla and Satun; nora vows made for cures.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** the mamuat rite and elephant-related healing lore.
- **Thai Chinese:** Chinese medicine shops and the vegetarian festival.
- **Hmong, Akha, Lahu, Mien, Karen:** spirit healing beside clinics; access for those without citizenship.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** sea-people remedies set against a single island clinic.
- **Migrant workers:** cross-border clinics on the Tak border serve them; separate in any data.
- **Patani, for contrast** (its own culture-nation): Malay Muslim healers who cure by recitation, and vaccination debated in halal terms (to check). **Thai patients abroad, for contrast:** Thai migrants in Europe and East Asia who still send home for herbal medicines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on public health and traditional-medicine policy; mixed on folk rites; thin on highland and sea peoples' illness theories in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including Ayutthaya-period prose; Tham Lanna script; Isan and Phu Thai for rites; Northern Khmer; Hmong for shamans' terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spirit-healing rites may be closed to outsiders; mental illness and HIV carry stigma; stateless people's records need care.
- **Held for review:** foreign classical medical treatises that some Thai remedy texts draw on, as a category; the Thai-language texts named stay in.
