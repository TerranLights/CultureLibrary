# Thailand: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Teochew loanwords in Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Southern Thai; Malay of Satun; Hmong; Lua. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok families:** competition for school places, and paechia.
- **Lanna and the Lua of Mae Hong Son:** the daughter who keeps the parents' house; plot allotment by need.
- **Isan:** one equal fund per village, and what migrant siblings owe parents at home.
- **Pak Tai rubber districts:** owner-tapper splits, now often with workers from Myanmar.
- **Thai Chinese (Teochew, Hakka, Hainanese):** sons' and daughters' places in a family firm; pooled chae.
- **Holders of colored ID cards (Mon, Tai Yai, Karen, Akha):** which rules apply depends on the card.
- **Maniq of Trang and Satun:** how a foraging band shares food, little written in Thai.
- **Patani, comparison only:** faraid shares under the 1946 Act cover most Muslim estates there; Satun shows the same rule in a Thai-speaking setting. **Thai workers in Korea and Israel, comparison only:** what counts as a fair broker's cut against the wage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on inequality figures; mixed on inheritance customs and turn-taking; thin on Maniq and Mlabri sharing.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** questions on royal wealth fall under Article 112; draft avoidance by the connected.
