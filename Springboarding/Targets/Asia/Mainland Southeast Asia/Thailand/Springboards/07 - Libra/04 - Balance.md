## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** commutes and overtime against rest; the spa and wellness trade's home.
- **Central Plains rice districts:** too much water or too little, and whose fields take the overflow.
- **Lanna and Tai Lue of Nan:** fate-renewal rites for people, cities and rivers.
- **Isan and Phu Tai:** khwan-calling after journeys and illness; migrants' rhythm between city and village.
- **Pak Tai:** Nora ancestor rites; Suan Mokkh's teaching from Chaiya.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** ancestor offerings that keep the dead content.
- **Karen of the western hills:** forest and field weighed in their own terms.
- **Hmong:** souls that wander and are called home.
- **Thai Chinese and the Phuket Baba:** heating and cooling foods; tonic soups.
- **Patani, comparison only:** Friday sermons on the "middle community" (wasatiyyah) set beside Thai Buddhist middle-way talk, with Satun's mosques in between. **Thai massage and spa workers abroad, comparison only:** how "Thai balance" is sold to foreign clients and explained in other languages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on sufficiency-economy policy and eco-monks; mixed on khwan and ancestor rites; thin on Maniq, Mlabri and Lua ideas of balance with the forest.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Kham Mueang for fate-renewal texts; Northern Khmer for Saen Don Ta; Hmong for soul-calling; Teochew and Hokkien for herb-shop terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the sufficiency teaching is royal, so critique runs into Article 112; constitutional-court rulings on party dissolutions.
- **Held for review:** expositions of the middle way written in the canonical scripture language, as a category; Thai-language teachers above stay usable.
