# Thailand: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai (legal, bureaucratic and slang registers); Isan; Kham Mueang; Malay of Satun; Akha, Lahu and Tai Yai for citizenship cases. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok ministries:** exam merit against placed protégés.
- **Suphanburi and other Central Plains provinces:** voters rewarding a patron who favors home.
- **Lanna:** budgets and offices tied to national leaders from the north.
- **Isan:** where "double standard" became a mass slogan; trace who used it and when.
- **Pak Tai:** Muslim citizens of Satun and Songkhla before mostly Buddhist officials.
- **Akha, Lahu, Lisu and Tai Yai of the border districts:** citizenship decisions that depend on the officer.
- **Yuan (Vietnamese Thais) of Isan:** decades under special rules, then citizenship; how evenly offices now treat them.
- **Thai Chinese:** hiring within the family firm as open, accepted partiality.
- **Military and police:** promotions shaped by academy class-year networks.
- **Patani, comparison only:** checkpoints and special laws applied to one population, against a claim of neutral enforcement. **Overseas Thai voters, comparison only:** embassy counts that each camp has accepted or contested.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corruption and election disputes; mixed on everyday favoritism; thin on how Mlabri, Maniq or Moken meet officials.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including bureaucratic and slang registers; Akha, Lahu and Tai Yai for citizenship testimony; Malay for Satun.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** judges swear before the king and rule in his name, so critique meets Article 112; military promotions; named officials in corruption cases.
- **Held for review:** commentaries on the four biases in the canonical scripture language, as a category; the Thai ethics code stays usable.
