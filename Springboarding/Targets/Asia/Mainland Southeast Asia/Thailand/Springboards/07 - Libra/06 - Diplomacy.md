## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok's foreign service:** whether posts run in families, and the habits the ministry teaches.
- **Lanna:** a kingdom joined by marriage; Kham Mueang softeners in everyday talk.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** kin, trade and radio ties across the Mekong.
- **Vietnamese Thais (Yuan) of Nakhon Phanom, Sakon Nakhon and Udon:** a bridge, and at times a suspect, in Thai-Vietnamese relations.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Sisaket and Surin:** living beside a world-court border case.
- **Pak Tai:** Satun's place in the 1909 bargain; Hokkien and Baba brokers between Phuket and Penang.
- **Mon of Pak Kret, Phra Pradaeng and Sangkhlaburi:** refugees welcomed by Thai kings, whose chronicle taught statecraft.
- **Tai Yai and Karen of the border:** peoples between Thai and Myanmar armies and talks.
- **Hmong from Laos:** camp years at Ban Vinai and the Thai-Lao repatriation talks of 2009.
- **Thai Chinese:** chambers of commerce as go-betweens with Beijing and Taipei.
- **Patani, comparison only:** kin and trade ties with Kelantan turn Thai-Malaysian diplomacy into a village matter there. **Thai workers abroad, comparison only:** the 2023 release of Thai hostages from Gaza through talks with Iran and Qatar, with Thai Muslims as intermediaries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state diplomacy and the colonial era; mixed on everyday tact; thin on how highland and sea peoples deal with outside powers in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai court register; French and English for treaty-era papers; Mon for the chronicle's source; Hokkien for the Khaw papers; Vietnamese for the Isan Yuan; Malay for Satun.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal marriages and the monarchy's role in foreign affairs; the Preah Vihear dispute stirs nationalism; Hmong repatriation.
- **Held for review:** none; no diplomacy pointer came from outside Thai, Chinese, Mon or Western sources.
