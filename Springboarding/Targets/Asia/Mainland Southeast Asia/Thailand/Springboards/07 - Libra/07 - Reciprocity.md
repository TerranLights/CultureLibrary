*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains:** harvest exchange largely replaced by hired machines; local vote networks.
- **Lanna:** ao mue labor days, and offerings that return care to the ancestors.
- **Isan and Phu Tai:** what migrant children send home, and whose labor is lent back at planting.
- **Pak Tai:** festivals feeding the returning dead; savings groups run on promises.
- **Thai Chinese and the Phuket Baba:** red envelopes given and matched at weddings and New Year; association funds.
- **Hmong:** clan duties at funerals and the New Year.
- **Maniq:** forest goods for rice, on terms set by others.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** debt to traders handed down in a family.
- **Vietnamese Thais of Isan:** whether decades under movement limits made families lean on each other; a question to test.
- **Patani, comparison only:** zakat as a duty owed to God beside merit-making as a return to the giver; Satun's mosques show both near Buddhist neighbors. **Thai temples abroad, comparison only:** households taking turns to feed monks overseas, and what the community owes them back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on merit-making and remittances; mixed on labor exchange today; thin on Maniq and Mlabri exchange in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Kham Mueang and Isan for labor-exchange terms; Southern Thai for the festival; Teochew for association records; Hmong; Kensiw for Maniq.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** vote buying is a crime, so informants may hold back; debt bondage among sea peoples and migrants.
- **Held for review:** the canonical scripture-language sources of the Phra Malai story, as a category; the Thai tellings stay usable.
