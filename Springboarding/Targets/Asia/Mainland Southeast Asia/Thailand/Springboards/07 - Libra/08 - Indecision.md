*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Southern Thai; Malay of Satun; Teochew and Hokkien; Sgaw Karen; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** megaprojects and policies stalled for years; poll-watchers of the undecided.
- **Central Plains:** wedding and house-moving dates set by reckoning; Wanthong's story is set here.
- **Lanna:** omen-lifting at temples; Phrae's dam left open.
- **Isan:** Pak Mun's seasonal gate decisions; yes-or-no rites at village temples.
- **Pak Tai:** the canal question; Satun Muslims' prayer for guidance before a choice.
- **Thai Chinese and Phuket Baba:** almanac days for openings, weddings and burials; fortune sticks.
- **Karen:** reading signs before a move or a planting.
- **Hmong:** throwing horn halves to ask the spirits whether to go ahead.
- **Mon and Tai Yai:** monks asked to fix a day; whose word ends the waiting.
- **Patani, comparison only:** talks begun, paused and restarted with each new government: a public decision deferred, as Satun watches from next door. **Thais in Los Angeles, comparison only:** the open question of return, kept unanswered across decades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political abstention and delayed projects; mixed on everyday choosing; thin on minority divination as used for decisions.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Hokkien and Teochew for fortune slips; Kham Mueang and Isan for omen rites; Sgaw Karen; Hmong; Malay for Satun.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal dates are set by court reckoning and not open to comment; some Karen and Hmong rites are not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none; the deciding aids here are Thai, Chinese, Karen, Hmong or Muslim in source.
