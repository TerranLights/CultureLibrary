*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok offices:** meetings where no one says no; harassment left unreported.
- **Central Plains villages:** road deaths settled privately with a payment.
- **Lanna:** a chronicle of long vassalage, retold as patience or as shame.
- **Isan:** villagers who signed promises to stay out of politics after 2014.
- **Pak Tai (Songkhla, Satun, Phatthalung):** Buddhist and Muslim neighbors keeping disputes quiet.
- **Thai Chinese:** wives tolerating minor wives; families that hid Chinese names during assimilation drives.
- **Hmong:** patience urged on wives, and clan pressure to settle at home.
- **Mlabri:** withdrawal as outsiders described it; ask the Mlabri themselves.
- **Shan (Tai Yai) migrants of Chiang Mai:** payments at checkpoints to avoid arrest.
- **Moken on park islands:** accepting rules after the 2004 tsunami, or not.
- **Patani, comparison only:** families who accepted state compensation payments after deaths in place of trials, under laws that shield officials from suit. **Undocumented Thai workers in South Korea, comparison only:** injuries kept quiet to avoid deportation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political silencing and police bribery; mixed on family silence; thin on minority forms of giving way, beyond outsider accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Thai with political slang; Tham-script Tai Yuan; Teochew for family memoirs; Hmong; Shan; Mlabri.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** self-censorship around the monarchy is itself the subject and limits what sources say; domestic violence and campus abuse need survivor-centered methods.
- **Held for review:** none; the peace-at-a-cost pointers are all Thai, Chinese, minority or Western-language.
