# Thailand: The domain, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai (with the royal register for court deaths); Isan; Kham Mueang; Pak Tai; Northern Khmer; Teochew; Hmong; Satun Malay. **Concept in one line:** how a people faces death, power and what is hidden: death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance and secrets, with sex ways and death ways as the folkways and power ways as the runner-up.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** the royal funeral register and a commercial funeral trade.
- **Lanna north:** female-line spirit cults policing courtship; monk-funeral pyres pulled through town.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** the huean di wake and spirit-medium healing.
- **Pak Tai outside Patani:** the tenth-month festival; Muslim Thai burial in Satun.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of the lower northeast:** a separate yearly return of the dead.
- **Thai Chinese and the Phuket Baba-Peranakan:** kongtek, Qingming at clan graves, family-firm succession.
- **Karen, Mon, Tai Yai and Lua of the west:** separate funeral orders; Karen wakes where the young sing.
- **Hmong, Mien, Akha, Lahu:** soul-guiding funerals; clan rules on marriage.
- **Moken, Urak Lawoi, Maniq, Mlabri:** barely written death and sharing customs.
- **Yuan of Isan:** death-anniversary feasts at home altars.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** compare only for Malay Muslim same-day burial and arwah feasts, against the cremation calendar. **Thai temples in Europe and the US:** compare only for who makes a cremation's merit when monks and kin are far apart.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on funerals, ghosts and cremation volumes; mixed on money in marriage; thin on foragers and sea peoples. No scope file exists and the post-war nation's lands are still to be settled, so the Scorpio sheets are written for today's core lands; only this sheet says so.
- **Languages needed:** court-register Thai for royal deaths; Tham-script Lanna and Isan for palm-leaf funeral texts; Teochew for kongtek liturgy; Hmong for funeral chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal cremations sit under strict limits on comment; exhumed remains; sex work and HIV; some Hmong and Karen funeral chants are taught only to apprentices.
- **Held for review:** the scriptural-language sources of the three-worlds cosmology and of funeral chanting (category only).
