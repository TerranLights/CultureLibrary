# Thailand: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai (plain, polite, royal); Isan; Kham Mueang; Pak Tai; Vietnamese; Hmong; Satun Malay. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are studied here, funeral practice under the domain.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** hospital deaths, temple crematoria, the seventh-, fiftieth- and hundredth-day merit.
- **Lanna north:** banners for the dead; monk funerals as public events.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** rice parcels for the dead; fear of the violently dead.
- **Southern Thai provinces (Nakhon Si Thammarat to Satun):** Nora troupes calling back the ta yai; Muslim Thai graves in Trang.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** mourning tied to the ancestor month.
- **Sino-Thai and Baba families of Phuket and Bangkok:** the forty-nine days of rites and spring visits to hillside clan graves.
- **Mon of Ko Kret and Sangkhlaburi; Tai Yai of Mae Hong Son:** their own monk and lay funerals.
- **Karen; Hmong, Mien and Akha; Lua:** wake singing, soul-guiding and hill graveyards.
- **Yuan of Isan and Catholic Thais of Tha Rae:** giỗ feasts and All Souls at the cemetery.
- **Moken and Maniq:** almost unwritten; ask the communities' own advocates first.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** compare only for deaths in the conflict mourned in a martyr idiom, beside merit made for Buddhist dead nearby. **Thai migrant workers in Israel, Korea and Taiwan:** compare only for deaths abroad and the bodies flown home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** death counts and the Mae Nak cult are well covered; grief as people feel it surfaces mostly in memoirs and cremation books; forager and sea-people deaths are barely recorded.
- **Languages needed:** the three registers of Thai death words; Northern Thai script for anisong texts; Vietnamese for giỗ; White and Green Hmong for funeral chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a king's death and mourning year are guarded topics; suicide reporting rules; abortion; the AIDS dying.
- **Held for review:** the funeral-night recitation texts, which survive in a classical liturgical tongue (category only).
