# Thailand: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Tai Yai (Shan); Kuy; Hmong; Sgaw Karen; Chong. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead through reincarnation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** short monkhoods before marriage or after a death; time-slip dramas made here.
- **Lanna:** fate-extending rites for persons and cities.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** soul-calling feasts; Vessantara festivals each spring.
- **Tai Yai and Lua of Mae Hong Son:** the jeweled novices beside Lua spirit rites that converts left behind.
- **Kuy and Khmer-speaking Thais:** elephant ordinations; their own healing returns.
- **Karen, Lahu and Akha:** Christian conversion beside the older order, often inside one village.
- **Hmong and Mien:** soul calling, and a highland economy remade after opium.
- **Chong, Mon and Nyah Kur:** language revival on a small scale.
- **Gulf and Andaman coasts:** Phuket's Baba families' renewal feasts; Moken villages rebuilt after 2004.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** compare only for conversion to Islam at marriage and the idiom of returning to faith, beside Buddhist re-ordination. **Thai American sons who ordain for a summer in their parents' villages:** compare only for a short monkhood taken as a homecoming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on ordination and festivals; mixed on conversion; thin on how ex-prisoners and recovering users speak of new lives.
- **Languages needed:** Shan script for poi sang long songs; Kuy and Chong for their own voices; Sgaw Karen and Lahu for church records; Hmong for New Year chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison programs carry royal patronage; past-life claims about children; missions remain contested among highland peoples; transition stories must stay in the teller's own terms.
- **Held for review:** the thousand canonical stanzas that Thai Vessantara sermons quote, read in their scriptural original (category only).
