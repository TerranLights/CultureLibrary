## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** palace rites, both Sangha orders and the densest mix of faiths.
- **Lanna north:** city-pillar and life-extension rites; a century-old Protestant church in Chiang Mai.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** a year set by hit sip song; soul-calling as daily religion.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** Nora teacher spirits, phram families of Nakhon Si Thammarat and Phatthalung, Muslim and Buddhist neighbors in Satun and Songkhla.
- **Thai Chinese and Phuket Baba-Peranakan:** shrine associations and Mahayana halls beside temple merit.
- **Yuan of Isan and Catholic villages of Sakon Nakhon and Nakhon Phanom:** an Anamnikai and Catholic layer in the northeast.
- **Karen, Lahu, Akha, Hmong and Mien:** own high gods and spirit orders, and large Christian shares in some villages.
- **Mon and Tai Yai of the west:** Theravada in their own scripts and festivals.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy of Surin, Sisaket and Buriram:** Khmer-era sanctuaries such as Phanom Rung as living sacred places.
- **Lua, Mlabri, Maniq, Moken and Urak Lawoi:** thinnest record; ask their spokespeople first.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, as the one place where Islam rather than the temple sets the public week and calendar. **Thai temples in Los Angeles, Sydney and Europe:** comparison only, for a temple becoming school, kitchen and embassy at once.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Buddhism and spirit cults; mixed on Islam outside the south and on churches; thin on non-belief. The post-war nation's lands are not yet settled, so all Sagittarius sheets are written for the present core lands; this note appears here only.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Tham-script Lanna and Isan for temple texts; Teochew for shrine boards; Sgaw Karen, Hmong and Lahu for hill religions; Malay in Jawi for Satun mosques.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the monarchy's sacral role is a guarded subject (see Capricorn for the spirit of the law); spirit-medium and initiatory knowledge some lineages keep closed.
- **Held for review:** the scriptural-language canon behind Thai chanting and monks' exams; South Asian-heritage communities' houses of worship in Bangkok (categories only).
