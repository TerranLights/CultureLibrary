## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok readers:** LINE-group rumor, state fact-checks and partisan television, each with its own believers.
- **Lanna north:** a separate chronicle tradition and a memory of autonomy that complicates the national story.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** the relic chronicles and naga stories; distrust of Bangkok's account of 1901-02 and the 1960s-70s.
- **Pak Tai outside Patani:** Nora origin tales and the White-Blooded Lady legend of Phatthalung as local truth.
- **Thai Chinese:** clan books and shrine records as family truth, beside Thai schoolbooks.
- **Akha, Lahu and Mien:** recited genealogies and ritual texts as proof of who belongs where.
- **Karen:** a tradition of a lost book of knowledge, and what literacy is said to restore.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** whose readings of tide and wave outsiders tested against instruments after 2004.
- **Mon communities of the lower Chao Phraya:** Mon chronicles of Hongsawadi read beside Thai war annals.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of the lower northeast:** whose heritage the Khmer-era temples are, an argument sharpened by the Preah Vihear dispute.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for an opposed history of the 1786 and 1902 conquests and of Tak Bai 2004. **Thai Facebook groups abroad:** comparison only, for rumor and counter-rumor posted from outside the reach of domestic rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on contested history and debunking; mixed on science trust; thin on how uplanders weigh evidence.
- **Languages needed:** archaic Thai for the chronicles; Tham-script Lanna and Lao for the northern and Mekong annals; Akha and Sgaw Karen through community interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** history touching the monarchy and the 1976 killings is risky to state openly; truth commissions and courts are Libra's field.
- **Held for review:** the classical scriptural treatise on the two truths that Thai preachers adapt (category only).
