## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** the top campuses, the scholarship elite and families paying for degrees abroad.
- **Lanna north:** Chiang Mai as a university town; Tham-script learning revived beside it.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** first-generation students, Rajabhat campuses and the trip to Khon Kaen.
- **Southern Thai (Songkhla, Satun, Nakhon Si Thammarat):** state universities beside pondok and Arab-world study.
- **Sino-Thai families and Phuket's Straits-linked Baba:** Chinese-language learning reclaimed after decades of limits.
- **Monks and novices from poor provinces:** the monastic route to a bachelor's degree.
- **Karen, Hmong, Akha, Lahu and Mien:** quota students and the first graduates of their villages.
- **Mon, Tai Yai and Tai Lue:** temple classes in a home script as a parallel ladder of learning, outside state credentials.
- **Northern Khmer and Kuy speakers of the lower northeast:** children who meet Thai as a second language, and their path to a degree.
- **Moken, Urak Lawoi, Maniq and Mlabri:** very few reach higher learning; ask their schools and advocates.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for pondok and Islamic university learning in Malay as a rival path to the Thai degree. **Thai students in Australia, the UK and China:** comparison only, for a degree abroad turned into rank at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on enrollment, admissions and elite universities; mixed on monastic and Islamic higher learning; thin on uplanders and sea peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Ayutthaya Thai for *Chindamani*; Tham script for Lanna classes; Arabic and Malay for Muslim study; Chinese for Sino-Thai programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** academic freedom touching the monarchy is risky; curriculum fights over history.
- **Held for review:** the scriptural-language grades that rank a monk's learning, set beside state degrees (category only).
