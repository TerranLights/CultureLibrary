# Thailand: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Isan; Satun Malay; Arabic (for Muslim reform debates); Lahu; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok:** Buddhist protection groups, megachurches and reformist mosques within a few kilometers.
- **Lanna north:** khruba devotion as an alternative to Bangkok orthodoxy; mission churches among the hill peoples.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** the memory of holy-man risings; forest monks' strict discipline admired by the urban rich.
- **Muslim villages of Satun, Trang and Krabi:** reformers and custom-keepers contesting mawlid, tomb visits and the village mosque.
- **Thai Chinese:** strict vegetarian sects and lay Buddhist halls, compared with temple practice.
- **Lahu, Akha and Karen:** prophetic movements and strict mission churches side by side.
- **Hmong:** Christian converts' break with clan rites, and the tension it brings to lineages.
- **Mlabri and Maniq:** forager bands whose first sustained teachers of a scripture were literalist missionaries.
- **Yuan of Isan:** Catholic tradition beside evangelical arrivals.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for scripture-first reform among Malay Muslims read by the state through a security lens. **Thai Buddhist temples and Dhammakaya branches abroad:** comparison only, for purity and mission projected outward.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Buddhist nationalism, the Dhammakaya dispute and the 1901-02 risings; mixed on Muslim reform outside Patani; thin on Mlabri and Hmong converts' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Isan; Malay and Arabic for reform debates; Lahu for prophetic movements; Hmong for church-clan disputes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "extremist" labels can endanger people; avoid naming private leavers; state religion and Sangha law belong to Capricorn.
