# Thailand: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Tai Yai; Pak Tai; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** where ministries and army headquarters sit, and where open defiance (2010, 2020) was staged.
- **Lanna north and the Tai Lue of Nan:** khruba loyalty that once ran against the Bangkok Sangha's ranks.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and the Phu Tai:** men-of-merit uprisings and the thao cham's spirit authority beside the kamnan.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** party and patron loyalty in Trang and Nakhon Si Thammarat; imams in Satun.
- **Thai Chinese:** the chao sua and clan-association elders as private authority.
- **Tai Yai and Mon of the west:** princely and monastic titles kept from across the border.
- **Hmong, Karen, Akha and Lahu:** clan elders, village priests and Christian pastors against appointed headmen.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** lay ritual masters and elephant masters whose word holds in their own sphere.
- **Maniq and Mlabri:** band life with no fixed chief, a test of what authority means at all.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for Malay religious teachers set against Bangkok-appointed officials, and armed refusal of the state's claim.
- **Thai temples abroad (Los Angeles, Sydney):** comparison only, for lay boards and abbots contesting who decides.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state, army and Sangha; mixed on workplaces and households; thin on hill peoples' own offices in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai including royal and military registers; Tham-script Lanna for khruba biographies; Tai Yai for Mae Hong Son temples; Hmong for clan meetings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** any study of the throne's authority is legally restricted; activists and their families face prosecution, so protect sources. Medium lineages may keep séance knowledge private.
- **Held for review:** the classical-language Vinaya behind monastic chains of command (category only).
