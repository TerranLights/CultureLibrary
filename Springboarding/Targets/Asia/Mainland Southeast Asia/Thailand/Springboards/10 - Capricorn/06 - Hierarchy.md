# Thailand: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai (including the royal register); Kham Mueang; Isan; Kuy; Iu Mien; Teochew. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** palace, ministries and old titled families, where the royal register is learned.
- **Lanna north:** a second princely aristocracy, and the Lua remembered in rite as the land's first owners.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and the Phu Tai:** towns once ranked by Bangkok as tributaries; check how the old aya si families fare.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** Nakhon Si Thammarat's old governing families beside the Nora master's ranks.
- **Thai Chinese:** dialect groups ranked by trade and wealth (Teochew, Hakka, Hainanese, Hokkien), and seniority within clan halls.
- **Bangkok and Ayutthaya Muslims:** a noble lineage at court beside ordinary mosque communities.
- **Kuy and Khmer-speaking Thais:** guild ranks around elephants; check how lowland neighbors rank them and how they rank themselves.
- **Mien, Hmong, Akha and Lahu:** ritual grades within; citizenship rungs from outside.
- **Mon, Tai Yai and Tai Lue:** princely or noble pasts and a present as border minorities.
- **Mlabri and Maniq:** how state paperwork places them, against the little formal rank reported among themselves.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for an old Malay sultanate order and its descendants under Thai rank.
- **Thai wives and workers in Europe:** comparison only, for how migrants are ranked abroad and rank themselves on return.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on court, titles and language; mixed on class; thin on hill peoples' own rankings.
- **Languages needed:** Thai with royal vocabulary; Tham script for Lanna chronicles; Isan and Lao for town histories; Kuy for the elephant guild; Mien for ordination texts; Teochew for clan-hall boards.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the throne's place at the summit is legally protected; stateless people risk exposure if named; Mien and Kuy ritual grades have restricted knowledge.
- **Held for review:** none tied to this concept; South Asian-heritage communities' place in Bangkok's commercial ranks would be filed here as a category if needed.
