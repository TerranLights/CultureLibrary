# Thailand: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Pak Tai; Teochew; Hmong and Karen (for quota students). **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** famous schools, scholarships and donations meet head-on here.
- **Lanna north:** poi luang sponsorship and the region's university quotas.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** temple schooling and village festivals as two different ladders of merit.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** Nora and shadow-play contests, where audiences judge skill.
- **Thai Chinese:** clan and dialect-group networks in business, and the donation-for-entry custom's Teochew name.
- **Hmong, Karen, Lahu and Akha:** quota students, and the stigma or pride attached to the quota.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** check how exam results in Surin, Sisaket and Buriram are explained by families and by officials.
- **Mon and Tai Yai:** monastic exams in their own temples beside Thai national ones.
- **Thai Vietnamese (Yuan of Isan):** check whether nationality rules kept them from state posts, and what earned standing meant meanwhile.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for special admission and civil-service quotas aimed at Malay Muslims, and how they are argued over.
- **Thai scholarship students in Britain and the United States:** comparison only, for state-funded talent sent abroad on bond to return.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on exams and scandals; mixed on festivals; thin on hill peoples' own views of earned standing.
- **Languages needed:** Thai including police and campus slang; Kham Mueang and Isan for festival talk; Teochew for school and clan history; Hmong and Karen for quota students' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal scholarships carry palace links, so critique is guarded; police-posting allegations carry defamation risk.
- **Held for review:** the classical-language monastic exam grades and their texts (category only).
