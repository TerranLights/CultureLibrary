# Thailand: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Pak Tai; Teochew and Hokkien; Mon; Hmong; Moken. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** sidewalk clearing, eviction and complaint data: order as urban policy.
- **Lanna north:** guardian spirits of village and city, and the Mon-rooted phi meng lineages.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** sanctioned ribaldry at rocket festivals, and the memory of state resettlement.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** processions in Surat Thani and Nakhon Si Thammarat; Hat Yai's border-town nightlife and its policing.
- **Thai Chinese and Phuket Baba-Peranakan:** ghost-feeding rites as order between the living and the dead.
- **Mon of Pathum Thani and Samut Prakan:** their own temple calendar beside the Thai one; check how lineage spirits are kept.
- **Hmong, Karen and Lahu:** village peace-making by elders, tested against lowland talk of the highlands as disorderly.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** a sea-season order set against park and village plans.
- **Maniq of the Banthat range:** forest movement that state settlement programs aim to fix in place.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for checkpoints and curfews as daily order in a conflict zone.
- **Thai Town, Los Angeles:** comparison only, for Songkran's street festival held under a foreign city's permits and police.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on urban policy, festivals and spirit cults; mixed on violence by region; thin on forest and sea peoples' own sense of order.
- **Languages needed:** Thai including official register; Kham Mueang for spirit rites; Isan for festival songs; Teochew and Hokkien for shrine committees; Mon; Hmong; Moken and Maniq only with community consent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coups have been announced in the language of order, so political speech on them carries risk; evicted communities may need anonymity; phi meng possession rites are lineage-restricted.
- **Held for review:** the classical-language cosmology behind the old court's sense of order, if pursued (category only).
