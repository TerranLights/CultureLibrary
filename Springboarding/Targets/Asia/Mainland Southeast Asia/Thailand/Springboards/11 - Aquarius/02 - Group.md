# Thailand: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Hokkien and Teochew; Hmong; Tai Dam. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** academy and school class-years as lifelong factions; color camps in the streets.
- **Lanna north:** matrilineal spirit groups that cut across villages.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** red-shirt villages and the migrant crowds they sent to Bangkok.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** party loyalty that runs across districts and families.
- **Tai Dam of the west and Phu Tai of the northeast:** captive-descended peoples who kept their own clan or town groupings.
- **Hmong, Mien and Akha:** clans and lineages as the first unit of decision.
- **Thai Chinese and Phuket's Baba-Peranakan:** surname and dialect associations; the Phuket shrine societies.
- **Maniq and Mlabri:** bands of a few families.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, where Malay ethnicity and Islam define a "we" set against the Thai state.
- **Thai workers in Israel and Taiwan:** comparison only, for crews formed by home province and village abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political camps and patronage groups; mixed on kin groups of the north and the hills; thin on Southern Thai party machines as groups.
- **Languages needed:** Thai; Hokkien and Teochew for association records; Hmong for clan narratives; Tai Dam for clan rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the color camps, the Village Scouts and the 1976 violence are politically charged; some clan rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** monastic disciplinary rules on schism and the unity of the order, in their original learned language (category only).
