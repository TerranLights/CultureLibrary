# Thailand: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Hokkien; Tai Yai. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** the court, ministries and universities where most reform programs were written.
- **Lanna north:** princely rule ended by provincial reform; northern monks against Sangha centralization.
- **Tai Yai (Shan) of Phrae and Mae Hong Son:** the 1902 rising as a reply to reform from Bangkok.
- **Isan, Lao Isan and Phu Tai:** reform felt as tax and conscription; villagers' research as reform from below.
- **Phuket's and Trang's Baba-Peranakan:** a reforming governor from their own Hokkien networks.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** Chaiya, home of Buddhadasa's forest monastery, as a southern source of religious reform.
- **Hmong, Karen, Akha and Lahu:** targets of crop and settlement programs.
- **Mon:** the Mon ordination line behind the reformed order, *check*.
- **Thai Chinese:** reformist and revolutionary currents from China in the Bangkok Chinese press.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for Islamic reformist teaching that reached the south by its own routes.
- **Thai students in 1920s Paris:** comparison only, for a reform party first planned abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on reform from the throne and the state; mixed on reform from below; thin on how hill and sea peoples judged the programs aimed at them.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, including old spelling for 19th-century essays; Hokkien for southern Sino-Thai records; Hmong and Tai Yai for those reformed from outside.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** proposals touching the monarchy carry heavy legal risk; constitutional and legal reform itself goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the canonical monastic texts in their original learned language that Mongkut's reformers took as their standard (category only).
