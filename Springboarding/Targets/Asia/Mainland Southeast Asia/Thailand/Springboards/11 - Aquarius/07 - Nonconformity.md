*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** indie scenes and the strict schools across the street from them.
- **Lanna north:** loyalty to monks who broke central rules.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** the Toad King and bawdy rocket-festival floats as permitted defiance.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** shadow-play clowns; how Phuket's Baba-Peranakan dress and customs fared under the 1940s Thai-dress mandates, *check*.
- **Kathoey, tom and di communities:** visible and tested in different ways in city and village.
- **Hmong and Karen Christians:** converts who left clan or village rites, and the friction that followed.
- **Mlabri and Maniq:** forest peoples whose way of life is itself the departure in state eyes.
- **Thai Chinese:** Bangkok sons who chose art or politics over the family firm, *check*.
- **Tai Yai and Lua of Mae Hong Son:** spirit and ordination customs that central Sangha and school officials have tried to standardize, *check*.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for refusal of Thai dress and language framed as faithfulness, not rebellion.
- **Young Thais in Berlin and London:** comparison only, for the artists and activists who left to differ freely.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on *kathoey* and youth subcultures; mixed on religious dissent; thin on the Mlabri's own view of being called deviant.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, with youth slang; Isan for the Toad King tale; Pak Tai for shadow-play scripts; Mlabri for its speakers' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dissent near the monarchy is legally dangerous; some Sak Yant rites and Lanna lineages keep knowledge closed; gender identity needs respectful terms.
- **Held for review:** monastic disciplinary texts in their original learned language cited against women's ordination (category only).
