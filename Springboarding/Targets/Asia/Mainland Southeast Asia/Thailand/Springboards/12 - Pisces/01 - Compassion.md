# Thailand: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Kham Mueang; Isan; Teochew; Mon; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** city outreach and slum foundations against temple alms.
- **Lanna north:** palm-leaf reward texts and Shan temples serving migrant laborers.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** the Vessantara festival and what giving costs a poor village.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** coastal relief after the 2004 tsunami, Buddhist and Muslim helpers side by side.
- **Mon and Karen of Kanchanaburi:** border temples where compassion is given to one's own refugees.
- **Thai Chinese:** Guanyin devotion and benevolent halls.
- **Moken and Urak Lawoi:** how outside pity reached sea peoples who share within the boat.
- **Hmong:** pity received in camps and how it is remembered.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for how *zakat* routes alms through the mosque where Thai Buddhists route them through the temple.
- **Thai temples abroad (for example in Los Angeles or Sydney):** comparison only, for whether they feed strangers or only their own members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundations and merit giving; mixed for public opinion on the poor; thin for minority words for pity.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Isan; Teochew for benevolent-hall records; Mon and Shan for border-temple sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** royal patronage of charities limits open critique; refugee and prisoner stories need consent.
- **Held for review:** the scriptural-language source of the four abodes taught in Thai schools (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Northern Thai manuscripts library for "อานิสงส์" titles on giving.
2. Search ThaiJO for "สงสาร" with "คนไร้บ้าน" (the homeless) and read how articles frame them.
3. Pull the World Giving Index Thailand rows across years.
4. Search Thai news archives for "วัดวังก์วิเวการาม" with "ผู้ลี้ภัย" (refugees).
5. Search "บุญผะเหวด" with "ร้อยเอ็ด" for festival records and sermons.
