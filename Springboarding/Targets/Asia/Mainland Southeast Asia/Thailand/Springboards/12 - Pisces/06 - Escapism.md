# Thailand: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Pak Tai; Isan; Kham Mueang; White Hmong; Lahu; Teochew. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** malls, series, betting on football, and the weekend trip.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** rice-wine drinking at village feasts and migrant drinking in the city.
- **Lanna north and Pai:** backpacker havens set among Shan, Lisu and Lahu villages.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** kratom culture among rubber tappers and fishermen.
- **Hmong, Lahu and Akha:** the opium past, the crop-replacement years and what elders say of both.
- **Tai Yai (Shan):** border towns on the drug routes and the people living beside them.
- **Thai Chinese:** the dens, the gambling halls and the state's turn against both.
- **Moken:** drinking and loss after settlement, in the people's own accounts.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for how a Muslim ban on alcohol shifts escape toward kratom and other things.
- **Thai migrant workers in South Korea and Israel:** comparison only, for which escapes travel with them and which they meet abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for drugs, drink and gambling surveys; mixed for media fandom; thin for minorities' own accounts of escape.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Pak Tai for kratom; Isan for drinking songs; Hmong and Lahu for the opium years; Chinese-language sources for the dens.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug policy is politically charged (the 2003 drug war); drug-user testimony needs anonymity; royal crop-replacement projects are written about cautiously.
- **Held for review:** none; every escape named here is local.
