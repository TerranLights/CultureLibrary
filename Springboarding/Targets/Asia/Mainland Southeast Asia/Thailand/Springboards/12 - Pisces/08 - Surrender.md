# Thailand: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan; Kham Mueang; Mon; White Hmong; Teochew. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Plains:** the national story of the 1767 fall and recovery.
- **Lanna north:** release rites with lanterns and life-extension rites as the opposite of yielding.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** returnees from the insurgency; temple hospice.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** the 1941 ceasefire at Chumphon and its local memory.
- **Mon:** a people who lost a homeland and settled in Siam.
- **Hmong of Nan and Phetchabun:** insurgent years and the terms of coming home.
- **Thai Chinese:** paper offerings, and the many Sino-Thai cadres who left the jungle.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais of Surin and Sisaket:** Khmer Rouge-era flights and returns along the border, as remembered locally.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for the "bringing people home" scheme that asks southern militants to give themselves up, and what its terms promise.
- **Older Thais abroad who return to die at home:** comparison only, for what is given up and what is held on to at the end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sermons on letting go and for insurgency memoirs; mixed for dying; thin for minority words for giving up.
- **Languages needed:** Thai and Isan; Mon; Hmong; Chinese for the Sino-Thai cadres' memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** insurgency history and 1976 are politically delicate; hospice choices involve private medical matters.
- **Held for review:** the original-language teachings on impermanence quoted at Thai deathbeds (category only).
