# Thailand: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Thailand, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Central Thai; Isan; Kham Mueang; Pak Tai; Northern Khmer; Teochew; Maniq. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bangkok and the Central Plains:** flood fear, crime fear and the horror-film audience.
- **Isan and Lao Isan:** *phi pop* accusations and their victims.
- **Lanna north:** *phi ka* suspicion and courtship.
- **Southern Thai outside Patani:** lullabies with warnings, and tsunami memory on the Andaman coast.
- **Khmer-speaking Thais and Kuy:** *krasue* lore and its regional names.
- **Thai Chinese and Phuket Baba-Peranakan:** hungry-ghost months and their rites.
- **Myanmar, Shan and Karen migrant workers:** feared as carriers of disease in 2020, and their own fear of raids.
- **Maniq and Mlabri:** forest peoples' fears of storms and of outsiders.
- **Moken:** life with the sea after 2004.
- **Patani (separate culture-nation):** comparison only, for how daily life beside bombings and checkpoints shapes what a neighboring culture fears.
- **Thai students in Australia and the United States:** comparison only, for which home fears (ghosts, the dark) fade or stay abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ghost lore and horror; mixed for anxiety measures; thin for the fears of the smallest peoples in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Thai, Isan, Kham Mueang and Pak Tai; Northern Khmer for *krasue* lore; Teochew for rites; Burmese for migrant accounts; Maniq.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *phi pop* and *phi ka* accusations harm living families, so never name them; the 1976 massacre remains politically raw.
- **Held for review:** none; the fear figures named belong to Thai, Lanna, Isan, Khmer-Thai and Sino-Thai lore.
