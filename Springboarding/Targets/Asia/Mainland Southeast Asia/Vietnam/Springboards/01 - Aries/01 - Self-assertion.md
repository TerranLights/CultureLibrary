# Vietnam: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; chữ Nôm for older verse); Cantonese for Hoa sources; Khmer, Cham, Hmong and Central Highlands languages for local fieldwork. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kinh of Quảng Nam:** a folk reputation for arguing back exists in sayings; test it, never adopt it.
- **Kinh of Hà Nội against Saigon:** two urban politeness codes; compare how a refusal is worded.
- **Women traders in the markets:** assertion in commerce may differ from assertion at home.
- **Migrant factory workers in Bình Dương and Đồng Nai:** collective voice away from home villages.
- **Hoa congregations in Chợ Lớn:** voice inside guild halls (hội quán) against voice toward outsiders.
- **Khmer Krom:** who speaks for the community; the role of temple elders.
- **Cham Awal and Ahier villages:** clergy and elders as spokesmen; women in matrilineal households.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** matrilineal houses; who speaks for the longhouse.
- **Hmong and Dao clans:** elders' and clan heads' speaking rights.
- **Young online Vietnamese:** call-out posts and reviews as new stages.
- Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only: how speaking up shifts in another country's schools and workplaces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, folk verse and Vietnamese educational writing; mixed in surveys; thin for minorities outside state ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Nôm for Hồ Xuân Hương's originals; Cantonese for Hoa materials; minority languages for village speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public speech toward the state and the Party is a political matter; the 1956 affair and later dissident writing remain sensitive, and sources may be partial. Formal complaints and petitions to authorities as procedures are law: point to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Vietnamese journals for "khẳng định bản thân" with "học sinh" and "người lao động" to find school and workplace studies.
2. Read a quan họ or hò đối đáp collection with notes on how the sides answer one another.
3. Pull PAPI's provincial tables on citizen contact with officials.
4. Read *Đoạn tuyệt* beside Ninh (2002) for two eras of speaking up.
5. Search "nhà rông" with "già làng" (village elder) for highland speaking order.
