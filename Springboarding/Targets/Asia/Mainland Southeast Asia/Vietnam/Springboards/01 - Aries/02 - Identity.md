# Vietnam: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; chữ Hán for early texts); Khmer, Cham, Hmong, Tày, Thái for each people's own self-naming; Cantonese and Hokkien for Hoa congregations; French for colonial categories. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northerners who moved south in 1954 and their children:** an identity carried and reshaped across a border.
- **Huế and the central coast:** an old capital's self-image beside the Red River and Mekong poles.
- **Mekong Delta Kinh:** a frontier-formed regional identity.
- **Hoa:** Minh Hương descendants against later dialect congregations; Chợ Lớn against Hải Phòng and the north.
- **Khmer Krom:** a people whose kin state lies across the border.
- **Cham Ahier, Awal and Cham Muslims of An Giang:** one ethnonym, three religious identities.
- **Tày and Nùng:** groups with their own Nôm-type script and ritual verse.
- **Mường:** a people the state ties closely to Kinh origins; test how Mường speak of it.
- **Hmong and Dao:** clan networks that cross state borders.
- **Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, Xơ Đăng, Cơ Tu, Bru-Vân Kiều:** highlanders named by outsiders; recover the self-names.
- **Catholics, Cao Đài, Hòa Hảo:** faith as a primary identity.
- Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only: the Việt kiều label and diaspora flag disputes show identity argued from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national and Kinh regional identity; mixed for Hoa and Cham; thin and filtered for highland peoples in state sources.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for early texts; Khmer, Cham, Thái and Hmong for self-naming; French for colonial categories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic classification, highland autonomy movements, Khmer Krom activism abroad and the 1975 to 1979 departures of Hoa are political; identity labels such as "Thượng" and "Montagnard" carry history. Citizenship and nationality rules are law: point to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Pelley (2002) on the official grouping of peoples, then Salemink (2003) on the highland labels.
2. Search Vietnamese journals for "bản sắc dân tộc" with each minority name in turn.
3. Read *Bình Ngô đại cáo* beside Kelley (2005) for the pre-modern frame.
4. Search for "Bắc 54" and "người Bắc di cư" for regional identity across the 1954 move.
5. Look up Inrasara's work and VTV5 Tây Nam Bộ programming for Cham and Khmer Krom self-presentation.
