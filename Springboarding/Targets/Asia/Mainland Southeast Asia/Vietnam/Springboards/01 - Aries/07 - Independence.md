## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural elders of the Red River Delta against urban retirees:** reliance on sons, daughters or pensions.
- **Labor-export provinces (Nghệ An, Hà Tĩnh, Thanh Hóa):** household independence financed from abroad.
- **Mekong Delta families with migrants in Saigon:** internal remittances.
- **Hmong and Dao in the far north:** subsistence farming and its limits.
- **Mnông, Ê Đê and Xơ Đăng:** land loss and food self-sufficiency.
- **Khmer Krom:** temple welfare as community independence.
- **Hoa:** congregation-based mutual aid.
- **Cham Muslims of An Giang:** community alms and trade networks.
- **Families of war invalids and martyrs:** state support framed as honor, not dependence.
- **Disabled people:** independence claimed through self-help groups.
- **Young adults in Hà Nội and Saigon:** moving out against living at home.
- Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only: the senders of remittances, whose earnings change how dependent households at home are.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household economics and national independence rhetoric; mixed for elder care; thin for minority subsistence outside policy studies.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for early sovereignty texts; French for colonial geography; Hoa varieties and Khmer for community welfare.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national-independence history is tied to official narratives; independent charity and religious welfare face oversight; highland land loss is sensitive. Welfare entitlements as rules are law: point to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
