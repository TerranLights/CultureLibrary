*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; older records in chữ Hán and chữ Nôm); Tày, Nùng, Thái, Mường, Hmong, Dao, Khmer, Cham, Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar and Hoa Chinese varieties; French for colonial records. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how is it held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta Kinh villages:** long-settled rice villages with communal-land history; a base case for scarcity and saving.
- **North Central coast (Thanh Hóa to Hà Tĩnh):** poor soils and outmigration; test how they shape what households keep.
- **Huế:** court cuisine and ritual; elite taste as its own standard of value.
- **Mekong delta Kinh:** frontier settlement and rice surplus; abundance against northern scarcity.
- **Ho Chi Minh City and the Southeast:** malls, consumer goods and migrant workers' budgets.
- **Khmer Krom:** pagoda-centered giving and their own festival calendar.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon, Awal/Bani and Cham Muslims of An Giang:** matrilineal households and separate food rules.
- **Hoa of Chợ Lớn and Hội An:** assembly halls as keepers of collective wealth.
- **Northern upland peoples (Tày, Nùng, Thái, Mường, Hmong, Dao, Hà Nhì, Lô Lô):** silver, cloth and livestock as stores of worth.
- **Central Highlands peoples (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, Sê Đăng, Mnông, K'Ho, Raglai):** gongs, jars and longhouse holdings.
- **Catholic, Caodaist, Hòa Hảo and Protestant communities:** religious teaching on spending and giving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Kinh majority; mixed for the large minorities; thin in English for smaller highland groups, where Vietnamese-language ethnology is the route.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; French for colonial ethnography; chữ Hán and chữ Nôm for pre-modern texts; minority languages for oral material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform (1953-1956), the post-1975 takeover of private commerce and the 1980s currency changes; highland land conflicts; religious minorities. Property and inheritance law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Scope:** Vietnam's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; these sheets are written for the core lands.
- **Held for review:** none.
