# Vietnam: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (chữ Hán for pre-modern registers); Thái, Mường, Ê Đê, Jarai, Hmong, Khmer and Cham for customary tenure; French for colonial cadastres. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta villages:** communal-field heritage and the many tiny plots handed out after collectives ended.
- **Mekong delta:** landlord and tenant history, then the 1970 southern program; test how claims rest on clearing versus title.
- **HCMC and Hanoi peripheries:** farmland turned to city; compensation disputes as the sharpest test of a claim.
- **Mường:** hereditary lords who once allotted land; the shift to household rights.
- **Thái:** chiefdom field allotment against today's certificates.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** land held through women's clans; state farms and settlers on the same ground.
- **Hmong and Dao:** shifting fields against fixed plots under the settlement program.
- **Khmer Krom:** household and pagoda land; memories of land loss.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani:** land passing through the female line; temple and cemetery land.
- **Hoa:** shop and business ownership seized and rebuilt.
- **Catholic parishes:** church land claims, some publicly disputed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land (surveys, archives, studies); thin for ownership of songs, names and designs, where only ethnology helps.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for land registers; French for cadastres; minority languages for customary terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1950s land reform; land petitioners (dân oan) and forced evictions; Central Highlands land unrest; church property. What the land law and constitution say is Libra's; the spirit of the law is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** none.
