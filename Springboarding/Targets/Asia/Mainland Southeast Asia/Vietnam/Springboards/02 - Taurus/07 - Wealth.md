*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hanoi old merchant families and the new đại gia:** inherited trade standing against reform-era fortunes.
- **HCMC:** the largest private fortunes; Chợ Lớn's Hoa commerce.
- **Mekong delta farmers:** rice and orchard wealth; land concentration; houses built with remittances.
- **North Central labor migrants (Nghệ An, Hà Tĩnh):** work in Japan, Taiwan and Korea; wealth shown in new houses.
- **Central Highlands:** coffee-boom fortunes of settlers against highland wealth reckoned in jars, gongs and buffalo.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** riches held by the matrilineal house.
- **Hmong:** silver and livestock as riches; the opium past.
- **Thái:** former chiefly families and their holdings.
- **Khmer Krom:** wealth given to the pagoda for merit.
- **Cham Muslims of An Giang:** cross-border cloth and trade networks.
- **Hòa Hảo and Catholic communities:** teaching on wealth and charity.
- **Officials against private entrepreneurs:** wealth from office, as the anti-corruption campaign frames it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rankings, poverty and prosperity rites; thin on the actual holdings of the very rich (opaque) and on minority wealth in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for legends; French for highland ethnography; Ê Đê, Hmong and Khmer for minority terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the anti-corruption campaign and officials' wealth; the 1978 takeover of private commerce; the opium economy; fraud victims. Wealth and inheritance law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
