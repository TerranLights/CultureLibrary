# Vietnam: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (chữ Nôm for classical poems); Thái, Hmong, Ê Đê, Cham, Khmer and Cantonese (Hoa). **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta patrilineal households:** sons' wives living under a mother-in-law; the classic setting of family possessiveness.
- **Craft villages (Bát Tràng, Vạn Phúc, Đồng Kỵ):** knowledge kept inside the lineage.
- **HCMC and Hanoi street economies:** sidewalk spots and parking claimed by use.
- **Mekong delta households:** test whether looser in-law residence eases a parent's hold.
- **Hmong:** clan claims in marriage, including capture marriage; treat as questions from Hmong sources.
- **Thái:** parents' power over a daughter's match, the theme of the Thái long poem.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** the husband moves into the wife's longhouse; who then claims whom.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani:** matrilocal marriage; jealousy in a household run by women.
- **Khmer Krom:** test how pagoda-centered sharing shapes scorn for stinginess.
- **Hoa family firms:** control over heirs and the family name.
- **Coastal fishers:** guarding grounds and gear.
- **Young urban couples:** phone and messaging-app checking.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on partner control (two national surveys) and media; thin on possessiveness over objects and territory, which must be built from news and ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Nôm for Hồ Xuân Hương; Thái; Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence; Hmong marriage customs (avoid outsiders' framing); viral attack videos involve private people. Inheritance disputes and property law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
