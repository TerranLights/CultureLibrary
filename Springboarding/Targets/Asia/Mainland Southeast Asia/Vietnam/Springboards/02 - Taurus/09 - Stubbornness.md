## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Central (Nghệ An, Hà Tĩnh):** labels of a "contrary" regional character circulate; record them as stereotypes and test them.
- **Red River delta villages:** long rivalries over precedence and village boundaries, and how they end.
- **Hanoi old families:** holding to standards through decades of upheaval.
- **HCMC traders:** test whether commerce rewards bending over holding firm.
- **Hòa Hảo and Cao Đài adherents:** groups that keep to their own religious bodies.
- **Catholic parishes:** long vigils in property disputes.
- **Mnông and other Central Highlands peoples:** memory of long resistance and of resettlement refused.
- **Hmong:** keeping old ritual against pressure to convert or modernize, and converts who hold to the new faith.
- **Khmer Krom:** keeping language and pagoda schooling.
- **Cham:** priests who keep script and ritual unchanged.
- **Hoa, including Hakka-speaking families:** the "hard neck" self-label, to be tested like any other.
- **Factory workers:** collective refusal through wildcat strikes.
- **Elders against the young:** who is called "bảo thủ", and who "bướng".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in sayings, literature and news of holdouts; thin in psychological measures.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for classical texts; Mnông and Hmong for local memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land standoffs (Tiên Lãng; Đồng Tâm, 2020); religious groups outside official bodies; Khmer Krom monks' protests (2007); strikes. National narratives of resistance are official history; treat them as sources, never as a national character.
- **Held for review:** none.

5. Search a library catalog for "N'Trang Lơng" and Mnông oral accounts.
