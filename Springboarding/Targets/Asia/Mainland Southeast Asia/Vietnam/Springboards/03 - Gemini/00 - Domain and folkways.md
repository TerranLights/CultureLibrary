## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta villages vs. Hanoi wards:** old hamlet units against the urban residents' group as settings of talk.
- **Nghệ Tĩnh and Huế:** strongly marked dialects; old court registers.
- **Saigon and the Mekong delta:** southern speech, frontier settlement, looser village forms.
- **Khmer Krom (Sóc Trăng, Trà Vinh):** pagoda learning, bilingual daily life.
- **Cham (Ahier, Awal/Bani; Cham Islamic communities of An Giang):** different scripts and schooling within one people.
- **Northern uplands (Tày, Nùng, Thái, Mường, Hmong, Dao):** market-day contact, uneven bilingualism.
- **Central Highlands (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, Mnông, Xơ Đăng, Cơ Ho):** communal-house talk, epic recitation.
- **Hoa of Chợ Lớn:** five speech congregations, association schools.
- **Deaf communities:** separate languages and schools.
- *Comparison only:* overseas Vietnamese heritage-language schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kinh speech and schooling; mixed for larger minorities; thin for small upland peoples and for neighborliness as measured behavior.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; classical Chinese and Nôm; French; Khmer, Cham, Tày, Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority schooling and Highlands land and religion are sensitive; broadcasting is state-run, so seek independent voices. Cham priestly manuscripts stay within the priesthood: approach through the communities.
- **Scope:** Vietnam's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Held for review:** classical liturgical-language texts taught in some minority religious schools (a category).
