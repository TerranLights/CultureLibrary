## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta villages with examination laureates:** lineage pride in learning against communes with no such history.
- **Southern Mekong households:** test whether the frontier's shorter examination history shows in how children learn a trade or a family business.
- **Urban middle classes in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City:** compare tutoring hours and exam stress with towns and villages.
- **Craft-village families:** skill learned by hand from parents.
- **Khmer Krom boys and novices:** temple learning beside state school.
- **Cham (Ahier and Awal/Bani) and Cham Islamic communities:** religious schooling in different scripts and languages.
- **Hmong and Dao uplands:** boarding schools far from home; church and state scripts.
- **Highlands children (Jarai, Bahnar, Ê Đê):** learning by joining farm and forest work; bilingual pilots.
- **Hoa families:** Chinese-language classes after school.
- *Comparison only:* overseas Vietnamese weekend Vietnamese schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on schooling, examinations and tests; mixed on home and craft learning; thin on learning by observation in minority villages.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; classical Chinese; French; Khmer, Cham, Hmong, Jarai, Ê Đê, Tày.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-language schooling, religious instruction and Hmong scripts are sensitive; test-score rankings invite national stereotypes, so separate scores from claims about culture.
- **Held for review:** texts in a classical liturgical language taught in Khmer Krom temple learning (a category).
