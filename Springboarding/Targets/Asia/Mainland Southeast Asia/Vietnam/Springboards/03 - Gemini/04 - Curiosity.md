# Vietnam: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm for scholars' miscellanies; Khmer, Hmong, Dao, Cham and Central Highlands languages for the limits on asking and looking. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kinh scholars' lineages vs. merchant families:** compare what kind of knowing each praises: books or the world of trade.
- **Urban youth and older relatives:** personal questions at Tết as a point of friction.
- **Hmong and Red Dao around Sa Pa and Bắc Hà:** on the receiving end of tourist curiosity.
- **Central Highlands peoples (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, Cơ Ho):** forest prohibitions and who may enter which places.
- **Cham Ahier and Awal/Bani communities:** temples and graveyards visited by outsiders; rules on photographs and entry.
- **Khmer Krom:** pagoda grounds as visited sites; how monks and lay people receive questions.
- **Mekong delta and Saigon:** test whether a port and river-trade history shows in interest in new goods and ideas.
- **Scientists and amateur naturalists:** birders and cavers as communities of the curious.
- *Comparison only:* overseas Vietnamese returning on "roots" trips.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in literary and pre-modern sources; thin in measured curiosity (no survey scale known for Vietnam; search before assuming one).
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; classical Chinese; Hmong, Dao, Cham and Highlands languages for community views.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some upland and Cham sites and knowledge are closed to outsiders; ask through communities. Tourism writing often exoticizes minorities; treat it as an outsider source.
- **Held for review:** none.
