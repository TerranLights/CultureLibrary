# Vietnam: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm for tale collections and lineage records; Tày, Thái, Hmong, Ê Đê, Khmer and Cham for kin terms. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Kinh lineage families:** compare the eldest son's ritual duty with what falls to younger brothers and to sisters.
- **Southern Kinh families:** numbered birth-order names and the youngest's place; compare inheritance and care of parents.
- **Hmong:** compare brothers' shared clan duties with sisters' ties after marrying into another clan.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** sibling roles where houses pass through women.
- **Tày and Thái:** compare sibling terms that also stretch to friends.
- **Khmer Krom:** test whether sons' time as novices changes sibling order and duties.
- **Cham:** matrilineal sibling ties across Ahier and Awal/Bani families.
- **Urban one- and two-child households:** fewer siblings, new expectations.
- *Comparison only:* overseas Vietnamese siblings split across countries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folktales, kinship and demography; mixed for adult sibling relations; thin for minority sibling customs beyond a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm; Hmong, Ê Đê, Jarai, Tày, Khmer, Cham.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** son preference and sex selection are sensitive; inheritance disputes touch on law (statute: Libra). Hmong and Highlands funeral roles may be ritual knowledge held by specialists.
- **Held for review:** none.
