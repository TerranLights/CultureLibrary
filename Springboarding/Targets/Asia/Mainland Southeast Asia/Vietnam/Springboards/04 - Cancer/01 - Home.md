# Vietnam: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese, with Khmer, Cham, Hmong, Tày-Nùng, Thái and Cantonese for minority homes; French and English for scholarship. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hanoi old-quarter tube-house families**, where several households came to share one house after 1954.
- **Saigon alley (hẻm) households**, where the lane works as an extra room.
- **Red River Delta villagers**, whose home is the bamboo-hedged village as much as the house.
- **North-central migrants living in the south**, who keep hometown associations (hội đồng hương) far from home.
- **Mekong flood-zone households**, whose homes are built around water.
- **Highland peoples moved under sedentarization**, for home as territory taken away.
- **Hmong, Dao, Tày and Nùng settlers in the Highlands**, who moved south from the northern uplands.
- **Cham palei** and **Khmer Krom srok**, where home is tied to a temple or mosque community.
- **Hoa families** with an ancestral hometown in China and a home in Chợ Lớn.
- **Northern Catholics who moved south in 1954**, and the houses they left.
- **Factory workers in rented rooms**, with a home in two places.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing measures and for home as literary longing; thin for minority ideas of home in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Khmer; Cham; Hmong; French for colonial housing reports; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land clearances and petitioners (dân oan) are politically sensitive; Highland resettlement and the overseas Vietnamese's lost homes after 1975 are charged subjects. Property and land belong mainly to Taurus; residence law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the latest VHLSS housing tables and compare owner and renter shares by region.
2. Search the World Bank's open repository for "household registration Vietnam".
3. Search VnExpress and Tuổi Trẻ for "về quê ăn Tết" with "công nhân".
4. Read *Thương nhớ mười hai* and list the home markers it names; then search Vietnamese literary journals for "nỗi nhớ quê" (longing for home).
5. Search library catalogs for "định canh định cư" with "Tây Nguyên".
