# Vietnam: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Hmong, Khmer, Cham, Mường and Highland languages for minority self-names; Chinese varieties for the Hoa; French and English for scholarship. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern villagers and incomer households**, where ngụ cư status once fixed a family's standing.
- **Saigon and Hanoi natives against newcomers from the provinces**, to see how long a newcomer stays a newcomer in each city.
- **Northerners who moved south in 1954 and after 1975**, and how each side named the other.
- **Hoa communities after 1978**: those who stayed and how they came to be counted.
- **Ê Đê, Jarai and Bahnar villages**, for whom the gong rite and the communal house mark membership.
- **Hmong households split by conversion**, where faith and clan pull apart.
- **Cham Ahier, Awal and Muslim Cham**, each with its own feasts of return.
- **Khmer Krom**, whose self-name and official label may not match.
- **Mường and Thổ**, peoples whose status as separate groups has been argued.
- **Small groups merged into larger ones by the official list**, whose members may name themselves differently.
- **Amerasians (con lai) and children of mixed marriages**, whose place was questioned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; village membership and ethnic classification are well studied, but felt belonging is rarely surveyed beyond PISA.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; English and French; Hmong, Khmer and Cham for self-accounts; Chinese for Hoa association records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hoa history around 1978-79, Khmer Krom identity claims and Highland identity movements are politically sensitive; regional prejudice talk can be inflammatory. Community and group belong mainly to Aquarius; citizenship law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
