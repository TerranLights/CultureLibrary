# Vietnam: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm and French for older ritual sources; Tày-Nùng, Hmong, Khmer, Cham and Ê Đê for protective rites; English for child-protection reports. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River Delta villages**, for the bamboo hedge, the village gate and the guardian god as layers of defense.
- **Central coast communes in typhoon country**, to set guarding against storms beside guarding against people.
- **Tày, Nùng and Thái households**, for the place of Then masters in guarding children.
- **Hmong families**, for the guarding of infants' souls.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai villages**, for rites made for the whole village's safety.
- **Khmer Krom hamlets**, guarded by neak ta and the pagoda.
- **Cham Ahier and Bani**, whose community rites for safety differ.
- **Northern border provinces**, where cross-border trafficking makes protection a pressing public question.
- **Urban working parents** who leave children with private minders.
- **Catholic households**, for guardian angels and Marian devotion as protection at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on violence surveys and disaster policy; thin and scattered on protective custom outside the Kinh.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; French for colonial ethnography; English; Tày, Hmong, Khmer, Cham and Ê Đê for rites in their owners' words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence, trafficking and child abuse need care with survivors' privacy; some protective rites belong to their practitioners and may not be shared; militia and defense matters are state-controlled.
