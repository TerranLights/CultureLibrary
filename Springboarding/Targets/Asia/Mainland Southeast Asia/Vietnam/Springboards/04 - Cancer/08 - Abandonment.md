# Vietnam: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm for the classical laments; Jarai, Bahnar and Hmong for minority rites and tales; English for war-legacy and adoption studies. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of soldiers missing since the wars**, for the dead left unburied and the search for remains.
- **Rural Red River and Mekong households with parents in the cities**, for the children and elders who stay when parents leave for work.
- **Wives of men working abroad (Taiwan, Korea, Japan)**, the modern "waiting wives".
- **Jarai and Bahnar villages**, for a rite that formally ends the living's duties to a grave.
- **Hmong girls and women in border provinces**, for whom leaving the family can mean danger or escape.
- **Amerasians and their mothers**, to trace how being left by a foreign father was judged.
- **Khmer Krom and Cham families with members in Cambodia or Malaysia**, for leaving across a border.
- **Unwed mothers and their children**, for how the "con rơi" label is applied and resisted.
- **Catholic orphanages and Buddhist pagodas**, for church and temple care of left babies.
- **Elders living alone** in hill and delta villages, to separate being left from choosing to stay.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; literature and folklore are rich; counts of abandoned children and elders are thin and may be incomplete.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Hán-Nôm for the classical laments; English; Jarai, Bahnar and Hmong for the peoples' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war missing, Amerasians, children of unwed mothers and abortion are painful and partly political topics; the grave-abandoning rite and other mortuary customs belong to their peoples. Betrayal and death belong to Scorpio, loyalty to an unplaced concept.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Nguyễn Đổng Chi's collection for tales of orphans and stepmothers and list their regional versions.
2. Read Kwon (2008), then search the Vietnamese press for "cúng cô hồn" by region.
3. Search library catalogs for "bỏ mả" with "Gia Rai" or "Ba Na".
4. Search VTV archives for "Như chưa hề có cuộc chia ly" episodes and list the kinds of separation they cover.
5. Search ISDS and UNICEF Vietnam for "trẻ em bị bỏ lại" or "left-behind children".
