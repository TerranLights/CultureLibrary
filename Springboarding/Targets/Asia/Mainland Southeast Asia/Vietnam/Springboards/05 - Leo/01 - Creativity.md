## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River Delta craft villages:** skills kept within lineages; how far a maker may depart from the founding ancestor's model.
- **Hà Nội painters and Hồ Chí Minh City designers:** a gallery-and-collector world against an advertising-and-fashion world.
- **Huế:** former court workshops (enamel, embroidery, lacquer) that made to imperial order.
- **Mekong Delta smallholders:** improvised machines for farm and river work.
- **Cham women potters and weavers:** craft carried by women and named as heritage.
- **Jarai, Bahnar and other Central Highlands carvers:** works made for the tomb-abandonment rite rather than for keeping.
- **Hmong, Dao and Thái textile makers:** patterns as markers of group and region, now sold to tourists.
- **Tày-Nùng:** the makers of ritual instruments and paper cut-outs for Then.
- **Khmer Krom:** pagoda painters and carvers, many trained in the pagoda itself.
- **Hoa artisans in Chợ Lớn:** lanterns, printing and lion-dance heads.
- **Overseas painters in France:** comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fine art, crafts and literature; mixed for minority makers (much documentation is by outside ethnographers); thin for everyday improvising outside the press.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese with Hán-Nôm; French for the colonial school; Cham, Khmer, Hmong and Central Highlands languages for makers' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exhibition and publication licensing; the Nhân Văn–Giai Phẩm history; commercial copying of minority designs. Who owns a design is Taurus's question; copyright law is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** none.
