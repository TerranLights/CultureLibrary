# Vietnam: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; Hán-Việt and chữ Hán for classical texts), Cham, Khmer, Ê Đê, Hmong; Chinese (Hoa); English for comparative studies. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hà Nội residents:** pride in old-quarter manners and "người Tràng An" refinement (a self-image to test).
- **Hồ Chí Minh City and the Mekong:** a frontier, open-door self-image against the northern one.
- **Huế:** pride rooted in the last royal capital.
- **Xứ Nghệ:** a regional self-image built on scholars and revolutionaries.
- **Quảng Nam:** the saying "Quảng Nam hay cãi" (fond of argument) as a point of local pride.
- **Delta villages:** pride in a famous son, a communal house or a laureate's stele.
- **Cham:** pride centered on towers, script and the Champa past.
- **Khmer Krom:** language and pagoda as the objects of pride.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** epic heroes and matrilineal clan houses.
- **Hmong:** clan pride and the honor of one's surname group.
- **Hoa:** pride in ancestral provinces, kept through dialect assembly halls.
- **Veterans and families of the former southern army:** whose wartime pride is publicly honored and whose is not.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** a different flag as the symbol of pride.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national pride in official and school sources; mixed for regional and ethnic pride; thin for pride in craft and work outside press features.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese and Hán (classical texts), Cham, Khmer, Ê Đê, Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride is tied to the official account of history; the former South's memory and the yellow-striped flag are sensitive; nationalism over the South China Sea can be volatile; Pride events face permit limits.
- **Held for review:** none.
