# Vietnam: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young urban women:** pageant and selfie visibility weighed against family reputation.
- **Saigon market traders:** appearance managed as part of trade.
- **Rural migrants in cities (street vendors, domestic helpers):** labor that the city uses but looks past.
- **Hmong women around Sa Pa and Hà Giang:** photographed by visitors daily.
- **Central Highlands peoples:** shown in state media mostly as festival performers.
- **Hoa:** a quieter public profile after the late-1970s exodus, to check.
- **Families of missing soldiers:** seeking a name and a grave for their dead.
- **Delta villages:** lane weddings and funeral bands as displays the whole hamlet watches.
- **Khmer Krom and Cham:** how often they appear on screen in their own languages.
- **Infants:** customs that keep them out of spirits' notice.
- **People living with disabilities linked to dioxin:** visibility through campaigns and charity drives.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** community television that shows a diaspora to itself.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pageants, urban display and media; mixed for minority representation; thin for privacy and the wish not to be seen.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, Hmong, Khmer, Cham, French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance by residential groups and police is a delicate topic (Capricorn for its spirit); photographing minority villagers raises consent questions; the Hoa exodus and the war dead of the former southern army are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.
