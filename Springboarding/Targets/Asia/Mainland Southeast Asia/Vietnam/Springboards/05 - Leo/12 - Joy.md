# Vietnam: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; Hán-Việt and chữ Hán; Nôm for older verse), Tày-Nùng, Mường, Khmer, Ê Đê, Jarai, Hmong; Chinese (Hoa). **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Kinh:** Tết as the measure of joy, kept in memoirs and sayings.
- **Southern Kinh:** everyday joy around food, drink and song, a claim to check rather than assume.
- **Đạo Mẫu followers:** ecstatic, music-driven joy.
- **Buddhists of the Plum Village line:** calm joy as practice.
- **Catholics:** Christmas lights and processions in Phát Diệm, Hố Nai and Gò Vấp.
- **Tày, Nùng and Thái:** celebratory Then.
- **Mường:** New Year blessing songs.
- **Central Highlands peoples:** new-rice feasts with gongs.
- **Khmer Krom:** New Year dancing and water.
- **Hoa:** lantern parades.
- **The bereaved:** music and laughter at funerals.
- **Elders:** longevity celebrations (mừng thọ) as late-life joy.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** New Year variety shows taped abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, festivals and survey indices; mixed for minority terms for joy; thin for private joy outside memoirs.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese with Nôm; Tày, Mường, Khmer, Ê Đê, Jarai, Hmong; Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Plum Village community had a dispute with authorities in 2009 (Bát Nhã, *check*); drinking culture invites stereotypes, so treat it as a question; noise disputes may reach local regulation (Libra).
- **Held for review:** none.
