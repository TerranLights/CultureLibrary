*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Huế:** families with a memory of court and mandarin service.
- **Nam Định, Hà Nam and Thanh Hóa villages:** sending women to Hanoi as live-in helpers.
- **Hồ Chí Minh City and the resort coast (Đà Nẵng, Nha Trang, Phú Quốc):** hospitality work and how customers treat servers.
- **Hòa Hảo (An Giang) and Cao Đài (Tây Ninh):** lay religious service with their own institutions.
- **Khmer Krom:** temporary monastic service by young men.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani:** priestly service held by lineage and office.
- **Bahnar, Ê Đê and Jarai:** elders' duties and communal-house roles.
- **Catholic parishes:** sisters running orphanages and clinics; parish councils.
- **Families of veterans and martyrs:** service honored and repaid.
- **Tày and Thái:** ritual specialists who serve the household and village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on state campaigns and press; thin on how servers and domestic helpers see their own work.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, classical Chinese, French, Khmer, Cham, Highlands languages, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service and "servant of the people" are politically charged; charity outside state-registered bodies can be sensitive; abuse of domestic helpers is a delicate field topic. Law on service goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** liturgical texts in a South Asian classical language used in Khmer Krom and Cham priestly service (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Search *Tuổi Trẻ* for "Mùa hè xanh" with a province name to map volunteer sites.

4. Download PAPI provincial reports and extract the public-service dimension.
5. Search ILO Vietnam publications for "domestic workers".
