*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Hanoi families:** a reputation for refined taste in food and tea, to test.
- **Hồ Chí Minh City shoppers and online buyers:** bargaining and exposure to fakes.
- **Rural elders:** frequent targets of phone and online fraud.
- **Young urban social-media users:** rumor, call-out posts and fact-checking.
- **Đạo Mẫu devotees:** judging the authenticity of mediums.
- **Catholic parishes:** examining one's conscience in confession.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** the women's side judging a husband.
- **Hmong and Giáy of Hà Giang:** choosing partners at markets.
- **Khmer Krom:** weighing the counsel of pagoda committees on village choices.
- **Hoa traders:** judging partners through dialect and clan networks.
- **Regional newcomers in the big cities:** prejudice between north and south, to separate from judgment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on scams and consumer press; good literary sources; thin on minority ways of judging people.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, classical Chinese, Nôm, Hmong, Ê Đê, Khmer, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fake news" is also a state censorship category, so sources on rumor need care; regional slurs and mediums' reputations are sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none.

4. Read Endres on authenticity, then search the press for "đồng giả" (false medium).
5. Pull the Vietnam wave of the Asian Barometer and tabulate trust in newspapers and television.
