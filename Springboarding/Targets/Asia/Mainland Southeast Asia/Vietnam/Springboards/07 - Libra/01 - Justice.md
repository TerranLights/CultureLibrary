# Vietnam: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ, with Hán-Việt roots), chữ Hán for pre-1900 penal texts, French for colonial courts, and the upland and delta minority languages. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River delta communes:** village fines and shaming beside the state's courts; mob beatings of dog thieves reported mostly in northern rural areas *check*.
- **Families touched by the 1950s land reform:** redress and memory of a state-run campaign that later admitted errors.
- **Former southern officials' families (re-education after 1975):** whose account of justice is told and whose is left out.
- **Ê Đê, Jarai, M'nông, Bahnar:** wrongs settled by elders with compensation; how far the state's grassroots mediation absorbs this.
- **Hmong of Hà Giang, Lào Cai and Điện Biên:** clan elders as judges, and the effect of Protestant conversion on who hears a wrong.
- **Thái of the northwest:** chiefly courts of the old muang system, remembered in their chronicles *check*.
- **Khmer Krom of Sóc Trăng and Trà Vinh:** monks and lay pagoda elders hearing quarrels.
- **Cham communities:** Bani and Muslim religious leaders handling family wrongs, beside Ahier/Balamon custom.
- **Cao Đài (Tây Ninh) and Hòa Hảo (An Giang) followers:** their own teachings on reward and retribution.
- **Agent Orange-affected families:** justice sought across borders, through foreign courts.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** diaspora accounts of re-education camps as a contrast to those told at home; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and press; mixed for courts (judgments partly published); thin for executions and political trials.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for pre-modern penal texts; French for colonial customary courts; Ê Đê, Jarai, Hmong and Khmer for local justice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** death-penalty figures are not public; land reform, re-education camps and political trials are sensitive; victims of crime need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
