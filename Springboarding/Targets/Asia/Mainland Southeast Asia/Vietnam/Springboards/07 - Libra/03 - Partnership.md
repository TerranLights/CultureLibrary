*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Tày, Nùng, Thái, Hmong, Ê Đê and Cham for couple and sworn-kin terms; Chinese varieties for Hoa business networks. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kinh couples in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City:** couple-centered words and dual-earner households, against rural families where the marriage joins two lineages.
- **Bắc Ninh and Bắc Giang Quan họ villages:** ritual partnership between villages and between singers who may not marry each other *check*.
- **Tày and Nùng of the northeast:** sworn lifelong friendship as a partnership outside kin.
- **Thái of the northwest:** reported bride service (ở rể) as a test of who leads inside the new couple *check*.
- **Hmong of Hà Giang and Lào Cai:** clan rules on which partners are allowed.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** reported residence in the wife's longhouse, a different starting point for equality between spouses *check*.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani of Ninh Thuận and Bình Thuận:** matrilineal custom alongside differing religious rules on the couple.
- **Khmer Krom of the Mekong delta:** couples and work partnerships organized around the pagoda.
- **Hoa business families:** firms run by kin and native-place partners.
- **Cooperative members in the Mekong delta and the north:** partnership in work after decollectivization.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** spouses separated by labor migration abroad, as a strain test of the couple; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the couple and for state diplomacy; mixed for business partnerships (mostly press); thin for sworn kin and village alliances (local studies).
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Thái, Tày-Nùng, Hmong, Ê Đê and Cham for local partnership customs; Chinese varieties for Hoa records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** same-sex couples have no legal recognition, so data are sparse and sensitive; violence inside couples needs care.
- **Held for review:** none.
