# Vietnam: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (many balance words are Hán-Việt; medical texts in chữ Hán and chữ Nôm), plus Cham, M'nông, Ê Đê, Khmer and Hoa terms for local healing and cosmology. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban office workers in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City:** long hours and the new wellness vocabulary.
- **Rice farmers of the Red River and Mekong deltas:** the farming year's rhythm of hard work and festival rest.
- **Practitioners of Eastern medicine and their patients:** hot-cold categories in daily eating, against biomedicine.
- **Hoa herbalists of Chợ Lớn's medicine street:** northern medicine as practiced by the Hoa, compared with Kinh healers.
- **M'nông, Ê Đê and Jarai:** balance with spirits and forest through rites, and its strain under resettlement and coffee farming.
- **Cham of Ninh Thuận:** the paired Ahier and Awal communities, read as a social balance *check*.
- **Khmer Krom:** pagoda-led festivals that mark the farming year.
- **Hmong and Dao herbal healers of the high north:** their own remedies and categories, apart from Kinh Eastern medicine.
- **Mekong delta coastal communities:** salinity and land loss lived as the land out of balance.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** Eastern-medicine practice kept abroad, a check on what changes away from home; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medicine and food; mixed for work hours (official data only); thin for upland cosmologies in recent Vietnamese-language work, richer in older French ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, chữ Hán and chữ Nôm for medical classics, French for early ethnography, M'nông, Ê Đê, Cham and Khmer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leadership "balance" is a matter of outside analysis, not official statement; upland ritual knowledge may be restricted to its keepers.
- **Held for review:** none.
