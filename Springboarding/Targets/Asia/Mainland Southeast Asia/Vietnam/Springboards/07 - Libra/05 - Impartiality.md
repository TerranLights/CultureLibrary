# Vietnam: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (official and colloquial registers differ sharply here), chữ Hán for dynastic administrative law, and minority languages for village headship and elders. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Kinh villages with strong lineages:** pressure on officials to favor their own clan.
- **Southern and Mekong delta Kinh:** looser lineages, possibly different pull of kin on officials, to test.
- **Urban students and parents:** blind grading and the cost of extra classes from one's own teacher.
- **Hoa business networks:** connections inside and outside the community, compared with Kinh networks.
- **Tày and Nùng of the old Việt Bắc base area:** how kin ties shape local posts there.
- **Hmong and Dao villages:** village heads chosen within clans, set against state rules.
- **Central Highlands peoples:** elders recognized by the state, and whether they are seen as neutral or as officials.
- **Khmer Krom pagoda committees:** how pagoda leaders are chosen and whether they arbitrate fairly between families.
- **Cham Bani and Ahier communities:** religious leaders chosen within lineages.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** remittance-funded families at home as a test of who gets favors; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press cases and corruption surveys; mixed for courts; thin for village-level favoritism outside studies of poverty lists.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; chữ Hán for dynastic law; Hmong, Tày-Nùng and Khmer for village leadership.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption of named officials is sensitive and coverage follows official campaigns; judicial independence is a political subject.
- **Held for review:** none.
