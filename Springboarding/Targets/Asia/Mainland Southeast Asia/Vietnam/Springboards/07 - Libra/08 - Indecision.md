# Vietnam: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (everyday, bureaucratic and ritual vocabularies), with Tày, Mường, Thái, Hmong and Cham terms for ways of deciding by sign or by elders. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban middle-class youth:** many options in study, work and partners, and the newer language of choice overload.
- **Rural families of the north and center:** decisions on marriage and migration taken by parents and lineage elders.
- **Mekong delta households:** labor migration choices made around the delta's farming calendar.
- **Officials and cadres:** whether choices wait for a superior's word, as the 2021 Party texts imply.
- **Southern small-business owners:** choosing opening days by almanac and temple.
- **Tày, Mường and Thái villages:** deciding by sign readers and elders.
- **Hmong households:** ritual specialists consulted before moves, marriages and healing.
- **Ê Đê and Bahnar villages:** elders' councils whose slow consensus may read as indecision from outside.
- **Cham communities:** ritual days fixed by the Cham calendar's keepers.
- **Khmer Krom:** monks and lay pagoda elders consulted on family choices.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** the choice to return or stay, as a contrast case; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for divination practice and bureaucratic delay; mixed for youth decision-making; thin for psychological measures of indecision.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Tày, Mường, Thái, Hmong, Cham and Khmer for ritual ways of deciding.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some divination is officially frowned on as superstition (mê tín dị đoan) and may be under-reported; officials' reluctance to decide is a political subject.
- **Held for review:** none.
