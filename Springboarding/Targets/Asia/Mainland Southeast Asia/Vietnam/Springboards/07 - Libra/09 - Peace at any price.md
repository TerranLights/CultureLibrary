# Vietnam: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (proverbs, family advice literature, court and Party documents), French for the treaties of the 1860s-1880s, and Ê Đê, M'nông, Hmong and Khmer for local ways of settling harm. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Daughters-in-law in patrilocal Kinh households:** where the cost of family calm is said to fall, to test.
- **Northern and central villages with grassroots mediation teams:** how often reconciliation is pushed in family cases.
- **Southern urban families:** separation and divorce rates set against northern ones, as one measure of what is endured.
- **Factory workers in industrial zones:** silence against strikes when pay or harassment is the issue.
- **Ê Đê, M'nông and Jarai:** whether harm is closed by payment and rite, and who may refuse the settlement.
- **Hmong of the high north:** clan pressure to keep quarrels inside the lineage, to test case by case.
- **Khmer Krom:** patience taught in pagoda schools, and its use in family disputes.
- **Cham Bani communities:** religious leaders urging reconciliation inside families.
- **Catholic parishes:** priests counseling couples to stay together.
- **Veterans and families of the southern side after 1975:** decades of public silence kept for peace at home.
- **Overseas Vietnamese, for comparison only:** abroad, open debate on wartime silences, a check on what stays unsaid at home; the nation's findings cover them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for proverbs, literature and domestic-violence surveys; mixed for workplaces and schools; thin for upland settlement practice outside older ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, chữ Nôm for Hồ Xuân Hương, French for nineteenth-century treaties, and the upland languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic and sexual violence need care; the 1979 war, relations with China and the post-1975 South are sensitive in public speech.
- **Held for review:** none.
