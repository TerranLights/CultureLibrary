# Vietnam: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese, with literary Hán for the proclamations; Ê Đê and Khmer for minority epics and tales. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore honor or standing, or deter the next wrong.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kinh households in the Red River Delta:** grudges between lineages over land and office.
- **Central Kinh (Huế, Quảng Trị):** sites of reprisal killings in two wars; memory of who answered whom.
- **Southern Kinh and the kiếm hiệp readership:** a revenge genre that took root first in the south.
- **Ê Đê, Jarai and Bahnar:** epic and customary ways to answer a wrong, as they describe them.
- **Khmer Krom:** tales where the ruler punishes, and their place beside Buddhist teaching on non-anger.
- **Hoa:** Cantonese and Teochew opera repertoires with revenge plots, beside the Kinh tales.
- **Urban youth and gangs (Hồ Chí Minh City, Hà Nội outskirts):** retaliation in street fights and debt collection.
- **Engaged Buddhists and Plum Village followers:** forgiveness as a practice, not only a teaching.
- **Families of the Tây Sơn and Nguyễn sides in Bình Định and Huế:** dynastic memory long after the fact.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** exile politics and the score left open since 1975.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in classical texts, folklore and fiction; mixed for crime reporting; thin for measured attitudes.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; literary Hán; Ê Đê; Khmer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Huế 1968; post-1975 reprisals; Plum Village's standing with the state; acid-attack survivors. Revenge against the state's punishment is the spirit of the law (Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
