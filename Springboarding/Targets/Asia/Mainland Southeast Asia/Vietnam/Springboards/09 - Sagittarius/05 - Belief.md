# Vietnam: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (chữ Hán for deity records); Dao (with its Nôm-type script), Hmong, Mường, Khmer, Cham, Cantonese for minority practice. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern delta villages:** spirit ranks fixed by court certificates, and the state's later anti-superstition drives.
- **Southern households:** almanac, geomancy and fortune-telling use in a commercial setting.
- **Dao of the northern mountains:** belief tied to ordination grades and ritual books.
- **Hmong with and without conversion:** soul and spirit beliefs before and after Protestant adoption.
- **Mường of Hòa Bình and Thanh Hóa:** chant-borne ideas of the soul's journey.
- **Khmer Krom villages:** place spirits held alongside monastic teaching.
- **Cham Ahier and Awal:** two belief systems sharing one calendar of rites.
- **Hoa of Bình Dương and Chợ Lớn:** goddess belief carried by temple associations.
- **Educated urban skeptics:** whether choosing auspicious days persists among those who reject folk belief (a question to test).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kinh folk belief and divination; mixed for minorities; thin for honest survey answers, since "mê tín" carries stigma.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, chữ Hán, Dao script, Hmong, Mường, Khmer, Cham, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some practitioners avoid outsiders because of past bans; Dao and Cham ritual books may be closed to non-initiates. Marking a belief as "superstition" is itself a political act; record who applies the label.
- **Held for review:** none.
