# Vietnam: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (with chữ Hán and chữ Nôm for pre-modern texts); Khmer, Cham, Ê Đê and Hmong for community sources; French and English scholarship. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Kinh lineages:** eldest-son duties to the ancestral altar and lineage hall, and whether daughters now share them.
- **Mekong Delta Kinh:** looser lineage halls; compare duty to parents where the family altar, not the clan, carries it.
- **War-martyr families and veterans:** a two-way duty, the state's to them and theirs to its memory.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani:** matrilineal households, where daughters may carry the rites.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** matrilineal houses, where husbands' duties run to the wife's lineage.
- **Khmer Krom:** sons' temporary ordination as a duty to parents, and pagoda-centered obligations.
- **Hmong and Dao:** clan duties at funerals and ordination rites.
- **Catholics:** duty to God and Church beside ancestor rites, after the accommodations of the twentieth century.
- **Migrant workers abroad and internal migrants to the cities:** duty kept at a distance, through money.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** comparison only; how obligations to parents and ancestors are kept from abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kinh family duty and state campaigns; mixed for Cham and Khmer; thin for Highland groups outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, Classical Chinese and chữ Nôm, Khmer, Cham (Akhar Thrah), Ê Đê, Hmong, French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military-service avoidance and the duties of former southern soldiers' families are political topics; some clan and lineage rites are for members only.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *Hịch tướng sĩ* in a school edition with its teaching notes.
2. Search "đạo hiếu" with "nghiên cứu" in Vietnamese journals for filial-duty studies.
3. Request the VNAS 2011 report and its tables on co-residence.
4. Search "gọi nhập ngũ" plus a province name for a year of call-up coverage.
5. Search a library catalog for Inrasara and for "Kate" to find Cham sources on household rites.
