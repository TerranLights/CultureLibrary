# Vietnam: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Classical Chinese for Trần-era Buddhist writing; Khmer, Cham and Hmong for community practice; English for survey reports. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban middle-class parents in Hà Nội and Hồ Chí Minh City:** tutoring timetables and the turn toward gentler correction.
- **Rural Red River Delta households:** older correction customs, and grandparents' role in them.
- **Mekong Delta families:** compare strictness where schooling ran shorter and farm work came earlier.
- **Northern mountain minorities (Hmong, Dao, Tày):** correction by elders and clan, and boarding-school routines far from home.
- **Central Highlands (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar):** how children are corrected in matrilineal households.
- **Khmer Krom:** discipline learned in the pagoda.
- **Cham Bani and Sunni Muslim Cham:** fasting and prayer as two different regimens.
- **Cao Đài and Hòa Hảo adherents:** lay regimens of diet and prayer.
- **Soldiers and police recruits:** institutional drill.
- **Factory workers in industrial zones:** clock and quota discipline set by foreign-owned firms.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** comparison only; how parents abroad correct children under other countries' child-protection rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's discipline (MICS), schools and army codes; mixed for workplaces; thin for minority child-rearing outside ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Classical Chinese; Khmer; Cham (Akhar Thrah); Hmong; English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violent discipline of children is a child-protection matter; the Bát Nhã dispute and religious regimens touch state-religion relations; some monastic rules are for the ordained only.
- **Held for review:** none.
