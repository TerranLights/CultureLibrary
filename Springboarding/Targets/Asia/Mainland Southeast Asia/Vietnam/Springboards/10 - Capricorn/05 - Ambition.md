# Vietnam: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (chữ Hán for pre-modern poetry); Hmong and Khmer for minority livelihoods; English for migration and business studies. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern Kinh villages with an exam heritage:** families that count laureates; whether striving still means study first.
- **Hồ Chí Minh City and the southeast:** trade and enterprise as the expected way up.
- **North Central Coast (Nghệ An, Hà Tĩnh):** poor provinces with a reputation for hard study and migration abroad, both to test.
- **Hmong and Dao of the northern uplands:** tourism trade and cross-border commerce against the state's schooling ladder.
- **Ê Đê, Jarai and Bahnar:** land loss to coffee and rubber, and what rising means after it.
- **Khmer Krom:** monastic study, schooling in Vietnamese, or work in the cities as rival paths.
- **Cham:** small trading and weaving communities, and Cham students in the universities.
- **Hoa:** commerce in Chợ Lớn, rebuilt after the late-1970s departures.
- **Women:** ambition allowed or blamed, in trade, office and politics.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** comparison only; the measure of making it that returnees bring home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban and Kinh striving, migration and business press; mixed for minorities; thin for honest talk of political ambition, since Party careers are not discussed openly.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, chữ Hán for classical poetry, Hmong, Dao, Khmer, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor-export debts, trafficking and the Essex deaths are painful for families; tycoons' trials are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
