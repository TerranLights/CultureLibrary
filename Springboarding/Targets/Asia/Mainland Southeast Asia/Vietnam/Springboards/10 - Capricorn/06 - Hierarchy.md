*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River Delta lineages:** branch and birth order within the lineage hall, still visible at ancestral feasts.
- **Huế families of the old court:** surviving prestige of descent from the Nguyễn house and its officials.
- **Mekong Delta Kinh:** later-settled lands; test whether landlord and tenant ties, more than lineage, set rank there.
- **Party and state elite in Hà Nội:** rank in the apparatus as the country's most visible ladder.
- **Mường and Thái:** hereditary noble families and commoners, and what the 1950s changed between them.
- **Dao and Hmong:** ritual grades and clan seniority, not state rank.
- **Ê Đê and Jarai:** matrilineal houses; ask how the wife's and the husband's lineages are ranked in household matters.
- **Cham Ahier/Balamon and Awal/Bani:** clergy ranks inside each community.
- **Khmer Krom:** how monks' standing over laity shows in speech register and seating.
- **Cao Đài and Catholic communities:** church hierarchies beside the state's.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** comparison only; which address terms and seniority rules survive in a second language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language, court and Party structures; mixed for class surveys; thin for minority rank systems in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, Classical Chinese, Mường, Dao, Thái, Khmer, Cham (Akhar Thrah), French, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land-reform history and its class labels remain politically sensitive and painful; some ritual-grade knowledge is restricted to initiates; elite family ties are reported cautiously.
- **Held for review:** none.
