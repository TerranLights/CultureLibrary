## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hà Nội:** state-led civility drives in the capital and their reception among old-quarter residents.
- **Hồ Chí Minh City:** sidewalk economies and newer planned districts as rival pictures of a good street.
- **Huế:** court ritual and its memory as a model of cosmic order.
- **Rural Red River Delta:** festivals of permitted disorder and their tidying.
- **Mekong Delta:** river-and-canal settlement, where ideas of a tidy place may differ.
- **Central Highlands (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, M'Nông):** customary ideas of village balance, and the state's security lens on them.
- **Northern uplands (Hmong, Thái, Dao):** border order and household rites against misfortune.
- **Khmer Krom:** temple-centered festival order (Ok Om Bok, Chôl Chnăm Thmây).
- **Cham:** temple-tower rites at Po Nagar and Po Klong Garai and who directs them.
- **Young urban people:** looser views of noise, nightlife and street life.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** comparison only; what a tidy, safe street means after life in another country's cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state campaigns, traffic and urban studies; mixed for cosmology; thin and politically filtered for crime and unrest.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, Classical Chinese, French, Khmer, Cham (Akhar Thrah), Ê Đê, Jarai, Hmong, English.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Highland unrest and public-security matters carry legal risk; police data are incomplete; temple-tower rites belong to Cham clergy.
- **Held for review:** none.
