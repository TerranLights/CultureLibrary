# Vietnam: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese; Classical Chinese for chronicles; French for colonial files; Khmer, Cham, Hmong, Jarai and Chinese dialects for minority groups. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Red River Delta villages:** compare lineage and age-ward loyalties when they pull a person two ways.
- **Mekong Delta:** compare sworn societies and religious sects as groups one joins rather than is born into.
- **Central coast fishing villages:** compare boat crews and whale-cult associations as work groups.
- **Hoa:** compare where dialect-group lines are drawn and when they are crossed.
- **Cham:** compare the Ahier, Awal and Muslim Cham divisions and how each draws "us".
- **Khmer Krom:** compare temple crews and hamlets in rivalry and in common work.
- **Central Highlands (Jarai, Ê Đê, Bahnar, Mnông):** compare how elders and village councils speak for the group.
- **Hmong and Dao:** compare the clan as the unit that owes and is owed.
- **Tày, Thái and Mường:** compare valley-polity loyalties with village and kin ones.
- **Catholic parishes:** compare the parish acting together under its priest.
- **Urban youth:** compare fan crowds, class groups and online groups.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** compare the political and generational lines drawn between associations abroad, as a contrast to the home country's official group forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on official mass organizations and village groups; mixed on youth and fan crowds; thin on gangs except in crime reporting.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Classical Chinese; French; Khmer, Cham, Hmong, Jarai and Chinese dialects.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independent groups outside the mass organizations; factions inside the ruling party; highland religious groups; some clan rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the Diên Hồng and Lũng Nhai entries in a translated *Toàn thư*; note who speaks for the group.
2. Download the Youth Union statute and a Women's Union congress report; list joining rules and duties.
3. Search the Emulation Council site for "tập thể lao động xuất sắc" and sample award citations.
4. Search Báo Sóc Trăng and VnExpress for "đua ghe ngo" and "đi bão"; collect crowd and crew accounts.
5. Read Dournes's *Pötao* and Nakamura's Cham ethnography; list each people's own group terms.
