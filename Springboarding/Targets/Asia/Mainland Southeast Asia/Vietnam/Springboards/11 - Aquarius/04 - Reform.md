# Vietnam: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Classical Chinese for 19th-century memorials); French for colonial-era reform; Cham, Khmer and Hmong for reforms seen from minority communities; English for overseas memoirs. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern cooperative villages (Vĩnh Phúc, Hải Phòng, Thái Bình):** compare memories of contracting from below before 1986.
- **Southern merchants and the Hoa of Chợ Lớn:** compare memories of the 1978 transformation of commerce.
- **Families of former Southern officers and officials:** compare accounts of re-education.
- **Hanoi and Huế scholar families:** compare how the 1900s reformers are remembered.
- **Buddhist, Catholic and Cao Đài communities:** compare reform from inside each faith.
- **Central Highlands peoples (Ê Đê, Jarai, Bahnar, Mnông, Cơ Ho):** compare their own accounts of sedentarization and land programs.
- **Hmong, Dao and Thái of the Northwest:** compare crop-replacement, resettlement and dam-relocation programs, as each people describes them.
- **Cham:** compare community-led reform of script and schooling.
- **Khmer Krom:** compare temple schooling with state school reform.
- **Rural households and urban business owners:** compare who gained from Đổi Mới and who paid for it.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** compare views of Đổi Mới-era reform held by first-generation emigrants and by their children raised abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Đổi Mới and early 20th-century reformism; mixed on ritual reform; thin and sensitive on re-education and on minority peoples' views of reforms aimed at them.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Classical Chinese; French; Cham, Khmer, Hmong and Highlands languages; English for overseas memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** re-education camps; the 1978 transformation of commerce; land reform; anti-corruption politics; rehab centers. Legal reform belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Marr's book, then Phan Châu Trinh's 1906 letter; list the reformers' proposals.
2. Read Kerkvliet and Đặng Phong side by side; list the local reforms named before 1986.
3. Search Tạp chí Cộng sản and Nhân Dân archives for "Những việc cần làm ngay" and "N.V.L.".
4. Read Malarney on ritual reform; search provincial papers for "bài trừ hủ tục" in a Highlands and a Northwest province.
5. Search library catalogs for re-education memoirs in Vietnamese and English ("học tập cải tạo hồi ký").
