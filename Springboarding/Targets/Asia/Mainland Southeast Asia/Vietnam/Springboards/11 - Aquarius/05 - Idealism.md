# Vietnam: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Nôm and Hán for pre-modern verse and essays); Ê Đê, Mnông and Thái for oral epics and verse tales; Khmer and Cham; English and French for scholarship. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality; it includes visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northern wartime generation:** compare ideals held then with what veterans write now.
- **Southern pre-1975 urban youth:** compare student, religious and literary ideals under the Republic.
- **Mekong Delta listeners of recited verse tales:** compare the righteous-hero model across generations.
- **Present-day urban youth (Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Đà Nẵng):** compare stated ideals with the "thực dụng" charge.
- **Teachers and doctors in remote postings:** compare calling ideals with pay and conditions.
- **Buddhist, Cao Đài, Hòa Hảo and Catholic service groups:** compare each faith's picture of the ideal person.
- **Ê Đê, Mnông, Jarai and Bahnar:** compare the epic hero's ideals as each people tells them.
- **Thái and Tày:** compare love and loyalty ideals in verse tales.
- **Cham:** compare ideals in Cham verse (ariya) of the Ahier and Awal communities.
- **Khmer Krom:** compare the ideal young man formed by temple life with school ideals.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** compare the service and homeland ideals taught in overseas youth and Scouting groups with those taught by the Youth Union at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and state youth campaigns; mixed for religious idealism; thin for minority peoples' own ideals beyond published epics.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Nôm and Classical Chinese; Ê Đê, Mnông, Jarai, Bahnar, Thái, Cham and Khmer; English and French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime and postwar disillusion writing (some once restricted); Buddhist movements of the 1960s; some epic performance is tied to rites.
- **Held for review:** none.

1. Read *Bình Ngô đại cáo* and *Lục Vân Tiên* in annotated editions; list the ideals each names.

3. Search the National Library catalog for "Kho tàng sử thi Tây Nguyên"; pick one epic per people.
4. Search Tuổi Trẻ and Tiền Phong archives for "lý tưởng sống" and "Mùa hè xanh"; sample features.
5. Read Hoskins on Cao Đài; collect the movement's own writings on world unity.
