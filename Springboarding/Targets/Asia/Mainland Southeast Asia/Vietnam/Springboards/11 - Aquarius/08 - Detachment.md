# Vietnam: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Nôm and Classical Chinese for pre-modern Thiền and scholar writing); Jarai, Bahnar, Khmer, Cham and Hmong for minority practice; English and French for scholarship. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) are valued and others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal) condemned, and which is which varies.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lay Thiền practitioners and monastics:** compare calm as discipline with calm as everyday manner.
- **Northern and Southern funerals:** compare how much grief may show and who may weep.
- **Elders in rural Delta villages and in cities:** compare living alone and distance from children.
- **Urban office workers in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City:** compare "chữa lành" withdrawal with work pressure.
- **Doctors, nurses and journalists:** compare professional distance with expected warmth.
- **Jarai and Bahnar:** compare grave-leaving with Kinh ancestor care.
- **Ê Đê and Mnông:** compare mourning restraint and release.
- **Khmer Krom:** compare elders' temple observance with family duties.
- **Cham (Ahier, Awal, Muslim Cham):** compare clergy retreats and lay reserve.
- **Hmong:** compare composure at funerals and in disputes.
- **Hoa:** compare reserve in business and in family life.
- **Overseas Vietnamese:** compare how elders living alone abroad describe distance from grown children, set against elders at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Thiền and scholar-recluse literature; mixed for funerals and public apathy (press-heavy); thin for minority emotional norms in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese; Nôm and Classical Chinese; Jarai, Bahnar, Ê Đê, Khmer, Cham and Hmong; English and French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political apathy as a topic; some grave and mourning rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** contemplative scripture of South Asian origin used in Khmer Krom and other temples (category only).
