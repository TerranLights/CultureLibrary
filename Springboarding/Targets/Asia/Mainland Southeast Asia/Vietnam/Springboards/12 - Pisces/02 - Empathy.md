# Vietnam: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese, Thái, Tày-Nùng, Cham, Khmer, Hmong. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hanoi and Saigon speakers:** how much is said aloud and how much is left to "hiểu ý".
- **Quan họ villages of Bắc Ninh and Tày-Nùng lượn singers:** sung answering as two traditions of attunement.
- **Thái of Sơn La and Điện Biên:** Thái-script readers' terms for sharing a lover's grief.
- **Cham authors and Kinh readers:** empathy across group lines, as Cham writers describe it.
- **Khmer Krom families in Trà Vinh and Sóc Trăng:** which Khmer and which Vietnamese words for feeling-with are used at home and at school.
- **Highland pupils in ethnic boarding schools (trường dân tộc nội trú):** their accounts of being understood by Kinh teachers.
- **Veterans of both former armies:** empathy toward the other side's dead and wounded.
- **Patients and medical staff:** how each side reports being listened to.
- **Older people and urban youth online:** the "thấu cảm" vocabulary against older idioms.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** how diaspora fiction asks readers to imagine the other side of the war.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in literature and folk song, thin in measured empathy and minority-language vocabulary.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese, Thái (with its script), Tày-Nùng, Cham, Khmer, Hmong.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** empathy for the former southern side and for the American side is a delicate public topic; online confession pages involve private minors; avoid treating indirect speech as one national trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
