# Vietnam: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (Quốc ngữ; chữ Nôm and Hán for Trần-era texts), Tày-Nùng, Thái, Hmong, Cham, Khmer, Jarai, Bahnar, Cantonese. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self losing its edges in something larger (a person, a crowd, nature, the divine), and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed definition; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mediums of the northern Mother Goddess temples and of the southern Bà Chúa Xứ cult:** how each says a spirit enters and leaves.
- **Tày, Nùng and Thái then masters:** a journey of the soul set beside possession.
- **Hmong txiv neeb:** shamanic trance as Hmong practitioners explain it.
- **Jarai and Bahnar gong villages:** group sound and dance, and who joins.
- **Cham Ahier and Awal:** who takes part in Rija and in what role.
- **Khmer Krom:** meditation in the temple against chol rup possession at shrines.
- **Cao Đài followers in Tây Ninh:** merging of faiths in teaching and in séance.
- **Hoa of Chợ Lớn:** Cantonese, Teochew and Hokkien use across three generations.
- **The smallest peoples (Ơ Đu, Brâu, Rơ Măm, Pu Péo):** a language kept, mixed or given up.
- **Residents of merged provinces and communes:** which names and loyalties end, and which carry on.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** loss of Vietnamese across generations abroad, set against language shift at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trance and Zen texts; mixed for divorce and dementia; thin for measured experiences of self-loss.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese (including Nôm), Tày-Nùng, Thái, Hmong, Cham, Khmer, highland languages, Chinese dialects.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** assimilation (đồng hóa) of minorities, the end of Champa and the Hoa exodus of 1978-1979 are sensitive; the end of the Republic of Vietnam is a guarded topic; drug-induced self-loss is illegal and belongs to escapism; some trance roles are clergy-held.
- **Held for review:** liturgical texts in South Asian-heritage languages used in Cham and Khmer Krom rites.
