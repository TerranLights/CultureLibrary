# Vietnam: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Vietnam, region Mainland Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Vietnamese (with chữ Hán and Nôm for older texts), Cham (Akhar Thrah), Khmer, Mường, Hmong, Jarai, Ê Đê, Cantonese, French. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside formal institutions (those are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hanoi's older women's pagoda groups and young pagoda-goers:** what each does at the altar.
- **Mekong households in the Hòa Hảo tradition:** practice at a home altar, set beside pagoda-going.
- **Huế:** monastery-centered Buddhism beside village shrine practice.
- **Cham Ahier at Katê and Cham Awal at Ramưwan:** two sacred calendars side by side.
- **Khmer Krom:** moon and ancestor offerings, and the temple's place in the village.
- **Mường of Hòa Bình:** mo chanting at death and its younger learners.
- **Hmong converts and non-converts in one commune:** household spirit altars kept or removed.
- **Jarai and Ê Đê:** sacred water, fire and forest, and their keepers.
- **Hoa of Chợ Lớn:** Thiên Hậu devotion and its annual procession.
- **State officials and Party members:** how they speak of private altar and pagoda practice.
- **Overseas Vietnamese (comparison only):** temples built abroad and pilgrimage trips back as spiritual practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Kinh popular practice; mixed on minority sacred life; thin on measured spiritual experience.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese (with Hán-Nôm), Cham, Khmer, Mường, Hmong, Jarai, Ê Đê, Cantonese, French.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** highland and Hmong Protestant movements and unregistered Buddhist groups face state control; some Cham and highland rites are closed to outsiders and are respected, not researched; the state's line between "tín ngưỡng" and "mê tín" shapes what sources say.
- **Held for review:** sacred texts and chants in South Asian-heritage languages kept by Cham and Khmer Krom clergy.
