# Aceh: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue languages; Indonesian for modern scholarship; Arabic ethical terms. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aceh Besar and Pidie:** check *seumapa* customs, since its form and the rank of its speakers may differ district by district.
- **The north coast around Lhokseumawe:** check how industrial-era workers and villagers pressed claims on the gas companies, as a non-ceremonial case of speaking up.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Nagan Raya):** check village deliberation records for who speaks first.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *melengkan* and the *sumang* rules side by side, since one licenses speech and the other limits it.
- **Alas of Southeast Aceh:** check which *merga* elders speak for a household at weddings and disputes.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check local council practice where several language groups meet.
- **Aneuk Jamee of the southwest coast:** check their wedding oratory against Minangkabau forms (comparison only).
- **Tamiang:** check the *pantun* exchange against the Acehnese forms.
- **Simeulue:** check whether *nandong* sessions allow a singer to answer back; data likely thin.
- **Women and men, young and old, *dayah* pupils and state-school pupils:** check who may question whom.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese diaspora groups in Malaysia and Scandinavia, whose public statements followed other norms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Verbal-art genres are recorded on video and in Indonesian theses; politeness research on Acehnese is likely sparse; Gayo is better covered thanks to Bowen.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for the verbal genres; Gayo for *melengkan*; Malay for *Taj al-Salatin* and *pantun*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** speaking up against the state was dangerous in the conflict years; truth-commission testimony involves victims; disputes settled through *adat* are law-adjacent (law questions to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** court-era advice on speech before the ruler written by South Asian-born scholars at the sultanate.
