# Aceh: Autonomy (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue languages; Indonesian for surveys and governance studies; Arabic theological terms; French and English for the sultanate's history. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check whether urban students and workers report more say over marriage and work than kin in the villages of Aceh Besar.
- **Pidie:** check whether trading journeys away from home (*meudagang*) change who decides in the household, and how studies measure it.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Aceh Utara):** check household decisions in families tied to the gas industry and its wages.
- **West coast fishing villages:** check *Panglima Laot* authority against the fishers' own choices about when to sail.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *angkap* and *juelen* marriages for who decides residence, and coffee farmers' independence from buyers.
- **Alas:** check clan rules on marriage choice.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check the campaign that made Subulussalam its own city.
- **Aneuk Jamee:** check whether marriage residence follows Acehnese or Minangkabau-style patterns (comparison only for the latter).
- **Tamiang:** check its 2002 split from Aceh Timur as a case of Malay self-rule.
- **Simeulue:** check its 1999 split from West Aceh as an island case.
- **Men and women; dayah pupils and university students:** check who may choose a teacher, a school or a spouse.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesian national survey averages; Acehnese families in Malaysia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. National surveys give provincial numbers; the customary institutions are well studied in Indonesian; personal autonomy in the Gayo and Alas lands is thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for surveys and governance studies; French for Lombard; Acehnese and Gayo for marriage terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** special autonomy and sharia rules are law (Libra for the letter, Capricorn for the spirit); mental-health confinement and youth arrests touch living people.
- **Held for review:** the sultanate's dealings with South Asian-born scholars over who may decide matters of belief.
