# Aceh: Recklessness (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue languages; Indonesian for police, health and disaster data; Dutch for colonial reports; Arabic religious terms. **Concept in one line:** courage or initiative pushed past its useful range; acting without regard for danger or consequence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check whether street racing and risky riding are framed as youth phases, crimes or moral failings.
- **Pidie and Aceh Besar:** check cockfighting and *beude trieng* records in village life.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Bireuen):** check the colonial *Atjeh-moord* files by district against local memory.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Meulaboh):** check gold-mining districts and post-tsunami return to the shore.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *kemali* rules and gold mining in Gayo Lues.
- **Alas:** check river risk for locals against rafting tourism.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check river and swamp travel risks; likely thin in print.
- **Aneuk Jamee and South Aceh:** check fishing-boat losses on the southwest coast.
- **Tamiang:** check the 2006 flood and later building choices.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check island fishers and surf tourism as two kinds of risk.
- **Young men and young women:** check how risk-taking is recorded for each, rather than assuming who takes it.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesian national road-safety data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Police, disaster and health data exist; colonial files are rich but slanted; studies of everyday daring and its moral judgment are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for colonial reports; Indonesian for police and disaster data; Acehnese and Gayo for folk terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Atjeh-moord* and *Aceh pungo* are colonial slurs as well as subjects; drug and gambling matters carry criminal risk for informants (the rules themselves go to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** any colonial or court-era pointer on rash or desperate acts that centers on South Asian-born figures or South Asian-heritage communities.
