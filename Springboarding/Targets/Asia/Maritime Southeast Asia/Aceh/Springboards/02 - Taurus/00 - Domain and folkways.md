# Aceh: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue languages (Devayan, Sigulai), Kluet and Singkil; Malay/Indonesian for official and scholarly material; Arabic loans in religious usage. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, food, dress and the ways wealth is gained, shown, shared and passed on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check what townspeople buy and keep against what villagers of Aceh Besar store, since the capital's gold and market trade may skew provincial figures.
- **Pidie:** check how far households hold their savings in gold, given long trading absences.
- **North coast around Lhokseumawe:** check gas-era wages and their aftermath for changes in what a household counts as wealth.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang, Lamno):** check post-tsunami household inventories against pre-2004 ones.
- **Gayo highlands:** check coffee gardens and gold against land as the store of value.
- **Alas of Southeast Aceh:** check rice land and cattle as holdings, and the words used for them.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whether oil-palm plots or river and swamp goods count first as a family's holdings.
- **Aneuk Jamee of the southwest:** check nutmeg and fishing as household wealth in Tapaktuan and Susoh.
- **Tamiang:** check heirloom goods and Malay terms on the border.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check cloves, coconuts and boats as island holdings.
- **Chinese Acehnese of Peunayong and Javanese settlers:** check shop stock and transmigration plots as distinct holdings.
- **Comparison only:** remittance-funded houses built by migrants abroad; Indonesian consumption averages as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Household spending and loss data are plentiful; folk categories of goods and the words for them are thin outside Acehnese and Gayo.
- **Scope:** which lands the post-war nation holds is still open (its tracker names only Indonesia as pre-war land); this sign's sheets take the former province as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for exchange words and *hadih maja*; Indonesian for statistics and theses; Dutch and Malay for court and colonial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land and inheritance quarrels after 2004 (any rules go to Libra or Capricorn); resource revenue was a conflict grievance.
- **Held for review:** for what the port treasured, the cloth imports and account books kept by South Asian-heritage families of traders.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Daud and Durie's dictionary for *hareuta*, *meuh*, *mayam*, *peng* and *jeulamee*; build a word list with dialect notes.
2. Search university repositories for "jeulamee Aceh" and "mahar emas mayam" and note district and year.
3. Pull BPS Aceh's spending tables by district for the latest Susenas.
4. Read the 2005 damage and loss assessment for household asset categories.
5. Search the Balai Pelestarian Nilai Budaya catalog for "kuliner Aceh", "pakaian adat" and "Simeulue".
