# Aceh: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Devayan and Sigulai; Indonesian for modern studies. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how families who lost houses in 2004 rebuilt their store of things, and what they chose first.
- **Pidie:** check whether men away trading leave their goods in the wife's house, and how such goods are spoken of.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Bireuen):** check heirloom gold and daggers against bought goods from the gas years.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check aid-supplied household kits against the objects families had before.
- **Gayo highlands:** check which ceremonial cloths a family keeps and which it lends.
- **Alas:** check heirlooms tied to *merga* lines and who holds them.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check how belongings move between river villages and new towns; likely thin.
- **Aneuk Jamee:** check heirloom objects and their Minang-style names (comparison only for the Minang side).
- **Tamiang:** check Malay heirlooms in former court families.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check what islanders saved in 2004 when they fled uphill.
- **Children and the old:** check what a child may call its own and what the elderly hand on before death.
- **Comparison only:** objects carried by Acehnese who settled in Malaysia or Scandinavia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Crafts, manuscripts and colonial loot are documented; everyday belongings, lending and keepsakes are barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese and Gayo for object names; Dutch for colonial collection records; Indonesian for pawn and crime data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** looted objects in foreign museums; lost and unclaimed property after the tsunami; the division of a dead person's goods is law (Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** keepsakes and devotional objects of South Asian-heritage families in Aceh's old port quarters.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Leigh's *Hands of Time* and list objects described as heirlooms, with district.
