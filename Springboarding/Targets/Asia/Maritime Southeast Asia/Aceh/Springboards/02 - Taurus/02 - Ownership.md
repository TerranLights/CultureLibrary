# Aceh: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Singkil and Simeulue languages; Indonesian for land and fiscal records; Arabic for endowment terms. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whose claims survived the loss of papers in 2004, and how heirs were named.
- **Pidie:** check *gala* and *mawah* in the rice plains, where absent traders hold land worked by others.
- **North coast around Lhokseumawe:** check land acquired for the gas fields and industrial zone, and how villagers recall the sales.
- **West coast (Nagan Raya, Meulaboh):** check plantation concessions against village gardens.
- **Gayo highlands:** check coffee gardens opened on forest land, and whether clearing or purchase grounds the claim.
- **Alas of Southeast Aceh:** check *merga* land and the Leuser park boundary.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check how concession maps drawn in Jakarta met village claims to peat land.
- **Aneuk Jamee and South Aceh:** check whether nutmeg trees, rather than the ground under them, are what a family claims.
- **Tamiang:** check customary Malay land against estates near the border.
- **Simeulue:** check clove groves and land opened along the ring road for who could prove a claim.
- **Women:** check whose names stand on titles and house papers, district by district.
- **Comparison only:** title practice of the national land agency elsewhere in Sumatra; houses bought by Acehnese in Kedah.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on post-tsunami land and on conflict-era seizures; mixed on folk tenure; thin on highland and island customary claims.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for tenure words; Indonesian for land and fiscal records; Arabic for endowment deeds; Dutch for colonial land files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land seized in 1946 and during the conflict; plantation disputes involve criminalized villagers; property law, inheritance shares and *qanun* rules go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** endowments and shop property in the old port held by South Asian-heritage merchant lineages.
