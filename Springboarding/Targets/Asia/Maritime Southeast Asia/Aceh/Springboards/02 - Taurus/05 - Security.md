# Aceh: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Devayan and Sigulai; Indonesian for program and hazard data; Malay and Arabic for protective texts. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether people who lived through 2004 in the coastal wards now rank tsunami risk, crime or money as the first insecurity.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check mutual-aid funds against kin support where many men are away.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Aceh Utara):** check how people speak of physical safety in former conflict hot spots, district by district.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Meulaboh):** check fishers' and farmers' reserves after losing boats and fields in 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check coffee-income swings and landslide exposure as two kinds of insecurity.
- **Alas of Southeast Aceh:** check flood and flash-flood exposure on the Lawe Alas, and village responses.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whether yearly floods there lower FSVA ratings, and how households stock up before the rains.
- **Aneuk Jamee and South Aceh:** check whether Tapaktuan and Labuhan Haji keep warding rites under Minang-style names.
- **Tamiang:** check flood recovery and plantation wages as the safety net.
- **Simeulue:** check how the *smong* warning is passed on now, and the island's food and fuel supply.
- **Women-headed households:** check whether widows from the conflict and the tsunami appear separately in food-security data.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese workers in Malaysia, whose earnings insure households at home; Indonesian national food-security averages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster risk, conflict violence and food security (agencies, NGOs, World Bank); thin for folk protective practice outside the coast.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for program and hazard data; Acehnese for rite names; Jawi Malay and Arabic for *Tajul Muluk*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conflict-era abuses; protective charms are disapproved of by some clerics, so informants may hide them; security policing and sharia patrols are law (Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** charms and warding formulas of South Asian origin that entered the Acehnese repertoire, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the World Bank conflict monitoring updates and tabulate incident types by district.
2. Pull the latest FSVA district ratings and BNPB risk index for Aceh.
3. Search university repositories for "Rabu Abeh", "tulak bala Aceh" and "pageu gampong".
4. Find JKA program reports and press coverage of its use and funding.
5. Search Jawi print catalogs for "Tajul Muluk" editions and note their protective sections.
