# Aceh: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue languages; Malay and Portuguese for early trade accounts; Indonesian for statistics. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a people counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how civil servants, contractors and gold traders rank against one another in local talk of who is rich.
- **Pidie:** check returning merchants' houses as wealth display, and whether neighbors read them as success or showing off.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Aceh Utara):** check the gas era's well-paid jobs against the villages around the fields.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check the old pepper fortunes against today's plantation and mining wealth.
- **Gayo highlands:** check coffee wealth by household and whether it is shown in houses, vehicles or pilgrimages.
- **Alas:** check cattle, rice and cacao as the valley's measures of a rich family.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check who in the district is called rich since palm oil: mill owners, officials or a few planter families.
- **Aneuk Jamee and South Aceh:** check whether reported disease in the nutmeg gardens changed which families count as well-off.
- **Tamiang:** check cross-border trade with Medan as a separate route to riches from the estates.
- **Simeulue:** check the clove years against the present.
- **Chinese Acehnese:** check their part in trade wealth as they tell it, without assuming it.
- **Comparison only:** fortunes made by Acehnese entrepreneurs in Kuala Lumpur; Gini ratios for neighboring provinces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trade history and for post-2005 political economy; mixed for inequality data; thin for folk words on wealth outside Acehnese.
- **Languages needed:** Portuguese, Arabic and English for early travelers; Dutch for colonial trade records; Acehnese and Gayo for wealth idioms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** new wealth after the peace is tied to political patronage; reconstruction money was contested; zakat and its use are governed by *qanun* (law to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** the port trade and credit of South Asian-heritage merchants in the sultanate era, and the travelers' passages on them.
