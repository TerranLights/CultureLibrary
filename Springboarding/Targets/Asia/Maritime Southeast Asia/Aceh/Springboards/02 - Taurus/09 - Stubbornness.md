# Aceh: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Simeulue and Singkil languages; Dutch for war-era records; Indonesian for press and theses. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check landowners in toll-road and port disputes, and whether urban and village holdouts give different reasons.
- **Pidie:** check how the long war is remembered as refusal at home, and whether younger people tell it the same way.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Aceh Utara):** check former GAM areas for how the end of armed refusal in 2005 is spoken of: as yielding, as victory or as pause.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Meulaboh):** check plantation-license protests by Nagan Raya villagers and how long each lasted.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the Linge and Tampur campaigns, and who among Gayo villagers supported projects instead.
- **Alas:** check how Kutacane villages argued with park rangers over enclaves, and who conceded.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check how long-running quarrels over places of worship were held and resolved; sensitive.
- **Aneuk Jamee and South Aceh:** check whether iron-ore mining near Tapaktuan met lasting refusal, and the words used on each side.
- **Tamiang:** check estate-land disputes that lasted decades.
- **Simeulue:** check whether island elders blocked or bargained over the airport and ring-road land; probably unrecorded.
- **Women and men; elders and the young:** check who is called stubborn and who determined, for the same behavior.
- **Comparison only:** the GAM leadership's decades in exile in Sweden; Indonesian national press framing of Aceh.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and peace-talk holdouts; mixed for land and project disputes; thin for everyday stubbornness in families.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the 1892 report; English for the mediators' papers; Indonesian for land and mining press; Gayo for highland campaigns.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a colonial and national stereotype of Acehnese obstinacy exists and must be studied as a label, not used; place-of-worship quarrels in Singkil and religious disputes on the north coast are live; land disputes may be in court (law to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** the stance of South Asian-born court scholars in the sultanate's quarrels over doctrine, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Leiden catalog for "Hikayat Prang Sabi" and note manuscripts with seizure records.
2. Read Awaludin's *Peace in Aceh* and list each deadlock and how it ended.
3. Search press archives for "tolak tambang Linge" and "PLTA Tampur".
4. Search for studies of colonial stereotypes of Aceh, starting with Dutch-language historiography of the Aceh War.
5. Query thesis portals for "anak keras kepala Aceh" and "grit mahasiswa Aceh".
