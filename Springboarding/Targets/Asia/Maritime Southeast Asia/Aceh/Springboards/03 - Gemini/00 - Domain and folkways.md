## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how far children in the capital still learn Acehnese at home, where a shift would show first.
- **Pidie:** check the dialect line and the *meunasah* reading lessons in Pidie villages against the capital's schools.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check the weight of large *dayah* against state schools in the Pasè dialect area.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang, Lamno):** check how tsunami-era relocation changed who lives next to whom and which dialect neighbors share.
- **Gayo highlands (Takengon, Bener Meriah, Gayo Lues):** check Gayo-Indonesian-Acehnese switching in markets and schools.
- **Southeast Aceh (Kutacane):** check where Alas, Gayo, Karo and Indonesian are each used, since the valley is a crossing point of languages.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check the Singkil language and its ties to Pakpak against Aneuk Jamee and Acehnese in the same towns.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check which of Devayan, Sigulai, Leukon and Haloban a child hears first, and when Indonesian enters.
- **Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Tamiang, Chinese Acehnese and Javanese settlers:** check the home language each keeps against the one learned for neighbors and school.
- **Deaf Acehnese:** check local signing against the national sign languages taught in Banda Aceh.
- **Comparison only:** the Acehnese of Malaysia and Scandinavia, for heritage-language loss; Indonesia's national school system as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Statistics and *dayah* studies are plentiful; speech ways (address, silence, gesture, turn-taking) are barely described outside Acehnese and Gayo.
- **Scope:** the post-war borders are not yet drawn; with only "Indonesia" listed as pre-war land in the tracker, the communication-and-learning sheets cover the districts of the former province, from Sabang to Singkil and Simeulue.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese and Gayo to hear speech ways in use; Indonesian for theses; Dutch for Snouck; Arabic for the *dayah* curriculum.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** schools were burned in the conflict years; language loyalty can touch separatist politics; sharia rules on mixed classrooms belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the speech and schooling of South Asian-heritage communities in the old port quarters, kept back as a category.
