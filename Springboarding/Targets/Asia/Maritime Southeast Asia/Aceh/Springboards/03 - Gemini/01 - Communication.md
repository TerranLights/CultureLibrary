# Aceh: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for prayer and sermon. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether city talk mixes Indonesian into Acehnese address, and with which listeners.
- **Pidie:** check the Pidie dialect's own forms of address against the Banda forms, since dictionaries lean on one or the other.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check whether gas-era workplaces brought new registers of talk with outsiders.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check how relief-era contact with foreign workers changed who interprets for whom.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *sebuku* and formal speech against everyday Gayo talk.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check how Alas speakers address Karo and Gayo neighbors, and in which language.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check which language a mixed household uses at the table.
- **Simeulue:** check how talk between Devayan and Sigulai speakers proceeds, and whether Indonesian is the bridge.
- **Aneuk Jamee, Kluet and Tamiang:** check verse exchange at weddings as a form of formal talk.
- **Deaf Acehnese and Chinese Acehnese:** check what language each uses with neighbors and officials.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese in Malaysia, for how address terms shift abroad; Indonesian state media, as the register Acehnese broadcasts are measured against.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Address and politeness have Indonesian thesis coverage for Acehnese; recorded conversation (pauses, overlaps, gesture) is likely absent for every language of Aceh.
- **Languages needed:** spoken Acehnese for address and recitation; Jawi Malay for the court letters; Indonesian for media and disaster studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** press freedom in the conflict and martial-law years; speech against sharia rules may carry risk (law to Libra or Capricorn); Deaf informants need an interpreter they trust.
- **Held for review:** letters to foreign rulers that South Asian-born court scholars may have drafted (category only).
