# Aceh: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian for schools and statistics; Arabic and Jawi Malay for religious texts. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution and higher learning go to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check the balance of preschool against home and *meunasah* teaching in city and nearby villages.
- **Pidie:** check how children learn a trade while helping in family shops before migrating to sell.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check how boys and girls divide time between state school and evening religious lessons.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang, Lamno):** check how far the generation schooled in relief camps learned differently.
- **Gayo highlands:** check what children learn by working in coffee gardens at harvest.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check the Alas arts and farm work as schools outside school.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check the Abdurrauf heritage in local teaching, and where Christian and Muslim children learn side by side.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check whether children learn island verse before they learn to read.
- **Aneuk Jamee, Kluet and Tamiang:** check which language a child first learns to read in.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check heritage-language and religious lessons kept at home.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese students in Malaysia, Egypt and Turkey; national scores for Indonesia as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. School statistics are plentiful; learning by watching and pitching in, and the lullaby and play tradition, are little studied outside a few theses.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay and Arabic for primers and the *Tarjuman*; Acehnese for lullabies; Indonesian for school data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** many schools were burned in 2003 and earlier; corporal discipline in some boarding schools; gender separation in classes is regulated (law to Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** teaching manuals produced at the sultan's court by South Asian-born scholars, as a category only.
