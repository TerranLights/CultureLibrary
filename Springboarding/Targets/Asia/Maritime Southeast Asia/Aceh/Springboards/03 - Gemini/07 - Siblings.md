## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how sibling duties work in small urban families against larger village ones nearby.
- **Pidie:** check whether one sibling keeps the home while another trades away, and how that is settled.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check how siblings divided roles when one joined the armed movement and another did not.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang, Lamno):** check households in which elder siblings raised younger ones after 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how the *sara ine* unit lines up with Acehnese households, and who counts inside it.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas sibling terms and clan ties between brothers.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check sibling ties within clans that include Muslims and Christians.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check whether elder siblings take younger ones to the mainland for school.
- **Aneuk Jamee, Kluet and Tamiang:** check the Tamiang telling of the Green Princess against the Acehnese court telling.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check sibling terms and seniority kept from home traditions.
- **Comparison only:** siblings split between Aceh and Malaysia by migration; national family-size averages for Indonesia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Fertility and orphan data are good; sibling terms for the smaller languages and the felt bond between siblings are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese and Gayo for kin terms; Malay for the Green Princess variants; Indonesian for child-protection and fertility data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tsunami and conflict orphans are vulnerable informants; inheritance among siblings and marriage guardianship are law (Libra for the letter, Capricorn for the spirit); siblings on opposite sides of the conflict.
- **Held for review:** household and sibling customs in port families of South Asian heritage (category only).
