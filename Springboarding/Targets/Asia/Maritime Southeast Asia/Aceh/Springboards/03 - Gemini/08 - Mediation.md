# Aceh: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for intercession; English for the peace-process record. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision, and more broadly the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether city residents turn to the *keuchik*, to clergy or to the police first, and in which kinds of quarrel.
- **Pidie:** check how feuds between families are brokered, and by whom, given the district's conflict history.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check go-betweens between villagers and the industrial companies.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Nagan Raya):** check mediation of plantation and palm-oil land disputes.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *sarak opat* alongside Acehnese village forms.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas elders and the Leuser-edge disputes between villagers and park authorities.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check the interfaith go-betweens after 2015.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check the island elders' work on land quarrels.
- **Aneuk Jamee, Kluet and Tamiang:** check who settles quarrels where these groups and Acehnese live together.
- **Javanese settlers:** check who mediated their return after displacement in the conflict years.
- **Comparison only:** the Finnish-led 2005 talks as an outside model; Indonesian national mediation bodies as the state alternative.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the peace process (English and Indonesian); mixed for village practice; thin for the smaller peoples and for intercession as lived practice.
- **Languages needed:** English for the peace-process studies; Indonesian for village and labor studies; Gayo for *sarak opat*; Arabic for the intercession texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peace-talk records still touch living negotiators; shrine visits are disputed by some clerics; customary justice, courts and *qanun* on dispute settlement are law (letter to Libra, spirit to Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** intercessory texts and shrines tied to South Asian-born religious figures in Aceh, as a category only.
