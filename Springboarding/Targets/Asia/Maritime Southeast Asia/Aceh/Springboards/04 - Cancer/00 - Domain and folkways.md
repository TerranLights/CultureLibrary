*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how the verandas and inner room of the old stilt house map onto the concrete houses built after 2004.
- **Pidie:** check, in families whose men trade in other towns, who keeps the house and children meanwhile.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check whether company housing near the old gas fields changed which kin live together.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang, Lamno):** check how aid-built houses on new plots altered which relatives share a lot.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the clan village against the household, to see which of the two people call "family".
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas clan-village households beside Gayo and Karo households in the same valley.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check who shares a roof in river-town households where Muslim and Christian kin both live.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check whether island houses and family words follow any mainland pattern.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet (South and Southwest Aceh):** check the house forms the Jamee carried from the Minangkabau side, and Kluet ones beside them.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay house and household forms at the North Sumatra border.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check which house layouts and family orders each brought.
- **Comparison only:** emigrant households in Kedah and Denmark; national household averages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Colonial ethnography and census tables are rich for the lowlands; Alas, Kluet, Singkil, Tamiang and Simeulue are thin; the Indonesia Family Life Survey left the province out (*check*).
- **Lands:** the tracker header carries only "Indonesia" as pre-war land, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets cover the former province's districts.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Jacobs, Kreemer and the Gayo monograph; early-modern Dutch and Malay for Houtman; Acehnese and Gayo for house-room words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tsunami and conflict losses inside families; widows heading households; marriage, divorce, guardianship and inheritance as rules go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** court-era manuals on the household and marriage by South Asian-born scholars at the sultanate, as a category only.
