# Aceh: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for teaching on upbringing. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how working parents in the capital split early care between day care, grandparents and paid helpers.
- **Pidie:** check who raises the children while a father trades in another town, and how he takes part from afar.
- **North coast (Bireuen, North Aceh):** check stunting and feeding data against village custom in the districts with the worst figures.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check how children raised after 2004 by relatives other than parents were fed and taught.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the *peri mestike* teachings against what coffee-farming households actually ask of children.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas soothing, feeding and teaching words against Gayo Lues neighbors.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check child-feeding custom in river villages where health posts are far away.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check upbringing in Devayan and Sigulai households, and how island children are sent to school on the mainland.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check which words for coaxing and scolding a toddler Jamee and Kluet parents use.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay infant customs shared with Langkat across the border.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check which infant-care customs each kept and which they took from neighbors.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese families in Malaysia and Scandinavia raising children in another school system; Indonesia's national nutrition averages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Nutrition and health-post data are rich; nurture as felt warmth, praise and comfort is thin; fathers and the smaller peoples are barely covered.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for cradle and rite words; Gayo for *peri mestike*; Indonesian for health surveys; Dutch or English for Snouck.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stunting figures can stigmatize districts and mothers; children cared for outside their parents' home after 2004 or the conflict; rules on breastfeeding and child protection go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** a baby's first foods and soothing practices as kept by families of South Asian heritage (category; no pointer named).

## 5. First queries
1. Read Snouck's childhood chapter and list every rite and object of infancy he names.

3. Search repositories for "peutron aneuk", "ayon bayi Aceh", "keterlibatan ayah Aceh" and "pengasuhan cucu Aceh".
4. Search folklore collections for "geunteut" and Gayo collections for "peri mestike".
