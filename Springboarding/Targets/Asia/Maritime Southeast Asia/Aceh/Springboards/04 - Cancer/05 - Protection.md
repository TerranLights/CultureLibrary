# Aceh: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for protective prayer. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how families who lived through 2004 now guard children near the shore, against families who moved inland.
- **Pidie:** check how households protected sons from arrest or recruitment in the conflict years, and who did the protecting.
- **North coast (North Aceh, East Aceh):** check civilian accounts of forced screening and of extortion in the districts where reports concentrate (*check*).
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Nagan Raya, Southwest Aceh):** check how fishers' families ask protection for boats and crews before the season.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how settler and Gayo villages protected themselves from each other's militias and from the armed movement.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check flash-flood protection in Alas villages along the Alas river and its tributaries.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check how mixed-faith villages describe protecting their places of worship and their neighbors.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check what islanders do to guard infants and the sick when the nearest hospital is a sea crossing away.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check what a child is told about the sea, the river and the forest edge on the south coast.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check river-flood protection along the Tamiang, set against the 2006 flood experience.
- **Women-led households and the elderly:** check who protects the households with no adult man present.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese in Malaysia and Scandinavia who fled the conflict, for protection sought abroad; Indonesia's national child-protection system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Disaster and conflict-protection records are rich; folk protection for infants and houses lives in manuscripts and practice; men's protective role is little studied.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay and Arabic for charm manuscripts; Acehnese for spirit and blessing terms; Indonesian for agency and rights reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced civilian screening and extortion name living perpetrators; manuscript owners may not want protective texts copied or shown; child-protection and sharia-policing rules go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** court-era treatises on the ruler's duty to protect his subjects, written by South Asian-born scholars, as a category only.
