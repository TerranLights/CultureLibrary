# Aceh: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; Chinese for the early account. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check who took in relatives after 2004 and for how long, against those who went to barracks.
- **Pidie and Pidie Jaya:** check how families sheltered beside their own damaged houses after the 2016 quake, and why.
- **North coast (Bireuen, North Aceh):** check the 2003 camps and how families chose between a camp and kin in town.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check barrack life in the districts with the longest stays.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how settler and Gayo families sheltered one another, or did not, in the 2013 quake.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check flash-flood shelter in Alas villages and who opens their houses.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check river-flood shelter and the role of churches and mosques for neighbors of the other faith.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check whether islanders keep hill refuges or huts for storms and quakes, and who may use them.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check field-hut and garden-hut forms in South Aceh's nutmeg and rice lands.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check shelter during the river floods along the Tamiang.
- **Javanese settlers displaced in 1999-2001:** check where they found refuge, inside and outside the province.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese refugees sheltered in Malaysia during the conflict; Indonesia's national disaster-shelter standards.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for post-2004 and post-quake shelter (donor, NGO and agency reports); thin for everyday lodging of travelers and for street homelessness.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese, or a translation, for Ma Huan; Acehnese for hut and lodging words; Indonesian for relief and program records; English for donor archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** barrack-era abuse and aid diversion; camp records may name displaced families still at risk; rules on who may lodge whom overnight go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** refugee boats from camps in South Asia that landed on Aceh's shores from 2015, and how villages sheltered them, as a category only.
