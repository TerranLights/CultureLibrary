# Aceh: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for funeral and mercy terms. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check who cares for elders in small urban households where grown children work long hours.
- **Pidie:** check how care for parents is divided when some children work in other towns and others stay.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check family care for those left with injuries or trauma from the conflict.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check care for elders who lost the children expected to look after them in 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check who keeps plant-remedy knowledge and who is sent for first when someone falls ill.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas healers and how they sit beside the district hospital in Kutacane.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check how neighbors of different faiths help one another in illness and at funerals.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check who travels with the sick on the sea crossing, and who stays to keep the household.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check who is called to a sickbed in a Kluet village, and whether Jamee families send for different healers.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay customs of tending the sick and the dying.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check what care customs they kept, and who washes and buries their dead.
- **Comparison only:** paid caregiving done by Acehnese women abroad, Malaysia first; Jakarta's national elder-care schemes as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Clinical and nursing studies are plentiful; care as daily practice and as a felt duty is little described; Alas, Kluet and Simeulue healers are almost absent from print.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for sickroom and healer words; Gayo for plant names; Jawi Malay for funeral manuals; Indonesian for nursing theses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mental illness and disability carry stigma for families; healers may not share remedies; sex-segregated care of the dying and dead is governed partly by rules that go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** physicians and remedy sellers of South Asian heritage once active in the capital (kept as a type, unnamed).
