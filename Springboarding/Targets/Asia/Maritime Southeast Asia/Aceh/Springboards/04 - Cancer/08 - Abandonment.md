# Aceh: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for orphan and foundling terms. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how elders end up in the provincial home, and whether families visit.
- **Pidie:** check when a trader's long absence stops being read as work and starts being read as desertion.
- **North coast (North Aceh, Bireuen):** check former fighters' talk of being left behind after the peace, set against their families' views.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check children who lost both parents in 2004 and how kin decided who would take them.
- **Gayo highlands:** check abandoned gardens and settler villages, and who came back.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check children left with kin while parents work in North Sumatra.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whether children in river villages are sent to children's homes in town, and why.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check households left when men fish far out or migrate, and who heads them.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check how tales and sayings in Jamee and Kluet speak of a parent who leaves.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check families split by work across the line in Langkat and Medan.
- **Javanese settlers:** check the settlements abandoned in 1999-2001 and families' accounts of being left unprotected.
- **Comparison only:** families with members in Malaysia or Scandinavia since the conflict; Indonesia's national child-welfare categories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Welfare counts and NGO reports exist; how families judge a leaver, and the fear of being left, are barely recorded; tales in the smaller languages are scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese and Jawi for the war epic; Indonesian for welfare tables and theses; Malay for Tamiang and older literary terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abandoned babies and their mothers face stigma and prosecution; children in care are protected informants; divorce for desertion and the conditional repudiation made at marriage are law (Libra for the letter, Capricorn for the spirit).
- **Held for review:** accounts of South Asian-heritage traders' spouses and children left in Aceh when the men sailed home, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the *Hikayat Prang Sabi* frame story in an edition and mark every line on wives, parents and leaving.
2. Pull PMKS tables for *anak terlantar* and *lansia terlantar* by district.
3. Search repositories for "istri ditinggal merantau Aceh", "anak TKI Aceh" and "kelekatan anak panti asuhan Aceh".
4. Search press archives for "bayi dibuang Aceh" and "mantan kombatan kecewa MoU".
