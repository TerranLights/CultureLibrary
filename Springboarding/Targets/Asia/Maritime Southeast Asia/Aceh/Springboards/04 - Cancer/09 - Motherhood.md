# Aceh: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for religious teaching on mothers. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how civil-servant and professional mothers in the capital combine paid work with the after-birth rest.
- **Pidie:** check what say mothers have in a child's schooling and marriage, and through which customs it is exercised.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check mothers' searches for sons missing since the conflict.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check how women who were pregnant or gave birth in the months after the 2004 wave describe becoming mothers then.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the *Atu Belah* telling against living mothers' views of what children owe them.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check Alas birth customs and the midwives who keep them.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check birth customs in Singkil-speaking families, Muslim and Christian.
- **Simeulue and Pulau Banyak:** check the island midwife's standing and how mothers reach hospitals.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check how a mother's brother and a mother's own kin weigh in on Jamee and Kluet children.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay pregnancy and after-birth customs.
- **Javanese settlers and Chinese Acehnese:** check which mothering customs each kept, and whether Javanese families keep a seventh-month rite of their own.
- **Comparison only:** mothers in the Scandinavian and Malaysian diaspora, for what is kept far from kin; national maternal-mortality rates as a benchmark.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Maternal-health data are rich; mothers' standing, authority and voice are little studied outside a few theses; the smaller peoples' birth customs are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for birth and rest terms; Gayo for the tale and highland midwifery; Indonesian for health profiles; English for Srimulyani.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal deaths and stillbirths; mothers of the disappeared; child marriage, polygamy and custody are law (Libra for the letter, Capricorn for the spirit).
- **Held for review:** pregnancy vows and shrine visits made by women of South Asian heritage in old Banda Aceh (category, no names).

## 5. First queries
1. Read two or more tellings of *Atu Belah* and note how the mother is described.
2. Pull *Profil Kesehatan Aceh* for maternal deaths and birth attendants by district.
3. Search repositories for "madeueng", "ba bu kehamilan Aceh", "mak blien" and "upacara kehamilan Alas".
4. Search NGO archives for testimony by mothers of the disappeared in Aceh.
