# Aceh: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Singkil, Devayan; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for the vocabulary of novelty. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether filigree workshops in the city and smithies in Baet speak of new designs the same way.
- **Pidie:** check embroidery and household textiles made for weddings, and how far makers alter inherited patterns.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Bireuen):** check *rapa'i* makers and the rhythm-masters who compose new pieces for village teams.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check how house and boat craft changed when builders worked to donor plans after 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *kerawang* makers against *ceh* poets, two kinds of Gayo invention with different owners.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check Alas wood, weaving and song craft; nearly absent from print.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check weaving and carving where Malay, Pakpak and Acehnese crafts meet.
- **Simeulue:** check boat and mat craft, and who on the island is known for a new technique.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check whether Jamee goldwork and textiles keep Minangkabau-side designs or have new local ones.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay embroidery and woodwork on the border with North Sumatra.
- **Chinese Acehnese of Peunayong:** check signboard painting, shop design and festival decoration as their own makers' tradition.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesian national craft councils' prizes; Malaysian revival of Acehnese-style crafts among emigrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Crafts and performance are well studied as heritage; makers' own ideas of invention, credit and copying are rarely asked; Alas and Kluet craft is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay and Arabic for Hamzah's verse and illuminated pages; Gayo for *ceh* composition terms; Acehnese for craft vocabulary; Indonesian for design and choreography theses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some smiths and healers treat techniques as family secrets; debate over which new forms of music and dance are acceptable in public goes, as rules, to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** ornament and textile designs brought to the port by South Asian-heritage artisans, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Drewes and Brakel's introduction for how Hamzah's authorship is established; note Jawi composing terms.
2. Search USK, ISBI Aceh and IAIN Takengon repositories for "tari kreasi Aceh", "motif kerawang Gayo" and "penciptaan syair didong".
3. Search press and craft studies for "pandai besi Baet" and "pinto Aceh sejarah".
4. Search the DJKI communal inventory for every Aceh entry and note the community named as owner.
