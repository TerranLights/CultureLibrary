# Aceh: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Singkil, Devayan; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; Dutch for the colonial campaigns. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check the memory of the 1903 surrender in the old capital, and how school lessons tell it.
- **Pidie:** check how the 1950 slight and the 1953 revolt are remembered in Daud Beureueh's home district.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Bireuen):** check what survivors of military sweeps say about being degraded before neighbors.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check whether depending on aid after 2004 is described as a loss of face.
- **Gayo highlands:** check Kuta Reh and the 1904 campaign in Gayo Lues memory and verse.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check Alas memory of the same expedition and of custom fines for insult.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check mockery and slights between Muslim and Christian villages, and how custom repairs them.
- **Simeulue:** check island satire and teasing in *nandong* or songs.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check whether jokes about origin and speech target Jamee or Kluet speakers.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check plantation-era treatment of laborers and how their families recall it.
- **Young couples and women:** check who is most often shamed in crowd actions, and who leads them.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese laborers in Malaysia and how they describe being looked down on; Indonesian national bullying surveys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Colonial and conflict humiliations are well documented; everyday teasing and private shame are thin; crowd shaming is covered mostly by press.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Kempees and treaty texts; Acehnese for Do Karim's verse and sayings; Gayo for highland memory; Indonesian for inquiries and press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual violence in the conflict, crowd shaming of couples and photographs of the dead; public punishments and custom fines go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** slurs and mocking names once aimed at South Asian-heritage residents of the port, kept only as a category.
