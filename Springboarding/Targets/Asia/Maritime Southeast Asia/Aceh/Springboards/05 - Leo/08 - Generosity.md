## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how urban households balance mosque giving, aid to kin and treating friends.
- **Pidie:** check Maulid trays and *kanduri apam* as giving that also shows a family's means.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, Bireuen):** check sponsorship of village feasts by those who earned in the gas years, and how that changed after.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check how survivors describe gifts received after 2004, and gifts they later gave on.
- **Gayo highlands:** check coffee-harvest sharing and wedding pooling in clan villages.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check feast sponsorship and clan obligations at weddings.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check gifts exchanged between neighbors of different faiths at feasts.
- **Simeulue:** check catch-sharing and island hospitality to visitors from the mainland.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet:** check whether wedding contributions follow Jamee or Kluet custom, and who records them.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay feast customs and what plantation households can afford to give.
- **Chinese Acehnese of Peunayong:** check festival gifts and any giving that crosses community lines, in both directions.
- **Comparison only:** remittances sent home by Acehnese in Malaysia; the CAF World Giving Index score for Indonesia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Feasts, alms and endowments are recorded; everyday treating and private help are thin; Alas, Singkil and Simeulue giving is barely in print.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic for endowment and alms terms; Acehnese for gift and feast words; Gayo for highland pooling; Jawi Malay for Hamzah's verse.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families may not wish to disclose gift amounts; alms collection by the state and endowment rules go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** alms and feasts sponsored by South Asian-heritage merchants in the port quarters, as a category only.
