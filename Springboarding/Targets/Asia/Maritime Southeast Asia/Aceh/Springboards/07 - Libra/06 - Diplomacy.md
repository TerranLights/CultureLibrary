# Aceh: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; classical Malay in Jawi for royal letters; English, Dutch and Ottoman Turkish for foreign archives. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether families descended from the court keep their own protocol for receiving guests.
- **Pidie:** check how news of the Helsinki talks was carried to villages in the district that produced the rebel leadership.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check talks between the Arun gas operator and nearby villages.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Calang):** check how villages dealt with foreign agencies after 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *melengkan* at weddings and the forms used in dealings with coastal officials.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check who speaks for a clan when two clans negotiate a marriage.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check the *telangkai*'s role and Malay courtesy forms near the border.
- **Simeulue:** check how island elders address mainland and national officials.
- **Singkil and Pulau Banyak:** check negotiation forms between Pakpak-related, Haloban and Acehnese-speaking villages.
- **Sabang (Pulau Weh):** check dealings with foreign visitors and traders in the free-port town.
- **Comparison only:** the rebel exile leadership in Sweden and its long-distance politics (Antje Missbach's study *check*); Indonesian foreign-ministry practice as the yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the sultanate's letters and the 2005 talks; mixed for wedding oratory; thin for address forms in Alas, Singkil and the island languages.
- **Languages needed:** classical Malay in Jawi for the royal letters and chronicle; English and Dutch for the early-modern visits; Indonesian for the negotiators' memoirs; Gayo for *melengkan*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the negotiators' accounts disagree with each other; rebel-era diplomacy touches living leaders; refusals and delays as tact must not be read as one voice for all.
- **Held for review:** letters and envoys exchanged between the sultanate and South Asian courts, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. View the 1615 letter at the Bodleian and read Gallop on its forms.
2. Read Awaludin and Kingsbury side by side, noting where they differ.
3. Search repositories for "seumapa", "melengkan" and "pronomina bahasa Aceh".
4. Search the UUPA and Aceh's bureau for the foreign-cooperation provisions.
