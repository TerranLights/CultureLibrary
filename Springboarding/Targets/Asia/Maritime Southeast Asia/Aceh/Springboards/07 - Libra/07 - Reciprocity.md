# Aceh: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for alms and vow vocabulary. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aceh Besar and Pidie:** check which villages invite which during the Maulid months and how the hosting turns are kept.
- **Banda Aceh neighborhoods:** check whether the funeral rice and feast invitations still circulate the same way in rented housing.
- **Fishing ports (Lampulo, Lhok Seudu, Kuala Bubon):** check who funds the sea feast and whether boat owners give more.
- **Gayo highlands:** check day-for-day labor trades at the coffee harvest.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check clan obligations at weddings and funerals, and how they are recorded.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check rotating savings among Malay women traders in Karang Baru and Kuala Simpang.
- **Simeulue:** check contributions brought to island weddings and their return.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check gifts at feasts between Muslim and Christian neighbors.
- **Javanese settler villages:** check *kondangan* gift-giving at weddings beside Acehnese practice.
- **Tsunami-hit and spared villages:** check how hosts of the displaced were later thanked or repaid.
- **Comparison only:** money sent home by Acehnese workers in Malaysia; Susenas national averages on mutual aid.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: feast and folk-tale studies are plentiful; household accounts of what is owed are thin; the island and Alas data are thinnest.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for feast and thanks terms; Gayo for feast obligations and labor words; Indonesian for surveys; Jawi Malay for older advice on gratitude.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the gold-donation story carries political weight about Aceh's place in the republic; debts between kin are private.
- **Held for review:** gifts and loans that ran between Acehnese hosts and South Asian-heritage traders in the port, held back as a category.
