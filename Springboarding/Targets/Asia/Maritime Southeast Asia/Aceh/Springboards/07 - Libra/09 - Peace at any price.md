# Aceh: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for patience and pardon; Dutch for submission documents. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pidie:** check how families of the disappeared weigh speaking out against living beside former perpetrators.
- **North Aceh and Bireuen:** check payments contractors make to former fighters, as courts and journalists recorded them.
- **Banda Aceh:** check the students and urban groups behind the 1999 rally and how they argued against armed struggle.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *sumang* norms and the quiet reconciliation, or its absence, between rebel and militia families.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how quarrels with neighboring peoples are closed and what each side gives up.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check the written terms of the church accords and the Christian residents' own account of them.
- **Women survivors across districts:** check who advises them to settle at home, and on what grounds.
- **Children in abuse cases:** check how often such cases end in a family settlement despite the written law.
- **West coast villages:** check how returning families lived beside those blamed for wartime informing.
- **Descendants of the *uleebalang*:** check how families of the old local chiefs speak of past accommodation with the Dutch and the 1946 upheaval.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesia's national amnesty and restorative-justice precedents as the yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for amnesty, peace and colonial records; moderate for violence-settlement studies; thin for Alas and island practice, and silence itself leaves few records.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the submission papers; Indonesian for statutes and rights reports; Acehnese for the war verse and sayings; Gayo for *sumang*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors of sexual violence and abuse; living former fighters and informants; the Singkil accords remain contested; whether law should yield to peace in spirit is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** recantations demanded of rival teachers at the sultanate, for the court's peace, by South Asian-born scholars (category only).
