# Aceh: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for creed and deathbed texts. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are studied here.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how families who lost many members grieve without bodies, against families who buried their own.
- **Aceh Besar (Lhoknga, Lampuuk):** check whether village memorial days have formed around the 2004 losses.
- **Pidie:** check how the disappeared are mourned where torture sites stood, and whether memorial projects are welcomed.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check how deaths at Simpang KKA (1999) are marked each year and by whom.
- **South Aceh (Jambo Keupok, Kluet):** check how a village massacre of 2003 is remembered among Kluet and Aneuk Jamee speakers.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the two sides in the dispute over rites for the dead, and how militia and rebel deaths are mourned in the same district.
- **Southeast Aceh:** check how Alas clans divide funeral duties.
- **Simeulue:** check how the island speaks of its 2004 dead and of earlier waves.
- **Singkil:** check where Christian residents may bury and how disputes over graves are handled.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check the Malay words for mourning and how many days the bereaved stay home.
- **Comparison only:** whether Acehnese who die in Malaysia are flown home for burial; national mortality tables to set Aceh's figures against.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mass death (tsunami, conflict); mixed for ordinary dying and grief; thin for suicide, stillbirth and the Alas and Singkil peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Gayo to follow the funeral arguments; Jawi Malay and Arabic for deathbed teaching; Acehnese for the martyr verse and laments; Indonesian for health profiles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the bereaved of 2004 and of massacres; the disappeared whose fate is unknown; suicide is seldom spoken of and should be approached through health workers first.
- **Held for review:** deathbed and consolation writing by South Asian-born court scholars, kept back unnamed.
