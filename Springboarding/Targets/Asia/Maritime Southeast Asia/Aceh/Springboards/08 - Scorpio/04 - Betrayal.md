# Aceh: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for religious terms; Dutch for the colonial war. **Concept in one line:** breaking the trust of those who relied on one: turning against, deserting, deceiving or exposing people or a cause to which one owed loyalty.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pidie:** check how informer suspicion is remembered in villages that sent many men to the rebel side.
- **North Aceh and Bireuen:** check how former fighters who joined rival parties after 2005 are spoken of by old comrades.
- **Aceh Barat (Meulaboh):** check local memory of Teuku Umar, who came from this coast, and whether his switch is debated there.
- **Aceh Besar:** check how the war heroine's capture and her commander's role are taught in schools.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how highland militias and rebel supporters frame loyalty, without assuming one Gayo view.
- **Bener Meriah and Javanese settler villages:** check how settlers targeted as suspected loyalists describe being singled out.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how clan obligations shape who reports a kinsman and who stays silent.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check how the old Malay realm's alliances and their breaking appear in local histories.
- **Simeulue and Singkil:** check whether island and Singkil memory casts mainland actors as promise-breakers.
- **Women married to suspected informers:** check how widowhood and stigma passed to them.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese exiles in Sweden and Malaysia and their accounts of the 1999 leadership split; Indonesian national-hero narratives as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the colonial war and the conflict; mixed for marital betrayal; thin for highland and island framings of loyalty.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for van 't Veer and Snouck's critics; classical Malay for the Pasai chronicle; Acehnese for the folktale and songs; Indonesian for rights and court records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "informer" is still a dangerous label for living people and their families; never name individuals from rights reports; adultery as a written offense is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** alliances with South Asian courts and trading powers that the sultanate's chronicles describe as broken, as a category only.
