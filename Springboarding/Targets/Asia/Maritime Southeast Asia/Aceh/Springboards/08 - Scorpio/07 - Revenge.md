# Aceh: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for retaliation terms; Dutch for colonial reprisals. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong, and the alternatives of payment, apology and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how the war heroine's story after her husbands' deaths is taught and retold, and what motive each telling gives her.
- **Pidie and North Aceh:** check whether families of the killed and families of alleged informers live beside each other, and what elders do to prevent payback.
- **East Aceh and Langsa:** check how crowd punishment cases begin, who joins and who intervenes.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how the 1904 reprisals and the later militia years are remembered, without assuming one view.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check clan mechanisms for ending feuds between *merga*.
- **West coast fishing villages:** check retaliation and settlement in disputes over nets and fishing grounds.
- **Aneuk Jamee and Kluet (South Aceh):** check how a 2003 village killing is spoken of and whether redress is still sought.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay customary settlement for injury between families.
- **Simeulue and Singkil:** check how quarrels between islanders and mainland traders are settled.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese in Sweden and Malaysia and their memory of wrongs; Indonesian national data on vigilante violence as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial reprisals and conflict aftermath; mixed for customary settlement; thin for Gayo and Alas feud terms and for women's experience of revenge.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese for the princely epic and payback sayings; Dutch for the colonial press label and expedition captions; Gayo for the curse legends; Indonesian for crisis reports and crowd-violence press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grievances from the conflict are live and can endanger people if published; colonial writers used the label for lone attacks to cast resistance as illness, so read it as their claim; private vengeance against state punishment is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** grudges in sultanate succession quarrels that involved South Asian-heritage merchant families, as a category only.
