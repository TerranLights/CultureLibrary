# Aceh: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic for trial and affliction terms; Dutch for colonial epidemic records. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, family, community or country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar coast:** check how neighborhoods with near-total losses and those inland differ in how they tell the first days.
- **Pidie Jaya and Pidie:** check how the 2016 quake was met by people who had also lived through 2004.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe):** check how the end of gas revenue is spoken of: crisis, decline or relief.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Meulaboh):** check who led the first response in villages without officials.
- **Simeulue:** check how *smong* memory is taught to children now, in Devayan and Sigulai areas.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how coffee-price crises and earthquakes are each handled by households and cooperatives.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how flash floods are blamed and what village elders do.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check memory of the 2006 flood among Malay and Acehnese villages.
- **Singkil and Pulau Banyak:** check how sinking land has changed where families live.
- **Kluet and Aneuk Jamee (South Aceh):** check landslide and flood responses along the southwest mountains.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese in Malaysia who sent aid home in 2005; Indonesian national disaster data as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 2004 and for agency disaster records; mixed for later quakes and floods; thin for Alas, Kluet and Singkil experience, and for personal crises.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay to read the omen texts; Dutch for the colonial medical journal; Devayan and Sigulai for island memory; Acehnese for post-2004 verse.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony is traumatic and some will not speak of it; trust in state relief is Capricorn's spirit of law, and emergency statutes are Libra's.
- **Held for review:** after 2004, how relief reached families of South Asian heritage in the old port (category; nothing named).
