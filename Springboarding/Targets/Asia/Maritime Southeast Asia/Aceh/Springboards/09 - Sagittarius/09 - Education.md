## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check how graduates of the two big campuses and of nearby dayah compare in standing at home.
- **Pidie:** check whether trading families steer sons toward commerce over degrees.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check the Samalanga dayah's graduates against those of the Lhokseumawe campuses.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Nagan Raya):** check what the arrival of a university changed for local families.
- **East coast (Langsa, East Aceh):** check whether the Peureulak learning claim is part of local pride and school teaching.
- **Gayo highlands:** check the share of Gayo students who study in Takengon against those who leave.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check whether the Gunung Leuser campus keeps Alas graduates in the valley or prepares them to leave.
- **South Aceh (Aneuk Jamee, Kluet):** check whether students from these coasts go to Meulaboh, Banda Aceh or West Sumatra.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check how many Tamiang students cross to Medan's universities.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check where Christian and Muslim school-leavers go to study, and whether the choice differs.
- **Simeulue:** check how far an islander must travel for a degree, and who returns.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesia-wide attainment tables, to place Aceh's district figures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and institutional histories; mixed for dayah higher learning; thin for pre-modern claims, which need sources checked, and for island and Alas access.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for statistics and campus records; Arabic for dayah licenses; Acehnese for family talk of schooling; Gayo for highland student life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims of an ancient university are points of local pride, so treat with care; campus speech and dress rules go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** for court-era higher learning, the curricula and teachers from South Asia credited in histories of the sultanate's colleges, as a category only.
