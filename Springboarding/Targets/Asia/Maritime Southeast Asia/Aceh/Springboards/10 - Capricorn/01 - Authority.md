# Aceh: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how civil servants weigh an order from an office head against a scholar's ruling on the same matter.
- **Aceh Besar:** check whether the *imeum mukim* or the district office now speaks for a cluster of villages.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check how far the *dayah* leaders' word carries outside their pupils into village decisions.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check how former rebel commanders' word sits beside elected heads where the war was fiercest.
- **West coast (Nagan Raya, Aceh Barat):** check whether the Syattariyah teacher's word overrides the provincial council's on religious dates and practice.
- **Gayo highlands:** check whether the *reje* or the state-appointed village head is the one villagers go to first.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check authority in mixed Muslim and Christian *kute*, where clan and church may each claim a say.
- **Aceh Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whom church congregations treat as their voice before district officials.
- **Simeulue:** check whether island elders or mainland-posted officials are the ones heeded when Sinabang's orders reach outer villages.
- **Women:** check who speaks for women's groups at village level, and whether widows heading households gained a say after 2004.
- **Comparison only:** national trust-in-institutions polls, to place the provincial numbers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious and state authority since 2001; mixed for family and village; thin for Alas, Singkil and Simeulue.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic for the Qur'anic and fatwa vocabulary of rule; Acehnese for heeding and command words in speech; Gayo for the highland council; Indonesian for polls and theses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military authority recalls conflict abuses; questioning a scholar's ruling can bring social cost to an informant; the permission share is scored from statutes in Libra, so here only how rule is felt.
- **Held for review:** the 17th-century court scholar of South Asian birth whose rulings bore on who held religious authority, as a category only.
