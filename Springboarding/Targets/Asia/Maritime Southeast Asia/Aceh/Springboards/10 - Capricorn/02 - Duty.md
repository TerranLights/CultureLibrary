# Aceh: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

English keywords: Aceh filial duty; Aceh communal work obligation; Aceh zakat on salaries; Acehnese son-in-law obligations; Aceh forced labor colonial.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check whether salaried households meet kin duties more with money than with presence.
- **Pidie:** check how men who trade far from home meet duties to wives' families they seldom see.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check whether night-watch and communal-work turns fell apart or hardened during the war.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Aceh Barat):** check how surviving relatives took on orphans' care after 2004, and by which rule of kin.
- **South Aceh (Aneuk Jamee, Kluet):** check whether duties pass through the mother's brother or the father's line.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how duties differ between a husband who joins his wife's group and one who brings his wife home.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check clan duties at weddings and funerals between wife-giving and wife-taking clans.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check whether Malay *kampung* custom assigns funeral and feast duties by household or by lineage.
- **Simeulue:** check which island duties fall on returned migrants versus those who stayed.
- **Chinese-Acehnese:** check duties to ancestors and temples alongside civic duties in Banda Aceh.
- **Comparison only:** remittances sent by Acehnese working in Malaysia, as duty kept from abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Religious duty is well written; family and communal duty sit mostly in theses; Alas, Tamiang and Simeulue are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay and Arabic for the commentary and the duty categories; Acehnese for the verse manual; Minangkabau-related speech for Aneuk Jamee kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war-era duties (flag ceremonies, levies, recruitment) can still endanger families; the force of village bylaws on paper goes to Libra, while this sheet takes how they are felt.
- **Held for review:** religious duty manuals composed at the court by scholars born in South Asia, as a category only.
