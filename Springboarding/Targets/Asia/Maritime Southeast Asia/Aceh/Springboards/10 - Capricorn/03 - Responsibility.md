# Aceh: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; English for one foreign lawsuit. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how civil servants speak of answering upward (to the office) against answering outward (to the public).
- **Aceh Besar:** check whether village heads near the capital face sharper questioning over funds than remote ones.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check how villages near the gas field talk of the company's share in wartime harm.
- **West coast (Nagan Raya, Aceh Jaya):** check how peat-burning and plantation disputes are explained by villagers, firms and officials.
- **Pidie:** check who is held to answer when a trading venture fails, the trader or his backers.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how coffee households divide blame for a ruined harvest between weather, buyers and themselves.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check whether a clan answers jointly for a member's wrong.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check how flood losses on the river are blamed, and whether minority villages are heard.
- **Simeulue:** check how islanders explain their low 2004 toll, and who they credit for it.
- **Women:** check whether the mental load of managing the household is named, and in which words.
- **Comparison only:** Indonesia-wide audit findings (the national audit board) as a yardstick for provincial ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official accountability and disaster blame are well covered; grammar and proverb sources exist but are scattered; Alas and Singkil are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Acehnese to read Durie's examples and the sayings; Jawi Malay and Arabic for the Sufi writer; English for the U.S. lawsuit; Indonesian for audit reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conflict-era abuses and the Arun case name living victims; blame for tsunami deaths stirs grief; questions of legal liability as written go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** works on predestination and free will by South Asian-born scholars at the court, as a category only.
