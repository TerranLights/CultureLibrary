# Aceh: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; Dutch for the colonial army. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, religious bodies, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh and Aceh Besar:** check whether office attendance rules bite harder in the capital than in district offices.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check how the big *dayah* of this belt run the pupil's day, and how former pupils recall it.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe):** check how factory shift work at the gas and fertilizer plants trained a generation to the clock.
- **West coast (Nagan Raya):** check Syattariyah followers' daily remembrance against neighbors' ordinary prayer timetable.
- **South Aceh (Aneuk Jamee, Kluet):** check whether martial-art schools and religious retreats share pupils and rules.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how children are corrected in coffee-farming households, and who does the correcting.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check discipline in Christian and Muslim schools in the same valley.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check whether Malay *kampung* childhood follows the coastal Acehnese Qur'an-lesson pattern.
- **Simeulue and Singkil:** check how fishing and river crews keep order aboard far from any office.
- **Former combatants:** check what wartime training left behind in men's routines after 2005.
- **Comparison only:** national surveys on harsh correction of children, to place any provincial figure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Institutional discipline (*dayah*, army, office) is reachable; family correction is thin and private; Alas, Tamiang and Simeulue are thinnest.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the colonial corps; Arabic and Jawi Malay for Sufi regimen terms; Acehnese for lesson and correction words; Minangkabau-related speech for the silat schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment of children and pupils; wartime training names people who may still face risk; caning and moral enforcement as statutes go to Libra, with only how they are felt here.
- **Held for review:** devotional disciplines of orders with South Asian founders, as practiced in Aceh, set aside as a category.
