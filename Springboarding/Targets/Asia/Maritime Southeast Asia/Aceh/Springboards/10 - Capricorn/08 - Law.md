# Aceh: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; Dutch for colonial policy. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Spirit only; what the *qanun* and statutes say is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check whether students, traders and civil servants call provincial rules protection, display or burden.
- **Aceh Besar and Pidie:** check how often families go to the village before the police, and what makes them skip the village.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check memories of living between the rebels' rules and the state's in the war years.
- **West coast (Aceh Barat, Nagan Raya):** check whether the Syattariyah community treats provincial religious rules as binding on its own practice.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how growers in the hills and townspeople in Takengon talk about cannabis law.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check whether Christian residents see the provincial order as a law that binds them or one that stands apart.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check how residents near the North Sumatra line compare the two provinces' rules in daily life.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check how church communities speak of permits and agreements as law that protects or confines.
- **Simeulue:** check whether island residents meet the provincial rules through officers at all, and when.
- **Women and youth:** check who says the rules fall hardest on them.
- **Comparison only:** national rule-of-law and legal-awareness surveys, as a yardstick.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the politics of sharia and for corruption talk; mixed for village settlement; thin for how island and highland minorities relate to law.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Jawi Malay for the 18th-century manual and the *maqāṣid* debate; Dutch for colonial policy; Acehnese for settlement words; Indonesian for surveys and legal-aid reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** caning, cannabis and conflict-era rebel rule involve living people and criminal exposure; statutes and judgments as written stay with Libra.
- **Held for review:** legal treatises and heresy rulings by South Asian-born scholars at the court, and reformist movements of South Asian origin active in Aceh, as categories only.
