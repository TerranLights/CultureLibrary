# Aceh: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; English for one key article. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how coastal neighborhoods rebuilt against the official plan, and what each side called orderly.
- **Aceh Besar:** check whether livestock and land-use quarrels are settled by village custom or by district officers.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check how market towns keep order on market days, and who does it.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe, North Aceh):** check how residents describe the years their area was labeled trouble-prone.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Aceh Jaya):** check how relocated villages arranged themselves when they rebuilt inland.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *tertib* in talk about households and villages, and whether it is used of public life.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how mixed villages arrange shared space between mosque, church and clan land.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check how border towns describe order on the road to North Sumatra.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check how river villages kept order through floods and the religious disputes of the 2010s.
- **Simeulue:** check whether island villages keep watch over shore and hillside by custom, and who sounds the alarm.
- **Street vendors and coffee-shop owners:** check how those who live by the street describe the order officers impose.
- **Comparison only:** how neighboring North Sumatra's residents answer the same safety question.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the conflict and the tsunami as breaks in order; mixed for everyday street and village order; thin for the island and highland minorities' own terms.
- **Languages needed:** English for Robinson; Jawi Malay and Arabic for strife and order words; Acehnese and Gayo for settled-village vocabulary; Indonesian for planning and police documents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law memories; looting testimony can shame named families; religious disputes in Singkil; the statutes on public morals go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the court chronicle of the royal garden and city written by a South Asian-born scholar, and the doctrine of grades of being that came through South Asia, as categories only.
