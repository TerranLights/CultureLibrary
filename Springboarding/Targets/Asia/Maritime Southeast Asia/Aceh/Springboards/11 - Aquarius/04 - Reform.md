# Aceh: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic; Dutch for colonial policy papers. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check campus activists and NGO staff as reformers who learned their methods during rebuilding.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check how the 1930s reformist schools are remembered where they began.
- **North coast (Lhokseumawe):** check whether the end of the gas era is spoken of as a chance for economic reform or a loss.
- **West coast:** check how relocated villages judged outsiders' building-code and planning changes.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *kaum muda* and *kaum tua* lines in mosque life today.
- **Gayo Lues and Southeast Aceh:** check how forest rules and the Leuser road are argued among farmers, conservation staff and officials.
- **Singkil:** check whether the Abdurrauf heritage is invoked for or against religious reform.
- **Simeulue and Aceh Tamiang:** check which reforms (fisheries, plantation, local office) residents say reached them at all.
- **Young drug users and their families:** check who seeks dayah rehabilitation and who seeks clinics.
- **Comparison only:** national *reformasi* after 1998, to see which Aceh reforms came from Jakarta and which from inside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1930s-1950s movement and post-2005 governance; mixed for religious schooling; thin for reform of persons and for island and Tamiang voices.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Snouck's memoranda; Jawi Malay and Arabic for Hamzah; Indonesian for program reports; Gayo for highland reformist history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reform-of-persons programs touch drug users and morality cases; questions of how reform is written into regulation go to Libra, and what it is for to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** for reform of personal piety, the door-to-door revival missions of South Asian origin that recruit in Aceh's mosques, kept as a category.
