# Aceh: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how students and young professionals form friendships across district origins.
- **Aceh Besar and Pidie:** check whether a term for sworn brotherhood survives, and between whom.
- **North coast (Bireuen to Lhokseumawe):** check comrades' bonds from the conflict against friendships formed in school.
- **West coast:** check how survivors describe friends lost in 2004 and friends made in the camps.
- **Gayo highlands:** check friendships formed around coffee work and *didong* clubs.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check friendships across Muslim and Christian households in the valley.
- **Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whether childhood friendships across faith lines held after 2015.
- **Simeulue:** check how island youth who study on the mainland keep or replace home friends.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check friendships between Malay and Javanese plantation families.
- **Women of different generations:** check where each may meet friends, and how often.
- **Comparison only:** Acehnese workers in Malaysia, for friendships formed among migrants abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Patronage and comrades' bonds appear in conflict and governance work; friendship as such is rarely studied; women's and children's friendships and the island districts are thin.
- **Languages needed:** spoken Acehnese for friendship terms and comedy; Dutch for the old dictionary; Gayo for highland friend words; Indonesian for youth and urban studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendship between unmarried men and women is a charged topic; patronage pointers touch named officials.
- **Held for review:** sworn bonds and business partnerships that sultanate-era Acehnese men formed with traders born in South Asia, kept back unnamed.
