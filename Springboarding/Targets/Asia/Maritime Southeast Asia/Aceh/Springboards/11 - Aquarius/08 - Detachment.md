# Aceh: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Kluet, Singkil, Devayan and Sigulai; Malay/Indonesian; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check whether city life and office work bring a cooler, more private style than village life.
- **Aceh Besar and the west coast:** check how families who lost many in 2004 speak of grief now, compared with those who lost few.
- **South Aceh (Aneuk Jamee, Kluet):** check who joins *suluk* retreats and how families view their absence.
- **North coast (Bireuen, Lhokseumawe):** check whether conflict survivors describe numbness and how it is treated.
- **Gayo highlands:** check *sebuku* and its decline, for how open grief is permitted or curbed.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check bridal weeping and other set times for showing feeling.
- **Singkil:** check how Abdurrauf's teaching on withdrawal is taught at his shrine and schools.
- **Simeulue:** check how islanders recall the calm, ordered flight to high ground in 2004.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check whether Malay mourning customs differ from Acehnese ones.
- **Men and women:** check who is expected to keep composure at funerals, and who to weep.
- **Comparison only:** national abstention rates, to place Aceh's turnout.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Distress and grief after the tsunami and the conflict are well studied; Sufi retreat practice is moderately covered; display rules, solitude and island material are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Jawi Malay for the Sufi manual; Gayo for laments; Alas for bridal rites; Indonesian for psychosocial and electoral reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trauma interviews need care and consent; *khalwat* in its legal sense is a matter for Libra; *suluk* communities may restrict access during retreats.
- **Held for review:** retreat and seclusion practices of Sufi orders whose founders came from South Asia, as a category only.
