*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how the old colonial ring and the mosque precinct still mark inside and outside in city talk.
- **Aceh Besar:** check how *peumeukleh* and house-visiting customs hold in villages near the capital against new housing estates.
- **Pidie:** check whether merchant families draw firmer lines around household money and news than farming families.
- **North coast (North Aceh, Lhokseumawe, East Aceh):** check fishermen's views of sea borders after detentions abroad.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, Calang):** check elephant fences as boundaries farmers build, mend or cut.
- **Gayo highlands:** check clan-section lines in marriage and district lines between Central Aceh and Bener Meriah.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check park boundaries cutting clan land, and lines between Muslim and Christian households.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check the islands dispute and the pure-impure line in mixed villages.
- **Simeulue:** check how island land and reef edges are marked between villages.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay village edges near the North Sumatra border, where two provinces meet.
- **Comparison only:** neighboring North Sumatra's version of the shared border disputes; Acehnese households in Malaysia, on privacy in rented rooms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land, park and border disputes; mixed for household and body boundaries; thin for island edges and minority purity lines.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the fortified ring; Acehnese for threshold and household words; Gayo and Alas for clan rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interfaith purity lines and domestic abuse are delicate; rules on mixing of the sexes and land titles go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** maritime border incidents with a South Asian state's island territory to the north, kept as a category only.
