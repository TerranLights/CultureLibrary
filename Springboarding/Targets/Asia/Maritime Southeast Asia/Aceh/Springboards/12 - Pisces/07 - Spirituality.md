# Aceh: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Singkil, Devayan, Aneuk Jamee; Jawi Malay; Arabic; Persian for some old inscriptions; Indonesian; Hakka for Chinese-Acehnese practice. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate: the experience, the practice, and the sacred outside institutions (faith and religion as institutions are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check church, temple and mosque retreat side by side as three ways of seeking the sacred in one city.
- **Aceh Besar:** check visits to old graves around the capital and what visitors ask for.
- **Pidie:** check sacred wells and springs as places of vows, and how reform-minded preachers respond.
- **North Aceh and Lhokseumawe:** check how villagers near the Pasai tombs treat them: heritage, holy ground or both.
- **West coast (Meulaboh, Nagan Raya):** check *ratéb meuseukat* as women's devotion against men's remembrance circles.
- **South Aceh (Aneuk Jamee, Kluet):** check shrine visiting along the coast against Acehnese districts to the north.
- **Gayo highlands:** check whether lake, mountain or grave sites carry blessing in Gayo speech.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check Christian and Muslim practice in the valley for places either side holds sacred.
- **Aceh Singkil:** check Abdurrauf's memory at home against his fame in the capital.
- **Simeulue:** check Teungku Diujung pilgrims and island prayers for the sea.
- **Chinese-Acehnese:** check temple practice in Peunayong and in smaller coastal towns.
- **Comparison only:** national surveys of religious experience; Acehnese in Malaysia, for home shrines kept from afar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Inscriptions, manuscripts and institutional practice are documented; private spiritual experience, "spirituality without religion" and highland holy places are thin and may be little spoken of.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Persian for Pasai inscriptions; Devayan on Simeulue; Hakka or Hokkien for temple practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grave and spring visits can be called deviant by some preachers; minority worship is a delicate topic; rules on worship and places of worship go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** prayer and offerings at a house of worship kept by South Asian-heritage residents of Banda Aceh, a further strand of lived devotion to set beside the parish, the Chinese temple and the mosque retreat (category only).
