# Aceh: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Aneuk Jamee, Tamiang Malay, Devayan; Jawi Malay; Arabic; Indonesian; Dutch for the war's end. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check hospital wards and mass graves as places where letting go is staged in very different ways.
- **Aceh Besar:** check how descendants of the old war leaders talk about the 1903 surrenders.
- **Pidie:** check memories of the 1962 descent from the hills among families of the Darul Islam fighters.
- **North coast (Bireuen, North Aceh):** check how former fighters who surrendered in 2003-2004 are now regarded at home.
- **West coast (Aceh Jaya, West Aceh):** check whether evacuation choices in later quakes reflect what villages took from 2004.
- **Gayo highlands:** check how Gayo families tell the end of the Darul Islam years in the hills around Takengon.
- **Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how surrender in 1904 is recalled by Alas clans, in Alas speech.
- **South and Southwest Aceh:** check endowment giving in Aneuk Jamee villages against Acehnese ones.
- **Simeulue:** check whether island verse on the great wave speaks of running, of accepting, or of both.
- **Aceh Tamiang:** check Malay funeral customs for release of the dead against Acehnese *talqin*.
- **Comparison only:** national surveys of health beliefs; Acehnese who left for Malaysia rather than surrender in the conflict.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the 1903, 1962 and 2005 endings are well recorded; dying, grief and fate talk sit in scattered theses; Alas, Gayo and Simeulue material on yielding is scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for 1903 reports; Acehnese and Jawi Malay for the sayings and court custom; Gayo for the Darul Islam memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surrendered fighters may be seen as traitors by some; terminal patients and the bereaved need care; amnesty, waqf rules and burial rules go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; no yielding, endowment or end-of-life pointer here crossed the standing rules.
