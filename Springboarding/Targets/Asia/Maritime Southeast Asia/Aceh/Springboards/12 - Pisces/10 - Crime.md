# Aceh: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Aceh, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Acehnese (Latin and Jawi), Gayo, Alas, Tamiang Malay, Aneuk Jamee, Singkil, Devayan; Jawi Malay; Arabic; Indonesian; Dutch for colonial police records. **Concept in one line:** what the culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. The law itself is researched in Libra (letter, including *qanun* as written) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet takes the idea of the wrong and the wrongdoer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Banda Aceh:** check how street robbery and petty theft are discussed in the city against village views.
- **Aceh Besar:** check whether ex-prisoners in villages near the capital find their way back through the mosque, kin or work.
- **Pidie and Bireuen:** check how village-fund cases are argued in coffee shops and on village notice boards.
- **East Aceh and North Aceh:** check Din Minimi's standing and fishermen-courier cases along the north and east coast.
- **Aceh Tamiang and Langsa:** check logging and smuggling across the North Sumatra line as wrongs locals rank low or high.
- **Gayo highlands:** check whether *kemali* is used of theft and cheating or only of taboo acts.
- **Gayo Lues and Southeast Aceh (Alas):** check how poaching and forest clearing are judged in villages bordering the national park.
- **West and Southwest coasts:** check which wrongs fishing towns handle among themselves before any report to police.
- **Aceh Singkil and Subulussalam:** check whether wrongs across religious lines are spoken of as crime or as insult.
- **Simeulue:** check how an island with few police describes thieves and how it shames them.
- **Comparison only:** national victimization figures; Acehnese in Malaysia caught up in drug cases, as seen from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press crime reporting; mixed for surveys; thin for minority-language words for wrongdoers and for how the excluded come back.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for colonial labels; Acehnese and Gayo for moral vocabulary; Indonesian for press and courts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informants may face real danger in drug, logging and armed-group topics; child-abuse cases need strict anonymity; all statute and sentencing questions go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** colonial-era police files on disputes in the old port that involved South Asian-heritage merchants, as a category only.
