## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese**: court culture of Yogyakarta and Surakarta, the north coast (*pesisir*), East Java (*Arek* Surabaya), Banyumas; speech levels and *priyayi* ideals vary.
- **Sundanese and Madurese**: neighbors of Java with their own speech levels and honor codes.
- **Balinese**: Hindu ritual life, caste titles and birth-order names.
- **Minangkabau**: matrilineal, with the custom of leaving home.
- **Batak** (Toba, Karo, Mandailing, Simalungun): patrilineal clans and genealogy.
- **Bugis, Makassar and Mandar**: *siri'* and seafaring.
- **Toraja, Minahasa, Dayak peoples, Nias, Mentawai**: Christian or ancestral-religion settings, tattoo and death rites.
- **Eastern islands** (Sasak, Bima, Sumba, Flores, Timor, Maluku): little-ethnographed variation.
- **Chinese Indonesians, Arab-descent families, Dutch-Indonesian Eurasians (Indo), Betawi**: urban, mixed and naming-changed identities.
- **Orang Rimba and Bajau or Sama sea peoples**: mobile groups outside village norms.
- **Diaspora** (Suriname Javanese, Moluccans in the Netherlands): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java and Bali, mixed for Sumatra and Sulawesi, thin for many eastern islands. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet set is written for the core lands. Aceh and West Papua are separate targets and are not covered here.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese (with krama), Balinese, Dutch for colonial ethnography; Buginese *lontara'* script and Javanese script for manuscripts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the religion field and the *kepercayaan* option; the 1960s Chinese-Indonesian name changes; gender-diverse identities (*waria*) under current pressure; 1965-66 violence in family histories; restricted ritual knowledge among Mentawai, Dayak and Toraja keepers.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Read Geertz 1966 and Keeler 1987 side by side, then search Garuda (Indonesian journal portal) for "konsep diri" with "Jawa", "Bali", "Minang", "Bugis".
2. Pull the 2010 census ethnicity tables from BPS and note which *suku* are merged or split.
3. Search "nama orang Indonesia" with "marga", "fam", "nama baptis", "urutan lahir" for naming studies by region.
4. Search Leiden University Libraries' Digital Collections for "Serat Wedhatama" and "La Galigo" manuscripts.
5. Search "pemutih kulit" and "standar kecantikan Indonesia" in Indonesian theses for regional body-image studies.
