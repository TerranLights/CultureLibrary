## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese court cities against the north coast and East Java (Surabaya *Arek*)**: refined indirection and blunt speech are both Javanese; the gap is the question.
- **Batak and Madurese migrants in cities**: labeled "outspoken" in popular talk; test the label rather than repeat it.
- **Minangkabau**: oratory as a formal right inside matrilineal custom.
- **Bugis and Makassar**: claims tied to honor and to deliberation among nobles.
- **Sundanese**: speech-level politeness separate from Javanese.
- **Betawi of Jakarta**: a colloquial register (*gue*, *lu*) heard as direct.
- **Chinese-Indonesian traders**: bargaining and complaint in shop life, and their caution after 1998.
- **Christian eastern islands** (Ambon, Kupang, Flores, Minahasa): church councils and synods as speaking venues.
- **Women and youth across groups**: who may refuse a parent, a husband or a boss.
- **Migrant domestic workers abroad** (Hong Kong unions): comparison only, for learned assertion away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Javanese politeness and speech levels, mixed for public deliberation, thin for eastern islands outside church records.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese with krama; Buginese for *lontara'* texts; Dutch for colonial reports of *pepe*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** online complaints have led to defamation cases (law to Libra); labor and student protest under the New Order; speaking against religious authority.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
