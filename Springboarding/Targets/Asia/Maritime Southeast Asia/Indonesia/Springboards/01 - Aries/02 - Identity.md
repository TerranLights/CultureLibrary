# Indonesia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Minangkabau, Balinese, Batak Toba, Ngaju Dayak; Chinese (Hokkien, Mandarin) and Dutch for community and colonial sources. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese of the court cities against Javanese elsewhere**: whether "Javanese" is one identity or several.
- **Minangkabau at home and in the *rantau***: identity tested by long absence.
- **Batak subgroups (Toba, Karo, Mandailing)**: Karo and Mandailing often reject the Batak label; a live boundary.
- **Dayak peoples and Banjar**: river and inland identities, Christian, Muslim and Kaharingan.
- **Kepercayaan communities** (Sedulur Sikep in Blora, Sunda Wiwitan, Parmalim): identity outside the six official religions.
- **Chinese Indonesians** (peranakan, totok, Cina Benteng of Tangerang): identity shaped by state pressure and 1998.
- **Arab-descent families**: sayyid descent and Indonesian homeland.
- **Dutch-Indonesian Eurasians (Indo)**: a mostly emigrated group; in-country remnants versus Netherlands memory.
- **Transmigrant villages** (Javanese and Balinese in Sumatra, Kalimantan, Sulawesi): second-generation identity on another island.
- **Moluccans in the Netherlands, Javanese in Suriname**: comparison only, for identity kept far from the source.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national identity and the Chinese minority, mixed for regional identities, thin for small eastern and interior peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for colonial-era identity sources; Chinese for community papers; Javanese and Old Javanese for classical texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *pribumi* and *putra daerah* rhetoric; 1998 anti-Chinese violence; communal conflicts (Maluku, Poso, Sampit) in which identity was a battle line; recognition of ancestral religions (law to Libra).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Search Garuda and university repositories for "identitas etnis" with "generasi kedua" and "transmigrasi".
2. Read Pemberton 1994, then visit or browse Taman Mini's provincial pavilions online and list the groups shown.
3. Search AMAN's site for member communities by province; note self-chosen names.
4. Search "peranakan Tionghoa identitas" and "Cina Benteng" in Indonesian theses and community magazines.
5. Search Leiden University Libraries for *Sutasoma* and *Desawarnana* editions and translations.
