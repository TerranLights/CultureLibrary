## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau**: matrilineal households where a maternal uncle, not a father, may speak for a young person.
- **Javanese priyayi and village Javanese**: parental matching and personal choice may sit differently by class.
- **Balinese**: strong village councils over personal and household matters.
- **Bugis and Makassar**: elopement and its honor stakes.
- **Sasak of Lombok**: elopement as an accepted path to marriage.
- **Batak**: clan rules on marriage partners (no marriage inside the clan).
- **Santri families and pesantren graduates**: religious authority over life choices.
- **Christian communities** (Minahasa, Toraja, Flores): church and clan as rival deciders.
- **Urban middle class in Jakarta, Bandung, Surabaya**: *kos* living, dating apps and delayed marriage.
- **Kepercayaan and ancestral-religion groups**: autonomy to keep a faith the state does not list.
- **Migrant workers' families**: remittances and decisions made from abroad, comparison only for the workers themselves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in survey data (DHS, IFLS) on household decisions; mixed for ethnography of choice; thin for eastern islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Minangkabau, Balinese, Buginese and Sasak for local terms; Arabic for religious texts; Dutch for colonial village administration.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** female circumcision debates; leaving Islam (*murtad*); same-sex relationships; child marriage; the law of marriage age and religion belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
