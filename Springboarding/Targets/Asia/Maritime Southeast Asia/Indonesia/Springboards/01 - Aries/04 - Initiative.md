*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau migrants**: starting eateries and shops in other provinces.
- **Javanese villages with *sambatan* and *rewang***: initiative as neighborly duty, not personal venture.
- **Balinese *banjar* members**: *ngayah* duty and who organizes it.
- **Minahasa**: *mapalus* work groups in a Christian setting.
- **Madurese, Tegal and Tasikmalaya migrants**: hometown niches in trade and services.
- **Bugis and Makassar sailors and traders**: inter-island ventures.
- **Chinese Indonesians**: shop and trading networks, without assuming one business style.
- **Batak in cities**: clan associations (*punguan marga*) that may back or steer a member's venture *check*.
- **Civil servants and would-be civil servants**: the prestige of a state post against starting one's own business.
- **Dayak and inland communities**: initiative in land and forest defense.
- **Indonesian founders in Singapore and the Gulf**: comparison only, for ventures started abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in business statistics and startup press; mixed for volunteering; thin for village-level ethnography outside Java and Bali.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese for village terms; Dutch for colonial-era merchant records; Chinese for *kongsi* sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *gotong royong* has been used by the state to compel labor, so official sources need balancing; Chinese-Indonesian enterprise is tied to past violence and stereotyping.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
