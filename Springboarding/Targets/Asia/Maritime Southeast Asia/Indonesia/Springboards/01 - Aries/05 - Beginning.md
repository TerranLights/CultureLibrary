## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese court cities**: new-year rites in silence or procession, distinct from village *wiwitan*.
- **Balinese Hindus**: a Saka new year with a whole island at rest.
- **Sundanese of Cigugur**: a harvest new year kept by an ancestral-religion community.
- **Sasak of Lombok**: a sea-season opening tied to a legend.
- **Bugis, Makassar and Luwu**: founding by a sky-descended stranger.
- **Chinese Indonesians**: a lunar new year that was banned from public view under the New Order.
- **Transmigrant settlers and their host peoples**: one side's new start was the other side's loss of land.
- **Young urban Muslims in the *hijrah* trend**: a chosen fresh start.
- **Former political detainees and their children**: a restart under stigma.
- **Dayak and Toraja**: harvest and house-founding rites outside the main religions.
- **Indonesian workers returning from abroad**: comparison only, for homecoming as a second start.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ritual and festival description, mixed for life-cycle rites outside Java and Bali, thin for personal fresh starts outside memoirs.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Old Javanese; Balinese; Buginese for *lontara'* founding texts; Dutch for colonial resettlement records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1965-66 detainees and their families; land conflicts behind transmigration; some birth and harvest rites are restricted to participants.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

1. Search Garuda for "mitoni", "tedhak siten" and "ari-ari" with regency names to compare birth rites.

4. Search news and theses for "hijrah" with "anak muda" and "media sosial".
