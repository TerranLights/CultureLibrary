## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese courts and their *satria* ideal**: courage bound to loyalty and inner calm.
- **Balinese**: last-stand memory and temple warrior dances.
- **Bugis and Makassar**: seafaring daring and the warrior title.
- **Madurese**: courage bound to honor, with care not to reduce it to violence.
- **Moluccans (Ambon, Saparua)**: a revolt and its heroine.
- **Manggarai, Sumbanese and Sasak**: ritual fights as rites of manhood and of fertility.
- **Nias**: stone jumping as a test of youth.
- **Bantenese**: invulnerability traditions.
- **Women across regions**: whose endurance is called courage.
- **Soldiers and police families**: courage as a career; the TNI's own codes.
- **Activists and journalists**: moral courage under pressure.
- **Indonesian seafarers and migrant workers abroad**: comparison only, for courage spoken of as leaving home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national heroes and the revolution, mixed for ritual fights, thin for moral courage outside activist circles.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Old Javanese; Balinese; Buginese and Makassarese for *lontara'*; Dutch for war records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hero lists are political (1965 and the military); some invulnerability practices are secret; *puputan* touches on mass death; military violence in conflict regions.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Download the Ministry of Social Affairs' Pahlawan Nasional list and tabulate heroes by province and gender.
2. Read the *Serat Tripama* in transliteration and Carey 2007, then note which virtues each names.
3. Search Garuda for "caci Manggarai", "pasola" and "peresean" with "maskulinitas" or "keberanian".
4. Search Delpher for "poepoetan" and "Badoeng 1906" for Dutch accounts of the last stands.
5. Search news archives for "penghargaan keberanian" and "Basarnas" for rescue awards.
