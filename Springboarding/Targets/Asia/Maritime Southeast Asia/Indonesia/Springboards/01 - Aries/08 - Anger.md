# Indonesia: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Madurese, Buginese; Arabic for Islamic teaching; Dutch for colonial medical and police records. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese**: words for swallowed anger beside open anger; court and village may differ.
- **Balinese**: calm faces and fear of harm, by caste and village.
- **Minangkabau**: a regional language's emotion words against Indonesian.
- **Madurese at home and in East Java and Kalimantan**: honor duels and their decline or persistence, without assuming all Madurese.
- **Bugis and Makassar**: anger when *siri'* is touched.
- **Batak**: popularly called hot-tempered; check that label, do not repeat it.
- **Chinese Indonesians**: as targets of mass anger and as narrators of it.
- **Moluccans and Central Sulawesi (Poso) communities**: anger in sectarian conflict and in later peacemaking.
- **Football supporters and school-gang youth**: anger channeled in groups.
- **Women**: anger at home and its silences.
- **Online users (*warganet*)**: outrage as a national pastime or a few loud accounts.
- **Indonesian workers abroad**: comparison only, for anger at employers far from kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots and conflict, mixed for emotion ethnography (Java, Bali, Minangkabau), thin for eastern islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese; Balinese; Madurese; Dutch for colonial *amok* and police records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1965-66 killings; May 1998 sexual violence; sectarian conflicts; stereotypes of Madurese and Batak temper; *amok* as a colonial label.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Read Heider 1991 and Wikan 1990, then search Garuda for "ekspresi marah" with "Jawa", "Bali", "Minang" and "Bugis".

4. Search Garuda for "carok" and "tawuran" studies published after 2005.
5. Search news archives for "suporter tewas" to list football-violence incidents by club.
