*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese of the court cities (Yogyakarta, Surakarta) against the north-coast (*pesisir*) towns and East Java, and the Balinese: courtly and temple display beside merchant towns.
- Sundanese of West Java, with the Baduy and the *kasepuhan* of Ciptagelar apart: rice-barn customs and deliberate refusals of goods.
- Minangkabau: matrilineal ancestral property and trader networks abroad (the legal side goes to Libra or Capricorn).
- Toba, Karo and Mandailing Batak: bride wealth, *ulos* cloth and feast duties differ among them.
- Bugis, Makassar and Mandar: seafaring trade and wedding payments.
- Toraja, Sumba, Flores and Alor peoples: livestock, cloth, ivory and bronze drums (*moko*) as exchange valuables.
- Dayak peoples (Ngaju, Iban, Kenyah and others) beside the Banjar: heirloom jars and gongs against river commerce.
- Chinese Indonesians, Peranakan and *totok*: Hakka Bangka-Belitung and Singkawang apart from Java's family firms.
- Arab Indonesians of Hadhrami descent: trade and religious giving.
- Maluku (Ambon, Ternate, Kei, Banda): spice history and seasonal harvest bans (*sasi*).
- Indonesian migrant workers abroad: their remittances, for comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java, Bali and the national surveys; mixed for Sumatra and Sulawesi; thin and often colonial-era for many eastern and inland peoples.
- **Lands:** Indonesia's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands. Aceh and the Papua provinces are separate targets in this setting and are not covered here.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (with its speech levels), Sundanese, Balinese; Dutch for colonial records; Jawi and Pegon for manuscripts; Old Javanese for the court poems.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chinese Indonesian wealth touches the violence of 1998; avoid stereotypes. Some ritual knowledge (Baduy, Kasepuhan, Kaharingan) is not open to outsiders. Property and land law: Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
