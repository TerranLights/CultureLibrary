# Indonesia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese; Ngaju Dayak, Alorese and Kodi for the eastern and inland cases; Dutch for museum records. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese court families against village households: heirloom blades and the honorific vocabulary of a superior's things.
- Balinese households: heirlooms and manuscripts kept in the family shrine compound.
- Kodi and other Sumbanese: personal objects woven into life stories.
- Alorese and Lamaholot peoples: drums and ivory that circulate rather than settle.
- Ngaju and other Dayak peoples: jars and gongs held across generations.
- Toraja and Minahasa: what goes with the dead, and the stealing of grave objects.
- Chinese Indonesian families: objects on the ancestral altar and what is kept after a death.
- Urban poor and waste pickers (*pemulung*): living from what others throw away.
- Migrant workers returning home: the goods they bring back, compared with what stays abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on heirlooms, cloth and blades; thin on ordinary everyday belongings, clutter and lending outside cities.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (speech levels matter); Dutch for museum accession files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some heirlooms and grave objects are restricted to kin or guardians; stolen effigies and colonial loot touch repatriation claims. Theft as an offense: Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
