*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese rice villages: sharecropping and patron-landholder ties, and spirits of the village.
- Balinese: temple and village holdings beside household property.
- Minangkabau: ancestral matrilineal holdings against earnings from migration.
- Toba and Karo Batak: founding-clan claims to a village and what later arrivals hold.
- Dayak peoples: forest gardens, longhouse claims and company concessions.
- Ambonese, Kei and other Maluku villages: harvest bans as joint control.
- Bugis, Makassar and Mandar fishing and trading crews: shares in a boat and its catch.
- Bajau sea people and the Orang Rimba of Jambi: ownership without fixed fields or towns.
- Chinese Indonesians: family firms, and the restrictions they faced under the New Order.
- Betawi of Jakarta: displacement as the capital grew.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on tenure and public finance; mixed on attitudes to public against private holding; strong colonial and adat scholarship that leans legal.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for colonial tenure records; Old Javanese for charters; Minangkabau and Maluku terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land conflicts with plantations and mining are live and sometimes violent; ancestral-property disputes are family matters. All land law, adat law as legislation and agrarian reform: Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
