## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Wet-rice farmers of Java and Bali against dryland farmers of Gunungkidul, Madura and the eastern islands: different seasons of want.
- North-coast and eastern fishers: idle months in the west monsoon.
- Baduy and Kasepuhan communities: stored rice against cash.
- Balinese: village-based credit and customary guards.
- Dayak communities in West Kalimantan: credit unions as shared safety nets.
- Civil servants against informal and app-based workers: two opposite levels of job security.
- Chinese Indonesians: protection after the violence of May 1998.
- Disaster-hit towns (Palu, Lombok, the Merapi slopes): security rebuilt after loss.
- Families of migrant workers in East Java, Lombok and Flores: remittances as their insurance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: national surveys and program records are strong; ethnography on rice storage and village credit exists but is scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Sundanese for rites and storage terms; Dayak languages for credit-union histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** May 1998; Baduy restrictions on outsiders and recording; police and security forces (laws on them: Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
