## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese pious merchant towns (Kudus, Laweyan) against salaried official families: trade wealth and office rank.
- Minangkabau traders and restaurant owners abroad: wealth sent home and shown there.
- Toba Batak: whether descendants are counted as wealth, as the song suggests.
- Toraja and Sumbanese: livestock and gold ornaments in funeral and marriage exchange.
- Bugis and Makassar: wedding payments as a public measure of standing.
- Balinese: ceremony spending beside tourism wealth.
- Chinese Indonesian families: small shopkeepers against conglomerates; avoid the stereotype of a uniformly rich minority.
- Arab Indonesians: trade and endowments.
- Bangka-Belitung tin and Kalimantan coal and palm-oil towns: boom wealth.
- Jakarta and Surabaya new rich online, against village elites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: rankings, declarations, surveys and fiction are plentiful; household wealth (not income) is thinly measured.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for court literature; Batak, Toraja and Sumba terms; Dutch for colonial fortunes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** anti-Chinese resentment; spirit-wealth accusations can lead to violence; corruption cases (corruption law: Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.
