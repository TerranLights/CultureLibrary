## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pesantren-rooted Javanese and Madurese families** against **secular-schooled urban families**: different first teachers and aims.
- **Minangkabau**, where *surau* and *merantau* framed boys' learning away from home.
- **Balinese**, with *pasraman* and lontar learning beside state schools.
- **Sundanese** and **Betawi** communities near Jakarta's tutoring economy.
- **Batak** families, widely described as pushing schooling hard: test that reputation.
- **Chinese-Indonesians** across the school ban and its end.
- **Small forest and sea peoples**: Orang Rimba, Sama-Bajau, Mentawai; learning on land and water.
- **Eastern islands** (NTT, Maluku): long distances to school and church-run schools.
- **Christian minorities** (Batak, Minahasa, Toraja, Flores Catholics) with mission-school histories.
- **Deaf and disabled learners**.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pesantren, Java and national test data; mixed for Sumatra and Sulawesi; thin for learning outside schools in eastern Indonesia and among forest peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for court texts; Arabic and Pegon for pesantren; Dutch for colonial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some pesantren and kebatinan learning is closed to outsiders; some Balinese and eastern-island knowledge is held by initiates; rankings by ethnicity invite stereotype. Education law belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
