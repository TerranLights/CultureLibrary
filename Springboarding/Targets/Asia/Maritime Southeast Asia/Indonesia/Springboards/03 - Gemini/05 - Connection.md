# Indonesia: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Minangkabau, Toba Batak, Buginese, Minahasan (Tombulu and others), Ambonese Malay; Chinese (Hakka, Hokkien) for kongsi and clan sources; Arabic for religious terms. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau** migrants and their home villages: ties built for leaving and returning.
- **Batak** clans: genealogy as the map of whom one may call on.
- **Javanese** *trah* and urban office networks; patron ties in villages.
- **Bugis and Makassarese**: seafaring and trade links across islands.
- **Chinese-Indonesians** (Hakka of West Kalimantan, Hokkien Peranakan of Java): kongsi, clan and business ties.
- **Hadrami Arab-Indonesians**: genealogical links overseas.
- **Toraja**: ties with ancestors through houses and funerals.
- **Minahasa** and **Malukans**: mutual-work customs.
- **Balinese** *banjar* members: ties built through temple and ward duties.
- **Madurese** settlers in Kalimantan: tight migrant networks, and the strain they faced in conflict.
- **Workers abroad** and their families (comparison only).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration, patronage and religious networks; thin for loneliness and isolation as such.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Minangkabau and Batak for community sources; Chinese and Arabic for minority networks; Dutch for colonial-era associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2001 Sampit violence and other conflicts touch migrant networks; patronage research names living officials; some ancestral ties belong to adat specialists. Nepotism as a legal offense belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Azra (2004), Pelras (1996), Naim (1979), Heidhues (2003), Ho (2006).
2. JSTOR: Geertz 1962 and Bowen 1986; then search "arisan" plus "social ties" plus a city name.
3. BPS: "Indeks Kebahagiaan" dimension tables on social relations, by province.
4. Kemenhub: "survei potensi pergerakan masyarakat Lebaran" for the latest homecoming survey.
5. Search "silaturahmi" plus "WhatsApp grup keluarga" in university repositories for studies of family group chats.
