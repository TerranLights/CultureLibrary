# Indonesia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Toba and Karo Batak, Buginese, Betawi; Hokkien loanwords for Chinese-Indonesian families; Arabic for religious terms. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters and the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balinese**: birth order fixed in the name; brothers and the family temple.
- **Javanese**: elder-younger respect and the "spirit siblings".
- **Minangkabau**: the brother as uncle to his sister's children, and sisters as the core of the house.
- **Toba and Karo Batak**: patrilineal clans and a special cross-sex sibling bond.
- **Bugis and Makassarese**: brothers and family honor.
- **Sundanese** and **Betawi**: urban and near-urban families in screen fiction.
- **Chinese-Indonesians**: sibling roles in family business.
- **Samin** (Sedulur Sikep): sibling as a social ideal.
- **Eastern islands** (Flores, Sumba, Timor, Maluku): clan and house siblings, often thinly studied.
- **Small and large families**: families after the family-planning era against older large households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kinship terms and matrilineal and patrilineal systems; mixed for adult sibling relations; thin for sibling violence and estrangement.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese, Minangkabau and Batak for kin terms and custom; Dutch for older ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor customs touching sisters can involve violence; inheritance disputes between siblings are a legal matter for Libra or Capricorn; some birth lore belongs to *dukun bayi* (traditional midwives) and family practice.
- **Held for review:** none.
