## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese, split by milieu:** court-town *priyayi*, village farmers, devout *santri* households; Javanese kin order varies sharply with these.
- **Sundanese and Betawi:** related to Java but with their own house words and urban histories in West Java and Jakarta.
- **Madurese:** the courtyard of related houses is a distinct compound form, and Madurese migrants carry it to East Java and Kalimantan.
- **Minangkabau:** matrilineal houses and male out-migration (*merantau*) reverse many Javanese assumptions.
- **Batak Toba, Karo and Mandailing:** patrilineal clans whose reach runs far beyond the household.
- **Balinese:** Hindu descent groups and walled family compounds with house shrines.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** bilateral kin, raised houses and a long seafaring diaspora inside the archipelago.
- **Toraja:** ancestral houses (*tongkonan*) as the frame of kin membership.
- **Dayak peoples (Ngaju, Kenyah, Kayan, Iban):** longhouse living against single-family houses.
- **Nusa Tenggara (Sasak, Sumba, Manggarai, Atoni of West Timor, Rote) and Maluku (Ambonese, Kei, Tanimbar):** house-based kin groups and inter-village ties.
- **Indonesian Chinese, Arab Indonesians, Dutch-Indonesian Eurasians (Indo):** family forms shaped by separate histories and religions.
- **Urban against rural:** Jakarta *kampung*, new suburban estates, transmigration settlements.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** migrant workers in Malaysia and Hong Kong, Javanese Surinamese, the Indo community in the Netherlands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich: census and surveys, a deep ethnographic record, provincial monographs; thin for small eastern peoples.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. These sheets are written for the core lands: Java, Bali, Sumatra outside Aceh, Kalimantan, Sulawesi, Nusa Tenggara and Maluku.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (with its speech levels); Dutch for colonial sources; regional languages for monographs and oral material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some ancestral-house rites and genealogies belong to their keepers; the Baduy (Kanekes) restrict outside contact. Marriage and family law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
