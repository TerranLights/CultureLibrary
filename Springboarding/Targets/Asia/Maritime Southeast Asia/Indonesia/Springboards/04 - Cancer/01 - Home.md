# Indonesia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Buginese, Banjar; Dutch for colonial material. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** the court-style *joglo* and the village *limasan* against crowded urban kampung rooms; home as ranked space.
- **Balinese:** the walled yard with family shrine, where the dwelling and the ancestors share one plot.
- **Minangkabau:** the great house belongs to the women's line; men's sense of home (mother's house, wife's house, the *surau* of old) needs its own look.
- **Batak:** the home village (*huta*) and clan monuments drawing migrants back.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** raised wooden houses that can be dismantled and moved; test whether home attaches to the house, the plot or the people.
- **Dayak peoples:** longhouse life against single-family houses pushed by state programs.
- **Sasak (Lombok), Sumba, Manggarai, Atoni (West Timor) round houses, Mentawai and Nias:** distinct house forms and rules of dwelling.
- **Betawi:** pushed to Jakarta's edges as the city grew; home and displacement.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** shophouse living and urban property.
- **Transmigrant Javanese and Balinese in Sumatra, Kalimantan and Sulawesi:** two places called "home".
- **Urban poor on riverbanks against middle-class gated *cluster* estates.**
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** domestic workers abroad who live inside employers' homes, a useful mirror for whose house is "home".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for dwellings and housing statistics; mixed for home as feeling; strong Javanese and Balinese bias in the scholarship.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese (speech levels change house words), Balinese, Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eviction cases are political; some customary villages limit visitors and photography. Land and tenure law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
