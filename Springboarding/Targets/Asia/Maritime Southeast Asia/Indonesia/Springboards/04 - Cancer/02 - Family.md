## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** bilateral kin with *trah* reunions; compare court-descended families, village farmers and *santri* families.
- **Sundanese:** bilateral like Java, but with their own kin vocabulary and reunion customs worth testing separately.
- **Minangkabau:** matriclans and the mother's brother's role.
- **Batak Toba, Karo and Mandailing:** patrilineal clans with distinct religions (largely Protestant, mixed, Muslim) inside one kin logic.
- **Lampung and Nias:** further patrilineal systems with titles and feasts tied to the family *check*.
- **Balinese:** patrilineal descent-group temples and the house yard as kin unit.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** bilateral kin ranked by status, with *siri'* (family honor) at stake.
- **Toraja:** membership in several ancestral houses at once.
- **Sumba, Flores (Manggarai, Ngada) and Timor (Atoni):** houses allied by marriage exchange; Ngada has matrilineal elements *check*.
- **Ambonese and other Maluku villages:** *pela* alliances across religions.
- **Minahasa:** bilateral, largely Protestant families in North Sulawesi.
- **Dayak longhouse peoples:** the apartment family within a longhouse community.
- **Indonesian Chinese and Arab Indonesians:** surname lineage and *sayyid* descent.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** families split by labor migration to Malaysia and the Gulf, where kin duty is carried across borders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich: decades of kinship ethnography for the large peoples; thin for smaller ones in Nusa Tenggara and Maluku.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese speech levels mark kin rank; Batak and Minangkabau terms; Dutch for colonial kinship reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan genealogies and *sayyid* lineages carry rank; the Chinese Indonesian family under New Order assimilation rules is a painful subject; 1999 Maluku violence. Marriage, adoption and inheritance law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
