# Indonesia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Balinese, Madurese, Dayak languages; Chinese and Dutch for older community sources. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** the majority whose sense of belonging is easy to mistake for the national one; split Central and East Java, coast and interior.
- **Minangkabau and Batak migrants in Jakarta and Medan:** belonging to the home village while building one in the city.
- **Madurese settlers and Dayak hosts in West and Central Kalimantan:** the sharpest recent conflict over who belongs.
- **Bugis, Butonese and Bajau traders and fishers across eastern Indonesia:** long-settled newcomers in Maluku and Nusa Tenggara.
- **Javanese and Balinese transmigrants in Lampung, Sulawesi and Kalimantan, with their host peoples.**
- **Balinese:** membership in the *banjar* and customary village as near-total belonging.
- **Betawi:** the "original" Jakartans, now a minority in the city.
- **Ambonese Christians and Muslims; Poso:** belonging split along religion after 1999.
- **Indonesian Chinese, Arab Indonesians, Dutch-Indonesian Eurasians (Indo):** citizens long asked "where are you from?".
- **Adherents of native faiths (*penghayat kepercayaan*):** belonging by faith outside the recognized religions.
- **Baduy and other customary communities:** belonging defined by keeping apart.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Moluccans in the Netherlands and Indonesian workers abroad, for how belonging is held from far away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on national identity and migration; good school and survey data; thin on felt belonging in small eastern communities.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; regional languages for in-group self-names; Chinese and Dutch for minority history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *pribumi* and *non-pribumi* are charged; 1998 violence against Chinese Indonesians, Kalimantan and Maluku conflicts and religious-minority exclusion need care and survivors' voices first. Citizenship and recognition law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. BPS: "Statistik Modal Sosial" or SUSENAS MSBP publications, province tables.
2. Library: Naim 1979 and van Klinken 2007, read as a pair (peaceful and violent belonging).
3. Indonesian web search: "putra daerah" + "pendatang" + a province name, for local debates.
4. AMAN and BRWA websites: lists of registered customary communities and their criteria.
5. Google Scholar: "kasepekang" and "krama banjar" for Balinese studies.
