# Indonesia: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Balinese, Buginese; Dutch for colonial-era child studies. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** the child "not yet Javanese" learning restraint; compare court-town, village and devout families.
- **Sundanese:** nurture words and songs of their own, often folded into "Javanese" studies.
- **Minangkabau:** the mother's brother guiding nephews and nieces; test the old part of the *surau* in raising boys.
- **Batak:** test how far schooling is framed as the parents' life work for a child.
- **Balinese:** a cycle of infant rites; the 1930s outsider record makes a dated comparison possible.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** counsel passed down as *pappaseng*.
- **Toraja and Dayak peoples:** raising children inside ancestral-house or longhouse communities.
- **Sasak, Sumba, Flores and Timor:** older siblings and grandparents in rearing; poverty and nourishment.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** tuition, language and enterprise in upbringing, with the closing of Chinese-medium schools under the New Order as context *check*.
- ***Santri* families against secular urban families:** nurture handed to the boarding school.
- **Urban middle class with domestic workers (*ART*, *mbak*) against rural families:** who actually feeds and holds the child.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** children raised by grandparents while mothers work abroad as migrant workers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nutrition and early education; good regional monographs; thinner on fathers' nurturing and on emotional warmth.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for court texts; Bugis Lontara for *pappaseng*; Dutch for colonial reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stunting talk can shame poor and eastern families; Bateson and Mead are outsiders; child-protection law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
