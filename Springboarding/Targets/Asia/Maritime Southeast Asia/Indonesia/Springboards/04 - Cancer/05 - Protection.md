## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** heirlooms, *ruwatan* and the night watch; court lines against villages.
- **Sundanese:** spoken charms and the region's silat schools.
- **Betawi and Banten:** the *jawara* who protects and threatens at once.
- **Madurese:** defense of honor and of women in the family, at home and in migrant settlements.
- **Balinese:** customary guards, offerings and village borders guarded by rite.
- **Minangkabau:** *silek* and the mother's brother as protector of his sister's children.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** *siri'*, honor that kin are bound to defend.
- **Batak:** clan solidarity when a member is threatened, to test.
- **Dayak peoples:** customary councils and guardians of forest territory.
- **Sedulur Sikep and other customary farming communities:** non-violent protection of land.
- **Merapi, Lombok and Palu villagers:** protection where disaster is routine.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** private security and neighborhood guarding after May 1998.
- **Ambonese and other Maluku communities:** community self-defense during the 1999-2002 conflict.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** migrant workers abroad and the state agency (BP2MI) charged with protecting them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good statistics on violence and disaster; rich ethnography on charms and security; thin on how ordinary families talk about guarding their own.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for wayang and heirloom lore; Balinese for lontar; Madurese for honor studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *carok*, militias, 1998 and Maluku violence; some charms and heirloom rites are kept by their owners. Child-protection and domestic-violence law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
