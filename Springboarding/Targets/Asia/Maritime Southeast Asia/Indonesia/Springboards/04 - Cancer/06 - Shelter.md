## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** lodging with kin (*numpang*) in cities; old norms of receiving wanderers.
- **Sundanese:** field huts and village refuges in landslide-prone hills.
- **Minangkabau:** the *surau* as shelter for boys and travelers; migrants lodging with kin from the same village.
- **Sasak of Lombok and Kaili of Palu:** recent mass displacement and tent life.
- **Javanese of Merapi's slopes:** repeated evacuation and host villages.
- **Jakarta's urban poor and Betawi:** street sleepers, cart people, riverbank shelters.
- **Bajau and other sea peoples:** shelter on the water.
- **Dayak swidden farmers:** field huts away from the longhouse.
- **Balinese:** the ward hall (*bale banjar*) as a place of refuge in emergencies, to test.
- **Riau Islands and North Kalimantan border towns:** refugees and deportees housed among residents.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** neighbors who sheltered them, or did not, in May 1998, as survivors tell it.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** workers who flee employers abroad to embassy shelters, a mirror of shelter given by the state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters; mixed for homelessness (official counts and NGO data differ); thin for everyday lodging of travelers and kin.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for *Serat Centhini*; regional languages in disaster zones; Dutch for colonial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors of 1998 violence and of violence at home; refugees' legal status; camp sweeps. Refugee and housing law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, refugees in transit among them (category only).
