# Indonesia: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Toraja, Buginese. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** filial care of parents, *rewang* among neighbors; compare village and city.
- **Sundanese:** reliance on kin against paid carers in Bandung's middle class.
- **Minangkabau:** which children care for an aging father in a matrilineal family, and where.
- **Batak:** daughters-in-law and sons as carers in a patrilineal family.
- **Balinese:** healers and free service to the community.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** healers and midwives in village care.
- **Toraja:** care of the dead as part of care of kin.
- **Dayak peoples:** longhouse neighbors as carers.
- **Nusa Tenggara (Flores, Sumba, Timor) and Maluku:** old people left behind by labor migration.
- **Minahasa:** church congregations as carers.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** paid carers and elder homes, to compare with kin care.
- **Urban middle class with a hired *pramurukti* or PRT:** care bought, not given.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** Indonesian women working as carers in Taiwan and Hong Kong, whose families back home are cared for by others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Good elderly and health statistics; ethnography rich for healers; thin on time spent caring (time-use data scarce *check*).
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese for healing texts; Toraja for funeral care.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some healing knowledge is private to its holders; domestic workers' abuse cases. Care-worker law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
