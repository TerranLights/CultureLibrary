# Indonesia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Balinese, Buginese, Madurese. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** test the common claim that the *ibu* holds the household purse, and the "sacrificing mother" ideal, against court and village sources.
- **Sundanese:** how far *paraji* still attend births beside clinic midwives; the Dayang Sumbi legend as local lore.
- **Minangkabau:** motherhood inside a matrilineal house order, with *Bundo Kanduang* as a title and an organization.
- **Batak:** motherhood in a patrilineal clan, where a mother of sons gains standing, to test.
- **Balinese:** pregnancy and infant rites, and the feared mother of performance.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** midwife-healers and birth customs of South Sulawesi.
- **Madurese:** the mother in Madurese poetry and sayings, with Zawawi Imron's work as the entry point.
- **Toraja and Dayak peoples:** birth customs tied to house and longhouse.
- **Nusa Tenggara (Sumba, Flores, Timor) and Maluku:** where clinics are fewest and home births most common, to check.
- **Indonesian Chinese:** mothers in family business.
- ***Santri* mothers and secular urban working mothers:** religious and career ideals of motherhood.
- **Diaspora, comparison only:** mothers working abroad who mother by phone and remittance, a test of what counts as mothering.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Strong health and demographic data; a large feminist literature on state ideology; regional rites well documented in Java and Bali, thinner elsewhere.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese for texts; Minangkabau for *kaba*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal deaths; teen and unmarried motherhood; Gerwani and 1965. Child-marriage and family law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
