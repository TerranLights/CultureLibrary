# Indonesia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Batak Toba, Lampung, Balinese, Minangkabau; Arabic terms as used in pesantren teaching. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Batak Toba, Karo and Mandailing:** whether the Toba triad applies to Karo and Mandailing at all, and whether Christian and Muslim Batak differ.
- **Lampung Pepadun and Saibatin:** two adat systems with different routes to titles and self-regard.
- **Sundanese:** pride of language and land beside Javanese weight in the nation.
- **Javanese:** court proverbs against presumption, to set beside everyday talk of pride in children and work.
- **Balinese:** cultural pride tied to tourism and to fear of losing the island.
- **Minangkabau migrants:** pride in the home village kept up from the *rantau*.
- **Toraja:** pride competed for through houses and funerals.
- **Tionghoa:** pride in heritage after decades of pressure to hide it; and pride in national sporting service.
- **Migrant-worker villages of Java, Lombok and Nusa Tenggara:** pride in remittance houses, and the gossip around them.
- **Santri milieus:** pride taught as a fault, set beside pride in one's *kiai* and pesantren.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national pride and Java; mixed for regional codes; thin for small eastern peoples' own words for arrogance and humility.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Batak Toba, Lampung, Balinese; Arabic for pesantren texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBT Pride has no open public form and is policed; regional pride touches separatist memory in some provinces; *Ajeg Bali* touches anti-migrant feeling.
- **Held for review:** none.
