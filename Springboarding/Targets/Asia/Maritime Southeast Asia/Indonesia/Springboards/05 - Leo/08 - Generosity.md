*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Toraja nobles and commoners:** funeral giving as competition, and what it costs families.
- **Nias:** feasts of merit remembered after church and state discouraged them.
- **Javanese villages and urban middle class:** feast ledgers against app-based giving.
- **Minangkabau migrants:** gifts sent home and the standing they earn there.
- **Batak, Minahasa and Ambonese Christians:** tithes and church auctions.
- **Balinese:** labor given to temple and *banjar*.
- **Muhammadiyah and NU circles:** separate channels (Lazismu, LAZISNU) for alms.
- **Tionghoa:** clan and Buddhist philanthropy, and how others perceive it.
- **Migrant-worker families of Lombok and NTT:** remittances as giving, duty or debt.
- **Fishing communities (Bugis, Bajau, Mandar):** shares of the catch to crew and kin, *check*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Islamic giving and Java; mixed for Christian and Hindu regions; thin for eastern islands' feast economies today.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Minangkabau, Balinese, Batak Toba, Toraja, Nias; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** charity scandals (the 2022 ACT case) color trust; funeral costs in Toraja and Sumba are debated as burdens; zakat law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
