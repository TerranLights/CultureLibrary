## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese court and village:** inherited ideas of potency and mandate against populist styles.
- **Sundanese:** village elders and religious leaders in Priangan; whether the Javanese model fits.
- **Minangkabau:** shared leadership by three kinds of leader.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** a leader bound by compact to the people.
- **Balinese:** ward leadership that rotates or is elected.
- **Batak:** clan-based leading at ceremonies (the *raja parhata*, *check*).
- **Dayak peoples:** customary heads and councils beside state office.
- **Pesantren communities:** the *kiai*'s following and its inheritance.
- **Women in office and in religious bodies (Muslimat NU, Aisyiyah):** routes to leadership.
- **Students and youth groups:** leadership as a school subject.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java, Minangkabau and national politics; mixed for Bugis and Bali; thin for eastern islands' customary leaders.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Minangkabau, Bugis, Balinese; Lontara script for older texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Anderson's thesis and other one-voice models are contested; vote-buying data are partial; formal authority and office law go to Capricorn or Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian works on the sources of the *Hasta Brata* (category only).
