## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese and Sundanese children:** sung group games, and which are for girls or boys.
- **Riau and coastal Malays:** old games turned into school sports.
- **Minangkabau:** *main kim* and other games at weddings and fairs.
- **Balinese:** kite clubs that link play to ritual and village rivalry.
- **Sasak, Sumba, Manggarai and Maluku communities:** contests where play and danger meet.
- **Madurese and Minangkabau farmers:** cattle races as seasonal play.
- **Malang and Yogyakarta youth:** wordplay as local identity.
- **Urban gamers and rural children:** screens against open-air games.
- **Adult men at *ronda* posts and coffee stalls:** cards, dominoes and talk.
- **Tionghoa Peranakan:** card games such as *ceki*, and their reputation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java, Sunda and Bali through the provincial monographs and heritage files; thin for how adults talk about their own play.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Sundanese, Malay, Balinese; regional terms for eastern contests.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** betting on races and card games is illegal; some ritual contests involve injury and are tied to rites not open to all; gambling law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
