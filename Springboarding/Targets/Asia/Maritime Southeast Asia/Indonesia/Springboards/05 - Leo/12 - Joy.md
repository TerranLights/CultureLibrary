# Indonesia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (with youth slang); Javanese, Sundanese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Balinese; Riau Malay; Arabic devotional terms. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** calm contentment against the lively bustle of fairs and *campursari* crowds; to test, not assume.
- **Minangkabau:** Heider's village and town speakers, a ready base for comparison.
- **Balinese:** a festival cycle that runs from noise to silence.
- **Batak, Minahasa and Maluku Christians:** joy voiced in choir and worship.
- **Santri and reformist Muslims:** devotional singing embraced by some and doubted by others.
- **Working-class youth of Java's north coast and East Java:** *dangdut koplo* and dancing.
- **Tionghoa and Dayak of West Kalimantan:** shared festivals at Cap Go Meh.
- **Belitung and Bangka Malays:** a region seen nationally through one joyful story; how locals respond.
- **Adolescents:** joy and distress in school and online.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and Java; mixed for regional word studies; thin for how small eastern peoples speak of gladness.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Minangkabau, Balinese, Batak Toba; Arabic devotional terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** music and dance meet piety disputes; mental-health data are sensitive; some ritual joy (*tatung* trance) belongs to practitioners.
- **Held for review:** none.
