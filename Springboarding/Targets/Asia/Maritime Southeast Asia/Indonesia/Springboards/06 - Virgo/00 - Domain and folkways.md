# Indonesia: The domain, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese:** court-centered south-central Java, the north-coast *pasisir* and East Java's Arek zone may split work and leisure differently.
- **Sundanese:** upland dry-field and tea-estate work beside wet rice.
- **Madurese:** salt making, cattle and out-migration as a work pattern.
- **Balinese:** the banjar and subak calendars set both labor and owed service.
- **Minangkabau:** going away to earn (*merantau*) as a stage of a working life.
- **Batak peoples (Toba, Karo, Mandailing, Simalungun):** separate reciprocal-labor customs.
- **Bugis, Makassar and Mandar:** seafaring and trade as work.
- **Toraja:** funeral feasts as the year's heaviest labor and spending.
- **Dayak peoples and the Banjar:** swidden and river work against Banjar trade and Islam.
- **Nusa Tenggara (Sasak, Bima, Sumba, Flores, Timor):** dryland farming and seasonal hunger as the frame for work.
- **Minahasa and Maluku:** named communal-work groups.
- **Chinese Indonesians and Arab Indonesians:** shopkeeping and trade lineages.
- **Adat communities (Baduy, Sedulur Sikep, Orang Rimba, Mentawai):** distinct ideas of which work is permitted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national surveys and Java; mixed for Sumatra, Bali and Sulawesi; thin for eastern Nusa Tenggara, Maluku and Indigenous communities. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; Aceh and West Papua are separate targets in this setting and are not covered here.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Dutch at minimum; Javanese (with its speech levels) and Balinese for texts; Old Javanese for inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor history meets the 1965 to 1966 killings and the banned union federation; sorcery talk in healing; some adat communities limit outsiders' entry and recording.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
