## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau:** the adat saying that assigns every person a task, to be tested against practice.
- **Javanese households practicing *ngèngèr*:** usefulness exchanged for schooling and keep.
- **Sasak and East Javanese villages with parents working abroad:** grandparents made central as child-raisers.
- **Balinese:** elders' standing in banjar duties after they step back from labor.
- **Urban pensioners (former civil servants and soldiers):** where the "post-power" label is used.
- **Chinese Indonesian family firms:** the old who keep working in the shop.
- **Dayak longhouse communities:** elders valued for forest and ritual knowledge.
- **NTT villages:** children's water and firewood chores in dry country.
- **Orang Rimba:** forest knowledge labeled "unproductive" by plantation and state.
- **Deaf communities using Bisindo:** a language and community judged by others' measure of use.
- **Scavengers and recyclers on city edges:** useful labor with low standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; elder and disability statistics are good, but felt usefulness is thin and must come from ethnography, sermons and media.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Minangkabau for sayings; Dutch for land records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability terms and the stigma of mental illness; children's work; land taken as "waste". Pension and disability law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
