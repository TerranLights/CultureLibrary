## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muhammadiyah and NU communities:** opposed methods for fixing sacred dates.
- **Javanese villages and court cities:** observation, wordplay and day-reckoning beside school reasoning.
- **Balinese:** manuscript-based day-reckoning alongside modern schooling.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** a long court tradition of dated records.
- **Minangkabau:** the home of several founding intellectuals, including Tan Malaka and Hatta.
- **Toba Batak and Minahasa:** long mission-school histories; check whether examinations and degrees weigh differently there.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** abacus training and bookkeeping in family trade.
- **Bajau and Bugis sailors:** reading waves, wind and stars.
- **Dayak and Orang Rimba:** forest observation knowledge held outside schools.
- **Bandung and Jakarta technical elites (ITB, startup firms):** the engineer and data-analyst class.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national debates and school tests; mixed for folk reasoning; thin for Indigenous knowledge systems in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Arabic for falak texts, Dutch for colonial statistics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tan Malaka's communist ties; ranking provinces by test scores; adat knowledge some communities keep from outsiders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
