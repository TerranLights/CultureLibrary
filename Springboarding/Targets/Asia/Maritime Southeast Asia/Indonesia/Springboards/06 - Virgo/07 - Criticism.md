## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Javanese of the court cities:** test the common claim that criticism of superiors travels by jokes and hints.
- **East Javanese Arek communities of Surabaya and Malang:** a ludruk tradition of open mockery; test the "blunter" label.
- **Minangkabau:** speech rules that set how criticism travels between ranks.
- **Toba and Karo Batak:** a reputation for directness to test, not assume.
- **Betawi:** lenong theater banter and plain talk.
- **Balinese:** criticism voiced in banjar meetings (*sangkep*) and by temple clowns.
- **Sundanese:** wayang golek comedy as a vent.
- **Bugis and Madurese:** shame codes (*siri'*; Madurese *malo* *check*) that may raise the stakes of open criticism.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** the risk of criticizing in public as a minority, sharpened by the Ahok blasphemy case.
- **Pesantren students:** taught rules against backbiting and for sincere counsel.
- **Student activists:** the 1998 movement and later campus protest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history, satire and literature; mixed for workplace feedback; thin for family criticism outside Java.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (including East Javanese dialect); Minangkabau; Arabic terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disappeared activists; blasphemy charges; the Ahok case; violence answering public insult. Prosecutions for online criticism under the electronic-information law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
