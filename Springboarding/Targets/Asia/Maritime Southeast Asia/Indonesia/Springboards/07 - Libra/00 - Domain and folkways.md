## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese of the court heartland (Yogyakarta, Surakarta), the north coast (*pesisir*) and East Java (Arek, Osing, Tengger), since their etiquette and marriage rites differ.
- Sundanese, with the Baduy (Kanekes) who keep their own rules apart; Madurese, whose honor quarrels are studied separately; Betawi.
- Balinese (Hindu, *desa adat* with written *awig-awig*) and Sasak of Lombok (elopement marriage).
- Minangkabau (matrilineal, Muslim) against Batak Toba, Karo and Mandailing (patrilineal, clan alliances).
- Malay, Palembang and Lampung peoples of Sumatra; Banjar and Dayak peoples of Kalimantan.
- Bugis, Makassarese, Mandar and Toraja of Sulawesi; Minahasa and Gorontalo in the north.
- Eastern Indonesia: Sumba, Flores (Manggarai, Sikka), Rote, Timor, Ambon and Seram, where bridewealth and alliance rules differ island by island.
- Chinese-Indonesians (Peranakan and *totok*), Arab-Indonesians (Hadhrami), Dutch-Indonesian Eurasians (Indo).
- Religious lines: Muslim (NU, Muhammadiyah, others), Protestant, Catholic, Hindu, Buddhist, Confucian, and *penghayat kepercayaan* (native-faith adherents).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java, Bali, Minangkabau and national law; mixed for Kalimantan and Sulawesi; thinner for small eastern islands. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the core lands. Aceh and the Papuan provinces are researched as their own targets.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (with *krama* registers); Dutch for colonial law and ethnography; Arabic for fiqh; regional languages for adat texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1965-66 killings, Ambon, Poso and Sampit conflicts; blasphemy cases; LGBT people under local by-laws; some adat knowledge (Baduy, Toraja rites) is not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
