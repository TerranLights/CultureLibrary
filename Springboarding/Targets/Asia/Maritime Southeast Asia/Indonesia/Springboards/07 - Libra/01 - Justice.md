## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Madura and Madurese migrants in East Java and Kalimantan, where honor fights sit beside state courts.
- Bugis and Makassarese, whose *siri'* tradition bears on revenge and redress.
- Bali, where *desa adat* sanctions and *karmapala* sit beside the criminal code.
- Dayak peoples (Ngaju, Kenyah, Kayan and others) and their customary fines; Minangkabau *nagari* courts of custom.
- Javanese villages (Just King movements, Samin followers who refused colonial demands) against the Jakarta metropolis.
- Victim communities: 1965 survivors and families; Chinese-Indonesians after May 1998; Muslim and Christian villages of Maluku and Poso; Shia and Ahmadiyah minorities after attacks.
- Indonesian migrant workers abroad facing foreign courts: comparison only, for what the home state promises them in law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national law, prisons and NGO monitoring; mixed for adat sanctions; thin and contested for 1965 and other mass violence.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for colonial *landraad* records; Javanese and Madurese for oral sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1965 is politically charged; victims' testimony needs consent; mob-killing footage; blasphemy convictions. What justice is for, and obedience, belong to Capricorn's spirit of law.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Compare UU 1/2023 with the old KUHP on the death penalty and *hukum yang hidup dalam masyarakat* (living law).
2. Search Kompas and Tempo archives for "*main hakim sendiri*" by province, 2010 onward.
3. Pull Ditjen Pemasyarakatan occupancy data by province.
4. Search Garuda and Leiden catalogs for "*sanksi adat*" with Bali, Dayak and Minangkabau.
5. Read Komnas HAM's public summaries on 1965-66 and the 1998 cases.
