*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Batak Toba, Manggarai, Bugis, Minangkabau; Hokkien loanwords; Old Javanese for classical texts. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese of the court cities against north-coast merchant families, where wives' trade roles differ.
- Batak Toba, Karo and Mandailing, whose clan alliances bind wife-givers and wife-takers.
- Minangkabau, with husbands living into their wives' houses.
- Manggarai, Sumbanese and other Nusa Tenggara peoples, where marriage sets up debts between houses.
- Bugis and Makassarese, where matches track status and *siri'*.
- Chinese-Indonesian family firms and Arab-Indonesian merchant families in Pekalongan, Gresik and Surabaya, with their own partner choices.
- Smallholders and plantation firms in Riau, Jambi and Kalimantan; fishing and village cooperatives across Java.
- Devout campus youth against secular urban couples; interfaith couples facing registration limits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage law, divorce data and Java; mixed for business partnership in practice; thin for sworn bonds outside the best-studied peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Batak; Dutch for colonial registers; Chinese for Kong Koan records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interfaith and same-sex couples face legal limits; ethnic tension around Chinese-Indonesian business.
