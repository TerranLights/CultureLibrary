## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Balinese Hindus, whose temple, village and household rules name paired and threefold balance.
- Javanese of *kejawen* leanings against reformist Muslims who reject some of those teachings.
- Minangkabau villages of the Bodi Caniago and Koto Piliang systems.
- Baduy (Kanekes) and Tengger highlanders, who keep ancestral limits on change.
- Maluku *sasi* villages, Muslim and Christian; Dayak communities claiming customary forest.
- Chinese-Indonesians, whose yin-yang medicine and temple practice differ from *jamu* traditions.
- Jabodetabek commuters and factory workers with long hours, against farmers and fishers with seasonal rhythms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Bali and Java; mixed for Minangkabau and Maluku; thin for balance-in-the-body ideas outside Java and Bali.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese, including manuscript forms; Dutch for colonial ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some lontar and Baduy knowledge is restricted to initiates; official uses of Tri Hita Karana in tourism marketing may differ from village usage.
- **Held for review:** none.
