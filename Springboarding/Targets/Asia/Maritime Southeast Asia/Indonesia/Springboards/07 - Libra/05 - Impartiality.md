# Indonesia: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Bugis, Ngaju Dayak; Arabic for Islamic witness rules; Old Javanese for classical rulership texts. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese bureaucratic families with *priyayi* roots, where deference to superiors meets merit rules.
- Banten, Kutai and other districts with family dynasties, against cities with more turnover.
- Chinese-Indonesians, whose dealings with offices changed after 1998.
- Native-faith adherents (*penghayat*), Ahmadiyah and Shia Muslims, as tests of equal treatment by officials.
- Dayak, Balinese and Minangkabau communities where customary judges know both parties.
- Bugis and Makassarese, with their own written ethics of rulers.
- Members of large mass organizations (NU, Muhammadiyah) and university alumni networks, as channels of preference.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, corruption surveys and journalism; mixed for everyday favoritism, which surveys capture only partly.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese for deference vocabulary; Bugis for Lontara texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** named dynasties and corruption cases carry defamation risk; ethnic and religious minorities' complaints. Obedience to authority and legal culture belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
