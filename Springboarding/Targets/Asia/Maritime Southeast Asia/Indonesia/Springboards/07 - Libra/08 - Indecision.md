# Indonesia: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (including youth slang); Javanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Toraja; Arabic for prayer terms; Hokkien for temple practice; Old Javanese. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Javanese families who set dates by reckoning, against reformist Muslim families who reject it.
- Balinese households and *banjar* assemblies, with their own day-reckoning and council rules.
- Minangkabau lineages, where decisions pass through maternal uncles and village councils.
- Toraja and Dayak communities whose councils decide feasts and land matters.
- Chinese-Indonesian temple-goers; Catholic and Protestant families with their own discernment habits.
- Urban students and young workers, where procrastination and "*galau*" talk concentrate.
- National and regional legislators and planners, for collective delay.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elections, almanac texts and student psychology; mixed for family decisions; thin outside Java and Bali for decision-by-reckoning.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese for almanacs; Hokkien terms; Old Javanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reckoning practices are criticized by some Muslim groups, so ask without ranking; Balinese *wariga* knowledge is held by priests.
- **Held for review:** none.
