*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sa'dan Toraja:** the dead kept at home; delay as the extreme case.
- **Plains Balinese against Bali Aga villages (Trunyan, Tenganan):** cremation against older exposure or burial.
- **Javanese families of Nahdlatul Ulama against Muhammadiyah households:** prayers for the dead held or refused.
- **Ngaju Dayak (Kaharingan) against Christian Dayak:** whether the second funeral survives conversion.
- **Batak Toba:** reburial as lineage status.
- **Gunungkidul and other dry upland districts:** a regional pattern of suicide with its own explanation.
- **Sumba (Marapu) and Catholic Flores:** megalith graves against church burial.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** Buddhist, Confucian and Christian funerals inside one community.
- **Jakarta's crowded public cemeteries:** stacked graves and the urban squeeze on the dead.
- **Migrant workers who die abroad:** comparison only; their repatriation shows what families insist on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for funeral ethnography; thin for death statistics, suicide and felt grief.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, regional languages for laments; Arabic; Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide is stigmatized and under-reported; Toraja and Trunyan dead are photographed by tourists, so seek villagers' own voices; some Marapu and Kaharingan mortuary chants are held by priests. Rules on bodies and burial in law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, for their own funeral grounds (category only).
