*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Madurese, on Madura and the East Java horseshoe coast:** honor duels and their regional variants.
- **Bugis and Makassarese:** honor and solidarity in family retaliation.
- **Dayak and Madurese of West and Central Kalimantan:** the 1997-2001 cycles; Madurese displaced to Madura carry their own account.
- **Sumbanese:** feud memory, horse-and-spear ritual and how women's revenge is received.
- **Balinese:** revenge through sorcery and its staging in ritual drama.
- **North Moluccans of Halmahera:** sorcery suspicion and the violence of 1999-2000.
- **Poso and Ambon:** reprisal between faiths.
- **East Java silat brotherhoods:** youth retaliation in organized groups.
- **Urban Javanese middle class:** karma and *azab* talk; quieter forms of getting even.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honor-violence ethnography and communal-conflict studies; mixed for event data; thin for everyday petty revenge and for forgiveness.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Madurese, Buginese and Makassarese, Javanese; Sumbanese and Dayak languages for local accounts; Dutch for colonial *amok* and raiding reports, read critically.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Kalimantan, Poso and Maluku conflicts involved mass killing; ethnic labeling of Madurese or Dayak as "violent" is a stereotype to avoid; sorcery accusations can endanger the accused. Retaliation in law (including *qisas* debates) goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, for any feud traditions of their own (category only).
