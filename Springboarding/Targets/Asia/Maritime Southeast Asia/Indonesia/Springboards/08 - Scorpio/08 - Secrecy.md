## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese *kebatinan* circles and court-linked mystics:** whether knowledge passes only to the tested.
- **Sufi order members in West Java, Madura and East Java:** initiation and confidential practice inside Islam.
- **Balinese healers and lontar keepers:** written knowledge with limited readers.
- **Kanekes (Baduy):** inner and outer villages with different rules for outsiders.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** the 1966-1998 era of hidden names, rites and schooling; older *kongsi* oaths.
- **Families of 1965 detainees:** pasts hidden from children and employers.
- **LGBT Indonesians:** disclosure managed by place, family and faith.
- **Sasak Wetu Telu communities (Lombok):** how practice is shown to, or kept from, reformist observers.
- **Kaharingan Dayak and *penghayat* believers:** faith hidden under an official label.
- **Urban youth online:** anonymity as a way to speak.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious and mystical groups and for colonial societies; mixed for state secrecy; thin for measured privacy attitudes.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese (including courtly register), Balinese, Sundanese; Arabic; Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kanekes rules, *kebatinan* teachings and Balinese lontar on harmful lore are restricted by their keepers; record that they exist, not their content. LGBT and 1965 families face real exposure risks. Secrecy law (state secrets, privacy statutes) goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, for their own closed societies (category only).
