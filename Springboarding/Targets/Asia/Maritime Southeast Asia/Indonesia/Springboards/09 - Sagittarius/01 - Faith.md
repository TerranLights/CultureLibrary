# Indonesia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese, Batak Toba, Buginese; Arabic for devotional vocabulary; Dutch for mission archives. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese** of NU-leaning villages against Muhammadiyah towns: shrine vows and intercession against reformist distrust of both.
- **Madurese**, where the kiai stands between the believer and God in ways worth measuring separately.
- **Sundanese** of greater Bandung, a center of televised preaching and the hijrah movement.
- **Minangkabau**, home ground of Islamic reform, where the surau shaped devotion.
- **Bugis**, whose *bissu* ritual specialists predate Islam (handle with care).
- **Batak Toba**, **Minahasa** and **Toraja** Protestants, where the church is also an ethnic institution.
- **Flores** Catholics (Larantuka, Manggarai) and **Sumba**'s Marapu adherents, side by side.
- **Balinese** Hindus against the **Javanese who became Hindu** after 1965 (Klaten, Blitar), whose faith has a different origin.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, split among Buddhist, Confucian and Christian faith, often within one family.
- **Dayak** Kaharingan adherents against Christian Dayak; **Sasak** Wetu Telu against Nahdlatul Wathan's reformed practice.
- Diaspora, comparison only: Indonesian domestic workers' prayer groups in Hong Kong, to see how faith holds under migrant strain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Muslim piety and large churches; mixed for Hindu and Confucian faith; thin for local faiths outside their own organizations.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Arabic first; Javanese for devotion and manuscripts; Balinese, Batak Toba and Dutch for minority churches and missions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion and apostasy (fears of Christianization, Islamization; their law goes to Libra or Capricorn); the bissu; Marapu and Kaharingan ritual knowledge, where only what communities publish is used.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. IFLS documentation: search "religiosity" in the IFLS-5 questionnaire to find the faith items and their provinces.
2. Kemenag Balitbang: "indeks kesalehan sosial" for the method and the provincial breakdown.
3. Library: Fealy and White (2008), then Quinn (2019), to set reformist and shrine faith side by side.
4. LAI and Perpusnas: "Alkitab Melayu Leydekker" for the scriptural vocabulary of faith.
5. YouTube and Instagram: "kisah hijrah" and "kesaksian iman" for first-person testimony, Muslim and Christian.
