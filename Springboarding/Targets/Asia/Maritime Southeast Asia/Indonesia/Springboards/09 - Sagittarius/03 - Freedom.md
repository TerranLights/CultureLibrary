*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Dutch (colonial and nationalist texts); Old Javanese, Buginese and Makassarese for status terms; Minangkabau; Arabic for Islamic debate. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese** court culture, where order and rank framed liberty, against the more egalitarian **Minangkabau**, whose freedom to leave home is a social institution.
- **Bugis-Makassar**, where the maradeka/ata distinction still echoes in family memory.
- **Balinese**, where caste titles and freedom from them are argued over.
- **Sumba** and **Nias**, whose old ranked orders bear on present freedom (sensitive).
- **West Sumatra** and **North Sulawesi (Minahasa)**, which once took up arms for autonomy.
- **Maluku**, where the 1950 separatist memory makes "freedom" a charged word (sensitive).
- **Chinese Indonesians**, for whom public culture (Imlek, Chinese script, names) was restricted until 1998-2000.
- **Yogyakarta**, a special region under a reigning sultan.
- **Women** as a cross-cutting group, from Kartini to present debates on dress and mobility.
- **Urban youth**, at the center of *pergaulan bebas* moral debate.
- Diaspora, comparison only: political exiles unable to return after 1965 (*eksil*), for freedom understood as the right to come home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national liberation and civil-liberties monitoring; mixed for regional and personal freedom; thin for pre-modern status terms outside Sulawesi and Bali.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for 1900-1950; Buginese and Old Javanese for classical status terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1965-1966 detention and exile; separatist memory in Maluku; religious freedom (blasphemy and registration law: Libra or Capricorn); slavery descendants' standing in Sumba.
- **Held for review:** none.
