*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Balinese**, for whom the puputan is a living story of holding firm.
- **Sedulur Sikep (Samin)** of Blora, Pati and Rembang: conviction as refusal, in their own terms.
- **Javanese** court-derived ideals of controlled resolve against the open protest culture of student cities (Yogyakarta, Bandung).
- **Bugis-Makassar**: firmness as a named virtue, tied to honor; test how far.
- **Minangkabau**: a tradition of public debate and principled intellectuals (Hatta, Natsir, Hamka).
- **Batak**: a reputation for plain, forceful speech, to test rather than assume.
- **NU and Muhammadiyah** members: two paths between principle and accommodation.
- **Catholics and Protestants** in Java and in Flores, for minority conviction in public life.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, whose stands carry added risk (Soe Hok Gie; Ahok's supporters).
- **Workers and farmers** (Kendeng; factory belts of Tangerang and Surabaya) against student activists.
- Diaspora, comparison only: Indonesian students abroad who organized against the New Order in the 1990s, to test conviction at a safe distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national heroes, students and NGOs; mixed for everyday conviction; thin for measured attitudes (few surveys ask how firmly people hold views).
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (and Middle Javanese for *Pararaton* and the babad); Balinese; Dutch for colonial-era resistance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enforced disappearances (1997-1998), the Munir case, 1965 political prisoners; heroic myths built by the state.
- **Held for review:** none.
