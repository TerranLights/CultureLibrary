## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese**, where polite indirection may count as courtesy rather than dishonesty: test, by class and region.
- **Minangkabau**, whose speech rules set candor by the listener's rank.
- **Bugis-Makassar**, with honesty named as a core virtue in their own texts.
- **Sundanese**, whose ideal person includes "right", and whose Si Kabayan cheats with charm.
- **Batak** and **Madurese**, each said to be blunt; treat as claims to test.
- **Balinese**, where temple oaths are reported as a check on lying *check*.
- **Chinese-Indonesian** traders, where credit and reputation networks enforce fair dealing.
- **Civil servants** against market traders and online sellers, for honesty under different incentives.
- **Students** in exam-heavy schools, against pesantren pupils taught the prophetic qualities.
- **Eastern islands** (Maluku, Nusa Tenggara), where village oaths and adat fines may handle lies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corruption (many surveys and indexes); mixed on everyday honesty; thin on regional speech ethics outside Java and Sulawesi.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (Krama for court texts); Buginese; Minangkabau; Old Malay for inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national-character essays (stereotype risk); corruption cases involving named living people. Anti-corruption law: Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
