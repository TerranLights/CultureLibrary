## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East Javanese** pesantren heartland (Jombang, Kediri) against **Yogyakarta**'s nationalist and university culture.
- **Madurese**, where pesantren may weigh more than state schooling: measure, don't assume.
- **Minangkabau**, whose surau and reformist schools fed the national intelligentsia.
- **Batak Toba**, where a song makes children's schooling a family's wealth.
- **Minahasa**, with early mission schooling and women founders.
- **Bugis-Makassar**, with large pesantren of their own (As'adiyah, Sengkang).
- **Banjar** of South Kalimantan, a Jawi scholarly tradition apart from Java's.
- **Balinese**, with Hindu schools (*pasraman*) and a tourism-driven vocational stream.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, whose own schools were closed and later partly revived.
- **Arab-Indonesians**, with reformist schools of their own.
- **Orang Rimba** and other forest peoples, where schooling meets mobile life.
- **East Nusa Tenggara**, where Catholic schools and low enrollment sit side by side (test).
- Diaspora, comparison only: Indonesian students at Al-Azhar in Cairo, to see how study abroad reshapes home ulama.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (heavy state data, long scholarly tradition on pesantren and colonial schooling); thinner for eastern islands and Indigenous schooling.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Arabic and Jawi; Dutch; Javanese; Mandarin for Chinese-school sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pesantren abuse cases; radical pesantren (see Fundamentalism); the 1966 closure of Chinese schools. Education law and curriculum mandates: Capricorn or Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
