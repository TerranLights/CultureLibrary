# Indonesia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Arabic for movement vocabulary; Javanese; Sundanese; Minangkabau; Dutch for colonial reports on revivalism. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau**: the Padri past and the kaum muda/kaum tua split, a long purist-traditionalist argument.
- **Sundanese** of West Java: Darul Islam's heartland, Persis's home, and present recruitment networks.
- **Bantenese**, with the 1888 revolt and strong kiai authority.
- **Bugis-Makassar**: Darul Islam's Sulawesi wing and Makassar-based Wahdah Islamiyah.
- **Central Javanese** of Solo, where purist pesantren and the court's syncretic heritage share one city.
- **East Javanese** NU villages, a counterweight, beside a few villages linked to militancy (sensitive).
- **Sasak** of Lombok, where Salafi and Nahdlatul Wathan communities have clashed.
- **Maluku** and **Poso**, where Christian and Muslim militias fought around 2000.
- **Balinese**, for Hindu boundary-keeping.
- **Minahasa** and **Batak** Protestants, set against charismatic newcomers; Christian militia in Manado *check*.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, many in charismatic churches.
- Diaspora, comparison only: Indonesians who studied in Saudi Arabia and Yemen and came back as Salafi teachers, to see what foreign study adds to home-grown purism.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (a large scholarly and security literature); risk of overweighting militancy, so balance with quietist and ordinary believers' accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Arabic; Javanese and Sundanese for local movements; Dutch for colonial reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** terrorism; communal violence; group bans and "deviant sect" rulings; stigma on Salafi dress. Blasphemy, religion-registration and organization-ban law: Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and Islamic movements founded in South Asia, as categories.
