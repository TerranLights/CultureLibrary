# Indonesia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian, Javanese, Sundanese, Madurese, Minangkabau, Balinese, Riau Malay, Buginese; Arabic for pesantren texts; Dutch for colonial reports. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mataram Javanese (Yogyakarta, Surakarta):** courts still active; deference speech strongest.
- **Arek culture of Surabaya and Malang:** a reputed bluntness, to test against court manners.
- **Samin (Sedulur Sikep) communities:** principled non-compliance as a living tradition.
- **Madurese:** obedience to the kyai as well as the ruler.
- **Minangkabau:** authority split among adat chiefs, Islamic scholars and the educated.
- **Balinese:** triwangsa houses against village assemblies (banjar) in who decides.
- **Bugis, Makassar and Luwu:** noble authority and revived kingdoms (Gowa, Bone).
- **North Maluku (Ternate, Tidore):** sultans with present-day ceremonial roles.
- **Dayak communities:** adat heads (kepala adat, damang) beside state officials.
- **NU and Muhammadiyah families:** a kyai's personal authority against an organization's.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** clan and temple associations as their own authorities.
