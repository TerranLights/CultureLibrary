# Indonesia: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese for ascetic practice; Balinese for Nyepi; Minangkabau for silek; Arabic for pesantren discipline; Chinese for Chinese-Indonesian schools. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Santri of large modern pesantren (Gontor and its branches) against traditional salaf pesantren:** different regimes, to research.
- **Military and police families living in asrama compounds:** home life near the barracks.
- **Javanese kejawen practitioners:** inward, ascetic discipline.
- **Madiun and other East Java silat towns:** organized training with a darker side of school rivalries.
- **Minangkabau:** surau and silek traditions, now largely changed.
- **Balinese:** collective abstention at Nyepi and temple duties.
- **Christian schools of Batak, Minahasa and Flores communities:** mission-school discipline legacies.
- **Chinese-Indonesian schools (revived after 1998):** study regimes and Mandarin classes.
- **Urban middle-class parents:** "gentle parenting" talk against older corporal correction; to test.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on schools and state campaigns; mixed on home discipline; thin on outer-island training traditions.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Arabic, Balinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child abuse, including in boarding schools; hazing deaths; deaths and clashes linked to silat rivalries; Ramadan raids on food stalls in some regions (handle as a local policy debate).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
