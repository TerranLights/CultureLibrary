## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minangkabau:** merantau as an expected stage for young men; tension between rantau success and village duty.
- **Toba Batak:** the hamoraon-hagabeon-hasangapon goals and heavy investment in schooling; to test.
- **Javanese priyayi milieu against pesisir traders:** restraint and office against commerce.
- **Bugis and Makassar:** trade and migration across the archipelago.
- **Madurese:** migrant entrepreneurs in other islands' cities.
- **Banjar of South Kalimantan:** trade and pilgrimage as routes to standing.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** family firms, and limits set on other careers in some eras.
- **Belitung and Bangka:** tin-mining towns with their own mobility stories.
- **Women:** the Kartini line, and later debates over women's careers.
- **Students and workers abroad:** comparison only, for ambition pursued outside the country.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on migration and education; mixed on attitudes to striving; thin on rural eastern islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for colonial-era sources; Minangkabau, Batak and Buginese for local idioms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** anti-Chinese resentment of business success (May 1998); avoid any claim that one people is "more ambitious" than another; test every such image.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia's lands (category only).
