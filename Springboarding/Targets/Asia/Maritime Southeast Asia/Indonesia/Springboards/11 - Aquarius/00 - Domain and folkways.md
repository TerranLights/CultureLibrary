# Indonesia: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Madurese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Batak languages, Buginese, Banjar, Sasak, local Malay varieties (Manado, Ambon, Kupang); Dutch for colonial sources. **Concept in one line:** the domain of community and ideals: what binds people into groups, and what they hope for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese of the court regions (Yogyakarta, Surakarta) and of the north coast and East Java:** court etiquette and *kejawen* against coastal *santri* towns.
- **Sundanese, Betawi, Madurese, Osing (Banyuwangi):** distinct neighborhood and hospitality customs on and beside Java.
- **Balinese:** the *banjar* and *subak* as formal associations.
- **Minangkabau:** matrilineal clans and *merantau*, men's coffee-stall life.
- **Batak (Toba, Karo, Mandailing, Simalungun):** clan associations that follow migrants to cities.
- **Bugis, Makassar, Mandar, Toraja:** seafaring diasporas; Toraja funeral feasts that gather whole kin networks.
- **Dayak peoples and Banjar (Kalimantan):** longhouse communities against river-town Islam.
- **Sasak, Sumbawa, Bima, Sumba, Flores, Timor, Alor:** eastern islands with clan houses and feast exchange.
- **Minahasa and Ambonese:** Christian majorities with cross-faith alliance customs.
- **Chinese Indonesians (Tionghoa), Arab Indonesians:** their own associations, schools and temples.
- **Small peoples on the margins (Orang Rimba, Baduy, Mentawai, Bajau sea people, Orang Laut):** communities that keep apart or move.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for Java and Bali, mixed for Sumatra and Sulawesi, thin for the eastern islands and small peoples; much ethnography is Dutch or English. Official community language (*gotong royong*, *musyawarah*) is abundant and needs reading against practice.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core lands. Aceh and Papua are separate targets in this setting and are left out here.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese (including *krama* registers); Dutch; regional languages for local sources; Arabic for Islamic texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic and religious violence (Maluku, Poso, West and Central Kalimantan) and 1965-1966; ask before recording customary knowledge held by elders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the WVS 2018 Indonesia file and tabulate the belonging and neighbor items by province and religion.
2. Search BPS for "PODES" and "Statistik Modal Sosial" to find which village-level community indicators exist.
3. Read Bowen (1986), then Geertz on the *slametan* and *arisan*, as a pair.
4. Search eHRAF for OCM 570 and 620 across its Indonesian files.
5. Search Indonesian-language theses (Garuda, the national journal portal) for "gotong royong" + a province name.
