# Indonesia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese, Minangkabau, Batak Toba, Buginese, Sasak, Manado and Ambon Malay, Dayak languages; Hokkien and Arabic loan terms; Dutch for colonial-era village studies. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest, cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban kampung against gated *perumahan*:** older neighborhood obligations against new estates sold on privacy.
- **Javanese village (*desa*) in the court regions and on the north coast:** *slametan*, *sambatan* and *rewang* may differ by religious orientation.
- **Balinese:** customary and administrative *banjar* side by side.
- **Minangkabau:** village communities with half their men away.
- **Batak, Bugis and Madurese migrants:** communities rebuilt far from home.
- **Transmigration settlements in Sumatra, Kalimantan and Sulawesi:** communities planted by the state among older peoples.
- **Dayak longhouse peoples:** the longhouse as a whole community under one roof.
- **Toraja and the eastern islands (Sumba, Flores, Timor):** clan-house communities.
- **Minahasa and Ambonese villages:** Christian *negeri* and their Muslim neighbors.
- **Chinese Indonesians (Glodok, Singkawang, Medan) and Arab kampung (Pekojan, Ampel):** enclaves with their own halls and mosques.
- **Orang Rimba and Bajau sea people:** small mobile groups whose community is not a fixed village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Javanese and Balinese villages; good on Minangkabau and Batak; mixed elsewhere. Much official writing praises community in fixed formulas; field studies are needed against it.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese and Balinese registers; Dutch for colonial village surveys.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** evictions; conflicts between transmigrants and older residents; Shia and Ahmadiyya communities driven from their homes; village law is for Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
