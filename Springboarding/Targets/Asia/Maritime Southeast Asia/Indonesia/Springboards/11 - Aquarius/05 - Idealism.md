# Indonesia: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese; Sundanese; Buginese; Dutch for the founders' early writings. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese court tradition:** idealized kingship and knightly duty in literature and performance.
- **Sundanese and Bugis:** their own ideal-person and ideal-leader formulas, to set beside Javanese ones.
- **Minangkabau:** home region of several founders (Hatta, Tan Malaka, Sjahrir, Yamin); check what each drew from it and from the *rantau*.
- **Santri circles:** Islamic ideals of society, from mainstream organizations to Darul Islam's heirs.
- **Christian minorities (Batak, Minahasa, Ambonese, Flores):** church-led ideals of service and education.
- **University students:** the self-image of the *mahasiswa* as the nation's moral force.
- **Workers and farmers:** ideals voiced by labor and peasant movements, then suppressed after 1965.
- **Orang Rimba and other forest peoples:** often the objects of outsiders' ideals; seek their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the revolution, the 1960s and 1998; thin for ideals as rural and eastern-island people state them.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Dutch for pre-1942 writings; Javanese for court literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the left's ideals remain politically charged; Darul Islam and later caliphate groups; hagiography around founders.
- **Held for review:** none.
