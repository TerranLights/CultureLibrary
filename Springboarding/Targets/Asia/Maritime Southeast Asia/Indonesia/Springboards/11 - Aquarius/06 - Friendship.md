*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pesantren alumni and Muslim student groups:** friendship framed as religious brotherhood.
- **Javanese towns:** the *kanca* of the stall against the more formal kin and neighbor ties.
- **Ambonese and other Central Maluku villages:** *pela* as friendship between communities, Christian and Muslim.
- **Batak and Minangkabau migrants:** friends from the home village as the first network in the city.
- **Chinese Indonesians:** sworn brotherhood and surname networks against school friendships with non-Chinese.
- **Balinese:** friendships within and across *banjar* and descent groups.
- **Gay and *waria* communities:** friendship as chosen family under pressure.
- **Urban middle-class youth:** cafés, apps and *circle* culture.
- **Rural men:** friendship around fishing, shared farm work, coffee stalls and, in Bali, the cockfight ring.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in film, fiction and online talk; thin in surveys asking directly about friends; thin on the eastern islands except Maluku.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian slang (fast-changing), Javanese, Ambon Malay; Dutch for colonial-era Maluku sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *pela* during the Maluku conflict; LGBT friendship networks face legal and social pressure; cronyism touches living figures.
- **Held for review:** none.
