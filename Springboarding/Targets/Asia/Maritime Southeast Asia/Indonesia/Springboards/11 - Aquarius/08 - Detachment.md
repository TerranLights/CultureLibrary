# Indonesia: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and youth slang; Javanese (mystical and ethical vocabulary); Balinese; Minangkabau; Sundanese (for the Kanekes); Arabic for Sufi terms; Dutch and English for the ethnographies. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, impartiality, equanimity), others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese of the court regions:** a written ethic of restraint, to test against everyday speech.
- **Balinese:** composure in public, as Wikan describes North Bali; compare South Bali and the towns.
- **Minangkabau and Batak:** their own emotion vocabularies (Heider's Minangkabau data first), compared with Javanese ones rather than assumed to contrast.
- **Toraja and Bugis:** Toraja grief customs and the Bugis honor vocabulary (*siri'*), two different settings for restraint and its breaking.
- **Santri in Sufi orders:** renunciation as a practice.
- **Kanekes (Baduy) and other withdrawn communities:** distance from the outside world as a rule.
- **Chinese Indonesian Buddhists:** meditation centers and their own vocabulary.
- **Urban young people:** slang of not caring and the Stoicism trend.
- **Men, rural and urban:** check mental-health studies for how distress is shown or kept back, by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Java and Bali ethnography; mixed elsewhere; very little survey data on emotion display.
- **Languages needed:** Javanese (including *krama*), Indonesian, Arabic, Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *pasung* (shackling of people with mental illness) at the extreme of withdrawal; esoteric teachings of orders and mystical groups may be given only to initiates.
- **Held for review:** none.
