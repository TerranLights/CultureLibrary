# Indonesia: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Javanese** of the court cities, with fine speech-level etiquette, against **East Javanese Arek** speech of Surabaya and Malang, often described as plainer (a description to test, not assume).
- **Sundanese**, whose ideal of friendly softness (*someah*) is worth comparing with Javanese restraint.
- **Balinese**, especially North and South Bali, since Wikan's work is one region.
- **Minangkabau**, where *raso* sits beside open debate in the adat council.
- **Bugis and Makassar**, where *pesse*/*pacce* and *siri'* may pull against each other.
- **Toba and Karo Batak**, whose speech norms are often contrasted with Javanese ones; check what Batak speakers themselves say.
- **Ambon and the Lease Islands**, where *pela* ties ask for feeling across Christian and Muslim villages.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, who move between family dialects and Indonesian norms of tact.
- **Deaf Indonesians** using BISINDO sign language, on being understood and misread by the hearing.
- Indonesians working in Gulf and East Asian households: comparison only, on reading employers across language lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Javanese and Balinese emotion ethnography; mixed for Sulawesi and Sumatra; thin for measured empathy scales in Indonesian samples.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian; Javanese speech levels (*ngoko*, *krama*) to read nuance; Balinese; Buginese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** empathy for 1965 perpetrators or victims remains politically charged; mental-illness narratives need consent.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities living in Indonesia.
