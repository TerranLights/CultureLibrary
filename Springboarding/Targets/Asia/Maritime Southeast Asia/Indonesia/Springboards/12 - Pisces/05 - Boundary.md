# Indonesia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese, Balinese, Sundanese (Baduy), Ambonese Malay and Central Maluku languages; Old Javanese for inscriptions; Arabic for modesty terms. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese** in court-city neighborhoods and in new estates, where open pavilions meet walled clusters.
- **Balinese** compounds and *desa adat*, with zoned space and village-held lines.
- **Baduy** and **Kampung Naga** (Sundanese communities with strict rules on visitors and change).
- **Central Maluku** villages with *sasi*, and **Ambon** neighborhoods divided after the 1999-2002 conflict.
- **Dayak** communities of West and Central Kalimantan, beside **Madurese** settlers, for group lines after Sambas (1999) and Sampit (2001).
- **Minangkabau** with collectively held land (*ulayat*) and **Toraja** with house-based kin groups.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, for walls, gates and closed shops after May 1998.
- **Bajau** sea people, whose sea-based life crosses many lines.
- **Timorese** at the Timor-Leste border.
- **Christians and Muslims** in mixed towns (Manado, Kupang, Medan), for touch, food and dress lines.
- Indonesian workers living in employers' homes abroad: comparison only, for privacy inside another's house.
