## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Toba Batak** with the *lapo tuak* (palm-wine stall) as a social place.
- **Minahasa** and **Maluku** and **NTT** Christians, where local liquors sit in daily and ceremonial life.
- **Balinese**, with *tajen*, *arak* and a tourist economy that sells escape to others.
- **Santri Muslims** of Java and Madura, for whom drink and gambling are sins, against **abangan** circles where they have been tolerated.
- **Pantura** (north coast of Java) working towns, with dangdut koplo and karaoke.
- **Jakarta and Surabaya youth**: games, *nongkrong* cafés, "healing" trips.
- **Minangkabau**, where *merantau* can be read as escape or obligation.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, for reading, games of chance and the opium-era past.
- **Dayak** and **Mentawai** communities, where rice wine or ritual drinking may sit apart from getting drunk, as each community describes it.
- Indonesians working abroad: comparison only, for migrant workers' leisure and debt from gambling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for drugs, music and gambling as public issues; mixed for everyday leisure; thin for ritual drinking in eastern islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian (and slang), Javanese, Batak, Minahasan Malay, Dutch, Old Javanese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug use carries severe penalties (drug law goes to Libra or Capricorn); alcohol is a religious dividing line; gambling is illegal, so data are partial.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Indonesia (category only).
