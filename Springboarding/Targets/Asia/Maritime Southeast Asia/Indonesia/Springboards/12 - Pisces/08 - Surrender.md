# Indonesia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Indonesia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian; Javanese (including Pegon-script pesantren Javanese); Arabic; Buginese and Makassarese; Minangkabau and Minahasan for rebellion memory; Dutch for colonial capitulations. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Javanese**, where *nrima* and *legawa* are named ideals, against **East Java's Arek** towns.
- **Santri Muslims**, for *tawakal* after effort, against **Pangestu** and **Subud** members for lay Javanese yielding.
- **Bugis and Makassar**, whose *siri'* (honor) and memory of the 1667 Bongaya treaty make yielding a different question.
- **Balinese**, whose *puputan* stories are a remembered refusal to yield.
- **Minangkabau** (PRRI) and **Minahasa** (Permesta), with memories of a lost rebellion.
- **Merapi slope villagers**, between evacuation orders and attachment to the mountain.
- **Sedulur Sikep (Samin)** communities, with a tradition of refusing without violence.
- **Chinese Indonesians**, on accepting or contesting post-1965 and post-1998 treatment.
- **Catholic and Protestant** communities in Flores, Minahasa and Batak lands, for resignation to God's will in Christian terms.
- Indonesians abroad: comparison only, for how surrender sayings travel into migrant hardship.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Javanese ethics and colonial-war history; mixed for end-of-life practice; thin for measured fatalism or acceptance.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese (literary and Pegon), Arabic, Dutch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surrender of separatist fighters is political; describing any people as "fatalistic" is a stereotype to record and test, never to adopt; end-of-life decisions are private.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities found in Indonesia (category only).
