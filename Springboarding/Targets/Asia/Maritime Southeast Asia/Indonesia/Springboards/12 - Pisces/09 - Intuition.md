## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Javanese farmers** using seasonal lore, against **urban Javanese professionals** who may consult a *primbon* privately.
- **Reformist Muslims** (Muhammadiyah, Persis), who reject omen-reading, against **traditionalist santri** who accept *firasat* and *istikharah*.
- **Balinese** households and priests, for day-choosing.
- **Toba Batak**, where the *datu*'s methods meet Protestant suspicion.
- **Bugis and Makassar** sailors, and **Bajau** sea people, for sea knowledge.
- **Merapi and Bromo** slope communities, for reading mountains.
- **Dayak** groups of Kalimantan with bird-omen traditions, as each shares them.
- **Chinese Indonesian** traders, with *hoki*, temple slips and geomancy consultants.
- **Minangkabau**, with the adat saying that unfolding nature is the teacher.
- Indonesians abroad: comparison only, for which omen habits survive in migrant life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Javanese and Balinese sign-reading and for sailors' knowledge; mixed for urban gut decisions; thin for measured intuition (no national studies known).
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian, Javanese, Balinese, Toba Batak, Buginese, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** almanac and divination traditions are recorded as practices people use, never as sources; *datu* knowledge may be restricted; politicians' spiritual advisers are a gossip-heavy topic.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities (category only, none named).
