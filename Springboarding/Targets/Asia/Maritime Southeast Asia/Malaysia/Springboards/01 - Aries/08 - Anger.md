# Malaysia: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi, plus Arabic loans in religious texts), Cantonese, Hokkien and Mandarin, Kadazandusun, Iban, Chewong, Malaysian English. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: healers who treat illness tied to strong emotion; check how anger figures.
- **Negeri Sembilan**: gendered talk of reason against passion, with anger on the passion side, to test.
- **Klang Valley commuters**: road rage and crowding.
- **Cantonese communities** (Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh, Sandakan): *da siu yan* and other grudge rites.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Rungus villages**: repair rites after an offense.
- **Iban and Bidayuh longhouses and villages**: insult, feud memory and cooling.
- **Chewong, Semai and Temiar**: restraint and the risks of open anger.
- **Men and women; youth**: who may show anger and where.
- **Communities touched by 1969**: inherited collective anger.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on amok (much of it colonial); mixed on everyday anger; thin in Borneo languages.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic religious vocabulary for *kitab* teaching; Cantonese for temple rites; Kadazandusun for *sogit* terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1969 and ethnic violence are restricted topics in public talk; amok is a stereotype that needs care; domestic-violence data are private.
- **Held for review:** anger rites and amok-related records of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
