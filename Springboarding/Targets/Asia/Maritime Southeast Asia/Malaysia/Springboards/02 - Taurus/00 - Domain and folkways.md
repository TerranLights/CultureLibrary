# Malaysia: Domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), English, Mandarin and Malaysian Chinese varieties, Iban, Kadazandusun, Bajau, Melanau and Orang Asli languages. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how it is held; with the folkways of food, dress and wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast Malays** (Selangor, Perak, Johor, Kedah) and **east-coast Malays** (Kelantan, Terengganu, Pahang): different market histories; Kelantan's women traders.
- **Negeri Sembilan** under *adat perpatih* against *adat temenggung* states: different lines of inheritance.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by dialect group and place: Penang Hokkien, Klang Valley Cantonese, Sabah Hakka, Sibu Foochow; and the **Peranakan** of Melaka and Penang, with their own material culture.
- **Sarawak peoples:** Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau (sago), Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan): different wealth objects and staples.
- **Sabah peoples:** Kadazandusun, Rungus, Murut, the Bajau of Kota Belud and the sea-going Bajau of Semporna.
- **Orang Asli** (Semai, Temiar, Batek, Jakun, Mah Meri): forest and sharing economies.
- **Kristang** Eurasians of Melaka and the **Siamese** of Kedah and Kelantan: small groups, own food ways.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for peninsular Malays and Chinese; mixed for Sabah and Sarawak; thin and outsider-written for many Orang Asli and Orang Ulu groups. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; this set of sheets is written for the core lands of pre-war Malaysia (the peninsula, Sabah, Sarawak, Labuan).
- **Languages needed:** Malay in Jawi as well as Rumi for the court texts and religious writing on wealth; Chinese (and dialect romanizations) for clan and trade records; Iban and Kadazandusun for heirloom and harvest vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wealth by ethnic group touches Bumiputera policy and the "3R" topics (race, religion, royalty); some heirlooms and harvest rites are kept by ritual specialists and shared only as the community allows.
- **Held for review:** domain-wide pointers on the material culture, food and dress of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
