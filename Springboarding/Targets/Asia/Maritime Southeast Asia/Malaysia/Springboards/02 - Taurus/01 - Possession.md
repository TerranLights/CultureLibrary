# Malaysia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), English, Mandarin with Hokkien and Cantonese, Iban, Kadazandusun and Rungus, Semai. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kedah and Perak courts against village households:** royal regalia and the everyday *barang* of commoners make two scales of possession.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu** against the **west-coast** cities: older craft goods (brass, songket, kites) still made and kept at home against mass-bought goods.
- **Iban** longhouse *bilik*-families, the **Bidayuh** of the Kuching hills and **Orang Ulu** aristocratic houses (Kayan, Kenyah): the same jars and beads may be held under different rules.
- **Rungus** and **Kadazandusun** households; **Murut** of the interior; **Bajau Laut** of Semporna, who keep a household on a boat.
- **Penan** and **Orang Asli** (Semai, Batek, Temiar): mobile peoples with few goods, a test case for what "keeping" means.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by dialect group, and the **Peranakan**, whose house contents were the family archive.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on objects (museums, colonial collecting, Borneo journals); thin on everyday belongings of ordinary urban households, and on what people carry or hoard today.
- **Languages needed:** Malay (with Jawi for older inventories and wills of goods), Iban and Rungus for heirloom names, Semai and Batek terms through the ethnographies, Chinese for funeral-trade catalogs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some heirlooms carry spirit guardians and are shown only by their keepers; human remains and grave goods in Borneo collections; claims on regalia touch royal sensitivities.
- **Held for review:** pointers on the household goods and funeral offerings of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
