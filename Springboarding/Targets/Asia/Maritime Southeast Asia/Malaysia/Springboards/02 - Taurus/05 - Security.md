## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Civil servants with pensions** against **small traders**, **gig riders** and **plantation workers** without them.
- **Kedah and Perlis rice farmers**; **east-coast fishers** of Kelantan, Terengganu and Pahang in the monsoon months.
- **FELDA settlers**, whose security was planned by the state, against **urban low-income households** in Klang Valley flats.
- **Sabah's east coast:** Bajau, Suluk and Tidong communities under a security command; stateless families with no safety net.
- **Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu** longhouse households against kin who moved to Kuching, Miri and Bintulu.
- **Orang Asli** villages, often far from aid during floods.
- **Malaysian Chinese** family firms, where security may rest on kin and associations rather than the state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal savings, pensions and floods (agencies publish); mixed for informal protection (charms, kin, associations); thin for stateless people and remote Orang Asli.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for agency data and Jawi for charm texts; Chinese for association records; Iban and Kadazandusun for rice-store words; Bajau and Suluk speech for east-coast testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Lahad Datu events and Sabah's east coast are security subjects; ASB and other Bumiputera-only schemes touch the "3R" topics; protective charms may be called unorthodox by religious authorities, so practitioners may not speak openly. Laws on social insurance go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** pointers on savings, insurance and mutual aid among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the latest BNM demand-side survey and note every emergency-funds measure.
2. Read KRI's latest *State of Households* for income bands and state breakdowns.
3. Search Malay press for *pengeluaran KWSP i-Sinar* and *simpanan persaraan tidak cukup* to find how workers describe the drawdowns.
4. Read Scott on Kedah, then the MADA reports, for village and state views of rice security.
5. Search Sabah papers (*Daily Express*, *Utusan Borneo*) for ESSCOM curfew stories told by residents.
