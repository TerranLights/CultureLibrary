## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Klang Valley** and **Penang** urban elites against **Kelantan**, **Kedah** and **Sabah**, whose place in the income tiers is to be measured.
- **Malay** wealth built through the state (FELDA, GLCs, ASB) against **Malaysian Chinese** family firms; **Peranakan** merchant fortunes of the nineteenth century.
- **Kelantanese** market women, a separate lead on who holds trading wealth in the home.
- **Iban** weavers and warriors, with forms of prestige beyond cash to study; **Kelabit** and **Kayan** feast and rank customs.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Bajau**: jars, buffalo and horses as candidate wealth objects in older custom.
- **Orang Asli** collectors of forest products; **Sabah** and **Sarawak** oil-producing districts and the debate over oil royalties.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income, policy and the very rich; thin for household wealth (assets, gold, land) and for how Borneo and Orang Asli peoples reckon wealth today.
- **Languages needed:** Malay (Jawi for *zakat* treatises and old coin legends), Chinese for business histories and temple rites, Iban and Kelabit for prestige terms, Kadazandusun for bride-wealth vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wealth gaps between groups are a "3R" subject; 1MDB remains a court matter; Datuk titles come from rulers, so criticism is handled with care. Inheritance law (*faraid*) goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** pointers on merchant fortunes, moneylending and trading wealth among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
