## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Negeri Sembilan** *adat* keepers against reformers, where holding to custom is argued openly.
- **Kelantan** and **Terengganu**, with their remembered risings and periods of opposition rule, against **west-coast** states.
- **Sabah** and **Sarawak**, whose claims of autonomy under the 1963 agreement are a collective firmness to study, without assuming its form.
- **Iban** (Rentap's people) and **Bidayuh**; **Penan** and **Kayan** in dam and logging disputes.
- **Temiar**, **Semai** and **Jakun** Orang Asli communities in land standoffs.
- **Malaysian Chinese** educationists; **Kristang** of Melaka; **Peranakan** families keeping old customs.
- Elders against the young: who is called *degil*, and who *teguh pendirian*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on resistance history and protest; mixed on everyday stubbornness in families; thin on Borneo and Orang Asli words for obstinacy.
- **Languages needed:** Malay (Jawi for colonial-era vernacular press on the risings), Chinese for education-movement records, Iban and Temiar for protest testimony, Kristang for Melaka voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some risings are disputed as rebellion or patriotism; Sabah and Sarawak autonomy is political; blockades are ongoing legal matters, and the law itself goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** pointers on plantation strikes and labor holdouts among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
