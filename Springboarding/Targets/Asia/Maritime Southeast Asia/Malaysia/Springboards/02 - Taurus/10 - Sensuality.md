*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan** and **Terengganu** (Mak Yong, *budu*, rich rice dishes) against **Johor** (zapin, ghazal) and the Klang Valley.
- **Muslim** and **non-Muslim** Malaysians: how far rules on drink, pork, dress and mixed dancing split sensory life is a lead.
- **Peranakan** of Melaka and Penang (Nyonya cooking, *dondang sayang* verse); **Kristang** with their own dances and food.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by dialect cuisine (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Hainanese, Foochow), and coffee-shop culture.
- **Iban**, **Bidayuh** and **Orang Ulu** (the *sape* lute); **Melanau** of Mukah; **Kadazandusun**, **Murut** and **Bajau** of Sabah.
- **Orang Asli:** Temiar and Semai (song, flowers, scent), Mah Meri (carving and mask).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, dance and healing (ethnography, heritage lists, tourism); mixed for touch, scent and comfort in daily life; sexual pleasure is thinly and cautiously documented (sex itself is Scorpio's).
- **Languages needed:** Malay (Jawi for the *kitab tib* and court romances), Kelantan Malay for Mak Yong texts, Chinese for food heritage, Iban, Kadazandusun and Temiar for festival and song terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state religious rules on performance, dress and alcohol vary by state and are contested; some Temiar songs and Mak Yong rites are spirit-linked and shared only with leave; sexuality and gender expression are restricted subjects.
- **Held for review:** pointers on cuisine, scent, music and dance traditions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
