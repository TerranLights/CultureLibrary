*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Bahasa Melayu, in Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin and the Malaysian Chinese varieties (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Foochow, Hainanese), Iban, Bidayuh, Kadazandusun, Sama-Bajau, Murut, Melanau, Orang Ulu and Orang Asli languages. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech ways, learning ways, neighbors and short journeys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast Malays** (Kedah-Perlis-Penang northern speech; Perak; Selangor; Johor): separate dialects and urban against village life.
- **Negeri Sembilan's matrilineal communities** (*adat perpatih*): a different kin and visiting order.
- **East-coast Malays of Kelantan and Terengganu:** strong dialects, *pondok* schooling, performance genres of talk; Patani across the border as comparison only.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by dialect group and by schooling stream (Chinese-, English- or Malay-educated); **Peranakan Chinese** of Malacca, Penang and Kelantan: mixed languages at home.
- **Sarawak:** Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau, Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan), Sarawak Malays: longhouse, river and town settings differ.
- **Sabah:** Kadazandusun, Rungus, Murut, west- and east-coast Sama-Bajau, Hakka-speaking Sabah Chinese.
- **Orang Asli** (Batek, Jahai, Semai, Temiar, Temuan, Jakun, Mah Meri): very different learning and speaking customs.
- **Kristang** Eurasians of Malacca, Siamese Malaysians of Kedah and Kelantan, and the Deaf community: small, distinct speech communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for peninsular Malays and Malaysian Chinese; mixed for Sabah and Sarawak; thin and outsider-heavy for Orang Asli and small Borneo peoples.
- **Lands:** no change to the pre-war lands is recorded and the exact territory is still to be settled; these sheets are written for the peninsula, Sabah and Sarawak.
- **Languages needed:** Malay, with Jawi for manuscripts and the older press; simplified Chinese for the Chinese press; English for colonial and academic work; Iban and Kadazandusun for community publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race, religion and royalty are touchy public topics; language-of-instruction and Jawi-in-schools debates are politically charged; some Borneo and Orang Asli ritual chants are restricted to their keepers.
- **Held for review:** domain material on speech, schooling and neighbors among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
