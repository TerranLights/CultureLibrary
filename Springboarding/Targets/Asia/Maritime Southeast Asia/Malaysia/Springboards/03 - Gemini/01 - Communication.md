# Malaysia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin, Hokkien, Cantonese, Iban, Kadazandusun, Bidayuh, Temiar and Semai. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East-coast Malays (Kelantan, Terengganu)** against west-coast and northern speakers: dialects, verse duels and different norms of directness.
- **Negeri Sembilan's *adat perpatih* communities:** the customary sayings (*perbilangan*) used in speech at gatherings.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by variety (Hokkien Penang, Cantonese Klang Valley and Ipoh, Hakka Sabah, Foochow Sibu) and by school stream: language choice changes who talks to whom.
- **Peranakan Chinese** of Malacca, Penang and Kelantan: mixed-language homes.
- **Iban, Bidayuh** (several mutually unintelligible varieties), **Melanau** and **Orang Ulu** in Sarawak; **Kadazandusun, Rungus, Murut** and **Sama-Bajau** in Sabah: ritual and everyday registers differ.
- **Orang Asli** (Temiar, Semai, Batek, Jahai): talk among very small, mobile groups.
- **Deaf Malaysians;** urban young speakers of Manglish against older rural speakers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Malay politeness and Malaysian English; mixed on Chinese varieties (often studied for language shift, less for talk itself); thin on Borneo and Orang Asli everyday conversation.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Jawi for royal letters and proverbs; Kelantanese dialect for *dikir barat*; Penang Hokkien and Cantonese for recorded talk; Iban and Kadazandusun for ritual speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ritual chants (*rinait*, Iban *pengap*) belong to their keepers; studies that rank peoples on "directness" invite stereotype, so record variation by setting.
- **Held for review:** communication studies centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. In the DBP PRPM portal, search "berlapik", "kiasan" and "menegur"; then search the proverb entries for *kata* and *mulut*.
2. In the British Library's digitized Malay manuscripts, look for Gallop's letter catalog and its Kedah and Johor items.
3. In Malaysian university repositories (UM, UKM, UPM), search "kesantunan bahasa" plus "perbualan" (conversation).
4. Search RTM and online video for "dikir barat tukang karut" and "berbalas pantun peringkat kebangsaan".
5. Contact the Malaysian Federation of the Deaf for publications on BIM and interpreting.
