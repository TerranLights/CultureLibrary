## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Klang Valley and Penang urban readers** against rural Kedah, Pahang and Johor villages: news sources and connectivity differ.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu:** religious talks (*ceramah*) and party channels as news routes beside the press.
- **Malaysian Chinese readers** of Chinese dailies against English- and Malay-press readers.
- **Older Jawi readers** against Rumi-only younger readers: who can read the older record.
- **Sarawak** (Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau, Orang Ulu longhouses upriver) and **Sabah** (Kadazandusun, Murut interior, Bajau coast): coverage gaps and their own presses.
- **Stateless Sama-Bajau and undocumented people in Sabah:** missing from official data.
- **Orang Asli:** information about them mostly held by others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national surveys and press history; mixed for Borneo media; thin for word-of-mouth news in villages and longhouses.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for the press before the 1960s; Chinese for a whole parallel press; Iban and Kadazandusun for local radio and pages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reporting on race, religion and royalty is touchy; press licensing and secrecy laws belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here; Orang Asli and Penan place knowledge is theirs to share.
- **Held for review:** news media serving South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
