## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East-coast villages with *pondok* and *tahfiz* traditions** against west-coast towns with national schools and tuition: different first teachers.
- **Negeri Sembilan's *adat perpatih* families:** customary sayings taught alongside religious study.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by school stream (Chinese primary, independent secondary, national schools) and dialect group.
- **Peranakan Chinese** of Malacca and Penang: a long history of mission and English schools.
- **Iban** (*bejalai*, the journey abroad for experience, as learning), **Kelabit** and **Penan** (very different access to schools), **Bidayuh**, **Melanau** in Sarawak.
- **Kadazandusun** (strong mission-school history), **Murut**, **Sama-Bajau** and stateless **Bajau Laut** children in Sabah.
- **Orang Asli:** Batek, Semai, Temiar, Jakun; learning in forest camps against boarding schools.
- **Siamese Malaysians** of Kedah and Kelantan: temple-based learning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal schooling and religious education; mixed for crafts and martial arts (mostly practitioners' own writing); thin for everyday learning in Borneo and Orang Asli families.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for *pondok* and *kitab* materials; Chinese for the independent-school movement; Iban and Kadazandusun for classroom-language studies; Semai and Batek terms from ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *silat* and ritual weaving knowledge is graded and partly secret; Orang Asli and stateless children's schooling is a live grievance; religious-school curricula are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** learning and apprenticeship among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
