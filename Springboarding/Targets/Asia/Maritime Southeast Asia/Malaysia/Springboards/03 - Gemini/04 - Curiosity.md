# Malaysia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay villages** in Kedah, Perak and Pahang against urban Klang Valley families: *pantang larang* and children's questions treated differently.
- **East-coast Malays (Kelantan, Terengganu):** healing and performance genres such as *Main Puteri* as a separate record of approaches to the unseen.
- **Malaysian Chinese** (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka speakers): the *kaypoh* label and the line between concern and prying.
- **Peranakan Chinese:** heritage curiosity and household secrets.
- **Iban and Bidayuh:** omen-reading and longhouse openness to visitors.
- **Kadazandusun and Dusun communities near Kinabalu:** sacred places and visitors' curiosity.
- **Kelabit and other Orang Ulu highlanders:** contact with explorers, missionaries and scientists.
- **Sama-Bajau of the Semporna coast:** visitors' gaze on sea villages.
- **Orang Asli** (Chewong, Semai, Batek): rules on asking, looking and naming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science-engagement surveys and museums; mixed for folk tales; thin for everyday nosiness and its limits outside ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for proverbs and *pantang larang*; Hokkien loanwords for urban nosiness talk; Iban for omen terms; Kadazandusun for Kinabalu responses; Chewong terms through Howell.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred sites (Kinabalu, Niah) and Orang Asli and Iban ritual knowledge; supernatural topics can bring religious objections.
- **Held for review:** curiosity, heritage and genealogy work about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
