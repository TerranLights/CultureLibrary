## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malays** across the peninsula: how custom and religion are paired, region by region; Negeri Sembilan's matrilineal custom against the *temenggung* custom elsewhere.
- **Kelantan Malays and Kelantan Peranakan Chinese:** border-zone mixing with Siamese and Chinese neighbors; Patani as comparison only.
- **Malaysian Chinese:** dialect against Mandarin; Chinese-educated against English-educated (insult labels described, not repeated).
- **Peranakan Chinese** and **Kristang Eurasians:** heritages built from two or more sources.
- **New Muslims (*mualaf*)** from Chinese, Iban, Kadazandusun and Orang Asli families: belonging to two communities.
- **Sabah's mixed-heritage people** (Sino-Kadazan and others) and **Sama-Bajau** between land and sea life.
- **Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu:** church and longhouse custom side by side.
- **Borneo-born people living on the peninsula:** two regional identities.
- **Orang Asli:** forest and town lives, and pressure from outside religions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on ethnic categories and code-switching; mixed on paired worldviews (mostly in ethnographies); thin on mixed-heritage people outside Sabah.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for the Tuah-Jebat debate and *adat* sayings; Mandarin and Hokkien for language-choice studies; Kristang for community material; Iban and Kadazandusun for origin and afterlife narratives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious conversion and double religious belonging are highly sensitive; Bumiputera status is political; Borneo autonomy talk is charged; Iban rites for the dead may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** mixed-heritage and two-language identities involving South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
