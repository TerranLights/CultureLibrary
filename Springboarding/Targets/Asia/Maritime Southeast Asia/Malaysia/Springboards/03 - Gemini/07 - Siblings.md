# Malaysia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi, with Kelantan and Perak usage), Mandarin, Hokkien and Cantonese, Iban, Bidayuh, Kadazandusun, Temiar. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malays of Kedah, Perak and the west coast:** birth-order naming sets and the eldest's duties differ by state.
- **Negeri Sembilan's matrilineal families:** the mother's brother and the brother-sister tie, to be examined in their own setting.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu Malays:** dialect kin terms and midwife customs at birth.
- **Malaysian Chinese** by dialect group: generation names, the eldest son and sisters' shares.
- **Peranakan Chinese:** mixed Malay and Chinese kin terms at home.
- **Iban** (bilek inheritance), **Bidayuh**, **Melanau** and **Orang Ulu** households in Sarawak.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut** and **Sama-Bajau** households in Sabah; Bajau Laut boat families.
- **Orang Asli** (Temiar, Semai, Batek): their own sibling terms and duties.
- **Muslim families with milk siblings or adopted children:** sibling status set by religious rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Malay kinship ethnography; mixed on Chinese sibling roles; thin on Sabah and Orang Asli sibling ties beyond older ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Malay state usages for birth-order names; Hokkien and Cantonese for Chinese kin terms; Iban, Bidayuh and Kadazandusun for Borneo terms; Temiar through Benjamin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inheritance shares among siblings fall under religious and customary law (see Libra and Capricorn); adoption and milk kinship touch religious rulings; stateless siblings in Sabah face hard choices.
- **Held for review:** sibling and kin-term material from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
