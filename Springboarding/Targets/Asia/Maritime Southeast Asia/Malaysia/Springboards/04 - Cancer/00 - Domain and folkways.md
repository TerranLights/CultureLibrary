# Malaysia: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin and the southern Chinese varieties (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Foochow), Iban, Kadazandusun, Bidayuh, Sama-Bajau, Orang Asli languages. **Concept in one line:** home and family (house, household, parents, roots, nurture); the domain question is what home is, and who counts as family.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast Malay states vs. Kelantan and Terengganu:** different house types, dialects and household economies on the east coast.
- **Negeri Sembilan's *adat perpatih* communities:** matrilineal clans make "family" a different unit.
- **Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau and Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan):** longhouse and non-longhouse ways of housing a family in Sarawak.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut, Rungus and Bajau of Sabah:** village, longhouse and sea-dwelling households.
- **Orang Asli peoples (Semai, Temiar, Batek, Jakun, Temuan, Mah Meri):** forest and resettled households.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect group, Peranakan Chinese, Kristang Eurasians of Melaka, Siamese of Kedah and Kelantan:** separate kin systems and home rituals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census, household surveys and Malay and Iban ethnography; mixed for Sabah's smaller peoples; thin for several Orang Asli groups. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of present Malaysia (the peninsular states, Sabah and Sarawak).
- **Languages needed:** Malay (Rumi, plus Jawi for manuscripts and religious print), Chinese (simplified and traditional script, dialect vocabularies), Iban and Kadazandusun for community publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnicity and religion are politically charged in household data; Orang Asli and Borneo peoples' ritual knowledge of house and lineage may be restricted to members.
- **Held for review:** pointers to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands and their household and kin systems, set aside under the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. OpenDOSM: search "isi rumah" and "living quarters" for the 2020 census household tables by state.

4. Library catalog: Carsten, Freeman, Peletz and Sather together, to set out the four household systems side by side.
