# Malaysia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi; Sabah and Sarawak Malay), Mandarin and the Chinese varieties, Iban, Kadazandusun, Malaysian English. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Peninsular Malays by state (Kelantanese, Terengganu, Kedahan, Johorean):** regional loyalty within one people, marked by dialect.
- **Sabahans and Sarawakians against the peninsula:** the "Semenanjung" contrast shapes Borneo belonging.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect, schooling (Chinese-medium or English-medium) and generation; Peranakan Chinese:** different answers to the "root" question.
- **Iban, Bidayuh, Kadazandusun, Murut:** longhouse and village membership against national membership.
- **Orang Asli peoples (Semai, Temuan, Jakun, Temiar):** belonging to customary land.
- **Bajau Laut and other stateless Sabah residents; Siamese and Kristang minorities:** belonging at the edges of official categories. Malaysians abroad count as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national unity and ethnic identity; mixed on felt belonging measured directly; thin on stateless people's own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for state unity material; Chinese for native-place associations and Mahua fiction; Iban and Kadazandusun for festival and longhouse membership.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Bumiputera status, citizenship in Sabah and the word *pendatang* are explosive subjects; citizenship and land law go to Libra. Present every side, never one voice.
- **Held for review:** belonging and associations of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
