# Malaysia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi; east-coast dialects for flood vocabulary), Mandarin and Hakka, Iban, Dusun varieties of Sabah, Malaysian English. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan, Terengganu and Pahang flood plains:** repeated monsoon evacuations; whether shelter has become a seasonal routine there is a question to test.
- **Kuala Lumpur and Penang urban poor:** flats, squatter settlements and rough sleepers.
- **Iban and other Sarawak farmers:** the field hut as second shelter.
- **Orang Seletar and Bajau Laut:** sea and shore shelters under pressure from development.
- **Chinese mining and plantation histories (Hakka, Cantonese):** communal lodging of migrant men.
- **Religious-school communities (*pondok*), mosques and churches:** places that lodge students and strangers under religious auspices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on floods and evacuation; mixed on homelessness (counts vary by agency); thin on Orang Seletar and on *langkau* life today.
- **Languages needed:** Malay disaster and welfare vocabulary; Chinese for mining-era lodging records; Iban for farm-season terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugees and undocumented people in shelters are a sensitive subject; police raids on rough sleepers and their removal are contested. Housing and refugee law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** shelter and lodging run by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
