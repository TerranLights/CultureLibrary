# Malaysia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi, including the Arabic-derived religious vocabulary), Mandarin, Malaysian Hokkien and Cantonese, Kadazandusun, Iban, English. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sabah and Sarawak vs. the Peninsula:** regional pride is tied to how the federation was formed.
- **Kelantan:** dialect, cuisine and football fandom as a self-aware state identity.
- **Johor:** a palace-led state identity.
- **Chinese-educated Malaysian Chinese:** pride organized around schools and language.
- **Peranakan Chinese:** pride in a creole heritage, distinct from both Malay and newer Chinese communities.
- **Kadazandusun, Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu:** language revival and festival pride differ by people.
- **Orang Asli peoples (Semai, Temuan, Jakun and others):** pride movements built through Indigenous networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national and ethnic pride in the press and surveys; mixed for personal pride and humility in daily life; thin for Orang Asli.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic-derived terms for the sin vocabulary; Mandarin for the education movement; Kadazandusun for language campaigns.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic pride touches politics and the "3R" (race, religion, royalty) limits; regional autonomy talk in Borneo is politically charged; LGBTQ pride events have been banned (the 2011 *Seksualiti Merdeka* case), and the legal side belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
