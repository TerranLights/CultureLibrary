# Malaysia: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi; colloquial and folk-belief vocabulary), Mandarin and Cantonese, Penang Hokkien, Sama-Bajau, Penan and Semai (through ethnographies), English. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sabah's stateless and undocumented sea peoples** vs. **settled Bajau of Kota Belud:** visibility to the state differs sharply within one people.
- **Penan, Kelabit and other Orang Ulu:** interior peoples seen chiefly through outside campaigns.
- **Semai, Temuan and other Orang Asli near cities:** seen by planners and tourists more than by the public.
- **Sino-Kadazan and other mixed-descent Sabahans:** visibility in forms and communities.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu vs. the west coast:** rules on who may perform in public differ.
- **Malaysian Chinese in Penang, Kuala Lumpur and Kuching:** signage, festivals and language channels.
- **Older people in villages vs. cities:** who notices whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census categories and viral media; mixed for folk belief; thin and sensitive for stateless people.
- **Languages needed:** colloquial Malay for slights and slang; Mandarin and Cantonese for limelight words; Sama-Bajau and Penan through ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stateless and undocumented people face legal risk (the legal side goes to Libra), so research must not expose individuals; Penan campaigns are politically charged; viral shaming of private people raises privacy concerns.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
