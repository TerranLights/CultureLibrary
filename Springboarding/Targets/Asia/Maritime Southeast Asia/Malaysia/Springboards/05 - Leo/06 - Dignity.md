# Malaysia: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi; Islamic ethical vocabulary), Mandarin and Cantonese, Iban, Rungus and Kadazandusun, English. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu *pondok* communities** vs. **urban west-coast professionals:** different teaching on bearing and worth.
- **Iban and Rungus longhouses:** standing kept through longhouse conduct rules.
- **Orang Asli peoples (Semai, Temuan, Jakun):** dignity tied to treatment by the state.
- **Malaysian Chinese clan and benevolent societies:** dignity for the dead poor.
- **The old in villages vs. in cities:** home care, homes and family expectations.
- **Disabled Malaysians:** naming and access as questions of dignity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious teaching and NGO reports; mixed for elder care; thin for Borneo-specific dignity norms outside ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi and Arabic-Malay terms for *adab* teaching; Mandarin for benevolent-society records; Iban and Rungus for longhouse conduct terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** detention and migrant treatment are politically sensitive; Orang Asli land disputes touch the courts (Libra); burial rites are religious and private.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
