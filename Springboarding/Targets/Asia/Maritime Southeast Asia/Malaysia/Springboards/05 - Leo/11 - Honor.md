# Malaysia: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi; classical court Malay), Mandarin, Hokkien and Cantonese, Iban, Kadazandusun, Semai (through ethnography), English. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay court tradition (Melaka, Johor, Perak) vs. Kelantan and Terengganu:** loyalty to ruler versus religious scholars as moral arbiters.
- **Silat lineages:** honor codes differ by school.
- **Iban, Kayan and Kenyah:** warrior renown and its modern forms.
- **Kadazandusun and Murut:** headhunting memory as heritage.
- **Malaysian Chinese:** lineage honor in clan halls, and brotherhood oaths.
- **Semai and other Orang Asli:** non-retaliation norms.
- **Women in factory towns vs. villages:** how reputation is watched.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Hang Tuah tradition and martial arts; mixed for Borneo warrior honor; thin for current family-honor practices.
- **Languages needed:** classical Malay for the epic; Cantonese and Hokkien for brotherhood oaths; Iban for warrior terms; Kadazandusun for legends.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *amuk* is an old stereotype: research it as a colonial and clinical category, never as a trait; secret societies shade into present-day gangs; family honor and women's conduct touch religious law (Libra and Capricorn) and must be researched in the words of those who live it.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
