*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu** (*Mak Yong*, *rebana ubi*, storytellers) vs. **Klang Valley concert and club life:** public joy under different limits.
- **Perlis and Kedah:** northern storytelling (*Awang Batil*) as a separate line.
- **Iban and Bidayuh:** Gawai joy in longhouses and towns.
- **Kadazandusun and Sama-Bajau:** harvest and wedding joy in Sabah's west and east coasts.
- **Malaysian Chinese:** New Year songs, lion dance and family reunion.
- **Temiar and Semai:** joy inside ritual gatherings.
- **Pious and secular registers:** *bahagia* as religious happiness against everyday *seronok*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, songs and well-being indexes; mixed for Borneo; thin for private, quiet joy and for the joyless (depression is under-reported).
- **Languages needed:** Kelantan and Perlis Malay for storytellers; Iban and Kadazandusun for festival blessings; Cantonese and Hokkien for festive slang; Temiar and Semai through ethnographic glosses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some entertainments are restricted in some states; ritual joy (*Magavau*, *sewang*) may be closed to outsiders; comedy that touches race or religion draws complaints and police reports.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
