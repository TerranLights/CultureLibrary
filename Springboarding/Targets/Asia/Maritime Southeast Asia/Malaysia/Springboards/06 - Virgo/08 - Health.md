## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East-coast Malays (Kelantan, Terengganu):** *main peteri* and village midwifery as recorded living traditions.
- **West-coast urban Malays:** Islamic healing centers and hospital use side by side.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect:** Cantonese "heaty" talk, confinement customs, Chinese-medicine use.
- **Peranakan households:** mixed Malay and Chinese postpartum and food rules (*check*).
- **Iban and Bidayuh:** *manang* healing against Christian and biomedical care.
- **Kadazandusun and Rungus:** *bobohizan* rites and Christian or Muslim converts' use of them.
- **Melanau:** a coastal healing tradition little documented.
- **Temiar, Semai and other Orang Asli:** song healing; access to clinics.
- **Bajau Laut:** stateless people's access to care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for biomedical surveys and Malay folk medicine; mixed for Bornean healing; thin for Melanau and Bajau Laut.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for *Kitab Tib*; Cantonese for confinement and herbal talk; Iban and Kadazandusun ritual language for chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' chants and spells are often restricted; mental illness, HIV and suicide carry stigma; religious authorities dispute some healing practices; health law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** pointers on healing systems and medical practice among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the latest NHMS report and its traditional-medicine module.

4. Search MOH's T&CM pages for the integrated hospital units and services list.
5. Search Sabah and Sarawak journals for "bobohizan" and "manang" with "healing".
