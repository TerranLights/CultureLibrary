*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin and the southern Chinese varieties (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Foochow), Iban, Kadazandusun, Bidayuh, Bajau, the Orang Asli languages. **Concept in one line:** partnership, marriage, contracts, open enemies, balance and justice; the domain question is what a fair bargain and a good partnership are.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast Malay states versus Kelantan and Terengganu:** marriage, divorce and syariah practice differ by state enactment and local custom.
- **Negeri Sembilan's *adat perpatih* versus *adat temenggung* elsewhere:** two inheritance and marriage orders among Malays.
- **Sarawak (Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau, Orang Ulu):** written native codes and longhouse courts.
- **Sabah (Kadazandusun, Murut, Bajau, Rungus):** separate native courts; the 1963 entry terms.
- **Orang Asli (Semai, Temiar, Batek, Temuan, Jakun):** consensus hearings and egalitarian camps.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect group, and the Peranakan of Malacca and Penang:** different wedding and partnership forms.
- **Kristang (Malacca Portuguese-Eurasians) and Siamese of Kedah and Kelantan:** small communities with their own marriage customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, marriage statistics and Malay classics; mixed for Borneo, where written codes exist but local practice needs fieldwork; thin for most Orang Asli groups beyond a few ethnographies. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for Malaysia's present lands.
- **Languages needed:** Malay in both scripts for classical and syariah sources; Iban and Kadazandusun for native-court material; Chinese for clan papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Article 153 and the inter-community bargain fall under the Sedition Act; apostasy and interfaith marriage are live questions in the courts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own marriage and family-law material, set aside for the author.
