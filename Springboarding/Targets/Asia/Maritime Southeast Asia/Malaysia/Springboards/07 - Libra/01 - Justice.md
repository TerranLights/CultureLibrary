## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu against the west-coast states:** the hudud enactments and their politics sit in the east.
- **Sarawak's Iban and Orang Ulu longhouses:** fines and headman hearings under written native law, and their land disputes with the state.
- **Sabah's natives against migrant and stateless people:** Bajau Laut and Suluk communities face a different justice system in practice.
- **Orang Asli of Perak, Pahang and Kelantan:** land claims and the Department of Orang Asli Development's authority.
- **Malaysian Chinese, especially the former New Villages:** historical grievances from the Emergency resettlement.
- **Urban middle class against rural poor:** who can reach a lawyer, and who settles locally.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, case law and political cases; mixed for customary justice in Borneo; thin for Orang Asli internal practice.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Arabic syariah vocabulary for Islamic courts; Iban for longhouse records; Chinese for the Batang Kali and New Village material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hudud, the death penalty and royal-related cases are politically charged; criticism of rulers falls under the Sedition Act; sodomy prosecutions of politicians draw strong partisan readings.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (their cases, redress campaigns and temple oaths), set aside here.
