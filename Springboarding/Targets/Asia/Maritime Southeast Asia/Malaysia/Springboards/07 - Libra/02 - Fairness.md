# Malaysia: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin, Cantonese, Hokkien, Kadazandusun, Iban, the Batek and other Orang Asli languages. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Peninsular Malays against Sabah and Sarawak natives:** both are bumiputera by law, with different experiences of reserved shares.
- **Orang Asli:** counted as bumiputera, yet outside most programs; their own sharing rules differ again.
- **Malaysian Chinese and Peranakan traders:** merit and examination debates, family-firm inheritance.
- **Kedah and Perlis rice farmers against Kelantan and Terengganu fishers:** two older share systems.
- **FELDA settlers:** land allotted on equal terms, a planned test of fair shares.
- **Kristang of Malacca:** a small community's place in the shares debate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on policy, quotas, zakat and income gaps; mixed on everyday splitting and turns, which need fieldwork; thin for Borneo markets.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for policy, zakat and faraid; Chinese for the Dong Zong archive and Chinese-press debate; Kadazandusun and Bajau for market life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** questioning Article 153 can be charged under the Sedition Act; election-boundary and quota debates are heated, so record each side in its own words.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose own fairness grievances in education and estate labor are kept apart.
