*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muslim couples under state syariah law against non-Muslim couples under civil law:** different terms, courts and exits.
- **Negeri Sembilan's matrilineal Malays:** a husband marries into his wife's clan land.
- **Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu:** the *bilek* or longhouse apartment and how a couple joins it.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Bajau:** marriage payments and native-court enforcement.
- **Malaysian Chinese dialect groups and the Peranakan:** betrothal gifts, clan halls, family firms.
- **Orang Asli (Chewong, Temiar, Semai):** couples in small, mobile settlements.
- **Interfaith and interethnic couples:** conversion rules shape which law applies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, finance and politics; mixed for couples' inner life; thin for Orang Asli and Murut marriage beyond older ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Arabic finance terms for contracts; Iban and Kadazandusun for native-court files; Cantonese and Hakka for clan and kongsi records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion and child-custody disputes between a convert and a non-Muslim spouse are fraught court cases; same-sex partnership is unrecognized and criminalized, so handle sources with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, including their business and marriage partnerships, left for the author's ruling.
