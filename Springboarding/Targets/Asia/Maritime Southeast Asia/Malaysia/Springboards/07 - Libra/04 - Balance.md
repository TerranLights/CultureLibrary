*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Terengganu and Kelantan healers against urban clinics:** the frame of hot, cold and wind is strongest in specialist hands.
- **Malaysian Chinese households:** confinement month, cooling teas and temple fasts.
- **Sabah and Sarawak against the peninsula:** written state powers that tilt the federal balance.
- **Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Penan):** dams and resettlement as land out of balance.
- **Iban weavers:** balance in pattern and in the women's ritual role.
- **Temiar, Semai and other Orang Asli:** healing by song and spirit guides.
- **Peranakan Chinese of Malacca and Penang:** household food rules to set beside both Malay and Chinese practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medical anthropology and constitutional history; mixed for work-life data; thin for Kadazandusun and Orang Ulu teachings in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for *kitab tib* and healers; Cantonese for the "heaty" vocabulary; Kadazandusun for Kaamatan liturgy; Kayan and Kenyah for river communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kaamatan and healing rites may hold knowledge the priestesses keep; royal powers are a sensitive subject under the Sedition Act; dam disputes involve ongoing land claims.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose own healing systems of bodily balance are set aside.
