# Malaysia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Hokkien, Cantonese and Mandarin, Iban, Kelabit, Kadazandusun, Mah Meri. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu villages against suburban Klang Valley:** where feast labor and death funds are pooled by neighbors, and where caterers may have taken over.
- **Malaysian Chinese dialect groups:** red-packet and condolence-money customs differ among Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka and Foochow families.
- **Iban and Bidayuh longhouses:** farm labor counted in returned days.
- **Kelabit, Lun Bawang and other Orang Ulu:** feasts that build standing.
- **Kadazandusun and Murut villages in Sabah's interior:** harvest help and Kaamatan exchanges.
- **Mah Meri, Temuan and other Orang Asli:** reciprocity with spirits and ancestors.
- **Peranakan families of Malacca:** festival visiting to set against both Malay and Chinese forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for proverbs, festivals and press; mixed for gift ledgers, which sit in private homes; thin for Borneo labor exchange beyond older articles.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for *budi* literature; Hokkien and Cantonese for ledgers and funerals; Iban, Kelabit and Kadazandusun for village exchange; Mah Meri for ancestral rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ancestral offerings may involve knowledge the community keeps; *dedak* and patronage cases may be before the courts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, including their festival visits and gift customs, held back for now.
