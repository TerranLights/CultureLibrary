# Malaysia: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Manglish, Cantonese, Mandarin and Hokkien, Iban, Temiar, Kadazandusun. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay families in Kelantan and Terengganu against Klang Valley professionals:** who decides a marriage or a job, and whether guidance prayer or elders are consulted.
- **Malaysian Chinese households:** almanac dates and temple sticks for weddings, moves and openings.
- **Iban and other Dayak longhouses:** omens and dreams before planting and travel.
- **Negeri Sembilan's matrilineal clans:** consensus by council.
- **Temiar and Semai:** dream guidance, read through the critiques.
- **Kadazandusun and Rungus:** whether ritual specialists set planting and wedding dates.
- **Young urban Malaysians of all communities:** Manglish talk of choice overload.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional events and folk tales; mixed for divination practice, which is partly private; thin and distorted for Orang Asli dream practice.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Jawi for kitab and proverbs; Cantonese and Hokkien for temple practice; Iban for augury; Temiar through careful translation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious rulings on *bomoh* practice may make practitioners guarded with researchers; palace decisions in a hung parliament are a sensitive topic.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and their own ways of fixing auspicious dates, set aside.
