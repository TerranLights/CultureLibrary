*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Manglish, Cantonese and Mandarin, Kadazandusun and Sabah Malay, Iban, Orang Asli languages for self-description. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu villagers:** sorcery and spirit explanations, and how healers handle blame.
- **Factory workers in Selangor, Penang and Johor:** possession at work and managers' readings of it.
- **Malays and Malaysian Chinese:** each community's images of the other, and the political uses of those images.
- **Sabah natives and migrants:** sorcery talk and blame of undocumented newcomers.
- **Orang Asli:** often pictured in peninsular folklore and textbooks; set those images beside their own accounts of outsiders.
- **Iban and Bidayuh:** spirit-caused harm against human blame.
- **Peranakan and Kristang:** how the larger communities picture them, and how they picture others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for spirit possession, *amok* and political rhetoric; mixed for everyday blame in families, which needs fieldwork; thin for Borneo accusation cases outside the press.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and Arabic healing vocabulary; Cantonese for sorcery and blame idioms; Sabah Malay and Kadazandusun for village cases.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interethnic slurs and antisemitic rhetoric must be quoted sparingly and kept as data; sorcery accusations can endanger the accused; refugee material involves vulnerable people; some topics fall under the Sedition Act.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, which also appear in majority stereotypes, held for the author's ruling.
