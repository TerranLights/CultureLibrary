# Malaysia: The domain, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin, Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Iban, Kadazandusun, Bidayuh, Bajau, Murut, Melanau, Semai and Temiar. **Concept in one line:** death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance and secrets; the domain question is how people here face death, power and what is hidden.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay Muslims, west coast against Kelantan and Terengganu:** the *kenduri arwah* calendar and *pondok* schooling differ by state.
- **Negeri Sembilan's *adat perpatih* lineages:** ancestral land passes through women, against *faraid* elsewhere.
- **Iban and Bidayuh:** a festival of the dead and, among Bidayuh, the older skull houses.
- **Melanau, Kenyah, Kayan and Kelabit:** secondary burial, burial poles and megaliths in Sarawak's interior and coast.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Bajau of Sabah:** priestess-led rites, jar burial, carved grave markers at sea.
- **Orang Asli (Mah Meri, Temiar, Chewong, Batek):** ancestors and dream guides without a scriptural afterlife.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect, and Peranakan households:** grave-sweeping, ghost month, paper offerings, columbaria.
- **Kristang of Malacca and the Kedah and Kelantan Siamese:** Catholic and Buddhist ways of handling the dead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Malay funeral rites, Chinese ritual and official statistics; mixed for Sarawak and Sabah, strong in museum journals but older; thin and dated for most Orang Asli groups. Malaysia's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for Malaysia's present lands (the Peninsula, Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan).
- **Languages needed:** Malay in Jawi for the *kitab* and court sources; Hokkien and Cantonese as well as Mandarin for funeral trades; Iban for dirges; Kadazandusun for ritual chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual diversity is policed by state religious offices; Iban and Kadazandusun ritual chants are owned knowledge; human remains and skulls in collections need the communities' consent.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own death, inheritance and festival material, set aside for the author.
