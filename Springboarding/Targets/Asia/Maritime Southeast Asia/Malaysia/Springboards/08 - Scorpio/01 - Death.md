# Malaysia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin, Cantonese, Hokkien, Iban, Kadazandusun, Berawan and other Orang Ulu languages, Batek. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are researched here.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay Muslims, urban Klang Valley against rural Kelantan:** how fast the burial, how long the *tahlil* nights, and how *ziarah kubur* at Hari Raya is kept.
- **Berawan, Kenyah, Kayan and Kelabit (Orang Ulu):** a corpse kept and reburied, beside same-day Muslim burial.
- **Iban:** the long mourning period and its formal ending; Christian and older practice side by side.
- **Kadazandusun and Murut:** a mountain as the dead's home; Catholic, Protestant and priestess-led rites in one district.
- **Batek, Chewong and Temiar:** forest peoples who move camp after a death; dead and soul without a scripture.
- **Malaysian Chinese, Cantonese Kuala Lumpur against Hokkien Penang:** Qingming, ghost month, Buddhist and Taoist funerals, and the Christian minority among them.
- **Bajau Laut of Semporna:** boat-dwellers burying on land; sea and shore at a death.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Malay religious teaching and Chinese funeral practice; strong but older for Sarawak's Orang Ulu; thin and scattered for Sabah's interior peoples and for the Orang Asli outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay for eschatological *kitab*; Cantonese and Hokkien for funeral talk that never reaches Mandarin print; Iban and Berawan for mortuary chants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide was a crime until recently and is still hard to discuss; the 1969 riot deaths are politically sensitive; Kadazandusun and Iban death chants are owned knowledge; human remains in museums.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own cremation and mourning material, set aside for the author.

4. Pull DOSM's Abridged Life Tables by state and ethnic group.
5. Ask Hospis Malaysia for published studies on where Malaysians die and on telling the patient.
