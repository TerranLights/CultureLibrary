# Malaysia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Malaysian English, Mandarin, Foochow, Kadazandusun, Kelabit, Iban, Kelantan Siamese (Southern Thai). **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay Muslims:** *hijrah* as personal piety in the cities against older *pondok* and Sufi-order paths in Kedah and Kelantan.
- **Converts to Islam (Chinese, Orang Asli, Iban, Kadazandusun):** a new name and community, and kin left behind.
- **Kelabit and Lun Bawang:** near-total conversion within living memory after the highland revival.
- **Foochow and other Chinese Christians:** born-again language beside Buddhist rebirth in the same families.
- **Kadazandusun:** renewal through the rice myth, kept by priestesses and by a state festival.
- **Kelantan and Kedah Siamese:** temporary monkhood as a remaking.
- **Orang Asli (Mah Meri, Semai, Jakun):** revival of carving and language set against state resettlement.
- **East-coast flood towns:** rebuilding Kuala Krai and Manik Urai, against Klang Valley urban renewal.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for conversion to Islam, drug rehabilitation and church histories; mixed for Borneo revivals (strong church sources, few critical ones); thin for Orang Asli revival and for those who leave a faith.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay for Sufi verse; Kelabit and Lun Bawang for revival testimony; Foochow for Sibu's church memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leaving Islam is not openly possible and is handled by religious authorities; disputed conversions of children are live court fights (law belongs to Libra); "faith rehabilitation" centers are restricted; Huminodun's story is a sacred narrative.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own renewal festivals and conversion histories, set aside for the author.
