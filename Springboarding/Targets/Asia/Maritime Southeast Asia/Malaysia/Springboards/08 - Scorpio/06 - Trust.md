## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kampung Malays and their *kedai runcit*:** a tab in the notebook and *kutu* among neighbors, against urban banking.
- **Malaysian Chinese small traders:** dialect and clan credit, *hui* clubs and a merchant's name as collateral.
- **Kadazandusun and Dusun river villages:** trust kept through *tagal* bans and village councils.
- **Iban longhouses:** shared space where trust and gossip run along one gallery.
- **Batek, Semai and Temuan:** wariness of traders and officials, set against sharing inside the camp.
- **Kelantan Malays and cross-border kin:** trust that runs across the border into Patani (comparison only).
- **Sabah's stateless and undocumented residents (including many Bajau Laut):** trust in a state that may detain them.
- **Peranakan and Kristang families in Malacca:** small creole communities with dense ties.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey data on institutional trust and for credit and scams; mixed for interethnic trust (much is politicized); thin for Borneo and Orang Asli trust in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Hokkien and Teochew for credit-club practice; Malay in Jawi for *amanah* in religious writing; Kadazandusun for *tagal* agreements; Batek and Semai for Orang Asli accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trust between communities touches matters covered by the Sedition Act; scam victims' data is confidential; Tabung Haji audits were contested in court. Trust in police and courts is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own credit and trust networks, set aside for the author.
