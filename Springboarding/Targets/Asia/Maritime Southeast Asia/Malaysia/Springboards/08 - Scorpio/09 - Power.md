## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Royal states against non-royal ones:** sultans in nine states; governors in Penang, Malacca, Sabah and Sarawak.
- **Negeri Sembilan:** clan chiefs who choose the ruler, against hereditary succession elsewhere.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu:** religious scholars as power holders, beside palace and party.
- **Sarawak:** a dynastic past, long-serving chief ministers, and Iban titles drawn into the state.
- **Sabah's Kadazandusun, Bajau and Suluk:** the *Huguan Siou*, Sulu-era titles, and quarrels over who belongs.
- **Penan and other Orang Ulu:** power exercised over them, and the blockade as counter-power.
- **Malaysian Chinese:** *Kapitan*, *towkay* and guild power, and the bought or granted Datuk title.
- **Orang Asli:** *Batin* headmen whose standing depends on the state's Orang Asli department.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court history, party politics and patronage; mixed for Borneo (strong on dynasties and timber, thinner on community views); thin for power inside families and for Orang Asli self-government.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi Malay for *Taj al-Salatin* and court texts; Hakka and Cantonese for *Kapitan*-era records; Iban and Kadazandusun for titles and oral history; Penan for blockade testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the rulers, *ketuanan Melayu* and special rights are protected by sedition rules; Sabah citizenship questions are highly charged; criticism of officeholders has drawn charges. Legitimacy and authority are Capricorn's; the letter of constitutional law is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own leaders and political history, set aside for the author.
