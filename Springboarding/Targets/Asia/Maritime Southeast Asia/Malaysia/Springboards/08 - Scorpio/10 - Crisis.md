## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan, Terengganu and Pahang villagers:** the yearly monsoon flood as an expected crisis, met with practiced routines.
- **Perak's Kinta Valley:** a regional economy that broke when tin did.
- **Klang Valley residents:** haze, slope collapse and the 1969 riots as city crises.
- **Iban and Bidayuh longhouses:** fire as a crisis for the whole community at once.
- **Bajau, Suluk and other east-coast Sabahans:** security crises and the question of who counts as a resident.
- **Batek, Temiar and other Orang Asli:** outbreaks and land loss, often reported late.
- **Malaysian Chinese small traders:** the 1998 crash and lockdowns as household crises, met with kin credit.
- **Penang and Kedah coastal villages:** the 2004 tsunami as a rare sea-borne disaster.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for economic crises, floods and haze (official data and press); mixed for 1969 (official account and declassified files differ); thin for Orang Asli and Borneo crises in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Kelantan and Pahang Malay for flood memory; Cantonese for household hardship talk; Iban for longhouse accounts; Batek for Kuala Koh; Bajau and Sabah Malay for Lahad Datu.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** May 13 is the most sensitive subject in the country and falls under the Sedition Act; Lahad Datu touches security and citizenship; victims' families must be approached with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, with their own crisis history, set aside for the author.
