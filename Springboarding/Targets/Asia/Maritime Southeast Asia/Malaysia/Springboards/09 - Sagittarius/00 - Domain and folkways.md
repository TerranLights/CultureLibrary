## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast Malays** against **east-coast Malays** of Kelantan and Terengganu, for the pondok tradition and Islamist politics.
- **Negeri Sembilan** Malays, for *adat perpatih* beside Islam.
- Malays of **Javanese, Bugis, Banjar and Hadhrami** heritage, for distinct Sufi orders and saint cults.
- **Chinese Malaysians** by variety and faith: Penang Hokkien temples, Hakka Christians in Sabah, Foochow Methodists in Sibu.
- **Peranakan Chinese** and **Kristang** Eurasians, as blended traditions.
- **Siamese Malaysians** of Kedah and Kelantan, for Theravada temples.
- **Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau** (Muslim, Christian and Liko) and the **Orang Ulu** (Kenyah, Kayan, Kelabit, Lun Bawang, Penan) of Sarawak.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut, Bajau** and **Suluk** of Sabah.
- **Orang Asli** peoples: Semai, Temiar, Batek, Jahai, Mah Meri, Jakun, Temuan.
- The religiously **unaffiliated** and humanists, mainly urban.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Islam and classical Malay texts; mixed for Chinese popular religion; thin for Orang Asli and smaller Borneo peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for *kitab* and older print; Arabic; Mandarin and the varieties; Iban and Kadazandusun ritual registers; English for colonial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** apostasy, proselytizing to Muslims, the "Allah" word dispute and deviant-teaching lists are live political issues; Iban *timang* and Kadazandusun *rinait* may be held by ritual specialists; Orang Asli conversion is contested. **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of present-day Malaysia (the Peninsular states, Sabah, Sarawak, Labuan).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
