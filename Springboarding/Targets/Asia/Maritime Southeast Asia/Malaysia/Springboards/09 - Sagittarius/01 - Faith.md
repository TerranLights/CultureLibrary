# Malaysia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Mandarin, Hokkien and Cantonese, Iban, Kadazandusun, Sama-Bajau, English. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu Malays**, where *Main Puteri* and *ruqyah* compete for the same afflictions.
- **Perlis** Muslims, whose state religious authority leans toward reformist (Salafi-influenced) teaching, against Sufi-inclined practice in Kedah.
- **Klang Valley Malay middle class**, the generation formed by the 1970s *dakwah* movement.
- **Penang Hokkien** temple communities against **Foochow Methodists** of Sibu and **Hakka** Basel Christians in Sabah: three Chinese faiths.
- **Kelabit and Lun Bawang**, for revival-born evangelical faith.
- **Kadazandusun** families split across Catholic, Protestant, Muslim and traditional lines.
- **Iban**, Anglican, Catholic and evangelical, with Gawai kept.
- **Bajau** of Semporna, for Islam beside ancestor rites; **Mah Meri** and **Semai**, for Orang Asli faith under mission pressure.
- **Kristang** Catholics of Melaka.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Islamic practice and Christian church records; mixed for Chinese temple faith (temple boards, Chinese press); thin for Orang Asli and Bajau faith outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Malay, with Arabic religious vocabulary; Mandarin plus Hokkien for temple life; Iban and Kadazandusun for church and ritual texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leaving Islam and converting Muslims are legally fraught; testimonies of conversion may endanger the speaker, so anonymity matters; healing rites can be under official disapproval; law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pew Research Center site: find the Southeast Asia religion report and extract Malaysia's prayer and importance-of-religion items by group.
2. Malay Concordance Project: search *iman* and *masuk Islam* in the *Sejarah Melayu* and *Hikayat Merong Mahawangsa*.
3. Library catalogs: "Bario Revival", "Sidang Injil Borneo history", "Kadazandusun Christianity".
4. Search "Darussyifa ruqyah" and "Main Puteri Kelantan" in Malay-language theses (UM, UKM, USM repositories).
5. Search Chinese-language press (Sin Chew, China Press) for 九皇爷 还愿 to collect vow accounts.
