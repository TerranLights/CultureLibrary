## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Kedah** pondok families against **Klang Valley** families aiming at international and private schools.
- **Malay rural students** in MRSM and MARA pipelines, for state-backed mobility.
- **Chinese Malaysians** in Chinese-medium primary and independent secondary schools.
- **Peranakan** and English-educated families of Penang and Melaka, for older mission-school ties.
- **Iban** and **Bidayuh** in Sarawak, for mission schooling and mother-tongue teaching.
- **Kadazandusun** and **Murut** in Sabah, for language programs and interior school access.
- **Bajau** and stateless children of Sabah's east coast.
- **Orang Asli** (Semai, Temiar, Jakun), for dropout and boarding.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policy, exams and universities; mixed for pondok and Chinese independent schools (community sources); thin for Orang Asli and stateless children's own views.
- **Languages needed:** Malay; Jawi for pondok materials; Mandarin for Chinese education; English for colonial reports; Iban and Kadazandusun for language programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** quotas, language policy and UEC recognition are politically charged; tahfiz-school abuse cases; stateless children; academic freedom (its legal side to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
