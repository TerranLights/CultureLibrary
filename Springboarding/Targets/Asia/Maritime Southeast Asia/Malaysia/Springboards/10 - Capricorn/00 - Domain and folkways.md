## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay court states with sultans versus Penang, Melaka, Sabah and Sarawak** (governors, no ruler): where royal authority frames status and where it does not.
- **Negeri Sembilan** (*Adat Perpatih*): matrilineal clan offices give a different chain of command.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: ulama and royal authority side by side on the east coast.
- **Sarawak's Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau and Orang Ulu** (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan): longhouse and aristocratic models of rank to separate.
- **Sabah's Kadazandusun, Murut, Bajau and Suluk**: native chiefs, village heads and maritime titles.
- **Orang Asli peoples** (Semai, Temiar, Batek, Jakun, Temuan): headmen appointed by the state against customary leaders.
- **Malaysian Chinese by variety, and the Peranakan of Melaka and Penang**: clan, guild and towkay hierarchies.
- **Kristang of Melaka and the Malaysian Siamese of Kelantan and Kedah**: small communities with their own heads.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Malay court texts and federal statistics; mixed for Chinese-language material; thin for Murut, Bidayuh subgroups and small Orang Asli peoples. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass covers Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah and Sarawak.
- **Languages needed:** Malay in both scripts for court and religious texts; Chinese for clan and press records; Iban and Kadazandusun for the domain's Borneo vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public comment on the rulers is legally sensitive; Malay special position and the 1969 riots are charged topics; some *pawang* and Iban ritual knowledge is held by specialists.
- **Held for review:** domain pointers touching South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- Patani, Brunei and Malaysians abroad are comparison only.
