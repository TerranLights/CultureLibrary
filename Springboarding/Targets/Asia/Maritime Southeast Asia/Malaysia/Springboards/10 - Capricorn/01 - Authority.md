## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: test whether the religious teacher outranks the prince in daily deference.
- **Johor and Perak**: assertive royal houses; how far a sultan's word reaches beyond ceremony.
- **Negeri Sembilan**: the *Undang* and clan chiefs share authority with the ruler, so obedience runs through lineage.
- **Sarawak's Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu**: elected headmen, Brooke-made titles and aristocratic chiefs to separate.
- **Sabah's Kadazandusun, Murut and Bajau**: native chiefs appointed by the state against village standing.
- **Orang Asli** (Batek, Semai, Temiar, Temuan): from peoples where commanding others is avoided to Proto-Malay groups with formal offices.
- **Malaysian Chinese** (Hokkien, Hakka, Cantonese, Foochow) **and the Peranakan**: clan elders, temple boards and towkays as non-state authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for royal and religious authority; mixed for Sabah; thin for Murut and small Orang Asli groups.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for older religious and court writing; Iban for Tun Jugah material; Mandarin and Hokkien for Kapitan-era sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** criticism of the rulers falls under sedition law; fatwa disputes and party rivalry over religion are charged; Orang Asli leadership disputes can involve land claims.
- **Held for review:** authority within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- Patani's own ruler texts may serve only as comparison.
