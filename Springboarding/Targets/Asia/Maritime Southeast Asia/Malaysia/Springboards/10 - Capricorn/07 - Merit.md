## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay families in the Klang Valley versus rural Kedah and Kelantan**: scholarships and quotas weighed against open competition.
- **Malaysian Chinese, by school stream**: national, vernacular and independent; credentials accepted unevenly.
- **Sabah's Kadazandusun and Murut**: local-merit claims in the state civil service.
- **Sarawak's Iban, Bidayuh and Melanau**: renown by deeds in the old order and degrees in the new.
- **Kelabit, Kayan and Kenyah**: achieved standing beside inherited rank.
- **Orang Asli peoples**: access to schooling shapes who can compete at all.
- **Malaysian Siamese and Chinese Buddhists**: merit as a religious ledger, not a résumé.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for admissions and civil-service debates; mixed for Borneo renown; thin for Orang Asli views of deserving.
- **Languages needed:** Malay for Hansard; Mandarin for the UEC debate; Iban and Kelabit for earned renown; Thai for Siamese temple usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Article 153 and *Bumiputera* policy are constitutionally protected subjects; questioning them in public can carry legal risk.
- **Held for review:** credentials and merit claims of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- Singapore's meritocracy is comparison only.
