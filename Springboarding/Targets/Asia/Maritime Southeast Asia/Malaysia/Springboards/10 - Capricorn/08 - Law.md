## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Negeri Sembilan's *Adat Perpatih* communities**: custom claimed as the older law beside sharia and state.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: religious law as the law's purpose, with two different party records.
- **Urban Malay professionals in the Klang Valley**: civil law and rights talk among lawyers and activists.
- **Iban, Bidayuh and Orang Ulu**: native courts and headmen as the first stop.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Bajau**: Sabah's native chiefs and courts beside stateless and migrant communities, whose standing before the law needs its own study.
- **Orang Asli peoples**: customary rights in land pressed through courts.
- **Malaysian Chinese and the Peranakan**: older clan and guild arbitration against reliance on courts and lawyers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional and syariah debates; mixed for native courts; thin for Bajau and stateless communities' encounters with the law.
- **Languages needed:** English and Malay for court culture; Jawi for older Islamic legal writing; Iban, Bidayuh and Kadazandusun for customary hearings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sedition, religion and royal matters carry legal risk; hudud and apostasy debates are inflamed; customary hearings may not admit outsiders.
- **Held for review:** legal culture within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- The letter of statutes is Libra's; Patani's Islamic courts only as comparison.
