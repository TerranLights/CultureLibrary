## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Johor Bahru**: urban order, crowds and protest compared with small towns.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: moral order enforced by state religious bodies.
- **Former New Villages of Perak, Pahang and Johor**: Chinese communities shaped by emergency order.
- **Sabah's east coast** (Bajau, Suluk, Kadazandusun): border security, stateless residents and cross-sea ties.
- **Sarawak's Penan, Kayan and Iban**: forest order versus state permits.
- **Semai, Temiar and Batek**: peace kept by withdrawal and moots, without police.
- **Peranakan, Kristang and Malaysian Siamese**: small enclaves whose order rests on their own elders and temples.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and state security records; mixed for urban protest; thin for stateless communities in Sabah.
- **Languages needed:** Malay and English for police and archive records; Mandarin and Cantonese for New Village memory; Semai and Penan for local peace-keeping.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** May 13 remains restricted in public talk; detention-law history, Lahad Datu and Penan land are charged; some Emergency files may still be closed.
- **Held for review:** order and policing within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- Patani's insurgency and the southern Philippines only as comparison.
