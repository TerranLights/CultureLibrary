## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West-coast urban Malays** (Klang Valley, Johor Bahru) against **kampung Malays of Kedah and Perlis**: whether work parties survive in housing estates.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**: *wakaf* roadside pavilions and *dikir barat* troupes as gathering forms; dialect sources.
- **Negeri Sembilan's adat perpatih villages**: the matrilineal *suku* as a different unit of belonging.
- **Malay sub-groups of migrant origin** (Javanese, Bugis, Banjar, Minangkabau, Mandailing), each with its own associations.
- **Malaysian Chinese by dialect and place** (Penang Hokkien, Ipoh Cantonese, Sabah Hakka, Sibu Foochow) and the New Villages: association halls as the unit.
- **Peranakan Chinese** of Malacca, Penang and Kelantan: settled mixed forms.
- **Sarawak**: Iban, Bidayuh, Melanau and Orang Ulu (Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Penan, Lun Bawang): longhouse and riverine forms.
- **Sabah**: Kadazandusun, Rungus, Murut, Bajau and Bajau Laut: hill, plain and sea settlement.
- **Orang Asli peoples** (Semai, Temiar, Jakun, Temuan, Mah Meri, Batek): small camps and resettlement villages.
- **Malacca's Kristang (Portuguese-Eurasian) Settlement** and the **Siamese of Kelantan and Kedah**.
- Comparison only: Patani's Malays (southern Thailand), Singapore, Brunei, Malaysians abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Malay and Chinese associations and unity policy; mixed for Borneo; thin and outsider-written for several Orang Asli and Orang Ulu peoples.
- **Lands:** the tracker records no change to the pre-war lands, and the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands (the Peninsular states and federal territories, Sabah, Sarawak, Labuan).
- **Languages needed:** Malay with Jawi for court texts and early newspapers; Chinese (both scripts) for association histories; Iban and Kadazandusun for self-description.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race, religion and royalty (the "3R") in public speech; May 13, 1969; statelessness in Sabah; restricted Orang Ulu and Orang Asli ritual knowledge.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
