# Malaysia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Mandarin and Cantonese, Hokkien, Iban, Kadazandusun, Malaysian English; Bidayuh and Melanau for Sarawak villages. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **FELDA settlers against old riverine kampung**: a chosen, planned community against an inherited one.
- **Klang Valley flats (PPR) and condominiums**: whether neighbors know one another in high-rise estates.
- **New Village Chinese of Perak, Pahang and Johor**: communities born of war-time resettlement.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu mosque parishes**: the *kariah* as the strongest local unit to test.
- **Negeri Sembilan adat perpatih villages**: clan-held land as glue.
- **Iban longhouse residents against Iban town migrants in Kuching, Sibu and Miri**: the longhouse kept as home community at a distance.
- **Bidayuh and Melanau villages**: non-longhouse Sarawak forms.
- **Kadazandusun, Murut and Rungus villages; Sabah water villages**: inland and sea settlement.
- **Orang Asli regroupment villages** (*Rancangan Pengumpulan Semula*) against older settlements.
- **Malacca's Kristang and Peranakan streets**: heritage communities under tourism.
- Comparison only: Singapore's HDB estates, Patani villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on New Villages, FELDA and Malay villages of the 1950s to 1970s; thinner on present-day estates and on Sabah's water villages.
- **Languages needed:** Kelantan and Terengganu Malay for local voices; Cantonese and Hokkien for New Village oral history; Iban for longhouse self-description.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Orang Asli resettlement and land; clearance of Sabah water villages and the undocumented; New Villages' Emergency history; sermons are state-written, so read them as official.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

3. Search JAKIM's sermon archive for "jiran" and "kariah" and note the years.

5. Search the Sarawak Museum and DBNA sites for "baruk" and "Kaul Mukah".
