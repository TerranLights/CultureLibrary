# Malaysia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Mandarin, Hokkien and Cantonese, Iban, Kadazandusun, Semai, Malaysian English. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu villages split by party**: a test of whether party loyalty overrode kin and mosque.
- **Malaysian Chinese dialect groups** (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Hainanese, Foochow): the *bang* as inherited group, and how far it still sorts work and marriage.
- **Iban river groups** (Saribas, Skrang, Rejang): sub-groups inside one people.
- **Kadazan, Dusun and Rungus**: the naming of one group out of several.
- **West-coast and east-coast Bajau** (Kota Belud against Semporna): one name, two ways of life.
- **The Orang Asli's official three-part grouping** (Senoi, Negrito or Semang, Proto-Malay) against the peoples' own names.
- **Malay sub-groups** (Javanese, Bugis, Minangkabau): absorbed into "Malay" or kept as groups.
- **Peranakan Chinese**: a group whose boundary runs through language and dress rather than descent alone.
- **University students in residential colleges**: orientation week as entry into a cohort.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chinese associations, ethnic categories and party politics; mixed for Bornean sub-groups; thin for present-day gangs (police and court sources only).
- **Languages needed:** Chinese in traditional script for 19th-century society records; Kelantan Malay for party talk in villages; Iban and Kadazandusun for self-naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic categories are tied to Bumiputera status; the 1980s Kelantan and Terengganu disputes over declaring rivals unbelievers; secret-society material is police-held; collective punishment and the legal side of associations belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
