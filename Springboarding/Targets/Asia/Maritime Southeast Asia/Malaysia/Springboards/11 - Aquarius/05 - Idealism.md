# Malaysia: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi and Jawi), Arabic phrases of Islamic political ideals, Mandarin, Iban, Kadazandusun, Foochow for Sibu, Malaysian English. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Malay left nationalists of the 1930s to 1950s** against **the Alliance founders**: two ideals of independence.
- **PAS supporters in Kelantan and Terengganu**: the ideal of an Islamic polity and how voters speak of it.
- **Klang Valley professionals of the "Bangsa Malaysia" generation**: a civic ideal and its disillusion.
- **Malaysian Chinese of the 1910s and of the communist era**: ideals imported from China and remade locally.
- **Sibu Foochow Christians**: an ideal written into a settlement.
- **Iban**: the Keling and Kumang ideals in today's longhouse pageants.
- **Kadazandusun**: the Huminodun ideal in festival form.
- **Penan and other Orang Ulu**: idealized by outsiders, with their own voices to recover.
- **Orang Asli**: the same risk of outsiders' ideals (the "peaceful people" image).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national visions and Malay literary movements; mixed for the left (memoirs, some banned for years); thin for Bornean ideals outside oral collections.
- **Languages needed:** Jawi for 1930s to 1950s left and literary periodicals; Arabic for Islamic-state phrases; Iban and Kadazandusun for epics and myths.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the communist past and its memoirs; Darul Arqam's ban; the Islamic-state debate; Penan advocacy is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
