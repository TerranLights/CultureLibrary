## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kelantan and Terengganu**, where a state government has restricted performing arts and sects, against **Penang and Kuala Lumpur**, home to most scenes.
- **Malay youth in FELDA schemes and urban flats**: the *lepak* and *rempit* labels fell hardest here.
- **Malaysian Chinese indie and art circles**: offbeat styles with less religious policing.
- **Iban, Murut and Kadazandusun**: rebels honored as local heroes; how each people tells the story.
- **Penan and other Orang Ulu**: dissent as collective refusal, not personal style.
- **Orang Asli who refuse resettlement or conversion**: nonconformity toward the state's plans.
- **Trans women and gender-nonconforming Malaysians**: the most sanctioned difference, to handle with care.
- **Women who change dress or faith practice**: personal nonconformity under public scrutiny.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press panics and literary debate; mixed for scenes (zines, blogs, interviews); thin and risky for sexual and religious nonconformity.
- **Languages needed:** Kelantan Malay for *Mak Yong* and local sects; Malaysian English slang for scenes; Chinese for indie press; Murut and Penan voices through translation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "deviant teaching", apostasy and sexual minorities fall under religious enforcement; sedition and censorship cases (Libra and Capricorn take the legal side); protect individuals named in panics.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
