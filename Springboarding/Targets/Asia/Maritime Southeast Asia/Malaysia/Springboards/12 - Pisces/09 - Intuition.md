# Malaysia: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Malaysia, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Malay (Rumi; Jawi for older texts), Cantonese and Mandarin, Baba Malay, Iban, Rungus and other Kadazandusun varieties, Jahai, English. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Fishing villages** on the east coast (Kelantan, Terengganu) set beside **office workers** in Kuala Lumpur and Petaling Jaya.
- **Malay mothers** and grandmothers against young professionals, on *naluri* and *firasat*.
- **Malaysian Chinese** consulting almanacs and geomancers against those who reject them; **Peranakan** cooks of Malacca and Penang.
- **Iban** dream-seekers and omen-readers in Sarawak; **Melanau** fishers.
- **Rungus** and other **Kadazandusun** of Kudat; **Bajau** and **Ubian** seafarers of Semporna.
- **Jahai** and **Batek** foragers, set beside settled Orang Asli.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; dream, omen and almanac sources are plentiful, measured studies few.
- **Languages needed:** Malay religious register for *firasat*, Cantonese track slang, Iban for dream accounts, Jahai for smell terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state religious bodies treat some divination as forbidden; some Iban and Rungus knowledge is held by specialists; avoid reading tradition as "superstition".
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their omen-reading and lucky-day customs are left for that review.
