## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland Christian majorities:** Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan, Pangasinan: shared Catholic rites, separate vocabularies of the self.
- **Cordillera peoples:** Ifugao, Kalinga, Bontok, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Isneg, Itneg: tattoo, prestige and lineage traditions outside Spanish rule.
- **Moro peoples:** Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Iranun, Yakan, Sama and Sama Dilaut: Islamic naming, rank and honor terms.
- **Lumad of Mindanao:** Manobo groups, T'boli, B'laan, Teduray, Subanen, Bagobo, Mandaya, Higaonon: dress, dreams and naming differ widely.
- **Mangyan of Mindoro, Palawan peoples (Tagbanwa, Palaw'an, Batak), Aeta, Agta and Ati, Ivatan of Batanes, Bugkalot of Aurora and Nueva Vizcaya:** small groups often left out of national samples.
- **Chinese Filipinos, Chavacano speakers, mestizo families:** naming and identity across lines.
- **Religious lines:** Catholic, Iglesia ni Cristo, Aglipayan, Protestant, Muslim; **gender lines:** *bakla*, *tomboy*, transgender Filipinas.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Tagalog Manila and for English-language scholarship; mixed for the big regional languages; thin for small Indigenous groups. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core archipelago.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish (colonial records), Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao and Tausug for the south; Hokkien for Chinese Filipino records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial photographs of Cordillera and Mindanao peoples (Worcester era) were made to rank peoples; some ritual and healing knowledge is restricted, and work in ancestral domains needs community consent; Mindanao conflict.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
