*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino (Tagalog), English (much workplace and school talk), Cebuano, Ilocano, Kapampangan, Maranao, Tausug, Philippine Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Manila Tagalog against provincial Tagalog** (Batangas, Bulacan, Quezon): regional reputations for bluntness or softness are claimed in jokes; test them, never assume them.
- **Cebuano and other Visayan speakers:** often contrasted with Tagalog in self-description; worth separate samples.
- **Ilocano and Kapampangan:** their own verbal-joust traditions.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug:** rank, honor and clan in claims.
- **Cordillera peoples:** elder-led speaking order.
- **Lumad and Mangyan communities:** consensus speech in community meetings.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** family business, two codes (Hokkien home, Filipino and English outside).
- **Women, *bakla* and young people:** different licenses to speak; **BPO workers:** trained codes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Manila and for the psychology debate; mixed for the regions; thin on Moro and Lumad speech norms outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano; Maranao and Tausug for the south.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** complaint programs can shame named private people; quote no one. Politeness rules toward elders and clergy are tied to faith; law on complaints belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
