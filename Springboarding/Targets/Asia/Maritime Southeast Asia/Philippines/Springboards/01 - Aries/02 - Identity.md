## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog Manila against the regions:** Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan and Pangasinan identities, often defined against "Imperial Manila".
- **Bangsamoro peoples:** Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Iranun, Yakan, Sama; one political name, several distinct peoples.
- **Lumad peoples:** Manobo groups, T'boli, B'laan, Teduray, Subanen, Higaonon, Mandaya; the term itself is not accepted by all.
- **Cordillera peoples:** village and ethnolinguistic loyalties under the pan-Igorot label.
- **Aeta, Agta and Ati; Mangyan; Palawan peoples; Ivatan; Bugkalot:** small groups with their own self-names.
- **Chinese Filipinos, Chavacano speakers of Zamboanga and Cavite, Japanese Filipinos of Davao, mestizo families.**
- **Faith lines:** Catholic, Iglesia ni Cristo, Aglipayan, evangelical, Muslim.
- **Gender and sexuality:** *bakla*, *tomboy*, transgender Filipinas, with their own social labels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national identity and on the Moro and Igorot cases; mixed on regional identities; thin on small Indigenous groups and on documentless people.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish (nineteenth-century sources), Cebuano, Ilocano, Kapampangan; Maranao, Tausug and Sinama for the south; Hokkien.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Moro and Lumad identities are tied to armed conflict and land; some group names are slurs in other mouths; who counts as Indigenous is decided partly by law, which belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
- **Diaspora:** Filipino American and overseas worker identities only as comparison.
