## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila, Cebu and Davao:** urban startup and small-business scenes, each with its own media.
- **Ilocano, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Bikol and Waray towns:** different traditions of cooperative labor and different migration routes.
- **Ifugao and other Cordillera peoples:** work groups tied to rice terraces.
- **Maranao traders and Tausug and Sama seafarers:** long trading traditions in the south; Marawi's market life before 2017.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** family firms and chambers of commerce.
- **Lumad and Mangyan communities:** cooperatives and their own enterprises.
- **Women:** market vendors, sari-sari owners, *paluwagan* organizers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business statistics and Manila civil society; mixed for regional cooperative labor; thin for small Indigenous economies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English first, then Cebuano and Ilocano; Hokkien for Chinese Filipino business records; Classical Chinese (or translations) for early trade.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** community pantries and some volunteer groups were "red-tagged"; name organizers with care. The "lazy native" image is a colonial claim, answered by Rizal's essay on indolence; treat it as a topic, never a finding.
- **Held for review:** none.
