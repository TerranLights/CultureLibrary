## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog provinces:** revolutionary memory (Cavite, Bulacan, Batangas) and *anting-anting* centers (Quezon, Laguna).
- **Ilocano and Cordillera peoples:** epic courage; Kalinga, Bontok and Ifugao warrior marks and their present meaning.
- **Visayan peoples:** Cebuano (Lapu-Lapu as a local hero), Boholano (the Dagohoy revolt), Hiligaynon and Waray (typhoon endurance).
- **Moro peoples:** Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon courage tied to rank and faith; check each separately.
- **Lumad peoples:** defense of land in their own words.
- **Women and *bakla*:** courage outside the male warrior model.
- **Soldiers, police and rebels:** each side's heroes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national heroes, war memory and epics; mixed for regional and gender variation; thin for Lumad accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Ilocano, Cebuano, Maranao, Tausug.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *sabil* and the colonial word *juramentado* have been used to paint Moro people as fanatics; handle as history, quote Moro sources. Martial-law memory is politically divisive; journalist killings involve living families. Some *anting-anting* prayers are guarded by their owners.
- **Held for review:** none.
