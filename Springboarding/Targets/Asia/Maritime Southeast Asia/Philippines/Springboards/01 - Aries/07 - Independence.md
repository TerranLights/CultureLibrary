## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and other lowland Christian households:** extended kin and godparent ties; compare urban renters with rural landholders.
- **Boholano, Ilocano and Cebuano regions:** their own revolt histories and migration-for-work traditions.
- **Tausug, Maguindanaon and Maranao:** sultanate memory and modern self-determination.
- **Cordillera peoples:** terrace self-provisioning and resistance to outside control.
- **Mangyan, Palawan peoples, Aeta, Agta and Ati:** forest and coastal self-provisioning under pressure.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** family businesses and community mutual aid.
- **Overseas workers' households:** independence for some members bought by another's absence.
- **Elders and disabled people:** the ages and conditions at which dependence is accepted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national independence, remittances and cash transfers; mixed for household self-reliance; thin for Indigenous self-provisioning today.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Spanish, English, Cebuano, Ilocano; Tausug and Maguindanaon for the sultanates and Moro writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Moro self-determination and secession are tied to armed conflict; the Sulu sultanate's claim to Sabah is an international dispute; welfare debates carry class contempt. The legal frame of autonomy belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
- **Diaspora:** overseas workers abroad only as comparison; their home households are in scope.
