# Philippines: Domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino (Tagalog-based) and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Bikol, Kapampangan, Pangasinan; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug and Sama; Cordillera languages (Ifugao, Ibaloi, Kankanaey); Lan-nang (Philippine Hokkien); Spanish for colonial records. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how is it held (money, resources, food ways, dress ways, wealth ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila against provincial Tagalog towns:** mall consumption against market-town habits.
- **Central Luzon (Kapampangan, Tagalog rice tenants):** tenancy history, a strong regional cuisine.
- **Ilocano north and Ivatan of Batanes:** different household economies and weather risks.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Ibaloi, Kankanaey, Bontoc, Kalinga):** rice terraces, feast ranks and heirloom wealth.
- **Visayan peoples (Cebuano, Hiligaynon of the Negros and Panay sugar lands, Waray, Aklanon):** hacienda wealth against fishing and trading towns.
- **Moro peoples (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Sama, Yakan, Iranun):** brass, gold, cloth and bride wealth in Muslim custom.
- **Lumad of Mindanao (T'boli, Manobo, Bagobo, Mandaya, Subanen, Higaonon):** woven and brass wealth, swidden land.
- **Mangyan of Mindoro, Palawan peoples (Palaw'an, Tagbanwa, Batak), Aeta and Agta:** sharing economies distinct from lowland money economies.
- **Chinese Filipinos, Chavacano speakers of Zamboanga, households of overseas workers:** trade, mixed heritage and remittance-built wealth.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national statistics and lowland Christian groups; mixed for Moro areas; thin for many Lumad, Mangyan and Negrito groups outside a few ethnographies. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the core archipelago.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English throughout; Spanish for colonial archives; the regional languages above; Lan-nang for Chinese-Filipino sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cordillera ritual objects (*bulul* figures, heirloom beads) taken into the antique trade; Moro clan disputes over land; ill-gotten-wealth cases of the Marcos era; ancestral-domain claims. Land and property law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** pointers on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
