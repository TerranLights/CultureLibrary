# Philippines: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Kapampangan; Maranao and Tausug; Ifugao and Ibaloi; Spanish for colonial dictionaries. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Kapampangan lowland Catholics:** *santos*, family altars and the *camarero* role tie belongings to devotion.
- **Ifugao, Ibaloi and Kankanaey:** heirloom beads, carved figures and the Kabayan burial customs; objects leaving through the antique trade.
- **Waray of Leyte and Samar:** mass loss in Haiyan; salvage and replacement.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** brass, gongs and carved chests as household treasure.
- **Tausug and Sama of Sulu and Tawi-Tawi:** boat-dwelling Sama with few fixed goods, against Tausug heirloom blades.
- **T'boli, Manobo and Bagobo:** brass, beads and woven cloth worn as one's own.
- **Mangyan of Mindoro:** bamboo tubes inscribed with *ambahan* verse, small personal objects.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** shop goods against household goods; ancestral objects.
- **Urban informal settlers:** belongings under threat of demolition and fire.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in museum and migration studies, thin on everyday belongings outside Manila and the balikbayan literature.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English and Spanish; Cebuano, Waray, Ilocano and the Cordillera and Moro languages for local accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *bulul* and other Cordillera ritual objects are sacred to their owners and often sold or stolen; Moro heirlooms lost in the Marawi siege; demolition of informal homes. Property law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the possession chapter of Schachter and Otanes and note the constructions to search for in other languages.
2. Open the Boxer Codex scans and list every object each people is shown holding or wearing.
3. Search PRECUP for privately held objects in Bulacan, Pampanga and Cebu, then search "camarero" with those towns.
4. Search Philippine university catalogs for "balikbayan box" and "material culture"; start with McKay.
5. Search Tacloban and Palo news archives and university repositories for Haiyan oral histories mentioning belongings.
