# Philippines: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino and English; Spanish for colonial land records; Hiligaynon and Cebuano (sugar and plantation lands); Ilocano and the Cordillera languages; Maranao and Maguindanaon; Lan-nang. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Luzon Tagalog and Kapampangan rice tenants:** the classic tenancy belt, shaped by friar and private haciendas.
- **Negros and Panay Hiligaynon:** sugar estates, seasonal cane cutters (*sakada*), concentrated holdings.
- **Ilocano smallholders:** a contrast of small owner-farms and out-migration.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Bontok, Kankanaey, Ibaloi):** family, clan and village tenure of terraces, woodlots and water.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** inherited clan lands, contested in feuds.
- **Lumad of Mindanao (Manobo, Higaonon, Subanen, B'laan):** ancestral domains against plantations and mines.
- **Sama and Tausug of Sulu:** sea and reef access rather than plots.
- **Palawan peoples (Tagbanwa, Palaw'an):** swidden and ancestral waters.
- **Metro Manila informal settlers against subdivision owners:** ownership without title against gated title.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** shop and firm ownership under historical restrictions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on agrarian history and statistics; mixed for ancestral domains; thin on how ownership is spoken of in everyday life outside tenancy studies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish; Hiligaynon, Ilocano, Cordillera languages, Maranao, Maguindanaon.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** agrarian violence (sugar estates, Central Luzon); clan feuds in Mindanao; killings of land defenders; demolitions. All land law, including ancestral-domain and agrarian statutes, goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.
