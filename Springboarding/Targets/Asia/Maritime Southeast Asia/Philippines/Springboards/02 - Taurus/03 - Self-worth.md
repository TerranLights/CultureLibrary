# Philippines: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino and English (much of the psychology literature mixes both); Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano; Maranao and Tausug; Kalinga and other Cordillera languages; Spanish loanwords. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is held to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog lowland Catholics:** the source of most *hiya* and *amor propio* studies; test them against other regions.
- **Cebuano and Hiligaynon Visayans:** their own honor and pride vocabulary, and planter-worker hierarchies.
- **Ilocano:** a separate honor and shame vocabulary, and long labor migration to Hawaii and the Gulf.
- **Kalinga, Bontoc and Ifugao:** earned marks of honor, feast rank, and the history of being looked down on as "Igorot".
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** *maratabat* and clan honor.
- **Tausug and Sama:** honor among Tausug, and the Sama Dilaut's standing as a low-status sea people.
- **Aeta and Agta:** worth under heavy prejudice against dark-skinned foragers.
- **Lumad of Mindanao:** dignity claims tied to land and school.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** worth across two communities.
- **Overseas workers' households:** worth bound to remittances.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Filipino psychology and lowland studies; thin for most minorities, whose own words for worth are barely recorded in print.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao, Tausug, Kalinga.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colorism and slurs against Aeta and "Igorot"; suicide data; honor-related violence in feuds; gender and *puri*. Tattoo revival raises questions of who may wear a people's marks.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Enriquez's chapters on *kapwa* and *hiya*, then the *Four Readings*, and list each claim to test.
2. Search the *Philippine Studies* and *Philippine Journal of Psychology* archives for "self-esteem", "hiya" and "dangal".
3. Request YAFS questionnaires and find the self-esteem items by wave.
4. Search Mindanao State University and Notre Dame University (Cotabato) catalogs for "maratabat".
5. Search Cebuano and Ilocano dictionaries for *dungog*, *garbo*, *dayaw* and *bain*, then search regional newspapers with them.
