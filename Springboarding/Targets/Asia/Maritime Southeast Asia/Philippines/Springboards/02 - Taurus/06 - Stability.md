## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Tagalog, Pampanga and Ilocos towns:** parish centers, ancestral houses and families on the same land for generations.
- **Metro Manila newcomers:** high churn, rented rooms, long commutes.
- **Cebuano and Hiligaynon Visayans:** sugar and port towns with old planter families, against seasonal workers.
- **Ifugao and other Cordillera peoples:** terraces and villages tied to one place, against recent out-migration.
- **Ivatan:** small islands, long-held stone houses.
- **Maranao:** royal lineages and houses, broken by the siege.
- **Tausug and Maguindanaon:** sultanate genealogies as continuity.
- **Sama Dilaut:** boat mobility against land settlement.
- **Lumad and Aeta groups with seasonal moves:** mobility as a way of life, or forced by displacement.
- **Overseas workers' families:** long separations within stable households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography and Spanish-era parish records; mixed for Moro genealogies; thin for the everyday meaning of routine outside Manila studies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Tausug, Maranao, Sama; Arabic and Jawi for the genealogies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Marawi siege and its losses; coup history and martial law; political dynasties. Marriage, family and property law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the 2018 National Migration Survey report and note the questions on years at current residence.
2. Search FamilySearch's Philippine collections for one Ilocos and one Visayan parish and trace family surnames across a century.
3. Read Saleeby's *tarsila* translations beside Nimmo on the Sama Dilaut.
4. Pull BSP and PSA price series from 1980 to the present.
5. Search *Liwayway* and *Bisaya* indexes for "noon" ("in the old days") and "lumang bahay" ("old house").
