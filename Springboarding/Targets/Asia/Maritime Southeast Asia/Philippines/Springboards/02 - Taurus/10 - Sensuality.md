## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kapampangan:** a cuisine known by its own name, and its food memory.
- **Tagalog lowlands:** *kundiman*, *harana* and the fiesta table.
- **Ilocano:** a regional cooking tradition with its own dishes and taste words, to be documented on its own terms.
- **Bicolano:** a regional cuisine and song tradition usually written about apart from Manila's.
- **Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Waray Visayans:** their own taste words, song traditions and coastal foods.
- **Maguindanaon, Maranao and Tausug:** *kulintang* music, court dances and spiced cooking, under Islamic norms of display and drink.
- **Ifugao and other Cordillera peoples:** rice wine, feasts, chant.
- **T'boli, Yakan and Mangyan:** weaving, beadwork and verse as crafts of touch and sight.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** a parallel food culture.
- **Generations:** older modesty norms against online "aesthetic" and food-video culture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, music and dance; mixed for touch and scent; thin for minority sensory vocabularies beyond music and textile studies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish; Kapampangan, Ilocano, Bikol, Visayan languages; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug; Ifugao; Lan-nang.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sexual content and censorship; sacred music and dances performed for tourists; ritual drink in Cordillera ceremonies; Islamic norms on music and alcohol vary by community and should not be generalized.
- **Held for review:** none.
