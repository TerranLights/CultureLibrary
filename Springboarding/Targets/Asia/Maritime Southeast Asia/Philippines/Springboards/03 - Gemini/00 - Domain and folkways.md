## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila against the Tagalog provinces** (Batangas, Bulacan, Quezon): standard against regional Tagalog.
- **Ilocanos** and the **Cordillera peoples** (Ifugao, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Bontoc, Kalinga, Isneg): oral epics (the Ifugao *hudhud*) and upland schooling.
- **Kapampangan** and **Pangasinan** speakers: literary languages between Tagalog and Ilocano.
- **Bikolanos** and the **Visayan peoples** (Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Kinaray-a, Aklanon): regional language pride against Tagalog-based Filipino.
- **Bangsamoro peoples** (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama, Badjao): Islamic schooling (*madrasah*), Arabic-script writing and the Maranao *darangen*.
- **Lumad peoples** (Manobo, T'boli, B'laan, Subanen, Higaonon, Mandaya), the **Mangyan** of Mindoro, Palawan's **Tagbanwa** and **Pala'wan**, the **Ayta** and other Negrito groups, the **Ivatan** of Batanes: small-community talk and schooling.
- **Chavacano** speakers (Zamboanga, Cavite) and **Chinese Filipinos** (Hokkien homes, Chinese schools): contact languages and separate school systems.
- **Deaf Filipinos** (Filipino Sign Language communities).
- Overseas Filipino households, as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Tagalog, English and Manila; mixed for Visayan and Ilocano; thin and often outsider-written for Lumad, Negrito and Mangyan peoples.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sheet and the rest of the sign are written for the core archipelago.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English first; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Spanish for 1565-1898 records; some Arabic-script and Hokkien reading for Mindanao and Chinese Filipino sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some epics and chants are performed only in set contexts; follow the communities and the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples' consent rules (*check*). Language politics is contested; give each side.
- **Held for review:** pointers on loanword origins and on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
