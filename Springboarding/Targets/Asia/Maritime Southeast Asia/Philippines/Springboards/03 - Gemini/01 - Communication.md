# Philippines: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Bikol, Maranao, Tausug, Philippine Hokkien, Filipino Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Batangueño, Caviteño and Manila Tagalog speakers:** local reputations for bluntness or softness in speech; test each, never assume it.
- **Ilocanos and Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Bontoc, Kalinga):** elders' councils and settlement talk where measured speech carries weight.
- **Cebuano and Hiligaynon speakers:** a Visayan contrast in tone and in how Tagalog-coded politeness is received.
- **Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon:** honor, kin and verbal art in a different etiquette of address.
- **Lumad and Mangyan communities:** the *ambahan* and other indirect forms where hints carry what plain speech would not.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** Hokkien, English and Filipino chosen by setting, inside the family and in business.
- **Deaf Filipinos:** FSL against signed English in schools.
- **Call-center workers** against other urban workers: a trained register against home speech.
- **Generations:** texters of the 2000s, today's chat users, and elders who prefer calls.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich on Tagalog and English, on texting and on call centers; thinner on everyday talk in the regional languages and almost none recorded for many small Indigenous languages.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano and Ilocano for regional media; Spanish for early grammars; FSL resources for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some sung verse is used in courtship and mourning and should not be recorded without consent. The older "smooth interpersonal relations" reading of Filipino talk is contested by Filipino scholars; treat it as a debate, not a description.
- **Held for review:** none.
