## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila** against **provincial towns** served mainly by AM radio and local papers: national against local news worlds.
- **Cebu, Davao, Iloilo and Baguio:** regional press centers with their own outlets and languages.
- **Bangsamoro areas** (Maguindanao, Lanao, Sulu): conflict reporting, risk to journalists, and mosque and clan networks as channels.
- **Cordillera and Lumad communities:** collective rules on who may share community knowledge with outsiders.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** Chinese-language dailies and business networks.
- **Remote island and upland areas (GIDA):** weak signal and few outlets.
- **Generations and classes:** heavy social-media users against television and radio audiences; English-language broadsheet readers against tabloid readers.
- **Overseas Filipino families,** as comparison only: news relayed home through relatives abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on disinformation, press freedom and national surveys; mixed for regional media; thin for how small Indigenous communities pass news.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano and Ilocano for provincial radio; Chinese for the Chinese-language press; Spanish for pre-1898 papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** journalist killings, red-tagging and political trolling are live and dangerous subjects; protect interviewees. Indigenous knowledge needs community consent. The ABS-CBN franchise denial (2020) is contested; present each side.
- **Held for review:** none.
