## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilocano farm families** against **Metro Manila middle-class households:** chores and watching against tutoring and enrichment classes.
- **Fishing communities** (Visayan coasts, Sama-Bajau): learning the sea from childhood, often outside school.
- **Muslim Filipinos** (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan): madrasah and public school side by side.
- **Lumad peoples** (Manobo, T'boli, Higaonon, Subanen) and **Cordillera peoples:** community schools and learning from land and elders.
- **Ayta and Mangyan children:** distance to school and language at school entry.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** two curricula and long school days.
- **Kapampangan, Pangasinan and Bikol speakers:** learning first in a home language that is not Tagalog.
- **Deaf learners:** FSL in schools.
- **Children of overseas workers** (left-behind households), as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on test scores, school policy and Manila; mixed on home learning in regional languages; thin and outsider-heavy for many Indigenous peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Ilocano and Cebuano for village ethnography; Arabic and Malay-script reading for madrasah materials; Spanish for colonial archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Lumad schools are tied to armed conflict and red-tagging; handle with care. Some masters' knowledge is taught only to chosen learners; follow the community's terms.
- **Held for review:** none.
