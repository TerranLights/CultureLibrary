# Philippines: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Igorot and other Cordillera peoples:** a long history as the object of outsiders' curiosity, from the 1904 fair to rice-terrace and Sagada tourism.
- **Muslim Filipinos** (Tausug, Maranao, Maguindanaon): questions about faith and dress asked by other Filipinos, and their own scholarly traditions of inquiry.
- **Lumad of South Cotabato and the T'boli:** the Tasaday aftermath and research fatigue.
- **Sama-Bajau:** tourists' and photographers' curiosity at sea and in cities.
- **Rural households** against **Metro Manila:** the place of nosy questions about marriage, money and work at reunions; vary by setting, never one norm.
- **Kapampangan and Bicolano** folk-belief zones: local limits on entering places held by spirits.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** questions about origins asked by other Filipinos.
- **Science-high-school students** against ordinary public-school students: channeled against unchanneled curiosity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for colonial curiosity about Filipinos and for national museums and media; thin for everyday questioning norms in regional languages.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English; Cebuano and Ilocano; Spanish and Italian for early sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnographic photographs and human-zoo material show real people; use with communities' views. Some places and sky lore are restricted by community custom.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read a modern annotated edition of Pigafetta for the passages on questions and exchanges in Cebu.
2. Search university catalogs for "Tasaday" "Hemley" and "Tasaday controversy" (Hemley's *Invented Eden*, 2003 *check*).
3. Search the National Museum and Mind Museum sites for annual visitor figures and school-tour programs.
4. Run Google Trends, Philippines by region, for "bakit", "paano" and "ngano", five-year span.
5. Search the NCIP site for its guidelines on research and documentation in ancestral domains.
