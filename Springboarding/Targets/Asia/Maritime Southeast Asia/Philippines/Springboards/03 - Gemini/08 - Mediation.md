*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug:** who settles a *rido* (elders, *datu*, religious leaders, officials), and whether a payment seals it.
- **Kalinga, Bontoc and Ifugao:** peace pacts and go-betweens between villages.
- **Lumad peoples** (Manobo, Higaonon, B'laan): their own elders' councils for settling disputes.
- **Tagalog and Visayan lowland barangays:** the barangay captain and *lupon* as the first stop for neighbors.
- **Catholic and Muslim communities in mixed towns:** interfaith go-betweens.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** clan and business associations that settle disputes among members.
- **Workers and employers in industrial zones:** formal labor conciliation.
- **Families of workers abroad:** recruitment brokers as intermediaries (overseas side as comparison only).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Mindanao conflict management and the peace process; mixed on everyday family go-betweens; thin on Lumad practice.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano; Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug for feud settlement; Kalinga and Ifugao terms via ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan feuds are ongoing and dangerous; never name parties to live feuds. Peace talks are politically contested. The letter of barangay conciliation and of customary law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
