# Philippines: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Ilocano, Maranao; Filipino gay lingo (Swardspeak); Spanish for colonial church sources. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct, with rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban poor neighborhoods in Metro Manila** against **gated subdivisions:** shared walls and pumps against walls and guards.
- **Small towns in the Tagalog and Bikol regions:** fiesta and church talk, *aswang* rumor in some places.
- **Visayan fishing villages:** news passed at the shore and the market.
- **Maranao and Tausug communities:** honor-sensitive talk and its consequences.
- **Cordillera villages:** small, close communities with their own sanctions.
- **Bakla and wider LGBT communities:** *chika* as shared speech.
- **Students and young workers online** against older relatives in group chats.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** talk inside a close business community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on showbiz media, online shaming and folklore; mixed on everyday gossip; thin on minority languages' terms and practices.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Bikol for village studies; Spanish for confession manuals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gossip research touches named, living people; anonymize. Rumor can still endanger people accused of being *aswang* or witches. Defamation and cyber-libel are matters for Libra's letter of the law.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the National Library and university catalogs for "confesonario" with "tagalog".
2. Read *Noli Me Tangere* for scenes of town talk, listing who speaks about whom.
3. Read Meñez (1996) on the *aswang* and Lansdale (1972) on its wartime use, side by side.
4. Download GSHS Philippines fact sheets for all rounds and pull the bullying items.
5. Run Google Trends for "Marites" by region, 2020 to now.
