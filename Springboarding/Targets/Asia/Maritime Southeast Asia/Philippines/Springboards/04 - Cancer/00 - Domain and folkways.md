## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila and the Tagalog provinces:** the national media voice; city and provincial households differ.
- **Ilocanos, Kapampangans, Pangasinenses, Bikolanos:** own languages, town elites and migration histories.
- **Cebuanos, Boholanos, Hiligaynon and Waray speakers:** the Visayan majority is several cultures, not one.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Bontok, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Kalinga, Isnag, Itneg) and the Ivatan:** distinct house forms, dormitories and lineages.
- **Mangyan and Palawan peoples (Tagbanwa, Pala'wan, Batak):** upland swidden households.
- **Moro peoples (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama, Sama Dilaut):** noble houses, Islamic family order, stilt and boat dwelling.
- **Lumad peoples (Manobo groups, Subanen, T'boli, Blaan, Teduray, Mandaya, Mamanwa) and Ayta, Agta and Dumagat bands.**
- **Chinese Filipinos, Chavacano speakers, OFW households:** own kin terms; homes rebuilt around a member abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lowland Christian households; mixed for Moro and Lumad peoples; thin and outsider-written for small upland groups. Much early ethnography is colonial-era: read critically.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao, Tausug, Maguindanaon (often in Jawi); Hokkien for Chinese Filipino papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some upland ritual and genealogical knowledge belongs to its keepers; armed conflict in parts of Mindanao affects fieldwork; the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled (the tracker records no change), so this sheet and the sign's other sheets are written for the core archipelago.
- **Held for review:** none.
