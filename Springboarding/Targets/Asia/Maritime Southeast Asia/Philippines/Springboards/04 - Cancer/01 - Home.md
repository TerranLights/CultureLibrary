# Philippines: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila informal settlers against gated-subdivision and condominium residents:** the sharpest split in what a home is and who may stay.
- **Provincial towns with ancestral houses (Vigan, Taal, Silay, Iloilo, Cebu):** colonial-era house culture still standing.
- **Ilocos and other high-migration provinces:** houses built with overseas earnings, often empty for years.
- **Ivatan (Batanes):** stone houses on wind-swept islands.
- **Ifugao, Bontok and Kalinga:** home tied to terraces, wards and granaries.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** the noble house and the lake or river settlement.
- **Sama and Sama Dilaut (Tawi-Tawi, Zamboanga):** stilt villages over water and former boat dwellers.
- **Mangyan (Mindoro) and Pala'wan:** upland homes that move with the swidden.
- **Marawi residents displaced since 2017:** home as a place still not returned to.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on house forms, heritage houses and housing policy; mixed on what home feels like (mostly literature and media); thin on Lumad and small upland peoples' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano and Hiligaynon for regional press; Maranao and Tausug for Moro homes; Spanish for colonial building records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** demolitions and evictions are politically charged; Marawi's return is unresolved; house-raising rites of upland peoples may be theirs to share or not. Property and tenure law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. In PSA OpenSTAT, pull APIS tenure and floor-material tables by region for the latest round.
2. Read Zialcita and Tinio for the room names of the *bahay na bato*, then search the same names in Alcina and in San Buenaventura's 1613 Tagalog *Vocabulario*.
3. Search the NCCA's Philippine Registry of Cultural Property for "torogan", "Ivatan house" and "bale".
4. Search Philippine Studies and the Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society for "home" with "OFW", "balikbayan" and "uwian".
5. Search YouTube for "bahay tour probinsya" and "OFW house tour" and note how the homes are presented.
