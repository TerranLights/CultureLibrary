## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Central Luzon lowlanders:** the national template for kin terms; test it rather than assume it.
- **Ilocanos:** own sibling and kin vocabulary; long migration chains that stretch families.
- **Cebuanos, Hiligaynon and Waray speakers:** separate kin vocabularies and fiesta-based reunions.
- **Bikolanos:** in-law and marriage obligations documented in ethnography.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** noble descent lines and recorded genealogies.
- **Tausug and Sama:** kindreds traced through both parents; island-to-island kin networks.
- **Ifugao, Kalinga and Bontok:** kin groups tied to fields and to peace pacts between villages.
- **Subanen, Manobo and T'boli:** smaller-scale Lumad kin systems with few published sources.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** surname associations and family firms.
- **Provincial political families:** the family as an office-holding unit.
- **LGBTQ Filipinos and their chosen families:** families outside the parish definition.
- **OFW households:** a family run across borders, with the Philippine side as the base.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on lowland Christian kinship and on elite families; mixed on Moro peoples (much in Jawi or oral); thin for many Lumad groups.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Tausug, Maranao; Hokkien for Chinese Filipino records; Spanish for baptismal and marriage registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political dynasties are a live controversy; noble genealogies may be guarded by their houses; family law (marriage, annulment, inheritance) goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
