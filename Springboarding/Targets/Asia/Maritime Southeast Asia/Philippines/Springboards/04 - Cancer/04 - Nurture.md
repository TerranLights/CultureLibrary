# Philippines: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino (Tagalog), English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Tausug and Maguindanaon (Moro schooling), Hanunó'o Mangyan, Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilocano and Panay farming villages:** the two classic ethnographic cases; test whether they still hold.
- **Urban middle-class families with live-in *yaya* or *kasambahay* help:** nurture partly handed to paid carers.
- **Children of overseas workers raised by grandparents or aunts:** nurture split between kin at home and a parent abroad.
- **Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon families:** madrasah schooling and Qur'an reading alongside public school.
- **Cordillera peoples:** learning in field and ward, now changed by schooling.
- **Agta, Ayta and Dumagat foragers:** shared care in small mobile groups.
- **Bugkalot (Ilongot) and other Sierra Madre peoples:** distinct ideas of growing into adulthood.
- **Hanunó'o and other Mangyan:** advice verse in their own script.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** separate schools and kin expectations.
- **Fathers who stay home or raise children alone:** a group most sources skip.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on lowland child rearing and child nutrition; mixed on Moro families (some studies, many in Filipino or Arabic-script materials); thin on most Lumad peoples' own teachings.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Tausug, Maranao, Maguindanaon; Hanunó'o for the ambahan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** studies of poor families can read as blame; discipline and corporal punishment go to Protection here and to Capricorn for authority; Mangyan verse belongs to its keepers, so follow the Heritage Center's terms.
- **Held for review:** none.
