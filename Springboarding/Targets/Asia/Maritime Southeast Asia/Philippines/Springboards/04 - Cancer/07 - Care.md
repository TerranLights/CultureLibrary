*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Central Luzon families:** the *bantay* at the hospital bed; elders at home.
- **Cebuanos and Siquijor:** the *mananambal* tradition beside clinics.
- **Ilocanos:** elders left with one child while siblings work abroad.
- **Subanen and Teduray:** household care in small upland communities.
- **Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon:** Islamic duties toward the sick and the old, and own healers.
- **Cordillera peoples:** rites for the sick, as communities share them, beside public health care.
- **Urban middle class with *kasambahay* or hired caregivers:** paid care in the home.
- **Remote islands and upland barangays:** distance from hospitals shifts care onto kin and healers.
- **Families of OFW caregivers and nurses:** care given abroad while kin at home care for their children and elders.
- **Men as carers:** sons, husbands and fathers who tend the sick, often left out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on nursing, caregiving migration and folk healing; mixed on elder care (new surveys); thin on care within Moro and Lumad households in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Tausug, Maranao; Subanen and Teduray terms through ethnographies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some healing rites are guarded by their practitioners; folk healing can be cast as backward, so read surveys of it critically; elder neglect and abuse cases need care. Health-care law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the LSAHP reports for "living arrangements" and "caregiver" tables by region.
2. Search the *Philippine Journal of Nursing* and *Philippine Population Review* for "family caregiver" and "*bantay*".
3. Read Brewer's chapters on healers, then search Spanish accounts in Blair and Robertson for *babaylan* and *catalonan*.
4. Search YouTube and Facebook for "hilot" and "albularyo" with province names to see how healers present their work.
5. Search Oxfam's site for "Household Care Survey Philippines" and list sites and years.
