*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **High-migration provinces (Ilocos, Pangasinan, Batangas, Iloilo):** many children raised by kin while parents work abroad.
- **Angeles, Clark and Olongapo (Kapampangan and Zambales areas):** Amerasian children of departed servicemen.
- **Metro Manila, Cebu and Davao street children:** left, run away or pushed out.
- **Elders in poor urban households:** left at hospitals, or alone after children move.
- **Moro communities:** displaced families split by conflict, and orphans of war.
- **Lumad and Ayta communities:** children separated from parents by schooling, displacement or work.
- **Sama Dilaut:** families scattered between islands and cities.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** children of mixed marriages or second households, and how they are acknowledged.
- **Japanese-Filipino children:** in the Philippines with absent fathers abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich on left-behind children of migrants and on street children; mixed on elder abandonment; thin on how Moro and Lumad families speak of being left.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Kapampangan; Spanish for Hospicio and parish records; Japanese for some Japanese-Filipino children's records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child cases involve minors; Amerasian and Japanese-Filipino children face stigma; birth outside marriage, mistresses and the absence of divorce are touchy topics (family and support law goes to Libra).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Scalabrini Migration Center site for "children left behind" and list the surveys, years and regions.
2. Read Parreñas's chapters on fathers and mothers abroad and note the Filipino terms her interviewees used.
3. Ask the Hospicio de San Jose about access to its admission records for foundlings, by decade.
4. Search the National Authority for Child Care's reports for "abandoned" and "foundling" counts.
5. Search YouTube for "Raffy Tulfo sustento" and "MMK iniwan" to sample how cases are presented.
