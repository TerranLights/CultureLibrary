## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog Metro Manila and Southern Tagalog:** media and pageant center, so national "fun" is often its local version.
- **Cebuano and Boholano Central Visayas:** Sinulog and a separate music and print scene.
- **Hiligaynon, Karay-a and Aklanon Western Visayas:** Dinagyang and Ati-Atihan, festivals built on imitating the Ati.
- **Ilocano north:** its own verse duel (*bukanegan*), weekly magazine and town feasts.
- **Kapampangan and Bikol:** Holy Week and fiesta forms with their own literatures.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kalinga, Bontoc, Kankanaey, Ibaloi):** prestige feasts and Hudhud chanting.
- **Bangsamoro (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama):** Islamic calendar celebrations, kulintang and boat display.
- **Lumad peoples (T'boli, Manobo, Bagobo, Subanen, B'laan) and Mangyan:** festivals often staged for outsiders; separate those from internal events.
- **Ayta, Agta and Ati:** often borrowed in others' festivals.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** Lunar New Year, mahjong and their own clubs.
- **Chavacano speakers in Zamboanga and Cavite:** a Spanish-creole festive vocabulary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lowland Christian fiestas, pageants and media; mixed for Bangsamoro and Lumad; thin outside festivals staged for tourism.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the islands now carried.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao and Tausug for the Bangsamoro; colonial Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** festivals imitating Ati or other Indigenous peoples; staged "cultural shows"; cockfighting and betting; some ritual songs and chants not for outside use.
- **Held for review:** none.
