## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila youth online:** the loudest digital voice, likely unlike the provinces.
- **Cebuano speakers:** choosing Cebuano over Filipino as a statement in media and politics.
- **Ilocano and Maranao oral poets:** set forms for saying what is otherwise held back.
- **Kalinga and other Cordillera peoples:** tattoo as identity, now also a tourist draw.
- **Bangsamoro women and men:** dress, veil and the *malong* in their own words, including disagreement among them.
- ***Bakla*, trans women and lesbian communities:** expression that brings both visibility and risk.
- **The Deaf community:** a separate language and its own expressive forms.
- **Evangelical and Iglesia ni Cristo congregations:** testimony and dress rules that pull in different directions.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** expectations about speaking up that may differ between generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gay culture, social media and verbal contests; thin for rural emotional display and for minority languages.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano; gay lingo; Filipino Sign Language; Maranao and Ilocano for oral forms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tattoo designs tied to particular families and deeds; SOGIE (sexual orientation and gender identity) topics; killings linked to karaoke songs. Speech rights as law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
