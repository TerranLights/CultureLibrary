## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban professionals in Manila, Cebu and Davao:** titles and licensure as public standing.
- **Public-school families in the provinces:** Recognition Day and the tarpaulin as family events.
- **BPO workers:** recognition tied to metrics and shifts.
- **Overseas workers' home towns:** honors for absent earners (diaspora itself as comparison only).
- **Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon titled families:** recognition through sultanate titles.
- **Cordillera and Lumad communities:** elders recognized through their own councils, set against NCIP certification.
- **Martial-law survivors and families:** recognition of wrongs rather than of achievement.
- **War survivors, including "comfort women":** recognition never fully given.
- **Chinese Filipino associations:** their own chambers and awards.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for awards, honors lists and school rites; mixed for workplaces; thin for everyday praise in minority languages.
- **Languages needed:** English and Filipino for official sources; Cebuano and Tausug for regional ones; Spanish for colonial titles; Japanese for redress material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law victims and wartime sexual slavery; disputes over whose name goes on memorials. Reparation and redress law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
