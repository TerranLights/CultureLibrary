# Philippines: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Ilocano; Maranao and Arabic religious vocabulary; Philippine Hokkien; Spanish for early colonial texts. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Urban poor of Tondo, Payatas and other Metro Manila settlements:** dignity defended under eviction threat.
- **Negros and Panay farmworkers:** hacienda labor, a history unlike smallholders elsewhere.
- **Maranao families displaced from Marawi:** dignity expressed in Islamic terms and in clan standing.
- **Ayta resettled after the 1991 Pinatubo eruption:** dignity after losing their lands.
- **Waray survivors of Haiyan:** relief, shelter and outside aid.
- **Ilocano and Cordillera elders:** respect for age in households and councils.
- **Domestic and contract workers:** daily courtesy or its absence.
- **Persons deprived of liberty in crowded jails:** bodily dignity at its lowest.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** respectable face (*thé-biān*) as a related but separate idea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for poverty and disaster studies; mixed for old age; thin for minority-language terms of worth.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Waray; Maranao and Arabic religious vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extrajudicial killings; jail conditions; displaced Moro and Lumad communities. Rights law goes to Libra, the purpose of law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
