## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila media and campuses:** satire, critics and online bashing.
- **Tagalog provinces:** indirect forms (*parinig*, *pasaring*) in families and neighborhoods.
- **Ilocano and Pangasinan towns:** family reproach and community talk.
- **Cordillera (Kankanaey, Bontoc, Ifugao):** elders' correction in ward councils.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** *maratabat*, public slights and mediation.
- **Tausug and Sama:** shame and honor in the Sulu islands.
- **Cebuano and Hiligaynon cities:** a strong regional press and radio commentary of their own.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** criticism inside family firms; generation differences.
- **Lumad and Mangyan:** correction through elders and customary councils, as each people describes it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on satire, the press and film criticism; mixed on workplace feedback; thin on how criticism works in minority languages day to day.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano; Maranao and Tausug; Spanish for colonial satire.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** journalist killings and *rido* violence are live dangers; criticizing elders and clergy can carry social cost for informants. Libel and cyberlibel law go to Libra (the letter) and Capricorn (the spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: Bulatao's essay, then Torres's *Rido*, for the face and honor side.
2. Catalog: "duplo" and "Lumbera Tagalog poetry" for the older verse forms.
3. Read McCoy and Roces, then *Pugad Baboy* collections, for satire then and now.
4. SWS site: net satisfaction ratings by area, one administration per decade.
5. CMFR archive: yearly press-freedom reports and journalist-killing counts.
