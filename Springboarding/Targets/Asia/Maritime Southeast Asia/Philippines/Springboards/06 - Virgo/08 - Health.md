## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila:** hospitals, pharmacies, and Quiapo's herb and amulet stalls side by side.
- **Tagalog and Kapampangan provinces:** *albularyo*, *hilot* and town clinics.
- **Siquijor, Bohol and Cebu:** *mananambal* healing and its reputation.
- **Ilocos and the Cordillera (Ifugao, Kankanaey, Kalinga):** ritual healing and terrace-village health.
- **Bikol and Samar-Leyte:** health after typhoons; Haiyan's aftermath.
- **Western Visayas:** Hiligaynon healers and sugar-belt nutrition.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Sama:** Islamic healing practice, the BARMM health ministry and conflict-zone care.
- **Lumad (Manobo, Higaonon, T'boli, Subanen), Mangyan, Ayta:** distance from clinics and their own healers.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** dual systems in the family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on surveys, colonial medicine and folk illness; mixed on healers' own accounts; thin for many Lumad and Negrito peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Tausug and Maranao; Spanish for colonial manuals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing rites and plant knowledge may be restricted; sorcery accusations (*kulam*, *barang*) can stigmatize people and places such as Siquijor and Capiz; leprosy, HIV and mental illness carry stigma. Health law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
