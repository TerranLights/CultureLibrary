# Philippines: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino and English; the main regional, Moro and Indigenous languages; Spanish for colonial records. **Concept in one line:** what a fair bargain and a good partnership are: marriage, contracts, sworn and ritual bonds, open enemies, the settling of quarrels, gender ways, and the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog heartland and Metro Manila:** law, media and the wedding trade center here; one voice among many.
- **Ilocano north:** its own wedding gifts and inheritance customs; marriage across migration distance.
- **Cebuano and the wider Visayas (Hiligaynon, Waray):** own terms for marriage and peace; the early sources treat them apart.
- **Bikol and Kapampangan:** lowland cultures with their own sponsor customs.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kalinga, Bontoc, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Isneg):** customary law and peace pacts with long documentation.
- **Moro peoples (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama, Iranun):** Muslim personal law, Shari'a courts, feud and honor.
- **Lumad peoples (Manobo, T'boli, B'laan, Subanen, Higaonon, Mandaya):** customary settlement under the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act.
- **Mangyan and Palawan peoples:** courtship poetry and dispute ways little seen in national data.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** arranged and intermarriage questions, trade partnerships, clan associations.
- **Chavacano Zamboanga and Christian settlers in Mindanao:** seams where Spanish-derived, Moro and Lumad ways meet.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, marriage statistics and lowland ethnography; mixed for Lumad and Mangyan peoples; the nation's exact lands are still to be settled, so the sheets in this sign are written for the core archipelago.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English for law and media; Spanish for colonial records; Cebuano, Ilocano and Hiligaynon for regional press; Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug (some in Arabic script) for Moro sources; Hokkien for Chinese Filipino records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** live feuds name families; Indigenous customary knowledge falls under consent rules of the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act; partner-violence and annulment cases are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
