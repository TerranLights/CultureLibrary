## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila's urban poor communities:** where anti-drug operations, wakes and church sanctuary efforts meet; the facet of self-help and state violence.
- **Davao City and its region:** local justice politics long tied to national leaders; test against Manila.
- **Ilocos provinces:** rival memories of martial law shape what historical justice means.
- **Negros and other sugar areas:** land reform (Republic Act 6657, 1988) as distributive justice and its conflicts.
- **Moro peoples (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug):** Shari'a courts, the *Luwaran*, and payment for harm between families.
- **Cordillera peoples:** customary hearings of wrongs alongside state courts.
- **Lumad peoples:** customary settlement under the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act, and justice for killed land defenders.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** the kidnapping-for-ransom wave of the 1990s and the community's demands on police and courts.
- **Overseas workers' families (comparison only):** cases abroad that bring justice questions home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, courts and the drug-war record; mixed for customary justice outside the Cordillera; thin for Mangyan and Negrito peoples.
- **Languages needed:** English for judgments; Filipino and regional languages for victims' testimony and media; Arabic script for some Moro sources; Spanish for colonial courts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** killings, red-tagging of activists and martial-law memory are politically live; victims' names need care; ongoing ICC proceedings.
- **Held for review:** none.
