# Philippines: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino for folk medicine and everyday speech; English for constitutional and environmental law; Cebuano for Visayan healing; Ifugao and other Cordillera languages; Maranao for Islamic teaching and design; Hokkien for Chinese medicine shops. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog provinces and Metro Manila:** folk hot-cold medicine beside hospital medicine; test how far *pasma* talk reaches.
- **Siquijor and other Visayan islands:** healers known nationwide; Cebuano terms of moderation.
- **Ifugao and other terrace builders:** woodlots, water shares and rice rites as balance with the land.
- **Hanunuo and other Mangyan peoples:** swidden rest and return.
- **Maranao and other Moro peoples:** design symmetry; the scale in sermons.
- **T'boli and other Lumad weavers:** balance in pattern made from dreams.
- **Chinese Filipinos in Binondo and provincial towns:** herbal shops and hot-cold food rules of another lineage.
- **Night-shift workers in call centers:** the work-sleep balance in the cities' newest trade.
- **Farm households:** balance kept by the planting and harvest year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law and folk medicine; mixed for work-life data; thin for most Lumad and sea peoples outside museum and weaving studies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino; English; Cebuano; Ifugao and other Cordillera languages; Maranao and Arabic terms; Hokkien.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some healers' knowledge is guarded and not for outsiders; ritual chants of the Cordillera belong to their communities; weavers' designs carry rights under the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act.
- **Held for review:** none.
