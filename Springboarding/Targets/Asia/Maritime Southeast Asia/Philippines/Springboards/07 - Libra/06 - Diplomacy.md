# Philippines: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino for everyday tact and indirection; English for foreign policy and peace accords; Cebuano and Hiligaynon for proposal visits; Tausug and Malay for Sulu treaties; Spanish and Italian for first-contact accounts; Hokkien for trade go-betweens. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog speakers:** respect particles and indirect hints in daily speech; test against regions without *po*.
- **Cebuano and Hiligaynon speakers:** proposal visits, poetic debate and their own words for coming to terms.
- **Bohol:** the blood-compact memory as local identity.
- **Tausug and the Sulu islands:** sultanate diplomacy, the Sabah question and ties across the sea.
- **Maguindanaon and Maranao:** decades of peace talks through third-party hosts.
- **Cordillera peoples:** peace-pact holders and token exchanges as the frame for state talks.
- **Lumad peoples:** negotiating with the state, rebels and mining or plantation firms at once.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** go-betweens in trade, and the stakes when relations with China sour.
- **Call-center workers:** scripted courtesy as daily labor.
- **Families of workers abroad:** consular help and appeals to foreign employers; diaspora accounts as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foreign policy and peace accords; mixed for everyday tact (strong theory, fewer large datasets); thin for Lumad negotiations outside advocacy reports.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano and Hiligaynon; Tausug and Malay; Spanish and Italian for early accounts; Hokkien.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Sabah claim and the sea dispute are live state matters; peace-talk records may name combatants; red-tagging affects talks with the left.
- **Held for review:** none.
