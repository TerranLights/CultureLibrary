# Philippines: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino for the words of yielding, enduring and shame; English for statutes and court rulings; Cebuano and Ilocano for patience words; Maranao for honor; Hokkien for harmony; Spanish and Tagalog in Baybayin for the earliest printed teaching. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog speakers:** where *pakikisama* and *hiya* were first studied; check whether younger urban speakers reject them.
- **Cebuano and other Visayan speakers:** their own words for patience and letting go.
- **Ilocano families:** endurance words in a migration-heavy region.
- **Maranao and other Moro peoples:** honor (*maratabat*) that can demand an answer rather than yielding.
- **Cordillera peoples:** peace kept by pacts with explicit terms, a contrast with peace kept by silence.
- **Lumad and Negros communities:** zones of peace between state forces and rebels.
- **Women in violent homes:** where village settlement and family pressure meet the law.
- **Contract workers:** short-term jobs that make speaking up risky.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** harmony norms in family firms, and the quiet handling of past kidnappings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; the older social-psychology literature is large; legal and survey data on reporting are newer; thin on how Moro and Lumad communities weigh yielding.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Cebuano, Ilocano; Maranao; Hokkien; Spanish and old Tagalog for the 1593 text.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases and desistance involve minors and victims; payments to armed groups are crimes and dangerous to research; avoid treating *pakikisama* as a national trait.
- **Held for review:** none.
