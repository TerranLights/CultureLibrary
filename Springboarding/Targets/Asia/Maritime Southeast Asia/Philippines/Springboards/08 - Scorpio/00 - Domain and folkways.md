## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Central Luzon lowlands:** the Manila-centered model the national media spreads.
- **Ilocano:** distinct lament, offering and mourning sequences; strong land-inheritance concerns.
- **Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Waray Visayans:** separate words and rites for the souls' days; sugar and fishing economies shape shared resources.
- **Bikol:** a well-studied case of mediums and of the dead in daily life.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Kalinga, Bontoc):** secondary burial, prestige feasts and inherited fields.
- **Muslim peoples of Mindanao and Sulu (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama):** quick burial, Islamic inheritance and rank by lineage.
- **Lumad peoples (Manobo, T'boli, Subanen, Teduray, B'laan):** their own death and pooling customs, as they share them.
- **Mangyan of Mindoro and the peoples of Palawan (Tagbanua, Pala'wan, Batak):** small-scale sharing, separate scripts.
- **Aeta and Agta:** forager customs; post-Pinatubo displacement.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** lineage, ancestor halls and separate cemeteries.
- **Overseas Filipino families (comparison only):** remittance pooling and bodies sent home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lowland Catholic death ways, vital statistics and sex surveys; mixed for inheritance practice; thin and community-held for many Indigenous and Muslim rites.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Spanish for colonial archives; Maranao, Tausug and Maguindanaon (often in Jawi script) for Muslim sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cordillera burial sites and human remains; *rido* and martial-law deaths; the drug-war dead. Inheritance and sex law go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the present archipelago.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
