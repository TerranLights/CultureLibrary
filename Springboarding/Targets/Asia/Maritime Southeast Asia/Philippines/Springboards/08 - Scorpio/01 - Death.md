## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog lowlands:** polite death vocabulary, nine-day prayers and the anniversary that ends mourning; the national template on television.
- **Ilocano:** the sung lament and offerings to the dead; a separate verbal art of grief.
- **Bikol:** relations with the dead through mediums and saints.
- **Waray of Eastern Visayas:** mass death after Haiyan; burial without kin present.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kankanaey, Ibaloi):** long rites, secondary burial and seated or smoked dead.
- **Ilongot (Bugkalot):** a people whose grief was studied in depth by outsiders; their own voice needed beside it.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug:** burial within a day, Islamic teaching on the grave and judgment.
- **Ivatan of Batanes:** island burial traditions, including stone boat-shaped markers *check*.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** ancestor rites, tomb-sweeping and the dead as household members.
- **Urban poor and families of the drug-war dead:** violent death, debt-funded wakes, mourning as protest.
- **Families of overseas workers who die abroad (comparison only):** the body returned in a box; delayed rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lowland Catholic practice, ghost lore and violent-death counts; mixed for end-of-life care; thin for Lumad and Sama views of death in their own words.
