# Philippines: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Kapampangan Holy Week towns:** bodily vows, reenactment, a yearly cycle of dying and rising.
- **Marinduque:** a single island's pageant of conversion.
- **Catholic charismatics, evangelicals and Iglesia ni Cristo members:** three different ideas of whether rebirth is sudden or gradual.
- **Balik-Islam converts and Muslim-born Maranao, Tausug and Maguindanaon:** what "return" means on each side.
- **Ilocano:** the epic of a revived hero in a region with its own oral canon.
- **Kalinga and Mangyan:** revival led by the people themselves, under tourism and market pressure.
- **Boholanos and Pinatubo-area Aeta:** rebuilding after earthquake and eruption, from different footing.
- **Drug surrenderers and prisoners:** rebirth pressed on them by the state.
- **Chinese Filipino evangelical congregations:** conversion away from family ancestor rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Holy Week and Christian renewal; mixed for Muslim converts and drug recovery; thin and community-held for Lumad passage rites.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Ilocano, Kapampangan, Cebuano; Chinese for Chinese Filipino churches; Maranao and Tausug for Muslim sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug surrenderers (coerced "rehabilitation"); conversion across faith lines; tattoo and initiation knowledge that belongs to Kalinga and other peoples. Legal status of drug programs goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Ileto, *Pasyon and Revolution*; Wiegele, *Investing in Miracles*; any annotated *Biag ni Lam-ang* edition.
2. Search news archives for "Cutud crucifixion" and "Moriones" by year, for continuity and change.
3. Dangerous Drugs Board site: national survey report; then "community-based rehabilitation surrenderers" in *Philippine Sociological Review*.
4. Mangyan Heritage Center site: script and *ambahan* programs; contact for school curricula.
5. Search "Balik-Islam" in Philippine university repositories (Ateneo de Davao, Mindanao State University).
