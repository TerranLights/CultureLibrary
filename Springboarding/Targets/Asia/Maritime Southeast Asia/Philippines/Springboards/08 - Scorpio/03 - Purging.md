# Philippines: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog lowlands:** confession, Lenten fasting and the vocabulary of folk illness most often studied.
- **Siquijor and other Visayan healing communities:** herbal and steam cleansing as a regional craft.
- **Ilocano:** cleansing after burial; a sharp line between the house of the dead and the living.
- **Bikol and Eastern Visayas:** healers and mediums who draw out harm.
- **Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon:** ritual purity, fasting and washing set by Islamic teaching.
- **Cordillera peoples:** cleansing rites after death or misfortune, as they share them.
- **Boracay residents and Manila Bay shore dwellers:** removed in the name of cleaning the place.
- **Iglesia ni Cristo members:** a church with its own expulsion practice.
- **Urban poor in drug-war barangays:** targets of "cleansing" rhetoric.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** new-year cleaning and bad-luck customs on their own calendar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial sanitation and political exposés; mixed for folk healing (much is sold to tourists); thin for Lumad and Sama cleansing rites.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Cebuano, Ilocano; Spanish for friars' manuals; Tausug and Maranao.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug-war killings and survivors; church expulsions; healers' own knowledge, which some keep within lineages. Anti-corruption law goes to Libra; what reform is for goes to Capricorn.
