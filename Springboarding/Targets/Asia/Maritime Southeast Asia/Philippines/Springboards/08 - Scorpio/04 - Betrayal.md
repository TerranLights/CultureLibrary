## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog lowlands:** Passion verse and school-canon tales shape a shared script of betrayal.
- **Kapampangan, especially Macabebe:** a town remembered for scouts who helped capture Aguinaldo (1901); a regional stigma to research carefully, not repeat.
- **Ilocano:** the press label "Solid North" as a claim to test; whether the betrayal charge in national politics follows regional lines.
- **Tausug:** the Jabidah memory and honor codes in Sulu.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** clan loyalty, defection between armed groups.
- **Members and former members of the communist movement:** informers, purges and the turned cadre.
- **Families of overseas workers:** left-behind spouses and children.
- **Politicians and their followers:** party switching treated as betrayal or as normal practice.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** business partnership and family firm loyalty.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, film and political history; mixed for measured infidelity; thin for Lumad accounts of betrayal by outsiders in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Kapampangan, Ilocano, Cebuano; Spanish for revolution-era documents; Tausug for Sulu sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional stigma (Macabebe); informers in insurgencies, whose names can still endanger people; infidelity and paternity cases on air. Treason and adultery law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Steinberg, *Philippine Collaboration*; Garcia, *To Suffer Thy Comrades*; a critical edition of the *Pasyon*.
2. Search "Jabidah" with "Moro" in *Philippine Studies* and Moro authors' histories.
3. Comelec site: candidate lists by year for one province; track party labels for the same names.
4. Search YouTube for *Raffy Tulfo in Action* "kabit" and "nag-abandona", then code complaints by region.
5. Search "Macabebe" with "historiography" to find how the town's own historians answer the charge.
