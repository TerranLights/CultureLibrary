## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog provinces around Manila:** seat of the national church and universities; Quiapo and Antipolo pilgrimages.
- **Central and Eastern Visayas (Cebuano, Boholano, Waray):** the Santo Niño devotion and *babaylan* memory.
- **Western Visayas (Hiligaynon, Kinaray-a, Aklanon):** Protestant colleges and the epic tradition of central Panay.
- **Ilocos and the Cagayan Valley:** the Aglipayan church's strongest early base, against Catholic and Protestant neighbors.
- **Bangsamoro (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Sama, Yakan):** Islam as the founding faith, with its own learning and pilgrimage ties to the Arab world.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Bontoc, Kalinga):** ancestral rites alongside Anglican and Catholic missions.
- **Lumad of Mindanao (Manobo groups, Higaonon, Subanen, T'boli, Blaan, Teduray):** distinct cosmologies under settler pressure.
- **Mangyan and Palawan peoples:** script-keeping verse on Mindoro; separate highland faiths on Palawan.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** temples, Christian schools and mixed practice in one community.
- **Iglesia ni Cristo, Evangelical and Pentecostal members:** congregations that cut across region.
- Diaspora (Gulf states, North America): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Catholic majority; mixed for Islam; thin for many Lumad and Palawan peoples, often known only through outsiders' ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Spanish for colonial archives; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon for regional media; Maranao, Tausug, Arabic for Islamic sources; Hokkien and Mandarin for temples.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous rites and chants are held by specialists and not for open circulation; follow each people's own consent rules. Religious-political tensions (bloc voting, the Mindanao conflict) need neutral handling. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the core archipelago.
- **Held for review:** none.
