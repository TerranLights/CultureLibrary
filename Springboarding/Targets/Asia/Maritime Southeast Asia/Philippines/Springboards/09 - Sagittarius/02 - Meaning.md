# Philippines: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao and Tausug; Mangyan languages; Hokkien; Spanish for older lexicons. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of overseas workers (Ilocos, Batangas, Pangasinan, Iloilo):** meaning tied to remittance and absence.
- **Central Luzon and Negros farm workers:** land, debt and struggle as the frame of a life's worth.
- **Metro Manila and Cebu call-center workers:** night-shift work and the search for purpose in a new middle class.
- **Clergy, religious sisters and lay missionaries:** vocation as a lifelong calling.
- **Martial-law-era activists and their children:** meaning given to a cause, and what came after.
- **Cordillera communities (Kalinga, Bontoc):** meaning tied to ancestral land and resistance to dam and mining projects.
- **Maranao after the 2017 Marawi siege:** rebuilding purpose after displacement.
- **Hanunuo and Buhid Mangyan:** verse as a way of holding life's lessons.
- **Chinese Filipino family-business heirs:** duty to lineage against personal calling.
- Diaspora (seafarers, Gulf workers): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in theology, psychology and fiction, thinner in surveys that ask about meaning directly.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino for the indigenous-psychology literature; Cebuano and Ilocano for regional voices; Tausug and Hanunuo for verse; Spanish for colonial lexicons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and despair need care; *parang sabil* touches holy-war violence and must be read in context, without stereotype.
- **Held for review:** none.
