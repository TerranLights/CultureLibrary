# Philippines: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog, English, Spanish (revolutionary-era texts), Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug and Arabic; Cordillera languages; Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog revolutionary heartland (Cavite, Bulacan, Batangas):** shrines and family memory of 1896.
- **Central Luzon tenant farmers (Nueva Ecija, Pampanga, Tarlac):** freedom from landlord debt.
- **Negros sugar workers and *sacadas*:** seasonal labor and its dependencies.
- **Manila student movements (UP, the 1970 First Quarter Storm):** speech and protest.
- **Maguindanaon and Maranao:** self-determination after the sultanates.
- **Tausug of Sulu:** a long memory of resisting Spanish, American and national rule.
- **Kalinga, Bontoc and other Cordillera peoples:** land and the right to refuse projects.
- **Lumad of eastern Mindanao:** community schools and military pressure.
- **Sama Dilaut:** freedom of movement at sea across modern borders.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** a long road to citizenship and its memory.
- Diaspora (Gulf domestic workers): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the revolution, martial law and press freedom; mixed for Moro and Cordillera movements; thin for Lumad accounts in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Tagalog and Spanish for 1890s texts; English for American-era and current sources; Maguindanaon, Maranao and Tausug for Moro voices; Kalinga and Bontoc for highland testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law memory is politically contested; red-tagging of activists and Lumad schools makes some sources risky to name or contact; armed movements need neutral wording.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search "Liwanag at Dilim" with "Jacinto" and "kalayaan" for a full Tagalog text.
2. Library catalog: Scott's *Cracks in the Parchment Curtain*, then look up *timawa* and *alipin* in San Buenaventura (1613).
3. Bantayog ng mga Bayani website: browse the honorees' biographies by region.
4. CMFR and NUJP: search "press freedom report" for the latest yearly figures.
5. Search "Macli-ing Dulag" with "Cordillera Peoples Alliance" for highland accounts.
