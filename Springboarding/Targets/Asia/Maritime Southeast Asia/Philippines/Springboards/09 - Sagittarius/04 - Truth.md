## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilocos Norte and the Ilocos coast:** the dictatorship's home region, where memory of its record differs.
- **Davao and southern Mindanao:** local media and loyalties shaped by a different political center.
- **Tabloid and AM-radio audiences in Metro Manila:** news through blocktime commentators and Tagalog tabloids.
- **Cebuano-speaking Visayas:** a separate press and radio sphere in the regional language.
- **Bangsamoro communities:** religious scholars (*ulama*) and lineage records as authorities.
- **Cordillera peoples:** oral genealogy and elders' testimony as evidence in disputes.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** Chinese-language papers in Manila as a separate news source.
- **Young online users against older radio listeners:** generational splits in where truth is sought.
- Diaspora (overseas workers in family group chats): comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on disinformation, press and science trust; thin on Indigenous ways of settling facts.
- **Languages needed:** Taglish for online material; Cebuano and Ilocano radio; Tausug and Maranao for *ulama* sources; Spanish for 16th-century accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** journalists and fact-checkers face harassment; martial-law memory is politically charged; name researchers' subjects only as they publish.
- **Held for review:** none.
