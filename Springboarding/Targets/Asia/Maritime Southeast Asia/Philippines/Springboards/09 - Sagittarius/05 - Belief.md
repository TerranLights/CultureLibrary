# Philippines: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Kapampangan, Waray; Maranao, Sama and Arabic; Mangyan languages; Hokkien; Spanish for mission accounts. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilocos towns:** a well-studied mix of Catholic, Aglipayan and folk beliefs.
- **Siquijor, Capiz and other islands outsiders link to sorcery:** reputation against what locals say of themselves.
- **Cebu city and province:** folk healers beside a strong church presence.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal congregations:** spirits reframed as demons to be cast out.
- **Iglesia ni Cristo:** doctrine taught as a fixed set of beliefs.
- **Maranao and Maguindanaon:** reformist teaching against older customary practice.
- **Sama Dilaut and Yakan:** maritime and island beliefs on the margins of formal Islam.
- **Buid, Hanunuo and other Mangyan:** highland belief systems on Mindoro.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** feng shui and temple guidance alongside church schooling.
- **Manila's professional class:** declared skepticism and quiet hedging.
- Diaspora: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief and Catholic belief; mixed for Islamic creed; thin for many small Indigenous peoples outside Mindoro and the Cordillera.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, Cebuano and Ilocano for folk terms; Spanish for mission sources; Arabic and Maranao for creed.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labels of "sorcery" can stigmatize whole islands and families; some Indigenous beliefs are held privately; avoid presenting any belief as a people's single voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
