# Philippines: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog, English, Cebuano, Hiligaynon; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug and Arabic; Spanish for colonial church records. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iglesia ni Cristo members:** a restorationist claim with tight internal discipline.
- **Members Church of God International (Ang Dating Daan):** literal readings argued on air against rival churches.
- **Independent Baptist congregations:** separatism taught as doctrine.
- **Rizalista communities of Quezon and Laguna:** a national hero held as sacred.
- **Maranao youth between traditional *ulama* and reformist teachers:** a dispute over the true practice of Islam.
- **Balik-Islam converts in Manila and Luzon:** a new identity often framed as return to the pure faith.
- **Tausug and Basilan communities:** areas where a small armed current drew notice beyond its numbers.
- **Settler towns in Cotabato and Lanao:** memory of Christian militias in the 1970s.
- **Chinese Filipino evangelical churches:** strict teaching in an urban minority.
- **Catholic traditionalist and charismatic Catholics:** different routes to a "purer" Catholicism.
- Diaspora: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on armed groups (with a security bias); mixed on Christian restorationism; thin on members' own accounts of strict faith.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano for Mindanao settlers; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug and Arabic for Islamic reform; Spanish for colonial archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fundamentalist" and "cult" are charged labels; never tie militancy to Muslims as a whole; several high-control groups and their leaders face live controversies, so cite only published work.
- **Held for review:** none.
