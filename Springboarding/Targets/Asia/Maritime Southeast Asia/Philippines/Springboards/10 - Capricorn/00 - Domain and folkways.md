*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino (Tagalog), English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan; Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug; Cordillera and Lumad languages; colonial Spanish; Philippine Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** the domain question, who commands, who obeys and what is owed, with its folkways of career, status, reputation, age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila and the Tagalog provinces:** the national image is drawn here; separate the capital's offices from Southern Tagalog towns.
- **Central Luzon (Kapampangan, Pangasinan):** the tenancy and Huk-revolt records bear directly on landlord authority.
- **Ilocos and the Ilocano diaspora into Cagayan Valley:** a political base with its own family dynasties and labor migration.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Kalinga, Bontoc, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Isneg):** councils of elders and peace pacts, to set beside chiefly models.
- **Bikol, Western Visayas (Hiligaynon, Kinaray-a), Cebuano lands, Eastern Visayas (Waray):** different hacienda, trading-city and smallholder histories.
- **Bangsamoro (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Sama-Bajau, Yakan, Iranun):** sultanates, *datu* lines and Islamic duty.
- **Lumad of Mindanao (Manobo groups, Tboli, B'laan, Subanen, Teduray, Higaonon, Mandaya):** *datu* and *bagani* roles, tribal councils.
- **Mangyan of Mindoro, Palawan peoples (Pala'wan, Tagbanua, Batak), Aeta and Agta, Ivatan:** small-scale authority, often missing from national data.
- **Chinese Filipinos and Zamboanga's Chavacano speakers:** family-firm and clan hierarchies; a Spanish creole city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Manila, lowland politics and English-language scholarship; mixed for the Visayas and the Bangsamoro; thin for most Lumad, Mangyan and Negrito groups. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the present archipelago.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Spanish for colonial archives; Maranao, Tausug and Arabic for Bangsamoro sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the drug-war killings, martial law (1972-1981) and red-tagging are live and contested; ritual chants and genealogies of Indigenous peoples and royal houses may be held privately; the 1960s values literature is itself disputed.
- **Held for review:** none.
