## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Davao City:** decades of one family's mayoral rule and strict city ordinances make it a test of how far local authority reaches.
- **Metro Manila's urban poor communities:** where police, barangay officials and church meet most often in daily life.
- **Negros and Panay sugar districts:** hacienda owners' authority over workers, with Hiligaynon and Kinaray-a sources.
- **Ilocos and Cagayan Valley:** the reputed "solid North" vote is a claim to test against local sources, not assume.
- **Maranao of Lanao and Tausug of Sulu:** royal houses, *datu* lines and ulama as rival or joined authorities.
- **Maguindanaon and the Bangsamoro government:** a former rebel movement now in office.
- **Cordillera peoples:** elders' councils beside the elected barangay; Kalinga peace-pact holders.
- **Lumad groups (Teduray, Manobo, Higaonon, Subanen):** *timuay*, *datu* and tribal councils recognized under the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act.
- **Areas long contested by the New People's Army:** dual authority; handle with care.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** the authority of parents and elders in family firms.
- **Iglesia ni Cristo members:** a centrally directed church inside a Catholic majority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national politics, the church and the drug war; mixed for Moro authority; thin for Lumad and Mangyan governance except in their own statements.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English first; Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano; Maranao, Tausug, Arabic for sermons and royal-house sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extrajudicial killings, red-tagging of activists and Lumad schools, and insurgency zones put informants at risk; succession disputes in royal houses are live; elders' council proceedings may be closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
