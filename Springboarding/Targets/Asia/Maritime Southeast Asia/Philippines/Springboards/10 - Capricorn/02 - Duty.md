# Philippines: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Kapampangan, Tausug and Maranao (with Arabic religious terms), Ifugao, Spanish (colonial and revolutionary texts), Philippine Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Provinces that send many overseas workers (Ilocos, Pangasinan, Batangas, Iloilo, parts of Mindanao):** duty to kin as migration.
- **Metro Manila's salaried middle class:** where "breadwinner" talk meets apartment life and smaller families.
- **Pampanga and Bulacan Holy Week towns:** the vow tradition at its most public.
- **Waray-speaking Eastern Visayas:** duties of rescue and rebuilding after Typhoon Haiyan (2013).
- **Ifugao and other Cordillera rice communities:** ritual and labor duties tied to the terraces.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug communities:** Islamic obligations alongside *adat* duties to clan.
- **Sama-Bajau:** sea-based communities whose obligations to kin and boat-mates may not fit land-based categories.
- **Lumad communities in Bukidnon and Davao:** duties to land and ancestors, in their own statements.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** duty to parents and to the family business, and ancestral rites.
- **Catholic, Protestant, Iglesia ni Cristo and Muslim households:** differing teaching on what is owed to parents and to God.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on remittances, migration and *utang na loob*; mixed on caregiving; thin for Lumad and Sama-Bajau obligations outside NGO reports.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Kapampangan; Arabic and Tausug or Maranao for Islamic sources; Spanish for 1890s texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family duty and remittances can involve debt bondage and trafficking; Holy Week self-mortification is a private vow for many; some Ifugao chants belong to ritual specialists and should be approached through the communities.
- **Held for review:** none.

1. Read the *Kartilya ng Katipunan* and *El Verdadero Decálogo* side by side, listing every duty each names.

4. Search for "panata" with "Holy Week" and "Cutud" in Philippine journals and newspapers.
5. Search the Longitudinal Study of Ageing and Health in the Philippines reports for "support from children".
