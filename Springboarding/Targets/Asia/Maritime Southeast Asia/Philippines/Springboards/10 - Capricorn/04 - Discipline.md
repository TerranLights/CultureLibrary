*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Davao City and other cities that copied its ordinances:** a local test of rule-bound public life.
- **Metro Manila commuters and street vendors:** the target of most national discipline campaigns.
- **Military and police families, Baguio's academy town:** career discipline across generations.
- **Kankanaey and Bontoc villages:** *inayan* and elders' teaching beside church and school.
- **Hanunuo and other Mangyan of Mindoro:** verse-based correction, outside lowland school norms.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug families:** Ramadan, prayer times and madrasah study.
- **Cebu's eskrima lineages:** craft discipline under a named master.
- **Chinese Filipino families and schools:** a separate school system with its own expectations.
- **Rural farm households in Ilocos, Bicol and the Visayas:** chores and correction outside urban parenting debates.
- **Fraternity and sorority members at universities:** initiation and its defenders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corporal punishment, hazing and state campaigns; mixed for religious discipline; thin for Lumad, Mangyan and Sama-Bajau child-rearing.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Kankanaey and Hanunuo for Cordillera and Mangyan sources; Arabic and Tausug or Maranao for Islamic schooling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child abuse, hazing deaths and martial-law memory; Mangyan verse may be shared by families on their own terms; school abuse cases involve minors.
- **Held for review:** none.
