## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilocos and Cagayan Valley:** a long record of out-migration (Hawaii, the Middle East, Manila) to weigh against those who stay.
- **Metro Manila's urban poor and its new middle class:** call-center and service jobs as a recent ladder to examine.
- **Cebu and Davao business communities:** regional elites who rise outside Manila.
- **Mindanao settler towns (Cebuano and Ilonggo migrants from the 1930s on):** homesteading ambition on land others claimed.
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug families:** titles, trade and education abroad in Muslim lands.
- **Lumad communities:** ambition framed around land claims and schooling, in their own words.
- **Cordillera students in Baguio's schools and universities:** schooling as a road from village to profession.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** firm-building and succession as a family project.
- **Women and men separately:** pageants, nursing and domestic work abroad against seafaring, construction and politics.
- **Pampanga and Central Luzon:** the former U.S. base economy and its job hopes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on migration, pageants, exams and tycoons; mixed on survey measures of aspiration; thin for Lumad and Sama-Bajau youth.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and Taglish, English; Ilocano, Cebuano, Hiligaynon; Maranao for title customs; Hokkien for Chinese Filipino oral histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trafficking and illegal recruitment sit inside migration hopes; investment-scam victims; dynasty research names living families.
- **Held for review:** none.
