## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila professionals and elite private schools:** where exam scores and family names compete for the same jobs.
- **Provincial civil servants (Bicol, Eastern Visayas, Caraga):** hiring where the local government is the main employer and a *backer* may outweigh an eligibility.
- **Military and police officers:** academy class ties against service records in who is promoted.
- **Cebu and Iloilo:** regional universities whose honor rolls and board passers rival Manila's.
- **Manobo, Mandaya and Bagobo:** deeds-based rank beside hereditary leadership, in their own accounts.
- **Kalinga and other Cordillera peoples:** earned warrior standing, later displaced by schooling and office.
- **Maguindanaon and Maranao families:** lineage titles against degrees from Cairo or Manila.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** whether the able heir or the eldest son is chosen, and the weight on Chinese-school grades.
- **Overseas-worker households:** foreign skills tests against hiring by referral at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on civil service, exams, honors and patronage; mixed on surveys; thin for Lumad and Mangyan ideas of desert.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Manobo, Bagobo and Kalinga for warrior-rank material; Maguindanaon and Arabic for religious standing; Spanish and Latin for Alcina.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *bagani* and *mengal* standing was tied to killing in war; use community sources, not colonial sensationalism; some armed Lumad militias use the word *bagani* today. Nepotism and leak cases name living people.
- **Held for review:** none.
