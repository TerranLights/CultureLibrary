## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug communities:** state law, Shari'a courts and *adat* in force at once; which one a family turns to.
- **Teduray, Lambangian and other Lumad:** their own assemblies against both national and Bangsamoro law.
- **Kalinga and Tinggian (Itneg) villages:** *pagta* terms honored beside state courts.
- **Cavite and Cebu:** Sidel's case regions, where bosses and law were long entangled.
- **Tondo, Caloocan and other drug-war hotspots:** police bargaining beside barangay mediation.
- **Negros sugar lands:** land-reform law on paper (CARP, 1988) against its slow use.
- **Chinese Filipinos of Binondo and provincial towns:** private protection after the kidnappings.
- **Catholic clergy and laity:** conscience against statute in fights over the RH Law (2012) and divorce.
- **Lawyers and law students ("Atty." culture):** the profession's own idea of what law is for.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on courts, martial law and corruption; mixed on customary and Shari'a practice; thin for Mangyan, Aeta and Sama legal orders; colonial evasion of Spanish law needs its own archival search (start from Phelan, *The Hispanization of the Philippines*, *check*).
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon; Maranao, Maguindanaon and Arabic; Teduray; Spanish for colonial archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the drug-war killings and the International Criminal Court case remain live; red-tagging endangers sources; customary hearings may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Scott on the Kalantiaw code, then search older textbooks for how it was taught.
2. Search DILG and Supreme Court reports for "Katarungang Pambarangay" settlement figures.
3. Read Schlegel's *Tiruray Justice* chapters on the assembly and its aims.
4. Search *Philippine Law Journal* and *Ateneo Law Journal* for "legal culture" and "dura lex".
5. Search SWS and Pulse Asia for their "death penalty" survey series.
