## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sugar and rice tenants (Negros, Tarlac, Nueva Ecija) and landowning families:** inequality rooted in land.
- **Elite villages of Makati and Alabang beside informal settlers:** inequality laid out in space.
- **Household workers from the Visayas and Mindanao in Manila homes:** rank inside one household, and origin as a marker.
- **Cordillera peoples and Aeta, Agta and Dumagat groups:** stigma, and the question of equal treatment against distinct rights.
- **Moro peoples against Christian-majority provinces:** equal citizenship as a contested question.
- **Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Waray speakers in Manila:** language and accent as rank.
- **Women in Christian and Muslim areas:** gender gaps compared across faiths and regions.
- **Bakla and transgender Filipinos (Quezon City against BARMM):** equal treatment argued place by place.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** past exclusion and later stereotypes about wealth, to be tested against their own accounts.
- **Mestizo families and darker-skinned citizens:** colorism inside one people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income, poverty and gender; mixed for colorism and language rank; thin for ranking inside Indigenous and Moro communities.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English, Spanish; Cebuano and Hiligaynon; Maranao and Tausug for Moro sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs must be described, not repeated; land disputes involve killings (Hacienda Luisita, 2004); SOGIE, Indigenous-rights and women's-rights statutes belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
