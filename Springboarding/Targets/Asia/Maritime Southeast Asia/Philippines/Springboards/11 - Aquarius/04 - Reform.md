*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ilustrado families of Manila, Batangas, Pampanga and Iloilo:** the long tradition of reform asked for from within.
- **Central Luzon and Panay farmers:** land reform as demand from below and program from above.
- **Kalinga, Bontoc and other Cordillera peoples:** reforms and projects done to them, and their refusals.
- **Moro peoples:** Islamic renewal and the autonomy experiments of 1989 and 2019.
- **Lumad peoples of Mindanao:** development programs on ancestral land, and resistance.
- **Urban middle class of Metro Manila, Cebu and Davao:** election watchdog volunteers and the 1986 and 2001 uprisings.
- **Davao City:** a local "model" cited for national change, and its critics.
- **Catholic social-action networks against evangelical and Iglesia ni Cristo bodies:** different churches' stances toward reform.
- **Chinese Filipino civic groups:** the 1990s anti-kidnapping campaigns as reform from a minority.
- **Drug-war "surrenderees" and their families:** reform of persons under coercion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1880s reformists and martial law; mixed for regional and Islamic reform; thin for Lumad accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English and Spanish (reformist writing); Cebuano and Hiligaynon; Maranao and Maguindanaon, including Arabic script.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martial-law memory is politically contested; drug-war rehabilitation involved killings; activists are red-tagged. Agrarian, election and autonomy statutes belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
