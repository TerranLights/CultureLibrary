## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men in Manila and Cebu neighborhoods:** the street-corner hangout and the drinking circle as the main friendship sites.
- **Rural men in the Tagalog, Bicol and Visayan provinces:** the cockpit and the basketball court as places friendships are made.
- **Women's kumare networks:** friendship tied to godparenthood and shared child-raising.
- **Bakla and other LGBT circles, including beauty-salon workplaces:** friends as chosen family.
- **Tausug of Sulu:** friend as armed ally where feuds can arise.
- **Teduray and other Lumad peoples:** friendship inside very small communities.
- **Settlers and Lumad in Mindanao:** friendships across the settler line, and how rare or common they are.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** sworn brotherhood and friendships with non-Chinese neighbors.
- **Provincial political and business elites:** godparent ties used as networks of favor.
- **Seafarers and overseas workers (comparison only):** friendships kept across distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on godparenthood and Filipino psychology; mixed on youth peers; thin on measured friendship outside surveys of the young.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English and Taglish slang; Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Ilocano; Tausug; Hokkien and Chinese characters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cronyism claims touch named living people; LGBT respondents need care; Sulu feud contexts. The law on corruption belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
