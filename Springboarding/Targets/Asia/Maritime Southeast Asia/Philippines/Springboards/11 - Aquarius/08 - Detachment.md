# Philippines: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino, English, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano, Kankanaey; Maranao and Tausug; Hokkien (Chinese Filipino temples); Spanish (colonial devotional texts). **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Metro Manila commuters and condominium residents:** urban anonymity against barangay familiarity.
- **Small provincial towns:** whether keeping to oneself is possible where everyone knows everyone.
- **Tagalog and Visayan wakes against Cordillera death rites:** different limits on showing grief.
- **Men and women:** the "men don't cry" norm, to be tested by region and class.
- **Call-center agents, nurses and seafarers:** composure as a job requirement.
- **Maranao and Tausug communities:** patience as a religious virtue beside honor and quick offense.
- **Chinese Filipino families:** Buddhist teaching on letting go beside business reserve.
- **Lumad peoples:** customs of not interfering, to be read in their own terms rather than as coldness.
- **Contemplative communities (Benedictines of Bukidnon, Carmelites, Banahaw groups):** chosen withdrawal.
- **Children of overseas workers:** distance inside the family as an ordinary condition.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Filipino psychology's vocabulary; mixed for emotional-display research; thin for measured loneliness and living alone outside census counts.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino, English; Cebuano; Kankanaey and Ifugao for highland rites; Maranao and Tausug; Hokkien.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** depression and suicide; journalists killed for their work (Maguindanao, 2009); some Cordillera death rites are closed to outsiders. Media regulation and impartial judging belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
