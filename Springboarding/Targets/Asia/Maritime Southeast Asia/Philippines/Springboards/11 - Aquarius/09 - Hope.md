## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Migrant-sending provinces (Ilocos, Pangasinan, Batangas, Iloilo):** hope placed in a family member's departure.
- **Settler families in Mindanao (of Cebuano, Ilonggo and Ilocano descent):** whether the promised land kept its promise.
- **Lumad and Moro peoples:** the same promise read as dispossession, and the hope of return to ancestral land.
- **Displaced residents of Marawi:** hope of going home.
- **Eastern Visayas after Typhoon Haiyan (2013):** hope through rebuilding, and its fatigue.
- **Quiapo devotees and other urban poor:** hope carried by vows.
- **Tayug (Pangasinan) and Surigao:** places with a memory of millennial hope.
- **Students and young people:** called "the hope of the nation", a phrase whose source is disputed.
- **Farmers and fishers in the typhoon belt:** seasonal hope for harvest and catch.
- **Chinese Filipinos in Binondo:** New Year customs for luck and a good year.
- **Cordillera rice growers:** rites for the coming year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for measured optimism, remittances and devotions; mixed for millenarian history; thin for Indigenous hope in the peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Cebuano, Ilocano, Pangasinan and Waray; Maranao for Marawi; Ifugao.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data and crisis services; illegal gambling and the officials tied to it; displaced communities; some harvest rites are restricted. The "resilience" label is disputed and should not be adopted as a claim.
- **Held for review:** none.
