# Philippines: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan; Maranao, Maguindanaon and Tausug (with Arabic-script religious terms); Philippine Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and transcendence: how a culture meets suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape, with magic ways (folk healing, charms, divination, sorcery) as its folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tagalog and Central Luzon lowlands:** the reference case for Holy Week devotion and the Manila institutions; not the voice of the nation.
- **Visayas (Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Waray, Kinaray-a speakers):** separate healer and sorcery traditions, with Siquijor and Capiz carrying regional reputations.
- **Bikol and the Ilocano north:** distinct regional devotions and spirit vocabularies.
- **Cordillera peoples (Ifugao, Bontoc, Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Kalinga):** their own ritual specialists and burial customs, as each people describes them.
- **Bangsamoro peoples (Maranao, Maguindanaon, Tausug, Yakan, Sama):** Islamic frames for the unseen alongside older local practice.
- **Lumad peoples (Manobo, T'boli, B'laan, Subanen, Teduray and others):** ritual specialists and spirit worlds outside both Catholic and Islamic frames.
- **Palawan and Mindoro peoples (Pala'wan, Tagbanwa, Batak; Mangyan groups):** small-scale traditions with written scripts in two cases.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** temples, ghost-month customs and mixed Catholic practice.
- **Ati, Aeta and other Negrito peoples:** their own healing and forest knowledge, to be heard in their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lowland folk belief and Catholic devotion; mixed for mental-health measures; thin and scattered for many Lumad and Palawan peoples. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the archipelago as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English for most scholarship; Spanish for colonial sources; Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon and other regional languages for folk material; Arabic script for Bangsamoro religious texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous ritual knowledge is not for outsiders; "sorcery" reputations attach to real provinces and families; mental-health stigma; Mindanao conflict zones.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
