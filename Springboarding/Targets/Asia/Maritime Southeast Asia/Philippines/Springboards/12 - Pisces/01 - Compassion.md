## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bikol:** an ethnographic baseline exists for pity between living and dead, useful as a control for other regions.
- **Eastern Visayas (Waray speakers):** the Haiyan experience shapes how receiving help is talked about.
- **Metro Manila's urban poor and street families:** the main targets of public pity, and its main critics.
- **Cebuano speakers of Cebu and Mindanao:** a second large language world with its own church and civic charity.
- **Maranao, Tausug and Maguindanaon communities:** obligatory Islamic giving sets a different frame for alms.
- **Sama Dilaut (sea-dwelling Sama):** often seen begging in cities; how they and their hosts describe the exchange.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** Buddhist and clan-association charity alongside Catholic charity.
- **Cordillera peoples (for example Ifugao, Kalinga):** community obligations to the bereaved, as each describes them.
- **Rural and urban households:** neighbor aid and *abuloy* customs may differ from city charity.

## 4. Flags
