# Philippines: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Philippines, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Filipino/Tagalog and English; Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano, Kapampangan; Maranao and other Bangsamoro languages with Arabic religious terms; Manobo (Agusan); Philippine Hokkien. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Panay (Capiz, Iloilo, Antique):** the region whose name is tied to the aswang, and how residents answer that.
- **Eastern Visayas and Bikol:** fear shaped by typhoons and storm surges.
- **Metro Manila's urban poor:** fear of killings and of eviction in dense neighborhoods.
- **Maranao of Lanao del Sur:** fear after the 2017 siege, and fear of God in Islamic teaching.
- **Manobo and other Agusan peoples:** fear of wildlife and of armed conflict in the uplands.
- **Ilocano and Cordillera north:** earthquakes and landslides; compare spirit fears.
- **Chinese Filipinos:** seasonal ghost customs and the memory of kidnapping waves.
- **Children, youth and elders:** bogeymen, anxiety and fear of crime differ by age.
- **Families of the disappeared and the killed:** fear that outlasts the event.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for horror media, folklore and disaster agencies; mixed for anxiety surveys; thin for Lumad and Cordillera sources.
- **Languages needed:** Filipino and English; Spanish for early reports; Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Ilocano, Kapampangan; Maranao; Hokkien.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the aswang label stigmatizes real people and places; drug-war and martial-law fear involves living victims; Marawi trauma; mental-health stigma.
- **Held for review:** none.
