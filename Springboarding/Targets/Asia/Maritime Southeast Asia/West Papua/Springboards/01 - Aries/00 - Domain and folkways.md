# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (the shared everyday language), standard Indonesian for official records, Dutch for the colonial record, and the peoples' own languages (Biak, Dani/Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Asmat, Kamoro, Amungme, Marind, Sentani, Moi, Hatam and Meyah of the Arfak area, Ma'ya of Raja Ampat). **Concept in one line:** the domain of self and identity: what a self is here and how it asserts itself (the person, the body, appearance, beginnings, naming).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Numfor, Cenderawasih Bay:** check how *keret* membership and the Koreri story enter life stories; the renewal tradition is best recorded here.
- **Baliem Valley Dani (Hubula), Lani and Yali, Central Highlands:** separate the three, because outsider sources often fold them into one "Dani" self.
- **Mee of Paniai and the Wissel Lakes:** check how wealth and exchange enter self-description, to test whether Baliem sources generalize.
- **Asmat and Kamoro, south coast:** compare carving and naming of the dead under different mission and mining histories.
- **Marind and other Merauke lowland peoples:** check age-grade naming against present-day plantation-era change.
- **Sentani and Jayapura coast:** check hereditary *ondofolo* titles as part of a person's identity.
- **Moi of Sorong, Arfak peoples (Hatam, Meyah, Sougb) and Ma'ya of Raja Ampat:** check whether any self-accounts exist; thinly written up.
- **Papuan Muslim communities of Fakfak, Kaimana and Raja Ampat:** check how Tidore-era links shape names and titles.
- **Migrant settler communities in the cities (Bugis, Makassarese, Javanese, Torajan and others):** part of the present-day picture, kept apart from Indigenous self-accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; dense older ethnography for a handful of peoples (Dani, Asmat, Marind, Biak, Mee), thin or none for many others. West Papua's exact lands are still to be settled; the sign's sheets are written for the core lands of western New Guinea and its offshore islands.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian for present-day self-talk, Dutch for mission naming records, and each people's language for its self-names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's-house and initiation knowledge may be restricted; mummies and ancestor remains; old photographs of undress; identity talk can carry legal risk for speakers.
- **Held for review:** none for the domain; the category of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands is not expected to touch Papuan self-accounts.
