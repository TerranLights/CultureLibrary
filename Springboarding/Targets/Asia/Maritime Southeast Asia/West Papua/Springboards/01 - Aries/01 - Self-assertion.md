# West Papua: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay, with Indonesian for press and official minutes; Mee, Yali, Biak, Amungme, Kamoro and Sentani for the speaking forums named below. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee of Paniai against Baliem Dani:** check whether speaking-up forums at exchange feasts follow the same order, since Pospisil and Heider describe different settings.
- **Yali of Yalimo:** compare dispute talk where state courts arrived late, to see when a claim is pressed by voice and when by force.
- **Biak and Cenderawasih Bay:** check sung challenge (*wor*) against spoken claims, a separate channel of assertion.
- **Sentani and the Jayapura coast:** check how hereditary rank limits who may contradict whom.
- **Amungme and Kamoro near Timika:** compare two neighbors facing the same mine, one highland and one coastal.
- **Moi around Sorong and Ma'ya speakers of Raja Ampat:** check whether village-meeting or land-claim speeches were ever recorded, to learn if the Bird's Head and islands can be covered at all.
- **Women traders against men leaders:** check who speaks for a group in public, and where women's voices are recorded at all.
- **Migrant settler traders in the towns:** part of the present-day market picture; separate from Indigenous sellers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; older ethnography covers Mee and Yali forums well; women's and coastal speech is thin; recent press is plentiful but political.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for refusals and requests as actually spoken; Mee and Biak for oratory and *wor* texts, which outsiders rarely translate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** public claims can lead to arrest; protect speakers' names in recent footage; some oratory belongs to initiated men.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside for this concept.
