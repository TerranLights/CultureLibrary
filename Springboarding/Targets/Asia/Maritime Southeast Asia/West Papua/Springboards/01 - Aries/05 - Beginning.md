# West Papua: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for festivals, holidays and folk-tale collections; Dutch for mission and post founding records; Amungme, Asmat, Kamoro, Mee and Marind for origin and first-rite terms. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amungme of the high country above Timika:** check how origin ties to a named mountain shape talk of new starts after mining.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** compare the opening rites of adulthood across two coastal peoples.
- **Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** check mission-era firsts against the *dema* origin accounts.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check how feast cycles mark when a new period begins.
- **Hubula of the Baliem:** compare first-contact memory with the festival version shown to visitors.
- **Manokwari and Mansinam (Bird's Head):** check how the 1855 landing is used as a beginning by different churches.
- **Sentani and the Jayapura coast:** check how the 1944 Allied landing at Hollandia is remembered as the start of town life by lake and coast villages.
- **Muslim communities of Kaimana and Fakfak:** compare Islamic and Tidore-era beginnings with the mission founding story.
- **Ma'ya of Waigeo, Raja Ampat:** check whether any first-catch or new-season observances are recorded.
- **Transmigrant settlers:** their arrival story is part of the present-day picture, set beside Indigenous origins, not instead of them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; origin stories are widely recorded but often only in mission or state retelling; the yearly reckoning of seasons is thin in print.
- **Languages needed:** Old Javanese (via translations) for the 1365 poem; Dutch for posts and missions; Amungme and Asmat for origin terms that retellings flatten.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some origin stories are owned by clans and told only to the initiated; first-contact images can shame; displacement accounts need consent.
- **Held for review:** nothing for beginnings; no South Asian-heritage material came up.
