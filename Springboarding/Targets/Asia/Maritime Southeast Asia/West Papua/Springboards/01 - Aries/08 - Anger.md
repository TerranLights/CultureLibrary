# West Papua: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for emotion talk; Indonesian for rights inquiries and health studies; Dutch for mission-era dictionaries; Biak, Dani and Mee dictionaries for the peoples' own anger words. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hubula and Lani of the Central Highlands:** check compensation and arrow-breaking rites against town courts, to see how group anger is closed.
- **Mee of Paniai:** compare dictionary terms and dispute accounts with Highlands neighbors to the east.
- **Biak and Numfor:** check how the 1998 events shape talk of grievance, and what older Biak words say.
- **Coastal Muslim communities of Kaimana and Fakfak:** check *suanggi* talk, which may differ from highland sorcery talk.
- **Ma'ya and Biak villages of Raja Ampat:** check anger at outside damage and the claims that follow.
- **Asmat and Marind of the south:** check whether any non-war anger sources exist beyond raiding stories.
- **Sentani and Jayapura neighborhoods:** compare town anger, alcohol and youth gangs with village settings.
- **Women and men:** check who may show anger in public and who must hold it.
- **Papuans and settler traders in towns:** check how clashes are told by each side; settler accounts are present-day context, not the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; collective anger and violence are heavily reported; everyday and household anger is thin; emotion vocabulary needs dictionary work.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch to use the Biak and Mee mission dictionaries; Dani grammar for word-building; Papuan Malay slang for rap and street talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** massacre memory; sorcery accusations can lead to killings; domestic violence; avoid the outsider stereotype of an angry people by tracking variation and context.
- **Held for review:** no anger pointer set aside under the standing rules.
