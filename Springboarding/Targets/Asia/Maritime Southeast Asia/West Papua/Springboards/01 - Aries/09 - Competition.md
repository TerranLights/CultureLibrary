# West Papua: Competition (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for fan, street and school talk; Indonesian for sports, festival and election records; English for older Highlands studies; Biak and Asmat for feast and festival terms. **Concept in one line:** striving against others for a prize, a rank or a result.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jayapura and Sentani football towns against Wamena:** check whether club rivalry follows coast and Highlands lines, without assuming it does.
- **Hubula of the Baliem:** check play and games against Heider's earlier observations, since much has changed.
- **Asmat carvers:** check whether rights over designs limit what may be entered in prize contests.
- **Biak and Supiori:** compare festival contests with older *munara* feasting.
- **Highland clans in elections:** check how the *noken* vote shapes rivalry between villages.
- **Marind of Merauke:** check whether mission or modern sources record any feasting or gardening contests at all.
- **Ma'ya and Biak islanders of Raja Ampat and the Moi of Sorong:** check small contests, where the record is likely thin.
- **School pupils in Highlands and coastal towns:** compare olympiad and scholarship stories by region.
- **Settler clubs and schools:** competitors in the same leagues and exams; part of the present-day picture only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; sport and festival coverage is plentiful but promotional; competition in exchange and feasting needs older ethnography; games are thinly recorded.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for festival and election sources; Papuan Malay for fan chants and social-media talk; Biak and Asmat for contest names that programs translate loosely.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** election rivalry has turned violent; carved designs may be owned by clans, so contest entries need care; avoid "naturally athletic" clichés.
- **Held for review:** nothing held for competition.
