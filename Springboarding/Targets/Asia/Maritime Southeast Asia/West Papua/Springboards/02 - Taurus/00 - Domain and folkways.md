# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for prices and markets; Indonesian for statistics; Dutch for colonial trade and museum records; Mee, Dani (Hubula), Biak, Maybrat and Asmat for valuables, pigs, shells, cloth and food. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how it is held (money, resources, land, food, dress and wealth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee, Moni and Dani-speaking peoples of the Highlands:** check how shells, pigs and gardens are counted, because the classic studies come from only a few valleys.
- **Asmat, Kamoro and Waropen of the coasts:** compare a sago-and-fish base with Highland pig economies before assuming one Papuan domain.
- **Maybrat, Moi and other Bird's Head peoples:** check whether cloth valuables still circulate and who keeps them.
- **Biak and Numfor islanders:** check how foreign plates and cloth from old voyages sit beside new money in marriage payments.
- **Marind, Kanum and Yei of the Merauke lowlands:** check the domain where land has been released to plantations.
- **Amungme and Kamoro near the mine:** separate what the mine's money changed from what came before it.
- **Muslim Papuans of Fakfak, Kaimana and the Raja Ampat islands:** check trade wealth tied to old Seram and Tidore links.
- **Migrant settler traders in Jayapura, Sorong, Timika and Merauke:** part of today's markets, not the culture's core.
- **Papuans in Papua New Guinea and the Netherlands:** comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: deep for a few exchange systems (Mee, Mejprat, Dani) and for provincial statistics, thin for most lowland and island peoples. West Papua's exact lands are still to be settled, and this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of western New Guinea and its islands.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for museum registers and trade reports; each people's language for valuables that have no Malay name.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some valuables and stones are sacred and not for outsiders; clan land boundaries are contested; resource companies' reports are partial.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on possessions was set aside under the standing rules.
