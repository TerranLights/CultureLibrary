# West Papua: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for "my thing" talk, borrowing and theft; Indonesian for crime and pawn data; Dutch and English for mission and museum records; grammars of Ambel, Hatam, Korowai and Dani for how a language marks what is had. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Papuan Malay | *sa pu*, *ko pu*, *dong pu* | my, your, their (from *punya*, "own") | the everyday marker of having; Kluge describes it *check* |
| Papuan Malay | *pinjam*, *kasi*, *minta* | borrow or lend, give, ask for | the verbs of passing things between kin and neighbors |
| Papuan Malay / Indonesian | *barang hilang*, *curi* | lost things, steal | for lost-and-found posts and theft reports |
| Indonesian | *barang pusaka*, *benda warisan* | heirloom, inherited object | used in heritage and museum writing |
| Dani (Hubula) | *kaneke* | sacred stones kept in the men's house | Heider's term *check*; restricted |
| Asmat | *bis* | carved ancestor pole | what becomes of it after the feast is the question; *check* |
| Biak / Papuan Malay | *piring* | plate, as an heirloom valuable | search with "Biak" for old porcelain *check* |
| Dutch | *bezit*, *erfstuk*, *verzameling* | possession, heirloom, collection | for collectors' and museum registers |

English keywords: possessive marking Papuan languages; alienable inalienable possession New Guinea; heirloom porcelain West Papua; sacred stones Dani; repatriation Papuan objects; conversion fetish burning Highlands.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dani of the Grand Valley and Western Dani of Mulia:** compare kept and burned ritual objects where conversion came at different times and through different missions.
- **Korowai and Kombai:** check what a mobile household keeps when houses are rebuilt every few years.
- **Asmat:** check objects made to be abandoned against those kept, sold or taken to museums.
- **Waropen and Biak of Cenderawasih Bay:** check old porcelain and cloth held across generations.
- **Ambel and Ma'ya of the Raja Ampat islands:** test possessive grammar and boat ownership in island households.
- **Hatam and Meyah of the Arfak area:** check how houses and gardens hold belongings in the Bird's Head mountains.
- **Sentani lake villages:** check bronze and stone heirlooms against present-day household goods.
- **Urban households in Jayapura and Sorong:** check new goods (phones, motorbikes) in mixed Papuan and settler neighborhoods.
- **Papuan students and workers in Java and Sulawesi:** comparison only, for what is carried away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; grammars and older community studies give good leads; today's household goods appear mostly in national data.
- **Languages needed:** grammars in English; heritage writing in Indonesian; collectors' registers in Dutch; each people's word for objects that must not be lent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred stones, mummies and men's-house objects may be closed to outsiders; museum holdings came in part through unequal deals.
- **Held for review:** none; the possession pointers needed nothing set aside.
