# West Papua: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for land claims, compensation and company dealings; Dutch for 1950s tenure surveys; Moi, Marind, Amungme, Moni and Sentani for clan land and its names. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indonesian | *hak ulayat*, *tanah adat*, *wilayah adat* | customary right over land, customary land, customary territory | the statute side goes to Libra; here, how people speak of it |
| Indonesian | *pelepasan tanah adat*, *ganti rugi* | release of customary land, compensation | the moment a clan's claim passes to others |
| Papuan Malay | *tuan tanah*, *pemilik dusun* | landholder, owner of a grove | who is named as owner in village talk |
| Papuan Malay | *marga pemilik* | the owning clan | common in disputes over town and airport land *check* |
| Amungme | *Nemangkawi* | the mountain the state calls Puncak Jaya | a named place whose holding is contested; *check* form |
| Marind | *anim-ha* | "the true people", as some Marind sources write it | *check*; a self-name tied to land claims |
| Kaimana area | *sasi nggama* | seasonal closure of a marine or forest resource | *check* the name and the language |
| Dutch | *grondenrecht*, *eigendom*, *bevolkingszaken* | land rights, property, native affairs | for colonial tenure files |

English keywords: customary land tenure West Papua; land release plantation Merauke; mining and landowners Timika; clan land Sorong; marine tenure Kaimana; airport land claims Sentani; participatory mapping Papua.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Marind, Kanum and Yei of the Merauke lowlands:** check how clan holdings are named and how release decisions are made, where plantations are largest.
- **Amungme and Kamoro near Timika:** compare a mountain people and a coastal people facing one mine, since their claims differ in kind.
- **Moi and Tehit of the Sorong area:** check clan land on the Bird's Head where oil, logging and towns press on it.
- **Moni and Mee of Intan Jaya and Paniai:** check individual garden claims against wider clan claims in gold country.
- **Sentani and Tobati-Enggros near Jayapura:** check how hereditary leaders speak for land under airports, campuses and suburbs.
- **Kaimana and Raja Ampat island peoples:** check who holds the reef and the right to close it.
- **Papuan women:** check whether women are named in releases and in clan maps.
- **Migrant settlers holding certificates in towns and transmigration areas:** present-day holders beside clan claims, not the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conflicts tied to mining, plantations and towns; thin on quiet everyday tenure in many valleys and islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for releases and press; Dutch for 1950s surveys; clan and place names in each local language, spelled several ways.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan maps and boundaries are evidence in live disputes; sacred sites may be left off maps on purpose; land activists face risk. The letter of land law goes to Libra and its spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; this concept's pointers raised nothing under the standing rules.
