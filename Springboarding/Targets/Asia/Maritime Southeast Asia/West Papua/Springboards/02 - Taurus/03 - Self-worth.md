# West Papua: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for teasing, pride and shame talk; Indonesian for health surveys, church and press writing; Dutch for colonial schoolbooks and the 1950s elite; Biak, Dani and Mee for titles of standing. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Serui (Yapen) islanders:** check how mission schooling and Dutch-era posts entered family accounts of worth.
- **Baliem Dani, Lani and Yali:** compare standing earned by giving with standing from church or government office.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check whether wealth-based leadership still shapes how men speak of themselves.
- **Sentani lake villages:** compare hereditary rank with earned standing.
- **Muslim Papuans of Fakfak and Kaimana:** check inherited titles from the old petty kingdoms against Christian-majority patterns.
- **Asmat and Kamoro carvers:** check whether skill, sale and festival prizes enter self-regard.
- **Papuan women traders and church workers:** check worth in their own words, apart from men's titles.
- **Urban youth in Jayapura, Sorong and Timika:** check self-talk under racism, unemployment and school pressure.
- **Migrant settler neighbors:** present-day background against which Papuans compare themselves, not the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; much on collective dignity and racism since 2019, little on personal self-esteem in local languages.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for *mop* and youth talk; Biak for the Koreri story's names; Dutch for the colonial image of Papuans.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** anthems and symbols tied to independence can bring arrest; racist material must be cited, not reproduced; mental-health data on small groups can identify people.
- **Held for review:** none for self-worth.
