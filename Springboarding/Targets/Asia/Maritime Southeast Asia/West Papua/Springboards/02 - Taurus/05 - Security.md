# West Papua: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for food, disaster, finance and conflict data; Papuan Malay for talk of feeling safe, saving and fleeing; Dutch for famine and raid records; Biak and Highland languages for the words of want and plenty. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indonesian | *keamanan* | security | in Papua it usually means the security forces; search with care |
| Indonesian / Papuan Malay | *rasa aman*, *tra aman* | the feeling of safety, not safe | the felt side, apart from the forces |
| Indonesian | *ketahanan pangan*, *rawan pangan*, *kelaparan* | food security, food insecure, famine | for agency maps and crisis reports |
| Indonesian | *jaminan sosial*, *tabungan* | social insurance, savings | for national programs and bank surveys |
| Papuan Malay / Indonesian | *pengungsi*, *mengungsi* | displaced person, to flee | for displacement in the Highlands and across the border |
| Biak | *Koreri* | the promised time without want or death | here as an ideal of security; *check* the scope |
| Dutch | *hongersnood*, *bestaanszekerheid*, *hongi* | famine, livelihood security, raiding fleet | for colonial and earlier records |

English keywords: food security Papua Highlands; famine Yahukimo; drought frost Jayawijaya 1997; displacement West Papua; financial inclusion Papua; disaster Biak tsunami 1996; Tidore raids New Guinea coast.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yali, Lani and Dani of the Highlands:** check how gardens, pigs and kin carry households through frost and drought, valley by valley.
- **Nduga and Intan Jaya communities:** check what fleeing households keep and lose; conflict areas need separate units.
- **Asmat and Kamoro of the south coast:** compare sago-based buffers with Highland gardens.
- **Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** check food security where forest has turned to plantation.
- **Biak and Numfor islanders:** check how disaster and old raids shape talk of safety.
- **Ma'ya and other Raja Ampat fishers:** check sea seasons, reef closures and tourism income as buffers.
- **Townspeople of Jayapura, Sorong and Manokwari:** compare state posts, wages and savings with village buffers.
- **Migrant settlers in the towns:** check their own accounts of safety and property in separate units; present-day neighbors, not the core.
- **Refugees and their children in Papua New Guinea:** comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conflict and crisis, thin on ordinary household saving and insurance by people.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for agency maps and finance surveys; Papuan Malay for felt safety; Dutch for colonial famine reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *keamanan* talk can endanger speakers; locations of displaced people should not be published; famine reporting is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none; the security pointers set nothing aside.
