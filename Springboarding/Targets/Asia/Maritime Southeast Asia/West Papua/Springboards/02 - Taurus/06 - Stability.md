# West Papua: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for migration, price and administrative records; Papuan Malay for talk of home villages and moving; Dutch for village-formation and mission histories; Biak, Dani, Hatam and Korowai for settlement and house words. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee and Moni of the Paniai lakes and Intan Jaya:** compare 1930s records with today's villages to see what stayed.
- **Asmat and Korowai of the southern lowlands:** check how resettled villages hold together, and who goes back to old places.
- **Hatam, Sougb and Meyah of the Arfak Mountains:** check house forms and garden sites kept across generations.
- **Biak and the coastal congregations of Cenderawasih Bay:** check continuity of church, clan and village since the nineteenth century.
- **Baliem Valley Dani:** check whether the garden round has held as roads, towns and markets grew.
- **Amungme and Kamoro workers in Timika:** compare work at the mine with village continuity.
- **Sentani lake villages and Jayapura suburbs:** check old stilt villages beside fast-growing towns.
- **Ma'ya villages of Waigeo and Misool, Raja Ampat islands:** check how tourism money and new roads alter the daily and yearly round.
- **Marind villages near the Merauke settlement zones:** check whether old village sites hold beside new farms and towns.
- **Migrant settler communities:** a growing share of the towns; separate units for the present-day picture, not the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; good on migration, prices and administration, thin on how people speak of continuity in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for resettlement and mission histories; Indonesian for statistics; local terms for house and village forms, often spelled several ways.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** demographic change is a charged topic; resettlement memories can be painful; the redrawing of provinces is contested.
- **Held for review:** none under stability.
