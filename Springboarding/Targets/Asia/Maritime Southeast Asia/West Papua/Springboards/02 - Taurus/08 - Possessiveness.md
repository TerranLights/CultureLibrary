# West Papua: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for jealousy, spouses and stinginess; Indonesian for family surveys and violence data; Dutch for 1950s writing on marriage and elopement; Ngalum, Asmat and Ma'ya contexts for guarded knowledge and sea territory. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Papuan Malay | *cemburu*, *cemburu buta* | jealous, "blind" jealousy | the core words in songs and complaints |
| Papuan Malay | *sa pu maitua*, *sa pu paitua* | my wife, my husband ("my old woman/man") | possessive endearments in daily speech |
| Indonesian / Papuan Malay | *pelit*, *kikir* | stingy, miserly | the object side: refusing to share |
| Indonesian | *posesif*, *kekerasan ekonomi* | possessive, economic abuse | used in counseling and women's-rights writing |
| Maluku and Raja Ampat Malay | *petuanan* | customary territory, often of the sea | turf claimed against outside fishers *check* |
| Indonesian | *kekayaan intelektual komunal* | communal intellectual property | claims over motifs, songs and designs |
| Dutch | *jaloezie*, *schaking* | jealousy, abduction or elopement of a woman | for colonial and mission writing on marriage |

English keywords: jealousy and marriage West Papua; polygyny Highlands; widow remarriage custom Papua; customary sea territory Raja Ampat; Asmat motif ownership; communal intellectual property Indonesia Papua; child custody clan bride wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngalum of the Star Mountains:** check guarded men's knowledge and marriage claims in a people little written up.
- **Dani, Lani and Yali:** compare plural marriage and widow remarriage across neighboring Highland peoples.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check how loans and bride wealth create claims over people as well as pigs.
- **Ma'ya, Biak settlers and other Raja Ampat islanders:** check sea turf and its guarding against outside fishers.
- **Asmat carvers:** check clan claims over designs against open sale.
- **Marind and Muyu of the south:** check claims over children and widows where clans are large and land is pressed.
- **Maybrat and Moi of the Bird's Head:** check whether cloth paid at marriage is cited when kin claim a wife's children.
- **Couples in Jayapura, Sorong and Timika:** check phone and money control among young town couples.
- **Mixed Papuan and settler couples:** check which customs of claim each side brings; present-day picture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed; national surveys rarely break out the Papuan provinces by people; most leads are case studies and church sources.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for songs and talk; Dutch for 1950s writing on marriage; local languages for kin terms that carry claims.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic abuse cases must stay anonymous; men's-house knowledge is closed; sea-territory disputes can turn violent. Family law as written goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on possessiveness was put aside.
