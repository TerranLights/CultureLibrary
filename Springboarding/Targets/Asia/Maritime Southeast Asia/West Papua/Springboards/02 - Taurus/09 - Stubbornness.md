# West Papua: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for refusal and "hard head" talk; Indonesian for blockades, campaigns and political slogans; Dutch for early mission letters and 1950s unrest; Awyu, Moi, Mee and Asmat contexts for holdouts. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Papuan Malay | *kepala batu*, *keras kepala* | "stone head", hard-headed | the everyday words, said of children and adults |
| Papuan Malay | *tra mau*, *tetap* | won't, (stay) firm | the short forms of refusal in speech |
| Indonesian / Papuan Malay | *palang*, *pemalangan* | customary blockade (of a road, office or school) | a common way of holding a claim *check* range |
| Indonesian | *harga mati* | "dead price": not negotiable | used on rival sides of the political question |
| Indonesian | *teguh pendirian*, *bertahan* | firm in one's stand, to hold out | the praising words |
| Indonesian | *menolak sawit*, *tolak tambang* | reject oil palm, reject mining | campaign phrases of land holdouts |
| Dutch | *koppigheid*, *verzet* | stubbornness, resistance | for colonial and mission records |

English keywords: customary blockade Papua; land defenders oil palm Boven Digoel; All Eyes on Papua; Raja Ampat nickel mining protest; Paniai resistance 1956; stalled dialogue Jakarta Papua; Asmat feast ban.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Awyu, Muyu and Marind of the southern lowlands:** compare land holdouts against plantations in neighboring peoples.
- **Moi of Sorong and other Bird's Head peoples:** check how refusals are argued and who speaks for the clan.
- **Ma'ya, Biak and other Raja Ampat villagers:** check splits between villages that refuse mining and those that accept it.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check memories of refusing outside rule from 1956 onward.
- **Asmat of the south coast:** check whether keeping feasts through bans is told as firmness or as quiet waiting.
- **Lani, Dani and Yali of the Highlands:** check how compensation quarrels end, and who is called stubborn.
- **Sentani and Jayapura landholders:** check blockades of airports and offices as a town tactic.
- **Women leaders and market traders:** check whether firmness is praised or called stubbornness depending on who holds firm.
- **Migrant settler communities:** present-day parties to some disputes, not the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press on blockades and land campaigns; thin on everyday stubbornness inside families and on personality data for Papuans.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for press; Papuan Malay for speech; Dutch for mission letters and 1950s files; local terms for refusal, rarely in dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land defenders and campaigners face pressure; the political slogans can bring charges; the 1950s and 1960s events are remembered differently by each side.
- **Held for review:** none; the stubbornness pointers set nothing aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Indonesian press for "pemalangan" with "Jayapura", "Sorong", "Wamena" and "Merauke", and log causes and outcomes.
2. Collect the 2024 "All Eyes on Papua" coverage and list the speakers and their words for holding out.
3. Search the Nationaal Archief for "Obano 1956" and Papuan accounts in Indonesian.
4. Read IPAC's Papua reports for each named deadlock and its dates.
5. Search Webonary for each available Papuan language and look up "stubborn".
