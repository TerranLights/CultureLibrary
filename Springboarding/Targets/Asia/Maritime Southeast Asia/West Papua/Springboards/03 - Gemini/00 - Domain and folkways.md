# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (Melayu Papua) and Indonesian; Dutch for the colonial and mission record; the peoples' own languages (Biak, Dani/Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Asmat, Amungme, Kamoro, Marind, Sentani, Moi, Hatam, Ma'ya and many more). **Concept in one line:** the domain of communication and learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam and Sougb of the Arfak, Tehit):** check how talk and initiation learning are recorded here, through other missions and the *kain timur* exchange networks.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen and Wondama:** check the long mission-school history and the spread of Malay against the vitality of Biak and Wandamen.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Dani/Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Damal, Mek):** compare valley by valley the footpaths, men's houses and late schooling that set where talk happens.
- **South coast and Asmat, with the Kamoro of Mimika:** check river travel, men's-house teaching and Catholic-mission schooling as distinct channels.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu, Kimaam islanders):** check neighborliness and language use where settler towns grew.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** separate the urban mix of Papuan Malay, Indonesian and lake languages.
- **Raja Ampat and the Fakfak and Kaimana coasts (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** check the Muslim coastal and Tidore-linked record as its own strand.
- **Migrant settler communities in the cities (from Sulawesi, Java and Maluku):** part of today's urban speech mix, kept apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for a few peoples (Dani, Biak, Asmat, Marind, Mee) through older ethnography; thin or absent for many others. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core Papuan lands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for nearly everything current; Dutch for the 1855-1962 record; German for some highland research; a local language for any one people's own speech ways.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's-house and initiation knowledge may be restricted by gender or rank; ask the communities first. Political speech is policed; protect Papuan sources.
- **Held for review:** none at the domain level: every language, archive and mission pointer above sits outside the held categories.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Kluge on Papuan Malay registers and address, then search *mop Papua* for performed speech.
2. Pull the BPS 2010 home-language tables for every regency and set them beside Glottolog's map.
3. Search the Het Utrechts Archief catalog for "Nieuw-Guinea zending school" to find the earliest school reports.
4. Search "Mambesak" and "Arnold Ap" for surviving recordings and their song lists.
5. Search "honai pendidikan" and "rumah adat laki-laki" for studies of men's-house teaching.
