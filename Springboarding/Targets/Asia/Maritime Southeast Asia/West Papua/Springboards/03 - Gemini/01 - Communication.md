# West Papua: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian first; Dutch and German for older records; Biak, Dani, Mee, Hatam, Eipo (Mek) and Korowai where a study exists. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and the Cenderawasih Bay islands:** check how long contact with Malay, missions and officials has shaped speech registers, against the inland peoples.
- **Baliem Dani (Hubula), Lani and Yali:** compare the three neighbors' terms for talk and for speaking in public, since the grammars and missions differ.
- **Mee of the Paniai lakes and Moni:** check speech used in persuasion and exchange, as the Paniai record is unusually old for the highlands.
- **Mek peoples (Eipo, Una) of the eastern highlands:** check the German recordings as a separate highland source, not as a stand-in for the Dani.
- **Korowai and Kombai of the southern lowlands:** check avoidance and address patterns as a southern contrast.
- **Hatam, Sougb and Moi of the Bird's Head:** check whether their talk studies exist beyond grammars.
- **Jayapura, Sentani and Sorong townspeople, with migrant settler communities:** compare urban Papuan Malay with migrants' Indonesian as two speech worlds side by side.
- **Deaf Papuans:** check as a language community of their own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Grammars exist for several languages; recorded conversation and studies of talk are thin outside the Eipo, Biak and Korowai work. Expect to rely on grammars and mission records first.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for any current talk; German for the Eipo corpus; Dutch for the mission letters; Biak or Dani for close listening.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political speech and congress records can put speakers at risk; anonymize living speakers. Some songs and speech forms may be restricted to initiated men or women.
- **Held for review:** none: the grammars, recordings and congress records chosen for talk raise no held-category issue.
