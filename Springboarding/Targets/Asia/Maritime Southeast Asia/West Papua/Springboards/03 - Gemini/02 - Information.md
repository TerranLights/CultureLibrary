# West Papua: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for the press, statistics and NGO reports; Dutch for the colonial newspapers and VOC files; local languages for clan histories and knowledge keepers. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how it is gathered, passed on, trusted, withheld and used: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland districts with conflict (Nduga, Intan Jaya, Puncak):** check how residents learn what is happening when access is restricted, as a test of the information chain at its weakest.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** compare the dense media scene of the capital, with its migrant settler readership, against Papuan-run outlets.
- **Biak and Yapen:** check how long-standing literacy and radio shape news there, and how clan histories are kept.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Yei, Kanum):** check who controls land information as plantations spread.
- **Mimika (Kamoro and Amungme):** check what information flows between the mine, its community bodies and the two peoples.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak coasts:** check mosque and Tidore-linked channels as a separate strand.
- **Asmat and the south coast rivers:** check river and radio channels where roads are few.
- **The diaspora (Netherlands, Australia, the Pacific):** compare only, as an outside producer of news on the region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for political and rights information; thin for how ordinary villages gather and trust news. Statistics are aggregated by province and regency, and the 2022 province split changes the series.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for nearly all current sources; Dutch for the press before 1962 and the VOC record; local languages for oral genealogies and evidential grammar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance and press restrictions make sources and journalists vulnerable; some clan and ritual knowledge is owned and must not be published without consent.
- **Held for review:** none: the press, archive and data pointers for information are all clear of the held categories.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Delpher for "Nieuw-Guinea Koerier" and for "Hollandia" in 1950-1962 newspapers.
2. Read the HRW 2015 report and the SAFEnet 2019 reports together for how access was cut.
3. Pull BPS ICT tables for Papua and Papua Barat, then the new provinces from 2022.
4. Search *Suara Papua* and *Cenderawasih Pos* for "hoaks" to compare how each treats rumor.
5. Search the Nationaal Archief VOC inventory for "Papoese eilanden" and "Tidore".
