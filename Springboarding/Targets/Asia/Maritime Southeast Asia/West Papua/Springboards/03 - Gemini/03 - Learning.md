# West Papua: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for schools and current studies; Dutch for mission and government schooling to 1962; English for the anthropology of childhood; local-language terms for initiation and craft learning. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wondama, Biak and Yapen:** check the earliest mission-school lines and the teachers they produced, as a contrast with areas schooled only from the 1950s on.
- **Baliem Dani (Hubula) and Lani:** check how children learn gardening, pig care and fighting skills alongside school, and how both are talked about.
- **Muyu and Ngalum of the south-east and Star Mountains:** compare two neighboring routes into schooling and labor.
- **Moi, Maybrat and Tehit of the Bird's Head:** check whether initiation schooling still runs and how it is described by its own people.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** compare craft apprenticeship with formal schooling where both are present.
- **Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** check what is said of the old initiation grades today, against van Baal's record.
- **Migrant settler teachers and pupils in towns:** treat them as part of today's school picture, not as the Papuan baseline.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak Muslims:** check Qur'an schooling and *pesantren* as a separate line of learning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. School statistics and policy reports are plentiful; studies of how children learn at home are scarce outside a handful of ethnographies. Learning in initiation is often old record or restricted.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for schools and reports; Dutch for mission school files; Maybrat, Moi, Marind or Asmat terms for customary learning, with community help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation content is secret for many peoples; record only what communities allow. School failure is a political topic; avoid framing it as a trait of Papuans.
- **Held for review:** none: the mission, school and initiation sources on learning need no holding.
