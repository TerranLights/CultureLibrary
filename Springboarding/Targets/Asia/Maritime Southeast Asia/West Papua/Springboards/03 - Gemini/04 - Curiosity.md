# West Papua: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch and English for explorers' and scientists' records, read against Papuan accounts; local terms for taboo, sacred places and the old tales. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Baliem Dani (Hubula) and Mee of Paniai:** compare their own memories of first meetings in 1938 and the 1930s, since both have outsider accounts to set them against.
- **Korowai and Kombai:** check how hosts speak about visitors' curiosity where tourism and film crews arrive.
- **Asmat:** check how carvers and elders discuss collectors and museum interest, past and present.
- **Arfak peoples (Hatam, Sougb) and Raja Ampat islanders:** compare guiding and ecotourism, where local knowledge meets visitors' questions.
- **Lakes Plains and Mamberamo peoples near the Foja range:** check how scientists' surveys were seen by landowners.
- **Biak and Numfor:** check tellings of the Manarmakeri cycle for how seeking and secrets are framed.
- **Townspeople in Jayapura and Manokwari, with migrant settler families:** check questions one may ask a stranger in mixed neighborhoods.
- **Papuan students studying in Java and abroad:** check their accounts of being asked about, as part of today's picture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed and lopsided. Outsiders' curiosity (explorers, scientists, collectors, tourists) is heavily recorded; Papuan curiosity in Papuan words is thin. Seek oral histories and Papuan writers first.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for current voices; English and Dutch for expedition records; Biak and Dani for tellings of first meetings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred places, men's-house objects and some tales are closed to outsiders; museums hold contested objects. Read explorers' language about "stone age" peoples as a source on the writers, not on Papuans.
- **Held for review:** none: the explorers, museums and science programs on curiosity raise nothing to hold.
