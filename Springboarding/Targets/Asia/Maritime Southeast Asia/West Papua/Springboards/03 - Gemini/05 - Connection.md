# West Papua: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English and Dutch for the exchange and trade ethnography; Tok Pisin only for the cross-border comparison; local terms for trade partners and exchange valuables. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maybrat, Tehit and Moi of the Bird's Head:** check how cloth exchange sets who is tied to whom, as a distinct network type.
- **Mee of Paniai and Moni:** compare credit and pig-feast networks with the stone and shell routes of their neighbors.
- **Baliem Dani (Hubula), Yali and Lani:** check what footpaths, airstrips and the road have each done to visiting.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya) and the Fakfak and Kaimana coasts:** check sea links to Maluku, through marriage, Islam and old courts.
- **Muyu and Yonggom kin along the border:** check how families split by the line keep in touch.
- **Asmat villages on the south coast rivers:** check river visiting and ties to ancestors as separate threads.
- **Jayapura and Sentani, with migrant settler networks:** set Papuan clan networks beside settlers' own home-province associations, as two systems in one city.
- **Papuan students and workers elsewhere in Indonesia:** part of today's picture of distance ties.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Exchange ethnography is rich for a few peoples; loneliness and isolation are barely studied; transport and phone data exist by regency.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for transport, church and survey sources; Dutch for older exchange studies; Maybrat or Tehit for *kain timur* terms; Tok Pisin for comparison across the border only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** border crossings, student dormitories and refugee histories touch on security cases; keep individuals unnamed. Some exchange debts are private family matters.
- **Held for review:** none: the exchange, transport and border sources on ties needed no holding.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Hampton's chapters on exchange routes, then search "kapak batu" with "Yeleme" or "Langda" for Indonesian studies.
2. Search UNIPA theses for "kain timur" with "Ayamaru" or "Teminabuan".
3. Map Pelni and *perintis* routes to see which districts are tied weekly and which monthly.
4. Search "asrama mahasiswa Papua" with "Surabaya", "Yogyakarta" or "Makassar" for student accounts.
5. Search "Kalana Fat" and "Raja Ampat Tidore sejarah" for local histories.
