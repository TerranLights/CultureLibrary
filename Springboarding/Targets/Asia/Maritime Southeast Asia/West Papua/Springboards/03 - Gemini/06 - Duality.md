# West Papua: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for identity and religion; Dutch and German for the early records of paired social halves; local terms for moieties, house divisions and outsiders. **Concept in one line:** how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once, dividing the world into pairs, and treating people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Baliem Dani (Hubula):** check how the two moiety names are used today in marriage and alliance, beyond the 1960s record.
- **Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** compare the early German and later Dutch accounts of social halves with what Marind writers say now.
- **Kamoro of the Mimika coast:** check paired ritual roles against the pressures of the mine town.
- **Fakfak and Kaimana (Mbaham-Matta and neighbors):** check how families holding two faiths talk about it.
- **Biak, Serui and Jayapura churchgoers:** check how people describe holding church and customary obligations together.
- **Children of Papuan and settler parents:** check how they are named and how they name themselves, as part of the present-day picture.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** compare how they frame "Papuan" and "newcomer" against Papuan usage.
- **Exiles and diaspora in the Netherlands:** compare only, for loyalty to two homelands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Paired social halves are well recorded for a few peoples in old ethnography; current double identity is debated mostly in political writing. Little on mixed feelings or two-faced conduct as such.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for identity debates; German for Wirz; Dutch for Pouwer and the 1950s; Dani or Marind for the paired terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Papuan and Indonesian loyalty is a security matter; quoting people on it can harm them. Moiety and ritual pairs may hold restricted knowledge. The highlander/coast pair can carry prejudice; study it, don't reproduce it.
- **Held for review:** none: the moiety, identity and faith pointers on two-ness leave nothing for this line.
