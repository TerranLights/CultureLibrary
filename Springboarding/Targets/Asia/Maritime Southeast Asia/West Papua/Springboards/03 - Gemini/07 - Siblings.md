# West Papua: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay kin vocabulary first; Indonesian for surveys; Dutch and German for grammars and kinship charts; each people's own sibling terms, which differ by speaker's sex and birth order in many languages. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Marind and Kimaam islanders of the south:** check how sibling ties are bound up with marriage exchange, comparing the two published records.
- **Asmat:** check sibling and cousin terms against the men's-house groupings.
- **Yali, Lani and Dani of the highlands:** compare duties between brothers in alliance, compensation and pig exchange.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check how the dense older record treats elder and younger brothers in credit and inheritance.
- **Biak and Sentani:** check how seniority among brothers is tied to clan offices on the coast and lake.
- **Ngalum and Muyu of the south-east:** check sibling migration, as brothers follow each other to towns.
- **Urban households in Jayapura, Manokwari and Sorong:** check fostering and kin children living with elder siblings, beside migrant settler families, recorded separately.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak Muslim families:** check sibling shares under Islamic family practice, pointing legal rules to Libra and Capricorn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin. Kin terms are recorded in grammars; how siblings feel about each other, rivalry and care in old age are barely studied. Demography gives counts, not meanings.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for everyday terms; Dutch for Drabbe and Serpenti; German for Zöllner; local languages for full terminologies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** avoidance rules between brothers and sisters, and some origin stories, may be restricted; inheritance and land shares between siblings are law and go to Libra and Capricorn; orphanage records concern minors.
- **Held for review:** none: the kin-term, fertility and folktale pointers raise nothing to hold.
