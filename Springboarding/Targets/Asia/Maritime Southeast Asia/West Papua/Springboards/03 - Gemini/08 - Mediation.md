# West Papua: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for peace processes, church and customary councils; English and Dutch for older studies of leadership and conflict; local titles of leaders who stand between parties. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach agreement without imposing a decision, and, more broadly, the go-between who stands between parties.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee of Paniai and Lani:** compare which kind of leader steps into quarrels, testing the ethnography's label of achieved rather than inherited leadership against local accounts.
- **Sentani and Tobati-Enggros:** check hereditary leaders as standing go-betweens on the lake and bay.
- **Biak, Numfor and Raja Ampat:** check titled men from the Tidore era against later church and state brokers.
- **Yali and Western Dani:** compare the two 1960s studies with how peace is made today.
- **Amungme, Kamoro and the highland clans in Timika:** check mediation between peoples, the mine and the town.
- **Fakfak, Kaimana and other mixed-faith areas:** check family and interfaith go-betweens.
- **Migrant settler communities and their associations:** check how disputes between them and Papuans are brokered, as part of the present-day picture.
- **Women's groups and church women's fellowships:** check whether they act as peacemakers and how others regard it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Peace processes and leadership types are well covered; everyday go-betweens in families and marriages are thin. Compensation payments and customary fines are law: send them to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for nearly all current material; Dutch for Ploeg-era and colonial reports; Mee, Dani or Biak for leaders' titles and peacemaking speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peace talks involve armed groups and security forces; protect informants. Settlement payments can be private. Do not frame any people as warlike.
- **Held for review:** none: the leadership, peace-process and church pointers on go-betweens need no holding.
