# West Papua: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian for everyday talk, rumor and hoaxes; Dutch and English for village ethnography; local terms where gossip shades into sorcery accusation. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct, together with rumor: unconfirmed reports passed from person to person.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Korowai and Kombai:** check how talk about absent people is tied to witch suspicion, and what happens to the accused.
- **Mek valleys (Sela, Eipo areas):** check reputation talk where the record is German and Dutch.
- **Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** check rumor about land deals, companies and kin who sign.
- **Baliem Dani and Lani in Wamena:** check rumor between Papuans and migrant settler traders during unrest.
- **Market women in Jayapura, Sentani and Abepura:** check how talk at market stalls carries news across peoples.
- **Asmat, Mimika and the south coast:** check *suanggi* talk in towns with mixed origins.
- **Fakfak and Raja Ampat:** check gossip across Muslim and Christian kin.
- **Papuan students and workers away from home:** check rumor that travels from the dormitories back to the villages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin for everyday gossip; richer for rumor in crises and for sorcery accusation. Expect to build much of this from fieldwork and social media.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for talk itself; Indonesian for press and hoax lists; Dutch for Godschalk and older material; Korowai or Mek for local accusation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations can lead to violence; never name the accused. Private groups need consent. Rumors about security forces and armed groups are dangerous to repeat. Do not frame sorcery belief as a Papuan trait; record variation.
- **Held for review:** none: the rumor, hoax and accusation pointers raise nothing to hold.

## 5. First queries
1. Search IPAC and Amnesty International for reports on "Papua 2019" and note the rumor chains described.
2. Read van Enk and de Vries on witch accusation, then Chao on plantation rumor.
3. Search turnbackhoax.id for "Papua" and sort by year.
4. Search Indonesian press for "suanggi" with "dibunuh" or "tuduhan" to list cases (do not publish names).
5. Search "gosip" and "isu" in UNCEN and Musamus theses on village life.
