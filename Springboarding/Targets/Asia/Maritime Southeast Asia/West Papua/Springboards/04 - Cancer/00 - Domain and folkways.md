# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; Dutch for the colonial and mission record; the peoples' own languages (Dani/Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Biak, Asmat, Kamoro, Marind, Sentani, Korowai, Moi, Maybrat and others). **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what is home, and who is family?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Sougb, Tehit):** compare mountain stilt houses with lowland villages, and how exchange-cloth debts bind households.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen, Waropen:** check whether clan houses survive on the coast and how mission-era houses changed who shares a roof.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Dani/Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Nduga, Eipo):** set compounds with separate men's and women's houses beside single family houses in other valleys.
- **South coast and Asmat, with the Kamoro and Korowai:** check longhouses, river villages and high houses, and what resettlement did to household size.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu, Kimaam islanders):** check how swamp-garden households are housed and what settler towns changed.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** separate lake and bay villages from mixed town households.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Biak-descended and Muslim coastal families):** check island households tied to Tidore-era kin lines.
- **Migrant settler communities in towns and transmigration areas:** their state-built houses are part of today's picture, kept apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Ethnography covers a handful of peoples; statistics mix settlers with Papuans; most peoples have no household study. West Papua's exact lands are still to be settled; the Cancer sheets point at the Papuan lands of western New Guinea and the islands off its coasts.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for statistics; Dutch for captions, mission reports and early house descriptions; French for the voyage plates; each people's language for its kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's houses and some house spaces may be closed to women or outsiders; old photos of the dead or of sacred objects need community consent. Marriage, adoption and inheritance law belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no house or kin pointer here falls under the standing rules.
