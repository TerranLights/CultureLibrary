# West Papua: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; Dutch for Tidore-era and colonial records; Sentani, Hatam, Sougb, Marind, Kamoro, Biak and Ma'ya among others. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Hatam, Sougb, Meyah, Moile, Moi):** check how peoples grouped under one outside name mark their own boundaries.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak:** compare Biak at home with Biak communities spread along the coasts and in Raja Ampat.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Walak, Yali, Nduga):** check how highlanders living in coastal towns are received, and how they receive coastal Papuans.
- **South coast, Asmat and Mimika (Asmat, Kamoro, Sempan):** check how lowland groups keep their place as towns fill with newcomers.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Kimaam islanders):** check belonging where settlers have long outnumbered Papuans in the town.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Kayu Pulo, Nafri):** compare landholding clans with Papuans from elsewhere living on their land.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Biak Beser, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check Muslim and Christian Papuans' membership in faith and clan at once.
- **Migrant settlers and their Papua-born children:** their place is part of today's picture, examined apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; political writing on Papuan identity is plentiful, while everyday belonging inside villages and between Papuan peoples is thinly studied.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch and Tidore-era sources (often in Malay) for the sultanate frame; each people's language for its own self-name.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** identity talk is policed; protect speakers. "Who is Papuan" debates touch settlers, mixed families and politics at once; record all sides. Citizenship law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no belonging pointer here touches a held category.
