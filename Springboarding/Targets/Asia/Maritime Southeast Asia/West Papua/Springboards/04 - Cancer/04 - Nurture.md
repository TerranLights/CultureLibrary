# West Papua: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; English for Baliem ethnography; Dani/Hubula, Lani, Mee, Asmat, Kamoro, Ma'ya, Biak and other local languages for lullabies and teaching stories. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Arfak peoples, Maybrat, Moi):** check how children learn gardens and forest work, a nearly unstudied area.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak:** compare a long mission-school tradition with what families teach at home.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni):** check infant feeding and carrying valley by valley, beyond the Baliem record.
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai):** compare sago-camp childhoods with life in government villages and schools.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** check how nurture is described in towns with large settler populations.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** compare urban PAUD and church care with lake-village upbringing.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Biak-descended and Muslim families):** check sea-based childhoods and Qur'an or Sunday-school teaching side by side.
- **Migrant settler families:** their child-rearing styles shape town schools and markets today, studied apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; health and nutrition data are abundant but framed as deficits; how Papuans themselves describe raising a child is thinly recorded outside the Baliem.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English for Butt and the program evaluations; each local language for lullabies and teaching stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-nutrition figures have been used to blame Papuan mothers; read them against Papuan accounts. Some teaching belongs to initiation and may be closed. Child-discipline law goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the nurture sources above fall outside the held categories.
