# West Papua: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; English for archaeology and asylum records; Asmat, Korowai, Dani/Hubula, Mbaham-Matta, Ma'ya and other local languages for camp and guest-house words. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Arfak peoples):** check caves and garden huts as shelters, and how far deep-time sites matter to clans now.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor:** check guest lodging on islands where traders and church workers have long come and gone.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Nduga, Damal):** compare the lodging of travelers in men's houses with shelter for the displaced in churches and towns.
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai, Citak):** check forest camps as a second home, and what happens to them as villages settle.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** check seasonal camps and how town kin lodge visitors from the interior.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** check who from the interior (patients, students, the displaced) seeks a roof in town, and with whom.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** check fishing huts and coastal hospitality to strangers by sea.
- **Migrant settlers:** their lodging networks for new arrivals are part of today's towns, studied apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed; archaeology and disaster reporting exist, but customs of lodging and refuge are almost unstudied for most peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English for Pasveer and the asylum record; Dutch for older camp descriptions; local words for camps and guest places.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shelters for the displaced and safe houses must stay unnamed where naming endangers people; some caves are sacred and closed. Asylum and refugee law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no roof or refuge pointer here falls under the standing rules.
