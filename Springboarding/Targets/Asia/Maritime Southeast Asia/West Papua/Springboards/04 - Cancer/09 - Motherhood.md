# West Papua: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; Dutch for the Marind health record; Tobati, Sentani, Dani/Hubula, Asmat, Marind, Mbaham-Matta and other local languages for birth words and tales. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Hatam and Sougb of the Arfak, Maybrat, Moi):** check birth customs, the mother's kin claims, and whether exchange cloth enters them.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak:** check food taboos for new mothers and how mission hospitals changed birth.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee):** check where births take place and how mothering fits around gardens and pigs.
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai):** check mothering amid river travel and sago camps.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** set the old depopulation record against present-day births and the family-planning debate.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Tobati-Enggros, Sentani):** check urban births, *Hari Ibu* and tales like Towjatuwa's in their home area.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** check Muslim and Christian birth rites side by side.
- **Migrant settler mothers:** their births and clinics are part of the town picture today, studied apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; maternal-health figures exist at province level, while each people's own account of being a mother is barely written down.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch for the Marind inquiries; local languages for birth words, songs and tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth knowledge is often women's knowledge, to be gathered by and with women; the family-planning debate is politically charged; maternal-death data can stigmatize. Reproductive law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no birth, mother or tale pointer here falls under the standing rules.
