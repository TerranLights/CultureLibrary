# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian; Dutch and German for expedition, colonial and mission records; the peoples' own languages (Biak, Dani/Hubula, Lani, Mee, Asmat, Kamoro, Marind, Sentani, Ma'ya, Moi and others). **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how do people shine, play and take pleasure?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Tehit, Inanwatan):** check which dances belong to exchange events and which to church life.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen, Waropen:** trace how island song and dance genres moved from village occasions to stages and recordings.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Nduga):** separate village feasts from festival arenas built for visitors.
- **South coast, Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai:** compare carving made for feasts with carving made for sale.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu, Kimaam):** ask who runs the town's sports clubs and stages, and where Marind and Kanum dances are performed today.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri, Skouw):** set the lake festival beside campus arts and urban nightlife.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Biak Beser, Mbaham-Matta):** compare how Muslim and Christian Papuan villages mark their feast days.
- **Migrant settler communities:** their karaoke and sports clubs belong to today's picture, studied apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to thin. Arts and festivals are described for museums and tourists; play, romance and gambling are barely studied. West Papua's exact lands are still to be settled (the tracker carries Indonesia as its pre-war land); the Leo sheets are written for the Papuan lands of western New Guinea and the islands off its coasts.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; German for Wichmann; Dutch for mission reports; each people's language for song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some songs, carvings and dances belong to initiated men or one clan; filming needs consent. Political songs at performances can bring arrest. Marriage goes to Libra, sex to Scorpio, law to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing in this domain pass falls under the standing rules.
