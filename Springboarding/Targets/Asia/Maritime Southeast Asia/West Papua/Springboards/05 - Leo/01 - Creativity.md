# West Papua: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch, German and French for museum and collectors' records; Asmat, Kamoro, Biak, Sentani, Marind and highland languages for makers' own terms. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Inanwatan, Hatam):** check which prized objects are made at home and which arrive by trade, since that splits making from holding.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen:** check what figure carving became after conversion: prows, drums, songs, or nothing.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Eipo):** ask whether these languages have a word for "art" or only words for making net bags, adzes, arrows and ornaments.
- **South coast, Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai, Awyu:** compare named masters with village styles, and contest pieces with sale pieces.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Kimaam islanders):** check which feast objects and masks are still made and who teaches them.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Skouw):** check which villages claim which motifs and who may carve them.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** look at boat-building and plaiting in Muslim and Christian villages.
- **Migrant craft and batik workshops in towns:** part of today's market, examined apart from Papuan makers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Museum and art-history writing is rich for Asmat, Kamoro, Sentani and *korwar*; thin for highland making, Bird's Head crafts and everyday improvising.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch, German and French for collection records; Indonesian for heritage files and the press; Asmat, Kamoro and Biak for the makers' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some objects in museums are sacred or ancestral, and return claims may be live; some designs may be shown only by their owners. Ownership law goes to Taurus and Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no maker or collection here touches a held category.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Smidt's *Asmat Art* essays and list carvers named by village.
2. Search the quai Branly and Basel online catalogs for "Sentani" and "Marind"; log dates and collectors.
3. Search the *Warisan Budaya Takbenda* site for every Papuan entry and its nominating body.
4. Search "Udeido" and "motif batik Papua" in the press.
5. Search the Balai Arkeologi *Papua* journal for "gerabah" and "ukiran".
