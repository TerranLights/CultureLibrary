# West Papua: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English for rights reporting; Dutch for older records; Biak, Dani/Hubula, Lani, Asmat, Muyu and other languages for songs and laments. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Tehit, Hatam):** check where grievances and feelings are voiced aloud, at exchange gatherings or in church, and by whom.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Yapen, Waropen:** check which sung genres carry teasing or complaint and whether young singers still learn them.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Nduga):** compare festival dress with what highland youth wear in Wamena and Jayapura.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check whether a dance or a carving carries a person's own story or only the group's.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu, Yei):** check how Muyu returnees from the border camps and local youth mark themselves in dress and speech.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** set student protest speech beside village meeting speech in Sentani and Tobati-Enggros.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** check how Muslim Papuan women combine head covering with customary dress at festivals.
- **Settler youth and Papua-born children of settlers:** part of today's style scene, compared apart from the core.
- **Exiles in the Netherlands and Vanuatu:** comparison only; check what they perform abroad that is not performed at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political expression is well recorded by rights groups; everyday emotional display, personal style and youth culture are thinly studied.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for press and online talk; English for rights reports; each people's language for laments and songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** flags, songs and speeches can lead to arrest; never name living speakers without consent. Gender and sexual minorities among Papuans are barely documented and exposed to risk; tread carefully. Speech law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no expression pointer here falls under a held category.
