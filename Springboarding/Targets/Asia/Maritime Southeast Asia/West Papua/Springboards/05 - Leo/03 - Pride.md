# West Papua: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch for the 1950s and 1960s; Biak, Marind, Maybrat, Dani/Hubula and other languages for songs, mottoes and tales. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Tehit, Hatam):** check what a household puts on show at cloth exchanges and how boasting there is judged.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen:** check how voyaging and warrior tales are retold today and who claims them.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni):** check how valley pride shows at festivals and football, and how elders rein in boasting.
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro, Sempan):** compare pride in a village's carving fame with pride in a single carver's name.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Kimaam):** check how local pride sits in a town whose official motto borrows Marind words.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** look at football fandom and at the pride of clans that hold the town's land.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** check how old titles and Islamic learning are spoken of in Muslim Papuan families.
- **Settler communities and their Papua-born children:** their homeland associations belong to today's picture, studied apart.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Group and political pride fill the press; personal pride, the line between pride and arrogance, and praise of children are thinly studied.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Biak for *Sampari* and *mambri* tales; Marind for the Merauke motto; Dutch for 1961 records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** showing the Morning Star flag can lead to prosecution; never record names of those who raise it. Answers to national-pride polls in Papua may be shaped by fear; read them with care.
- **Held for review:** none; pride pointers here stay clear of every held category.
