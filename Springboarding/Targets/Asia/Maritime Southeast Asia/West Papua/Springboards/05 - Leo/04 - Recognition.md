# West Papua: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English for regional and UN statements; Biak, Amungme, Maybrat, Sentani and highland languages for names, titles and feast speech. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Tehit, Moi):** check how givers are named at cloth exchanges and who keeps the record of debts.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Wondama:** check which ancestors and evangelists get monuments and holidays, and which are forgotten.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Mee, Yali, Nduga):** compare how contributors to pig feasts and peace payments are thanked aloud.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check credit for carvers whose works hang in museums with no name attached.
- **Amungme and Kamoro of Mimika:** set the peoples' own place names beside company and state maps.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check how plans for large estates acknowledge customary owners on paper and in meetings.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** check how government and church recognize *ondofolo* titles.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** check whether old sultanate-era titles are still acknowledged, and by whom.
- **Settler residents:** check who receives town honors and contracts, as part of today's picture, kept apart from the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Formal honors and rights acknowledgments are online; everyday thanks, feast credit and recognition between peoples are unwritten.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for honors and state statements; English for Pacific and UN records; Biak for Koreri tellings; Amungme for summit names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** acknowledgment of past violations is politically charged; protect victims' families. Summit names are tied to the mine and to security operations named after them.
- **Held for review:** none; the award lists, state statements and monuments named here involve no held category.
