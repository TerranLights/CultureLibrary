# West Papua: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; English, Dutch and Russian for outsiders' accounts; Mee, Dani/Hubula, Asmat, Amungme and other languages for the peoples' own tellings. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

English keywords: outsiders' images of Papuans, first contact accounts, media access West Papua, surveillance Papua, representation of Papuans, census of Indigenous Papuans.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head and the Kaimana coast (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Mairasi, Irarutu):** check which groups early visitors recorded and how those first meetings are told today.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor:** check which coastal villages were drawn and photographed most, and what villagers say of those pictures.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni):** separate valleys that became the outside world's image of Papua from those barely photographed.
- **South coast (Asmat, Korowai, Awyu, Wambon):** compare how the most-filmed peoples speak of camera crews with peoples nearby who were never filmed.
- **Amungme, Kamoro and Moni near the mine and the summit:** check whose names and labor appear in company and climbing stories.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check how they are counted against newcomers in census and regency data.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** look at students' and journalists' experience of being watched on campus and in town.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Biak Beser):** check whether dive-tourism images show or leave out the villagers.
- **Settler residents:** check how they appear in local media, as part of today's picture.
- **Papuan students in Javanese and Sulawesi cities:** comparison only; check how being stared at or watched there is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** uneven. How outsiders saw Papuans is richly recorded in books, films and photos; how Papuans wish to be seen, and their own customs of gaze and greeting, are thinly written.
- **Languages needed:** English and Dutch for expedition and press writing; Russian for Miklouho-Maclay (translations exist); Indonesian; Mee and Dani for the peoples' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** old images often show people as exotic or show the dead; ask before reusing. Surveillance means sources may be at risk; protect names.
- **Held for review:** none; being-seen pointers here touch no held category.
