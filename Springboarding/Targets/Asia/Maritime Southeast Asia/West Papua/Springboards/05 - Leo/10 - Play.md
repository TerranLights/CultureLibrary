# West Papua: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; German and Dutch for ethological and expedition records; English for comparative work; the peoples' own languages (Eipo, Dani/Hubula, Biak, Asmat, Marind, Moi and others) for game names. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam) and Sorong town:** compare games in mountain villages with street play in Sorong.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen:** check water play, canoe games and swimming, and which games come from sea life.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Eipo, Mee):** look at village children's games, festival archery and how churches treat cards and dancing.
- **South coast, Asmat, Kamoro, Korowai:** check toys made from sago and wood, and how children's time is split between work, school and play.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** set savanna-village play beside town playgrounds and tournaments.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** check mobile gaming, campus sport and lake play among children of the lake villages.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** check what children play on feast days in Muslim and Christian villages.
- **Displaced children (Nduga, Intan Jaya, Maybrat):** check how play is kept up in camps and church shelters.
- **Settler children and esports clubs:** part of today's play scene, studied apart from the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin. Beyond festival contests and football, children's games and adult play are barely recorded; the Eipo studies are a rare close record.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; German for Eibl-Eibesfeldt; Dutch for *Nova Guinea*; each people's language for game names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Independence Day games carry political weight in some places; children in camps need consent and protection; some games may belong to initiated boys only.
- **Held for review:** none; no play pointer here falls in a held category.
