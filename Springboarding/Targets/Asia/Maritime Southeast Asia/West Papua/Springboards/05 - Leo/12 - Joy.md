# West Papua: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch and English for ethnography; Biak, Lani, Dani/Hubula, Asmat, Kamoro and other languages for words, songs and feasts of gladness. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Sougb):** check which occasions call for stamping dances, and how church holidays changed them.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor, Yapen:** check sea festivals and the welcome of returning travelers.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni):** compare harvest, pig-feast and Christmas joy, and greetings between kin.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check feasts where mourning and celebration meet.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Kimaam):** check which celebrations are their own and which are town-wide fairs.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** look at victory convoys, New Year fireworks and bay festivals.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** check visits between Muslim and Christian households on Idul Fitri and Christmas.
- **Settler communities:** their festivals are part of today's calendar, studied apart from the core.
- **Families of the displaced and the detained:** check when joy is held back in mourning or danger, and when it is shown anyway.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Festivals and songs are well recorded for tourism and schools; the inner sense of joy, its words and its rules are barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch and English for Kamoro and Asmat ethnography; Biak, Lani and Dani for joy words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** celebrations on contested dates or for released prisoners can bring arrests; some feasts include parts closed to outsiders. Pleasure in food belongs to Taurus, grief and death to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none; every song, feast and festival named for joy is clear of the held categories.
