# West Papua: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for jobs, wages and press; English for plantation and mining studies; Hubula (Grand Valley Dani), Marind and the coastal languages for garden, sago and fishing words. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam, Inanwatan):** see whether timber, oil and civil-service jobs are spoken of as "work" in the same way as gardens.
- **Cenderawasih Bay (Biak, Numfor, Yapen, Waropen, Wondama):** check how sailing, fishing and early mission schooling shaped which trades people sought.
- **Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Walak, Mee, Moni, Damal):** compare garden labor with coffee, gold panning and town jobs, and who goes to each.
- **Mimika and the south coast (Amungme, Kamoro, Asmat, Sempan):** set mine employment beside sago and river work, and look at who is hired.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu, Mandobo, Wambon):** check how estate jobs, sago and rubber tapping sit side by side.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Nafri, Skouw, Kemtuk):** compare office jobs, lake fishing and street trade as ways to earn.
- **Islands and the southwest coast (Matbat, Ma'ya, Iha, Mairasi, Mbaham-Matta):** separate fishing, nutmeg and tourism work, and see how prayer days pace them.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settlers hold much town trade, transport and estate work; part of the present picture, kept apart from Papuan work ways.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Mining and plantation labor are well reported because of conflict; garden labor, women's paid work and young people's job hopes are thin outside local theses.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for company, union and BPS sources; Hubula and Marind to see how "work" divides into tasks; English for Chao and mining studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mine and gold-field reporting touches armed conflict and security operations; protect informants. Labor law is not for this sheet (Libra and Capricorn); pay and self-assertion are Aries.
- **Held for review:** none; the work pointers (estates, mines, fields, sermons) needed no holding.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Bromley's word lists for Hubula terms for work and tasks, and note which share a root.
2. Search "asal usul sagu" and "mitos ubi jalar" for origin tales by people.
3. Read Chao's chapters on estate work, then search "buruh sawit orang asli Papua" for later reports.
4. Search "pengangguran terdidik Papua" and "Institut Pertambangan Nemangkawi" in the press archive.
5. Search seminary repositories for "etos kerja" with "Papua".
