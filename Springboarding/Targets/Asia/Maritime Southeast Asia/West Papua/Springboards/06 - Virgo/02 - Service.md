# West Papua: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (church, civil-service and military vocabulary); Papuan Malay; Dutch for expedition and wartime files; Japanese-era terms as borrowed into Indonesian; Biak, Ma'ya and highland languages for duties owed to kin and leaders. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Wondama:** trace how the early mission centers sent out evangelists, and whether that service is still a family or clan tradition.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Matbat, Mbaham-Matta, Onin):** compare memories of tribute and fleet service to Tidore with mosque and church duties now.
- **Highlands (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Wolani, Ketengban):** check who carried for missions, explorers and soldiers, and how that service is recalled.
- **South coast and Mimika (Asmat, Kamoro, Sempan, Jaqai):** look at lay catechists and the service roles men's houses once held.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Kimaam):** check service to clan elders in feast work against service in estate and town jobs.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Kayu Pulo, Ormu):** compare civil service counters with customary service owed inside clans.
- **Bird's Head (Arfak peoples, Maybrat, Moi, Tehit):** see how church offices and village posts are shared between clans.
- **Migrant settler communities:** many shop, transport and counter jobs are held by settlers; who serves whom in towns is part of today's picture, studied apart from Papuan forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Church and mission histories are rich; forced and wartime service is recorded unevenly; customer service and personal service are almost unstudied for Papuans.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the 1920 exploration report and carrier files; Indonesian for church orders and Ombudsman data; Biak and Ma'ya for tribute memories that rarely reach print.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** service in the police and army is tied to armed conflict; families of the conscripted may not wish to be named. Duty and rank are Capricorn's concepts, and any legal side of service is left to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the evangelist, wartime and civil-service pointers raised nothing to hold.
