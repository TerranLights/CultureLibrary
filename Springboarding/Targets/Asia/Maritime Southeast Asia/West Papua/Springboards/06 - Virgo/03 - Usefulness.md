# West Papua: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; early modern Dutch for plant records; the peoples' plant and tool names (Kanum, Marind, Asmat, Hubula, Sentani, Ma'ya and others), found mostly in theses and word lists. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or a piece of knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Makleuw):** compare how "useful land" is judged by clans and by planners, since estate projects turn on that word.
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Citak, Awyu):** check how sago and river knowledge mark a person as needed, and how carving for sale changes that.
- **Highlands (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Ketengban, Una):** see how the old stone-axe makers' skills were regarded before and after steel arrived.
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Sougb, Meyah, Irarutu):** compare elders' standing as land witnesses with their standing in town households.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Ansus, Waropen, Wandamen):** check whether skills of the sea and of the church office decide who is needed.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Nafri, Tobati-Enggros):** look at language shift and vocational schooling, where "useful" knowledge is debated most openly.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Matbat, Biak Beser) and the Kaimana coast:** see how tourism has changed which skills and which family members are counted on.
- **Migrant settler communities:** check whether settler-run trades and training centers set town skill standards; part of today's scene, studied on their own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** useful-plant studies are many; how people feel about being useful, or a burden, is barely written down in any Papuan language, so theses and interviews will carry most of it.
- **Languages needed:** early Dutch and Latin for Rumphius; Indonesian for training-center and land-project sources; Kanum, Marind and Hubula for plant, tool and age-role words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some plant knowledge belongs to clans or healers and is not for print; land-project reporting is politically charged. Land law goes to Libra and Capricorn; self-worth to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** none; plant, tool, land and training pointers set nothing aside.
