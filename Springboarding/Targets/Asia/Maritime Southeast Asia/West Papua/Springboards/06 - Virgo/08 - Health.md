# West Papua: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for health-ministry, outbreak and insurance sources; English for malaria and tropical-medicine research; Dutch for 1950s campaign reports; Papuan Malay for how sickness is described; Arfak, Biak, Asmat and Marind languages for remedies and causes of illness. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Sawi, Citak):** compare how measles, malaria and hunger are explained by families, churches and officials.
- **Highlands (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Nduga, Moni):** check how clinics, pastors and healers share the sick, and how conflict cuts care.
- **Bird's Head (Hatam, Sougb, Meyah, Moi):** look at bark and plant remedies and how they are taken alongside hospital medicine.
- **Biak, Numfor and Yapen:** see how old mission hospitals shaped trust in biomedicine compared with inland valleys.
- **Merauke lowlands and Kimaam (Marind, Kanum, Ndom, Riantana):** check filariasis and diet change, and how illness is linked to lost land.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** compare urban diabetes, HIV and mental-health care, where services are densest.
- **Raja Ampat and the Kaimana coast (Ma'ya, Biak Beser, Koiwai):** see what island families do between clinic boat visits.
- **Migrant settler communities:** many doctors, nurses and pharmacy owners come from elsewhere; their role in care is part of the present scene, studied apart from Papuan ideas of health.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Biomedical and outbreak data are fairly rich; Papuan accounts of what health is and what causes illness are thin outside a few ethnographies; mental health is barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** English for malaria research; Dutch for the yaws campaigns; Indonesian for ministry and insurance data; Arfak, Biak and Asmat terms for remedies and causes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** HIV status, mental illness and sorcery accusations need strict privacy; some healing knowledge belongs to its holders. Conflict reporting risks informants. Care and nurture belong to Cancer; death to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none; disease, remedy and insurance pointers raised nothing to hold.
