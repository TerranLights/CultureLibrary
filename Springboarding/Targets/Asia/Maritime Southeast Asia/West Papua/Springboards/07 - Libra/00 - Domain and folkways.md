# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for statutes, court files and registries; Papuan Malay for everyday talk of deals and quarrels; Dutch for colonial adat-law reports; Biak, Hubula (Dani), Sentani, Mee and Marind for marriage payments, councils and peacemaking. **Concept in one line:** the domain of partnership and justice: what is a fair bargain and a good partnership (marriage, contracts, open enemies, settling disputes, gender ways, and the letter of the law)?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Highlands and the Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Walak, Nduga):** check how war alliances, pig payments and marriage exchange link up, where partner and enemy sit side by side.
- **Mee, Moni and Wolani of the Paniai lakes:** compare loans, debts and settlement; this area has the oldest written legal ethnography.
- **Biak, Numfor and Cenderawasih Bay:** trace bride-wealth processions and old ties to Tidore, a sea-borne model of alliance.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Tehit, Sougb, Hatam):** look at cloth payments in marriage and debt, to see where exchange becomes obligation.
- **Sentani, Jayapura and Nimboran:** check the *ondoafi* system against town courts, where custom and statute meet.
- **South coast and Merauke (Asmat, Kamoro, Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** compare marriage exchange between groups and the arrival of plantation and mine contracts.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** separate Muslim marriage and religious-court practice from church and adat forms.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settlers from elsewhere in Indonesia marry, trade and litigate in the towns; part of today's picture, kept apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Statutes and judgments are open; adat rules are scattered in theses and colonial reports. West Papua's lands are not yet settled (the tracker lists only Indonesia as the pre-war land); this sign is scouted for the western half of New Guinea and its islands.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for law and courts; Dutch for the adat-law bundles and Broekhuijse; Biak, Sentani and Hubula for council and payment terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** treason trials, the 1969 vote and armed conflict are dangerous topics; protect witnesses' and litigants' names. Some peacemaking rites are closed to outsiders. Legitimacy of courts and trust in police belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the colonial adat-law and treaty pointers raise no issue under the standing rules, so the domain list stands whole.
