# West Papua: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for codes, judgments and rights reports; Papuan Malay for demands and testimony; Dutch for colonial court and patrol records; Mee, Hubula, Lani, Biak and Amungme for wrongs, payments and the tales that judge them. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee of Paniai and Deiyai:** compare old case-by-case settlement with present rights cases; both are recorded in depth.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali of the Baliem and its rim:** check who demands and who pays compensation after a death, and when police step aside or step in.
- **Amungme and Kamoro of Mimika:** look at how mine-related deaths and land loss are pursued, in court and through company funds.
- **Biak and Numfor:** trace the Koreri story of vindication and the 1998 events, to see how a historical wrong is named.
- **Nduga and Intan Jaya:** check how displacement and military operations reach (or fail to reach) any court.
- **Jayapura, Sentani and Abepura:** separate town criminal justice, where most trials and prisons are, from village handling of wrongs.
- **Merauke (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** compare plantation land disputes in court with adat claims for compensation.
- **Fakfak and Raja Ampat Muslim communities:** see whether religious courts and Islamic teaching on wrongs shape settlement.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settler drivers, traders and victims meet adat claims in towns; their cases belong to the present-day picture, held apart from Papuan justice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Judgments and NGO reports are plentiful for political cases; ordinary adat settlements leave few records outside theses and the press.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for codes and verdicts; Mee for Pospisil's cases; Biak for Manarmakeri tellings; Dutch for older court and patrol registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims, witnesses and defendants in political trials face real danger; never record names from case files. Trust in courts and whether the law is legitimate belong to Capricorn; revenge to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none; every court, rights and oral-tradition lead stayed.
