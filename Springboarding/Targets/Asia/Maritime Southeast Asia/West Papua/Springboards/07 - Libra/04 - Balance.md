# West Papua: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for press law, provincial structure and nutrition guides; Papuan Malay for talk of a body or a life out of order; Dutch for older studies of exchange; Maybrat for cloth circulation, Hubula for moieties, Biak for closed seasons, Amungme and Marind for land teachings. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maybrat, Tehit and Aifat:** check how cloth debts are kept moving and what counts as a debt left unbalanced too long.
- **Hubula, Lani, Walak and Nduga:** compare moiety pairing and war payments to see whether matched halves frame exchange.
- **Amungme and Kamoro:** look at how mountain and lowland neighbors describe damage to land and water in their own terms.
- **Biak, Numfor and Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Matbat):** compare who has the right to close and reopen a reef.
- **Marind, Kanum and Yei of Merauke:** check how park rules, plantations and burning seasons are weighed.
- **Sentani and Tobati:** see how the Cyclops floods are talked about, by elders, churches and officials.
- **Mee and Moni:** check whether the sense of a body or a household "out of order" is described in health terms or kin terms.
- **Migrant settler communities:** transmigrant farms and town growth press on forests and reefs; measure that pressure as a present-day factor, distinct from Papuan teachings of balance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Statutes, court rulings and park records are online; teachings on land and body are thin in print and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for law and health rules; Maybrat and Hubula for exchange and moiety terms; Biak for closed-season words; Dutch for older film and survey notes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some land and healing teachings are clan property; ask before recording. The Sentani floods caused deaths. Health as a whole is Virgo's; stability is Taurus's.
- **Held for review:** none; exchange, moiety, park and press-law leads raised nothing.
