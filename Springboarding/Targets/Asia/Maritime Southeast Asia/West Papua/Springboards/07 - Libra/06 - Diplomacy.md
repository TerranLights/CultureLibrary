# West Papua: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for dialogue initiatives, ministry and commission records; Papuan Malay for how people talk a quarrel down; Dutch for VOC and colonial treaty files and the 1950s-60s Pacific conferences; English for Pacific and UN records; Biak, Sentani, Nduga and the Kaimana coast languages for welcome, envoy and truce customs. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Supiori:** check how sea-borne envoys, titles and welcome songs were used with Tidore and the Dutch, and how they are used now.
- **Kaimana and Fakfak coast (Koiwai, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** compare raja-house protocol with outsiders across three centuries of contact.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya, Matbat, Biak Beser):** look at how island heads negotiate with dive operators and conservation bodies.
- **Nduga, Lani and Yali:** study truce-making between clans and talks with armed groups, where go-betweens risk most.
- **Hubula of the Baliem:** compare festival welcome protocols with real negotiation between valley alliances.
- **Mee, Moni and Wolani:** check how dialogue proposals were received and argued in the Paniai churches.
- **Sentani and Tobati:** see how *para-para adat* sittings and *ondoafi* run deliberation.
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro, Marind):** compare river-mouth exchanges with traders, missions and companies.
- **Bird's Head (Arfak peoples, Moi, Maybrat):** look at how land talks with oil, timber and road projects are opened and closed.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settlers' regional associations negotiate with Papuan bodies after town clashes; log those talks as today's layer, not as Papuan protocol.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. National dialogue efforts and hostage cases are documented; everyday tact and village protocol are thin and must be heard directly.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for dialogue texts; Dutch for Fort Du Bus and New Guinea Council files; English for UN and Pacific records; Biak and Sentani for welcome and deliberation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence diplomacy, armed groups and hostage talks are dangerous; protect negotiators and go-betweens. Some welcome rites belong to clans. Peace and truces belong to this sign's peace concept; truth against tact to Sagittarius.
