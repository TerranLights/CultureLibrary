# West Papua: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay for everyday give-and-take; Indonesian for surveys, church giving and savings groups; Dutch for older exchange ethnography; Moi, Sougb, Hatam, Asmat, Sentani, Biak and Hubula for counter-gifts, funeral payments and exchange partners. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moi, Sougb, Hatam and the Arfak peoples:** check how counter-gifts in marriage and debt are timed, and who keeps the account.
- **Maybrat and Tehit:** compare cloth returns with Moi webs of debt to see whether neighboring peoples keep accounts alike.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** look at funeral pig gifts and their return, and how war deaths create debts between alliances.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** study ritual exchange partners and feast duties along the rivers.
- **Sentani and Tobati:** trace lake-coast trade in pots and sago, and town fundraising feasts.
- **Biak and Numfor:** check old tribute ties and present church giving in island congregations.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** compare Muslim and Christian festival gifts and how families return them.
- **Merauke (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** look at exchanges between villages at feasts and how plantation wages change who can return a gift.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settlers run many town savings groups and sell on credit; check how shop debt and gift debt differ, treating the settler side as a present-day layer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Exchange is well described for a few peoples in Dutch and English ethnography; savings groups, remittances and church giving appear in theses and the press; many peoples are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Haenen; Indonesian for surveys and theses; Moi, Sougb and Hubula for counter-gift words; Asmat for partner terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** headhunting history is painful for Asmat communities today; cite with care and their own accounts first. Some exchange ties are kept private. Generosity is Leo's; trust and revenge Scorpio's.
- **Held for review:** none; gift, funeral and savings-group leads needed no setting aside.
