# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for statistics, church and press material; Papuan Malay for how death, taboo and pooling are talked about day to day; Dutch for mission and colonial medical records; Biak, Hubula (Dani), Moi, Asmat and Marind for the dead, the hidden and the shared. **Concept in one line:** the domain of death and transformation: how death, power and what is hidden are faced (death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance, secrets).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Cenderawasih Bay:** check how the house dead were kept and later set aside, to date the change.
- **Central Highlands (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Walak):** compare cremation, burial and pig debts at death, since conversion reached valleys at different times.
- **Asmat and Kamoro on the south coast:** separate feasts that release the dead from carving revivals made for buyers.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** test colonial depopulation records against what families say happened.
- **Moi, Maybrat and Arfak of the Bird's Head:** check how closures, cloth and inheritance tie the living to shared land.
- **Fakfak, Kaimana and the Raja Ampat islands:** set Muslim death and inheritance practice beside church and older forms.
- **Sentani and Jayapura:** look at town funerals, livestreams and mutual-aid groups.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note their separate cemeteries and burial societies as part of today's towns, without letting them stand for Papuan death ways.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: statistics and church material exist, mortuary ethnography is rich for a few peoples, sex ways are thin. Which lands the nation holds is not yet fixed (its tracker names only "Indonesia" as the pre-war land); these Scorpio sheets take the core to be western New Guinea with Biak, Yapen and Raja Ampat.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch above all for the depopulation and mission files; Biak and Moi to read *korwar* and *egek* in their own terms; Indonesian and Papuan Malay for funerals and inheritance today.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ancestors' remains, seclusion customs and sexual health are private; names of the dead and of people in HIV outreach stay out. Some funeral rites are closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none; the domain's death, inheritance and sex pointers hold no item that the standing rules set aside.
