# West Papua: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for health data, registers and press; Papuan Malay for talk around a death; Dutch for mission and colonial accounts of the dead; Asmat, Korowai, Biak and the Mimika (Kamoro) language for what happens to the dead and where they go. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are researched here, funeral practice belongs to the domain sheet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Asmat river villages:** check whether *jipae* and pole feasts still mark a death or now serve festival and sale.
- **Korowai and Kombai:** compare talk of the dead with talk of strangers, since *laleo* joins the two.
- **Mimika (Kamoro) beside the Amungme uplands:** separate coastal and mountain treatment of the dead along one mine corridor.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** check the time and marks of mourning valley by valley, and what changed with church burial.
- **Biak, Numfor and the lake people of Sentani:** look at graves kept near houses as a coastal habit to be tested.
- **Ma'ya and Matbat of Raja Ampat:** compare cave burial evidence with present Muslim and Christian graves.
- **Muyu and Mandobo near the border:** check how death among refugees and returnees is remembered.
- **Settler families in the towns:** their cemeteries and funeral customs are part of today's mixed towns, not of the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Modeled statistics and violence counts exist; death registration is weak; Asmat, Kamoro and Korowai have deep ethnography while the Bird's Head and Merauke are thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Asmat and Korowai for the land of the dead and the walking dead; Biak for the soul; Dutch for mission accounts of mourning; Indonesian for registry and violence data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names of the dead may be avoided; photographs of bodies and remains need consent; violent deaths are politically charged; suicide needs care in how cases are cited.
- **Held for review:** none; every pointer on dying and the dead stayed on the list.
