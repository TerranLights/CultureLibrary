# West Papua: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for church, rehab and disaster records; Papuan Malay for testimony and street talk; Dutch for early Biak and mission sources; Biak, Western Dani (Lani), Maybrat, Ngalum and Marind for renewal teachings and rites of passage. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Supiori, Biak and Numfor:** ask how Koreri expectation is told now: as history, as faith or as politics.
- **Western Dani, Lani and Walak:** compare first-generation and later converts on what "new life" meant at the time.
- **Maybrat, Tehit and Imian of the Bird's Head:** test whether initiation survives beside school and church.
- **Ngalum and the other Mountain Ok groups:** check how much of initiation may be described at all.
- **Asmat and Marind on the south coast:** separate revival for the community from revival for festivals and visitors.
- **Jayapura and Merauke youth:** look at street-to-rehab stories in town programs.
- **The Raja Ampat islands:** compare reef renewal talk with older closure customs.
- **Settler communities:** converts and returnees among migrants belong to the present picture, not to the Papuan core of this concept.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Koreri and Dani conversion are well covered; initiation in the Bird's Head and Star Mountains, rehab and disaster recovery are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Feuilletau de Bruyn and mission files; Biak and Lani for the renewal teachings; Maybrat and Ngalum for the rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation teaching is often secret; Koreri is a political symbol and may draw state attention; youths in rehab are minors.
- **Held for review:** none; the conversion and revival sources raise nothing to set aside.
