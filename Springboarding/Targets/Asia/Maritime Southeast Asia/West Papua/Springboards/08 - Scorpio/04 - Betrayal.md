# West Papua: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for press, movement history and household statistics; Papuan Malay for the insults and accusations people trade; Dutch for the 1960s and for Tidore-era alliance records; Biak, Maybrat, Muyu and Hubula for broken promises inside clans and marriages. **Concept in one line:** breaking the trust of those who relied on one: turning against, deserting, deceiving or exposing people or a cause to which one owed loyalty.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Yapen:** ask how stories of abandonment by foreign allies sit beside older Koreri hopes.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** compare how adultery is settled in pigs or money, and who may demand it.
- **Maybrat and Tehit:** look at unpaid cloth debts as a test of where obligation ends and betrayal begins.
- **Muyu, Mandobo and Marind near the border:** check how suspicion and collaboration are remembered after flight and return.
- **Sentani and Jayapura:** separate political sellout talk in town from betrayal inside families.
- **Ma'ya and the Raja Ampat ruling lines:** test the old side-switching record against local tellings.
- **Muslim families of Fakfak and Sorong:** compare how a broken marriage is handled by mosque, church and clan.
- **Settler households:** their own marriage and divorce patterns are part of today's town picture, not of the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political betrayal is well documented; betrayal inside marriages and clans lives in press items and theses; native vocabulary is a real gap.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Nuku-era and 1960s files; Biak for the Kaisiepo-era sources; Maybrat for cloth debts; Indonesian for the statistics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** traitor labels can endanger people; names in adultery and abuse cases stay out; movement splits are still raw.
- **Held for review:** none; betrayal's pointers raised no case under the standing rules.
