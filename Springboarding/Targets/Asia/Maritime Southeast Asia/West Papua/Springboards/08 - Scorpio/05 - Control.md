# West Papua: Control (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for security, permits, schools and press; Papuan Malay for how people speak of being watched and of what lies in God's hands; Dutch for the opening of government posts; Arfak (Hatam and Sougb), Hubula, Mee, Sentani, Biak and Kanum for control of land, water, animals and households. **Concept in one line:** directing or restraining people, events and one's own life, and the felt need to do so; self-control proper belongs to Capricorn, freedom from control to Aries.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hubula and Lani of the Baliem:** check how ownership of salt, gardens and pigs sets the rules of access.
- **Paniai lakes (Mee, Moni):** compare memories of the first government post with present security posts.
- **Moni, Nduga and Lani in conflict regencies:** look at how people talk about being stopped, searched or moved.
- **Arfak, Abun and Miyah of the Bird's Head:** test conservation declarations against clan say over forest.
- **Sentani lake villages and the Biak and Padaido islands:** compare how fresh and salt water are closed to others.
- **Kanum and Marind of the Merauke grasslands:** check who controls fire and who is blamed when it spreads.
- **Raja Ampat fishing villages:** separate park patrols run by villages from those run by outsiders.
- **Settler-owned businesses and households in towns:** their place in money, transport and trade is part of the present picture, not the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Security, permits and conservation rules are documented; household control and fate talk are thinly written and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Arfak and Abun for forest rules; Kanum for fire; Dutch for post histories; Papuan Malay for talk about God's arranging and being helpless.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** security-post reporting can endanger informants; household abuse cases need anonymity; some resource rules are clan property.
- **Held for review:** none; every control pointer passed the standing rules.
