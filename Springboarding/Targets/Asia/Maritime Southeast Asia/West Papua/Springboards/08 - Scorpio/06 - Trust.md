# West Papua: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for surveys, health data and press; Papuan Malay for credit, promises and suspicion in daily dealings; Dutch for early trade and mission records; Mee, Asmat, Biak, Marind and Hubula for exchange partners, adopted kin and what seals a deal. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down, in the expectation of not being harmed; loyalty is researched here for now.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mee and Moni of Paniai:** check how trust in shell money gave way to cash and banks.
- **Asmat and Mappi river villages:** compare dealings with outside buyers across the eaglewood and timber years.
- **Lani, Nduga and Moni in conflict areas:** look at how suspicion of strangers shapes trade and travel.
- **Biak and Yapen islanders in Manokwari and Jayapura:** check which bonds, kin or church, carry trust away from home.
- **Ma'ya and Biak-descended fishers of Raja Ampat:** compare credit from traders with credit from kin.
- **Marind and other Merauke peoples beside transmigrant villages:** test the motto's trust against market and land dealings.
- **Hubula of the Baliem and Sentani lake villages:** compare whom patients trust first, clinic or healer.
- **Settler kiosk owners and traders:** they decide who gets credit in many towns today; record their side of the ledger as a distinct voice, not as the Papuan view of trust.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. National trust surveys barely sample Papua; vaccination and bank data exist by region; everyday trust needs theses and fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Mee for shell money; Asmat for trade with outsiders; Papuan Malay for credit talk; Dutch for early barter records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informer suspicion can cost lives; health fears touch on charged claims about the state; church-fund cases name people.
- **Held for review:** none; the trust list held nothing for the standing rules to remove.
