# West Papua: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for press and rights reporting on clashes; Papuan Malay for payback talk among neighbors and youths; Dutch for mission letters on raids and the colonial *sneltocht* files; Damal, Amungme, Hubula, Lani, Asmat and the Arfak languages for owed deaths and the ghosts who press for them. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong; with forgiveness and making amends as its alternatives.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Damal, Dani and Amungme settlers at Kwamki Lama:** check who counts the deaths owed and who may call a fight closed.
- **Lani and Damal of Puncak:** compare fights tied to elections with older causes.
- **Hubula of the Baliem:** look at how the dead are said to press for payback, and what church teaching changed.
- **Arfak peoples and Numfor-Biak coastal settlers:** test the old raid records against both sides' memories.
- **Asmat river villages:** separate Asmat tellings of old feuds from outside headhunting writing.
- **Moi, Auyu and Wambon facing company land:** check when damage to equipment is framed as justice and when as crime.
- **Raja Ampat island villages (Ma'ya, Matbat, Biak-descended):** compare sea-boundary quarrels with older ways of settling them.
- **Settler drivers, traders and workers:** when they are caught in payback, their accounts form a distinct present-day thread, not the Papuan view.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Press and rights reports on clashes are plentiful; the native vocabulary of owed deaths and the inner logic of payback are thinly written outside a few classic ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Damal and Amungme for Mimika fights; Hubula and Lani for owed deaths; Asmat for raid histories; Dutch for the mission letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names of fighters and the dead can draw new attacks; clan-war reporting can feed stereotypes, so record variation; intimate-image cases need victims protected. How the state punishes, set against private payback, belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the payback pointers needed nothing set aside.
