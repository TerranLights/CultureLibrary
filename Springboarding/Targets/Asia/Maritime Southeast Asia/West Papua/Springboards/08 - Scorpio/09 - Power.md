# West Papua: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for elections, mining, budgets and press; Papuan Malay for talk of bosses, big men and pull; English for policy studies; Hubula, Lani, Mee, Biak, Arfak and Marind for big men, war leaders and clan heads. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how it is judged who holds it and how it is used; legitimate authority and power ways belong to Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hubula, Lani and Walak:** check how standing is earned through war, pigs and speech, and what elected office adds.
- **Mee of Paniai:** compare lending-based standing with church and regency posts.
- **The Mimika mine corridor (Amungme uplands, Kamoro coast):** check how mine money shifts who leads.
- **Biak and Supiori:** test clan-house heads against national projects and their brokers.
- **Arfak peoples of Manokwari:** check who confers customary titles and why.
- **Marind and Kanum of Merauke:** compare clan heads' signatures with the clan's own say.
- **Raja Ampat ruling families and village heads:** set inherited rank beside elected and tourism-based power.
- **Settler business and political networks:** their role in town economies and parties belongs to the present-day picture, but this concept is scouted for Papuan forms of power first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political economy is well studied in English and Indonesian; customary power is documented for a few peoples; how power is felt in daily life is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Hubula and Lani for war leaders; Mee and Biak for clan heads and lenders; English for policy reports; Indonesian for elections and mining.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming powerful people invites risk to researchers and informants; military business is guarded; the 2011 Congress led to prosecutions. Authority and rank as a system belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the power pointers are clear of the standing rules' categories.
