# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (census and ministry material); Papuan Malay (sermons, songs, road talk); Dutch (mission files before 1962); Biak, Hubula (Dani), Lani, Mee, Sentani, Ma'ya and Marind (names of the holy, the wise and the far away). **Concept in one line:** the domain of belief and philosophy: what is true and where meaning is found (religion, philosophy, higher learning, long journeys).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Cenderawasih Bay (Biak, Waropen, Wandamen):** how the oldest mission region dates its own conversion, against highland valleys reached after 1950.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem Valley (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Nduga):** which church came first, valley by valley, since the religious map follows mission frontiers.
- **Raja Ampat islands, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** where mosque and church share families on the Muslim coast.
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Sawi):** where Catholic schooling shaped higher learning differently from the Protestant north.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** the line between the Catholic archdiocese and the evangelical inland.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** lake and bay villages beside the capital's seminaries.
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam and Sough of the Arfak mountains):** which older teaching houses and exchange cults lasted beside the churches.
- **Migrant settler communities (Bugis, Butonese, Makassarese, Javanese, Torajan, Moluccan, Chinese Indonesian):** their mosques, churches and temples as part of today's towns, not the culture's core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Statistics and church histories are rich; the peoples' own teaching is thin in print outside a few peoples. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (its tracker names only "Indonesia" as pre-war land); these Sagittarius sheets take the core to be western New Guinea with Biak, Yapen and the Raja Ampat islands.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the zending and missie archives before 1962; Papuan Malay for worship and travel talk today; Biak, Sentani and the highland languages for names of the holy and of counselors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sacred objects, men's-house knowledge and initiation teaching are closed to outsiders in many peoples; church politics and religious tension in towns are touchy and should be read in several voices.
- **Held for review:** temples and movements founded in South Asia among settler communities, as a category only; no named pointer.
