# West Papua: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for worship, testimony and church press; Dutch and German for mission records; Biak, Lani, Yali, Mee, Asmat and Hubula for how trust in God and in older sacred powers is put into words. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension faith in people, progress or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Cenderawasih Bay:** check how Koreri hopes and church faith sit together over the generations since 1855.
- **Lani and Hubula of the Baliem and its western valleys:** compare valleys that turned quickly with those that waited, to see what "conversion" meant in each.
- **Yali and Mek peoples (Eipo and neighbors) to the east:** check how a later and more violent mission encounter shaped what faith is said to require.
- **Mee and Moni of the Paniai lakes:** set prophet-led movements beside the main churches.
- **Asmat and Kamoro of the south coast:** check Catholic inculturation against older feast cycles that continue.
- **Marind and Kanum around Merauke:** look at a century-long Catholic presence and what lay faith looks like there.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak Muslims, and Walesi:** compare coastal Muslim faith of long standing with a newer highland Muslim community.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** note their congregations and mosques beside Papuan ones, without taking their practice for the Papuan pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: conversion narratives and mission records are rich, measured religiosity is thin (no survey gives faith items by Papuan regency).
- **Languages needed:** German for Zöllner's Yali study; Dutch for MSC and Utrecht mission letters; Lani, Mee and Biak to read scripture and song for faith words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** destroyed sacred objects and burned bundles are painful history for some families; prophet movements were policed and may still be touchy; ask before using testimony videos of named people.
- **Held for review:** none; the faith pointers chosen here hold nothing the standing rules set aside.
