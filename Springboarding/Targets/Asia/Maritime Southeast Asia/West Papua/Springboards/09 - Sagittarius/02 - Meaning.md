# West Papua: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for press, theses and health data; Papuan Malay for how young people and elders talk about purpose and drift; Amungme, Mee, Asmat, Biak and Sentani for the older teachings on why people are here. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amungme of the Nemangkawi highlands:** check how the meaning of the mountain is argued over since the mine, and by whom.
- **Mee of Paniai and Deiyai:** compare the triad as elders teach it with its use in student and church writing.
- **Asmat and Kamoro of the south coast:** set carving and initiation as purpose-giving against wage work and the art market.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali of the Central Highlands:** check whether the house, the garden or the church is named first when people say what life is for.
- **Biak and Cenderawasih Bay:** look at which life passages are sung, and which songs have faded.
- **Sentani and Jayapura:** compare lake villages with the city's young people who drift or study.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam):** check whether exchange of cloth and wealth is spoken of as the point of a man's or a woman's life.
- **Marind and Muyu of the Merauke lowlands:** check whether clan origin beings are named when people explain where a life's purpose comes from.
- **Ma'ya and Biak-descended villages of the Raja Ampat islands:** check whether sea work, mosque or church is named first as what makes life worth it.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note their own sources of purpose (work, trade, mosque, church) as part of town life, not as the Papuan baseline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin for measured meaning (no well-being survey with meaning items by Papuan regency); richer in oral tradition and local theses.
- **Languages needed:** Amungme and Mee to read Beanal and the triad in full; Biak for *wor* texts; Indonesian for youth and health studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and child glue-sniffing involve minors and grief; use counts and reports, never names. Some origin narratives belong to clans and are told only by their owners.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on the meaning strand had to be set aside under the standing rules.
