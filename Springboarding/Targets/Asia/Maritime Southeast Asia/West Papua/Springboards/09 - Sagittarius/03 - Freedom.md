# West Papua: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay for slogans, sermons, songs and the press; Dutch for the decolonization years; Biak and Lani for the older words for a world set free; Tok Pisin and English only for the refugee and exile comparison. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

English keywords: merdeka West Papua meaning, Morning Star commemoration, press freedom Papua, Papuan political prisoners memoir, slave raiding Tidore Papua, West Papuan refugees East Awin.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Cenderawasih Bay:** check where Koreri vocabulary is used for political freedom and where people keep it apart.
- **Lani, Hubula and Nduga of the Central Highlands:** compare valleys by the level of security presence, to see what freedom of movement means in each.
- **Muyu and Marind of the Merauke lowlands:** check how families who crossed to PNG in 1984 and families who stayed tell it.
- **Ma'ya and Biak-descended villages of Raja Ampat, and the Bird's Head coast:** look for memory of raiding and bondage in clan histories.
- **Amungme and Kamoro near the Grasberg mine:** check how access to land and river is talked about as freedom since the mine.
- **Papuan Muslims of Fakfak and Kaimana:** set their talk of freedom of worship and of *merdeka* beside the churches' talk.
- **Students and young urbanites in Jayapura and Sentani:** compare their freedom words with village usage.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note how they speak of freedom of movement and trade, as part of today's towns, not as the Papuan frame.
- **Exiles in the Netherlands, Vanuatu and Australia:** use only as comparison for how freedom is framed abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich on the political movement and its suppression, thin on everyday and inner freedom.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the 1950s and 1960s self-determination files; Biak and Lani for the older words of release; Tok Pisin for the refugee settlements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Morning Star and *merdeka* are dangerous to display or say in many places; never name informants, protesters or prisoners' families. Anything legal on these matters is left to Libra and Capricorn.
