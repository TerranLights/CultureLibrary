# West Papua: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for official history, fact-checks and health messaging; Papuan Malay for rumor, swearing and everyday "is it true?"; Biak, Sentani and the highland languages for clan histories and how a speaker marks what he saw or heard; Dutch for the colonial record that later accounts dispute. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it, and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Cenderawasih Bay:** check how *keret* histories and Koreri tellings are held true beside church history.
- **Lani, Hubula and Yali:** compare first-contact accounts across valleys, since each dates its own "first sight" differently.
- **Mee and Moni of the Paniai lakes:** check what weight dreams and signs carry beside the pastor's word.
- **Amungme and Kamoro around Timika:** set company, state and community accounts of land and harm side by side, to see whose word holds.
- **Marind and Kanum of the Merauke lowlands:** check how clan origin tellings are used when land claims are disputed.
- **Sentani and Jayapura's Tobati-Enggros:** look at how *ondoafi* lineages keep their own record, and how the city's press treats it.
- **Ma'ya of Raja Ampat and the Muslim coasts of Fakfak:** check whether sultanate-era histories from Tidore are cited as proof locally.
- **Arfak and Maybrat of the Bird's Head:** check whose recited history decides between rival clans.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note which news sources and religious authorities they trust, as part of the town picture, not as the Papuan pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: contested political history is heavily documented; everyday truth-testing in local languages is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for handover memoranda; Biak and Sentani for lineage histories; the highland languages' grammars for evidential marking.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eyewitness material from conflict areas can endanger the people filmed; clan histories may be owned and told only by entitled speakers. Proof in court is left to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the truth strand drew on no source the standing rules set aside.
