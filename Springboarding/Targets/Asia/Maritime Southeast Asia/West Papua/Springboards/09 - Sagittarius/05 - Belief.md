# West Papua: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for registry data, catechism and theses; Papuan Malay for ghost stories, warnings and talk of what is held true; Dutch for nineteenth-century coastal ethnography; Biak, Waropen, Marind, Sentani and Ma'ya for older beliefs about spirits, places and the dead. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and the Waropen coast:** check how belief cycles such as Koreri are told within families that are also long-standing church members.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali of the highlands:** compare valleys on which older beliefs pastors treat as acceptable and which they forbid.
- **Marind and Kanum around Merauke:** check what belief vocabulary outlived a century of mission, epidemic and in-migration.
- **Mbaham-Matta and Iha of Fakfak and Kaimana:** look at how families with Muslim and Christian members describe holding different beliefs.
- **Sentani and Tobati-Enggros near Jayapura:** check place-bound beliefs on the lake and bay against the city's churches.
- **Ma'ya of the Raja Ampat islands:** separate island beliefs from the layers brought by sultanate and mission.
- **Moi and Tehit of the Sorong hinterland, Bird's Head:** check which forest and spirit beliefs are spoken of openly.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note their own folk and religious beliefs as part of town life, not as the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: older ethnography is rich for Biak, Waropen and Marind; measured belief is close to absent at regency level.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for De Clercq and Held's original; Biak and Waropen for Koreri and spirit terms; Marind for the Mayo literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation beliefs such as the Mayo were secret and may still be; ancestor bodies are kin, not exhibits; registry data on belief is personal.
- **Held for review:** beliefs of settler communities whose religions were founded in South Asia, as a category only.

## 5. First queries
1. Find Kamma's *Koreri* and Held's *Waropen* in a library catalog.
2. Search Delpher and the KITLV digital collections for De Clercq's 1893 description.
3. Ask Dukcapil or search its portal for *penghayat kepercayaan* by Papuan regency.
4. Search Garuda and Google Scholar for "Mayo Marind" and "kepercayaan Suku Ma'ya".
5. Search YouTube for "cerita horor Papua" and note the peoples and places named.
