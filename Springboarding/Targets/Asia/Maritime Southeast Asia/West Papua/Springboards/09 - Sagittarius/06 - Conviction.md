# West Papua: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for pastoral letters, campaign statements and the press; Papuan Malay for how holding firm or giving way is said aloud; Hatam, Amungme, Marind and Mee for stands taken on land and custom; Dutch for the first highland patrols and the resistance they met. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amungme and Kamoro around Timika:** check whether women and men take and tell stands against the mine differently.
- **Mee and Moni of the Paniai lakes and Intan Jaya:** set the 1956 refusal beside present-day church witness, to see how firmness is spoken of across generations.
- **Lani and Hubula of the highlands:** compare pastors' public stands with what congregations say they expect of a leader.
- **Hatam, Sough and Moile of the Arfak mountains, Bird's Head:** check how land-keeping sayings are invoked when a project arrives.
- **Marind, Yei and Kanum of the Merauke lowlands:** look at which clans sign refusals and which accept, and why each says so.
- **Biak and Yapen:** check how prisoners and families from the bay speak of keeping faith with a cause over decades.
- **Mbaham-Matta of Fakfak and the Raja Ampat islands' Ma'ya:** check what principled stands look like where mosque and church share families.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note their own civic and religious stands in the towns, without reading them as Papuan positions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: public stands are well reported; private conscience and wavering are thin in print.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the Paniai patrol reports; Mee for remembered resistance; Hatam and Marind for land sayings in their own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** activists, prisoners and pastors face real danger; use published statements only and never identify relatives. Treatment of protest under law is Libra's and Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** none; the conviction pointers are all Papuan, church, mosque or state sources the standing rules allow.
