# West Papua: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for transport, hajj and tourism figures; Papuan Malay for leaving, wandering and coming home; Biak for sea voyaging; Hubula, Asmat and Marind for trails, canoes and border crossings; Dutch and English for explorers' narratives. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Yapen:** check how a long seafaring past is told against today's ship and plane travel.
- **Raja Ampat islands (Ma'ya and Biak-descended villages):** look at sea routes toward Maluku and how far islanders now travel for school or hajj.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali of the Central Highlands:** compare valleys with a road to those reached only by air, for who travels and why.
- **Amungme and Mee near the high peaks:** check how expeditions and climbers crossing their land are spoken of.
- **Asmat and Kamoro of the south coast:** set river and canoe travel beside the move to the towns of Agats and Timika.
- **Marind, Kanum and Muyu of the Merauke lowlands:** check cross-border kin travel to Papua New Guinea, as comparison and as daily life.
- **Moi of Sorong and the Bird's Head:** look at the city as a hub where Papuans from many regions arrive.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note arrivals by ship and transmigration as part of the present picture, not as the Papuan journey.
- **Exiles in Australia and Papua New Guinea:** use only as comparison for journeys of flight.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: transport and hajj statistics are good; Papuan accounts of journeys are mostly oral and thinly written.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Kamma's articles and Colijn; Biak for voyaging terms; Marind and Kanum for the border crossings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some routes and sacred places on trails may not be shown; travel in conflict areas can expose travelers and hosts. Rules on permits for entry are Libra's, not this sheet's.
