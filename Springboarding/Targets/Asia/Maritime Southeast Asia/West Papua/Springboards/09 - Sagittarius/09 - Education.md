# West Papua: Education (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for school statistics, scholarships and universities; Papuan Malay for how graduates and dropouts are talked about; Dutch for colonial and mission schooling before 1962; Maybrat, Wandamen, Hubula and Asmat for older teaching institutions and mother-tongue programs. **Concept in one line:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wandamen and Biak of Cenderawasih Bay:** check how the earliest mission schooling shaped later attainment and teaching careers.
- **Hubula, Lani and Nduga of the Central Highlands:** compare valleys with mission airstrip schools against those still without teachers, and what parents say schooling is for.
- **Asmat and Kamoro of the south coast:** check Catholic schooling and boarding against river-village life.
- **Amungme of the Nemangkawi highlands:** look at company scholarships as a route, and how families weigh them.
- **Maybrat and Moi of the Bird's Head:** set older lineage teaching against the school, generation by generation.
- **Marind and Muyu of the Merauke lowlands:** check where Catholic schooling and the new university change who leaves and who returns.
- **Mbaham-Matta of Fakfak and the Raja Ampat islands:** compare Muslim and church schooling where families share both.
- **Sentani and Jayapura:** look at the capital's universities and the families nearest them.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note their children's schooling and teachers as part of the town system, not as the Papuan pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich: statistics, assessments and a few strong ethnographies exist; Indigenous teaching institutions are thinly written.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the colonial school tiers and OSIBA; Maybrat and the highland languages for older teaching; Indonesian for most present-day material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation teaching is closed to outsiders and to the other sex in many peoples; students' experiences of racism should be cited from published work, not gathered from minors.
- **Held for review:** none; the education pointers are church, state, mosque and Papuan sources clear of the standing rules.
