# West Papua: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian for church, mosque and interfaith statements and the press; Papuan Malay for preaching and end-times talk; Dutch and German for the pietist roots of the first mission; English for the evangelical mission societies' own literature, read critically; the highland languages for how strict rules are put to congregations. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against change; whether the word fits at all is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Manokwari (Doreh Bay):** check how the first mission's strict rules and today's Gospel-city campaign are told by older and younger church members.
- **Lani, Hubula and Walak of Tolikara and the Baliem:** compare churches by how strictly they rule on feasts, alcohol and other faiths, rather than taking one church for all.
- **Hubula Muslims of Walesi and highland Muslim converts:** check how outreach and its critics are described from inside the community.
- **Moi and Maybrat around Sorong:** look at memories of the 2000 to 2002 tensions in a mixed city.
- **Mbaham-Matta of Fakfak and Muslim villages of Raja Ampat:** check how families with members of both faiths respond to strict voices on either side.
- **Asmat and Marind of the south coast and Merauke:** compare Catholic dioceses' handling of custom with stricter evangelical newcomers.
- **Sentani and Jayapura's charismatic congregations:** check what end-times teaching asks of members.
- **Migrant settler communities:** note settler mosques, churches and their preachers as part of town religious life, never as a frame for Papuan faith.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed: incidents are reported, but literalism and strictness are hardly measured; much press coverage is partisan.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Gossner sources; Dutch for the Utrecht mission bans; Indonesian for the 2015 and 2001 reports from each side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious violence and accusations of forced conversion are explosive; quote no one, present every account as one side's, and name no private persons. Local regulations on worship are for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** movements founded in South Asia present among settlers, as a category only.
