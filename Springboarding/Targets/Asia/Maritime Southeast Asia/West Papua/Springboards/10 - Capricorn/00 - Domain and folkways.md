# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (state and census records); Papuan Malay (how elders and officials are addressed); Dutch (district files before 1963); Sentani, Marind and the Tidore-derived island titles for rank and office. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed, with career, status, reputation, age, time, rank, order and power as its folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** check where hereditary heads and the capital's offices overlap on the same shores.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha, Koiwai):** check how old sultanate titles fit beside regency offices in Muslim and Christian villages.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Tehit, Hatam, Sough):** check whether new regencies and old exchange networks rank people by the same measures.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor and Yapen:** compare clan seniority with the church and school careers the coast entered early.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Nduga):** check how standing won in feasts and fighting meets the regent's office.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check what became of men's-house standing after the missions and the mine.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu):** check how age grades in the old accounts compare with the authority of clan heads today.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns (Bugis, Butonese, Javanese, Torajan, Moluccan):** a present-day layer in trade and state jobs, studied apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. State and colonial records of office are rich; the peoples' own accounts of command and duty are thin and scattered across theses. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these Capricorn sheets work from western New Guinea with Biak, Yapen and the Raja Ampat islands as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for personnel and census tables; Dutch for district handover reports; Sentani, Marind and the island titles to tell customary rank apart from state rank.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence politics, the security forces and named officials need care; ritual offices tied to men's houses and initiation hold knowledge that is not shared with outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none; the office, rank and time pointers for this domain called for no setting aside.
