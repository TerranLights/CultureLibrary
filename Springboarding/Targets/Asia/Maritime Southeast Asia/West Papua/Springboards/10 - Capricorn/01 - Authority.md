# West Papua: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch for colonial administration; Sentani, Biak, Hubula (Dani), Lani and the Raja Ampat languages for titles of those who may decide for others. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** check how the right of a hereditary line to decide fares against elected and appointed officials.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Biak-speaking islanders, Mbaham-Matta):** compare authority traced to the sultan with authority traced to the regency and the mosque or church.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni):** check where consent to a leader's call is given case by case and where the regent or army post overrides it.
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Tehit):** look at who may speak for a clan in land and regency matters.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak and Yapen:** check how prophets and pastors have claimed a right to direct people apart from clan heads.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check who decides for a village now that ceremonial-house leaders share the field with priests and company liaisons.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu):** check which clan elders are accepted as speaking for land and kin.
- **Migrant settler communities (Bugis, Butonese, Javanese, Torajan):** part of today's towns; their associations and the state they deal with are a separate layer, not the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political ethnography exists for a handful of peoples; household authority and acceptance of the state are thinly documented.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for edicts and indexes; Dutch for appointment files; Sentani and Biak for titles whose meaning is lost in translation as "chief".
- **Sensitive or restricted:** security-force conduct, independence bodies and named officials need care; ritual authority tied to men's houses or initiation is not public knowledge. The prison-and-probation share and the letter of edicts belong to Libra; here they are read only for what they show about acceptance.
- **Held for review:** none; the sources on who may command, here, gave no reason to set any aside.
