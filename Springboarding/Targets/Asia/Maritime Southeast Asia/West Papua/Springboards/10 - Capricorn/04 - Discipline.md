# West Papua: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay; Dutch for mission-school rules (*tucht*, *internaat*); Biak, Yali, Ngalum, Hubula (Dani) and Asmat for seclusion, training and correction of the young. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak and Numfor:** compare what the young men's house demanded with what the oldest mission schools demanded.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Yali, Lani, Ngalum, Ketengban):** check how correction of children and the training of boys differ valley by valley and by church.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** look at the discipline of carving, paddling and sago work as each is taught.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check which seclusion rules from older initiation are still kept or remembered.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam, Meyah):** check how initiation schooling and church schooling were set against each other.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana:** compare fasting and Qur'an study among Papuan Muslims with church fasts nearby.
- **Jayapura, Sentani and the towns:** check how town youth meet school rules, drinking bans and police curfews.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settler schools and pesantren in the towns are a separate present-day layer, compared but not taken as Papuan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. School and church records exist; parents' own accounts of correction, and initiation training, are thin and often closed.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for mission-school rules; Indonesian for surveys and the press; Biak and Yali for seclusion and correction words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** initiation seclusion is restricted knowledge in several peoples; abuse in schools and barracks must be heard from survivors first; physical punishment of children needs careful wording.
- **Held for review:** none; no training, fasting or correction pointer had to be withheld.
