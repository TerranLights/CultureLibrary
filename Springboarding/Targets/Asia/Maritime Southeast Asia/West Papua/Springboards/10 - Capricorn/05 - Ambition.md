# West Papua: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (school, church and campaign talk); English for diaspora students and scholarship; Biak and Mee for older paths to standing; the languages of the Asmat, Kimaam and Mimika coasts for children's hopes as recorded locally. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Mee, Moni, Nduga):** check how pig wealth, schooling and office compete as ways up.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Yapen and Waropen:** check whether the dates of first mission schooling shape what families plan for the next generation.
- **South coast, Asmat, Kamoro and Kimaam:** look at children's hopes in villages far from a secondary school.
- **Mimika (Amungme, Kamoro):** check the mine's jobs and scholarships as a ladder, and who reaches it.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam, Tehit):** check hopes tied to the new regencies' civil-service posts.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check how Indigenous youth speak of rising beside settler farmers.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak:** look at tourism and fishing as new ways up for island youth.
- **Migrant settler communities (transmigrants, traders):** their striving is part of the present-day towns, studied separately from Papuan hopes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Education and politics are well covered in the press; personal accounts of ambition outside the highlands are thin, and national mobility data may skip Papua.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for press and hearings; English for students abroad and scholarship; Mee and Biak for older words of standing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *aspirasi* can mean independence demands, so wording must be careful; named young figures may be targets of online abuse.
- **Held for review:** none; the striving and mobility pointers brought nothing under the standing rules.
