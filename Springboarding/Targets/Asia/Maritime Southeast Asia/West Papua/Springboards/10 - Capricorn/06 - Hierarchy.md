# West Papua: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (address, insult and census categories); Dutch for colonial ranking of peoples and posts; English for demographic studies; Marind, Sentani and the Kaimana and Fakfak coastal languages for ranked lineages and age grades. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

English keywords: social stratification Papua, Papuans and migrants demography, age grades Marind, ranked lineages Sentani, gender inequality Papua regency, Papuan bishops and generals.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri, Skouw):** check how ranked lines and clans fit beside a capital full of offices.
- **Kaimana, Fakfak and Raja Ampat (Mbaham-Matta, Koiwai, Ma'ya):** compare old ruling houses and commoners with the regency's own ladder.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check what is left of age grades and who now counts as senior.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Nduga):** check how standing between men, and between men and women, is talked about in valleys and towns.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam, Tehit):** look at ranking by wealth in cloth and by church and state office.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak and Waropen:** check how families with early mission ties and those without them are placed.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check the rank order among men's houses and clans as people describe it today.
- **Migrant settler communities:** part of the towns' present-day ladder of trade and office; a separate unit, so that origin-based ranking can be traced without treating settlers as the culture's core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Official rankings and ethnographies of a few ranked peoples are rich; Papuans' own talk about rank among themselves is thin in print and sharp online.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for indexes and church records; Dutch for colonial ranking of peoples; Marind and Sentani for grade and lineage names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin-based and racial ranking is painful and political; mountain-coast talk must be recorded without endorsing it; age-grade rites hold closed knowledge.
- **Held for review:** none; no ranking, rank-table or address pointer here needed holding back.
