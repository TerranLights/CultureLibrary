# West Papua: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (hiring, schooling and quota debates); Dutch for colonial collectors' notes on skilled makers; Asmat, Mee, Awyu and Wambon for standing earned by skill, effort or a good catch. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check how skill in carving and in the sago forest is recognized, and how the art trade changed that.
- **Central Highlands (Mee, Moni, Hubula, Lani):** check whether wealth from pigs and gardens is spoken of as earned or inherited.
- **Boven Digoel and the Digul river (Awyu, Wambon, Muyu):** look at how hunters and fishers divide what they bring home.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** compare hereditary office with town jobs filled by test or connection.
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Hatam):** check how new regencies hire, and how villagers judge who deserved the post.
- **Biak, Yapen and Cenderawasih Bay:** compare how coastal and highland families speak of quotas meant for all Papuans.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak:** look at fishing and tourism jobs and whether locals or outsiders are judged more able.
- **Migrant settler communities:** settler applicants who lose places under quotas are part of today's debate; record their view as one side, not the culture's core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Quota and hiring disputes and test results are well documented; how individual peoples judge earned standing is thin and must come from theses and oral sources.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for civil-service rules and test results; Dutch for collectors' notes on makers; Asmat and Mee for terms of earned standing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** quota debates touch ethnic tension; never name private persons in nepotism or forgery claims beyond court records.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing among the hiring, prize and quota pointers met the exclusion rules.
