# West Papua: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (police, city and festival talk); English for conflict analysis and ethnography; Yali, Hatam and Meyah (Arfak), Sentani and the Imyan area's language for how land, villages and the world are kept in their proper arrangement. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Imyan, Hatam, Meyah, Sough, Moi):** check how land zoning and talk of confusion express an idea of right arrangement.
- **Central Highlands (Yali, Hubula, Lani, Nduga):** compare older limits on fighting with police and military ways of keeping order.
- **Jayapura and Sentani:** look at stilt-village layout beside the capital's street rules and riots.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak and Yapen:** check how coastal towns handle festivals and order in mixed neighborhoods.
- **South coast, Asmat and Kamoro:** check how villages on boardwalks set and enforce rules of space and noise.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** look at town ordinances and village rules side by side.
- **Raja Ampat islands (Ma'ya and Biak-speaking island communities):** check village rules for tourists and fishers.
- **Migrant settler communities:** check how settler traders deal with public-order police in markets, as part of town life recorded apart from Papuan ideas of order.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Conflict and public-order sources are plentiful but political; ideas of cosmic and household order are thin outside a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for police and city records; English for IPAC and Timmer; Hatam and Meyah for land zones; Yali for conflict terms in Koch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** riots and armed conflict are highly political and dangerous; sacred places and their rules may be closed to outsiders.
