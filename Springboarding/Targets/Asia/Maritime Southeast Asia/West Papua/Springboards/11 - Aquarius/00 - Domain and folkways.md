# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (towns, markets, churches); Indonesian (census, village and church records); Dutch (mission files before 1963); Biak, Hubula (Dani), Sentani, Marind, Asmat and Kamoro for house, clan and gathering words. **Concept in one line:** the domain of community and ideals: what binds people into groups and what they hope for, with settlement, migration, association and hospitality as its social ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Hatam, Sougb, Tehit):** check how hamlets and Sorong's suburbs decide who counts as a neighbor.
- **Cenderawasih Bay, Biak, Numfor and Yapen (Biak, Wandamen, Waropen, Ansus):** check whether clan councils and congregations gather the same people.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee, Moni, Damal, Ngalum):** check how compound, men's house and church each draw the line around "us".
- **South coast (Asmat, Kamoro):** check what replaced the men's house as a gathering point once government villages were laid out.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu):** check where Papuan hamlets and transmigrant settlements meet: markets, schools, churches.
- **Jayapura, Sentani and the Tabi coast (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri, Skou):** check how lake villages host the capital's crowds and festivals.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check whether communal work days cross the line between mosque and church.
- **Newcomers from Sulawesi, Java and Maluku in the towns (migrant settler communities):** check which home-region *paguyuban* they join; a present-day layer, apart from the Papuan core.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Vanuatu, the PNG border camps):** comparison only, for how exile groups organize.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Church, mission and census records of groups are fairly rich; Papuans' own accounts of what binds a village and what it hopes for lie scattered in theses, songs and oral tradition. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; for Aquarius the working core is the western half of the New Guinea mainland with the Biak-Numfor, Yapen and Raja Ampat island groups.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for how "we" and "they" are said today; Dutch for mission congregation files; Biak and Hubula for house and clan words that census categories flatten.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's-house rites and initiation knowledge are closed to outsiders; the 1971 proclamation and independence hopes are charged, so sources and speakers need care.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer about villages, gatherings and associations had to be set aside.
