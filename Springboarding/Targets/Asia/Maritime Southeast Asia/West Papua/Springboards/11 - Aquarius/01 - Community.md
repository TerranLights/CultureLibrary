# West Papua: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay and Indonesian (village, church and relief records); Dutch (resettlement and mission files); Biak, Sentani, Kamoro and Amungme for words of place and belonging. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amungme and Kamoro of Mimika:** check what holds a moved village together, and what of the old village is still claimed.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali of the Baliem and its side valleys:** compare compound, church and market as the place where "my people" is felt.
- **Muslim Papuans (Walesi, Fakfak, Kokas, Raja Ampat):** check how a mosque congregation and its Christian neighbors share village tasks.
- **Biak, Numfor and Supiori islanders:** check whether those living in Jayapura or Manokwari still count as village members, and what they send home.
- **Sentani, Tobati-Enggros and Nafri:** check how founding stories of the lake and bay villages are used to keep a line against the capital's sprawl.
- **Moi and Maybrat around Sorong:** check how old hamlets became suburbs and what each side calls the other.
- **Marind, Kanum and Yei near Merauke:** check where Papuan and transmigrant hamlets share a school or church and where they do not.
- **Company and government towns (Tembagapura, Timika, Wamena's town quarters):** mixed Papuan and migrant settler residents; separate units for today's town life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Church and relief records are fair; studies of how villagers feel their bond are few, and many smaller peoples (Mamberamo, Sarmi, the Kaimana coast) have almost none.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the resettlement files; Papuan Malay for the *baku-* forms of mutual help; Amungme and Kamoro for what a moved village keeps of its old names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mining relocation and displacement are tied to conflict and compensation claims; elders' and survivors' accounts need consent.
- **Held for review:** none; the village, church and relocation pointers raised no setting-aside question.
