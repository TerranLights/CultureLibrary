# West Papua: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (census categories, movement papers, press); Dutch (party politics of 1960-1962); Hubula, Marind, Kamoro and Asmat for clan, moiety and war-party words. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and how members are expected to act together.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indonesian | *kelompok*, *golongan*, *suku*, *marga* | group; faction or class; ethnic group; clan | *suku* is also a census label |
| Papuan Malay | *fam* | clan name, clan | shared with Maluku usage *check* |
| Papuan Malay | *kitong pu orang*; *massa* | our people; the crowd | the second used of crowds in unrest |
| Indonesian | *SARA* | the New Order shorthand for ethnic, religious, racial and intergroup matters | a word for what was not to be discussed |
| Hubula (Dani) | *waya*, *wita* | the two moieties | spellings vary *check* |
| Marind | *boan* | clan or clan group | from older ethnography *check* |
| Kamoro | *taparu* | land-holding descent group | *check* |
| Indonesian | *sistem noken* | a group's votes cast together | rules belong to Libra; here only as group behavior |

English keywords: clans and moieties West Papua, Papuan parties 1960s, crowd violence Papua, Papuan student alliance, collective blame Papua villages, census ethnicity Papua.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** check whether moiety, confederation or church district is the "us" named first when trouble comes.
- **Mee and Moni of the Paniai lakes:** check how far the clan, the village and the church each act as one in disputes.
- **Kamoro and Amungme:** check how group lines are redrawn when compensation is paid as a lump.
- **Asmat river villages:** compare old raiding alliances between villages with today's festival crews and church parishes.
- **Biak and Numfor:** check how *keret* and *fam* gather when members live across Jayapura, Manokwari and the islands.
- **Marind and Muyu near Merauke:** check which groups speak with one voice on plantation land.
- **Moi, Maybrat and Arfak peoples of the Bird's Head:** check how new regencies drawn on group lines changed who belongs together.
- **Students and young urban Papuans:** check how pan-Papuan groups sit beside each person's clan.
- **Sentani lake villages and the Ma'ya of Raja Ampat:** check whether a hereditary head still speaks for the village as one group.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** check their *paguyuban* and party branches as groups, in separate units, as part of the present-day picture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Movement and rights records on groups in conflict are plentiful but partisan; studies of everyday clan groups are thin outside the Baliem, Mimika and Biak.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the 1960-1962 party press; Hubula and Kamoro for moiety and *taparu* terms that the census never records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** armed groups, defectors and collective punishment are dangerous topics for sources; name no living private persons from rights reports.
- **Held for review:** none; moiety, student-alliance and supporters' pointers all stand under the rules.
