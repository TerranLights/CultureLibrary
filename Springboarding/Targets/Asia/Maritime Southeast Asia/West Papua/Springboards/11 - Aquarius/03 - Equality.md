# West Papua: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (indexes, policy and press on discrimination); Papuan Malay (everyday talk of pride and sameness); Hubula, Biak and the Raja Ampat and Onin coastal languages for big-man and titled-rank words. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hubula, Lani and Mee:** check whether standing won by giving is held in check, by whom, and who may refuse a man of renown.
- **Ma'ya and Biak of Raja Ampat:** check whether old titled families still claim first shares and seats.
- **Mbaham-Matta and Iha of Fakfak:** compare titled houses and commoners at shared Muslim and Christian feasts.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** check how carving fame and feast giving change a man's standing against others.
- **Marind and Kanum:** check how age-mates are treated alike or ranked inside one grade.
- **Sentani and the Tabi coast:** check how hereditary heads and ordinary villagers stand in church, where all are "brothers and sisters".
- **Papuan women across regions:** check how claims of equality differ between church women's groups, market mothers and university women.
- **Moi and Tehit around Sorong:** check how newcomer wealth beside Papuan hamlets is talked about as fair or unfair.
- **Migrant settler communities in towns and transmigration areas:** their own sense of being treated unequally belongs to the present picture; record it separately from Papuan views.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Indexes of gaps between regions and sexes are plentiful; Papuans' own ideas of who is equal in what are thin and lie in theses and testimony.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for the leveling vocabulary of pride and sameness; Hubula and Mee for what a big man may and may not claim over others.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racism and the 2019 slurs are raw; quote slurs only as needed and describe rather than repeat.
- **Held for review:** none; the leveling, gender-gap and racism pointers raised no conflict with the standing rules.
