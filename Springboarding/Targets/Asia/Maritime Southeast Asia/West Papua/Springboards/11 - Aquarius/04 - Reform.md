# West Papua: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (programs, evaluations, press); Papuan Malay (how people speak of change in church and village); Dutch (administrators' memoirs and mission plans); Biak for prophetic renewal; Asmat and the Misool coastal language for changes in custom. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole, and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Numfor:** check how earlier prophetic renewals and today's church reform programs are told side by side by the same families.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check who leads changes to bride wealth and feasting, and who argues against them.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** check how former boarding-school pupils judge the mission's changes to village life.
- **Ma'ya and other Misool villagers:** check whether women-led reef rules shift who decides village matters.
- **Hubula and Lani:** check which church-led changes to custom are now treated as finished and which are reopened.
- **Moi and Maybrat of the Bird's Head:** check how new-regency reforms after 2022 were explained to villages, and by whom.
- **Marind of Merauke:** check how plantation and food-estate plans were sold as improvement, and how Marind answered.
- **Sentani and Jayapura civil servants:** check how Papuan officials describe carrying out reforms they did not write.
- **Migrant settler teachers and officials:** many reforms reach villages through them; record their part as present-day carriers, apart from the Papuan core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Evaluations of state programs are many but written from above; Papuan accounts of reforms they chose for themselves (bride wealth, reef closures, renewal movements) are fewer and scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for van Baal and the 1950s plans; Biak for prophetic renewal; Mee for village debate over bride wealth.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** special autonomy and corruption claims are politically charged; boarding-school memories may include harm, so former pupils' consent comes first.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on renewal movements, school change or custom reform was set aside.
