# West Papua: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian (peace documents, youth data, student and church writing); Papuan Malay (talk of dreams and selling out); Moi, Asmat and the Raja Ampat languages for ideal lands and right living; English for tourism and conservation material. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

English keywords: Papua Land of Peace indicators, Papuan student movements, idealism and disillusion Papua activists, Raja Ampat last paradise, community reef patrols, teachers in the interior Papua.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moi and Tehit of the Sorong hinterland:** check what land-based ideals are invoked against plantation and city growth, and by whom.
- **Asmat river villages:** check how the old ideal land and the Christian heaven are spoken of together or apart.
- **Ma'ya and Biak-speaking villagers of Raja Ampat:** check whether reef patrols are described as an ideal, a job, or a duty owed to the clan.
- **Hubula and Lani youth in Wamena:** check what young people name as their *cita-cita*, and how that shifts after school.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check how teachers, catechists and church workers explain staying in poorly paid service.
- **Biak, Serui and the Cenderawasih Bay coast:** check how older nationalist hopes and church ideals are joined or kept apart in sermons and songs.
- **Marind and Kanum of Merauke:** check what ideal of a farming future each side holds in plantation talk.
- **Students in Jayapura and in Java:** check whether ideals of the campus years survive return home.
- **Migrant settler volunteers and teachers:** their own ideals of service are part of the present picture, studied separately from Papuan visions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. Peace documents and student statements exist; studies of Papuan ideals as such, and of disillusion, are few, and smaller peoples like the Moi have little in print.
- **Languages needed:** Moi for *Malamoi* and its meanings; Asmat for *safan* and other tellings; English for the foreign tourism and conservation material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence ideals can bring danger to speakers; the sellout charge can harm named people, so keep to public statements.
- **Held for review:** none; peace-vision, poetry and service pointers raised no standing-rule issue.
