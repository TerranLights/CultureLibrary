# West Papua: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (address words between friends); Indonesian (press, film, school and church sources); German and Dutch (nineteenth-century travel and mission accounts); Biak, Hubula, Lani and Mee for trading-friend words and greetings. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Numfor islanders:** check whether trading-friend ties still shape who hosts whom on Raja Ampat and the bay.
- **Hubula and Lani:** check how greeting words and the finger-snap handshake are used with friends, strangers and elders.
- **Mee and Moni:** check whether friends outside the clan help with pigs, school fees and bride wealth, and how that is repaid.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** check what word or bond names a non-kin companion, and whether it came from mission schools or older ways.
- **Marind and other Merauke peoples:** check how boyhood friends from the same village grow apart or stay close.
- **Sentani, Skou and the Jayapura area:** check how cross-border and cross-lake friendships are kept.
- **Raja Ampat Muslim and Christian villages:** check whether friendship across faith is marked at the other side's feast days.
- **Arfak peoples (Hatam, Sougb, Meyah):** check how friendship with outsiders is made through birding guides and hosts.
- **Migrant settler communities:** friendships between their children and Papuan children belong to the present-day picture; study both sides separately.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin. Friendship as its own bond is rarely studied here; most material sits inside kinship and exchange studies, so the researcher must read across them.
- **Languages needed:** German for von Rosenberg; Biak for *manibob*; Hubula and Mee for greeting and friend words, which lexicons may record only as kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** favoritism and HIV are touchy topics; keep to public reporting and do not name private persons.
- **Held for review:** none; trading-friend, film and greeting pointers met the standing rules.
