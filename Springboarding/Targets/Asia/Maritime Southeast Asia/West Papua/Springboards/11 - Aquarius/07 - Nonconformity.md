# West Papua: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (street and youth talk about the odd one out); Indonesian (press, church and movement papers); Dutch (nineteenth-century mission periodicals); Biak, Hubula and Mee for words on stubbornness, refusal and the person who goes their own way. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature, and how far the departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Numfor:** check how people who fell away from the early mission are remembered in island families.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** check how converts to Islam and members of breakaway churches are received at clan feasts.
- **Mee of Paniai:** check how outspoken young men and women are treated in village meetings, and when a stand is honored.
- **Sentani and Jayapura youth:** compare punk, rap and tattoo scenes with church youth groups for who sets the norm.
- **Asmat carvers:** check how carvers who change styles are judged by elders, buyers and festival juries.
- **Marind and Kanum near Merauke:** check how villagers speak of young people who take up town styles or new faiths.
- **Moi and Maybrat of Sorong:** check how street youth and their families describe being "different" in a growing city.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak Muslim villages:** check how a member who joins a different faith or teaching is spoken of.
- **Migrant settler youth in the towns:** check where their youth scenes mix with Papuan ones, as part of the present-day town picture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin. Political dissent is well documented; everyday nonconformity (dress, faith, staying single, art) is scattered across press and social media, with few studies.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for the mission periodicals; Papuan Malay for street labels; Mee and Hubula for words for a headstrong or rule-breaking person.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dissent can bring arrest or violence; conversion and gender topics can expose people, so use public sources and no private names.
- **Held for review:** none; mission-record, youth-scene and church-split pointers needed nothing set aside.
