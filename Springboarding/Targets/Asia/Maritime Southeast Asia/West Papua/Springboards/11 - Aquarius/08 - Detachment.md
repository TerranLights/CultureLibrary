# West Papua: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (phrases of not caring and letting be); Indonesian (psychology theses, election and census data, church counseling); French (an 1820s voyage account); Hubula, Mee, Biak and Asmat for words of calm, mourning's end and the dead person's goods. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized, others condemned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak and Sentani:** check how long mourning lasts before *tutup duka*, and who may still show grief afterward.
- **Hubula, Yali and Lani:** check whether men and women are expected to show or hold back grief in different ways, and how churches changed that.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** check what is done with a dead person's house, canoe and tools, and whether distance from them is required.
- **Mee and Moni:** check how calm speech and outbursts are judged in a dispute.
- **Marind and Kanum:** check how people living near Merauke town describe the difference between village closeness and town distance.
- **Arfak and Moi peoples:** check how reserve toward newcomers is described by hosts and by visitors, and keep the two apart.
- **Raja Ampat islanders:** check how families of fishers who are away for weeks speak of absence and worry.
- **Survivors of violence in Wamena, Nduga and Intan Jaya:** check how counselors and families describe withdrawal after displacement.
- **Migrant settler residents in the towns:** their own sense of urban distance belongs to the present picture; record it apart from Papuan views.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin. Few studies treat calm, reserve or indifference here; most leads are hidden in mourning, mental-health and media sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for Lesson; Biak and Sentani for the closing of mourning; Hubula and Mee lexicons to find their own words for calm and self-control, which this sheet could not supply.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trauma and grief call for consent and care; outsiders' remarks that a people is "cold" or "hot-tempered" are stereotypes to record, never to adopt.
- **Held for review:** none; the mourning, retreat and turnout pointers raised no standing-rule question.
