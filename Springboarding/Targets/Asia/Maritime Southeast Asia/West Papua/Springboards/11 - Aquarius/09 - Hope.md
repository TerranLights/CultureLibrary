# West Papua: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Biak (the Koreri tradition); Dutch (Kamma's original study and mission files); Indonesian and Papuan Malay (church, health, press and survey sources); English (later scholarship); Marind and Hubula for hopes tied to new provinces and to healing. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biak, Numfor and Supiori:** check how Koreri hope is spoken of by elders, pastors and the young, and where they differ.
- **Yapen and Waropen:** check whether Koreri-like hopes were told on Yapen and the Waropen coast, or something else.
- **Hubula, Lani and Yali:** check what people hope for from healing services and from the church's promises of a new life.
- **Mee and Moni:** check how hopes for schooling and jobs are voiced by parents who send children away.
- **Marind, Kanum and Yei:** check what they expect of the new southern province and of plantations, and who sounds hopeful or wary.
- **Asmat and Kamoro:** check hopes voiced after health crises and mine payments, and who voices them.
- **Moi, Maybrat and Tehit of the Bird's Head:** check how hopes are pinned on new regencies and on land returned to clans.
- **Ma'ya and Biak-speaking villagers of Raja Ampat:** check what tourism income is hoped to bring and to whom.
- **Migrant settler communities:** their own hopes for land and work are part of the present picture; record them apart from Papuan hopes.
- **Exiles in the Netherlands and the Pacific:** comparison only, for hopes kept far from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Biak's Koreri tradition (mission and scholarly writing), thin for everyday hope elsewhere; survey measures of hope are national and seldom give Papuan detail.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Kamma's 1954 text; Biak for Koreri terms and songs; Marind for hopes tied to the new southern province.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Koreri and flag-raising hopes are tied to independence and repression; families of the missing need consent; healing and HIV accounts are private.
- **Held for review:** none; the Koreri, healing and new-province pointers met the standing rules.
