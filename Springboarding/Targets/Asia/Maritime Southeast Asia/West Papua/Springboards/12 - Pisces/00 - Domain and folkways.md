# West Papua: Domain and folkways (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (daily talk of spirits and trouble); Indonesian (health surveys, church records); Dutch (mission periodicals before 1963); Biak, Hubula (Dani), Asmat and Marind for soul, ghost and healer words. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and transcendence: how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape, with magic ways (healing, charms, sorcery, signs) as its folkway and order ways as runner-up.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Meyah, Inanwatan):** check how healers and sorcery suspicion are handled where church, mosque and older practice share a district.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Wandamen, Ansus, Waropen):** check what replaced *karwar* figures and seers after mission burnings.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Damal, Ketengban):** check soul and ghost words valley by valley; neighbors may not match.
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Auyu, Mandobo):** check how duties to the dead are now spoken of in Catholic parishes.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu):** check how *dema* places fare beside plantations.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri, Skou):** check how sacred lake and hill sites sit inside a growing city.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya and Muslim island families):** check charms and sea spirits where Islam and Christianity share villages.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns (Sulawesi, Java, Maluku, Flores):** a present-day layer; check which healers each side consults, and who consults whom across that line.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Vanuatu, PNG border camps):** comparison only, for how exile shapes talk of suffering.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Mission, museum and conflict sources are fairly rich; Papuans' own accounts of distress and healing lie scattered in theses and oral tradition. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is scouted for the western half of New Guinea with Biak, Yapen and Raja Ampat.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for how sorcery and madness are named day to day; Dutch for the *Annalen* and boarding-school files; Biak, Hubula, Asmat and Marind for soul words that Indonesian glosses flatten.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sorcery accusation can lead to violence, so named cases need care; initiation knowledge stays closed; prison testimony carries risk for speakers.
- **Held for review:** meditation or devotional movements of South Asian origin in the towns, if any surface, as a category only.
