# West Papua: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (how people say they feel or grasp another); Indonesian (school, clinic and media usage); Dutch (voyage and patrol records); Korowai, Biak, Lani, Hubula, Mee and other languages for how other minds are spoken of. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

English keywords: empathy Papua; opacity of other minds; perspective-taking New Guinea; emotional contagion schools Indonesia.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Tehit, Inanwatan):** check when, and by whom, another's intentions may be guessed aloud.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Ansus, Wandamen):** check who must share a sorrow aloud, and across which clan and church lines.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Lani, Hubula, Yali, Ketengban):** check where feeling is said to sit in the body, people by people.
- **South coast and lowland forests (Asmat, Korowai, Kombai, Auyu):** check talk about the unknowability of others' minds, and how far it reaches.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Yei, Kanum):** check whether people speak of feeling with plants and animals of their clan.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Skou, Nafri):** check how townspeople describe reading newcomers, and being read by them.
- **Raja Ampat (Ma'ya and island families):** check how guides speak of tourists' feelings and of the sea creatures they show.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check what each side says it misreads in the other.
- **Diaspora and students in Java, Sulawesi and abroad:** comparison only, for being misunderstood away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin to mixed. The opacity debate gives a strong scholarly start for the lowlands; measured empathy data for Papuans are close to absent, and most material is outsiders' accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for everyday feeling talk; Korowai, Lani and Biak to test whether "empathy" divides into feeling and knowing; Dutch for the 1616 journal's editions and patrol notes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** school possession cases involve minors; social-media posts by young Papuans should be cited without exposing them.
- **Held for review:** empathy or mindfulness courses of South Asian origin, if any are taught in the towns, as a category only.
