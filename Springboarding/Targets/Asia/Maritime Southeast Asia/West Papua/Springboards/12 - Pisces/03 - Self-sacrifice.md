# West Papua: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (church, news and family usage); Dutch (mission obituaries, wartime records); Japanese and English for the 1944 battles; Marind, Biak, Lani and Ngalum for offering and pledge words. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Arfak peoples, Maybrat, Moi):** check whether kin expect a son's or daughter's career to be given up for family duties.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Numfor, Yapen):** check war memory: whose sacrifice in 1944 is marked, and by whom.
- **Central Highlands (Lani, Hubula, Ngalum, Nduga):** check pig offering, fighters' deaths and nurses' deaths as three kinds of giving.
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Mimika):** check what parents give up so children can attend school far away.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kimaam, Kanum):** check crop-origin myths of a death that feeds, and how they are told today.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros):** check whether town blood drives and volunteer rescuers draw Papuans and newcomers alike.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check Ramadan fasting and sea rescue as different forms of giving.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check how settlers' stories of leaving home for their children compare.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Vanuatu):** comparison only, for how exiles speak of what they gave up.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. War, mission and conflict sources are many; family-level sacrifice sits mostly in theses and features, and no national survey item on it is known to count Papuan respondents separately.
- **Languages needed:** Indonesian for *korban* and *gugur* in their double uses; Dutch for mission obituaries; Japanese and English for the 1944 record; Marind and Lani for offering words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** death notices of fighters and soldiers, and the Kiwirok case, are contested and charged; offering rites may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none for self-sacrifice; no fasting or vow tradition here required setting aside.
