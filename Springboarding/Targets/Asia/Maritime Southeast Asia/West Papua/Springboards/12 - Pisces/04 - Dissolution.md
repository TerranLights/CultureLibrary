# West Papua: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (how breakups, losses and mixing are named); Indonesian (census, church, news); English (rights literature); Biak, Tobati, Korowai and highland languages for transformation and union words. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Mpur):** check whether clan names and languages hold in Sorong's suburbs.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Numfor, Serui):** check how the skin-shedding story is used in sermons, songs and protest.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee):** check trance, tongues and dance in churches founded by different missions.
- **South coast and lowland forests (Korowai, Kombai, Asmat):** check what moving into a village does to house, clan and speech.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check language loss and mixed marriage near transmigrant settlements.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Tobati-Enggros, Sentani, Nafri):** check which languages lose their last speakers first, and why there.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** check how conversion over centuries changed names and clan memory.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check the place of their children of mixed descent, from both sides.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Australia):** comparison only, for language loss in the second generation abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political and language-loss writing exist; ecstatic worship, possession and dementia are known almost only through videos, news and a few theses.
- **Languages needed:** Biak for the skin-shedding episode; Tobati and other small languages for the vitality studies; Papuan Malay for how *amber* and *peranakan* are used.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "minority in our own land" debate and the 2011 congress are charged; children of mixed descent and possession cases need care with names.
- **Held for review:** none yet; a trance or meditation practice of South Asian origin met later in the towns would go here, as a category only.
