# West Papua: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (borders, fences, forbidden places); Dutch (old charts and survey notes); Tok Pisin only for the PNG side, as comparison; Marind, Hatam, Hubula, Ma'ya and Mbaham-Matta for clan, dress and taboo lines. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Hatam, Sougb, Meyah, Moi):** check how conservation lines and clan edges are matched or set against each other.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Padaido islanders, Waropen):** check where land lines end and sea lines begin.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Walak, Mee):** check dress, house and photograph lines in tourist valleys against remote ones.
- **South coast and lowland forests (Asmat, Korowai, Kamoro):** check who decides which places visitors may not enter.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei, Muyu):** check clan totem lines and the national border that runs through Marind and Kanum country.
- **Jayapura and the border (Skou, Tobati-Enggros, Sentani, Keerom peoples):** check how life on the national line shapes kin and garden visits.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha, Koiwai):** check sea zones and the pork-halal line in shared villages.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check where settlements were placed against clan lands and how residents speak of the edge.
- **PNG border peoples and the diaspora:** comparison only, for how the line looks from the other side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. National-border, map and conservation records are fairly rich; personal and family boundaries (privacy, touch, personal questions) are almost unstudied in Papuan towns.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for charts and survey notes; Marind and Hatam for clan and boundary words; Papuan Malay for "your business" and "may not enter" in daily use.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan boundary stories can be evidence in land disputes and may be closed; checkpoint accounts carry risk for speakers.
- **Held for review:** none; no boundary or purity tradition of South Asian origin appears among these pointers.
