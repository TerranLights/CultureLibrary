# West Papua: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (drinking, boredom and getting-away talk); Indonesian (drug surveys, police and news); Dutch (early trade and mission notes on drink and tobacco); Biak, Hubula, Lani and Marind for older words for leisure, smoke and palm drink. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Maybrat, Arfak peoples):** check whether drink came first through Seram and Maluku traders here, and how early.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Serui, Numfor):** check palm-wine customs against shop liquor and whose gatherings use which.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee):** check tobacco and liquor as old and new escapes, and church responses to each.
- **South coast and Mimika (Kamoro, Asmat, Amungme):** check how mining wages changed leisure in Timika and nearby villages.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** check border cannabis, settler gambling and Papuan leisure as three separate scenes.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Skou):** check beaches, malls and screens as the town's weekend escapes.
- **Raja Ampat and Fakfak (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta):** check how Muslim villages handle drink and gambling beside Christian ones.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check which escapes are shared and which stay apart.
- **Students and workers outside Papua:** comparison only, for escape and homesickness away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Drink and drug coverage is plentiful but mostly police and church framing; leisure, fantasy and screens are known only through features and fan pages.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for *pusing* and *mabuk* in their own settings; Dutch for early trade notes on liquor and tobacco; highland languages for older words of smoking and resting.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blaming Papuans for drink risks stereotype, so causes and suppliers must be traced; prison-escape and drug cases need care with names.
- **Held for review:** none for escapism; no South Asian work or practice was needed among these pointers.
