# West Papua: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Indonesian and Papuan Malay (devotional practice, blessings, prayer sites); Dutch (expedition and mission records); Arabic-derived Indonesian terms on the Muslim coast; Biak, Hubula, Hatam, Ma'ya and Ngalum for sacred beings, stones, lakes and sea. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside the church.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Hatam, Sougb, Meyah, Moi):** check whether the Arfak lakes and forests are spoken of with church words, custom words, or both.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Numfor, Wandamen):** check how *Manseren Nanggi* is placed beside the Christian God in prayer and song.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Ngalum, Ketengban):** check what was done with sacred stones and houses at conversion, valley by valley.
- **South coast and Asmat (Asmat, Kamoro, Auyu):** check Marian and saints' devotions in parishes that also keep ancestor feasts.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check grottoes, *dema* places and grave customs side by side.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** check prayer hills, online worship and lake rites in one urban area.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha, Koiwai):** check *maulid*, *zikir* and sea offerings among Muslim and Christian fishers.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check which shrines, prayer sites and groups are shared.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Australia):** comparison only, for devotion kept away from the land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Church media and mission records are abundant; practice outside church, ancestral sacred places and Muslim village devotion are known mainly through theses and local news, and no survey of spiritual experience is known for Papua.
- **Languages needed:** Hubula and Biak for sacred stones and sky beings; Dutch for the 1959 expedition; Ma'ya for sea offerings; Indonesian Islamic vocabulary for coastal devotion.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *kaneke* and men's-house knowledge are restricted and should be approached only as their keepers allow; apparition claims can split congregations.
- **Held for review:** any South Asian-origin devotional or meditation group meeting in Jayapura or Sorong, as a category only; it would belong to the newcomer layer, not the Papuan core.
