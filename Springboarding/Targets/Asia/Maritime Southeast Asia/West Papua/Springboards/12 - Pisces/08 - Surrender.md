# West Papua: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (letting-go phrases); Indonesian (church, health, news); Dutch (colonial posts and the 1963 handover); Arabic-derived Indonesian terms on the Muslim coast; Marind, Biak, Hubula and Lani for yielding, mourning's end and peace. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher: God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death; release and letting go too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Maybrat, Moi, Tehit):** check how quarrels end, and whether a fixed payment or meal marks who gave in.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Serui, Waropen):** check mourning's formal end and the farewell to the dead's things.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Walak, Yali):** check what defeat in fighting has meant for land and return.
- **South coast and Mimika (Asmat, Kamoro, Mimika):** check how patients and families speak of stopping or refusing treatment.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check memory of 1902 against later surrenders of land.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Skou):** check how 1 May 1963 is marked or avoided in the capital.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check *ikhlas* and *tawakal* against Christian *berserah* in shared villages.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check how newcomers phrase acceptance, and whether Papuans hear it as the same.
- **Diaspora (the Netherlands, Vanuatu, Australia):** comparison only, for exiles who refuse the 1963 settlement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Political surrender and handover are well documented; personal letting go (quarrels, mourning, dying) is scattered in theses and videos, and the national survey items on felt control give no Papuan breakdown.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for *biar sudah* and *baku bae* in tone; Dutch for the 1902 records; Marind and Biak for mourning and defeat words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1 May 1963 is charged, and commemorations carry risk; patients' reasons for stopping treatment need privacy.
- **Held for review:** none; acceptance teachings here come from church, mosque and custom, with no other tradition to set aside.
