# West Papua: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (names for wrongdoers, shame and gossip); Indonesian (news, surveys, church statements); Dutch and English (colonial reports to the United Nations); Tok Pisin and English for PNG comparison only; Biak, Lani, Hubula and Marind for theft, shame and wrongdoing words. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law is researched under Libra and Capricorn, not here.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Arfak peoples, Moi, Maybrat):** check how wildlife catching and selling is judged by those who do it and by neighbors.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Numfor, Yapen):** check which wrongs old tales punish and whether today's talk matches.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Yali, Mee):** check pig theft, adultery and killing as wrongs against a clan, not only a person.
- **South coast and Mimika (Kamoro, Amungme, Asmat):** check which wrongs a mining wage town reports, and who is blamed.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Muyu):** check whether wrongs by companies are spoken of with the same words as a neighbor's theft.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Skou):** check how gangs and street crime are tied in talk to origin.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check how blast fishing and smuggling are judged in island villages.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check how each side describes the other's wrongdoing, as claims to record.
- **PNG border peoples and the diaspora:** comparison only, for sorcery-related violence and cross-border smuggling seen from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Police and conflict reporting is heavy; how ordinary people rank wrongs, shame wrongdoers and take them back is thin and must come from theses, church records and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Papuan Malay for insult and shame words; Dutch for 1950s reports; Lani and Hubula for theft and compensation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "KKB" label, sorcery-related violence and gang reporting are charged and may stereotype; victims of sexual violence need protection of identity.
- **Held for review:** none; the comparative sorcery volume is Melanesian, not of South Asian origin.
