# West Papua: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** West Papua, region Maritime Southeast Asia. **Language(s) for searching:** Papuan Malay (fright, dread and taboo talk); Indonesian (disaster agencies, psychology, news); Korowai, Asmat, Kamoro, Biak and Ma'ya for spirit, monster and danger words. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bird's Head (Moi, Tehit, Maybrat):** check forest taboos and what is feared in clan forests now opened to logging.
- **Cenderawasih Bay and Biak (Biak, Numfor, Wandamen):** check tsunami memory and drills against feared capes and reefs.
- **Central Highlands and Baliem (Hubula, Lani, Nduga, Damal):** check how trauma is spoken of in valleys with and without recent armed conflict.
- **South coast and lowland forests (Korowai, Asmat, Kamoro, Auyu):** check how the fear of outsiders, animals and spirits is named, people by people.
- **Merauke lowlands (Marind, Kanum, Yei):** check fears tied to land loss and to newcomers' arrival.
- **Jayapura and Sentani (Sentani, Tobati-Enggros, Nafri):** check sirens, haunted sites and school fears in the capital.
- **Raja Ampat, Fakfak and Kaimana (Ma'ya, Mbaham-Matta, Iha):** check the sea's feared places and fears across the faith line.
- **Migrant settler communities in the towns:** a present-day layer; check what settlers fear in Papua and what Papuans fear of settlers, each as stated.
- **Papuan students in other Indonesian cities, and the diaspora:** comparison only, for fear of racism and of returning home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Fear of violence is heavily documented in rights writing; fear of spirits, animals and the sea and social fear are mostly in folklore posts and theses; no fear survey is known that breaks out Papuan regencies.
- **Languages needed:** Korowai for *laleo*; Ma'ya and Biak for feared places at sea; Papuan Malay for *ngeri* and *trauma* as used in testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trauma testimony carries risk for speakers; fears across the faith line can inflame; raid memories must not be framed as a trait of any people.
- **Held for review:** none; fear sources here are local, Indonesian and missionary, with nothing set aside.
