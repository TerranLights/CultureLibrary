## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol and East Tyrol:** check whether a Tyrolean identity runs ahead of an Austrian one; South Tyrol only as comparison, since it belongs elsewhere.
- **Vorarlberg:** check Alemannic self-description against the Bavarian-speaking east, and the 1919 Swiss vote as a memory.
- **Franconia, Bavarian Swabia, Upper Palatinate:** check how strongly each names itself apart from Old Bavaria.
- **Innviertel and Rupertiwinkel:** check identity in lands that changed state.
- **Carinthian Slovenes, Burgenland Croats, Burgenland Hungarians:** check generational shift from language-based to chosen identity.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check how Burgenland Roma, Lovara and Bavarian Sinti name the difference between themselves.
- **Protestants of Carinthia, Upper Austria and Franconia:** check confession as identity, where it set families apart from Catholic neighbors.
- **Sudeten German, Danube Swabian and other expellee descendants:** check how far a lost homeland persists in the third generation.
- **Turkish- and Bosnian-Austrians and Bavarians:** check hyphenated labels in use, and religion against origin as the first answer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; identity has been surveyed and argued over for decades in both Austria and Bavaria; thinner for Roma, Slovaks and Burgenland Hungarians.
- **Languages needed:** Slovene, Croatian and Hungarian for minority self-accounts that never reached German print; Turkish and Bosnian for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era identity, the Waldheim debate and the victim narrative; the Carinthian plebiscite memory; expellee politics; Roma persecution.
- **Held for review:** none; every community named above is one the standing rules allow as a pointer.
