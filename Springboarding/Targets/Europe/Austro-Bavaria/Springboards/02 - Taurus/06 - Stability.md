*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Burgenland Croat villages:** check what has stayed constant since the sixteenth-century settlement and what changed after 1921 and 1989.
- **Weekly commuters from Burgenland, the Waldviertel and East Tyrol:** check how a family keeps a stable home while its earners live elsewhere.
- **Salzburg and Tyrolean resort towns:** check seasonal churn of staff and guests against long-settled hotel families.
- **Vienna's regulars of cafés and *Heurigen* against newer districts and suburbs:** check whether "my café" is a habit of old districts or of particular people.
- **Yenish and Sinti families:** check how they tell the shift between traveling and settled life, and the pressures behind it; sources are scarce, so begin with community associations and oral history.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Turkish-Bavarians:** check how intentions to return or stay have changed across three generations.
- **Polish and Ukrainian newcomers:** check how long arrivals expect to stay and what makes them settle.
- **Old Bavarian farm families against Munich's commuter belt:** check how fast villages around the city have changed and what residents say about it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions, currency, elections and heritage; mixed for personal routine, which needs diaries, interviews and time-use material.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for monastic chronicles; Burgenland Croatian for parish continuity; Turkish for return-migration accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yenish and Sinti histories of forced settlement and removal of children; nostalgia for the monarchy or for one-party eras is politically loaded.
- **Held for review:** none; monastic, monetary and heritage-protection pointers remain in full.
