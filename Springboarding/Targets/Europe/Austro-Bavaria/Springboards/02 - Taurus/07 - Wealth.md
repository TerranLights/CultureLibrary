## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Augsburg and Bavarian Swabia:** check how the Fugger legacy is told locally, as pride, charity or warning.
- **Kitzbühel, Lech and Lake Starnberg against the Waldviertel and Upper Palatinate:** check how residents of rich enclaves and poorer districts each describe the other.
- **Burgenland villages on former Esterházy estates:** check memory of the estate as employer, landlord and patron.
- **Upper Styria (Erzberg) and Tyrol (Schwaz):** check how mining towns remember past riches after the boom ended.
- **Viennese Czech heritage families:** check accounts of rising from craft and service jobs into the city's middle class.
- **Serbian-, Croatian- and Bosnian-Austrians:** check wedding spending and the houses built in the home village as signs of success abroad.
- **Turkish-Austrian and Turkish-Bavarian business owners:** check how founders and their children speak of wealth earned in trade and gastronomy.
- **Burgenland Roma:** check how wealth and poverty are named inside families; sources are scarce, so begin with Romani-language media and community associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dynastic wealth, mining history, literature and inequality research; thin on the very rich, who disclose little.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German for Fugger records; Hungarian for Esterházy estate papers; Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian and Turkish for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antisemitic tropes about wealth that recur in old sources (cite only as material to study); wealth-tax debates are partisan; poverty data on Roma.
- **Held for review:** none; dynastic, mining and inequality pointers were kept without exception.
