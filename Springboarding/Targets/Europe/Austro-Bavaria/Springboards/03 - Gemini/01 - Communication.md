# Austro-Bavaria: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German; Bavarian and Viennese varieties; Alemannic, East Franconian, Swabian; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; ÖGS and DGS; Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian; Yenish cant. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts locally as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Bavaria and Lutheran Franconia:** check whether greetings and address forms split along the confessional line or the dialect line.
- **North Tyrol and East Tyrol:** check valley-level speech differences and whether listeners from the east follow them.
- **Styria and Carinthia against Vienna:** check how southern Bavarian intonation is heard by other Austrians (perceptual dialectology).
- **Carinthian Slovenes in mixed families:** check how Slovene address forms map onto German *du* and *Sie* at home.
- **Turkish- and Bosnian-Austrian and Bavarian children:** check language brokering for parents, and what it does to who speaks for the family.
- **Deaf signers of ÖGS and of DGS:** check contact across the border between two sign languages.
- **Yenish and Roma:** check where cant or Romani shields talk from outsiders, and whether speakers want it studied.
- **Viennese Czech and Slovak heritage families:** check traces in Viennese vocabulary and in household bilingual talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialectology, pragmatics and media; mixed for everyday conversation norms (few recorded-talk studies); thin for Yenish and Sinti speech.
- **Languages needed:** ÖGS video literacy for Deaf sources; Rotwelsch and Yenish glossaries; Slovene and Hungarian for address-form research in minority homes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yenish and Romani as in-group languages, which some speakers do not want taught to outsiders; children's interpreting in medical settings.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing in the interpreting, sign-language or dialect pointers falls under the standing rules.
