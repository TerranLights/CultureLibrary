## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol and Vorarlberg families of former *Schwabenkinder*:** check how child work abroad is remembered as learning or as loss.
- **Lutheran Franconia against Catholic Old Bavaria:** check catechism against Bible reading as the road to early literacy.
- **Burgenland Croats and Hungarians:** check which language families choose for schooling, and what it leads to.
- **Polish and Ukrainian communities of Vienna and Munich:** check parish Saturday schools as the channel for language and faith.
- **Turkish-, Serbian- and Croatian-Austrian families:** check paid tutoring and the choice at ten between *Gymnasium* and *Mittelschule*.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check placement in special schools in the past and Romani teaching now; data are thin, so start with community associations.
- **Deaf children and their parents:** check oral against bilingual schooling in Vienna and in Bavaria.
- **Upper Palatinate and Weinviertel rural households:** check apprenticeship against academic routes when schools are far away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history, tests and apprenticeship; mixed for learning at home; thin for Romani, Yenish and Ukrainian learning histories.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for university rolls and monastic school records; Ukrainian for Barbareum sources; ÖGS for Deaf school memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma school segregation; abuse in church and state boarding schools; children's records.
- **Held for review:** none; the school, tutoring and apprenticeship pointers fall outside every rule that would set one aside.
