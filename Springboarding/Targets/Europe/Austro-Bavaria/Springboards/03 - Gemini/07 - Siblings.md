# Austro-Bavaria: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German; Bavarian and Viennese varieties; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian; Latin and Middle High German for dynastic records. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mühlviertel and Innviertel farm families:** check what became of non-inheriting brothers and sisters: servants on the home farm, emigrants, priests and nuns.
- **Turkish-Austrian families:** check whether *abi* and *abla* keep their weight in Austrian-born generations.
- **Bosnian-Austrians:** check which sibling migrated first and whom each brought after.
- **Burgenland Hungarian speakers:** check whether the age-graded sibling words survive in German-dominant homes.
- **Roma and Sinti families:** check sibling bonds after the genocide's losses; sources are few, so start with memoirs and community archives.
- **Sudeten German and Danube Swabian families:** check siblings separated by flight and expulsion, using the German Red Cross tracing service in Munich.
- **Vienna against rural Lower Austria:** check the share of only children and what it does to care in old age.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for family statistics, dynastic history and literature; mixed for everyday sibling roles; thin for minority kin terms in use today.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Middle High German for partition charters; Hungarian and Turkish for age-graded kin terms; Romani for Lovara family memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse in children's homes; genocide survivors' family records; inheritance disputes involving living people.
- **Held for review:** none; the family-history and statistics pointers meet the standing rules without change.
