# Austro-Bavaria: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German, Bavarian (Boarisch) and Viennese, Alemannic (Vorarlberg), East Franconian; Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Romani, Czech; Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian; Yenish (cant words) where sources exist. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vienna tenants against Häuslbauer districts of Styria and Upper Austria:** check whether "home" in speech and surveys tracks renting or owning.
- **Upper Palatinate and Waldviertel villagers moved for training areas:** check how the dispossessed name and visit the lost home.
- **Burgenland Roma:** check Roma-run sources on rebuilt settlements; academic material is limited, so start with community associations.
- **Yenish families of Tyrol (the *Karrner*):** check how a home on the road is described; sources are scarce, so try Tyrolean folk-life archives and Yenish associations.
- **Turkish- and Bosnian-Austrian households of the guest-worker era:** check the move from company barracks to rented and owned flats, in community media.
- **Chechen-Austrians:** check how a first home after asylum is described, in the community's own associations and refugee-aid reports; little is published.
- **East Tyrol:** check whether being cut off from North Tyrol in 1919 changed which place locals call home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing statistics and literature; mixed for the feeling of home; thin for Yenish and Chechen-Austrian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for *Helmbrecht*; Turkish and BCS for *gurbet* writing; dialect for village memory books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** persecution-era seizures of homes (1938-1945); asylum residents' privacy; evictions involving living persons.
- **Held for review:** none; the housing, displacement and literary pointers all pass the rules.
