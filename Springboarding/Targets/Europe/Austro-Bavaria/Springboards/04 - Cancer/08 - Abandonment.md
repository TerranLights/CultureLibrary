# Austro-Bavaria: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German, Bavarian (Boarisch), Viennese, East Franconian, Swabian; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani, Yenish; Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian; Russian and English for occupation-era records; Middle High German. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Carinthian and Styrian farm districts:** check how far historical illegitimacy and placing children with others show up in parish registers and life stories.
- **Mühlviertel and Lower Austria (former Soviet zone) against Salzburg and Upper Austria south of the Danube (US zone):** check occupation-children cases and the help each received.
- **Turkish- and Yugoslav-era guest-worker families:** check grown children's own memoirs of years with grandparents, not outside accounts.
- **Yenish families:** check whether children were removed into care in Austria and Bavaria; sources are scarce, and Swiss cases are comparison only.
- **Danube Swabian children separated in the 1944-1948 camps:** check tracing-service files and memoirs.
- **Upper Palatinate and Bavarian Forest villages:** check how emptied farms and lone elders appear in local press.
- **Burgenland Croat and Hungarian villages:** check minority-language accounts of families left by emigrants to America.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundling history, illegitimacy and care-home abuse; mixed for present-day abandonment; thin for Yenish and Roma child-removal records.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for *Gregorius*; Russian and English for occupation-era files; Turkish and BCS for *Kofferkinder* memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foundling and adoption files; care-home survivors; occupation children are living persons.
- **Held for review:** none; the foundling, migration and depopulation pointers needed no setting aside.
