# Austro-Bavaria: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German, Bavarian (Boarisch), Viennese, Alemannic (Vorarlberg), East Franconian; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani; Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian, Tagalog, Romanian, Slovak; Early Middle High German for the earliest sources. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Carinthian Slovene and German-speaking Carinthians:** check how Hemma is honored in each language and in which parishes.
- **Vienna against Tyrol and rural Bavaria:** check the length of leave and mothers' part-time work in each, from labor-market data, not opinion.
- **Lutheran Franconia against Catholic Old Bavaria:** check how Marian devotion and Mother's Day sermons differ between the confessions.
- **Filipino-Austrian mothers:** check accounts of raising children across two countries, in Filipino community and parish sources.
- **Slovak and Romanian carers who are mothers:** check their own accounts of children left at home while they care in Austrian homes.
- **Turkish- and Bosnian-Austrian mothers:** check their own associations and the course participants' accounts, never outside summaries.
- **Romani women in Vienna and Burgenland:** check Romani women's associations for mothers' own accounts; little is published.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth statistics, maternity history and literature; mixed for mothers' own accounts today; thin for Romani and Chechen-Austrian mothers' voices.
- **Languages needed:** Early Middle High German for Frau Ava; Tagalog, Romanian and Slovak for transnational mothers; Slovene for the Hemma cult.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lebensborn births and records; maternal deaths; abortion and reproductive law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the saint, welfare and literary pointers on mothers stay in.
