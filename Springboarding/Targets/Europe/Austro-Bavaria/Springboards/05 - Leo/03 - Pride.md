# Austro-Bavaria: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German, the German of Bavaria, Bavarian and Viennese dialect, East Franconian, Tyrolean Bavarian; Carinthian Slovene; Burgenland Croatian and Hungarian; Romani; Turkish; Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol and East Tyrol against Vienna:** check how each tells its founding pride (1809 against the imperial city), since the Länder may hold rival stories.
- **Franconia and Bavarian Swabia against Old Bavaria:** check whether regional pride in the north and west is told within or against a Bavarian pride.
- **German-speaking and Slovene Carinthians:** check both sides' commemorations and press, since one date carries opposite meanings.
- **Burgenland Croats and Hungarians:** check how the youngest Land's centenary (2021) was marked in each minority's own media.
- **Serbian- and Croatian-Austrians of the guest-worker era:** check community media on which national team their children cheer, and why; avoid any assumption.
- **Sudeten German descendants in Bavaria:** check how pride in a lost homeland is kept by the second and third generations, in their associations' periodicals.
- **Lutheran Upper Franconia:** check sermons and church papers for how a Protestant tradition treats pride and humility beside the Catholic one.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state symbols, sport and regional history; mixed for personal pride and bragging norms; thin for Yenish and Roma pride writing and for Burgenland Hungarian youth.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for Trimberg; Latin for the *Privilegium maius*; Slovene for Carinthian counter-memory; Turkish and BCS for community sports media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pride symbols used by the far right; Hofer and plebiscite commemorations tied to ethnic conflict; minors in national-team youth coverage.
- **Held for review:** none; each anthem, sport and symbol pointer here could stand as written.
