*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vienna's civil-service milieu against Lower Bavaria and the Mühlviertel:** check whether title use in address is urban, generational or both, using dialect and pragmatics surveys.
- **Swabian and Franconian Bavaria against Old Bavaria:** check whether sayings on withheld praise are local or shared, in the dialect dictionaries.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check their own associations' accounts of the struggle for acknowledgment, beside state records.
- **Yenish families in Tyrol and Bavaria:** check whether and how they have asked to be recognized as a group; sources are scarce, so start with oral history and press.
- **Polish and Ukrainian workers and nurses:** check counseling reports on which credentials were refused and how that was felt.
- **Filipino and Serbian-Austrian health workers:** check community media for public thanks during the pandemic and what followed.
- **Waldviertel and Weinviertel volunteers:** check fire-brigade and Red Cross long-service decorations as the main local honors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honors lists, titles and official apologies; mixed for workplace praise; thin for Yenish recognition and for the minority associations' own prizes.
- **Languages needed:** Kurrent and court German for the almanacs; Slovene and Croatian minority press; Polish, Ukrainian and Tagalog for credential stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims' files in compensation archives; revoked honors of living people; the legal side of minority status and nobility titles goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the honors and title pointers carry no name that needed setting aside.
