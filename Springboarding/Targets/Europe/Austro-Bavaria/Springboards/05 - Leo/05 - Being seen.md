# Austro-Bavaria: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Viennese and Upper Palatinate dialect; Carinthian Slovene; Burgenland Croatian and Hungarian; Romani; Czech and Slovak; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Carinthian Slovenes and Burgenland Hungarians:** check what each said about being uncounted after 2001, in their own press and associations.
- **Vienna's Czech and Slovak heritage:** check family memoirs and school-association records for when names and language stopped being shown in public, and why.
- **Muslim women in Vienna and Munich:** check documentation-center reports and community writing on being looked at in public, in their own words.
- **Bavarian Forest and Upper Palatinate border villages against Munich:** check greeting and noticing norms by settlement size, since village and city may set opposite rules.
- **Styrian and Lower Austrian older people living alone:** check social-service and press data on isolation, by district.
- **Roma of Burgenland and Sinti in Bavaria:** check how their associations want them shown in museums and memorials.
- **Turkish-Austrian families:** check whether the *nazar* bead and Bavarian *Fraisen* charms answer the same worry, using each tradition's own sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court protocol, census method and media studies; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting; thin for Yenish and Hungarian Burgenland voices on visibility.
- **Languages needed:** court German and French for protocol records; Slovene and Hungarian minority press; Romani for the Graz material; Turkish for evil-eye lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census data on minorities was used against them in 1938-1945; surveillance records; names of people found dead alone.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing among the census, gaze and surveillance pointers had to be held back.
