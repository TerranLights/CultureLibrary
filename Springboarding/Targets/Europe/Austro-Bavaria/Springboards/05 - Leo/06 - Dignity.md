# Austro-Bavaria: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese dialect; Carinthian Slovene; Burgenland Croatian and Hungarian; Romani; Czech; Polish; Turkish; Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vienna's municipal care system against Upper Palatinate and Lower Bavarian church-run homes:** check whether care charters and inspection findings use the same dignity words.
- **Burgenland Roma and Bavarian Sinti:** check their associations' accounts of daily treatment by offices, schools and police, beside official reports.
- **Bosnian- and Serbian-Austrians of the guest-worker era:** check hostel and housing histories and community memoirs for the indignities first arrivals describe, without assuming one story.
- **Polish and Slovak care workers in Austrian homes:** check their own forums and union reports on how they are spoken to and housed.
- **Carinthian farm laborers and servants (*Knechte*, *Mägde*):** check life-story collections for bearing kept under hard work.
- **Disabled people in Styria and Upper Austria:** check self-advocacy groups' own writing on the "dignity of risk" in assisted living.
- **Danube Swabian and Sudeten German expellees:** check camp and arrival memoirs for how being stripped of property was told as a loss of standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church teaching, Nazi-era testimony and care policy; mixed for everyday courtesy; thin for Yenish experience and for carers from abroad in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German for dance-of-death verses; Polish and Slovak for care workers; Romani and BCS for community memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivor testimony under archive conditions; care-home residents' privacy; assisted-dying law goes to Libra and its purpose to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the care, poverty and testimony sources needed no setting aside.
