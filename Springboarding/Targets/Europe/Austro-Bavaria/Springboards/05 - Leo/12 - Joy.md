# Austro-Bavaria: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Tyrolean and Viennese dialect; East Franconian; Burgenland Hungarian and Croatian; Carinthian Slovene; Romani; Bosnian; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol and the Salzkammergut against Vienna:** check whether the sounds of joy (whoop, yodel, brass) and the urban forms (Heuriger song, concert) differ by place more than by Land.
- **Lutheran Middle Franconia against Catholic Lower Bavaria:** check church and festival calendars for which days are kept for gladness.
- **Upper Franconia's brewing villages:** check local festival records for how the village beer feast is described by its hosts.
- **Bosnian-Austrians:** check *sevdah* evenings and Bayram gatherings in Vienna, in community media, for where joy and longing sit together.
- **Turkish-Austrian families:** check how Bayram is celebrated and shared with neighbors, in their own reports.
- **Roma of Burgenland:** check song archives and association events for joyful music kept or revived after 1945.
- **Ukrainian newcomers since 2022:** check whether community events speak of joy at all in wartime, and how.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music, festival and survey data; mixed for everyday gladness; thin for Yenish joy and for Burgenland Hungarian celebration in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Baroque German for Easter sermons; dialect for *Juchizer* and Wienerlied; Romani and Bosnian for song archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era use of Alpine joy and fires for propaganda; mental-health data on depression; sacred meaning of some fires.
- **Held for review:** none; the music, festival and well-being sources were all clear to name.
