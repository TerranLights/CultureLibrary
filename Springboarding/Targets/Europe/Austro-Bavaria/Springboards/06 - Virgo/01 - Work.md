# Austro-Bavaria: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Viennese, Alemannic, East Franconian, Swabian; Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; Turkish and Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic Old Bavaria and Lutheran Franconia:** check whether sermons, school readers and parish records frame work as calling differently, a local test of the confession-and-work debate.
- **Lower Bavaria and the Innviertel against Vienna and Linz:** check farm memoirs against factory memoirs to see whether "work" means the same chores and hours.
- **Waldviertel and Mühlviertel cottage weavers:** check textile-museum and parish records for home-based weaving as household work, a trade that left fewer company records.
- **Upper Palatinate and Bavarian Forest glassworks, Upper Franconian porcelain towns:** check company and museum archives for how a skilled trade passed through families.
- **Yenish itinerant trades (knife-grinding, basketry, peddling):** check Yenish associations in Tyrol and Bavaria for their own accounts; sources are scarce, so start with oral history.
- **Turkish-, Serbian- and Bosnian-Austrians and Bavarians:** check whether children of guest workers describe their parents' work in different terms from the parents themselves.
- **Polish and Ukrainian harvest and care workers:** check their own-language community media for working conditions; official data may miss them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor-movement, survey and memoir sources; mixed for undeclared work, which is estimated rather than counted; thin for Yenish and Roma own-voice accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for *Meier Helmbrecht*; Kurrent for workhouse and parish records; Turkish and BCS for migrant memoirs; Polish and Ukrainian for seasonal workers' media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi forced labor and the *arbeitsscheu* label; workhouse records naming individuals; undeclared-work data.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing about labor here needed setting aside.
