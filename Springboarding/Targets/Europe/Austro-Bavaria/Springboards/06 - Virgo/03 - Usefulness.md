## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alpine hill farms against lowland farms in the Weinviertel and Lower Bavaria:** check handover customs and what the retired generation keeps doing, since farm size shapes the arrangement.
- **Styria:** check Joanneum and farm-society records for how "useful knowledge" was carried to villages, rather than assuming Vienna's reforms reached them.
- **Shrinking towns in Upper Franconia, the Upper Palatinate and the Waldviertel:** check community studies on older residents' roles where young people left.
- **Disabled people and the families of Nazi "euthanasia" victims:** check self-advocacy publications and memorial archives for their own words, not institutional ones.
- **Burgenland Croats and Carinthian Slovenes:** check language-revival projects for how they describe elders as keepers of the language.
- **Turkish-, Bosnian- and Serbian-Austrian and Bavarian families:** check studies of children's language brokering and grandparents' care across borders.
- **Danube Swabian and Sudeten German refugees after 1945:** check refugee-association memoirs for how arrivals spoke of being needed or surplus in the postwar economy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Enlightenment reform, aging surveys and the euthanasia history; mixed for disability workshops; thin for Romani and Yenish sources on elders and children's help.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-century chancery German for Josephine and Rumford records; Hungarian and Croatian for minority press; Turkish and BCS for family studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi "euthanasia" records name victims and need archive permission; disability and end-of-life data; children as interpreters.
