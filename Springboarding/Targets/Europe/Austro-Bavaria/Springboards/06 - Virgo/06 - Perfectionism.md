*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Augsburg and Nuremberg craft traditions against Alpine valley crafts:** check guild and workshop records for whether exactness was enforced by town bodies or learned in families.
- **Bavarian school districts with different shares of migrant families:** check transition-rate studies for how grade pressure falls on Turkish-, Bosnian- and Serbian-heritage pupils; a research question, not an assumption.
- **Viennese Czech tailors, shoemakers and craftsmen:** check trade registers and Czech association papers for how their work was judged.
- **Trachten associations in Upper Bavaria, Salzburg and Styria:** compare their rules on "correct" costume, which vary by district.
- **Danube Swabian costume and embroidery groups:** check how they keep exact patterns away from the villages where those patterns began.
- **Upper Austrian scythe smiths and Styrian iron towns:** check family and company histories for craft standards.
- **Musicians and riders in Vienna and Salzburg:** check memoirs for how they describe the cost of flawlessness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for craft history, music, literature and school surveys; mixed for workplace perfectionism; thin for Romani and Yenish standards of craft and performance.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German for guild and assay records; 18th-century German for Leopold Mozart; Czech for Viennese trade papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pupils' mental health and eating disorders; the stereotype of national "precision" or "sloppiness", which a researcher must not adopt.
- **Held for review:** none; no exactness or craft pointer here was set aside.
