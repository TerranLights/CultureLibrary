## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrolean and Salzburg mountain valleys and Vienna:** check folk-medicine records against hospital history for where prayer charms and herbal remedies stayed in use beside doctors.
- **Spa towns (Bad Ischl, Bad Gastein, Bad Füssing) and industrial towns (Linz, Steyr, Eisenerz):** compare who took a cure and who carried the illnesses of heavy industry.
- **Mining and salt districts (Schwaz, Hallein, the Erzberg):** check miners' health records and memorials for work-related illness across centuries.
- **Burgenland Hungarians:** check Hungarian ethnographic sources on wise women healers, and whether Oberwart records match those across the border.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check studies of access to care; sources are scarce, so look to Roma associations and public-health reports rather than general European material.
- **Turkish-, Bosnian- and Serbian-Austrians and Bavarians:** check transcultural psychiatry services in Vienna and Munich, and community media, for how illness and the evil eye are discussed.
- **Seasonal harvest and tourism workers:** check union and health-insurance reports for their access to care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for spa history, pilgrimage, hospital history and health surveys; mixed for folk healing today; thin for Romani, Yenish and seasonal workers' health.
