## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Burgenland Roma and Sinti:** check their own historical associations and Baumgartner's sources for how forced "improvement" is remembered; outside sources are thin.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check school histories and Slovene memoirs for how German-language schooling was framed as advancement.
- **Moor and forest districts (Donaumoos, Bavarian Forest, Waldviertel):** check local histories for land "improvement" and the trades or habitats it ended.
- **Red Vienna and Catholic rural Bavaria:** compare two programs for improving people, socialist and Catholic, in their own publications.
- **Danube Swabians in Upper Austria:** check association memoirs for how their ancestors' 18th-century settlement is told as improving land, and set that beside the accounts of the peoples already living there.
- **First-generation students of Turkish, Bosnian and Serbian heritage:** check mobility studies for their routes upward; a research question, never assumed.
- **Lower Bavarian and East Tyrolean villages in renewal programs:** check meeting records for who had a say.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for monastic reform, adult education, Red Vienna and land improvement; mixed for self-help reading; thin for Roma, Sinti and Yenish own accounts of imposed betterment.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** Habsburg and Nazi-era records on Roma; names of children removed from families; residents displaced by renewal.
