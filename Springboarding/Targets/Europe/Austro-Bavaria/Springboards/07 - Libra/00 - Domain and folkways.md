## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic Old Bavaria and Lutheran Middle Franconia:** check how church marriage rules on divorce and mixed confessions shaped registers before civil marriage became compulsory.
- **Vienna against the Alpine districts of Salzburg, Carinthia and Styria:** check marriage-permit and illegitimacy records, since the right to marry once depended on property.
- **Burgenland Croats and Burgenland Hungarians:** check bilingual parish registers and wedding songs for marriages across language lines.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check their own weeklies and associations for how the Article 7 promise is read on their side.
- **Roma and Sinti of Burgenland and Bavaria:** check their own organizations' publications for wedding forms in their own words; outside accounts are thin.
- **Same-sex couples in Vienna and Munich:** check registry data and community archives from 2010 onward.
- **Vorarlberg against Tyrol:** check village law and marriage contracts across the Alemannic–Bavarian dialect line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, treaties and registers; mixed for wedding custom (much collected, often romanticized); thin for minority marriage practice in its own voice. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the pre-war lands carried are Germany and Austria, read here as Bavaria and Austria.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the folk law, Early New High German for the 1346 *Landrecht*, Kurrent for parish and court hands, and Slovene and Burgenland Croatian for the minorities' side of the treaty.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era marriage bans and forced dissolutions after 1938; adoption and illegitimacy records; Article 7 remains politically charged in Carinthia.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on marriage, treaties or settlement here needed setting aside.
