## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Burgenland:** check the Schattendorf files and local memory, where a national crisis of justice began in one village.
- **Sinti of Bavaria and Roma of Burgenland:** check compensation files and their own organizations' histories of recognition, which ran decades behind other victims'.
- **Jewish communities of Vienna, Munich and Nuremberg:** check restitution case records and community archives for how redress was sought.
- **Carinthian Slovenes and Burgenland Croats:** check court-language provisions and their use, since procedure in one's own language is written into law.
- **The Waldviertel and Bavarian Swabia:** check outlaw trials against regional ballads, two cases of popular verdicts on the law.
- **Middle Franconia (Nuremberg):** check how a city carries both the 1935 race laws and the 1945–46 trials in its memorial work.
- **Serbian-, Croatian- and Turkish-Austrians of the guest-worker era:** check court-interpreter rosters and legal-aid use as measures of access to fair procedure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, trials and redress law; mixed for lower-court practice before 1900; thin for Roma, Sinti and Yenish accounts of courts before the 1980s.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German and Kurrent for the 18th-century codes and trial files; Slovene and Burgenland Croatian for bilingual court practice; Hebrew and Yiddish names in restitution files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era files and victims' personal data (archive restrictions apply); the ethnic policing records against Sinti and Yenish; juvenile records.
