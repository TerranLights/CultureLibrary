*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese; Alemannic, East Franconian, Swabian; Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; Turkish and BCS; French and Latin for older treaties. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vienna's diplomatic and international-organization district against provincial capitals:** check protocol offices and hosting records for how each presents itself to guests.
- **Salzburg and the Innviertel:** check local histories of the 1816 transfer, since their people were the object of the bargain.
- **Burgenland Hungarians and Burgenland Croats:** check the 1921 transfer of Burgenland from Hungary and local records of how the change was negotiated and received.
- **Viennese Czech and Slovak heritage:** check interwar association records on the Brno treaty between Austria and Czechoslovakia (1920 *check*), where minority schooling was bargained between states.
- **Turkish-, Serbian- and Bosnian-Austrians:** check interpreting and mediation programs in hospitals and schools, where language brokering is daily work.
- **Tyrol and East Tyrol:** check *Schützen* honor-guard customs at state receptions and the press over their use.
- **Lutheran Upper Franconia:** check how a Protestant region relates to a Catholic state's concordat in school and university appointments.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties, congress papers and state archives; mixed for everyday tact (mostly in literature and sociolinguistics); thin for Romani and Yenish go-betweens' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** French and Latin for 1814–16 papers; Kurrent for legation files; Italian for the South Tyrol comparison; Turkish and BCS for interpreting studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some 20th-century foreign-ministry files remain closed; interpreting research involves patient data.
- **Held for review:** none; no diplomacy source was set aside.
