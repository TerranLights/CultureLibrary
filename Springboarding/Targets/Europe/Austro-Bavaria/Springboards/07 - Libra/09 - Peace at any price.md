# Austro-Bavaria: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese; Alemannic, East Franconian, Swabian; Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; Turkish and BCS; Ottoman Turkish and Latin for the old treaties. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vienna against Upper Austria and Salzburg:** check 1938 press and diaries for the range of responses to the Berchtesgaden and Munich concessions.
- **Viennese Czech heritage and Sudeten German descendants:** check their associations' publications, since the Munich Agreement fell on both and is remembered from opposite sides.
- **Jewish communities of Vienna and Munich:** check community archives and memoirs for what accommodation before 1938 bought and failed to buy.
- **Carinthian Slovenes and Burgenland Croats:** check minority press for when they chose public protest and when quiet negotiation over rights.
- **Alpine sports clubs in Tyrol, Salzburg and Vorarlberg:** check federation inquiries for how silence was kept in tight local communities.
- **Old Catholics and Lutheran parishes:** check their histories of accommodation within Catholic-majority districts.
- **Women in rural Styria and the Upper Palatinate:** check counseling-center records against urban ones for unreported violence at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1938, pacifism and abuse inquiries; mixed for everyday conflict avoidance (mostly in literature and idiom); thin for Romani, Yenish and migrant families' own accounts of keeping quiet, and Romani terms are still to be found.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish and Latin for the tribute treaties; Czech for the Munich side of the story; Slovene and Croatian for minority press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse victims' testimony; 1930s political violence; Nazi-era memory in small towns, where living families are named.
- **Held for review:** none; the sources on yielding and silence raised no flag.
