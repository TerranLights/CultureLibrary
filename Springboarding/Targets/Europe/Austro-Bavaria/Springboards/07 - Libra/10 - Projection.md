*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese; Alemannic, East Franconian, Swabian; Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Romani, Czech, Slovak; Turkish and BCS; Latin and Early New High German for trial records. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others what one disowns in oneself, and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol against Salzburg:** check trial records, since one territory stopped a witch hunt and the other ran a long series of trials.
- **Lower Bavaria (Deggendorf) and the Innsbruck district (Rinn):** check parish and diocesan files on how local devotion carried accusations and how communities reacted when they were ended.
- **Roma and Sinti of Burgenland and Bavaria:** check their own organizations' documentation of how they were blamed in press and policy, and how they answered.
- **Jewish communities of Vienna, Munich and Nuremberg:** check community and survey data on how blame aimed at them shifted over time.
- **Sudeten German descendants and Viennese Czechs:** check their associations' publications for each group's images of the other after 1945.
- **Yenish families:** check the scarce sources on how "travelers" were cast as outsiders in local records; sources are thin, so start with Austrian and Bavarian Yenish associations and regional police and welfare archives.
- **Bavarian Swabia and Lutheran Upper Franconia:** check local newspapers for the images each holds of "Old Bavaria", a projection inside Bavaria itself.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for witch trials, legal texts, surveys and postwar memory; mixed for everyday blame in couples and families; thin for Yenish and Romani voices, and Romani terms for blame are still to be found.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Early New High German for trial files; Kurrent; Czech for postwar images; Romani for Roma organizations' own records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antisemitic and anti-Roma material must be quoted only as evidence; victims' names in trial files; hate-crime case data.
- **Held for review:** none; the sources on blame and projection were kept.
