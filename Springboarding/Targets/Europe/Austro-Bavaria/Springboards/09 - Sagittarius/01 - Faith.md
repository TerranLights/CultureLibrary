# Austro-Bavaria: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and Bavarian German; Old High German for the earliest prayer; Bavarian and Viennese idiom; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani; Serbian, Turkish and Bosnian; Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic Bamberg and Lutheran Bayreuth within Upper Franconia:** check prayer practice and vows across a confessional line that runs through one district.
- **The Waldviertel and Vienna:** check self-rated faith against membership, so that rural loyalty to a parish is not taken for belief.
- **Vorarlberg and Bavarian Swabia:** check Alemannic and Swabian prayer and pilgrimage forms against Old Bavarian ones.
- **Serbian and Romanian Orthodox households:** check fasting calendars and house blessings in each church's own parish literature.
- **Alevi and Sunni Turkish-Austrians and Bavarians:** check the Alevi federations' and mosque associations' own wording of faith, kept apart.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses and free churches:** check conversion and testimony accounts in their own publications.
- **Burgenland Roma:** check, with their own associations, which churches they attend and in what language they pray; sources may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic practice and survey trends; mixed for Protestant and Orthodox faith in daily life; thin for Romani and Yenish faith in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Old High German for Wessobrunn; Latin and Kurrent for miracle books; Serbian Cyrillic for parish bulletins; Slovene and Croatian for minority devotional books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse-related leaving touches victims; conversion away from a family faith; Witnesses' Nazi-era persecution.
- **Held for review:** devotional movements and meditation teachers whose roots lie in South Asia, kept as a category only.
