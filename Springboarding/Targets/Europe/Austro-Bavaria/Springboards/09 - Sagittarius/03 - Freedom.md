# Austro-Bavaria: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and Bavarian German; Viennese stage idiom of 1848; Latin; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani; Bosnian and Turkish; Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of the Protestant enclaves in Carinthia and Upper Austria:** check how parish histories tell freedom of conscience, and whether they tell it as their own.
- **Lutheran Upper Franconia against Catholic Old Bavaria:** check whether the 1918 *Freistaat* and the *Liberalitas* motto carry the same weight across the old confessional line.
- **German-speaking and Slovene-speaking Carinthians:** check which liberation each group marks, using both groups' own publications.
- **The Upper Palatinate and the Bavarian Forest against Burgenland:** check how two Iron Curtain borders are remembered in local oral history.
- **Bosnian-Austrians who arrived as refugees (1992–95):** check their associations' memory work for freedom from fear as a reason for staying.
- **Yenish families:** check whether freedom of movement appears in their own few published accounts; sources are scarce.
- **Vienna's working-class districts:** check Naturfreunde and union archives on freedom from want and open land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1848, the confessional exiles and commemorations; mixed for present-day attitudes; thin for minority and Yenish voices on freedom.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the motto; Kurrent for exile letters; Slovene for Carinthian memory; Hungarian for the 1989 border story.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the plebiscite memory in Carinthia stays contested; camp survivors' testimony. Anything legal (the recognition of religious societies, minority-language rights, rights of way) is routed to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit), not traced here.
- **Held for review:** liberation teachings from South Asian traditions taught in the region, marked by category and not pursued.
