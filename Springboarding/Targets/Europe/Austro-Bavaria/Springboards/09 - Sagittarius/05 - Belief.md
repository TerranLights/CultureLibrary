*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and Bavarian German; Old High German; Upper Palatinate (Northern Bavarian) and Salzburg dialect forms for folk belief; Latin for the catechism; Carinthian Slovene, Hungarian, Burgenland Croatian, Romani; Turkish. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Catholics and Roman Catholics in Munich and Vienna:** check how each states the authority behind belief, in their own catechisms.
- **Alevis and Sunni Muslims of Turkish heritage:** check each body's own creed statements and the disputes between Alevi bodies; never infer one from the other.
- **The Upper Palatinate, Salzburg's Pongau and the Bavarian Forest:** check whether folk beliefs recorded in the 19th century survive, are revived for tourism, or are gone.
- **Lutheran and Reformed Burgenland Hungarians:** check Reformed catechism use and village belief recordings.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses:** check how members explain their beliefs to neighbors, from their own literature and from leavers' memoirs, read side by side.
- **The non-religious in Vienna and Munich:** check humanist publications for what unbelievers say they do hold true.
- **Burgenland Roma and Sinti:** check, only through their own organizations and published speakers, what they choose to share about belief; sources are scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for catechisms, folk-belief collections and surveys; mixed for Muslim and Orthodox belief in their own terms; thin for Romani belief, which should not be filled from outsider folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Old High German for *Muspilli*; Latin for Canisius; Kurrent for Schönwerth's papers; Slovene and Hungarian for village records; Turkish for Alevi texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outsider "superstition" collections often mocked minorities; Alevi identity is contested between bodies; leavers' accounts of the Witnesses.
- **Held for review:** belief systems of South Asian origin found among the region's believers, recorded only as a category.
