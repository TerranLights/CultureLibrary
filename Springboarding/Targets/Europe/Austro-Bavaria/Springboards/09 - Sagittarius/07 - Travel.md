# Austro-Bavaria: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and Bavarian German; Middle High German; Tyrolean and Vorarlberg Alemannic forms; Yenish; Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani, Czech; Turkish, Bosnian and Serbian. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Burgenland's emigrant villages, including Croatian-speaking ones:** check letters home and return visits for who went to America and why some came back.
- **Tyroleans and Vorarlbergers whose ancestors were Swabian children:** check the memoir literature on child labor journeys to Bavarian Swabia and Württemberg.
- **Yenish families of Tyrol and Bavaria:** check travel and settlement in their own organizations' accounts; sources are scarce and outsider records need critical reading.
- **Lovara and Burgenland Roma:** check how their own associations tell their families' journeys and settlement; do not draw on outsider travel lore.
- **Turkish-, Bosnian- and Serbian-Austrians and Bavarians:** check how the second and third generations describe the summer trip "down there".
- **Pilgrims from Upper Franconia and the Upper Palatinate against Vienna's Mariazell walkers:** check routes, confraternity books and who walks today.
- **Residents of Hallstatt, the Zillertal and Berchtesgaden:** check host attitudes to tourists in local press and surveys, by village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tourism, pilgrimage and emigration history; mixed for guest-worker homeward travel; thin for Yenish and Romani travel in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for the epic; Kurrent for journeymen's books and emigrants' letters; Croatian and Hungarian for Burgenland emigrant letters; Turkish and Bosnian for family travel accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Yenish and Romani travel was policed and persecuted; refugee journeys involve trauma.
- **Held for review:** none; the journeys, pilgrimages and migrations named here all stay as pointers.
