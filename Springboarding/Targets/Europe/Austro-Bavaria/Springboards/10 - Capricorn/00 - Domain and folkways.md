## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of the nobility after 1919:** check family archives for how lost or kept titles changed standing in Austria against Bavaria.
- ***Beamte* in Vienna and Munich ministries:** check service rules, title use and memoirs for how office confers rank.
- **Vorarlberg:** check municipal assembly records and the Land's own historiography of self-government against Vienna.
- **Burgenland, once Esterházy estate land in Hungary:** check estate records and Croatian and Hungarian village histories for authority under magnate lordship.
- **Lutheran Middle and Upper Franconia:** check parish and town records for lines of authority outside the Catholic hierarchy.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check their own press for who they recognized as leaders when state authority was not on their side.
- **Turkish- and Bosnian-Austrians and -Bavarians:** check association minutes and family studies, in their languages, for the age and rank ways each community describes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court ranks, bureaucracy and party power; mixed for village authority; thin for Roma, Sinti and Yenish forms of leadership in their own words. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the pre-war lands carried are Germany and Austria, read here as Bavaria and Austria.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the twelfth- and fourteenth-century privileges; Kurrent for the rank schematisms before 1900; Hungarian for Esterházy estate papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** party and civil-service files are personal data; Nazi-era careers in both administrations.
- **Held for review:** none; no rank, title or time-use pointer for this domain had to be set aside under the standing rules.
