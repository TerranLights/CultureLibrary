## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Innviertel and the Mühlviertel:** check parish chronicles and local memory of priests, teachers and gendarmes for whether one office led in village authority or several shared it.
- **Clergy and religious orders:** check abbey archives and order statutes for how obedience inside a house differs from a parish priest's authority over laypeople.
- **Conscripts and *Zivildiener*:** check service-era memoirs and youth surveys for how each group describes the authority it serves under.
- **Vienna's working-class districts against the Tyrolean valleys:** check vote returns and police-complaint statistics before reading either as a type.
- **Lovara and Burgenland Roma:** check their own associations' publications for who carries authority within families and before outsiders; sources are few and mostly oral.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Turkish-Bavarians:** check how imams appointed from abroad and locally trained imams are described by their own congregations.
- **East Tyrol:** check whether its separation from North Tyrol after 1919 shows up in how distant authority is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, police and survey sources; mixed for household authority before 1950; thin for Romani and Yenish accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the Melk customaries; Early New High German for customals; Turkish for congregation newsletters that discuss who may lead prayer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** police and gendarmerie files under privacy rules; abuse of authority in church and state homes; crisis-era protest data. Anything scored from the law in force (the permission share) belongs to Libra's letter-of-the-law work.
- **Held for review:** none; the obedience, clergy and policing pointers chosen here stay within the standing rules.
