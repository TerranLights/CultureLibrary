*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol against Lower Austria:** check estate records and farm histories, since one had peasant representation and the other large lordships.
- **Rural servants and cottagers of the Mühlviertel and Lower Bavaria:** check life-story collections for how farmhands and maids describe their place at the table.
- **Viennese Czech and Slovak heritage:** check maids' and craftsmen's records for movement up the city's ranks over two generations.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check census categories and school records for the effect of the *Windische* label on standing.
- **Nobility's descendants after 1919:** check associations and family archives for which marks of rank were kept in Bavaria and dropped in Austria.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check their own organizations' accounts of being placed at the bottom of rural and urban orders, and of their own leadership roles.
- **Turkish- and BCS-speaking workers in Linz, Steyr and Ingolstadt plants:** check works-council records for how rank and origin overlapped on the shop floor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for estates, nobility, servants and urban inequality; mixed for church precedence today; thin for Romani internal rank, which outsiders should not presume.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the *Lex Baiuvariorum*; Czech for Viennese maids' letters; Hungarian for Burgenland estate-servant records (*cselédek*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic categories in census history; the *Windische* debate; wealth data on named families.
