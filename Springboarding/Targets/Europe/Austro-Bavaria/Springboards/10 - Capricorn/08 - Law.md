# Austro-Bavaria: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese; Swabian (Memmingen); Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Romani, Czech; Turkish and BCS. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law; the letter (codes, statutes, rulings as written) is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bavarian Swabia (Memmingen) and the Allgäu:** check town archives and peasant-war memory for how a higher-law tradition is remembered locally.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check their press on Article 7 of the 1955 State Treaty for how a minority describes law kept on paper and not in practice.
- **Lovara and other Roma:** check their own organizations and sound ethnographies before describing any internal council; it is not to be presumed.
- **Rural districts of the Waldviertel and the Upper Palatinate:** check local press and court statistics for how far quarrels go to court and how far they are settled at the inn or the town hall.
- **Clergy and religious orders:** check sanctuary cases and order statements for how conscience is weighed against state law.
- **Turkish-, Bosnian- and Chechen-Austrians:** check police-contact studies, in their languages, to separate experiences rather than merge them.
- **Lutheran Franconia:** check Protestant church teaching and practice on obedience to rulers against Catholic Old Bavaria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal theory, trust surveys and famous cases; mixed for neighborly settling; thin for Romani and Yenish ways of settling disputes, which may be private.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German for the Twelve Articles; Chechen and Turkish for police-contact studies; Romani only with community consent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** psychiatric and prison files (Mollath, Unterweger); asylum cases; a group's own dispute customs, which may be restricted. Anything on codes, statutes or rulings as written goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; legal-culture pointers stay here and the letter-of-the-law ones are routed to Libra rather than held.
