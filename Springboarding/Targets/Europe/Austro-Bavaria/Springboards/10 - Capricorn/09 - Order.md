# Austro-Bavaria: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian and Viennese; Vorarlberg Alemannic, Swabian; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Czech, Romani; Turkish and BCS; Latin. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Bavaria's commuter villages:** check press and court records on cowbells, church bells and roosters for how old residents and newcomers each describe rural order.
- **Vienna's *Gemeindebau* residents and Munich's housing cooperatives:** check house rules and tenant surveys for who sets order in shared buildings.
- **People who beg in Austrian cities and those who speak for them:** check Roma organizations', homeless-aid and social workers' reports beside municipal ones, and record who is affected rather than assume it; sources in Romani are scarce.
- **Vorarlberg and Tyrol carnival guilds:** check guild statutes and minutes for how disorder is organized and kept within bounds.
- **Long-settled and newer tenants of Vienna's outer districts, Turkish- and BCS-speaking families among them:** check tenant and neighborhood studies for how shared-courtyard rules are set and argued over, and how each household tells it, rather than tying disputes to any one group.
- **Allotment gardeners and *Schrebergarten* clubs of Linz, Vienna and Nuremberg:** check club minutes for the rules they enforce on one another.
- **The Bohemian Forest and Mühlviertel border:** check Stifter-related local memory for how a literary idea of quiet order is used in regional identity.
