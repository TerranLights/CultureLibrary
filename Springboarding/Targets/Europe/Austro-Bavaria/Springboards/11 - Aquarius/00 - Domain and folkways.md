## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Bavaria and Lutheran Franconia:** check parish and association histories for how church membership and club life overlap in Catholic and Protestant places.
- **Vorarlberg and Vienna:** check association registers and municipal histories for the contrast between small Alemannic municipalities and a capital of large housing estates.
- **Burgenland Croats:** check their cultural association (*Hrvatsko kulturno društvo*) and village tamburica groups for how community is organized in a minority language.
- **Viennese Czech heritage:** check the records of the Czech *spolky*, schools and the Sokol in Vienna for associations that kept a community together over generations.
- **Danube Swabians in Upper Austria and Bavaria:** check homeland societies and their yearbooks for community rebuilt after expulsion.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Turkish Bavarians:** check mosque associations and *hemşehri* associations, in Turkish, for how hometown ties shape groups.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check their own associations (Oberwart, Vienna, Munich) for how they describe community, not outsiders' accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations, volunteering and ball culture; mixed for hopes and ideals, which are scattered across surveys and literature; thin for Yenish and Romani community life in their own words. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the pre-war lands carried are Germany and Austria, read here as Bavaria and Austria.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old High German name forms for the Salzburg confraternity book; Czech for Viennese *spolek* minutes; Turkish for hometown-association newsletters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugee reception records hold personal data; Nazi-era dissolution and "coordination" of associations.
- **Held for review:** none; the association, ball and refugee pointers here needed no setting aside.
