# Austro-Bavaria: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Viennese, Tyrolean, Upper Palatinate dialect; Carinthian Slovene, Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian; BCS (Bosnian), Hebrew and Yiddish for Jewish community records; Kurrent for older parish books. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Waldviertel and the Upper Palatinate:** check local chronicles and planning data for how villages near the old Iron Curtain describe holding together as people leave.
- **East Tyrol:** check commons records and parish books for an Alpine valley's ways of sharing work and land.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check the federations' archives and the Slovene weekly press for how village and national community overlap.
- **Sudeten German descendants:** check expellee-town museums and homeland-society newsletters for a community made of origin rather than place.
- **Jewish communities in Vienna, Munich and Salzburg:** check the communities' own publications and oral-history projects for membership, survival and renewal.
- **Bosnian-Austrians and -Bavarians:** check *džemat* newsletters and association records, in Bosnian, for congregation and neighborhood ties after the 1990s arrivals.
- **Upper Austria's and Carinthia's Protestant valleys:** check congregational histories for community held by a minority faith among Catholic neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for parishes, villages and Jewish communities; mixed for the expellee towns, whose records sit in many small museums; thin for loneliness by region before the 2020s.
- **Languages needed:** Kurrent and early New High German for village customals; Bosnian for *džemat* records; Hebrew and Yiddish for older Kultusgemeinde registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** membership rolls of religious communities; testimony of former Friedrichshof members, including children; Nazi-era destruction of Jewish communities.
- **Held for review:** none; the village, parish and commons records here needed no setting aside.
