# Austro-Bavaria: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Viennese, Swabian; Romani (Burgenland Roman, Lovara, Sinti varieties); BCS (Serbian and Croatian); Burgenland Croatian, Carinthian Slovene; early New High German for the peasants' war. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ. Anything legal (equal treatment in statutes and courts) goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bavarian Swabia:** check how Memmingen and its villages remember the Twelve Articles, beside Old Bavarian memory of the same war.
- **Serbian- and Croatian-Austrians:** check workers' associations, club papers and family oral histories for how equal treatment at work was sought and described.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check their own organizations and publications in Burgenland, Vienna and Bavaria for equality claims, kept apart from outsiders' accounts.
- **Yenish (Tyrol, Vorarlberg, Bavaria):** sources are scarce; check folk-life archives and any Yenish association's own publications, and say where little exists rather than borrowing material from neighboring countries.
- **People with disabilities:** check self-advocacy groups' newsletters for their terms of equal standing, beside charities' terms.
- **LGBTQ communities in Vienna, Munich and Graz:** check the archives above and community magazines for how arguments shifted across decades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for class, gender and pay; mixed for migrants' own accounts before the 1990s; thin for Yenish voices.
- **Languages needed:** BCS for guest workers' club papers; Romani varieties for *dROMa* and community recordings; early New High German for the 1525 articles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs appear in sources and must be quoted only as needed; Nazi-era persecution of Roma, Sinti, Yenish, disabled and gay people; personal data in migration oral histories.
- **Held for review:** none; the pay, address and movement sources here needed no setting aside.
