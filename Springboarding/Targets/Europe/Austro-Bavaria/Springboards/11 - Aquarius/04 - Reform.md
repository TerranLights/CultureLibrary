# Austro-Bavaria: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Viennese, Bavarian, Upper Austrian (Mühlviertel) dialect; Carinthian Slovene; Czech for the cross-border protests; Latin and early New High German for church and monastic records. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer. Legal reform as written law goes to Libra; what law is for goes to Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Styria:** check Protestant congregations' histories and the State Archive for how the Counter-Reformation is remembered by those whose families were forced to choose.
- **The Mühlviertel:** check local campaign archives and press for how anti-Temelín activism drew farmers, priests and teachers together.
- **The Upper Palatinate:** check local memory of the Wackersdorf protests beside official accounts, as a second Bavarian case of reform from below.
- **Youth of the 1968 and Hainburg generations:** check memoirs and anniversary interviews for how former activists judge what they changed.
- **Carinthian Slovenes:** check cooperative and federation records for reform led inside a minority, in Slovene.
- **Jewish communities in Vienna:** check their own press of the Lueger years for how a reforming city government looked from the targeted side.
- **People with disabilities:** check self-advocacy writing on Red Vienna's welfare and the later killings at Am Spiegelgrund, for reform seen by those it classified.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state reforms and the 1980s protests; mixed for village views of secularization; thin for disabled people's own records before 1945.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Tegernsee's last chronicles; Czech for the Temelín side of the blockades; Slovene for the *posojilnice*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eugenics and the Nazi-era killings of disabled children; antisemitism in Lueger's movement; living activists named in police files.
- **Held for review:** none; the secularization, cooperative and protest sources here needed no setting aside.
