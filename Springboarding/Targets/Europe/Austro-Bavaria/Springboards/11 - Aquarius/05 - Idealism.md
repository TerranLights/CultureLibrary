# Austro-Bavaria: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Bavarian, Viennese, Tyrolean; Hebrew and Yiddish for Zionist and Jewish socialist sources; Bosnian and Turkish for present-day youth movements; early New High German for Anabaptist chronicles. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol:** check how Hutterite origins are remembered in Tyrolean towns today; any Hutterite colonies abroad serve only as comparison.
- **Jewish communities in Vienna:** check Zionist, socialist and religious papers of 1900–1938 for rival ideals of a better life, each in its own press.
- **Upper Bavaria:** check King Ludwig societies and local museums for why devotion to an idealized king lasts, and who mocks it.
- **Schwabing's bohemia and the Monte Verità circle:** check their letters and memoirs for how ideals of free love, diet and work held up in practice.
- **Styria:** check Graz archives and present-day communities for a line from Otto Gross's ideas to today's experiments, or its absence.
- **Bosnian- and Turkish-Austrian youth:** check youth associations' own publications for the ideals they hold up, and which ones are contested within those communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for utopian writing and political movements; mixed for present-day communities; thin for ordinary people's ideals outside surveys.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Yiddish for Jewish movement papers; early New High German for the Hutterite chronicle; Turkish for the publications of youth and student associations across the political range.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Coudenhove-Kalergi's name is misused in conspiracy theories; Otto Gross's psychiatric files; youth-group membership is personal data.
- **Held for review:** none; the utopian, life-reform and movement sources here needed no setting aside.
