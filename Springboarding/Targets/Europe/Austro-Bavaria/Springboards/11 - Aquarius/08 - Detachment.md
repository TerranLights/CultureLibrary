# Austro-Bavaria: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian Standard German and the German of Bavaria; Viennese and Bavarian dialects; Carinthian Slovene (for Handke's Carinthian setting), Czech (for Viennese medical biographies), Latin (monastic rules and chronicles). **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monastic and eremitic communities (Benedictines of Melk and Admont, Carthusian heritage, the Saalfelden hermits):** check their own rules and chronicles for how silence and distance are prescribed and limited.
- **Physicians, nurses and rescuers in Vienna, Munich and Innsbruck:** check professional memoirs and training materials for how detachment is taught and what it costs.
- **Journalists of ORF and Bayerischer Rundfunk:** check their own editorial guidelines and memoirs for the ideal of distance from parties and causes.
- **People who live alone, urban and rural:** check household and loneliness data together, so that living alone and being lonely are not taken as the same thing.
- **High-pasture workers and herders:** check their life stories for solitude as a job, not as a temperament.
- **Writers of Carinthia and Styria:** check how critics in each Land read distanced prose, and whether the readings differ.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medicine, science and literature; mixed for everyday reserve, which is mostly anecdote and outsiders' claims; thin for monastic solitude after the Josephine closures.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the Carthusian statutes; Czech for Škoda's early life and letters; Slovene for Carinthian readings of Handke.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide in Handke's account and in loneliness research; living hermits' privacy; rescuers' trauma.
- **Held for review:** none; the hermitage, clinic and survey sources here needed no setting aside.
