*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jewish communities:** check survivors' memoirs and community oral history for how hope is told, and where tellers refuse the word.
- **Burgenland Croats and Hungarians:** check petition books and parish pilgrimages for hopes written in their own languages.
- **Danube Swabians:** check homeland-society yearbooks for how hopes of return turned into hopes for a new home.
- **Turkish-, Bosnian- and Serbian-Austrians and -Bavarians:** check family oral histories, in their languages, for the plans of the first arrivals and how their children describe them.
- **Ukrainian community:** check its own media since 2022 for hopes of return set against plans to stay.
- **Roma and Sinti:** check their associations' publications for hopes voiced for children and schooling, in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious hope, youth surveys and crisis lines; mixed for children's letters; thin for petition books, which sit in parishes and are rarely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for the Melk song; Ukrainian for community media since 2022; Croatian and Hungarian for shrine petitions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** petition books and crisis-line data are private; suicide statistics; survivors' testimony.
- **Held for review:** none; the shrine, survey and crisis-line sources here needed no setting aside.
