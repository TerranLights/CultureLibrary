*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lutheran Franconia and Catholic Old Bavaria:** check deaconess and parish charity records against those of the nursing orders to see where mercy was taught and who did the work.
- **Styria:** check Graz city-council debates and VinziWerke reports for how a regional capital argued over beggars.
- **Bosnian-Austrians and Serbian- and Croatian-Austrians:** check their own associations and memoirs for how they recall being received in the 1990s and how they later organized help themselves.
- **Ukrainian and Polish communities:** check parish and community bulletins in Ukrainian and Polish on hosting and being hosted since 2022.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Turkish Bavarians:** check mosque associations' Ramadan and disaster appeals (the 2023 earthquake) in Turkish.
- **The non-religious:** check secular welfare bodies and giving surveys for how people without church ties explain why they help.
- **Hospice and palliative workers:** check their training material and memoirs for the limits of compassion over long careers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundations, giving reports and charity history; mixed for everyday pity and its tone; thin for compassion as Roma and Yenish describe receiving or withholding it.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and early New High German for hospital charters; Ukrainian for host-family accounts after 2022.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recipients' files in charity archives name people in need; appeals that show suffering children raise consent questions.
- **Held for review:** compassion teachings of South Asian-origin traditions taught in the nation's cities, set aside as a category.
