## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Franconia and the Upper Palatinate:** check border-village chronicles on the inner German and Czech borders for how a closed line shaped daily life.
- **Waldviertel, Weinviertel and Burgenland:** check local memory projects on the Iron Curtain years for what the line meant on the Austrian side.
- **Salzburg and the Rupertiwinkel:** check cross-border commuting studies for how a state line runs through one labor market.
- **Burgenland Hungarians:** check Hungarian-language village sources on *határ* to see whether village lands and state borders share one word in use.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Turkish Bavarians:** check family studies in Turkish on *mahrem* for who may enter which rooms.
- **Jewish communities of Vienna and Munich:** check community publications for Sabbath and dietary boundaries in practice.
- **Carmelite and other enclosed communities:** check their chronicles for how enclosure is kept and opened.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for border history and abuse reports; mixed for everyday privacy and touch; thin for neighbor quarrels outside press anecdotes.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian and Czech for border-village sources; Turkish for family studies; Hebrew terms for Jewish practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse reports name victims only in part; ritual-slaughter debates can be used against religious minorities.
