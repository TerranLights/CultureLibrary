## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyrol and the Bavarian Alps:** check mountain-rescue and guide training for how gut feeling is taught or distrusted among those who must decide fast.
- **Mühlviertel and Lower Bavaria:** check folk-medicine archives for how healers describe sensing, and how doctors in the same districts speak of them.
- **Bavarian Forest and the Upper Palatinate:** check publishers and local papers for how prophecy books are produced and read, without assuming who takes them seriously.
- **Burgenland Hungarians and Carinthian Slovenes:** check minority-language folklore archives for words for foreboding that German sources miss.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Bosnian-Austrians:** check community media and imams' guidance for how dreams and hunches are weighed against religious teaching.
- **Hospital nurses and paramedics:** check their journals and union reports for how their hunches are recorded and heard.
- **The non-religious and skeptics:** check skeptics' groups for how they argue about intuition, separate from paranormal belief.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk calendars, seers and decision research; mixed for professional intuition; thin for minority-language premonition lore.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian and Slovene for folklore archives; Turkish for dream-reading media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some prophecy literature carries apocalyptic or political messages; folk healers may not wish to be named.
- **Held for review:** intuition and "inner sight" teachings from South Asian-origin traditions sold in courses, set aside as a category.
