# Austro-Bavaria: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Austro-Bavaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Austrian and Bavarian German; Bavarian (Boarisch), Viennese; Burgenland Croatian, Hungarian, Carinthian Slovene, Czech; Romani (Sinti and Burgenland Roma); Turkish and Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian (BCS); Latin and Early New High German for penance-cross inscriptions and chronicles. **Concept in one line:** what the culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law is not researched here: the letter of the law is in Libra, its spirit in Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Palatinate and Franconia:** check penance-cross inventories and legend books for how villages tell old killings and atonement.
- **Waldviertel:** check local museums and tourist material for how an outlaw is remembered and sold.
- **Sinti and Roma:** check their own associations' documentation for how they record criminalization by authorities, and how they wish it described.
- **Turkish-Austrians and Bosnian-Austrians:** check Turkish- and BCS-language media for how crime reporting names a suspect's origin and how readers respond.
- **Danube Swabians and Sudeten German descendants:** check their memoirs, beside Czech and Hungarian accounts, for crimes suffered in 1944-1946.
- **Jewish communities of Vienna and Munich:** check community documentation of antisemitic incidents as wrongs, in their own reporting.
- **Victims' families and people leaving prison:** check support groups for how each side speaks of guilt, shame and return.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime fiction, museums and crime statistics; mixed for public ideas of sin and shame; thin for wrongdoers' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Early New High German for penance-cross records; Romani and Turkish for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims' privacy (Kampusch and similar cases); police statistics can be misread against minorities, so they are used only as recorded counts.
- **Held for review:** writings on sin and moral law from South Asian religious traditions, set aside as a category.
