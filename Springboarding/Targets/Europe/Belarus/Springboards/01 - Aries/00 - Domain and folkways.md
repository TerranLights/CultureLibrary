# Belarus: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of the Hrodna area, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script (*kitab*), Romani. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself (self, body, appearance, beginnings, initiative), with naming as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against the regional towns and villages:** check whether urban self-presentation (dress, names, online bios) departs from the village record.
- **Hrodna region and the Polish border:** Catholic name days against Orthodox saints' days; Polish- and *prosta mowa*-speaking households.
- **Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** costume, body rites and belief vocabulary in the West Polesian speech zone; the Polesian record is distinct enough to need its own unit.
- **Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley; the Braslaŭ Lake District:** Old Believer and Catholic communities side by side; check naming and dress for each.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper; Homiel and the Chernobyl-affected south:** records of the resettled and their lost villages test whether the self is located in the land.
- **Navahrudak:** gentry, Lipka Tatar and former Jewish communities in one district; useful to test how far confession sets naming.
- **Greek Catholics (Uniates), Protestants, Roma, Lithuanians around Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south:** check each group's naming and dress record separately.
- **Litvak Jews (historical):** the shtetl record of naming and *yikhes* needs Yiddish sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief, costume and naming; mixed for present-day self-presentation; thin for Roma and the post-Chernobyl resettled.
- **Lands:** no change to the pre-war lands is recorded and no borders are settled yet; these sheets are written for Belarus as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** both Belarusian spellings (the spelling marks a source's stance), Russian for state records, Polish for interwar western material, Yiddish for the Litvak record, Arabic script for *kitabs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** since 2020 several independent outlets and researchers are labeled "extremist" in Belarus; contacting them from inside the country can put people at risk. Treat ethnic self-identification data with care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the National Library of Belarus catalog for "антрапанімія" and "асабовыя імёны" and pull Biryla's volumes.
2. Read the *Bielaruskaja mifalohija* entries for "душа", "доля" and "цень", noting the regions each entry cites.
3. Download the 2019 census tables on nationality and language from Belstat and compare the questions with the 2009 form.
4. Query the National Historical Archives' finding aids for Catholic, Orthodox and Jewish metrical books of the Navahrudak district.
5. Search "народны строй" plus each district name (Слуцк, Пінск, Навагрудак) for costume studies.
