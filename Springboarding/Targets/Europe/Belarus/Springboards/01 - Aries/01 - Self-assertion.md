# Belarus: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Romani. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk office and IT workers against village and small-town workplaces:** check how salary talk, complaints and saying no to a superior differ.
- **Women and men:** check what each is told about raising the voice, at home and in public meetings.
- **Hrodna-region Poles and *prosta mowa* speakers:** compare the minority press with local Belarusian sources on requests to officials.
- **Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** look at the speech-community record for teasing songs and disputes between neighbors.
- **Old Believers (Vietka in the Homiel region; villages of the Viciebsk lakes around Braslaŭ):** check how their own records describe dealings with officials and priests of other churches.
- **Homiel south, resettled villages:** check letters and petitions about compensation as a test of asking for what one is owed.
- **Litvak communities (historical):** *kahal* minutes and the Yiddish press as the record of who spoke in communal affairs.
- **Roma, Lipka Tatars, Greek Catholics, Protestants:** small groups whose dealings with authorities are best read in their own newsletters and parish records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literary and folk sources; mixed for present-day workplace and consumer behavior; thin and politically constrained for open dissent after 2020.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian and Russian side by side (the choice between them is itself part of the concept), Polish for Hrodna minority media, Yiddish for communal minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** protest-era voice is a live legal risk; never identify individual speakers from 2020-2021 material. Complaint and voice in law (appeals procedures) go to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
