## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna region:** check how confession, language at home and census answers line up or diverge among Catholics, including Poles and *prosta mowa* speakers.
- **Brest region and Polesia:** *paliashuki* and earlier "local" answers; compare district by district.
- **Ukrainians of the south (Brest and Homiel border districts):** check the language-at-home and nationality questions separately.
- **Viciebsk and the Dvina; Braslaŭ Lake District:** Old Believers, Catholics and the Latvian border; how each group names itself.
- **Mahilioŭ and Homiel; resettled after Chernobyl:** whether the lost village replaces the region as the answer to "where are you from?".
- **Minsk:** Russian-speaking city dwellers against the regions; check how language and self-label interact.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars; Astraviec and the Lithuanians of the border:** small groups with long records.
- **Russians, Greek Catholics, Protestants, Roma; the historical Litvak and shtetl communities:** each to be scouted in its own sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history and literature; mixed for surveys (independent polling inside the country has been curtailed; online samples skew urban).
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian in both spellings (which spelling a source chooses is itself an identity datum), Russian, Polish for interwar and minority sources, Yiddish, Arabic script for *kitabs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national identity is politically charged; some symbols and organizations named in identity debates are restricted inside the country. The Belarusian diaspora's identity writing is comparison only. Neighbors' national histories are not sources for this sheet.
- **Held for review:** none.
