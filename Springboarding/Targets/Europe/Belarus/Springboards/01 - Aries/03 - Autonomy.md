## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against the agro-towns and collective-farm villages:** check how much daily life is set by an employer or a farm administration.
- **Brest region and Polesia:** the petty gentry's villages (*zaścianki*) and the Pinsk marshes; separate them from neighboring peasant villages.
- **Homiel and the Chernobyl south:** the evacuated, the resettled and those who returned; each made a different choice about where to live.
- **Hrodna region and the Polish border:** Catholic parish life and Polish schooling as fields of community self-organization.
- **Viciebsk, the Dvina valley and the Braslaŭ Lake District:** check which Old Believer congregations are priestless and how their own records name their leaders.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper:** town records and brotherhood archives with long runs.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars; Lithuanians around Astraviec; Greek Catholics; Protestants:** each a candidate for community self-government records.
- **Litvak communities (historical):** the *kahal* and land-council record.
- **Young adults, women, elders:** check age and gender in the household surveys before assuming one family pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for early modern town, church and Jewish communal records; mixed for present-day household data; thin for Roma and Old Believers.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian (Old Belarusian), Latin and Polish for early modern town books; Hebrew and Yiddish for the *pinkas*; Russian for Soviet and present-day records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collective autonomy questions (regional self-rule) touch politics; personal autonomy in medicine and reproduction is sensitive. Legal rules on self-government and consent go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
