## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against the regions:** check whether the city's start-of-year and first-day rites (New Year tree, 1 September) push out village calendars.
- **Polesia and the Brest region:** spring-calling songs and first-sowing rites; compare Pahost with West Polesian villages.
- **Viciebsk region and the Dvina; Braslaŭ Lake District:** lake-origin legends and Catholic against Orthodox start-of-year customs (two Christmas dates).
- **Hrodna region:** Polish-language parish customs at Christmas and christenings; compare with Belarusian-speaking neighbors.
- **Homiel and the Chernobyl south; resettled villagers:** the main group whose "new start" is recent and documented.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper:** harvest-start rites in eastern villages and their collectors' notes.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars; Old Believers; Greek Catholics; Protestants:** check each group's own calendar and its birth or conversion rites (for example adult baptism in Protestant congregations).
- **Litvak communities (historical):** the Jewish calendar's New Year and the yizkor founding stories.
- **Roma and Lithuanians of the border:** check their own new-year and birth customs before assuming the majority's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for calendar rites and legends; mixed for present-day fresh starts; thin for Roma and for personal stories of starting over after failure.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian and Polish for 19th-century collections, Old Belarusian and Church Slavonic for the saint's life and chronicles, Yiddish and Hebrew for yizkor books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** competing national founding dates are politically charged; 25 March observances are restricted inside the country. Treat both dates as data to compare, not as a ruling.
- **Held for review:** none.
