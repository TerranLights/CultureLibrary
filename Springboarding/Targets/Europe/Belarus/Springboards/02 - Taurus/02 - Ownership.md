# Belarus: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (both spellings), Russian of Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish, Lipka Tatar Arabic-script Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on. The letter of property law belongs to Libra; this sheet takes ownership as lived and spoken.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Belarus (Hrodna, Brest, Navahrudak) against the east:** collectivization came to the two sides at different dates (*check*); compare family memory of a farmstead on each side of the 1939 line.
- **Former *chutar* families against village-street families:** compare how families who held isolated farmsteads speak of land with those who lived in clustered villages.
- **Catholic, Orthodox and Greek Catholic parishes:** each lost and reclaimed buildings on its own timeline; check which side's account is on record.
- **Lipka Tatars:** service-based landholding in the Grand Duchy needs its own reading.
- **The Jewish *balebos* and the leaseholder (historical):** examine householder status and tenure rights in the shtetl apart from peasant holdings.
- **Chernobyl south (Homiel region):** land now outside use; check how former owners and new settlers describe a claim to it.
- **Flat-owners, dacha owners and renters in Minsk, Mahilioŭ and Viciebsk:** separate the urban forms of ownership.
- **Old Believers near the Dvina and the Sož; Roma; Lithuanians at Astraviec; Polish-speaking farmers:** look for each community's own records of how land was held or leased.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the land registry, collectivization and fiscal data; mixed for attitudes to private property; thin for Roma and Lipka Tatar holdings after 1945.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian and Polish for early survey books, Polish for interwar land files, Russian for Soviet and present records, Yiddish for tenure inside Jewish communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collectivization and dekulakization files may be restricted; the restitution of Jewish and Polish property and of church buildings is contested; questions on private versus state property are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on holding or claiming property was set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the National Library catalog for "валочная памера" and "пісцовыя кнігі" and list surviving survey books for Brest and Hrodna.

3. Download the latest *Земельный фонд Республики Беларусь* report and tabulate land by owner type and region.
4. Pull Ministry of Finance consolidated-budget spending against GDP for the last ten years.
5. Search the press archive of *Zviazda* and *Naša Niva* for "чэкі Маёмасць".
