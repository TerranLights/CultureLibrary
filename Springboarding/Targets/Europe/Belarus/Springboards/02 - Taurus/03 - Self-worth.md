## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belarusian speakers, Russian speakers and *trasianka* speakers:** test whether language choice moves a speaker's standing differently in Minsk, district towns and villages.
- **Gentry-descended families in the Hrodna region:** examine worth claimed through descent against worth through work, in Polish and in Belarusian.
- **Collective-farm villages of the Mahilioŭ and Viciebsk regions:** compare the official honors given to farm workers with how villagers rank one another.
- **Evacuees from the Homiel south and the families who received them:** check how each side spoke of the other's worth.
- **Shtetl Jews of Pinsk, Mazyr and Slonim (historical):** separate standing by learning, wealth and lineage; Yiddish sources needed.
- **Lipka Tatar families of noble rank:** check the record for noble status kept by a Muslim minority over centuries.
- **Romani families in Homiel, Babrujsk and Viciebsk:** look for Roma-authored sources on respect and shame; outsiders' accounts need care.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina and Sož, Lithuanians at Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** note where worth is framed through faith, and read each group's own writings.
- **Men and women, the young and pensioners:** set provider, carer and retiree roles side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and official honors; mixed for psychology; thin for Roma and the shtetl's own voice outside memoirs.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for health and press data, Belarusian for literature and the women's press, Polish for the gentry record, Yiddish for *koved*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "dignity" is politically loaded since 2020; suicide data must be handled with care; slurs and ethnic nicknames should be described, not repeated.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on worth, honor or shame was withheld.
