## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Generations:** compare people schooled before 1991, in the 1990s, and under the ideology course, using the same survey items.
- **Orthodox, Catholic, Greek Catholic and Baptist or Pentecostal believers:** check whether each church's teaching on money, family and work matches what its members say.
- **The Hrodna-region Polish minority:** examine values carried through Polish-language parish life and schools.
- **Villages of the Pinsk and Stolin marshes:** test whether village moral talk (conscience, shame, what is proper) has its own words.
- **Minsk against district towns:** read whether "traditional values" means the same in the capital and the provinces.
- **Old Believers near Viciebsk and Vietka:** look at a community whose values are framed by old ritual and separation.
- **Jewish tradition of the Liadi and Valožyn heritage (historical) and today's small communities:** separate the historical record from present congregations.
- **Lipka Tatars, Roma, Lithuanians near Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south:** each needs its own sources, not borrowed ones.
- **Chernobyl-resettled families:** check whether forced loss shifted what people rank first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official statements and survey series; mixed for lived values; thin for Roma and the Polesian village record outside folklore.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for state ideology texts, Belarusian for literature and church texts, Polish for Catholic minority life, Yiddish and Hebrew for *Tanya* and the yeshiva record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "traditional values" is a political slogan; surveys inside Belarus face fear of honest answers; contact with independent outlets labeled "extremist" carries risk.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on values or their ranking was withheld.
