## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk and its suburbs against the rest:** check whether wealth gaps run mainly between the capital and the regions or within each city.
- **Salihorsk, Navapolack and other industrial towns:** compare company-town prosperity with farming districts nearby.
- **IT professionals, state-sector employees and private entrepreneurs:** separate three kinds of income and how each group speaks of the others.
- **Magnate heritage in Nieśviž, Mir and Ruzhany:** test how locals today relate to the wealth of former lords.
- **Polesian and Chernobyl-zone villages:** look for how want and sufficiency are described where incomes are lowest.
- **Jewish merchants and industrialists of Pinsk, Babrujsk and Viciebsk (historical):** read Yiddish and memoir sources for rank by wealth.
- **Polish-speaking families near Hrodna, Old Believer traders of the Dvina, Lipka Tatars:** check each one's record of trade and property.
- **Roma, Ukrainians of the south, Lithuanians around Astraviec:** sources on their economic life are few; look for community-authored accounts before outsiders' reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for magnate history and state statistics; mixed for present-day fortunes; thin for what ordinary people count as being rich today.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for business press and statistics, Polish and Latin for magnate archives, Yiddish for shtetl rank, Belarusian for the wishing songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wealth of officials and state-linked businessmen is a sensitive subject; some business outlets have been closed or labeled "extremist"; personal wealth figures are often unverifiable.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer about riches or poverty was put aside.
