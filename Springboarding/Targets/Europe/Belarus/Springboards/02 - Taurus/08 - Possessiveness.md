## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural and urban couples:** test whether talk of jealousy and control differs between Minsk and district villages, and between generations.
- **Three-generation households in the Mahilioŭ and Homiel regions:** examine rivalry between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law where families live together.
- **Polesian villages of the Brest region:** check courtship guarding and dance-night fights in the West Polesian record.
- **Catholic families near Hrodna and Orthodox families in the east:** compare church marriage teaching on jealousy and on marrying out.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina valley and the Lipka Tatars of Navahrudak:** look at how each handles marriage outside the community and the guarding of members.
- **Shtetl Jews (historical):** read Yiddish songs and memoirs for matchmaking and the guarding of daughters.
- **Roma families, Lithuanians at Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south:** each needs its own sources; avoid assumptions borrowed from outside reports.
- **Children in kindergartens and schools:** compare what curricula teach about sharing with what teachers report.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk song and charms; mixed for present-day jealousy and control; thin for minority communities.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian for ballads and charms, Russian for surveys, press and NGO material, Yiddish for the Jewish song record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence and coercion are sensitive; several women's NGOs have been closed since 2021, so some archives may be offline; heritage disputes with Lithuania are charged.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on jealousy, control or turf was held back.
