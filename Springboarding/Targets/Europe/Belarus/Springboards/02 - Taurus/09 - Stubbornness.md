## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholics (Uniates) and their descendants in the north and west:** examine how memory of the 1839 abolition is told today.
- **Old Believers of the Viciebsk region and Vietka:** compare their account of keeping the old rite with outsiders' descriptions.
- **Belarusian-language writers and *Taraškievica* users against Russian-speaking majorities:** test whether firmness on spelling or language is praised or mocked in each circle.
- **Factory towns (Minsk, Orša, Salihorsk) and their strike histories:** separate who held out and who yielded, by plant.
- **Shklow, Mahilioŭ and Viciebsk Jewish communities (historical):** read both Hasidic and opposing sources.
- **Lipka Tatar congregations of Iŭje, Navahrudak and Slonim:** check how keeping Islam through centuries of language change is described in their own writings.
- **Polish-speaking Catholics near Hrodna and Lithuanian speakers at Astraviec:** look for school and parish disputes as tests of holding firm.
- **Polesian and Chernobyl-south villages, Roma families, Protestant congregations:** each needs its own record of refusal and compromise.
- **Older and younger generations:** compare who is called "set in their ways".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church and language history; mixed for everyday obstinacy; thin for Roma and for present-day village feuds.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian, Polish and Latin for the polemics, Hebrew and Yiddish for the Jewish conflict, Belarusian in both spellings for the language dispute, Russian for strike records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent protests, strikes and vigils are politically dangerous to research inside Belarus; avoid framing a whole people as stubborn or as yielding.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on refusal or firmness was kept back.
