## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The dialect and ethnographic zones (Paasiečča in the north-east, Central Belarus, Paniamonne, Padniaproŭje, Polesia):** check whether greetings, address forms and riddle stock shift at dialect borders.
- **Minsk against the district towns:** compare home language, commuting and neighbor contact.
- **Hrodna region Catholics who speak *po prostu*, and the region's Poles:** separate "plain speech" from standard Polish when mapping who speaks what with whom.
- **Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** sample the West Polesian speech zone on its own.
- **Viciebsk region, the Dvina valley, the Braslaŭ lakes and their Old Believer villages:** check which language children learned at home and which at prayer.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper; Homiel and the resettled Chernobyl south:** trace neighbor networks and local speech after whole villages were moved.
- **Lithuanians near Astraviec and Ašmiany; Ukrainians of the south:** record border bilingualism village by village.
- **Lipka Tatars, Roma, Protestant congregations, the historical Litvak communities:** compare where and in which script each group's children learned to read.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialects, folklore and language statistics; mixed for neighbor life and travel; thin for Romani and present-day Lipka Tatar teaching.
- **Scope:** there is no `00 - Scope.md` and no change to the pre-war lands is recorded, so the Gemini sheets cover Belarus as it stands; no borders or units are settled here.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian in both spellings, Russian, Polish for the interwar west, Yiddish and Hebrew, Arabic script for the *kitabs*, and Belarusian Sign Language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language choice is politically charged; courtyard chats and the liquidated language society are labeled "extremist", so contact with people inside Belarus needs care.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on talk, neighbors or learning was set aside.
