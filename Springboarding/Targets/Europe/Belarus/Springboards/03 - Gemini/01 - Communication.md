# Belarus: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian-speaking Minsk and the Belarusian-speaking village:** check whether *vy* and *ty*, name-and-patronymic and kin terms for strangers follow language, age or place.
- **Hrodna region Catholics and Poles:** compare greetings and church formulas spoken in Polish, *po prostu* and Belarusian in the same parish.
- **Brest region Polesians:** record turn-taking and leave-taking in West Polesian villages separately from standard-language data.
- **Viciebsk region and the Braslaŭ lake villages:** check how Old Believer households addressed outsiders and clergy.
- **Mahilioŭ and Homiel; the Chernobyl resettled:** compare how evacuees describe what officials told them, and in what words.
- **Navahrudak and Iŭje Lipka Tatars:** trace where Arabic, Belarusian and Polish each enter speech at prayer and at home.
- **Roma:** check whether Romani is kept for in-group talk and which language is used with officials; sources are scarce, so record that.
- **Deaf Belarusians:** treat signed talk as its own channel, not as a deficit.
- **Litvak Jews in the historical record:** compare Yiddish letters home with Russian and Hebrew ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, phraseology and political speech; mixed for everyday conversation; thin for Romani and Tatar household talk.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian chancery script for the Metrica, Belarusian in both spellings, Russian for state speech, Polish for the Hrodna parishes, Yiddish for letter manuals, Arabic script for *chamaiły*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recording political talk is risky inside Belarus today; frank-speech studies should rely on archives and émigré interviews where possible.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on speech or letters was set aside.
