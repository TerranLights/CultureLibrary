## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against rural districts:** check whether tutoring, early reading and after-school clubs reach village children.
- **Belarusian-medium and Russian-medium families:** compare the language of the first book read at home with the language of school.
- **Hrodna region Poles:** trace how children learn Polish when state schooling in it shrinks.
- **The Astraviec and Pialiesa Lithuanian villages:** check the role of Sunday and weekend schools.
- **Brest region and Polesia:** compare children's work and learning by doing in marsh farming with the north.
- **Viciebsk region Old Believers:** check whether reading was taught from church books at home.
- **Homiel and the Chernobyl south:** look at schools closed and children bused or resettled after 1986.
- **Greek Catholics and Protestant congregations:** compare Sunday schools and Bible-reading circles as places of learning.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check where children learn Arabic letters and prayers today; records are thin.
- **Roma:** schooling records are scarce; look in NGO reports and say what is not known.
- **The historical Litvak world:** compare *kheyder*, yeshiva, Russian state schools and Yiddish Soviet schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history and statistics; mixed for home learning; thin for Roma and Lipka Tatar teaching.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Old Belarusian for Zizanij, Latin and Polish for the Jesuits, German and Yiddish for Wengeroff, Russian for Soviet and current data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-language schooling and the Flying University are politically charged; avoid pressing teachers inside Belarus on these subjects.
- **Held for review:** none; no learning pointer was set aside.
