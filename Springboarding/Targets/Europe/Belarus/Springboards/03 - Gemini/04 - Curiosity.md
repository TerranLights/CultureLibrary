## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Children in Minsk and in district towns:** check access to clubs, planetarium visits and museum trips by place.
- **Belarusian-reading and Russian-reading families:** compare which children's magazines and question books are in the house.
- **The Navahrudak area and the Hrodna gentry hamlets:** test whether local pride in explorers and scientists shapes school projects.
- **Polesians of the Brest region:** check folk knowledge of marsh plants, fish and birds as a curiosity of its own, distinct from book science.
- **Viciebsk region and the Braslaŭ lakes:** compare lake-district tourism and nature clubs with farm districts.
- **Homiel region and the Chernobyl resettled:** ask whether outsiders' interest in the zone is welcomed, tolerated or resented, and by whom.
- **Old Believers and Protestant congregations:** check which questions their teaching treats as proper and which it discourages.
- **The historical Jewish towns (Shklow, Minsk, Pinsk):** compare traditional study with Enlightenment inquiry.
- **Lipka Tatars and Roma:** questions outsiders ask them are part of this concept; look for community statements on intrusive research, and note how few exist.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and Soviet-era popular science; mixed for present-day hobbies; thin for minorities' own views of curiosity.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for Pačobut and Domeyko, Hebrew for Schick, Belarusian and Russian for everything modern, Spanish for Domeyko's Chilean papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exclusion-zone tourism touches evacuees' grief; asking outsiders' questions in Belarus about politics or ethnicity can be unsafe for respondents.
- **Held for review:** none; no curiosity pointer was set aside.
