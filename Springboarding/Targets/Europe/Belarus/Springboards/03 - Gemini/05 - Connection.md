# Belarus: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Belarusian | сувязі (suviazi), знаёмствы (znajomstvy), адзінота (adzinota) | ties or contacts, acquaintances, loneliness | *suviazi* covers both kin ties and "pull" |
| Belarusian | кумоўства (kumoŭstva) | godparent kinship; also favoritism | the double sense is itself a search lead |

| Russian (Belarus) | связи, по знакомству, одиночество | connections, through someone you know, loneliness | official and popular press |
| *Trasianka* | spoken forms of "кум", "свой чалавек" / "свой человек" | godfather, "one of ours" | from recorded speech only |
| Polish (Hrodna area) | znajomości, kumostwo | acquaintances, godparenthood | Catholic parish usage |
| West Polesian | кум, кума *check* | godfather, godmother | address terms between co-parents |
| Yiddish | פּראָטעקציע (protektsye), שדכן (shadkhn) | pull or patronage, matchmaker | shtetl memoir vocabulary |
| Romani | *kumpania* *check* | a group of families traveling or working together | confirm use among Belarusian Roma |

English keywords: Belarus informal networks, godparenthood Belarus, Belarusian matchmaking customs, loneliness Belarus elderly, Belarus solidarity networks 2020, Belarusian village festivals.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk newcomers and their home villages:** check how often city migrants return, and which ties the weekend trip keeps alive.
- **Rural elders living alone:** compare the reach of kin, neighbors and social workers by region.
- **Hrodna region Catholics and Poles:** check whether *kumostwo* runs across Catholic and Orthodox families.
- **Brest region and Polesia:** compare godparent ties in villages where nearly all families are related.
- **Viciebsk region, the Braslaŭ lakes and Old Believer communities:** look at whether marriages and godparents are chosen inside the community.
- **Homiel and the Chernobyl south:** see how resettled villagers kept or lost each other after being spread among new towns.
- **Ukrainians of the south and Lithuanians near Astraviec:** check cross-border kin visits and how border rules shape them.
- **Lipka Tatars:** record intermarriage and mosque networks; sources are thin.
- **Roma:** look at family networks and travel ties; few sources exist, so note gaps.
- **Protestant congregations:** check whether church membership supplies a network of help beyond kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kinship and wedding ethnography; mixed for modern networks; thin for isolation and for minority networks.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for *silva rerum*, Belarusian dialects for rites, Russian for sociology, Yiddish for memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** solidarity funds and their donors face prosecution; never collect names. Talk of favors and connections can expose people to corruption charges.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on ties or networks was set aside.
