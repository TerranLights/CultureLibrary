# Belarus: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** living with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once, dividing the world into pairs, and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belarusian speakers in Minsk and Russian speakers in the same blocks:** check where each switches language, and with whom.
- **Mixed Catholic-Orthodox families of the Hrodna and Brest regions:** compare which feasts, prayers and baptisms each household keeps.
- **Hrodna region Poles with and without the Karta Polaka:** check whether holding the card changes how they describe their belonging.
- **Polesians of the Brest region:** see how people name themselves when offered Belarusian, Ukrainian and Polesian labels.
- **Viciebsk region Old Believers:** compare old and new church calendars kept side by side.
- **Ukrainians of the Homiel south and Lithuanians near Astraviec:** check paired identities at the borders.
- **Greek Catholics:** look at how a two-rite church explains itself to Orthodox and Catholic neighbors.
- **Lipka Tatars:** trace the pairing of Slavic speech with Muslim prayer, in their own writings.
- **Roma:** check how settled and once-traveling families describe two ways of life; few sources exist.
- **The historical Litvak communities:** compare the Yiddish-Hebrew pair with the later Yiddish-Russian one.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language and script history; mixed for family practice; thin for Roma and for the West Polesian movement's later years.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian in Cyrillic and *łacinka*, Russian, Polish, Yiddish and Hebrew, Arabic script for the *kitabs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the white-red-white flag is treated as an "extremist" symbol; displaying or discussing it can bring arrest. The Karta Polaka is politically charged in Belarus.
- **Held for review:** none; no duality pointer was set aside.
