# Belarus: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural large families and small urban families:** check how many siblings children in Minsk and in villages grow up with, and who minds the younger ones.
- **Hrodna region Catholics and Poles:** compare godparent choices among siblings and cousins in Catholic families.
- **Brest region and Polesia:** check whether cousin terms and sibling duties differ in the marsh villages.
- **Viciebsk region Old Believers:** look for marriage rules between cousins and how far kin counts.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper:** compare postwar orphaned households, where older siblings took over.
- **Homiel and the Chernobyl south:** check how resettlement split adult siblings between new towns.
- **Ukrainians of the south and Lithuanians near Astraviec:** compare kin terms across the language borders.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check sibling and cousin marriage rules in the community's own writings; few exist.
- **Roma:** check kin terms and the eldest brother's role in their own words; data are thin.
- **Protestant congregations:** "brother" and "sister" as address between members; compare with blood kin terms.
- **The historical Litvak families:** compare sibling chains in migration and study (one brother at the yeshiva, one in trade).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kin terms and family statistics; mixed for sibling relations; thin for siblings as caregivers of old parents.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for the chronicle, Belarusian dialects for kin terms, Polish and Yiddish for minority families, Russian for state data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children in state care are a protected group; work only with published figures and adult testimony. Inheritance between siblings is a Libra topic.
- **Held for review:** none; no sibling pointer was set aside.
