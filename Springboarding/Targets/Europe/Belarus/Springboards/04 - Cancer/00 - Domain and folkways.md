# Belarus: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** what home is and who counts as family: household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, the house itself, and the ways families are ordered and children raised.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk flats against district towns and villages:** check who lives with grandparents and whether a village house is kept as a second home.
- **The Hrodna region (Catholic villages, Poles, the old petty-gentry hamlets):** compare household rites and home blessings across the Catholic and Orthodox calendars.
- **The Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** sample Polesian house types and older household records apart from central Belarus.
- **The Viciebsk region, the Dvina valley and the Braslaŭ lakes:** separate Old Believer households and the farmstead pattern near the Latvian border.
- **The Mahilioŭ Dnieper lands and the Homiel south:** set household records before and after the Chernobyl evacuations side by side.
- **Navahrudak, Iŭje and Slonim:** read Lipka Tatar marriage, naming and household rites in the community's own sources.
- **Lithuanians near Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south, Roma, Greek Catholics, Protestants:** treat each as its own household tradition, not a variant of the majority.
- **The Litvak shtetl record (Pinsk, Babrujsk, Kamianiec, Mir):** check memoirs against communal records for household size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century ethnography and archives; mixed for home life today; thin for Roma and Lipka Tatar households now.
- **Lands:** the tracker records no change to the pre-war lands and there is no `00 - Scope.md` yet, so the home-and-family sheets take Belarus as it stands; borders and units are left to the author's Stage 0.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian (both spellings) and Russian; Polish for Moszyński and the Tatar survey; Yiddish for Kotik; old Russian orthography for the revision lists.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family policy is tied to state ideology; household books name private people; archive access has narrowed since 2020. Family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on homes, households or kin was set aside.
