# Belarus: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk microdistrict flats against the private-house quarters (*pryvatny sektar*) at city edges:** check whether "home" means the flat one lives in or the parents' house one returns to.
- **The Hrodna region (Catholic villages and Poles):** compare the words *dom*, *chata* and *ojcowizna* in Polish and Belarusian speech near the border.
- **The Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** check how drained wetlands and moved villages changed which places people name as home.
- **The Viciebsk region, the Dvina valley and the Braslaŭ lakes:** sample Old Believer villages, where house and prayer house may sit apart.
- **The Homiel south and the Mahilioŭ Dnieper:** compare evacuees resettled in new villages with those given flats in towns.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars of Iŭje and Slonim:** check how manor memory and mosque neighborhoods each mark a home place.
- **Astraviec and the Lithuanian-speaking villages:** set old farmsteads against the new town built for the nuclear plant's staff.
- **Roma and the Litvak shtetl record:** check what counted as a home before and after forced settlement or destruction.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, dictionaries and housing data; mixed on how people feel about home; thin for Tatar and Roma homes.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for Skaryna and the historical dictionary; Yiddish for Bella Chagall; Polish for Navahrudak and Hrodna sources; Russian for housing records and listings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chernobyl losses and evictions are painful topics; visits to the reserve need permits.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on dwellings or homecoming was withheld.
