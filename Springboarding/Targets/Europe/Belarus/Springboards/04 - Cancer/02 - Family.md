# Belarus: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polesia and the Brest region:** test Obrębski's village kin groups against later collective-farm records in the same districts.
- **The Hrodna region's Polish Catholic families:** check which language a family uses at home, in church and with the in-laws.
- **The Viciebsk region and the Braslaŭ lakes:** separate priestless Old Believer families from Orthodox and Catholic neighbors when counting marriages.
- **Minsk:** compare families formed by migrants from villages in the 1960s-1980s with their village kin.
- **The Homiel south:** check how resettlement after 1986 scattered or kept kin together.
- **Mahilioŭ, Babrujsk and the Dnieper towns:** read the Litvak family record (Freeze; testimonies) against the Belarusian village record.
- **Navahrudak, Iŭje and Slonim Lipka Tatars:** check marriage within and outside the community over generations.
- **Lithuanians near Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south, Roma, Protestant congregations:** check each group's own kin terms and family size before grouping them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for censuses and the historical record; mixed for kin relations today; thin for Roma and Tatar families.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Dovnar-Zapolski and every census; Polish for Obrębski and the Hrodna diocese; Yiddish and Hebrew under Freeze's sources; Belarusian dialect forms for kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family policy carries state ideology; testimonies and registers name private people. Marriage and divorce law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no kin or family pointer was set aside.
