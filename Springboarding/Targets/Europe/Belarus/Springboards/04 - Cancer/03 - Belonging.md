# Belarus: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna and the Polish border:** check whether the label *tutejšy* or *swój* still names belonging, and who uses it.
- **Polesia and the Brest region (*paliashuki*):** compare a village or Polesian self-label with the national one in interviews.
- **Minsk:** separate people born in the city from those who came from villages after the war, and from post-1991 arrivals.
- **The Viciebsk region, the Dvina valley and the Braslaŭ lakes:** check how Old Believer and Catholic villages mark who is "ours".
- **The Homiel south:** compare those who left the zone with *samasioly* and newcomers who settled there later.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars:** check what marks Tatar belonging: faith, family name, cemetery or service record.
- **Astraviec and its Lithuanians:** check parish, school and language as tests of belonging.
- **Roma, Russians and Ukrainians of the south, Protestants and the Litvak record:** check each group's own in-group words before applying any national frame.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national-identity debate and survey data; mixed for local belonging; thin for Roma and for Lithuanian villages in Belarusian sources.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish (or a translation) for the *Risale*; Polish for Tyszkiewicz and Engelking; Lithuanian for Hieraviaty; Yiddish and English for Levin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national identity, language and symbols are politicized; church disputes and minority identity touch state security concerns.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on insiders, outsiders or membership was withheld.
