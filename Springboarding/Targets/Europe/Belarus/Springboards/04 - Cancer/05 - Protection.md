*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Hrodna region (Catholics and Poles):** check house blessings, Marian devotion and parish protection rites against Orthodox practice nearby.
- **The Brest region and Polesia:** compare Tolstoy's Polesian amulet data with older and newer field records district by district.
- **The Homiel south:** check how protective rules after Chernobyl were followed, adapted or ignored in daily life.
- **The Viciebsk region and the Braslaŭ lakes:** separate Old Believer protective prayers and icons from Orthodox and Catholic ones.
- **Minsk and the large towns:** check reliance on police, patrols and locks against village-level ways of guarding a house.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars:** check prayers and objects used to guard household and travelers.
- **Roma, the Litvak record, Lithuanians near Astraviec, Protestants:** check each group's own guardians, amulets and duty to defend.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk magic and church calendars; mixed for policing and child protection; thin for Tatar and Roma protective customs.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Tolstoy and state sources; Belarusian and Polish for church practice; Yiddish and Hebrew on amulets; English for the ETHOS reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** figures on violence at home are likely incomplete; police and patrol sources are official; the hymn and national symbols are politically loaded.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on guarding, charms or defense was set aside.
