# Belarus: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk:** compare night-stay beds, dormitories and charity shelters with what families do for kin who lose a home.
- **The Hrodna region and the Polish border:** set the 2021 migrant camps beside older parish almshouses and Catholic charity.
- **The Brest region and Polesia:** check flood refuges, forest huts and the record of villages burned in the war.
- **The Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley:** check how Old Believer and Orthodox villages lodged wanderers and pilgrims.
- **The Mahilioŭ Dnieper and Homiel south:** compare shelter after the war with shelter after the 1986 evacuation.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars:** look for the community's own record of lodging travelers; expect little in print.
- **Roma:** compare tent and wagon life before 1956 with the housing given afterward.
- **The Litvak record:** check the *hekdesh* and the ghetto hiding places against Christian neighbors' testimony.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and Holocaust shelter; mixed for homelessness today; thin for Tatar and Roma lodging customs.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian, Latin and Polish for almshouse charters; Russian for social-service data; Yiddish and Hebrew for the *hekdesh*; English for UNHCR.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2021 border crisis and asylum are politically charged; reporting on homelessness is likely sparse; rescue stories may be contested within families.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on refuge, lodging or asylum was set aside.
