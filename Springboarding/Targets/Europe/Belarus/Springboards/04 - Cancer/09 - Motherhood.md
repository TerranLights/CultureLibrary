## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against villages:** compare mean age at first birth, length of leave and grandmothers' share of child care.
- **The Hrodna region (Catholics and Poles):** check Marian devotion, Polish-language prayers and the church's teaching to mothers.
- **The Brest region and Polesia:** compare midwife and birth-rite records with the Kupalle songs collected there.
- **The Viciebsk region and the Braslaŭ lakes:** separate Old Believer birth and churching customs from Orthodox ones.
- **The Homiel south:** check mothers' fears and health care for children born after 1986 in the affected districts.
- **The Mahilioŭ Dnieper and Žodzina:** compare state war memory of mothers with family memory.
- **Navahrudak and the Lipka Tatars:** check birth and naming customs; sources are likely scarce.
- **Roma mothers, Lithuanians near Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south, Protestant families, the Litvak record:** check each group's own birth and mothering customs separately.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health statistics and war memory; mixed for mothers' own voices; thin for Roma and Tatar mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Russian for the saint's life; Belarusian for songs and birth rites; Yiddish and Hebrew for *tkhines*; Russian for health and labor data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abortion, war losses and mothers' protests are charged topics; the mothers' movement faces state pressure.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on mothers or birth was set aside.
