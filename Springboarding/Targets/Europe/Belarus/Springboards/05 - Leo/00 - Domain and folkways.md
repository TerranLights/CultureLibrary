## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Capital and farm towns (*ahraharadki*):** which pastimes need a club, stadium or bar, and who travels to Minsk for them.
- **Catholic parishes near Hrodna and the Polish border:** set *odpust* fairs and Polish dance parties against Orthodox feast days in the same districts.
- **Polesian villages of the Brest region (*paliashuki*):** gather dance tunes and courtship games as a separate sample.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina valley and Braslaŭ:** read the communities' own rules on singing, drink and dance before comparing them with lake-resort leisure.
- **Dnieper towns of Mahilioŭ; the evacuated Homiel south:** trace where feasts and village teams went after Chernobyl.
- **Lipka Tatar families of Navahrudak:** list the Muslim-calendar gatherings and who hosts them.
- **Smaller communities (Lithuanians by Astraviec, southern Ukrainians, Greek Catholics, Protestants, Roma):** separate their feast days and amateur groups; ask whether a congregation sets rules on dancing.
- **The shtetl record:** Purim, weddings and the Yiddish stage, from Yiddish sources.
