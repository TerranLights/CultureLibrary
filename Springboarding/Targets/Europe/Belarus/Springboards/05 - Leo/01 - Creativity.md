# Belarus: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viciebsk and the Dvina valley:** set the city's avant-garde school beside Old Believer icon painting and book copying in nearby villages; check how each treats copying a model.
- **Weavers of the Homiel south:** check what became of village patterns after Chernobyl resettlement broke up workshops.
- **Polesian craft villages of the Brest region:** pottery and weaving taught inside families; check who may teach whom.
- **Catholic parishes around Hrodna:** Palm Sunday palms, shrine decoration and Polish-language craft circles; check whether makers sign work.
- **Minsk designers and programmers:** check how game studios and design schools speak of originality, against village makers.
- **Lipka Tatars of Navahrudak and Iŭje:** calligraphy and textile work; check who in the community may make sacred objects.
- **The shtetl record:** carvers, *badkhonim* and klezmer players; check whether artisans were named or anonymous.
- **Smaller confessions and peoples:** Greek Catholic icon revival, Protestant hymn writing, Roma smithing and Lithuanian sashes near Astraviec each need their own sample; sources may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk craft and the avant-garde; mixed for invention statistics; thin for Roma metalwork and Lipka Tatar textile art.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for Radziwiłłowa's plays; Russian for the Moscow craftsmen debate; Yiddish for wedding memoirs; Arabic script for *muhirs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independent galleries and artists have been closed or pushed abroad since 2020; whose heritage a removed object is (Moscow, Warsaw, Vilnia) is disputed.
- **Held for review:** none. Attitudes to machine-made art in Belarus were left out for want of a sure source, not under a rule.
