# Belarus: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk students and young workers:** set online style and music against district-town peers; check which language each uses for feeling.
- **Polish-speaking parishes of the Hrodna borderland:** amateur theater, church choirs and sodalities; check whether personal feeling goes into Polish there and public speech into Russian.
- **Polesian villagers (*paliashuki*):** older women's song and lament against younger relatives' speech; check what each says plainly.
- **Old Believers of the Viciebsk lakes and Braslaŭ:** beards, dress and plain speech under community rule; check what a member may change.
- **Homiel and Mahilioŭ families after Chernobyl:** how anger, grief and fear were voiced in letters, petitions and song.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check where religious self-presentation (head covering, names) is public or kept private.
- **Congregations with spoken testimony (Pentecostals, Baptists) and Greek Catholics:** check where a church gives members a turn to speak about themselves.
- **Roma families; the Astraviec Lithuanians; southern Ukrainians:** what clothing and choice of tongue signal; written sources are few, so plan interviews.
- **The Litvak record:** letters, Yiddish poetry and memoirs as the places feeling was written down.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and protest material; mixed for everyday emotional display; thin for Old Believer, Roma and Tatar self-presentation.
- **Languages needed:** old Belarusian print for Marašeŭski, Yiddish to read Charik in the original, Russian slang for the rap lyrics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** many 2020 materials, the PEN Center and LGBT media are labeled "extremist" or restricted in Belarus; sharing or citing them can endanger people inside.
- **Held for review:** none; the rap, LGBT and protest pointers stay, each flagged for safety above.
