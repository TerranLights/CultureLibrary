# Belarus: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk and the regional capitals:** check whether city pride rests on industry, sport or history, and whether regional towns define theirs against Minsk.
- **Hrodna borderland Catholics:** Polish-language commemorations and parish jubilees; check which past they take pride in.
- **Polesia:** Polesian self-names and the dialect movement; check pride in a separate Polesian identity against the national one.
- **Viciebsk and Polatsk:** "the oldest town" claims and Old Believer communities nearby; check how each places itself in time.
- **The Chernobyl-affected south:** check whether pride in surviving or rebuilding appears in local writing.
- **Lipka Tatars of Navahrudak, Iŭje and Slonim:** pride in service and faith; check community publications against outsiders' accounts.
- **Lithuanians at Astraviec, Ukrainians of the south, Old Believers, Roma:** pride in a minority tongue or faith; sources thin outside interviews.
- **Litvak Jews:** *yikhes*, learning and the *musar* critique of pride, read in Yiddish and Hebrew.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for heraldry, festivals and old literature; mixed for survey data; thin for Roma and for LGBT pride inside the country.
- **Languages needed:** Russian dictionary Cyrillic for Nasovič; Hebrew and Yiddish for Novardok; old Polish and Latin for panegyrics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national symbols and their history are contested (two flags, two coats of arms); LGBT activism is under pressure; naming LGBT activists can harm them.
- **Held for review:** nothing on pride, boasting or symbols met a standing rule.
