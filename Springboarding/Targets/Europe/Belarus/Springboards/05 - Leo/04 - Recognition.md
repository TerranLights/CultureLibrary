# Belarus: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State employees against private-sector and IT workers:** check whether certificates and titles still carry weight, or whether pay, ratings and clients matter more.
- **Hrodna borderland Poles and Catholics:** parish honors and Polish-state recognition (scholarships, the Pole's Card) alongside Belarusian titles; check how each is valued.
- **Polesian collective-farm villages:** milkmaids, machine operators and teachers honored in district papers; check who is never named.
- **Viciebsk and the Dvina valley:** credit for artists and teachers in the city's art tradition; Old Believer communities' own forms of honor for elders.
- **Homiel south and the Chernobyl zone:** recognition of cleanup workers, doctors and evacuees; check the gap between status papers and public credit.
- **Lipka Tatar communities:** how the community credits imams, teachers and benefactors; few written sources, so start with community publications.
- **Women in science and medicine:** check whose names appear in prize lists against who did the work.
- **Lithuanians at Astraviec, Roma, Protestants, Greek Catholics:** check whether their leaders and teachers receive any state or local credit, and through which channels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** title decrees and prize lists are plentiful; informal credit (thanks, reputation) is scattered; community honors among Tatars, Roma and Old Believers are barely written down.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for *sinodiki*; Yiddish and Hebrew for synagogue honors; Russian for decrees and diploma rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** since 2020 some prize winners and titled artists were stripped of honors or emigrated; independent prizes are treated with suspicion. Avoid naming living people inside the country.
- **Held for review:** none; credit, thanks and titles raised no pointer under the standing rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the President's and BelTA's sites for "присвоено почетное звание Народный артист Беларуси" and list recipients by year and art.
2. Request a published *sinodik* from a Belarusian monastery and list the donors named.
3. Pull the Giedroyc Prize shortlists from 2012 on and note authors' home regions.
4. Search district newspapers for "выказваем шчырую падзяку" to sample thank-you notices by region.
