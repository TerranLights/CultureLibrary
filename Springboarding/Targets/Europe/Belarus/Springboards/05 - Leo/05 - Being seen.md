# Belarus: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against the farm towns:** city cameras and feeds; village neighbors who watch each other's gates.
- **Polish speakers near Hrodna:** whether speaking Polish, wearing a cross or flying a red-and-white flag makes someone visible, and where.
- **Polesian *paliashuki*:** check how far Polesians appear in national media and textbooks, against their own festivals.
- **The Braslaŭ lakes and the Dvina valley:** Old Believer dress and beards; check where members choose to stand out and where to blend in.
- **The resettled of the Homiel south:** check who still visits the closed villages on remembrance days, and who notices them.
- **Navahrudak and Iŭje Tatars:** mosques, *mizars* and head coverings; check what is shown to visitors and what is kept inside the community.
- **Roma of Homiel, Babrujsk and other towns:** check visibility in news against absence from culture and school materials.
- **Smaller faiths and the border Lithuanians:** check whether Protestant churches, Greek Catholic parishes and Lithuanian schools appear on signs, maps and in the local press.
- **The Litvak towns:** which shtetl sites are visible today; check the *eyn-hore* customs in memoirs against Belarusian evil-eye charms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for portraits, pageants and broadcast media; mixed for everyday attention and greeting; thin for Roma and Tatar visibility in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for portrait inscriptions; Russian for surveillance and police reporting; Yiddish for evil-eye phrases in memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance and identification of protesters are live issues; do not link images or names of people filmed in 2020. Press about Roma may carry prejudice; read it as a record, not a source of facts about Roma.
- **Held for review:** none; the camera and Roma-press items carry cautions, not holds.
