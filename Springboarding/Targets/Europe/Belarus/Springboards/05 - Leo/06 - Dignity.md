# Belarus: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Older pensioners in villages against city pensioners:** check how help from the state, children and the church is accepted or refused.
- **Hrodna Catholics and Polish families:** Catholic charity and teaching; check the parish role in burying and visiting the poor.
- **Polesian villages:** check care of the sick and the dead in Polesian communities, and how the old are spoken to.
- **The Braslaŭ and Dvina Old Believers:** check community rules on burial and charity, and how they recall persecution.
- **Evacuees of the Homiel south:** check what resettled people say about how they were moved and housed.
- **Lipka Tatar communities:** check Tatar funeral rites and care for the poor in the *džamaat*.
- **Roma families:** check treatment by police, schools and clinics in Roma accounts, not only in official ones.
- **Men working abroad, and their families at home:** check how dignity is spoken of in migrant work.
- **Litvak communal life:** burial societies and charity rules in the *pinkasim*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** memorial and reburial records are plentiful; everyday respect shows up only in letters and surveys; Roma and Tatar first-person accounts are scarce, and rights monitoring from inside has stopped.
- **Languages needed:** Yiddish and Hebrew for *khevre kadishe* books; Russian for migrant-labor press; Belarusian for Adamovič and district letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Viasna's leaders have been jailed and the organization is banned; detention accounts can identify people at risk. Treatment by courts and police as law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; detention, migrant and Roma pointers are kept, the first under a safety caution.

## 5. First queries
1. Search YIVO and the Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People for "chevra kadisha" with Belarusian town names (Slonim, Pinsk, Mahilioŭ).

4. Pull Viasna's annual reviews (2020-2023) and list the kinds of degrading treatment cited.
