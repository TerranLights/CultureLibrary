# Belarus: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Young men in conscript service against those who avoided it:** check how hazing is recalled, laughed off or kept silent in families.
- **The Catholic and Polish Hrodna region:** interwar Polish rule and its camps, against Soviet-era humiliations; check which a family names first.
- **Polesia:** gate-tarring and village shaming in Polesian villages; check the dialect words and whether the custom outlived collectivization.
- **Priestless communities by the lakes:** persecution under tsars and Soviets; check how elders retell being driven out or mocked.
- **Mahilioŭ and Homiel towns:** the 1903 pogrom record against later Soviet and Chernobyl-era experiences of being looked down on.
- **Lipka Tatars:** the loss of mosques and of public religious life; check community accounts of being mocked as "Tatars".
- **Roma:** check police, school and press treatment as Roma describe it.
- **Urban and rural speech:** check whether *trasianka* speakers and villagers describe being laughed at in the city.
- **The shtetl record:** cantonists, pogroms and communal shaming (the *kahal*'s penalties) in Yiddish sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** pogroms, camps and hazing are well documented; folk shaming survives mostly in older ethnographies; Roma and Tatars rarely speak for themselves in print here.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for trial records and army press; Yiddish for cantonist memoirs; Belarusian dialect for wedding songs and gate-tarring accounts; Polish for Bereza Kartuska studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2020 testimonies and "repentance" videos involve living people at risk; quote no names. Sexual shaming customs need care with family names in field notes.
- **Held for review:** none; the detention testimonies are kept but handled as restricted.
