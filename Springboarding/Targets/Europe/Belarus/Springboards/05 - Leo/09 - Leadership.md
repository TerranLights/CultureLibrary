# Belarus: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **State enterprises against private firms and IT companies in Minsk:** check what employees call a good boss and whether consultation is expected.
- **The Catholic and Polish west (Hrodna, Lida, Ašmiany):** parish priests and lay leaders of Polish associations; check who speaks for a village.
- **Polesia:** the brigade leader and the farm chairman in isolated villages; Hasidic courts in the old record of Stolin and Pinsk.
- **Old Believers around Braslaŭ and the Dvina:** lay elders; check how they are chosen and replaced.
- **The Homiel and Mahilioŭ lands:** check who led during evacuation and resettlement after 1986, and how they are remembered.
- **Lipka Tatars:** imams and community chairs; check whether religious and secular leadership are separate.
- **Protestant and Greek Catholic congregations:** elected presbyters and pastors; check how lay members choose.
- **Roma communities and Astraviec Lithuanians:** check who represents each to officials, and who is followed inside.
- **The Litvak record:** *rebe*, *rosh-yeshive* and *parnes* as three different leaders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Grand Duchy figures, Soviet farm chairmen and the yeshiva world; mixed for workplace leadership; thin for Roma, Tatar and Old Believer leaders.
- **Languages needed:** the chancery Ruthenian and Polish of Sapieha's letters; rabbinic Hebrew for yeshiva and Hasidic sources; Russian for the management literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political leadership is the most dangerous field in Belarus to study; 2020 leaders are jailed or exiled, and naming supporters can harm them. The right to command and political hierarchy go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; political pointers kept, with the authority side left to Capricorn.
