# Belarus: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk blocks against village childhoods:** check how far children roam, and which games are called "village" or "city".
- **Hrodna Catholic families and the Polish west:** Easter and Christmas games tied to the Catholic calendar; check Polish-language counting rhymes.
- **Polesian villages of the Brest region:** swings, Easter games and spring play; check for games found only in the marshlands.
- **Priestless Old Believer villages (Dvina valley, Braslaŭ):** check whether cards, dancing or toys are restricted on fast days and feast days.
- **Families resettled from the Homiel south:** check which games and toys were left behind in the evacuations and how children played in new towns.
- **Lipka Tatars of Navahrudak and Iŭje:** children's games at Muslim feasts; sources thin.
- **Roma children:** check play and music at home, and whether schools record it; seek community voices.
- **Protestants, Greek Catholics and the Lithuanians at Astraviec:** check Sunday rules on play and the games taught in Lithuanian-language classes.
- **Litvak children:** *kheyder* boys' brief play time, girls' games and holiday play in memoirs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** puppetry, folk games and online nostalgia give plenty; adult play and gaming are patchy; for Roma, Tatar and Old Believer children almost nothing is in print.
- **Languages needed:** Belarusian for *batlejka* texts and game rhymes; Yiddish for holiday play; Russian for nostalgia forums and KVN.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** little, beyond animal-welfare debates over bear shows and gambling risks among young gamers; KVN and comedy may touch politics.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; the Smarhoń bear pointer is kept as history despite animal-welfare debate.
