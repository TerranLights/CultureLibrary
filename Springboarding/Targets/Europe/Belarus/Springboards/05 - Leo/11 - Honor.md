# Belarus: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former gentry hamlets near Lida and Navahrudak:** check whether family names, crests and gentry memory still carry standing.
- **Hrodna Poles and Catholics:** *honor* and *słowo honoru* in Polish-language family memory; check how the gentry past is told.
- **Polesian villages:** a daughter's reputation and village feuds; check whether customs differ from the north.
- **The Dvina valley, Braslaŭ and Old Believers:** check honesty in trade and the community's own sanctions on oath-breakers.
- **Army and police families of Minsk and garrison towns:** "honor of the uniform" as a family value; check how it is taught at home.
- **The Homiel south:** Chernobyl cleanup workers' sense of duty and its official honoring; check how they speak of it.
- **Lipka Tatar families:** noble standing, military service and family honor; check community sources beside the 19th-century files.
- **Roma communities:** whether *pativ* applies here, and to what; ask, do not assume.
- **Litvak households:** a good name (*shem-tov*) in commerce and marriage, as memoirs record it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the gentry, the army and the old romances leave much on paper; family honor now is known mainly from interviews; Roma and Tatar views barely reach print.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian for the romances; Polish for gentry memory and duels; Russian for 19th-century files and prison-code studies; Yiddish for good-name sayings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bride-honor customs and family violence need care; the Slucak uprising and the armed forces are politically charged. Oaths and courts of honor as law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; even the bride-honor customs stay, as history to be read with care.
