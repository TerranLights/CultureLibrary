# Belarus: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk crowds and village feasts:** check whether joy is shown loudly at city concerts and quietly at village tables, or the reverse.
- **Catholic Hrodna and the Polish border:** *Rezurekcja*, first communions and parish festivals; check what Polish-language sources call a joyful day.
- **Polesian villages:** spring songs and dances kept in Polesia; check whether outsiders' festivals have replaced them.
- **Viciebsk, the Dvina and Braslaŭ:** Old Believer Easter and the Hasidic past of the same region; check how each sanctions joy.
- **The Homiel south:** check whether resettled villagers return for feast days, and what joy at such gatherings looks like.
- **Lipka Tatars:** feast days in the Muslim calendar; check what is shared with Christian neighbors.
- **Roma families:** weddings, music and feast days, recorded with families' consent, not from outside.
- **Greek Catholics, Protestants and the Astraviec Lithuanians:** praise worship, harvest services and Lithuanian song days; check each separately.
- **Litvak towns before 1941:** weddings with klezmer bands, *freylekhs* and Hasidic feasts; check Misnagdic towns against Hasidic ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** feasts, dances and festivals are amply recorded; happiness measures exist only at country level; little of Roma, Tatar or Old Believer celebration is written by those communities.
- **Languages needed:** a reader of 17th-century musical notation for the Polatsk notebook; Chabad Hebrew and Yiddish; Belarusian for dance-song texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state festivals are tied to official ideology, and independent celebrations (for example of March 25) are restricted; keep pointers neutral and name no participants.
- **Held for review:** none; the March 25 remark is a safety note, not a hold.
