# Belarus: The domain: work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of the Hrodna area, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** what counts as good work and a well-kept life: work, service, the daily round, the kept house, health, craft, and rest as work's counterpart.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk against the farm towns (*ahraharadki*):** set city and village time-use results side by side, including garden-plot labor.
- **The Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley:** check Nikiforoŭski's northern daily round against today's village records in the same districts.
- **The Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** compare marsh-country seasonal work (fishing, hay on floodplains) with farm work elsewhere.
- **Hrodna-area Catholics and Poles:** check *porządki* before Easter and Christmas against Orthodox cleaning on Clean Thursday.
- **Old Believers of Braslaŭ and Vietka:** read the community's own rule texts on washing, dishes and rest days.
- **Homiel south and the resettled:** check how work and the daily round changed for families moved after 1986.
- **Lipka Tatars, Roma and Lithuanians by Astraviec:** separate samples for rest days and the household round; sources are scarce, so start with community bodies.
- **The shtetl record:** the Sabbath week and livelihoods, from Yiddish memoirs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the farm year and the official statistics are well documented; office and factory work culture less so; Tatar, Roma and Old Believer households barely.
- **Lands:** the tracker records no change to the pre-war lands and Stage 0 is not written, so the Virgo sheets point into Belarus as it stands; units and borders wait for the author.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Belstat and imperial ethnography; Polish and Old Belarusian for estate inventories; German or Yiddish for Wengeroff and shtetl memoirs; dialect Belarusian for reaping songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** labor protest and independent unions after 2020 are dangerous ground; work-related law (the "social dependency" decree, labor contracts) goes to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none; no daily-round or craft pointer touched a standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. On belstat.gov.by, search "использование времени" and download the latest time-use tables by sex and settlement type.
2. In the National Library of Belarus catalog, search "Никифоровский Очерки простонародного житья-бытья".
3. Search library catalogs for "Жніўныя песні" and list the districts the volume draws on.
4. Search the Akty series indexes for "инвентарь" with a Hrodna or Mahilioŭ estate name.
5. Search district newspaper sites for "некралог" with "працоўны стаж"; sample twenty obituaries from four regions.
