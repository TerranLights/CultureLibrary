# Belarus: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk service workers and village shop staff:** check how each speaks of customers and of being spoken to.
- **The western Catholic parishes and Orthodox villages of the Mahilioŭ region:** compare lay roles in church service and who may fill them.
- **Polesia and the Brest region:** set evangelical congregations' service ministries against Orthodox parish practice in the same towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check the community's own accounts of service to the Grand Duchy and to the mosque, in their writers' words.
- **The Homiel south:** check who took on unpaid service in resettled villages after 1986.
- **The Lake District around Braslaŭ:** farmstead hosts; check whether old hospitality customs carry into paid lodging.
- **Old Believers and Greek Catholics:** small communities; ask how church service is shared without a large clergy.
- **The Litvak record:** the *shames* and the *dinst* as two kinds of service, read in Yiddish.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the state's service bodies and army press leave long paper trails; shop-floor and café service is known mostly through complaints; Roma and Tatar roles of service are barely written down.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for the Radziwiłł papers; Russian for the census and army press; Yiddish for servant-girl memoirs; Belarusian dialect for wedding roles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** army hazing and conscientious objection are touchy subjects; veterans' and conscripts' names stay out. Duty and rank belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the army, church and servant pointers raised no rule.
