## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk's IT and academy workers against farm-town specialists:** check where analysis is a profession and where it is the agronomist's or bookkeeper's task.
- **The Brest region:** the Brisker tradition in the Jewish record beside today's Brest schools and engineering colleges.
- **The Viciebsk region:** Polack's Jesuit past and the Hasidic learning of Liady; check which reasoning styles each record shows.
- **Vietka and the Braslaŭ lakes:** Old Believer textual checking as a lay skill.
- **The Hrodna region and its Poles:** Polish-language schools and quiz leagues; check the language of analytic contests.
- **The Homiel south:** residents' own radiation readings and dosimeters against official maps after 1986.
- **Women and men in engineering and IT:** check how analytic work is split, using enrollment data.
- **Lipka Tatars and Roma:** no sources on analytic tradition known; ask community scholars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the history of science and computing and for Jewish learning; mixed for everyday reasoning; thin for folk reckoning outside riddles.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Siemienowicz and the Jesuits; Hebrew and Yiddish for Brisk; Russian for computing history and audit reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independent economic and opinion analysts work from exile and some are labeled "extremist"; citing them inside Belarus carries risk.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; Brisk, the *Minsk* machines and the audit reports all sit within the rules.
