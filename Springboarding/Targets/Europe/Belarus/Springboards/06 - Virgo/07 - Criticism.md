# Belarus: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, and the forms this takes: feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk intelligentsia and district officials:** check which channels each uses for criticism: the press, appeals, or the kitchen table.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper towns:** the Old Belarusian pamphlets and later town humor; check whether criticism of rulers has a local strand.
- **Orthodox and Greek Catholic communities of the Brest region:** read the Union polemics from both sides before comparing their present relations.
- **Hrodna-area Poles:** Polish-language press and parish life; check how criticism of officials is voiced in a minority language.
- **Polesian villages:** wedding reproach songs and anecdotes as a sample of licensed criticism.
- **The Homiel south:** check how complaints about Chernobyl compensation were raised and answered.
- **Old Believers, Lipka Tatars and Roma:** no sources on internal rebuke known; ask how a community corrects its members.
- **Yiddish Minsk:** Kulbak's satire and the Yiddish Soviet press, until the 1930s purges.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literary satire, Soviet meeting records and old polemics; mixed for everyday criticism in families and workplaces; very thin for minority practice.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian and Polish for the early satires and polemics; Yiddish for Kulbak; Russian for party minutes and appeals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** open political criticism has been prosecuted since 2020; satirists, critics and theater staff have been jailed or exiled; no individual from those years should be named. Law on appeals belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; every satire, polemic and archive pointer passed the rules.
