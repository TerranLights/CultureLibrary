*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Homiel and Mahilioŭ lands:** set registry data against residents' own accounts of illness; separate evacuees, those resettled and those who stayed.
- **Village and city:** compare access to a feldsher or a polyclinic, and the use of folk cures beside them.
- **Catholic Hrodna and Orthodox Mahilioŭ:** check which healing shrines and springs each uses.
- **Polesia:** folk healers and home births in the older record; check how long they lasted.
- **Old Believers of Vietka and the Dvina valley:** read the community's own rules on medicine and doctors before assuming.
- **Lipka Tatars:** healing prayers in the *chamaily*; Tatar scholars first.
- **Roma:** health access and trust in clinics; check NGO reports, and ask Roma bodies directly.
- **Protestant congregations:** prayer for healing and rehab ministries; compare with state treatment.
- **The shtetl record:** TOZ and OZE files beside Yiddish memoirs of sickness and cure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mortality tables and radiation medicine fill shelves; living folk cures are recorded mostly in older ethnography; Roma and Tatar health has barely been studied.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and French for Gilibert; Russian for statistics and the registry; Yiddish and Polish for TOZ; dialect Belarusian for charms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chernobyl health figures are disputed; the COVID-19 response of 2020 is political; mental illness, alcoholism and compulsory treatment carry stigma. Patients and doctors stay unnamed.
- **Held for review:** empty; nothing about clinics, springs or charms had to be withheld.
