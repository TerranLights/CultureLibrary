# Belarus: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polesia and the Brest region:** set each drainage era (imperial, interwar, Soviet) against villagers' memories of the land before and after.
- **The Navahrudak area:** Chreptowicz's estate, the Tatar mosque and the Mir yeshiva sit close together; compare three ideas of betterment in one district.
- **Minsk and the regional centers:** check who renovates, who joins trainings, and who sees self-development as foreign.
- **Hrodna-area Poles:** interwar improvement under the Polish state against their present community projects.
- **The Homiel south:** check whether resettlement and rebuilt villages were framed as improvement by planners and by residents.
- **Viciebsk-region villages:** the reading-hut campaign and the Old Believer communities nearby; check who took part and who stayed apart.
- **Roma:** settlement and schooling programs aimed at Roma; read Roma accounts before official ones.
- **The shtetl record:** *muser* striving and the Haskalah's plans for "improving" Jews, kept separate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land reclamation and state campaigns; mixed for self-help; thin for minority views of being "improved".
- **Languages needed:** Polish for Chreptowicz and the interwar archive; Russian for the Western Expedition; Hebrew and Yiddish for *muser*; Belarusian and Russian for the press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** improvement imposed on Roma, Jews and peasants (collectivization, forced settlement) must be told in those people's voices; the land-reclamation debate overlaps with Chernobyl politics.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; drainage, *muser* and self-help pointers needed no setting aside.
