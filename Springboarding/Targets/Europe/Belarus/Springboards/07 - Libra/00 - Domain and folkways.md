*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of the Hrodna area, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani; Old Belarusian (Ruthenian) for the Grand Duchy's law. **Concept in one line:** what is a fair bargain and a good partnership: marriage, contracts, bonds between equals, open enemies, settling disputes, civility, and the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox, Catholic and Greek Catholic couples:** set each church's rules on mixed marriage and annulment beside the one civil code.
- **Brest region and Polesia (*paliashuki*):** compare betrothal steps and the matchmaking women with northern accounts.
- **Hrodna-area Poles and Lithuanians by Astraviec:** check how church weddings in Polish or Lithuanian pair with the civil registry.
- **Lipka Tatars:** separate the *nikah* from civil marriage; sources are scarce, so start with Tatar community scholars.
- **The Litvak record:** read the Council's rulings on partners and debts apart from Christian courts.
- **Old Believers and Protestants:** check in-community marriage and whether quarrels stay out of state courts; little in print.
- **Roma:** whether any internal tribunal exists here is unknown; ask community bodies, not foreign accounts.
- **Minsk against the Homiel south and the resettled:** compare marriage ages in the capital and in districts emptied after 1986.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, statistics and Grand Duchy law; mixed for church courts; thin for Tatar, Roma and Old Believer practice.
- **Lands (said once, here):** no `00 - Scope.md` exists and the tracker carries Belarus's pre-war lands unchanged, so every Libra sheet is written for the present republic; nothing here settles a border or a unit.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for the 1588 code; Hebrew for the Council's minutes; Russian for the family code and Belstat; Arabic script for Tatar marriage records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the KGB list names people still imprisoned or in exile; cite the list, never a private person from it. The spirit of the law goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none: the Statute, the Council's minutes, the codes and the KGB list all stay open to research.
