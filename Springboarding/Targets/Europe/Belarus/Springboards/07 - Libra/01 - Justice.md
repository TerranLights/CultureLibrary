## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viciebsk and Mahilioŭ court books against Brest and Hrodna ones:** the Statute ended earlier in the east; check what that changed.
- **Polesian villages (*paliashuki*):** seek kopa and lynching records in the Pinsk marshes, far from state courts.
- **Hrodna-area Catholics and Poles:** set interwar Polish-court records (1921-1939) beside BSSR courts of the same years.
- **The Litvak record:** check when Jews took a case to a rabbi and when to the state's Yiddish chambers.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether the Statutes or later codes treated Tatar parties differently; few studies.
- **The Homiel south and the resettled:** read compensation claims and refusals; start with district social-protection offices.
- **Orthodox, Uniate and Old Believer churches:** compare how each paints and preaches the Last Judgment.
- **Roma:** check how often Roma appear as parties in court records; thin and slanted, so read critically.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes and old court books; mixed for statistics (some withdrawn after 2020); thin for Roma and Tatar cases.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian and Polish for the court books; Russian for imperial and present codes; Yiddish for the BSSR court chambers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political trials, executions and the genocide law are live issues; name no defendant or victim from current cases. Trust in courts and the spirit of the law go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none: codes, kopa records and Last Judgment art all stay in.

## 5. First queries
1. Find vol. 18 of the Vilna commission's *Akty* ("Акты о копных судах") in a digital library and list the districts its cases come from.
2. On court.gov.by, find the yearly judicial statistics and note the acquittal figures by year.
3. Search catalogs for "Историко-юридические материалы Витебской и Могилевской" and list volumes by town.
4. On pravo.by, open the Criminal Code and note the articles that allow the death penalty and the ages of responsibility.
5. Search "яўрэйскія камеры народных судоў БССР" and "Yiddish courts Soviet Belarus" for studies.
