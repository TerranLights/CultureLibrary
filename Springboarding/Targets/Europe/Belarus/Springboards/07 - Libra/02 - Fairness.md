*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna-area Poles and Catholics:** check how daughters' and sons' shares were divided under interwar Polish civil law against the BSSR's at the same time.
- **Minsk against the district towns:** compare complaints about short weight and price breaches; markets differ by size.
- **Chernobyl cleanup workers and Homiel-region evacuees:** read how out-of-turn rights are claimed and disputed in their own forums.
- **Rural against city school-leavers:** target places and exam exemptions; check who uses them, by region.
- **The Litvak record:** *hasoges-gvul* rulings, set beside the Christian town's price lists for the same towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether inheritance followed Islamic shares or the Statute's; very few sources.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina valley and Vietka:** check how community property and inheritance were divided; little in print.
- **Roma:** look for anything on dividing earnings within a family group; sources are almost absent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes and price rules; mixed for market practice; thin for minority inheritance and folk division.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for the 1566 code; Hebrew and Aramaic for responsa; Russian for decrees; Polish for interwar civil law.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** price controls and connections (*blat*) are touchy topics in the press; the spirit of the law goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. The 1566 chapters, the price decree and the responsa all stay open.
