# Belarus: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani; Old Belarusian and Latin for the Duchy's courts. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna-area Catholics and Poles:** read the faith law's preamble and registration cases from the Catholic side, especially for foreign-born priests.
- **Protestant congregations:** check registration refusals and whether a decision named the faith itself.
- **Roma in Mahilioŭ, Homiel and Brest:** look at police and court treatment through Roma organizations; official sources are slanted.
- **Navahrudak and Minsk against Vilnia (as Tribunal seats):** check whether cases differed when the Tribunal sat on Belarusian ground.
- **The Litvak record:** compare the lot-drawn kahal with the Christian town's elected council in the same towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether Tatar parties had their own judges or went to the general courts; scarce.
- **Minsk and the regional centers:** compare anticorruption cases by region in the Prosecutor General's releases.
- **Old Believers:** check how a priestless community picks its leader and settles disputes without favoring a family; thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes and election reports; mixed for corruption cases; thin for folk sources and Tatar and Old Believer practice.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for the Tribunal; Hebrew for kahal records; Russian for codes and court practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** election reports, the Roma detentions and recent corruption trials touch living people; cite reports, not individuals. Whether courts are trusted is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** none. Tribunal books, kahal lots and ODIHR reports are open sources.
