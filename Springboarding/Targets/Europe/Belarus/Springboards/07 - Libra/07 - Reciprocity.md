# Belarus: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna-area Catholics and Greek Catholics:** compare *vota* and vows to saints with Orthodox thank-offerings of towels and candles.
- **Polesian villages (*paliashuki*):** check slaughter-sharing and bread-lending rules against northern accounts from the Dvina valley.
- **Homiel-region resettled families:** read how help received after 1986 is described: as a debt, a gift or a right.
- **Minsk factory workers:** the mutual-aid fund, in union and enterprise records, against private lending among neighbors.
- **The Litvak record:** *hakhnoses-orkhim* and free-loan societies in yizkor books; check how far each reached across towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** alms and sacrificial meat in their own sources; check whether non-Muslim neighbors received a share.
- **Old Believers:** check rules on separate dishes for outsiders and how far they limit shared meals; few studies.
- **Roma and Russians of the east:** help exchanged within extended families; ask the families' own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the codes and decrees are easy to reach; votive and slaughter customs sit in scattered ethnography; minority mutual aid and Roma practice are barely recorded.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for grants; Yiddish and Hebrew for yizkor books; Polish for Catholic shrine records; Russian for regulations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sanctions and counter-sanctions are political; revenge proper goes to Scorpio, generosity to Leo.
- **Held for review:** none; grants, decrees and shrine offerings were all kept in scope.
