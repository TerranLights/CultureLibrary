# Belarus: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox and Catholic faithful:** set Forgiveness Sunday and Easter confession side by side as rites of making peace, and check church leaders' statements after August 2020.
- **Protestant congregations:** check who applies for alternative service and on what grounds; registration records are the start.
- **Hrodna-area Poles and Lithuanians by Astraviec:** check what "peace and quiet" in a mixed parish has meant for school and language disputes.
- **Women in Minsk and in district towns:** compare protective-order use and crisis calls by region; do not assume who stays silent.
- **The Litvak record:** *sholem-bayis* in rabbinic and memoir sources, and when it kept a marriage together at a cost.
- **Lipka Tatars and Roma:** accommodation with authorities as each describes it; scarce, so begin with their own organizations.
- **Older and younger cohorts:** test the war-memory saying by generation in surveys and interviews, without presuming a result.
- **Old Believers of Vietka and Braslaŭ:** check the record of flight rather than confrontation under persecution, from community sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the codes and church calendars are well documented; sayings and the reconciliation drink need dialect and memoir searches; how minorities accommodated power is barely studied.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian for the 1588 clause; Russian for procedure codes; Yiddish and Hebrew for *sholem-bayis* sources; Polish for parish customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2020 debates, protest essays and domestic violence touch people at risk; cite essays and laws, not private persons. The saying about war is a stereotype trap: use it only as a hypothesis to test.
- **Held for review:** none. The 1588 clause, the service law, Forgiveness Sunday and Makajonak's play are all clear of the standing rules.
