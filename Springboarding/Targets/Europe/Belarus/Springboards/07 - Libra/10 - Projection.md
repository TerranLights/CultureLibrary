# Belarus: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brest region and Polesia:** read the 1937 pogrom records beside village memory of Jewish neighbors in the same district.
- **Hrodna-area Poles:** check how they read the September 17 holiday and the old *pan* image in schools.
- **The Litvak record:** blood-libel charges and pogrom memory as told in yizkor books, apart from state accounts.
- **Lipka Tatars:** their own histories against the raider of legend; few sources, so start with Tatar scholars.
- **Roma:** check how often Roma are named as culprits in rumor and press; read critically and seek Roma voices.
- **Minsk against Viciebsk and Mahilioŭ:** compare who is cited as a threat in regional newspapers since 2020.
- **Protestant and other minority congregations:** check whether "sect" labels in state and church writing cast them as outsiders.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the east and south:** check how images of each neighbor changed after 2014 and 2022.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for laws, purges and propaganda archives; mixed for folklore of the other; thin for hate-crime data and minority voices.
- **Languages needed:** Old Belarusian and Polish for witch trials; Polish for the 1937 records; German and Belarusian for occupation press; Yiddish and Hebrew for yizkor books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the extremist list itself can make citing a source risky inside Belarus; ethnic slurs are recorded as speech, never repeated as description. Revenge and betrayal go to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none. Witch trials, the 1930 case, the 1937 records and the occupation press raise care, not a standing rule.
