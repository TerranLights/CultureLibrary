## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox and Catholic families:** set Radaŭnica beside All Souls and see where mixed villages keep both.
- **Greek Catholics:** look for a separate memory of the dead after 1839, parish by parish.
- **Hrodna-region Poles:** compare *Zaduszki* candles and Polish epitaphs with Belarusian graves nearby.
- **Polesia (*paliashuki*):** test whether Polesian laments and grave customs stand apart from northern ones.
- **Old Believers (Braslaŭ lakes, Dvina valley):** priestless burial and cemeteries kept to themselves.
- **The Homiel south and the resettled:** who tends graves in emptied villages, and how they are reached.
- **Lipka Tatars (Navahrudak, Iŭje, Slonim):** Muslim burial against church rites; little in print, so ask the mosques' own historians.
- **The Litvak record:** burial and mourning in town records, read on their own.
- **Funerals of smaller groups:** Roma, Baptist and Pentecostal, Astraviec Lithuanian and southern Ukrainian burials, each kept as its own case.
- **Minsk against villages:** what the city funeral firm takes over from the family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on folk rites, war graves and mortality; mixed on inheritance practice and sex ways; thin on Roma, Old Believer and Tatar funerals.
