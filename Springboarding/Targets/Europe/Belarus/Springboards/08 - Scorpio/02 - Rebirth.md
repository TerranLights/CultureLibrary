# Belarus: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian, in the standard and the *Taraškievica* spellings; Russian as used in Belarus; *trasianka*; Hrodna Polish; West Polesian; Yiddish for the past; Arabic-script Belarusian of the Lipka Tatars; Romani. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholics of the Hrodna region and Orthodox of the east:** check how each counts adult baptism and confirmation in the 1990s.
- **Greek Catholics:** the refounded parishes; ask who joined from Orthodoxy, Catholicism or no faith.
- **Protestants (Baptist, Pentecostal):** look for conversion testimony and adult baptism as their own record.
- **Old Believers of the Viciebsk region and Braslaŭ:** check how converts from other churches are received; scarce print.
- **Polesia (*paliashuki*):** look for where healing "rebirth" rites were recorded and by whom.
- **The Homiel south and the resettled:** whether new villages are spoken of as a fresh start or as an exile.
- **Navahrudak and the Tatars:** how mosque rebuilding and teaching were begun again; a handful of authors only.
- **The Litvak record and today's Jewish communities:** keep the historic *bal-tshuve* apart from post-Soviet returners.
- **Roma:** look for any Roma congregation or mission in Belarus and its own testimony; assume nothing.
- **Minsk:** the IT retraining wave is mostly urban; compare district towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church history and postwar rebuilding; mixed for recovery; thin for Tatar, Roma and Old Believer conversion.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for Sirotka and Smatrycki; Church Slavonic for Kiryla; German for Bohn; Hebrew and Yiddish terms for return to observance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recovery and prison faith touch private lives; Greek Catholic history touches the 1839 abolition; the IT exodus after 2020 is diaspora comparison only.
- **Held for review:** none. Sirotka, Smatrycki and the prison chapels raise no rule question.
