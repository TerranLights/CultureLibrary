# Belarus: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard; *Taraškievica*), Russian in its Belarusian use, *trasianka*, Polish around Hrodna, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic letters, Romani. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk and the old party cities (Viciebsk, Mahilioŭ):** compare purge files by town; the eastern BSSR had two decades of Soviet rule before the west.
- **The Hrodna region and the Polish border:** the 1939-1941 and 1944-1946 removals, and how Poles who stayed tell it.
- **The Homiel south and the resettled:** decontamination workers against families whose houses were buried.
- **Polesia (*paliashuki*):** whether fright-pouring and other cures survive, and who still practices them.
- **Catholics and Orthodox:** fasting and confession as cleansing, in two calendars.
- **Old Believers:** what counts as defiling contact with the "world," and where Braslaŭ and Vietka communities draw the line.
- **The Litvak record:** mikvah, *kheyrem* and the washing of the dead as separate purity domains.
- **Lipka Tatars:** ablution and the fast; few sources beyond community publications.
- **Roma:** whether purity rules are kept here at all is unknown; ask Romani community groups.
- **State employees and private-sector workers:** post-2020 dismissals hit them differently; compare.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Stalinist repression and Chernobyl cleanup; mixed for post-2020 dismissals; thin for folk cures and minority purity rules.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for party files and decrees; Polish for 1944-1946 records; Old Belarusian and Latin for 1495 documents; Yiddish and Hebrew for mikvah and ban texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** KGB case files stay largely closed; naming people dismissed or disbarred after 2020 can put them at risk, so use aggregate reports only. Criminal law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. The repression register and the Ministry list are hard topics but not set aside under any rule.
