## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mahilioŭ-Dnieper against Hrodna parishes:** read the 1897 tables before any survey, to see how old the east-west contrast is.
- **Greek Catholics:** a small revived church; map where its parishes sit.
- **Old Believers (Viciebsk and the Dvina, Braslaŭ lakes, the Homiel south):** keep priestly and priestless groups apart; records are held locally.
- **Brest-region Protestants:** separate Baptist, Pentecostal and the older Calvinist layer.
- **Lipka Tatars (Iŭje, Navahrudak, Slonim, Minsk):** a Muslim landscape with its own sources.
- **The Litvak record:** Misnagdic, Hasidic and secular strands, town by town.
- **Hrodna Poles and Astraviec Lithuanians:** in which language each parish prays, and since when.
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*), Roma, and secular Minsk:** test each against the folk calendar, church life and unbelief.
- **Chernobyl resettlers:** whether shrines and parish ties moved with them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** church histories and the Jewish town record are plentiful; present-day belief rests on surveys alone; Roma, Old Believer and Tatar religious life is barely in print.
- **Lands (said once, here):** the folder has no Stage 0 file, and the tracker carries the old lands without change; every Sagittarius sheet therefore looks at present-day Belarus and draws no unit or border.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Church Slavonic for census and church records; Polish for Čačot; Hebrew and Yiddish behind *Pinkas ha-kehilot*; Arabic script for the Iŭje community's books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet persecution of believers, church-state ties today and the March 25 date are politically charged. Religion law goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the domain's Muslim and Jewish pointers fall outside the standing rules.
