# Belarus: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and classical *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viciebsk-Dvina Orthodox and Hrodna Catholics:** compare attendance and prayer items region by region, not only the label people give.
- **Greek Catholics:** how a faith suppressed in 1839 and revived after 1990 is passed on; few parishes, so count each.
- **Old Believers around Braslaŭ and in the Homiel south:** whether lay-led prayer without priests changes how faith is kept at home.
- **Brest-region Baptists and Pentecostals:** conversion stories against inherited Orthodoxy; their own church histories.
- **Lipka Tatars:** ask how practice is taught where the community is small and scattered (Navahrudak, Iŭje, Slonim).
- **Jews of Hrodna, Minsk and Babrujsk:** read synagogue minute books of before 1941 apart from what small congregations publish now.
- **Hrodna Poles:** whether Polish-language worship and faith are tied together for them; check generations apart.
- **Roma:** check whether evangelical churches have Roma congregations; little is published.
- **Minsk against villages:** the urban self-described believer who seldom attends, against rural feast-day practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Church records and literature give plenty; the measured share of practice rests on a few surveys; Roma and Tatar faith practice will need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for the Brest Bible and Hrodna parishes; Russian for diocesan gazettes and Soviet files; Hebrew for synagogue records; Arabic-script Belarusian for the *iman* passages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet-era files name informers and clergy; current church-state ties and believers' political stances in 2020 are delicate. Religious law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; every faith named on this sheet lies within the standing rules.
