# Belarus: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian (Belarusian usage), *trasianka*, Hrodna Polish, West Polesian (Brest region), Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar kitab Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the road, the foreign and the traveler are regarded (short trips and commuting stay in Gemini).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Litvak emigrants and Belarusian peasants:** the Hamburg lists hold both; compare towns of origin and family patterns.
- **Viciebsk-Dvina and Hrodna regions:** check where seasonal earners went from each (Riga against Warsaw and the west).
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*):** marsh travel by boat and the Pinsk market; separate river and road villages.
- **Orthodox and Catholic pilgrims:** compare diocesan travel offers by destination.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether any hajj accounts exist, old or recent; ask the Muslim community first.
- **Roma:** what Roma organizations say of past travel routes, in their own words, beyond the 1956 settlement already covered elsewhere.
- **Old Believer families near Braslaŭ and along the Dvina:** collect their own accounts of the forebears' arrival, as migrants seeking refuge.
- **People resettled from the Homiel south:** the forced move as a journey; check oral histories.
- **Lithuanians of Astraviec and Hrodna Poles:** cross-border family visits and the rules that shape them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** emigration records and Soviet travel are well documented; Tatar and Roma journeys are thinly recorded; post-2020 emigration is out of scope except as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Hamburg lists; Polish for Syrokomla; Church Slavonic for the 1522 traveler's book; Yiddish for emigrant letters; Russian for *vachta* forums.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** border statistics after 2020 and travel bans on some citizens are political; diaspora material counts only as comparison. Freedom of movement is in sheet 03.
- **Held for review:** none; the travel pointers raise no standing-rule concern.
