# Belarus: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard; *Taraškievica*), Russian as written and spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna Polish, Brest-region West Polesian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical and current), the Lipka Tatars' Arabic-script Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Priestly and priestless Old Believer groups:** each keeps a different degree of separation from outsiders; read each group's own rules.
- **Brest-region Baptists, Pentecostals and charismatics:** compare their statements of faith; the label "sect" comes from outside.
- **Orthodox Mahilioŭ and Viciebsk:** check whether strict parish movements exist, and how bishops speak of them.
- **Catholic Hrodna:** check for traditionalist currents and how the diocese meets them.
- **Greek Catholics:** see whether restoration of the pre-1839 rite is framed as fidelity or as revival.
- **Litvak Jews:** set Misnagdic, Hasidic and post-1991 Chabad strictness apart, each in its own sources.
- **Lipka Tatars and newer Muslims:** the "traditional" and "new" labels each side uses need careful, neutral handling.
- **Minsk secular townspeople:** check what "fanatic" means to them, and whom they apply it to.
- **Roma evangelical congregations:** check whether any exist and how they are described; no source is known.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** church polemics and imperial records are plentiful; Soviet atheism is archived; current strict groups are documented mostly by their critics, which skews the record.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Zizanij; Hebrew and Yiddish for Wasserman; Polish for the antitrinitarian expulsion; Arabic-script Belarusian for *bidat* debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "sect" and "extremist" are legal and political labels in Belarus that can harm small communities; quote their own statements first. Registration and extremism law go to Libra and Capricorn; control goes to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** one pointer set aside under the South Asian rule (a new religious movement often labeled a sect); category only: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
