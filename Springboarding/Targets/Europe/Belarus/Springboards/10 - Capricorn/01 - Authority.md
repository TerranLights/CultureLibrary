# Belarus: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Church Slavonic for the oldest texts, Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viciebsk-region and Polatsk Orthodox parishes against Hrodna Catholic ones:** compare whose word settles a dispute in the parish, priest or lay committee.
- **Greek Catholics:** check how memory of 1839 shapes their view of church command today.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina and Braslaŭ lakes:** check how a community that once refused the state church treats state orders.
- **The Litvak towns:** compare the crown rabbi's standing in Minsk with that in small Polesian towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check how the imam's word and the state's are weighed in Iŭje and Navahrudak.
- **The Homiel south and the resettled:** compare trust in doctors, physicists and officials in contaminated districts.
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*), Roma, Protestants of the Brest region:** for each, find who is asked first when trouble comes.
- **Soviet-schooled and post-2000 cohorts:** check whether trust in the militia and the army splits more by age than by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** church, Jewish and Chernobyl sources are deep; trust polls are rich until 2016 and patchy after; Roma and Tatar views of authority are scarcely published.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Kiryla; Russian for the 1839 council papers and the 1989 dose debate; Yiddish and Russian for crown-rabbi files; Polish for the interwar Muftiate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** obedience to police and the state is the most charged subject since 2020; quote no current protester or officer, and treat opinion polls run inside the country after 2020 with care.
- **Held for review:** none; neither the rabbinic, Tatar nor church-council pointers came under a standing rule.
