# Belarus: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), the Tatars' Arabic-script Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic gentry families of Hrodna against Orthodox clergy families of Mahilioŭ:** compare how each kept its rank closed.
- **Greek Catholics:** check what the Latin-rite pull did to family standing.
- **The Litvak record:** compare *balebatim*, scholars and laborers; check memoirs from Minsk and from small Polesian towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check how *murza* families stood toward ordinary Tatar soldiers and farmers.
- **Interwar Polish-ruled villages (Brest, Navahrudak):** compare *batraki*, settlers and landholders.
- **Russians and Ukrainians of the Homiel south:** check whether rank followed origin in factory and farm posts.
- **Old Believers, Protestants:** check how far each community keeps its own internal ranks.
- **Roma groups in Belarus:** check how groups name and rank one another; outsiders' labels are unreliable.
- **Minsk and the districts:** compare how city and village describe each other's place.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state and church rank records; mixed for class self-placement; thin for Roma and for the inside of Old Believer and Tatar communities.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for rite change; Russian and Church Slavonic for consistory and imperial files; English and Hebrew for the Pinsk history; Russian prison argot for the caste reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison ranks involve degrading labels and living people; describe the system only and name no one. The nomenklatura's heirs may still hold posts.
- **Held for review:** empty; no estate, clergy or prison-rank pointer was moved here.
