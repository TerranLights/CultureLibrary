# Belarus: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Latin for the oldest record, Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of Hrodna, West Polesian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Tatar Arabic-script Belarusian. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polatsk and the Viciebsk region:** check how local writing uses Skaryna's rise as a model.
- **The Litvak record:** compare reward by learning with reward by wealth in memoirs from Minsk, Pinsk and Mir.
- **Homiel and Mahilioŭ farm villages:** check whether brigade leaders' relatives were credited with more workdays, as members sometimes claimed.
- **Brest-region Protestants:** check whether talk of grace changes how members speak of earned success.
- **Hrodna Catholics, Orthodox and Greek Catholic families:** compare teaching on merit and reward.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether *sawab* appears in everyday talk or only in the books.
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*) and Old Believers:** ask whether a bride's weaving was judged openly and by whom.
- **Roma and Lithuanians around Astraviec:** sources are thin; ask schools and community bodies what counts as earned.
- **Athletes from district towns and from Minsk clubs:** compare routes to the top grades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** title lists, grade tables and farm books are abundant; opinion on effort against pull is sparse; Tatar, Roma and Old Believer ideas of desert are scarcely in print.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Padua; Yiddish and Hebrew for *kest* memoirs; Russian for contracts and civil-service hiring; Belarusian for dowry ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** connections at the top of the state are a dangerous subject to research inside the country; use published sources only.
- **Held for review:** blank for this sheet; Padua, guild and *kest* sources all remain.
