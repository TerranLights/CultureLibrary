# Belarus: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Old Belarusian, Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-area Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Lipka Tatar Belarusian in Arabic script, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages of the Minsk and Viciebsk provinces after 1861:** compare custom in volost courts with the imperial code.
- **The Litvak record:** compare rabbis who stressed obedience to the state with those who set limits.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check how kitabs place religious law beside the state's.
- **Old Believers of the Dvina and Braslaŭ lakes:** check whether some refused state papers on religious grounds, and when.
- **Hrodna Catholics and Brest-region Protestants:** compare church teaching on conscience against civil law.
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*):** look for "God's law" set beside the state's in expedition records.
- **Roma:** check with community workers whether disputes are settled inside families before police are called.
- **Soviet-trained and post-2000 jurists:** compare how each generation states the purpose of law in memoir and interview.
