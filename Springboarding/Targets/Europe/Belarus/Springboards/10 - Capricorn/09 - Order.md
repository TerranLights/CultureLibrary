# Belarus: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian and its classical spelling, Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Polish (Hrodna parishes), West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), the Lipka Tatars' Arabic-script Belarusian. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna and Navahrudak gentry districts:** check how kaptur courts worked in each district in an interregnum.
- **The Litvak record:** check who paid for and inspected the eyruv wire, and what happened when it broke before a Sabbath.
- **Hrodna Catholics and Poles:** compare the order of processions with Orthodox church feasts in the same town.
- **Western villages in 1939 and eastern villages in 1941:** compare the two breakdowns of order in memoirs.
- **The Homiel south and families resettled after 1986:** check how new settlements set up their own order.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether *adab* guides daily routine, in community writing.
- **Old Believers of the Braslaŭ lakes and Protestants of the Brest region:** check house rules and church order in each.
- **Roma:** check how settled Roma families describe the order of their streets, in their own words.
- **Apartment blocks against village streets:** check whether shared space is kept by residents, the housing office or no one.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for planning, church ritual and wartime memoirs; mixed for perceived safety; thin for the inner order of small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Old Belarusian for kaptur records; Yiddish and Hebrew for eyruv matters; Russian for Interior Ministry and riot-case sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "mass disorder" cases involve current prisoners; cite published sources only and name no defendant. The letter of public-order law is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** nothing; order, riot and evacuation leads all remain in the table.
