# Belarus: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-borderland Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar Arabic-script Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for: friends, groups, hopes and humanitarian ideals, with settlement, migration, association and affiliation (social ways) as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna Catholics and Poles against Mahilioŭ villages:** check whether the parish, a club or a school society carries group life in each.
- **Polesians (*paliashuki*):** map what *hromada* covers in Brest-region marsh villages, meeting by meeting.
- **Minsk tower blocks against district towns:** note where neighbors meet and who is counted among them.
- **Old Believers and Greek Catholics:** read their own accounts of joining and leaving next to the state counts.
- **Lipka Tatars:** find how a congregation reckons members spread over distant villages.
- **The Litvak record:** sort the voluntary *khevres* from the *kehile* that housed them.
- **Lithuanians near Astraviec:** trace how the plant entered local hopes and fears.
- **Homiel-south resettlers:** set the community they left beside the one they were placed in.
- **Roma, Protestants, southern Ukrainians:** locate who keeps each group's records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** movements, shtetl fellowships and village gatherings are well documented; survey runs are patchy; Roma and Tatar group life has few records of its own.
- **Lands:** Belarus has no scope file yet, and its tracker lists no change to its pre-war lands; every Aquarius sheet therefore covers today's republic and draws no new border.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for the 1928 trial; Yiddish and Hebrew for fellowship books; Russian for resettler files; Arabic script for congregation papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** many associations were closed after 2020, some labeled extremist; use published sources and name no living activist. Registration rules are Libra's.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the migration, movement and congregation rows were all kept.
