# Belarus: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian as used in Belarus, *trasianka*, Polish of the Hrodna parishes, West Polesian, Lithuanian (Astraviec and Voranava border villages), Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar *kitab* Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polesian marsh villages against Hrodna farm villages:** test whether help after a fire or flood came from kin, neighbors or the parish.
- **Hrodna Catholics and Poles:** weigh parish groups against street and workplace ties.
- **Brest-region Protestants:** compare their congregations' mutual help with that of Orthodox parishes nearby.
- **Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley:** follow villages from many households to a few, and ask who still comes.
- **Families resettled from the Homiel south:** trace whether former neighbors kept in touch after being scattered.
- **Lithuanians of the border villages:** see what a school or parish keeps together.
- **Lipka Tatars, Old Believers, Greek Catholics, Roma:** locate community records each keeps in its own hands.
- **The Litvak record:** compare the communal body with the street, the prayer house and the market.
- **Minsk new districts against Mahilioŭ old streets:** compare how long a newcomer stays a newcomer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** wartime and Chernobyl losses of community are documented in depth; present neighbor ties are measured only here and there; Tatar and Roma community life is thin in print.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for Federowski and the Rosary circles; Lithuanian for the Pialiasa school; Yiddish for Mendele; Russian for congregation histories and Onliner.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Protestant congregations and some Catholic clergy have faced official pressure; quote no member by name. The burned villages are a state memory site; read its framing as one view.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the parish, village, shtetl and housing-block rows all stand.
