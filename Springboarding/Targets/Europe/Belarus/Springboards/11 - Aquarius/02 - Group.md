# Belarus: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Russian of Belarusian workplaces, *trasianka*, Hrodna Polish, West Polesian, Lithuanian (border), Yiddish (historical), Old Polish and Arabic-script Belarusian for the Tatar record, Romani. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mahilioŭ and Dnieper villages against Polesian marsh villages:** ask which unit (the village end, the whole village, the kin) formed the side in fights and feuds.
- **Hrodna Poles:** follow how the 2005 split ran through families, parishes and schools.
- **Lipka Tatars:** trace whether the banner and the congregation drew the same line around members.
- **The Litvak record:** set strike-fund oaths beside the artisans' own prayer-house *minyanim*: two kinds of chosen group.
- **Greek Catholics (Uniates):** see what memory of 1839 is kept in Polack-region parishes.
- **Hrodna and Lida Polish youth against BRSM members in the same towns:** compare the promises each group asks of a newcomer.
- **Minsk factory staff against Homiel-south farm staff:** note where a work collective acts as one.
- **Astraviec Lithuanians, Braslaŭ Old Believers:** check which events make each close ranks.
- **Roma subgroups:** record the groups' own names before any outside label.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** war and labor records are deep; Tatar banners have a specialist literature; group behavior today is little measured; Roma groups barely documented.
- **Languages needed:** Old Polish and Latin for banner musters; Yiddish for strike-fund records; Polish for scouting and the 2005 split; Russian for union papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2020 strikers, closed unions and the unrecognized Polish union face prosecution; name no one. Wartime reprisals are told differently by Soviet, Polish and village sources; read several sides.
- **Held for review:** empty; fist-fight, banner, union and scouting leads all remain.
