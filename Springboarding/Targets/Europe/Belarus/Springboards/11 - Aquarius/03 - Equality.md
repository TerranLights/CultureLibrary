# Belarus: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian in its two spellings, Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of Hrodna's gentry hamlets, West Polesian, Lithuanian, Yiddish (historical), Old Belarusian and Latin for the Grand Duchy, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects. Which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna gentry hamlets against Polesian villages:** see whether gentry descent still marks who counts as equal at weddings and in parish seating.
- **The Litvak record:** follow how the 1920s Yiddish schools and papers of the BSSR were received in Minsk and Babrujsk.
- **Lipka Tatars:** compare Tatar gentry with Christian gentry in the dietine records.
- **Minsk against the Mahilioŭ and Viciebsk villages:** test whether city and village people describe each other as equals, by generation.
- **Roma:** collect Roma accounts of school and work alongside outside reports.
- **Baptists and Pentecostals of the Brest region, and Old Believers:** check whether "brother" and "sister" among members carry a teaching of equal standing.
- **Women in the Homiel south and in Minsk:** compare who holds farm and office posts.
- **Poles and Lithuanians of the border:** read how each minority describes its schools beside those of the majority.
- **Greek Catholics:** set parish memory of the 1839 change beside the status of their priests now.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the gentry, the 1920s nationality policy and Soviet memoirs; mixed for value surveys; thin for Roma and disability voices.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Belarusian for dietine records; Polish for zaścianek memoirs; Yiddish for 1920s school papers; Russian for privilege memoirs and surveys.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma reports and gender attitudes are charged; name no family. Equal rights as written in constitutions and codes are Libra's.
