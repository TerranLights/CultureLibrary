*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholics of the Polack and Brest lands:** weigh the 1720 changes against parish memory of older rites.
- **Old Believers against their Orthodox neighbors:** read how each speaks of the 17th-c. reform and of later Soviet campaigns against both.
- **The Litvak record:** set *maskilim* beside rabbinic and Hasidic opponents in the same towns.
- **Hrodna gentry families:** compare the Commission schools with Jesuit and Piarist schools they replaced.
- **Villages of the Viciebsk and Mahilioŭ regions:** ask how school closures were decided and felt.
- **Minsk parents:** note which families fought the twelve-year school, and why.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check what the interwar revival changed in teaching.
- **Polesians and the Homiel south:** record which Soviet rites took root and which were dropped after 1991.
- **Roma:** look for Roma accounts of reforms done to them, beside official ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Commonwealth church and school reforms and Soviet campaigns have deep records; attitude data are patchy; the Tatar revival and Roma views are hardly written up.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for the synod and Commission records; Church Slavonic and Russian for Old Believer polemics; Hebrew and Yiddish for the Haskalah; Russian for economic programs and IMF replies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** economic reform is a sharp political divide; quote programs and critics, not persons. Reforms written as statutes are Libra's.
- **Held for review:** no reform lead, whether church, school, ritual or economic, was withheld.
