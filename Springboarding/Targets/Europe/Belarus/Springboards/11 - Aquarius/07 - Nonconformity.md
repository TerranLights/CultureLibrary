## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hasidic Polesia and Viciebsk against Litvak Hrodna:** test which communities treated which as deviant, in each one's writings.
- **Old Believers of the Braslaŭ lakes:** see how a member returning from the city after years away is received.
- **Minsk scenes against district towns:** ask where a punk or hippie could live openly in the 1980s, and where not.
- **Hrodna Poles and Catholics:** check how youth who drop the parish are spoken of in Polish-language families.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check how members who marry out or stop praying are counted.
- **Brest-region Protestants:** look at how congregations treat members who leave.
- **Polesian and Mahilioŭ villages:** compare what villages tolerated of the bachelor, the widow and the eccentric.
- **Roma:** collect Roma accounts of members who left the community's ways.
- **Ukrainians of the south and Lithuanians of the border:** find whether keeping one's language reads as conformity at home and difference outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Soviet reports and Jewish conflict literature are rich; folklore of misrule is scattered; recent dissent is documented mainly by monitors abroad.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Yiddish for the 1772 ban; Russian for Komsomol reports and psychiatry memoirs; Belarusian for *Bum-Bam-Lit* and the label debates; Polish for parish talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2020 dress cases and dissidents' memoirs involve people still at risk; name no one. Religious and political dissent overlaps with Sagittarius's conviction sheet.
- **Held for review:** none; dissent, scene, label and misrule leads remain open.
