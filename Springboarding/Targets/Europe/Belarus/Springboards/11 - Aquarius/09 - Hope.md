# Belarus: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica*), Old Belarusian and Church Slavonic for early inscriptions, Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna-parish Polish, West Polesian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Lipka Tatar *kitab* Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley:** see how the Boris stones are used in local talk of an old and lasting faith.
- **Hrodna Catholics and Poles against Orthodox parishes nearby:** compare *Roraty* with Orthodox Advent and Christmas-eve waiting.
- **The Litvak record:** set New Year wishes on cards beside what memoirs say families actually hoped for.
- **Families of the Homiel south:** compare hopes for returning land with fears in the same villages.
- **Minsk IT workers against farm workers in the Mahilioŭ region:** check whose future the "IT country" idea included.
- **Lipka Tatars:** trace where "if God wills" appears in kitab prayers and in family speech today.
- **Brest-region Protestants:** note how hope for the life to come is preached.
- **Navahrudak and Naliboki villages:** ask how the bison's return is spoken of locally.
- **Roma and Old Believers:** find each community's own words for hope and patience, where recorded.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** state programs, children's wishes and folklore are easy to find; hope after 2020 is studied mostly from abroad; Tatar and Roma hopes are barely written down.
- **Languages needed:** the stones are in Old Belarusian Cyrillic; *Roraty* material is Polish; the New Year cards mix Hebrew and Yiddish; poll, IT and land-return sources are mostly Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hopes tied to the 2020 protests and the moved Independence Day are politically charged; cite published sources and name no living person. Despair and suicide need care.
- **Held for review:** no hope pointer was set aside; protest-era hopes are reached through published polls, not dropped.
