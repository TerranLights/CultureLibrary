# Belarus: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (official spelling and *Taraškievica*), Russian as Belarusians speak it, *trasianka*, the Polish of the Hrodna region, Brest-region West Polesian, Yiddish for the Jewish past, Belarusian in Arabic script from the Tatar *kitabs*, Romani. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western Catholic and eastern Orthodox parishes:** set the prayers asking mercy beside the deeds each expects after them.
- **Greek Catholics and Protestants (Baptists, Pentecostals):** look for their charity ministries and how they choose whom to serve.
- **Minsk against Homiel-region villages:** see whether help to a stranger goes through a fund, a church or one's own hands.
- **Polesians:** check the beggar-and-lodger customs remembered in the marsh villages near Pinsk.
- **The Litvak record:** read the poor-relief societies against the town's own memoirs of hunger.
- **Lipka Tatars:** find out whether help to the poor runs through the community or the family.
- **Roma:** look for Romani-led accounts of how they were helped or refused; outside reports alone mislead.
- **People resettled after Chernobyl:** compare how they speak of help received from the state and from neighbors.
- **Lithuanian villages by Astraviec, and the Hrodna Poles:** find out whether each keeps a separate aid list for its own people.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** confraternity and *khevre* papers survive in quantity; welfare figures are partial; Roma and Tatar acts of mercy are seldom written down.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Old Belarusian for Skaryna and the brotherhoods; Hebrew and Yiddish for society books; Polish for the Mercy cult; German for POW camp files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2017 dispute and later charity rules carry political weight; animal-catching footage is graphic. Mercy in sentencing goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** empty for this concept; the brotherhood, *khevre*, kitab and welfare pointers all qualify.
