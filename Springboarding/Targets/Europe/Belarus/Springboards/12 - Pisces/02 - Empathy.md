# Belarus: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (both the official norm and *Taraškievica*), Belarusian-variety Russian, *trasianka*, Polish around Hrodna, West Polesian (Brest region), Yiddish (pre-1941 communities), Tatar Belarusian written in Arabic letters, Romani. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna-region Poles and Catholics:** check whether Orzeszkowa is read locally as a model of seeing across faith lines.
- **The Litvak record:** compare Hasidic (Liozna, Liady) and *misnagdic* (Valožyn) texts on feeling for another.
- **Orthodox villages of the Mahilioŭ region and Minsk office workers:** test whether "entering someone's situation" is used of kin only or of strangers too.
- **Homiel-south resettlers and their hosts:** hear both sides of the same move.
- **Ukrainians of the south:** ask how families with kin across the border speak of the war's other side.
- **Lipka Tatars:** look for any Tatar-language or kitab term before importing an outside one.
- **Old Believers near Braslaŭ:** check how a closed community describes understanding outsiders.
- **Protestants:** their counseling ministries may use imported empathy training; trace its sources.
- **Roma:** seek Romani writers' or activists' own words on being misjudged, since outside descriptions dominate.
- **Polesians:** note dialect idioms for "hearing someone out."

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** literature and psychology theses are plentiful; measured empathy is spotty; Tatar and Roma sources are almost absent.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Yiddish for Reyzen and the *Tanya*; Polish for Orzeszkowa; Russian for most psychology work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Ukrainian war is politically charged in Belarus, and public statements about it can carry risk; use published sources and keep individuals anonymous.
- **Held for review:** for empathy the list is clean; Hasidic, fiction and survey leads were all kept.
