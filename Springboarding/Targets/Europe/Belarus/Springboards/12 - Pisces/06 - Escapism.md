# Belarus: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian in both spellings (official and *Taraškievica*), Russian as heard in Belarus, *trasianka*, Hrodna Polish, the West Polesian speech of the south-west, Yiddish (communities destroyed in 1941-1944), the Lipka Tatars' Arabic-script Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minsk students and Homiel-region villages:** compare screens and fandom against drink and fishing as the weekend way out.
- **Orthodox and Catholic parishes:** check which teach sobriety vows, and in which language.
- **Protestants (Baptists, Pentecostals):** look at their teetotal rule as a line against escape.
- **Lipka Tatars:** check whether their communities treat alcohol differently from neighbors, from their own sources.
- **The Litvak record:** sort the Yiddish pulp-reading and the tavern-keeping record into separate files.
- **Polesians:** look at river and forest as everyday refuges, apart from tourism.
- **Viciebsk and the Braslaŭ lakes:** check lakeside summer houses and who comes from the city.
- **People resettled after Chernobyl:** set talk of drinking and leaving beside their own accounts of loss.
- **Roma:** look for Romani-led sources before using outside reports on gambling or drink.
- **Old Believers:** check their rules on drink, smoking and television, as they state them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** drink and drug statistics exist but are patchy; reading and travel data are scattered; fandom is almost unstudied.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for health data and media research; Yiddish for Shomer and the critics; Belarusian for Kupala and folk songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug offenses carry heavy sentences and families campaign about them; addiction stories must stay anonymous. Penalties go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** this list keeps all thirteen escape leads; none fell under a rule.
