*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (the post-1959 standard and pre-1933 *Taraškievica*), Russian in Belarusian use, *trasianka*, Polish as prayed and spoken near Hrodna, West Polesian, Yiddish and Hebrew for the Hasidic and Litvak past, Tatar Belarusian in Arabic letters, Romani. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside the church.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polack and the Viciebsk lands:** check how the Euphrosyne cult shapes local devotion apart from official ceremonies.
- **Hrodna-region Catholics and Poles:** compare silent adoration, charismatic prayer and pilgrim vows.
- **Mahilioŭ and the Dnieper villages:** look for home prayer kept by elderly women where parishes were closed for decades.
- **Polesians of the Brest region:** record sacred trees, springs and stones still visited, and who tends them.
- **Greek Catholics:** check how they recover Eastern prayer forms in Belarusian.
- **Old Believers:** describe priestless prayer in their own published words.
- **The Litvak record:** keep Hasidic devotion (Liachavičy, Karlin, Kobryn) apart from Litvak study-centered piety.
- **Lipka Tatars:** collect what their community writes on prayer beyond the mosque.
- **Protestants:** gather their own testimonies of the Spirit's gifts in the Brest and Minsk regions.
- **The Chernobyl-affected south:** look at shrines raised in emptied villages and who returns to them.
- **Roma:** ask Romani church members and leaders about their own devotions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** church devotion and Hasidic writing fill libraries; lay and home prayer is recorded unevenly; neo-pagan and Tatar devotion have few studies.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew for Hasidic sources; Church Slavonic for chant books; Polish for Catholic renewal; Russian for most sociology.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** unregistered groups and new movements may be under pressure; name no member; treat Tatar and Roma devotions as their own to share.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a category only; the withheld item was a lead on Minsk studios teaching a meditation and body practice from that region.
