# Belarus: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (standard and *Taraškievica* spellings), Russian as spoken in Belarus, *trasianka*, the Polish of Hrodna and the western borderland, West Polesian, Yiddish (historical), Lipka Tatar *kitab* Belarusian, Romani. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. The letter of the law goes to Libra and its spirit to Capricorn; this sheet keeps to the moral and social side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hrodna region and the Polish border:** compare how smuggling is remembered in Polish- and Belarusian-language accounts.
- **Viciebsk region:** see how the serial-murder case is retold locally, and by whom.
- **Bychaŭ and the Dnieper villages:** set the annalist's verdicts beside 19th-c. ethnographers' notes on how villages treated a thief.
- **Polesians and the Brest region:** check forest and marsh takings (game, wood, fish) as wrongs or as rights.
- **Catholics, Orthodox and Greek Catholics:** set the confession lists side by side, sin by sin.
- **The Litvak record:** check how memoirs speak of Jewish thieves and of Jews accused, in the same towns.
- **Lipka Tatars:** look at whether the grave-sins lists are still taught.
- **Roma:** record how Roma answer the label of thief, from Romani-led sources only.
- **Protestants:** gather prison-conversion testimonies their churches publish.
- **Minsk and the Homiel south:** check which wrongs (drug dealing, theft, fraud) the press links to each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** confession manuals, folk tales and the press are plentiful; victim surveys are lacking; postwar underground records are archived and contested.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for Piasecki and Catholic manuals; Church Slavonic for Uniate prayer books; Russian for Soviet records and TV.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the postwar underground is politically charged; state TV crime shows may expose private people; name no living offender or victim. Criminal law and procedure go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nil; the chronicle, smuggling, kitab and underground leads were all usable.
