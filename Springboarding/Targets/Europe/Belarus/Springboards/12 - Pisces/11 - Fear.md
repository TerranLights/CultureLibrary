# Belarus: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Belarus, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belarusian (official spelling and *Taraškievica*), Russian of Belarusian use, *trasianka*, the Hrodna region's Polish, Brest-region West Polesian, Yiddish (pre-1941), the Arabic-lettered Belarusian of Lipka Tatar books, Romani. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polesians of the Brest region and the Homiel south:** compare bogeymen of water and marsh with forest ones further north.
- **Viciebsk region and the Dvina valley:** map where werewolf tales are recorded, district by district.
- **Polish-speaking Catholics around Hrodna:** test whether the *upiór* (revenant) or the werewolf dominates their night stories.
- **People resettled after Chernobyl:** record how invisible danger is spoken of, by generation.
- **Minsk:** collect urban fears (metro, tower blocks) apart from village ones.
- **The Litvak record:** read Yiddish memoirs for fear of pogrom and conscription, kept apart from fear of Heaven.
- **Lipka Tatars:** look at whether Tatar villagers shared their Christian neighbors' night fears.
- **Old Believers and Greek Catholics:** check their preaching on the end times.
- **Roma:** look for Romani-led accounts of what they feared on the road and from officials.
- **Lithuanians near Astraviec:** note fears tied to the new plant in their own press.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** folk fear lore is rich; anxiety studies exist but are small; national safety data are thin and partly politicized.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for psychology and school material; Polish for *upiór* tales; Hebrew and Yiddish for fear of Heaven; Arabic letters for Tatar texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fear of the state is recent and live; oral histories of arrests involve families; poll-fear research is itself contested. Crime fear sits partly in Crime.
- **Held for review:** fear, too, is complete without exclusions.
