# Bulgaria: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Shop, Rhodope and Balkan dialect forms), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia and the Shopluk**: check Shop jokes as a genre of quarrel, separating what Shops tell from what outsiders tell.
- **Rhodope villages, Christian and Pomak**: check who speaks for a household or village to outsiders and how a dispute is carried to the mayor.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie**: check how memory of 1984-89 resistance appears in Turkish-language media and personal accounts of speaking up.
- **Pomak villages of the western Rhodopes** (e.g. Ribnovo, Kornitsa *check*): check how accounts of the 1970s renamings record refusal and its price.
- **Roma groups** (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Pentecostal and Muslim congregations): check who speaks in a congregation or before a council, and in which language.
- **Alevi Babai and Kızılbaş communities**: check the ordered speaking of their communal rites against everyday claim-making.
- **Paulician Catholic villages near Plovdiv**: check parish records for disputes with clergy as a measure of voice within a minority church.
- **Armenians and Gagauz of the Black Sea coast and Dobruja**: check community bulletins for how small groups press claims; sources thin.
- **Generations**: check pre-1989 party-meeting speech against post-1989 workplaces and online talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for folklore and protest, thin for everyday conversation and small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, including dialect corpora for the Shopluk; Turkish for the 1984-89 record; Romani for church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 'Revival Process' protests, the 1989 mass departure to Turkey, officially called "the Big Excursion" (*Голямата екскурзия*, a contested euphemism) and the Pomak renamings involve deaths and living witnesses; State Security files (COMDOS) need careful handling. Satire about pushy types risks being read as a stereotype.
- **Held for review:** none; the legal side of consumer and ombudsman complaints is routed to Libra rather than held.
