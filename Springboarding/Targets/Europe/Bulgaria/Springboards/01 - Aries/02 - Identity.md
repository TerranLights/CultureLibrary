## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bulgarian Orthodox of the Shopluk, Upper Thrace, the Balkan towns and the Danubian Plain**: check whether people introduce themselves first by village, region or nation.
- **Pirin region (Blagoevgrad)**: check how regional, Bulgarian and Macedonian self-descriptions are recorded, a contested question.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region, the Ludogorie and Dobruja**: check self-names and how family migration to Turkey after 1989 enters identity talk (the emigrants themselves are comparison only).
- **Pomaks of the Rhodopes and the Chepino valley**: check each available label and who uses it; the record includes state-imposed names.
- **Roma groups**: check which groups name themselves Roma, Turks, Romanians (Rudari) or other, and why census and self-description diverge.
- **Alevi Kızılbaş and Babai**: check whether faith or Turkish language comes first in self-description.
- **Paulician Catholics, Protestants, Armenians, Jews**: check how religious and national identity are combined in each community's own writings.
- **Karakachans, Aromanians, Gagauz, Lipovans, Crimean Tatars of Dobruja**: check what their associations print about origin; sources thin.
- **Descendants of Thracian and Macedonian refugees**: check family societies and memorial days as identity work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich at the national level (much scholarship and politics), contested for Pomaks, Turks and Roma, thin for the smallest communities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, Ottoman Turkish (Arabic script) for the *defters*, modern Turkish, Romani, Judeo-Spanish, Greek for Karakachan sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic labels, the Macedonian question, the 'Revival Process', and 1943 (rescue inside the old borders, deportation from the occupied lands) are live political issues; present each position as a position. Census ethnicity data is self-declared and voluntary.
- **Held for review:** none; every identity pointer above stays in play pending its check.
