# Bulgaria: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Balkan and Strandzha speech), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Armenian, Judeo-Spanish (historical). **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stara Planina and Sredna Gora towns** (Koprivshtitsa, Panagyurishte, Batak in the western Rhodopes): check local museums and memoirs of 1876 at the uprising's main sites.
- **Strandzha villages**: check *nestinari* families and their own accounts against folklorists' descriptions.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana)**: check memory of the 1923 September Uprising, a different and contested courage story.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie**: check Turkish-language folk heroes and accounts of the 1980s resistance, in their own words.
- **Pomaks of the Rhodopes**: check local legends and memoirs that tell refusal or endurance under conversion and renaming campaigns, without one reading imposed.
- **Alevi Babai and Kızılbaş communities**: check saints' legends and shrine rites for their own picture of courage.
- **Roma groups**: check Roma soldiers' and partisans' records and community memory; little is published, so record the gap.
- **The Sephardic community**: check how rescued and deported families tell 1943, as two distinct histories.
- **Armenians and Thracian refugee families**: check war and flight narratives as courage stories; sources mostly in community archives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national-heroic material; mixed for moral courage; thin for Roma, Gagauz and Karakachan sources.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, including Revival orthography; Ottoman and modern Turkish for Alevi hagiography; Italian or translations for Nissim; Hebrew and Judeo-Spanish for some 1943 testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Batak, 1923, the partisans, the *горяни*, the Belene camp and 1943 are fought over in politics and memory; present each side as a position. Nestinari rites are sacred to their keepers.
- **Held for review:** none; war and resistance sources are kept, with their contested standing noted above.
