# Bulgaria: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Shop and Rhodope speech), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Armenian, Church Slavonic for monastic texts. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia and the Shopluk**: check swearing and cursing corpora by dialect, to see whether curse formulas vary by region.
- **Rhodope Christian and Pomak villages**: check how quarrels were cooled by elders, priests or imams, and how folk healers name vexation.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie**: check grievance and memorial speech in Turkish-language media and at the 1985 memorials.
- **Roma groups**: check each group's own means of settling disputes (community courts, elders, church) and the anger directed at them in riots, kept as two separate questions.
- **Alevi communities**: check rites of reconciliation before communal worship, distinct from Sunni practice.
- **Football supporters of Levski, CSKA, Lokomotiv Plovdiv and Botev Plovdiv**: check fan codes and rivalries as one arena of public anger.
- **Varna and the Black Sea coast**: check how the 2013 protests began and were remembered there.
- **Armenian, Sephardic and Paulician Catholic communities**: check sermons and community memoirs on restraint and grievance; sources mostly community-held.
- **Men and women**: check how anger is reported by each in surveys and service records, without assuming one pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; much on collective and political anger, thin on everyday display rules and on small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, Ottoman Turkish for the *sicil*, Church Slavonic, modern Turkish, Romani.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** riots against Roma, the 1985 deaths, self-immolations and domestic violence involve real victims; cite reports, not rumor. *Време разделно* is contested by Pomak and Turkish readers and by historians; present it as one telling.
- **Held for review:** none; the *sicil* stay in as an emotion source, their legal reading routed to Libra.

## 5. First queries

5. Build a small corpus from news-site comments and search for "нерви", "яд" and "беснея".
