## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia against Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas and Ruse**: check how city rivalries appear in bids, rankings and fan culture.
- **The Shopluk and the Pirin region**: check singing contests (*надпяване*) and festival rules for traces of older group competition.
- **The Rhodopes**: check piping and singing contests at Rozhen in Christian and Pomak villages, and who judges.
- **Turks and Pomaks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie**: check fair wrestling and its prizes and honors, in Turkish and Bulgarian sources.
- **Roma musicians of several groups**: check rivalry and reputation among bands, from musicians' own interviews; avoid treating musicianship as an ethnic trait.
- **Alevi and Sunni communities of the Ludogorie**: check whether fair contests are shared or kept apart.
- **Elite-school pupils and families**: check the grade-7 exam culture in Sofia, Plovdiv and Varna, using exam data and parents' forums.
- **Armenians and the Sephardic community**: check community sports clubs and schools as sites of contest; sources mostly community-held.
- **Athletes from small towns**: check how rural sports schools under socialism fed the national teams.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport and exams; mixed for workplace and business rivalry; thin for envy beliefs today and for small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for the *надпяване* ethnographies, the socialist-emulation fonds and the grade-7 exam tables; Turkish of Bulgaria for *pehlivan* wrestling at Kardzhali-region fairs; Romani and English to follow the Stambolovo wedding-music contests through Silverman's study.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** doping, match-fixing and the role of State Security in sport are sensitive; cite proven cases. Envy is easy to turn into a national stereotype: record what sources say, never a trait.
- **Held for review:** none; competition law and its regulator are routed to Libra, not held.
