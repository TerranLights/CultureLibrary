## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Miners' families of Madan and Rudozem (Smolyan region):** check how job loss after the 1990s figures in men's and women's own accounts.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** check testimony on the forced name changes and on returning to one's name after 1989; it is a question of personal dignity as told.
- **Pomak communities (Rhodopes, Pirin, the Teteven area):** check the earlier name campaigns (1960s-70s) separately; record each self-name (Pomak, Bulgarian Muslim, Turk) as the person uses it, since labels are contested.
- **Roma groups (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Muslim Roma):** check which self-names each group uses and how it answers the exonym *tsigani*.
- **Pensioners of the Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check how worth after work is spoken of where young people have left.
- **Young emigrants and returnees (Sofia, Plovdiv):** check how work abroad changes standing at home.
- **Gabrovo and the Stara Planina towns:** check the House of Humor and Satire's collections for self-irony as a local genre.
- **Armenian families of Plovdiv and Varna; Thracian refugee descendants:** check how a refugee past is carried with pride or pain in community memory.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** check whether community associations speak of being overlooked; sources are scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in essays and literature on a people's self-image; mixed in psychology; thin for small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian essayistic and Revival-era registers; Turkish for testimony on names; Romani for church and community voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national self-image essays slide toward stereotype: use them as debate, not description; forced name changes; suicide data.
- **Held for review:** none; no dignity, honor or self-esteem pointer needed setting aside.
