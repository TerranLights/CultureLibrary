# Bulgaria: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects (Shop, Strandzha and Rhodope speech among them), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Pomak Bulgarian speech, Romani varieties, Aromanian, Western Armenian, Judeo-Spanish (historical), Gagauz, Russian of the Lipovans. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Shopluk (Bistritsa and the villages round Sofia):** check how singing groups keep a repertoire beside a fast-growing capital.
- **Strandzha villages:** check rites kept and villages lost after the closed border zone ended.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and the Danubian Plain:** check which villages hold their people and which do not.
- **Turks of the Ludogorie and the Kardzhali region:** check family histories of leaving and returning since 1989, which interrupt residence in distinct waves.
- **Rhodope Pomak villages:** check continuity of village life against labor migration, using each person's self-name.
- **Kalderash, Rudari and other once-itinerant Roma groups:** check each group's own account of settling, kept apart from the settled Roma groups.
- **Karakachans:** check the shift from seasonal movement to settlement, from their associations.
- **Lipovan Old Believers of Tataritsa and Kazashko:** check how long-standing villages keep their worship; sources likely few.
- **The Sephardic community:** check how emigration in 1948-49 broke continuity for those who stayed.
- **Paulician Catholic villages near Plovdiv (Rakovski):** check parish records for long family continuity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography and heritage files; mixed for political stability as lived; thin for Lipovans, Gagauz and Karakachans.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for marginal notes; Turkish for return-migration testimony; Russian for Lipovan sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** nostalgia for socialism is politically charged; words for itinerant life can be slurs; election comment is partisan.
- **Held for review:** none; no continuity, heritage or demographic pointer was set aside.
