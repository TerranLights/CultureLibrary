# Bulgaria: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Pomak Bulgarian speech, Romani varieties, Aromanian, Western Armenian, Judeo-Spanish (historical), Gagauz, Karakachan Greek. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gabrovo and the Stara Planina towns:** check how local humor about stinginess is made and sold, and whether residents embrace or resist it.
- **Stara Zagora and Upper Thrace:** check local press and women's groups after the 2023 case.
- **Shop villages around Sofia:** check mother-in-law songs and wedding customs for the place of the son's bride.
- **Pomak and Turkish communities (Rhodopes, Kardzhali region, Ludogorie):** check elopement and courtship customs in each community's own words; record self-names.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Erli, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Roma):** check marriage customs group by group from their own testimony and from community organizations, never from press clichés.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** check rules about marrying within or outside the community, from their own publications.
- **The Black Sea coast and Strandzha (Varna, Burgas, Tsarevo):** check beach concessions and campaigns, where shared places are claimed.
- **Football supporters of Sofia and Plovdiv:** check how neighborhoods are claimed as "ours".
- **Armenian and Sephardic communities:** check community memoirs on courtship and family control across generations.
- **Lipovans of Dobruja:** check marriage within the Old Believer community; sources likely few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and recent violence surveys; mixed on everyday jealousy; thin for small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, including folk-song registers; Turkish for elopement customs; Romani for community voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence and its victims; bride "theft" as a loaded subject; envy and the evil eye easily slide into stereotype; Gabrovo humor is a genre, not evidence of a trait.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; partner-violence law was routed to Libra rather than held.
