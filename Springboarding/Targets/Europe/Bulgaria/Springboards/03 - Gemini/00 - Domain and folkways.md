## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sofia and the Shopluk against Plovdiv and Upper Thrace: check how the capital's migrant speech compares with older town quarters.
- The Rhodopes (Smolyan, Chepelare) and the Kardzhali region: compare Christian and Muslim villages that share dialect features, to see whether faith or locality shapes everyday talk.
- The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and the Danubian Plain (Ruse, Pleven): transitional speech and old riverside multilingualism make language contact checkable here.
- The Stara Planina towns (Gabrovo, Tryavna, Koprivshtitsa): early schools sat beside craft guilds; check how learning was tied to trade.
- The Ludogorie (Deliorman) and Dobruja: Turkish, Alevi, Gagauz, Tatar and Lipovan villages sit close together; check what language neighbors use with one another.
- The Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas) and Strandzha: check what speech the Thracian refugee families of the early twentieth century kept.
- Roma of several groups (Erli in Sofia, Kalderash, Turkish-speaking and Romanian-speaking groups): separate them before any study of home language or schooling.
- Pomak villages of the Pirin region (Blagoevgrad) and the Rhodopes: self-names are contested; check how people name their own speech.
- Smaller communities (Karakachans near Sliven, Aromanians, Armenians, the Sephardic community, Bulgarian Catholics around Plovdiv): check whether a separate home language survives and where it is taught.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialects, folklore and schooling history; mixed for neighbors and short journeys; thin for Karakachans, Gagauz and Lipovans (try their associations and field archives).
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian with its dialects for the domain as a whole; Turkish of Bulgaria and Romani dialects for household speech; Ottoman Turkish for the *sicil* material; Judeo-Spanish and German for the Canetti thread.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census ethnic and language categories are contested, above all for Pomaks and Roma; minority-language use was suppressed during the 1984-89 'Revival Process'; *mahala* can stigmatize.
