# Bulgaria: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its regional speech; Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Greek of the Karakachans and Armenian for the smaller communities. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sofia professionals and Shopluk villages: check whether formal and familiar address differ by workplace and settlement, since the T/V choice is central here.
- Plovdiv and Stara Zagora (Upper Thrace): compare coffeehouse and market talk with the capital's, using the Neuburger thread.
- Kardzhali and Ludogorie Turks: check which language is used at home, in the mosque and with officials, because code choice is part of communication.
- Pomak villages of the Rhodopes and the Pirin region: check how speakers talk about their own speech; labels are contested.
- Roma of Sofia (Erli), Kalderash families and Turkish-speaking Roma: compare in-group and out-group speech separately for each.
- Gabrovo, Tryavna and Koprivshtitsa: check humor and storytelling styles in Balkan towns, a known local genre.
- Danubian towns (Ruse, Vidin): check the historical record of several languages in one street.
- Karakachans and Armenians (Plovdiv, Varna): check whether Greek and Armenian survive as home speech and in what settings.
- Protestants and Bulgarian Catholics: compare church registers of speech (sermon, prayer, testimony).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for written registers and parliamentary speech; mixed for everyday conversation (few recorded corpora outside Sofia); thin for Karakachan and Gagauz speech habits.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for corpus and pragmatics work; Turkish of Bulgaria and Romani to study code choice; Church Slavonic for the damaskini; English for the gesture and coffeehouse studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-language broadcasting and the 1984-89 ban on public Turkish speech remain political; *Бай Ганьо* is read by some as a national caricature, so cite it as a text, not evidence.
- **Held for review:** none; BNT's Turkish bulletin stays despite the politics of minority broadcasting, while the legal record of the 1984-89 ban on public Turkish speech is Libra's question.
