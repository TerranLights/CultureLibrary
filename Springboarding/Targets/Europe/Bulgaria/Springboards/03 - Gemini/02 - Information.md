## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sofia readers against the Northwest (Vidin, Montana): compare where people get news when local papers are few; check the regional access gap.
- Ruse and the Danubian Plain: check how the 1987-89 pollution protests shaped local trust in official data.
- Kardzhali and the Ludogorie: compare Turkish-language and Bulgarian-language sources and which ones people say they trust.
- Pomak villages of the Rhodopes and Pirin: check how the 1970s name changes appear (or do not) in local records and family memory.
- Roma quarters in Plovdiv (Stolipinovo), Sofia and Sliven: check what community media exist and whether the sources are thin.
- The Sephardic community: check the prewar Judeo-Spanish press and the records of 1943 as an information question (what was known, when).
- Alevi communities (Kızılbaş, Babai): check how religious knowledge is held and who may receive it, without asking for restricted content.
- Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas): check tourist-season information flows and local radio.
- Dobruja's Gagauz and Lipovan villages: sources likely scarce; look for association bulletins rather than general Dobruja material.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history and media-freedom reports; mixed for regional news use; thin for Romani, Gagauz and Karakachan community media.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for most of the record; Ottoman Turkish for the *defters*; modern Turkish for the Kardzhali press; Judeo-Spanish for the prewar Sephardic papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** State Security files name living people; the 1984-89 name records are contested; Alevi and other restricted religious knowledge must not be sought; reports on disinformation are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing about news, records or trust in sources was held back.
