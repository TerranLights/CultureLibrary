# Bulgaria: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sofia and Plovdiv against small villages of the Shopluk: check whether personal questions to strangers are framed as interest or intrusion in each setting.
- The Rhodopes (Smolyan, the observatory area): check how a remote region's schools and families use local science sites.
- Stara Planina towns (Gabrovo, Koprivshtitsa) and the Sredna Gora: check the Revival-era reading rooms and early travel, where organized curiosity began.
- The Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas): check how tourism shapes curiosity about outsiders, and outsiders' curiosity about locals.
- Kardzhali and the Ludogorie: compare *merak* as used in Turkish and in Bulgarian, and which questions are thought improper to ask.
- Pomak communities (Rhodopes, Pirin): check how outside researchers' questions were received, and start from community-led work.
- Roma of several groups: check which questions about origins, faith or income are treated as prying, before any fieldwork design.
- Alevi communities (Kızılbaş, Babai): check where religious knowledge is open to outsiders and where it is not.
- The Sephardic community and Armenians: check community museums and memoirs for curiosity about their own histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Revival periodicals and popular science, thinner for everyday nosiness and children's questions; scarce for Karakachan, Gagauz and Lipovan communities.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian for the *Шестоднев*; Bulgarian for the magazines and quiz archives; German for Kanitz; Turkish of Bulgaria for *merak*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** questions about ethnicity, faith and names touch the contested 1984-89 period; restricted religious knowledge in Alevi communities is not to be sought; any fieldwork design among Pomaks and Roma needs a consent review first.
- **Held for review:** none; Kanitz's travel survey is kept as an outsider's record to read critically, and fieldwork among Pomaks and Roma waits on a consent review instead.
