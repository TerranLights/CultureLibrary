*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Greek of the Karakachans; German and English for the ethnographies. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and the Danubian Plain (Ruse, Pleven): place the two village ethnographies on the map (check each site) and compare them before generalizing about favors.
- Sofia's incomers: check which native-place ties survive in the capital and which fade.
- The Rhodopes and the Kardzhali region: check how ties run across Christian, Pomak and Turkish villages, and whether *кумство* crosses faith lines.
- The Ludogorie (Deliorman): check how the Alevi communities (Kızılbaş, Babai) and Sunni neighbors connect through shrines and markets.
- Strandzha and the Black Sea coast (Burgas): check the Thracian refugee families' associations and their reunions.
- The Pirin region (Blagoevgrad): check refugee-descendant brotherhoods, with care because the labels are contested.
- Roma of several groups and faiths: check marriage and music ties within and between groups; do not treat them as one network.
- Bulgarian Catholics (Rakovski and nearby villages) and Protestants: check whether parish life sets different sponsor customs.
- Gagauz and Lipovan Old Believers in Dobruja: sources likely thin; start with village histories and parish records rather than regional overviews.
- The Bulgarian Turks who emigrated in 1989 and after: comparison only, for cross-border family ties seen from inside Bulgaria.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in ethnography of village ties and ritual kinship; mixed for loneliness data below country level; thin for Gagauz and Lipovan networks.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for *кумство* and refugee-association records; German for Brunnbauer; Turkish of Bulgaria for *torpil* and kin networks; Church Slavonic for the *поменици*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *връзки* and nepotism shade into corruption claims; refugee and Macedonian questions are politically contested; family separations of 1989 remain painful.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on ties, sponsorship or networks was set aside.
