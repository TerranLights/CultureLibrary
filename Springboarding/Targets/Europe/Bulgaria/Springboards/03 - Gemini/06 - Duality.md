# Bulgaria: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; Old Bulgarian for the anti-Bogomil texts; Latin and Italian for Catholic mission reports; Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani for Ederlezi customs. **Concept in one line:** (proposed) how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Bulgarian Catholics of the Plovdiv villages and the Danube villages (Belene, for one): check how a dualist past is remembered or rejected locally.
- Pomak villages (Rhodopes, Pirin, the Teteven area): check two-name and two-language histories, with self-chosen names first.
- Kardzhali and Ludogorie Turks: compare bilingual practice by generation, since school and home languages differ.
- Alevi communities (Kızılbaş, Babai): check how their own teaching treats pairs, without asking for restricted knowledge.
- Roma of several faiths (Orthodox, Muslim, Protestant): check how Ederlezi and St. George's Day are kept by each.
- The Shopluk and Pernik: check whether masked rites and two-part singing are linked in local explanations.
- Strandzha: check the fire-dancing *нестинарство* and its pairing of saints and fire, as the villages present it.
- Gagauz of the Varna area: check Orthodox faith and a Turkic language held together; sources thin, start with local museums.
- Karakachans and Aromanians: check two-language households and seasonal two-place lives (summer and winter pastures).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Bogomil history and masked rites; mixed for bilingualism; thin for Gagauz and Aromanian experience of double belonging.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian for Cosmas; Latin and Italian for Bakshev and the mission archive; Turkish of Bulgaria for code-switching; Romani for Ederlezi songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bogomils are often claimed for national narratives; *Време разделно* and the name changes are contested; "two-faced" can become a slur against minorities, so record such labels as contested, never as findings.
- **Held for review:** none; *Време разделно* is kept as a contested text about loyalty, not as history, and the legal side of the forced name changes is a matter for Libra.
