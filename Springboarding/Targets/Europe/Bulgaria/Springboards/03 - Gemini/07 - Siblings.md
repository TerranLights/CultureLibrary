# Bulgaria: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialect kin terms; Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Ottoman Turkish for household registers; Armenian and Aromanian. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Shopluk and the Northwest (Vidin, Montana): check joint households of brothers in the west, where much *задруга* material was gathered (*check*).
- Upper Thrace (Plovdiv, Stara Zagora) and the Danubian Plain: check smaller household patterns against the west.
- The Rhodopes (Christian and Pomak villages): compare sibling roles in long-distance herding and seasonal migration.
- Kardzhali and Ludogorie Turks: check whether *abla* and *ağabey* are used for non-kin as *кака* and *бате* are.
- Roma of several groups: check birth-order duties and sibling care separately for each group, never as one.
- Bulgarian Catholics around Plovdiv and in the Danube villages: check godparent and sibling roles in parish life.
- Karakachans and Aromanians: check brothers' joint herding units in transhumant families.
- Armenians and the Sephardic community: check sibling ties across emigration waves (Israel after 1948 is comparison only).
- Stara Planina towns (Tryavna, Gabrovo): check brother-run workshops and family firms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household history and kin terms; mixed for adult sibling relations today; thin for Romani, Karakachan and Aromanian kin practice.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian with dialect forms for kin address; Ottoman Turkish for the registers; Turkish of Bulgaria for kin terms; Romani for group-level study.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the record of children in state institutions, and the ethnic data on it, is painful and disputed; inheritance between siblings is law and goes to Libra or Capricorn; the 1943 record is contested in parts.
- **Held for review:** none; the Mogilino and child-protection pointers stay despite the painful record of children in care, with inheritance between brothers and sisters sent to Libra or Capricorn.
