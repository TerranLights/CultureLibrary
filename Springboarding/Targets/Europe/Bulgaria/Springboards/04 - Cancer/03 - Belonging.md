# Bulgaria: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (including Shop and Rhodope speech), the Bulgarian speech of the Pomaks, Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Judeo-Spanish, Armenian, Karakachan Greek. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia and the Shopluk against provincial towns:** check how "local" and "newcomer" are spoken of in the capital and in smaller towns, and whether the words differ.
- **Pomaks in the Western, Central and Eastern Rhodopes:** self-names are contested ground; check how each community names itself before applying any label.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie (Deliorman):** check whether belonging is spoken of differently in the two areas, including after 1989.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths:** check which groups call themselves Roma and which use other names, and where they place themselves.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families (Burgas, Strandzha, Pirin, Plovdiv):** check their associations' own memory of arrival.
- **The Sephardic community, Armenians (Plovdiv, Varna), Karakachans, Aromanians:** small groups with their own organizations; check membership records and gatherings.
- **Gagauz and Lipovan Old Believers of Dobruja:** check whether any community records exist and in which language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and national-identity history; mixed for minorities' own voices; thin for Gagauz, Lipovans and Aromanians.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian, Turkish and Romani for the three largest communities' own words for "us"; Judeo-Spanish and Armenian for older community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "Revival Process" and the 1989 expulsions; whether Pomaks are counted apart is disputed; the 1943 history has rival tellings, so read the deportations from occupied lands alongside it. Diaspora Bulgarians (Bessarabian, Banat) and the Bulgarian Turks who emigrated to Turkey are comparison only.
- **Held for review:** none; citizenship and minority-rights law go to Libra and Capricorn, so no belonging pointer was withheld.
