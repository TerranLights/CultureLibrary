# Bulgaria: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Balkan and Rhodope dialect forms), Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Aromanian, Karakachan Greek. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and Danubian Plain villages against Sofia:** compare how much of a child's raising falls to grandparents when parents work away.
- **Rhodope Pomak villages and Turkish villages around Kardzhali:** check children's part in household and field work, and how parents talk of it.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths (Erli in Sofia, Kalderash, Protestant congregations):** check whether church, school and family each claim a role in raising the young.
- **Upper Thrace towns (Plovdiv, Stara Zagora) and the Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas):** urban middle-class parenting; check what advice parents seek and from whom.
- **Sredna Gora and Stara Planina towns (Koprivshtitsa, Tryavna, Gabrovo):** check Revival-era school and church-school records for what children were taught.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** small herding communities; check whether any records describe how children learned herding.
- **Armenians and the Sephardic community:** check community schools and youth programs, past and present.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and Revival schooling; mixed for current parenting attitudes; thin for minority-language lullabies and child-rearing terms.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for nursery speech and dialect lullabies; Turkish and Romani for home-language upbringing words; Revival-era Bulgarian for Beron and the *damaskini*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's field labor and early school-leaving are debated in ways that can stigmatize Roma, Turkish and Pomak families; prefer the communities' own accounts and named sources.
- **Held for review:** none; the lullaby, *proshtapulnik* and parenting-manual pointers above all stay in play, with the legal rules on children left to Libra.
