*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and Danubian Plain (Pleven) villages against Sofia and Plovdiv:** compare who cares for the old where kin have left and where they stayed.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie; Pomaks of the Rhodopes:** check religious and kin-based care side by side, and what each community says of care homes.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths:** check the role of churches (Pentecostal and other Protestant), mosques and kin in caring for the sick.
- **Paulician Catholics around Plovdiv and Protestant congregations:** check parish and convent care.
- **The Sephardic and Armenian communities:** check their own welfare societies, past and present.
- **Gagauz and Lipovan Old Believers of Dobruja:** check who nurses the sick and sits with the dying in these villages, and whether priests or elders wrote any of it down.
- **Strandzha and Sredna Gora villages:** check for herbal and folk healing records, region by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for social-service figures and folk medicine; mixed for family caregiving (few surveys); thin for small minorities' care practices.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian dialect for healers' vocabulary; Ottoman Turkish for foundation deeds; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for Sephardic society records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** care homes' conditions and elder neglect touch private families; folk healers' knowledge may be guarded; nurses' emigration is a charged topic.
- **Held for review:** none; health-insurance and social-assistance statutes belong to Libra, and no caregiving pointer was withheld.
