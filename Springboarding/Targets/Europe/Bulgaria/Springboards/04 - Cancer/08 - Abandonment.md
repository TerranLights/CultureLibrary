## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and Danubian Plain (Pleven, Ruse):** check how deserted villages and elders living alone are described by those who stayed.
- **Pirin (Blagoevgrad) and Stara Planina towns (Tryavna, Gabrovo):** compare old *pechalbar* and *gurbet* traditions with today's labor migration.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** check accounts of 1989 and after, and of families split between the two countries (the emigrants themselves as comparison only).
- **Pomaks in the Smolyan and Blagoevgrad districts:** check migration patterns and what the left-behind say.
- **Roma of several groups:** check whether the ethnic make-up of children in institutions was ever published, and prefer sources that give families' own reasons.
- **Strandzha and the Black Sea coast (Burgas):** check emptied border villages of the socialist era.
- **Karakachans:** check accounts of what families left behind when nomadic herding ended, and when that was.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children in institutions and depopulation figures; mixed for elders left alone; thin for how small minorities speak of leaving.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for policy and press; Turkish for 1989 memoirs; Romani for families' own accounts; English for international NGO reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's institutions (privacy of named children; disability); Roma families are often blamed in public talk; the 1989 departures are painful family memory.
- **Held for review:** none; child-support and guardianship law go to Libra; no pointer on leaving or being left was held back.
