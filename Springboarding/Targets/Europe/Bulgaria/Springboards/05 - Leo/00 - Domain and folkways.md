## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia and the Shopluk against the Rhodopes:** check whether mumming, sung courting and fairs are organized differently in highland and capital-region villages.
- **Pomak villages of the Western Rhodopes (Garmen, Satovcha) and the central Rhodopes (Smolyan, Madan):** compare wedding display and festival calendars, since Muslim and Christian calendars meet there.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie:** compare sport and festival practice (wrestling, bayram fairs) between the two zones.
- **Roma of several groups (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Muslim Roma) and faiths:** separate musicians' families from others when checking music as livelihood and as pleasure.
- **Dobruja and the Danubian Plain (Ruse, Pleven):** compare town leisure with village fairs.
- **Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas) and Strandzha:** check resort leisure against the nestinari and saint's-day gatherings.
- **Stara Planina towns (Gabrovo, Tryavna, Koprivshtitsa):** check craft-town festivity and the humor carnival as local display.
- **Armenians, Sephardic Jews, Karakachans, Aromanians, Gagauz, Lipovans:** smaller community calendars; sources may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for folklore, music and sport (a century of collecting); mixed for modern leisure by region; thin for the smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for the IEFSEM catalog (седянка, събор, кукери) and НСИ time-use releases; Old Bulgarian for Presbyter Cosmas's sermon; German for Kanitz's *Donau-Bulgarien*; Turkish of Bulgaria and Romani for the Hıdrellez, sünnet and Erdelezi festival records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1984-89 'Revival Process' name changes touch Muslim festival life; how chalga and Roma music are discussed can carry prejudice. Bulgaria's lands are carried with no recorded change; its exact territory and units are still to be settled by the author, so this run is written for Bulgaria as it stands.
- **Held for review:** none; the domain survey set aside no source, and the folk-calendar pointers (kukeri, survakari, Babinden) were kept as customs only.
