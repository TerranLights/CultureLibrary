*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Armenian, Aromanian and Karakachan Greek for craft vocabularies; Church Slavonic for medieval texts. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stara Planina craft towns (Tryavna, Gabrovo, Kotel):** check whether masters were trained as a lineage or by guild, and how signatures appear.
- **Pirin region (Bansko, Razlog, Melnik):** compare the Bansko painters with the Samokov ones on patrons and travel.
- **The Northwest (Chiprovtsi, Vidin, Montana):** check the weaving tradition's link to the Catholic past of Chiprovtsi before 1688.
- **Rhodope and Pomak villages (Smolyan, Madan, the Chepino valley):** check woolen weaving and who taught it.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie:** check coppersmithing, embroidery and wood crafts, and Turkish-language terms for mastery.
- **Alevi communities (Kızılbaş and Babai):** check tekke decoration and song-making as separate traditions.
- **Roma of several groups:** separate craft-named groups (Kalaydzhii, Rudari, Kalderash metalworkers) and check how craft and identity are linked in their own accounts.
- **Armenians of Plovdiv and Sofia; Karakachans; Aromanians:** smaller craft traditions (jewelry, photography, woolens); sources likely thin.
- **Sofia against Plovdiv and Varna:** compare the modern creative scenes and their funding.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Revival and medieval art and for crafts; mixed for modern creative industries; thin for Karakachan, Aromanian and Gagauz making.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for the "Тревненска школа" and "Самоковска школа" catalog searches; Church Slavonic for Hrabar's *On the Letters* and *khudozhistvo*; Turkish for *usta* and *ustalık* among Kardzhali coppersmiths; Armenian for *steghtsagortsutyun* in Plovdiv's community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** name-change campaigns against Muslim communities (to 1989) may have reshaped how their crafts were recorded and credited, so check attributions; socialist-era censorship files are in state-security archives.
- **Held for review:** none; no craft or art-history pointer had to be dropped, though attributions of tekke and Revival works stay open until checked.
