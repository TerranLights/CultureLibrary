# Bulgaria: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Armenian; Church Slavonic and Greek for medieval and khanate texts. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plovdiv and Upper Thrace against Veliko Tarnovo and the old capital's region:** check how each town frames its claim to first place.
- **Sofia and the Shopluk:** check self-mocking Shop jokes beside local pride, as told by Shopi themselves.
- **The Kazanlak rose valley and the Rhodopes:** compare regional emblems (rose, bagpipe, songs) as sources of pride.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie:** check pride in language and religious heritage after 1989, from Turkish-language sources.
- **Pomaks:** check self-naming ("Pomak", "Bulgarian Muslim", "Ahryan", "Turk"), each contested, and what each label carries for those who use it.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths:** compare group pride (craft lineage, music, faith) across Erli, Kalderash, Rudari and Muslim Roma.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families; Armenians; Karakachans; Gagauz:** check whether and how each keeps up pride in its origin, through its own associations and press.
- **LGBTQ organizers in Sofia (search Bilitis Foundation):** pride as a movement word, a different sense of the same term.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national pride (surveys, canon, holidays); mixed for regional pride; thin for Gagauz and Karakachan sources.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for Paisius's preface, the guild *kondiki* and Alpha Research polls on "гордост"; medieval Greek for Beshevliev's khan inscriptions; Church Slavonic for *gordyni* in *damaskini* sermons; Turkish for *gurur* and *kibir*; Romani for *barimos* around April 8.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Macedonian identity questions are disputed between states; Pomak and Turkish identity labels are contested; ethnic-nationalist sites carry hostile content.
- **Held for review:** none; the fringe ancient-history material is kept as a phenomenon to study, not as a source, so nothing on pride was set aside.
