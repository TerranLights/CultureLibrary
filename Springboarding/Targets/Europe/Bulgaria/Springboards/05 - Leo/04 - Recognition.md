# Bulgaria: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for the Jewish community's records; Armenian; Church Slavonic for medieval texts. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia against Kardzhali and the Ludogorie:** compare whose names go on streets, plaques and honorary-citizen lists.
- **Turks and the 1989 expellees who stayed or returned:** check how acknowledgment of 1984-89 is discussed in Turkish-language media.
- **Pomaks:** check census answers and the contested labels, since recognition of the group name is itself the issue.
- **Roma of several groups:** check how Roma appear (or not) on honors lists, and how Roma NGOs credit their own figures.
- **The Sephardic Jewish community and Shalom (Organization of the Jews in Bulgaria):** check how the community frames 1943 recognition.
- **Bulgarian Catholics (Rakovski, and the Danubian villages around Nikopol and Belene):** check church honors and local memory.
- **Armenians:** check community commemorations and parliamentary debates over naming the 1915 events.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** check whether their associations seek recognition as distinct groups; sources thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state honors and the 1943 memory; mixed for workplace recognition now; thin for smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Boril's Synodikon and *vechnaya pamyat*; Bulgarian for State Gazette order decrees and municipal honorary-citizen pages; Turkish for media on the 2012 expulsion declaration; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for Shalom's 1943 commemorations; Armenian for 1915 remembrance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 story is told in competing ways; acknowledgment of 1984-89 and of the 1915 events is politically live; legal redress belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no honors list, award or commemoration pointer was withheld, though the 1943 and 1915 framings need balanced sourcing.
