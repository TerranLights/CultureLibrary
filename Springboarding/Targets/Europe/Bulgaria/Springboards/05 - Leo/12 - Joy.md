# Bulgaria: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Karakachan Greek; Armenian; Judeo-Spanish; Church Slavonic for liturgy. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Thrace (Plovdiv, Stara Zagora) and its wine villages:** compare Trifon Zarezan customs with the Danubian Plain's.
- **Strandzha and the southern Black Sea coast:** check nestinari feasts against coastal summer festivity.
- **Alevi communities in the Ludogorie and near Haskovo:** check gatherings at Demir Baba and Otman Baba as separate traditions.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region:** check bayram joy and Hıdrellez in Turkish-language sources.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths:** compare Erdelezi festivities, wedding music and evangelical worship, group by group.
- **Pomak villages:** check how Muslim and shared calendar feasts are celebrated; labels contested.
- **Karakachans; Lipovan Old Believers of Dobruja; Armenians; the Sephardic community:** check their own festival calendars (Easter for Lipovans and Armenians; Purim and Hanukkah, then and now); sources thin.
- **Generations:** compare Golden Orpheus memories with July Morning and festival culture after 1989.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and survey well-being; mixed for felt joy; thin for smaller communities.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for *radost* and *shtastie* in Gerov and the academic dictionary, and its early modern form for the *damaskini*; Church Slavonic for Easter liturgy; Turkish for Hıdrellez and Demir Baba gatherings; Romani for *bax(t)* in evangelical worship; Karakachan Greek for *chará*; Armenian and Judeo-Spanish for festival calendars.
