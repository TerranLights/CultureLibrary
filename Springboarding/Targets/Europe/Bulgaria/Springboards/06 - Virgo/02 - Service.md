# Bulgaria: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (Cyrillic) and Church Slavonic; the Turkish of Bulgaria and Pomak Bulgarian speech; Armenian; Judeo-Spanish; Ottoman Turkish for court records. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox monastic centers (Rila, Bachkovo, Troyan):** check novices' and pilgrims' accounts against parish laity's.
- **Turkish and Pomak villages of the Kardzhali region and the Rhodopes:** check how *hayır* deeds and the *kurban* are organized and who serves the meal; compare with the Christian village *kurban* of the same region.
- **Alevi communities (Kızılbaş, Babai) near Razgrad and Haskovo:** check service roles at the Demir Baba and Otman Baba tekkes; sources are few and partly oral.
- **Sofia and the Shopluk:** state-service city; check the image of the *чиновник* (clerk) against the volunteer.
- **The Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas, Nesebar):** seasonal hospitality staff; check where workers come from and how they describe guests.
- **The Sephardic community (Sofia, Plovdiv, Kyustendil):** check the community's own records of mutual service; separate the 1943 rescue inside Bulgaria from the deportations from the lands Bulgaria then occupied.
- **Paulician Catholic villages around Plovdiv and Catholics near Svishtov (Belene, Oresh):** check confraternity service as distinct from Orthodox brotherhoods.
- **Armenian parishes (Plovdiv, Varna, Ruse):** check church boards and charities as service structures.
- **Roma conscripts and laborers:** check construction-troop records by origin before reading any account of who served.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families:** check their benevolent societies (Sofia, Burgas).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, Revival and state service; mixed for hospitality; thin for Alevi and Lipovan service roles.
- **Languages needed:** Greek, Georgian and Armenian for the three versions of the Bachkovo typikon; Ottoman Turkish for the sicils; Turkish for mufti publications and Kardzhali interviews; Judeo-Spanish for the older Sephardic community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1943 (rescue and deportation must be told together); minority conscripts in the construction troops; patronage behind *хатър*. Military service rules and civil-service statutes are law: Libra for the text, Capricorn for the spirit.
- **Held for review:** none; the service pointers, monastic and Muslim charity sources included, needed no holding back.
