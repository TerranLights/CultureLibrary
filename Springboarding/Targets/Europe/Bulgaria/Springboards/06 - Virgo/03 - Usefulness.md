## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and depopulated Balkan villages:** check how the remaining elderly describe their tasks; SHARE and local press may disagree.
- **Sofia's younger professionals:** check whether usefulness is spoken of through jobs, volunteering or family help, against the village pattern.
- **Kardzhali and Rhodope Turkish and Pomak households with members working abroad:** check how those who stay frame their contribution; emigrants themselves are comparison only.
- **Roma neighborhoods (Stolipinovo in Plovdiv, Fakulteta in Sofia, *check*):** check public-work program records against residents' own accounts; separate groups and faiths.
- **Disabled workers' enterprises (Union of the Blind, Union of the Deaf):** check how their histories describe work after 1989.
- **Karakachan families (Sliven, Kotel):** check working-dog and horse lore; sources are mostly breed-society and museum material.
- **Upper Thrace and the Danubian Plain:** check *зимнина* and kitchen-garden work as a measure of a household's usefulness.
- **Armenian community (Plovdiv, Varna):** check elders' roles in community schools and churches; few published sources.
- **Dobruja and Ludogorie Alevi villages:** check elders' roles in community gatherings; oral sources mostly.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disability and pension statistics and for socialist campaigns; mixed for elders' roles; thin for Armenian and Karakachan sources.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for Beron, the damaskini (with Church Slavonic) and НОИ data; German for the 1995 tale-type index; Turkish for households in the Kardzhali region; Greek for Karakachan breed lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "parasite" labeling fell on Roma and dissidents; disability language in older sources; the rules on parasitism and on work-capacity certification are law (Libra for the text, Capricorn for the spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on old age, disability or useful knowledge was set aside.
