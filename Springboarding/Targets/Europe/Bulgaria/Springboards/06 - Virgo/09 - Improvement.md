## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gabrovo, Tryavna and Koprivshtitsa:** check Revival school and merchant records for local improvement projects, against later state ones.
- **Upper Thrace (Plovdiv, Sadovo):** check farm-improvement records and how farmers adopted or resisted new methods.
- **The Sredna Gora and Sofia-district villages (Mirkovo):** check cooperative minutes for members' own aims.
- **The Kardzhali region and the Rhodopes:** Turkish and Pomak communities; check how "modernization" campaigns appear in their own oral history, set against state reports; the 1984-89 "Revival Process" name is itself contested.
- **Roma neighborhoods:** check program evaluations against residents' accounts; separate groups and faiths.
- **Burgas, Strandzha and Haskovo refugee villages:** check resettlement records and descendants' memory.
- **Karakachan families:** check the 1950s settlement and herd collectivization as an imposed change; few sources.
- **Sofia:** check the self-help market and life-coaching scene against small-town reading lists.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check EU-funded projects and how locals judge them.
- **Danubian Plain (Pleven, Ruse):** check agrarian-union strongholds' records of village improvement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Revival, agrarian and socialist programs; mixed for personal self-improvement; thin for Karakachan settlement.
- **Languages needed:** Revival-era Bulgarian for *Неделник* and *Любословие*; Russian for Aprilov's 1841 tract; German for Brunnbauer; Turkish for Kardzhali oral history on the campaigns; French and English for League of Nations refugee files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced name changes and dress campaigns (1970s, 1984-89) and the 1989 expulsions; Roma "integration" wording; the agrarian era's labor-service and reform statutes are law (Libra for the text, Capricorn for the spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; no reform, schooling or self-help pointer was set aside.
