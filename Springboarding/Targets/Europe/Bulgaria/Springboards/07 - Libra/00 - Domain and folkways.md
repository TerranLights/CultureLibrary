## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox Bulgarians of the Shopluk, Upper Thrace and Dobruja: check how the wedding cycle and the *kum*'s role differ, since surveys merge them.
- Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie: check the *nikâh* and memories of kadı-court divorce, given their different village histories.
- Pomaks (a contested label; record each village's own term): check wedding rites and the name changes in marriage registers.
- Roma groups (Kalaydzhii, Kalderash, Erli, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Roma): check who arranges a match and who settles a dispute.
- Paulician Catholics of Rakovski and the Catholic villages near Svishtov: check church rules on marriage and annulment.
- Sephardic community: check the *ketubá* and communal courts before 1948.
- Armenians, Karakachans, Gagauz, Lipovans of Kazashko and Tataritsa: check each one's marriage rules; sources are scarce, so start with their associations.
- Thracian and Macedonian refugee families: check whether marriage stayed within refugee networks after 1913 and 1919.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Orthodox majority, the law and the Ottoman courts; mixed for Turks, Pomaks and Roma; thin for Gagauz, Lipovans, Karakachans and Aromanians.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for Bobchev and the codes; Ottoman Turkish in Arabic script for the *sicil*; Old Church Slavonic for the ninth-century code; Latin for the *Responsa*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Kalaydzhii "bride fair" at Stara Zagora is often reported in an exoticizing way; the 1984-89 "Revival Process" (*Възродителен процес*) changed names in civil registers, so record linkage needs care.
- **Scope:** Bulgaria's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the republic as it stands.
- **Held for review:** none; the Kalaydzhii bride-fair material is kept, but read through scholarly Roma studies, because press coverage of the Stara Zagora fair tends to exoticize it.
