# Bulgaria: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Armenian, Judeo-Spanish. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox Bulgarians of Danubian Plain villages against Sofia: check whether the court, the village or the family is named first to answer a wrong.
- Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie: check what redress for the 1984-89 renaming and the 1989 expulsions has been sought, and through which channels.
- Pomaks of the Rhodopes and of the Pirin region (a contested label; use each village's own name): check the memory of the 1912, 1940s and 1970s name changes and whether any were answered in law.
- Roma groups (Kalderash, Erli, Turkish-speaking Muslim Roma and Pentecostal Roma): check whether a communal court or elders still hear wrongs, and how cases against police are pursued.
- Sephardic families: check claims for property taken in 1940-44 and again after 1944.
- Armenians of Plovdiv and Varna: check how the 2015 parliamentary declaration on 1915 is framed as a matter of historical justice.
- Families of Belene and Lovech camp inmates, and families of those sentenced by the People's Court: check how each side frames what is owed.
- Karakachans: check claims tied to the collectivization of their flocks in the 1950s.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the codes, the People's Court and the communist camps; mixed for redress of the "Revival Process"; thin for Roma communal courts, where field ethnography is the main source.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish where the *Fontes* translations stop; Church Slavonic for the Synodikon and Kozma; Turkish for testimony of the 1989 expulsions; Romani for any communal-court fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the People's Court, the camps and the "Revival Process" divide families and parties; Roma deaths in custody; the 1943 events involve occupied territories now in other states, so read each side's scholarship.
- **Held for review:** none; *Nachova and Others v. Bulgaria* remains despite touching the deaths of Roma men at the hands of military police, since the ruling is a primary record of failed procedure.
- **Spirit of the law:** trust in courts, legitimacy and obedience belong to Capricorn, section 8.
