# Bulgaria: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Armenian, with Gagauz and Romani for community fieldwork. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Stara Planina craft towns (Gabrovo, Tryavna, Kotel) against the Shopluk villages: check whether guild-era rules of fair dealing left traces in local sayings and businesses.
- Tobacco growers of the Kardzhali region and the Smolyan Rhodopes (Turks and Pomaks; use each community's own name): check how purchase prices and quotas were judged fair or unfair across regimes.
- The Northwest (Vidin, Montana): check how talk of regional fairness and neglect appears in local press and surveys.
- Dobruja grain villages: check fairness between landowners, tenants and seasonal hands, from Yovkov's time to the cooperatives.
- Roma groups (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Roma): check how equal treatment is claimed in schools, housing and policing, without folding the groups together.
- Lipovan fishing families on the Danube and Black Sea: check how a catch is shared within a crew; sources are scarce, so start with local museums.
- Alevi villages of the Ludogorie: check how food at communal gatherings is shared out, from their own accounts.
- Heirs in Paulician Catholic villages near Plovdiv: check whether church and custom shaped inheritance shares differently from Orthodox neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, surveys and restitution; mixed for guild records, which are scattered across town museums; thin for Lipovan and Alevi distribution customs.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish for untranslated *defters*; nineteenth-century Bulgarian orthography for the *кондики* and Slaveykov; Turkish for Kardzhali tobacco growers' testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Gabrovo's self-made humor about thrift and the literary figure Bay Ganyo are easily misread as traits; treat both as texts to study, not evidence about people. Restitution quarrels are live in many villages.
- **Held for review:** none; the 1991 land restitution act and Kaneff's village study stay in, although quarrels over returned land are still live, because they show fairness argued in practice.
