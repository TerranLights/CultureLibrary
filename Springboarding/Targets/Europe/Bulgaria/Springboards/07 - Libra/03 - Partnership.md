# Bulgaria: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Karakachan Greek, the Russian of the Lipovans, Armenian. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox villages of the Northwest and of Upper Thrace: check how the joint household and later the collective farm reshaped who counted as a partner.
- Turks of the Ludogorie and the Kardzhali region: check how religious and civil marriage are combined, and how family firms are shared.
- Pomak villages of the Western Rhodopes and the Smolyan region (a contested label; use local names): check couples' work partnerships in tobacco and seasonal migration.
- Roma groups (Kalderash, Erli, Kalaydzhii, Rudari): check alliance between families at marriage, and musical and trade partnerships, group by group.
- Karakachans of the Sliven and Stara Planina pastures: check the *tselingato* and what became of it after flocks were collectivized.
- Lipovan Old Believers of Tataritsa and Kazashko: check fishing crews and marriage within the faith; sources are scarce, start with parish and village museums.
- Sephardic and Armenian families of Plovdiv, Ruse and Varna: check commercial partnerships and marriage within each community.
- Same-sex couples in Sofia and Plovdiv: check how partnership is arranged without legal recognition.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cooperatives, collectivization and marriage statistics; mixed for Muslim religious marriage; thin for Lipovans, Karakachans and Gagauz.
- **Languages needed:** Karakachan Greek for *tselingato* terms and oral accounts; Russian for Lipovan parish material; Romani for family-alliance fieldwork; older orthography for the 1861 song collection.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** same-sex partnership is politically contested; collectivization testimony touches family losses; Roma marriage ages draw sensational coverage, so rely on scholarly sources.
- **Held for review:** none; *Koilova and Babulkova v. Bulgaria* is retained though same-sex partnership is politically contested, as the clearest statement of what current law does and does not recognize.
