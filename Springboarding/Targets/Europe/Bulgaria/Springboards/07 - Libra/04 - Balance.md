## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox Bulgarians who keep the fasts and urban secular households in Sofia and Varna: check whether the fast-feast rhythm still orders the year.
- Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie, and Muslim Roma: check how Ramadan and Kurban Bayram structure work and rest.
- Alevi communities (Kızılbaş and Babai) around the Demir Baba tekke: check their own fasting cycle (Muharrem) as a different calendar of restraint.
- Pomaks of the Smolyan and Velingrad Rhodopes (a contested label; use local names): check how mountain work seasons and religious calendars are fitted together.
- Lipovan Old Believers of Dobruja: check the strictness of fasts on the old calendar; scarce sources, so begin with their parishes.
- Paulician Catholics near Plovdiv and Armenians in Plovdiv and Varna: check how different Easter dates and fasts sit beside the Orthodox majority.
- Black Sea coast resort workers (Varna, Burgas, Nesebar): check the seasonal swing between summer overwork and winter idleness.
- Stara Planina craft towns and Strandzha villages: check whether local design and dance traditions are described in terms of measure and symmetry.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, time use and EU surveys; mixed for fasting practice, where church statistics are scarce; thin for Alevi and Lipovan calendars.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and early modern Bulgarian for the *damaskini*; Turkish for Muslim calendar terms in Kardzhali; Russian for Lipovan service books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the balance of powers is a live political fight (the prosecutor's office, judicial appointments), so cite texts, not sides; Pirin and Black Sea coast development disputes are ongoing.
- **Held for review:** none; the Venice Commission opinions on the Supreme Judicial Council remain, cited as texts and not as sides, since the prosecutor's office is a live political fight.
