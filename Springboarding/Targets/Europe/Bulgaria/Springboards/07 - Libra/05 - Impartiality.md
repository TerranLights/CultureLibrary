# Bulgaria: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Church Slavonic for the older vocabulary), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Armenian. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Small towns of the Northwest (Vidin, Montana, Lom) against Sofia: check whether recusal and merit hiring work differently where officials know every family.
- Municipalities of the Kardzhali region and the Smolyan Rhodopes with Turkish or Pomak majorities (use each community's own name): check how hiring in local administration is reported and contested.
- Roma groups in Sofia, Plovdiv (Stolipinovo) and Sliven: check complaints of biased policing and schooling through the ombudsman and court records.
- Pirin region families of Macedonian refugee descent: check how the interwar period of IMRO rule is remembered against later state rule.
- Paulician Catholic and Protestant congregations: check whether they report unequal treatment by officials, and through which church bodies.
- Armenian and Sephardic community bodies: check how their own boards are chosen, as small-scale models of impartial office.
- Party members and non-members of the socialist era: check memoirs from both for the role of the party card in jobs and housing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, corruption surveys and court administration; mixed for regional hiring practice; thin for minority communities' internal governance.
- **Languages needed:** Old Church Slavonic for the codices; nineteenth-century Bulgarian for the Revival press; Turkish for local politics in the Kardzhali region.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** named corruption cases are live and litigated; Bay Ganyo is read as a national caricature, so treat it as satire about resented conduct, not as evidence about people; Petrich-district history bears on relations with North Macedonia.
- **Held for review:** none; the studies of IMRO's grip on the Petrich district stay despite bearing on relations with North Macedonia, because they test how far impartial rule reached in Pirin.
- **Spirit of the law:** trust in judges and the legitimacy of officials belong to Capricorn, section 8.
