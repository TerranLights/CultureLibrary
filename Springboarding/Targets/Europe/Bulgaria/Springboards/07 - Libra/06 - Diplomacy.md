*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox villages of the Shopluk and of Upper Thrace: check how the matchmaker's visit and the bride's family's answer are staged, and how a refusal is softened.
- Turks of the Ludogorie and the Kardzhali region: check the *görücü* visit and community leaders' roles as go-betweens with the state, especially around 1968 and 1989.
- Pomak villages of the Rhodopes (a contested label; use local names): check who speaks for a village to officials, and how that changed after the name campaigns.
- Roma groups (Kalderash, Erli, Turkish-speaking Roma): check the role of the *булюбашия* or other elders as intermediaries, without assuming one model for all.
- Sephardic families: check the *kazamentero* and the community leaders' dealings with ministers in 1943.
- Karakachans: check the *proxenio* between herding families and how they negotiated grazing rights.
- Thracian and Macedonian refugee organizations: check how they lobbied governments over treaties and property after 1913 and 1919.
- Armenians of Plovdiv and Varna: check how community bodies speak to Bulgarian and Armenian state institutions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and church history; mixed for village go-betweens; thin for Karakachan and Gagauz practice.
- **Languages needed:** Greek for the Omurtag inscription and the *Fontes Graeci*; Latin for the Kaloyan letters; Ottoman Turkish for the Exarchate firman; Turkish for 1968 and 1989 testimony; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic family accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relations with North Macedonia over language and history are disputed, and Greek and Turkish scholarship on the same treaties is needed only as comparison; the 1989 departures remain painful.
- **Held for review:** none; Greek and Turkish scholarship on the shared treaties enters only for comparison, while the Exarchate studies and the Craiova records remain the working sources.
