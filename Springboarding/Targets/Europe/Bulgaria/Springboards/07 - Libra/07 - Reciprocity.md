# Bulgaria: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Judeo-Spanish, Armenian. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox villages of the Danubian Plain and of Upper Thrace: check whether *мобá* still operates at harvest and building, and how a debt of labor is remembered.
- Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie: check *imece*, the neighbor's due and the shared *kurban*, and how seasonal migration changed them.
- Alevi villages around the Demir Baba tekke: check offerings and shared meals at gatherings, from their own accounts.
- Pomak villages of the Rhodopes (a contested label; use local names): check whether *kurban* and labor exchange cross religious lines with Christian neighbors.
- Roma groups (Kalderash, Erli, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Roma, Pentecostal congregations): check hospitality and help within and between groups, separately.
- Sephardic families: check the communal societies of mutual aid before emigration in 1948-49 and what remains.
- Gagauz of the Varna area and Lipovans of Dobruja: check gift and help customs; sources are scarce, so start with village museums and parishes.
- Strandzha villages: check how the shared feasts at chapels and springs bind households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ethnography and philanthropy; mixed for present-day informal help; thin for Gagauz, Lipovan and Alevi exchange customs in print.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the Rila commemoration books; Turkish for *imece* and the Muslim calendar of feasts; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic society records; Romani for hospitality terms in fieldwork.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** socialist-era favors shade into corruption claims; Alevi rituals include parts that are closed to outsiders, so record only what communities share.
- **Held for review:** none; Mikov's Alevi studies of shared meals and offerings remain, limited to what communities choose to share, since parts of their rituals are closed to outsiders.
