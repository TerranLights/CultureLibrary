# Bulgaria: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Judeo-Spanish, Armenian. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orthodox families in Sofia against villages of the Northwest: check how violence at home is kept within the family or reported, using hotline and police data, not impressions.
- Turks of the Kardzhali region (Mlechino, Benkovski, Momchilgrad) and of the Ludogorie: check accounts of the choices between protest, endurance and departure in 1984-89.
- Pomaks of the Smolyan and Western Rhodopes (a contested label): check how accommodation and resistance to the name campaigns are remembered by families and by community activists.
- Roma groups (Erli, Kalderash, Muslim Roma, Pentecostal Roma): check reporting of harassment and police abuse, group by group.
- Sephardic families and the town of Kyustendil: check who spoke up in March 1943 and how silence and protest are told in community memory.
- Catholic villages of the Danube (Belene, Oresh) and the Paulician villages near Plovdiv; Protestant congregations: check how persecution shaped a choice between quiet faith and witness.
- Alevi communities around the Demir Baba tekke: check how sharing a shrine with Christians is described by the communities themselves.
- Karakachans: check family accounts of the forced settlement of the 1950s and whether it was resisted or endured.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political history and surveys; mixed for domestic silence; thin for Karakachan and Alevi accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the Theodosius *Life*; early nineteenth-century Bulgarian for Sofronii; Turkish for 1984-89 testimony; Judeo-Spanish for older Sephardic family memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941 and 1943 are among the most argued episodes in Bulgarian history, and the occupied lands are now in other states; the 1984-89 campaign and its victims; domestic violence survivors' identities.
- **Held for review:** none; the Animus hotline reports remain with survivors' identities protected, and Groueff's sympathetic Boris III biography must be weighed against critical histories of 1941.
