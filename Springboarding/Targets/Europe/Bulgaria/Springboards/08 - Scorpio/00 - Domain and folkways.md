# Bulgaria: Death and transformation, domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with its Rhodope, Shop and Balkan dialects), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani; Aromanian, Armenian, Gagauz, Karakachan Greek and Judeo-Spanish for smaller communities. **Concept in one line:** how a culture faces death, power and what is hidden, and its ways with death, sex, shared resources and inheritance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Shopluk around Sofia against the Rhodopes:** check whether *СбНУ* recorded their funeral and *делба* customs with the same questions.
- **Pomak villages (Smolyan, Madan, the Gotse Delchev area):** check which death and inheritance customs are recorded under Islamic or local forms, in the community's own terms.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** check Ottoman-era registers against present practice to see what the 1984-89 period changed in burial and naming.
- **Alevi Kızılbaş and Babai communities (Razgrad, Shumen, Dobruja):** check whether shrine-keeping and saint-veneration records exist apart from Sunni Turkish material.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Muslim and Evangelical Roma):** check each group's own accounts of funerals and pooling, never one "Roma" file.
- **Sephardic Jewish community (Sofia, Plovdiv) and 1943:** check community archives for memorial and burial practice and for how survival and loss are kept.
- **Paulician Catholics (Rakovski), Protestants, Armenians, Lipovan Old Believers, Gagauz, Karakachans, Aromanians:** check for each group's own ethnography; the handbooks may skip them.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families:** check how lost property and graves left behind entered family memory and inheritance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodox village ethnography; mixed for Turks and Pomaks (much written under political pressure); thin for Karakachans, Gagauz and Lipovans, where community associations are the likely route.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for *СбНУ*, IEFEM and Pelin; Ottoman Turkish for the *kassam* registers; modern Turkish for Kardzhali and Ludogorie fieldwork; Romani and Aromanian for the smaller groups' own words on death.
- **Lands:** the tracker records no change to Bulgaria's pre-war lands and Stage 0 has not named units; this Scorpio set is written for Bulgaria as it stands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1984-89 "Revival Process", 1943, Pomak identity labels (contested), sexual diversity (informants may face hostility), and human remains in ossuaries.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside on this domain sheet; the law of wills and marital property is passed to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not held.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the National Library catalog (COBISS) for *погребални обичаи* and *делба*, sorted by region.
2. Pull the *СбНУ* index and list every volume with funeral, wedding or inheritance sections, region by region.
3. Ask the National Library's Oriental Department which *kassam* registers survive, by court and year.
4. Download the NSI tables on deaths by district and births outside marriage, 1990 to the latest year.
5. Search *Български фолклор* and *Българска етнология* for "смърт", "тайна" and "наследство".
