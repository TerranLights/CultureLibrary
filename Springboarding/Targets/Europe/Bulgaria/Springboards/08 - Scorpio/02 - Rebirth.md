# Bulgaria: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Armenian. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pomaks of Madan and Smolyan against those of the Gotse Delchev area (Ribnovo, Kornitsa):** check how each community describes its post-1989 religious life and names, using its own terms for itself.
- **Turks of Kardzhali and the Ludogorie:** check the restoration of names after 1989 in family papers and oral history, kept apart from the state's records.
- **Roma congregations (Evangelical, Orthodox, Muslim):** check conversion accounts in Sofia, Sliven and Kyustendil separately, so one group's testimony is not read as all.
- **Protestants (Bansko, Samokov, Svishtov) and Paulician Catholics (Rakovski):** check older conversion histories in their own archives.
- **Armenians who arrived as refugees after 1915:** check their community press for rebuilding a life and a church.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families:** check village histories of resettlement as a new start.
- **Strandzha nestinari villages:** check how a small set of villages keeps a rite the state now lists as heritage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the National Revival and Orthodox Easter; mixed on conversion (much written by the converting side); thin on recovery from addiction outside NGO reports.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Bulgarian for the 866 letter and the vita; Bulgarian for *Зорница*; English for American Board papers; Turkish for Ludogorie name-restoration accounts; Romani for congregations' testimonies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced name changes and conversions (1912-13, the 1970s, 1984-89) are contested and painful; Alevi rites may be kept within the community; converts may fear family conflict.
- **Held for review:** nothing held on this sheet; disputes over which conversions were forced remain open questions to check, not pointers set aside.
