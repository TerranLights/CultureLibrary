# Bulgaria: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; French and English for exile and émigré sources; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic community writing. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie:** check how 3 March, 1989 and the founding of the Movement for Rights and Freedoms are each remembered, using interviews and Turkish-language press.
- **Pomaks of the Rhodopes and of the Pirin region:** compare family memories of 1912-13, 1942, 1970-74 and 1984-89, since name campaigns reached villages at different dates.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families (Haskovo, Burgas, Blagoevgrad):** check how freedom days and refugee loss are joined in their associations' calendars.
- **Kalderash and other once-traveling Roma:** check oral histories of the 1958 settlement against those of long-settled Erli in Sofia.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** check the sedentarization of herders under collectivization and how it is told by each.
- **The Sephardic community:** check community writings on 1943 and on emigration to Israel after 1948 as two different freedoms.
- **Sofia against small Northwest towns (Vidin, Montana):** compare press-freedom and protest participation reports, since civic freedoms may be lived differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Revival and 1989; mixed for Muslim and Roma experience (recent scholarship, few community-written sources); thin for Karakachan and Aromanian memory. Russia's part in 1878 is comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for the songs, Levski, Hitov, Gruev and Kalyonski; Turkish for muhacir memoirs and Kardzhali interviews; French for Todorov's original; English and Bulgarian for RFE/RL holdings; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic writing on 1943 and emigration.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1912-13, 1984-89 and the camps involve living victims and perpetrators; "робство", "Pomak" and "Bulgarian Muslim" are disputed labels; record each community's own name. Political freedom touches law only as history; the letter and spirit of law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing held; the one cross-border source type (Turkish historiography of 1877-78) is kept as comparison, as the run's rules require.
