# Bulgaria: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (Rhodope and Pirin dialect forms for folk-belief terms); the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Church Slavonic for apocrypha. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Pirin region (Petrich, Sandanski) against the Rhodopes (Smolyan, Chepelare):** check whether beliefs about seers and samodivi are told differently in each mountain area.
- **Christians, Turks and Muslim Roma of Dobruja and the Ludogorie:** compare beliefs attached to shared shrines such as Ak Yazılı Baba, where the same place carries two holy names.
- **Erli and Kalderash Roma:** check Ederlezi customs and evil-eye remedies by group, since the two groups' calendars and faiths differ.
- **The Shopluk (Sofia, Pernik, Breznik):** check votive-cross and survakari customs as a western belief complex.
- **Pomak villages of the western Rhodopes (Velingrad, Yakoruda):** compare household folk-belief practice with what local imams teach, as researchers report both.
- **Catholic Paulician villages and Orthodox neighbors near Plovdiv:** check whether saints' days and folk beliefs differ across the confessional line.
- **Gagauz and Karakachans:** check for any recorded folk-belief material; likely thin, so start with local museums and community associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief (a century of ethnography); mixed for present-day survey belief; thin for Gagauz, Karakachan and Aromanian belief.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the apocrypha; Bulgarian and Rhodope dialect for Arnaudov, Valtchinova and museum notes; Turkish for nazar and Hıdrellez customs; Romani for Ederlezi songs and remedies; English for Hasluck.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** charming and healing knowledge may be passed only to chosen heirs, so ask before recording words of charms; Vanga's legacy is commercialized and politicized; apotropaic-burial stories invite sensational reporting.
- **Held for review:** none for belief; the healing-charm texts are flagged above as possibly restricted rather than set aside.
