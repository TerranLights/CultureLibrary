## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pomaks of Kornitsa and the Gotse Delchev valley against Pomaks of the Smolyan region:** check how each remembers 1972-73 and the Rodina society, since stands were taken in both directions.
- **Turks of the Ludogorie against Turks of the Kardzhali region:** compare memoirs of May 1989, as the protests did not reach every area at the same time or in the same way.
- **The Sephardic community and Kyustendil residents:** check how the town where the 1943 delegation began and the community itself each tell that spring.
- **Catholics of the Nikopol diocese (Belene, Oresh) and of the Plovdiv villages:** compare how each keeps the memory of the 1952 trial.
- **Ruse and the Danubian Plain against Sofia:** check whether 1988-89 activists from the provinces tell a different story from capital-based dissidents.
- **Protestant, Tolstoyan and pacifist circles of Burgas and Sofia:** check records of refusals of military service before 1944.
- **Generations of protesters (1989-90, 1997, 2013, 2020):** compare manifestos for what each called "a matter of principle".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1943, the Revival and 1989; mixed for minority stands (recent scholarship, some memoirs); thin for Tolstoyans and Catholic lay memory outside church publications.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the martyrs' lives; Bulgarian for Donchev, Ralin, Botev and COMDOS files; Italian for Nissim; Turkish for 1989 memoirs from the Ludogorie and Kardzhali.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "потурнак" and conversion ballads are offensive to many Muslims and must be quoted only as recorded terms; the 1943 story has two sides (rescue and deportation), and both must be kept; security files on the 1972-73 and 1989 events name informers and victims.
- **Held for review:** nothing held for conviction; the conversion ballads are kept with a warning, not set aside, as the research needs them as contested evidence.
