# Bulgaria: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Ottoman Turkish for one early travel account; German for border-death research. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Shopluk and Pirin villages against the Tarnovo and Gabrovo area:** check how "печалба" and "гурбет" are used in each, since the words map different labor routes.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of Dobruja:** compare memories of 1950-51, 1969-78 and 1989 departures and of seasonal work in Turkey and Western Europe.
- **Pomaks of the western Rhodopes:** check present seasonal work abroad and the Hajj as two kinds of long journey.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** compare how each recalls pastoral routes and settlement after the 1950s.
- **Ursari, Kalderash and Rudari Roma:** check how once-mobile trades and present labor migration differ by group.
- **Black Sea resort towns (Nesebar, Sozopol, Golden Sands) against inland Northwest towns (Vidin, Montana):** check tourism jobs and emigration rates as two opposite pulls.
- **Thracian refugee families and the Sephardic families who emigrated after 1948:** check each community's own record of leaving and arriving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Revival-era travel writing, emigration statistics and tourism; mixed for labor migration songs; thin for Karakachan and Aromanian routes. Bessarabian and Banat Bulgarians and the Bulgarian Turks now in Turkey are comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman and modern Turkish for Evliya Çelebi and 1989 accounts; Bulgarian for Konstantinov, Vazov, Miletich and the labor songs; German for the border research; Romani for Ursari and Kalderash narratives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "изселник" and "Голямата екскурзия" carry a political charge (the second is ironic), so record each family's own words; border deaths involve living relatives; Bai Ganyo has been read as a national caricature, so treat the book as a source on how the traveler was seen, not as a portrait.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside for travel; the Turkish and German materials enter only for the journeys that crossed Bulgaria's own borders.
