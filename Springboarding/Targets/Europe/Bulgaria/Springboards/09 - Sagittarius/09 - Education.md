## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and of the Ludogorie:** compare uptake of Turkish mother-tongue classes and of Islamic secondary schools, since local school offers differ.
- **Pomaks of the Smolyan and Gotse Delchev regions:** check memoirs and studies on schooling under the name campaigns and the choice of religious instruction after 1991.
- **Roma quarters of Vidin, Sliven and Plovdiv:** compare desegregation programs and community-run schools by city.
- **Gabrovo, Koprivshtitsa and the Stara Planina towns against Sofia:** check how Revival school heritage is used in local identity and present school choice.
- **Sephardic, Armenian and Catholic families:** check each community's school history before 1948 and what replaced it.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and the Danubian Plain against Sofia and Varna:** compare matura and attainment data, as the regional gap is a research question, not a given.
- **Karakachans and Aromanians:** check community associations for Greek- or Aromanian-language classes; likely thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Revival and for statistics; mixed for minority education (good NGO and IMIR work, fewer histories); thin for Karakachan and Aromanian schooling.
- **Languages needed:** Old Church Slavonic for Hrabar; Bulgarian for Beron, Vazov and МОН data; French for the AIU archive and Saint-Augustin records; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic memoirs of AIU schools; Turkish for Islamic-school and mother-tongue sources; Romani for parents' accounts in Vidin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** school segregation and dropout data can be read as blame on Roma families; present them as questions about schools and policy; Turkish-language schooling is tied to socialist-era restrictions that are still debated, so date each change from documents.
- **Held for review:** nothing held for education; Robert College in Istanbul, where many Bulgarians studied, enters only as comparison under the run's rule on Turkey.
