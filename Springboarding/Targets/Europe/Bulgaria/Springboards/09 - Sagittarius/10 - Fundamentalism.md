# Bulgaria: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Church Slavonic and Byzantine Greek for medieval anti-heresy texts; Arabic religious terms as used by Bulgarian Muslims; Romani for Roma congregations. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular and political certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pazardzhik's Iztok quarter and other Roma Muslim neighborhoods:** check how residents themselves name the religious currents there, since outside labels and police language dominate the record.
- **Kızılbaş and Babai Alevi villages of the Ludogorie and the east Rhodopes:** compare their accounts of new mosques and Sunni teachers with the muftiate's account.
- **Turks and Pomaks of the Kardzhali and Smolyan regions:** check where young people learned Islam after 1989 (local hodzhas, Turkey, Arab countries) and how their elders respond.
- **Old Calendarists and Lipovan Old Believers of Dobruja:** compare two restorationist Orthodox minorities that kept an older rite or calendar.
- **Roma Pentecostals of Sliven and Plovdiv against Orthodox neighbors:** check which practices each calls strict or worldly.
- **Protestants, Jehovah's Witnesses and the White Brotherhood:** check how each recalls being called a "sect" in the 1990s.
- **Sofia's secular public:** check press usage of "фундаментализъм" against church spokesmen's usage, since the word may be aimed one way only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval heresy and post-1989 Islam; mixed for Orthodox strictness; thin for Old Calendarists and neo-pagan groups.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the Synodikon and Theodosius's Life; English for Obolensky and Merdjanova; Arabic religious vocabulary for Evstatiev's subject; Turkish for Alevi and Kardzhali interviews; Romani for Roma church services.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "радикален ислям", "уахабити", "секта" and "сектант" are charged labels that have led to prosecutions and harassment; quote them only as others' words; trials on religious charges belong to Libra; no group is to be described as fundamentalist on outside say-so.
- **Held for review:** nothing held for fundamentalism; security-service sources on present-day radicalization are left out as too restricted for a springboard.
