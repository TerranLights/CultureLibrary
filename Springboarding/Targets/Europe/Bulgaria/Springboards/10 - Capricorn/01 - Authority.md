## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bulgarian Orthodox parishes, rural and urban:** check how far the priest's word reaches in village life against Sofia or Plovdiv; the rival-synod years may show different patterns by diocese.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** compare where Sunni authority (imam, mufti) and local office (mayor) meet; their written sources differ in language.
- **Pomak villages of the Rhodopes and the Pirin region (Gotse Delchev area):** check which figures communities name as authorities; contested labels for the community must stay labeled as contested.
- **Kızılbaş and Babai Alevi communities:** check hereditary religious authority, distinct from Sunni structures, as the communities describe it.
- **Roma of several groups (Kalderash, Erli, others), Christian and Muslim:** check how a leader is recognized and whether evangelical pastors now hold that role in some neighborhoods.
- **Bulgarian Catholics of the Plovdiv villages and Protestants:** compare clergy authority under two church polities.
- **Sephardic families of Ruse, Sofia and Plovdiv (historical):** household authority in memoirs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state, church and survey sources; thinner for Gagauz, Lipovan and Karakachan communities, where the regional museums and community associations are the place to start.
- **Languages needed:** Greek for Beshevliev's inscriptions; Old Bulgarian for Cosmas; Turkish for the mufti disputes and Hoca tales; German for Canetti; Armenian for community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the State Security files name people still living; the synod split and the mufti disputes remain contested.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; every authority pointer here is local or a pan-European dataset.
