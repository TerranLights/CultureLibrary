# Bulgaria: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Armenian, Judeo-Spanish (historical), Gagauz. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bulgarian Orthodox villages of the Danubian Plain and the Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check memorial-feast duties in villages emptied by migration, where the dead may outnumber the living kin.
- **Pomak communities of the Rhodopes and Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** compare *kurban* vows and religious obligations in two languages; separate the communities by their own names.
- **Kızılbaş and Babai Alevi villages:** check duties tied to the *muhabbet* gatherings and to companionship vows (*musahiplik*), as communities describe them.
- **Roma of several groups and faiths:** check duties among kin and to evangelical congregations; Muslim, Orthodox and Protestant Roma may differ.
- **Bulgarian Catholics of the Plovdiv villages (Rakovski area):** compare saint's-day vows and parish duties under a Catholic calendar.
- **Sephardic community, before and after 1943:** compare duties within the community and to the state in memoirs and community records.
- **Emigrant households of the Pirin region and Upper Thrace:** check how remittance duty is shared among siblings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Bulgarian Orthodox ritual duty and socialist-era labor duty; mixed for Muslim communities (strong in Turkish and Bulgarian ethnology, weaker in survey data); thin for Gagauz and Armenian community duties.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and early vernacular for the *damaskini*; Turkish for *Мюсюлмани* and the *kurban* literature; French for Todorov; Judeo-Spanish for Sephardic community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1943 is contested history (rescue within the old borders, deportation from the occupied lands); give both. The brigade movement mixed volunteering and pressure; let participants' own accounts lead.
- **Held for review:** none; the vow, memorial and 1943 leads all come from the nation's own Christian, Muslim and Jewish records.

5. Search *Ethnologia Balkanica* for "kurban".
