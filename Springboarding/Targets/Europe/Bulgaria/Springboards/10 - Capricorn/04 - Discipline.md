## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rila, Bachkovo and other monastic communities:** check whether the old rules are still read and kept; monastic and lay discipline need separate treatment.
- **Turkish villages of the Kardzhali region and Pomak villages of the Rhodopes and Pirin:** compare how Ramazan and Qur'an schooling shape children's routines, in each community's own language.
- **Roma converts and non-converts in the same neighborhood (for example in Plovdiv, Sliven or Kyustendil):** check whether accounts of a changed rule of life differ by church and by group.
- **Bulgarian Catholics of the Plovdiv villages:** compare Lenten rules under a Catholic calendar with the Orthodox fasts next door.
- **Stara Planina craft towns (Tryavna, Gabrovo):** check how apprenticeship discipline was remembered as guilds gave way to factories.
- **Military towns and the naval academy in Varna:** check cadet and officer accounts against conscripts'.
- **Karakachan and Aromanian herding families:** check whether pastoral work routines are described as a training of the young; sources likely scarce, start with community associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school, army and sport; mixed for the home (surveys exist, ethnography is scattered); thin for Gagauz and Lipovan Old Believer communities, whose fasting rules are known mostly from religious sources.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian and Greek for the Rila testament and the hesychast texts; Turkish for Ramazan sermons; Romani for Pentecostal Roma testimony; Russian (Lipovan) for Old Believer fasting rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child punishment, abuse in children's institutions and in elite sport involve identifiable people; use published reports. Conversion accounts belong to the communities.
- **Held for review:** none; the monastic, school and sport leads here are all Bulgarian or Orthodox-world sources.
