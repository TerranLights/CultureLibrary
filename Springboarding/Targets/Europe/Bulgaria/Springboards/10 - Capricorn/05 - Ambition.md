# Bulgaria: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani, Armenian, Aromanian. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pomak and Turkish tobacco villages (Rhodopes, Kardzhali region):** compare what counts as getting ahead when tobacco, migrant work and schooling compete.
- **Roma of several groups:** separate musicians', traders' and laborers' paths; check how communities speak of success rather than reading outsiders' views.
- **Pirin region (Blagoevgrad, Bansko) and the Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** compare long migrant traditions with recent emigration from shrinking towns.
- **Sofia's tech and finance workers against provincial towns:** check whether aspirations and ladders differ by place.
- **Armenian merchant families of Plovdiv, Varna and Ruse:** check the community's own histories of trade and craft.
- **Aromanian and Karakachan families who moved from herding to towns:** sources scarce; begin with community associations.
- **Bulgarian Catholic villages near Plovdiv:** check migration to Western Europe as a path, through parish records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Revival merchants, literature and youth surveys; mixed for socialist careers; thin for Aromanian, Karakachan and Gagauz paths to advancement.
- **Languages needed:** Greek for Mystikos; Old Bulgarian for the Exarch; Turkish for Rhodope and Kardzhali tobacco testimony; Romani for musicians' own accounts; Armenian for community histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chalga and "new rich" debates carry class and ethnic contempt; record them as a quarrel. The "neighbor's goat" saying on envy is a self-stereotype: search for its use, do not adopt it.
- **Held for review:** none; the striving leads here come from Bulgarian, Romani and Byzantine-era records.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Paisius's preface and Dimov's two editions side by side; list what changed in the ambitious characters.
2. Search COBISS.BG for "печалбарство" and "Брацигово майстори".
3. Download the FES Bulgaria youth study and list items on work, luck and connections.
4. Search the Central State Archive guide for Hero of Socialist Labor award files.
5. Search Jobs.bg for one month of ads in Sofia, Plovdiv and Kardzhali and note the qualities asked for.
