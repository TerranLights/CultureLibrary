# Bulgaria: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Aromanian; Karakachan Greek. **Concept in one line:** the domain of community and ideals: what binds people into groups (friends, associations, neighbors, social ways) and what they hope for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shopluk villages against Stara Planina craft towns (Gabrovo, Tryavna, Koprivshtitsa):** check whether village assemblies and guild-town associations made different routes into a group.
- **Pomaks of the Rhodopes and the Chech (Satovcha, Ribnovo):** check mosque-and-village gatherings, and how the name "Pomak" is used or refused locally (contested).
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie:** check *cemaat* and *imece* life and how the 1984-89 name changes and 1989 departures altered village membership.
- **Alevi (Kızılbaş, Babai) near Demir Baba Teke and Otman Baba Teke:** check whether teke pilgrimages draw people across community lines.
- **Roma groups and faiths (Erli, Kalderash, Rudari, Turkish-speaking Muslim Roma, Evangelicals) in Fakulteta, Stolipinovo, Nadezhda:** check the boundaries each draws in its own names.
- **Paulician Catholics around Rakovski; Banat Bulgarian returnees at Bardarski Geran:** check how far the parish frames community.
- **Smaller groups:** Karakachans, Aromanians, Armenians of Plovdiv and Varna, the Sephardic community, Gagauz near Varna, Lipovans of Tataritsa and Kazashko, Vlachs of the Vidin Northwest, Thracian and Macedonian refugee families: check each one's own societies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodox village ethnography and the chitalishte; mixed for Turks and Pomaks; thin for Gagauz, Lipovans, Karakachans and Aromanians, whose own associations and local museums come first.
- **Lands:** the exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; this sign's sheets are written for Bulgaria as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for SbNU and the IEFEM-BAN notebooks; Ottoman Turkish for the *sicil*; German for Kanitz; Romani and Aromanian for groups' own names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census ethnic categories; Pomak naming; the "Revival Process"; refugee associations whose claims touch North Macedonia and Greece.
- **Held for review:** none; the gathering and association sources here are Bulgarian, Ottoman or Central European, and none had to be set aside.
