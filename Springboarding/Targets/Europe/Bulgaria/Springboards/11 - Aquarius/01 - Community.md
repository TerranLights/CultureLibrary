# Bulgaria: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Armenian; Judeo-Spanish (historical); Romani. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest, cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Emptying villages of the Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and the Strandzha:** check what keeps a community going when most members are elderly or absent, and who returns for the *sabor*.
- **Old Sofia families against migrants from the provinces:** check whether *землячества* act as a substitute community in the capital.
- **Pomak villages of the western Rhodopes (Yakoruda, Ribnovo):** check how seasonal work abroad reshapes who counts as a resident member.
- **Turkish villages of the Kardzhali region:** check the mosque board as a community frame and how it relates to the village mayor.
- **Roma quarters (Stolipinovo, Fakulteta, Nadezhda):** check internal divisions by group and faith before treating any quarter as one community.
- **Sephardic Jews, Armenians, and Lipovan Old Believers of Tataritsa:** check how small congregation-centered communities sustain themselves.
- **Karakachans:** check the yearly gathering near Sliven (Karandila *check*) as a dispersed people's way of meeting as a community.
- **Danubian towns (Ruse, Svishtov) and Black Sea towns (Varna, Burgas):** check whether port and river towns show different neighborhood forms from inland towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodox villages and towns (monographs, parish records, literature); mixed for Muslim and Roma communities; thin for Lipovans and Karakachans, best reached through their own associations.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for the monographs, parish records and Pamporov; Turkish for mosque-board and Kardzhali-region material; Armenian for parish publications; Judeo-Spanish for older Sephardic records; English for Kaneff and Eurofound.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "mahala" can carry a slur for Roma quarters; parish and mosque records may hold personal data; village depopulation is politically charged in regional press.
- **Held for review:** none; the community pointers above are local, ecclesiastical or European-survey sources and nothing was set aside.
