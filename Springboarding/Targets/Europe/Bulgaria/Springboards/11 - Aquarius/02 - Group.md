# Bulgaria: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani (several dialects); Aromanian. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plovdiv against Sofia football publics (Botev, Lokomotiv, Levski, CSKA):** check whether club loyalty maps onto neighborhood or class divisions in each city.
- **Northwest cooperative villages against Rhodope hamlets:** check how collectivization met different household and kin structures.
- **Turks of the Ludogorie and the Kardzhali region:** check how "us" is drawn after the 1984-89 name changes, without treating the community as one voice.
- **Pomaks of the Chech and the western Rhodopes:** check self-names (Pomak, Bulgarian Muslim, Turk, Ahryan) as contested labels, and who uses which.
- **Roma subgroups and Roma Pentecostal congregations:** check whether conversion creates a new in-group cutting across older subgroup lines.
- **Alevi (Kızılbaş) of Razgrad and Isperih:** check how *cem* gatherings mark membership apart from Sunni neighbors.
- **Paulician Catholics around Rakovski:** check the parish and old surnames as markers of a bounded group.
- **Thracian refugee families in Haskovo and Harmanli, and Macedonian refugee families in Blagoevgrad and Sofia:** check how refugee associations formed "us" in a new town.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revolutionary bands, socialist mass organizations and party politics; mixed for minority group boundaries (good scholarship, contested labels); thin for Alevi and Aromanian group rules, where community associations and fieldwork come first.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for Stoyanov, Gruev and the DKMS fonds; Turkish for Ludogorie Alevi and Kardzhali material; Romani for subgroup self-names in Marushiakova and Popov's field data; English for Todorova and Neuburger.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "Revival Process"; Pomak and Roma labels; State Security files (named informers, living persons); football violence and racist chanting; claims of bloc voting by ethnicity.
- **Held for review:** none; the group sources here are Bulgarian, Ottoman-era and European, and none was set aside under the standing rules.
