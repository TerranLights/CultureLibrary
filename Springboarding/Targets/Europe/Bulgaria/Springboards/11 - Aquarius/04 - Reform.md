## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gabrovo, Tryavna and Koprivshtitsa against the Danubian towns (Ruse, Svishtov):** check whether Revival reform came more from merchant towns or from the Ottoman reforms of the Danube Vilayet.
- **Agrarian villages of the Danubian Plain and Dobruja against Sofia:** check how the Agrarian Union's reforms were remembered in villages and in the capital.
- **Turks of the Ludogorie and Shumen:** check modernist and conservative camps in Muslim schooling, and how they answered state campaigns on dress and script.
- **Pomaks of the Smolyan and Pirin regions:** check how the Rodina program and later name campaigns are described by different Pomak families today (contested; no single voice).
- **Roma converts and non-converts in the same quarter (e.g. Stolipinovo):** check whether conversion is described as reform, rupture or both.
- **Protestant converts of Bansko and Samokov:** check temperance and schooling reform in a small minority's own records.
- **Ruse and Vratsa:** check environmental reform movements in industrial towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Revival education, the Agrarian Union and 1987-89; mixed for Muslim reform debates; thin for Karakachan and Aromanian experience of settlement and collectivization as reform imposed on them.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish for *Tuna* and Tanzimat-era sources; Revival-era Bulgarian spelling for the periodicals and *Неделник*; Turkish for the Nüvvab and interwar Muslim press; English for Bell.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Rodina society and the "Revival Process" as reforms imposed on Muslim communities; living persons in corruption reporting; judicial and legal reform belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** none; reform pointers here are Bulgarian, Ottoman, Protestant-missionary or EU-level sources, and none was set aside.
