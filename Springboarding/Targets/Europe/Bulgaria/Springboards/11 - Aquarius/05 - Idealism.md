# Bulgaria: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (including Old Bulgarian for medieval texts); the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani for Roma organizational sources. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society (utopias, model communities, golden ages).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Strandzha and the Burgas coast:** check how the Tolstoyan colony and the 1903 rising are remembered locally, and by Thracian refugee families.
- **Rila and Sofia followers of the White Brotherhood against Orthodox clergy:** check how each describes the movement, without taking either account as fact.
- **Generations in Sofia, Plovdiv and Vidin:** check whether talk of socialist ideals, "bright future" irony and post-1989 disillusion differs by age and town.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and Pomaks of the Smolyan region:** check how ideals taught through schools and the state press were received or refused.
- **Roma activists and congregations:** check uplift visions in Roma organizations from the interwar years to present NGOs.
- **Armenians of Plovdiv and the Sephardic community:** check how each community's own ideals (schools, charities, emigration to Armenia or Israel after 1945) are written about.
- **Bulgarian Catholics of the Plovdiv villages and Protestants:** check whether their schools and missions frame different public ideals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Revival and socialist-era ideals and literature; mixed for intentional communities (small, scattered records); thin for Roma and Turkish idealist movements before 1989.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian and Church Slavonic for Preslav texts; Bulgarian for Botev, Milev and Gerdzhikov; Turkish for *Yeni Işık*; Romani and Bulgarian for Pashov-era material; English for Peace Corps accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1923 uprising and its repression; Zhivkova's program (family politics); the White Brotherhood's place in Orthodox debate; the Strandzha rising's ties to Macedonian-Thracian movements claimed by several countries.
- **Held for review:** none; the movements and texts named here, including the spiritual ones, are Bulgarian or Christian-sectarian in origin, so nothing needed setting aside.
