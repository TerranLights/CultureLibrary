# Bulgaria: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian and its dialects; the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Armenian; Aromanian. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men's *кафене* circles in villages of the Danubian Plain against young people in Sofia's student town:** check where friendships are now made and kept.
- **Turks and Pomaks of the Kardzhali and Smolyan regions:** check whether *kirve* bonds and neighbor friendships cross faith and language lines, village by village.
- **Shopluk and Pirin villages:** check how *kumstvo* is chosen among friends and inherited in families, and whether the two regions differ.
- **Roma of several groups in Sliven and Plovdiv:** check friendship ties across subgroups and with non-Roma neighbors, from the groups' own accounts.
- **Armenian and Sephardic communities of Plovdiv and Sofia:** check friendship across community lines in memoirs.
- **Karakachan and Aromanian families:** check whether old pastoral partnerships became lasting friendships after settlement; data likely scarce.
- **Hunters, football supporters and veterans' groups:** check activity-based friendship among men across regions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ritual friendship in folklore and for survey contact rates; mixed for present-day men's and women's friendships; thin for Karakachans, Aromanians and Gagauz, where oral history and local associations are the way in.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian (including Rhodope dialect) for the folk-song and Marinov material; Turkish for *kirvelik* fieldwork; Romani for subgroup ties; Armenian for community memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *връзки* and cronyism touch named living people; Soviet-friendship symbols (monuments) are politically charged; minority friendship accounts after 1984-89 can be painful.
- **Held for review:** none; ritual friendship, film and forum pointers are all Bulgarian, Balkan or EU-level, and nothing was set aside.
