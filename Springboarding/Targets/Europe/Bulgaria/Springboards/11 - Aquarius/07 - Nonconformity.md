# Bulgaria: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (modern, and Old Bulgarian for medieval polemic); the Turkish of Bulgaria; Romani; Church Slavonic and Russian for the Lipovan Old Believers. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature, and how far that departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sofia and Plovdiv youth scenes against small towns of the Northwest and Strandzha:** check where subcultures could form and how they were treated by schools and police.
- **Alevi (Kızılbaş, Babai) of the Ludogorie and Sunni neighbors:** check how heterodoxy is described from inside and outside, without taking either as fact.
- **Bulgarian Catholics of the Paulician villages:** check how their descent from a medieval dissenting movement is told in their own parish histories.
- **Lipovans of Tataritsa and Kazashko:** check dress and rule-keeping as deliberate difference from neighbors.
- **Roma musicians and Roma Evangelical converts:** check whether musicians and converts face different kinds of pressure to conform.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and the Ludogorie in 1989:** check how protest and refusal are remembered by those who stayed (emigrants are comparison only).
- **Protestants of Bansko and Samokov:** check how a small dissenting church was seen in Orthodox towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval heresy, socialist dissent and pop-folk debate; mixed for subcultures (fragmented press, fan memory); thin for Lipovans and Babai Alevis, where parish and community contacts come first.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian for Cosmas and the *Synodikon*; Bulgarian for Markov and the youth-institute reports; Turkish for the May 1989 documents and Alevi rites; Romani for musicians' own accounts in Silverman's field sites; Russian and Church Slavonic for Lipovan records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBTI people and counter-protests; the 1989 protests and expulsions; State Security surveillance of subcultures; Alevi rites may be closed to outsiders, so ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** none; dissent, music and heterodoxy pointers here come from Bulgarian, Balkan and Anglo-American scholarship, and none had to be withheld.
