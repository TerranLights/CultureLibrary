*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox monastic communities (Rila, Bachkovo, Troyan) and lay pilgrims:** check how withdrawal is taught and how visitors describe it.
- **Alevi-Bektashi communities near Teketo and Demir Baba Teke:** check renunciation teachings in their own words, separate from Sunni practice.
- **Rhodope Pomak and Christian villages, and Shopluk villages:** check lament and mourning customs for differences in voiced grief.
- **Elderly residents of the Vidin and Montana districts:** check whether living alone is described as chosen or imposed.
- **Sofia professionals (doctors, journalists, judges' staff):** check how professional distance is taught and judged.
- **Karakachan and Aromanian former shepherds:** check how pastoral solitude is remembered after settlement; sources likely scarce.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region and Roma of several groups:** check how civic withdrawal and distrust are described in each, without taking any single account as the group's voice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for monastic and hagiographic sources and EU survey data; mixed for everyday emotional style (few direct studies); thin for pastoral solitude and for Alevi renunciation practice.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian and Church Slavonic for the Rila and hesychast texts; Ottoman Turkish for the Otman Baba *velayetname*; Bulgarian dialect for lament recordings; Karakachan Greek for pastoral memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** laments and funeral rites are private; Alevi rites may be closed to outsiders; outsiders' "cold or warm" claims must stay labeled as claims.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist meditation groups in Bulgarian cities, a real lead for contemplative detachment, held as a category only while Buddhism is an open question for the author; none is named.
