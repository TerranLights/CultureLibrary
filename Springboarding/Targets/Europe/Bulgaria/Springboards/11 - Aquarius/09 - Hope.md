# Bulgaria: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Strandzha villages (Bulgari, Brodilovo) and the Burgas coast:** check how the nestinari feast is described by villagers, Thracian refugee families and visitors.
- **Rhodope Christians and Pomaks:** check petitions at Krastova Gora and at local *türbe* or chapels, and whether the same families visit both.
- **Turks of the Kardzhali region:** check hope tied to gurbet in Turkey and Western Europe, as families who stayed describe it.
- **Dobruja and the Ludogorie:** check Christian and Muslim visits to Akyazılı Baba and Demir Baba, from each side.
- **Roma of the Nadezhda quarter (Sliven) and Stolipinovo (Plovdiv):** check young people's stated hopes in NGO and school surveys.
- **The Sephardic community of Sofia and Plovdiv:** check how 1943 and the 1948-49 departures for Israel are remembered by those who stayed.
- **Armenians of Plovdiv and Varna and Gagauz near Varna:** check hopes voiced in community papers and parish life.
- **Vidin and Montana (the Northwest):** check whether hope is framed around emigration of the young.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the folk calendar, pilgrimage and survey expectations; mixed for minority hopes; thin for Gagauz and Armenian community voices, which need their own associations.
- **Languages needed:** Old Bulgarian for the Apocryphal Chronicle; Bulgarian for Arnaudov and Vaptsarov; Turkish for gurbet accounts from the Kardzhali region; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for older Sephardic records; Armenian for community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1943 memory (the rescue and the deportations from the occupied lands) is contested between countries and communities; Vanga's cult is disputed; shrine visits are private.
- **Held for review:** none; the hope sources here are Bulgarian, Balkan, Sephardic or international surveys, and none was set aside.
