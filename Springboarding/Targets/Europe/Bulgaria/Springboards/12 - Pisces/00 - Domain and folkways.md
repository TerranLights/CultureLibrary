# Bulgaria: The domain: the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (Cyrillic; Rhodope, Shop and Balkan dialect forms), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani (Erli, Kalderash); Armenian for one term. **Concept in one line:** how a culture meets suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape (the hidden and the unconscious, confinement and institutions, sacrifice, compassion, transcendence), with magic ways as the domain's folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pomak villages of the central Rhodopes (Smolyan, Madan) and Turkish villages around Kardzhali:** check each one's words for spirits, amulets and whoever reads over the sick; same mountains, different languages.
- **The Shopluk and the Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check whether rusalii healing dances and survakari masks keep a separate calendar of the unseen; sources are dialect-heavy.
- **Strandzha and Thracian refugee families settled after 1913:** check how newcomers took up or reshaped rites such as the fire dance.
- **Alevi Kızılbaş and Babai of the Ludogorie (Deliorman), beside Sunni Turks:** scholarship treats their shrines apart; check both before merging.
- **Roma groups (Erli of Sofia, Kalderash, Muslim and Pentecostal Roma):** check whether conversion changed dealings with the dead and healers.
- **Paulician Catholics near Plovdiv (Rakovski) and Protestants:** parish and mission records are a separate confessional source to check.
- **Sephardic, Armenian, Aromanian, Karakachan, Gagauz and Lipovan Old Believer communities:** sources are thin; check community associations, registers and memoirs.
- **Sofia and Varna against Danubian Plain towns (Pleven, Ruse):** check where therapy is used and where healers are still sought.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Bulgarian folk belief (a century of collecting); mixed for institutions (communist-era files partly open); thin for smaller communities. Bulgaria's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this pass is written for Bulgaria as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian with dialect help for SbNU and the folklore archive; Turkish for the Demir Baba and Kardzhali shrine material; Romani for Marushiakova and Popov's field texts; Armenian for the madagh term.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the camps, the 1984-89 "Revival Process" name changes and Pomak identity are contested ground; healers' knowledge only as they share it.
- **Held for review:** none here; the Bogomil pointer stays, but only as hostile testimony, never as the movement's own voice.
