## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox villages against Pomak and Turkish villages (Smolyan, Kardzhali):** check Задушница giving beside kurban and sadaka; both feed the poor through rites for the dead.
- **Roma neighborhoods (Fakulteta in Sofia, Stolipinovo in Plovdiv, Nadezhda in Sliven):** check whether help arrives through kin, churches or the state, and how recipients name it.
- **Stara Planina towns (Gabrovo, Tryavna, Koprivshtitsa):** check merchant benefactors' wills and endowments, a distinct donor tradition.
- **Jewish families of Sofia, Plovdiv and Kyustendil:** check how 1943 is told inside the community and how others tell it.
- **Armenian families of Plovdiv and Varna, many from post-1915 refugees:** check their own records of how they were received.
- **Thracian and Macedonian refugee families:** check settlement-era petitions for what was asked and what was given.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) against Sofia:** check whether giving and social aid reach the poorest region at the same rate.
- **Karakachan and Lipovan communities:** likely thin; check their associations for mutual-aid records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1943 rescue and Revival-era charity; mixed for present-day giving; thin for small communities.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish (Arabic script) for the waqf deeds; Turkish for the Mufti's Office and village sadaka; Romani for church-based aid in Roma quarters; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for Shalom's older papers; French for the League of Nations files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 story is politically used; keep the occupied-territory deportations in view. Begging and Roma poverty are stigma-laden; record words, not judgments. Mercy to offenders as law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the charity-of-the-state sources from 1944-1989 still need a pointer of their own.
