## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sredna Gora and Upper Thrace towns (Koprivshtitsa, Panagyurishte, Batak):** check how each town's 1876 commemoration differs, since the uprising's memory is local.
- **Rhodope Pomak villages near Batak:** the memory of 1876 there is contested; check how local accounts name the events, and never assign blame by group.
- **Strandzha and Thracian refugee families:** check whether 1903 and 1913 displace 1876 in family stories.
- **Turks of Kardzhali and the Ludogorie:** check şehit memorials for 1989 and the vocabulary of sacrifice in Turkish sources.
- **Alevi Kızılbaş and Babai villages:** check Muharrem fasting and aşure as a separate calendar of self-denial.
- **Roma (Erli and others) at Gergiovden:** check the lamb kurban as offering, and its meaning in each group's own words.
- **Catholic and Protestant communities:** check their martyrs of the 1950s, absent from the national canon.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check the 1923 September Uprising cult, built up under socialism and since reassessed, beside the migration of working-age parents abroad.
- **Armenian families of Plovdiv and Varna:** check survivors' memoirs for sacrifice in flight.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national martyrdom (textbooks, memorials); mixed for family sacrifice; thin for Alevi and Roma sacrifice in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Pop Peyo's vita; Bulgarian for Stoyanov and Vazov; Turkish for 1989 testimony and Alevi Muharrem sources; Romani for Gergiovden kurban; Armenian for survivors' memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1876 and its neighbors' memory is contested; the 1989 events and the self-immolations involve living families; the socialist-era martyr cult is a political object.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the 1923 and 1944 commemorations need a second pointer from an archive not tied to either side.
