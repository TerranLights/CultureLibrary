# Bulgaria: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Sea coast (Varna, Burgas, Sozopol):** check resort workers' and residents' views beside visitors' accounts of escape.
- **Bansko and the Pirin ski slopes:** check how rapid resort growth is discussed by long-time townspeople.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana) and Danubian Plain towns:** check emigration and drinking data together, since both are proposed routes out of hardship there.
- **Turkish families of the Kardzhali region:** check seasonal work abroad and Turkish television as separate escapes; the 1989 emigration itself is comparison only.
- **Pomak families of the Chepino valley (Velingrad):** check how the spa economy and seasonal work shape leisure.
- **Roma musicians of Upper Thrace (Haskovo, Stara Zagora):** check the wedding circuit as work for some and release for others.
- **Protestant, Pentecostal and Lipovan Old Believer congregations:** check what each community's own church rules say about drink and gambling, and how members describe keeping them.
- **Sofia's young adults:** check club, gaming and streaming habits against village elders' leisure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tourism history and substance-use surveys; mixed for gambling; thin for minority leisure in its own words.
- **Languages needed:** Bulgarian for Markov, Vezhinov and union records; Turkish for viewers' talk about the dramas and for Kardzhali work-abroad interviews; Romani for musicians' accounts of the wedding circuit; English for ESPAD reports; Church Slavonic (Middle Bulgarian) for the medieval Lives of Ivan of Rila, where no translation serves.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction and gambling debt involve private suffering; Roma and drinking are stereotype-laden topics, so record only sources, never group traits; Markov's 1978 murder is a political case.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; psychoactive-substance laws are left to Libra.
