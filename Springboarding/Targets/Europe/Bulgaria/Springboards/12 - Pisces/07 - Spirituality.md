# Bulgaria: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian, the Turkish of Bulgaria (with Ottoman Turkish for one text), Judeo-Spanish and Armenian for community pointers, Romani. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; institutions of faith belong to Sagittarius, so this sheet takes experience, practice and the sacred outside the church.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rhodope Christians at Krastova Gora and Bachkovo against Pomak villages nearby:** check, shrine by shrine, who visits which holy places and whether any draw visitors of more than one faith.
- **Alevi Kızılbaş around Haskovo and Babai of the Ludogorie:** check how each speaks of its saints and tekkes.
- **Turkish Sunni villages of Kardzhali:** check mevlit practice against Pomak practice in Smolyan.
- **Followers of the White Brotherhood:** check where in the country, town or village, they gather and how they describe their path.
- **Shopluk and Northwest villages:** check ayazma and obrochishta density and which saints' days are kept.
- **Roma of several faiths:** check whose shrines each group visits and in what words it speaks of God and the saints.
- **Catholic Paulician villages near Plovdiv:** check their saints' feasts and processions apart from Orthodox ones.
- **Sephardic and Armenian communities:** check their own publications rather than majority accounts.
- **Sofia seekers against village believers:** check survey splits by settlement size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk religiosity and pilgrimage; mixed for new movements (partisan literature on both sides); thin for Alevi and Roma practice in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Greek for the Bachkovo Typikon; Ottoman Turkish for the Otman Baba *Vilâyetnâme*; Turkish for mevlit and Alevi fieldwork; Judeo-Spanish and Hebrew for synagogue records; Armenian for parish histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Alevi rites are closed in part; vision cults and the White Brotherhood draw mockery and church opposition, so describe neutrally; church institutions go to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist communities and centers in the nation's lands (an open question for the author); South Asian-heritage and South Asian-derived practice communities in the nation's lands. Both were real pointers, set aside under the standing rules.
