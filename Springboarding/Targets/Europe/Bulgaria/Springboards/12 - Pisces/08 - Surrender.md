# Bulgaria: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Bulgaria, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bulgarian (with Middle Bulgarian for one text), the Turkish of Bulgaria, Romani. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Veliko Tarnovo and the old capital region:** check how 1393 is commemorated locally beside the national account.
- **Rhodope Pomak villages (Smolyan, Chepino valley):** check local and community accounts of conversion against *Време разделно*; the history is contested, so record each source's framing.
- **Turks and Alevi of the Ludogorie and Kardzhali:** check tevekkül and teslim in sermons, nefes and everyday speech, separately for Sunni and Alevi sources.
- **Southern Dobruja and Thracian refugee families:** check family memory of 1913 and 1919 losses of home.
- **The Northwest (Vidin, Montana):** check reports of resistance to collectivization and how it ended there.
- **Karakachans:** check how the handing over of flocks in the 1950s is remembered.
- **Roma Pentecostals against Orthodox and Muslim Roma:** check whether conversion talk of surrender differs from older speech of fate.
- **Catholics near Plovdiv and Protestants:** check their deathbed and funeral customs against Orthodox ones.
- **Sofia against villages:** check palliative-care access and place of death.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory and death rites; mixed for recovery and palliative care; thin for minority devotional sources in Bulgaria specifically.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Bulgarian for Tsamblak; Turkish for nefes and tevekkül sources; Romani for Pentecostal testimony recorded in Roma churches; Bulgarian for collectivization memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced conversion and the Pomak past are contested ground; abandoned children and dying are private; "fatalism" is an outsider's label, to record and test, never to adopt. Euthanasia law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; a chronicle claimed for the Chepino conversions is widely considered inauthentic and is left out until a historian's assessment is found.
