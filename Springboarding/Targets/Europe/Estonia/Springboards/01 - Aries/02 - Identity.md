# Estonia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects, Võro, Seto, Mulgi, the Russian of Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Ingrian Finnish, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Estonian speakers of Tallinn, Tartu, Pärnu and the countryside:** check whether national, regional and home-place labels come first in self-description.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Ida-Virumaa and of Tallinn:** compare the labels each chooses (citizen, local, Russian, Estonian Russian), since the TIES design invites a place contrast.
- **Võro, Seto and Mulgi:** check how each separates its regional identity from the national one, and which revival body speaks for it.
- **Islanders, including Kihnu:** check how island belonging is named against the mainland.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** compare religious against ethnic self-labels in their own congregations.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Belarusians, Ukrainians, Ingrian Finns and Estonian Swedes:** compare the membership rules each community society states for itself.
- **Lutherans, both Orthodox churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare how far each ties faith to nationality.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national identity and the Russian-speaking population; mixed for regional identities; thin for Ingrian Finns, Belarusians and Tatars, where community societies and the census are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian, Russian, Võro, Seto; Swedish for the coastal community; Finnish for Ingrian sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** citizenship and the undetermined-citizenship "grey passport", the Soviet deportations and the 2007 Bronze Soldier events shape identity talk; present each account as an account and name each community by its own name. The Seto split by the border is political; Pechory and the Estonian diaspora (Sweden, Canada, Australia, the US) are comparison only.
- **Held for review:** none.
