*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Estonian speakers of the North coast and of South Estonia:** compare the folk dates on which the year or a season was felt to begin.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Sillamäe, Narva and Tallinn:** check arrival and founding stories by generation, from the first post-war workers to their grandchildren.
- **Orthodox (both churches) and the Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check how the church calendar shapes the turn of the year and christening.
- **Seto and Võro:** compare birth customs and the start of the farming year as their own archives record them.
- **Kihnu and the other islands:** check weddings and first-catch customs as starts of a household or season.
- **Estonian Swedes:** check settlement and re-settlement narratives after 1944.
- **Ukrainians, Belarusians and Ingrian Finns:** compare the adaptation experience by arrival period (Soviet era, 1990s, since 2022).
- **The Jewish community and Lutheran parishes:** check which new year each keeps in public and at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the folk calendar, founding history and creation songs; mixed for modern starting-over data; thin for island re-settlement and Belarusian arrival narratives.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian, Russian, Ukrainian; Latin and Swedish for the early university register; German for Baltic German-era almanacs; Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish calendar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the school-language transition and post-war arrivals touch citizenship and integration debates; present each community's account in its own words. Birth records less than a century old are privacy-restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: Hiiemäe's *Eesti rahvakalender*, the volumes on the turn of the year and spring.
2. Regilaul database: search *loomine* and the bird-and-egg motif; map variants by parish.
3. DIGAR: search *tähtraamat* for good and bad days for beginning work.
4. ERR archive: compare 1 September broadcasts in Estonian and Russian across 2000 to 2025.
5. Sillamäe Museum: ask for oral-history holdings on the town's first residents.
