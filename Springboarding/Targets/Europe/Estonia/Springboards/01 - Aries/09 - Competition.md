## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Estonian- and Russian-language schools:** compare olympiad and PISA participation by school type and funding, so school system is not mistaken for a group trait.
- **Tartu and Tallinn:** check how city rivalry is played out in basketball, universities and the press.
- **Islanders of Saaremaa, Muhu and Hiiumaa:** compare regatta and island-games records.
- **Seto and Võro:** check contests inside their own festivals and who judges them.
- **Former Kreenholm and oil-shale workers of Ida-Virumaa:** compare how Soviet work contests are remembered by the language of the teller.
- **Ukrainian and Belarusian youth sports clubs:** check how newcomers' clubs enter national leagues.
- **Women and men:** compare entries and prizes in mass races and olympiads.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox and Old Believer congregations:** check what each teaches about envy and striving, from its own sermons and texts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport, school contests and rankings; mixed for envy beliefs; thin for Ukrainian and Belarusian club history, where community associations are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian, Russian; Latin and Swedish for Academia Gustaviana disputations; Seto for Kingdom Day; Ukrainian for newcomers' clubs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** comparisons of school results by language feed a live policy debate; report structure (funding, teaching language, region) before any group contrast. Soviet work-contest memory is mixed; record each teller's view as their own.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Education and Youth Board: download olympiad results by county and school language for five years.
2. Ministry of Education and Research: read the latest PISA national report's chapter on school language.
3. ERA: search *jõukatsumine* and *kade silm*, by parish.
4. Sports and Olympic Museum: ask for its exhibition texts on wrestling and chess.
5. National Archives: search Kreenholm fonds for *социалистическое соревнование*.
