## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Estonian speakers of the north coast, Hiiumaa and Kihnu:** compare smuggling, seafaring and tall-tale traditions to see how sea risk is narrated on each coast.
- **Old Believers and other residents of the Peipsi shore:** check ice-fishing rescue reports and community comment in Russian and Estonian.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Ida-Virumaa:** check drug-risk and road data against unemployment and service access, so place structure is in view before any group reading.
- **Estonian Swedes of Ruhnu and Vormsi:** check seal-hunting accounts in Swedish sources.
- **Students in Tartu, past and present:** compare Baltic German, Estonian and Russian-language student-society rituals of drinking and daring.
- **Young men and young women:** compare TAI and traffic figures by sex and age.
- **Seto and Võro villages:** check midsummer and village-feast customs where bonfire and water risk appear in their own records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health, traffic and rescue statistics; mixed for folklore of daring; thin for seal hunting and smuggling, where island museums and the Folklore Archives are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German for church, tavern and university records; Swedish for Ruhnu and Vormsi; island dialects for Kihnu and Hiiumaa material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug use, HIV and drinking data can be turned into a stereotype of a region or community; report them with their social causes and in aggregate. Drowning and crash victims have families; avoid naming private persons.
- **Held for review:** none.
