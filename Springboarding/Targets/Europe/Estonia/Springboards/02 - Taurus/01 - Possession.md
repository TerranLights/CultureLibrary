# Estonia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (standard and dialect), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war community. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm Estonians of Harjumaa, Järvamaa and Mulgimaa:** compare granary inventories and dowry chests, since farm size and buying-out dates may differ by county.
- **Võro and Seto households:** check which objects (silver, linen, icons) their own museums record as inherited, and whether all-Estonian sources fold them in.
- **Kihnu, Muhu and Saaremaa islanders:** check boat and net shares and the women's dress kept in chests.
- **Russian-speaking families of Narva, Sillamäe and Kohtla-Järve:** check what post-1945 arrivals brought and acquired, and what the 1990s changes did to it.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check icons and old books as household possessions and how they pass between generations.
- **Estonian Swedes, Baltic Germans (resettled 1939-1941), the Jewish community:** compare what each left behind in a forced or chosen departure.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars:** check what arrival with little (wartime, Soviet postings, 2022 refugees) meant for each, from their societies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for museum collections, hoards and folklore of theft; mixed for present-day household belongings; thin for Ingrian Finnish and Tatar keepsakes, where community societies and family interviews are the way in.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; Middle Low German and Latin for the medieval testaments; Swedish for the boat-flight accounts; German for resettlement papers; Yiddish for pre-war household records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** objects from deportations and from the 1939 Baltic German departure carry rival accounts of loss and gain; treat each as an account. Hoard find-places can be withheld to protect sites.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the possession chapter of *Estonian Language* (adessive "having") before choosing other search words.

4. Tallinn City Archives: ask for the catalog of medieval testaments.
5. Memento Union: list memoirs that name objects carried in 1949.
