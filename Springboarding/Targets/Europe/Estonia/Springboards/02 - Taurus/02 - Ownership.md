# Estonia: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian, Russian, Võro, Seto, Swedish (coastal), German (Baltic German records), Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mulgimaa, Järvamaa and Läänemaa farms:** check when farms were bought out and how settler farms of 1919 were spread, since the manor share of land may have differed by county.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check smallholding, partible holdings and village land as each community's own records show them.
- **Islanders of Saaremaa, Muhu and Kihnu:** compare claims to fishing grounds, meadows and shore with mainland farm tenure.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** check apartment privatization and enterprise ownership in the 1990s, separating citizens, holders of Russian passports and holders of the grey passport.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check village plots and fishing rights on the lake as their congregations recorded them.
- **Baltic Germans, Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community:** check how each lost or regained property between 1939 and the 1990s, from each community's own archives.
- **Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars:** check housing tenure in each society's surveys; little else may be in print.
- **Lutheran and Orthodox parishes:** compare church land before 1940 and its return after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for manor, land-reform and restitution history; mixed for present-day attitudes to owning; thin for Tatar and Belarusian property histories, where interviews and community records stand in.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian, Russian; German for manor and Knighthood records; Swedish for the reduction and coastal leases; Latin for the chronicle; Yiddish for confiscated pre-war holdings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restitution, the tenants in returned houses, and property linked to the deportations and to citizenship status are contested ground; give each side's account in its own words. Land titles of private persons are restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
