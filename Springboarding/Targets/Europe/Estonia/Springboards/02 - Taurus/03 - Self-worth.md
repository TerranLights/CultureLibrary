## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Tartu townspeople against farm families in Järvamaa and Läänemaa:** check whether worth is spoken of through work, schooling or land in each.
- **Võro and Seto:** check whether speaking the home language is recorded as a mark of lower or higher standing in school memoirs and surveys, and how that changed.
- **Kihnu and Muhu islanders:** check women's standing through dress, song and work as their museums record it.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Narva, Kohtla-Järve and Sillamäe:** check self-reported standing across citizens, Russian citizens and grey-passport holders, and before and after job losses.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check church teaching on humility and pride in their own books.
- **Baltic Germans (historical), the Jewish community, Estonian Swedes:** check family standing (*yikhes*, noble lineage, coastal privileges) as each community's records frame it.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars:** check newcomers' accounts of how their education and trades were recognized.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox, Maausk and Taarausk teaching:** compare where each places a person's worth.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for psychometric and health data and for literature; mixed for honor talk in daily life; thin for Belarusian and Tatar self-accounts, where society newsletters and interviews are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German for Moravian and Baltic German-era texts; Seto for *leelo* roles; Yiddish for family-standing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data and school-language reform touch living people and contested politics; insults aimed at language groups should be described, not quoted. Present each community's account of second-class standing as its account.
- **Held for review:** none.
