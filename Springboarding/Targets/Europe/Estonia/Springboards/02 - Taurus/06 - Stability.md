## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa farm families:** check how many farms were handed on across the 1940s and 1990s breaks, from local histories.
- **Tallinn's old districts against new suburbs in Harjumaa:** compare residence length and turnover.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check families who arrived after 1945 and how many generations now count themselves local.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check village continuity and calendar keeping since settlement.
- **Seto and Võro:** check village depopulation against family return in summer homes.
- **Kihnu, Muhu, Ruhnu and Vormsi:** compare islands that kept their people with islands emptied in 1944.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Tatars:** check each community's story of settlement, removal and return, from its own society.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians:** compare Soviet-era arrivals with those who came after 2022.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census, labor and family panels; mixed for felt continuity; thin for small-island resettlement and for Tatar family continuity, where parish books and community memory are the way in.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German for older farm calendars and parish books; Swedish for Ruhnu and Vormsi; Church Slavonic for the Old Believer calendar; Yiddish for the pre-war community.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the break of 1940-1944, postwar migration and the doctrine of the state's legal continuity carry rival accounts (the doctrine itself is for Libra and Capricorn); present each community's account as its own.
- **Held for review:** none.
