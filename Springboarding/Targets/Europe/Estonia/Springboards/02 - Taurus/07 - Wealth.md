## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mulgimaa farm families:** check the reputation of Mulgi farm wealth as an account, against farm-purchase and tax records.
- **Harjumaa and Tallinn against Võrumaa and Põlvamaa:** compare where income and wealth sit and how residents describe the gap.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check mining and industrial wages across the Soviet and later periods, and who in each language group says they gained or lost.
- **Peipsi Old Believer families:** check trade wealth in onions and fish through village and church records.
- **Baltic Germans (historical) and Estonian Swedes:** compare manor and coastal-farm wealth before 1939-1944 from each community's archives.
- **Seto and Kihnu women:** check silver, dress and dowry as household wealth in their museums.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check trades and enterprises each community records as its own.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox and Maausk teaching:** compare sermons and statements on riches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income statistics, guild and manor history; mixed for attitudes to the rich; thin for Soviet-era informal wealth and for small-community fortunes, where memoirs and community archives stand in.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; Middle Low German for guild records; German for manor accounts; Yiddish for merchant and charity records; Mulgi for local-press usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fortunes made in 1990s privatization and wealth lost to Soviet confiscation are told differently by different groups; quote each as an account. Personal tax data is restricted; company-level data is public.
- **Held for review:** none.
