# Estonia: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with Hiiumaa and Saaremaa speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish, Finnish (for Aino Kallas). **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hiiumaa, Saaremaa and Kihnu:** check courtship, chaperoning and family claims over daughters as island records tell them, not as mainland sources assume.
- **Tallinn against rural Võrumaa and Järvamaa:** compare survey answers on partner control by place.
- **Russian-speaking families of Narva, Kohtla-Järve and Tallinn:** check wedding-ransom customs and talk of jealousy in Russian-language media and services, separately from Estonian-language sources.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check rules about marrying outside the congregation and how families explain them.
- **Seto:** check wedding laments and bride customs in Seto collections.
- **Hunters, fishers and pickers:** compare guarding of grounds across language and regional lines.
- **Estonian Swedes, the Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check marriage within or outside each community and how its own writings frame family claims.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for partner-control surveys and wedding customs; mixed for everyday jealousy and turf; thin for stinginess and guarding in Tatar, Belarusian and Ingrian Finnish households, where interviews are needed.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; Finnish for Kallas's originals; German for the 16th- and 17th-c. sources behind them; Swedish for the coastal records; Yiddish for marriage-match material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence and control in close relationships involve living victims; use only published aggregate data. Mixed-marriage objections touch the language divide; report each side's account.
- **Held for review:** none.
