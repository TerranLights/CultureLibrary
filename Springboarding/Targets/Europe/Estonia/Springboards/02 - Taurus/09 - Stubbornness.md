## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu:** check island anecdotes and jokes about obstinacy as material to study, not as a trait to report.
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa farm families:** check neighbor quarrels in parish-court and local-history records.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check how the congregations describe their own steadfastness in rite and dress across three centuries.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Tallinn and Ida-Virumaa:** check how holding firm and giving way are described in Russian-language coverage of the 2007 events and of school-language change, beside Estonian-language coverage.
- **Seto and Võro:** check claims kept alive in community forums and papers.
- **Lutherans, the two Orthodox churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare how each speaks of steadfastness in faith.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check each one's own accounts of what it kept under pressure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literary, folk and protest sources; mixed for measured personality data; thin for small communities' own accounts of holding firm, where their societies and parishes are the way in.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; Latin for Wartberge; Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts; Swedish for the return movement; Seto and Võro for community forums.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the forest brothers, the Soviet deportations and the 2007 events are contested ground; set out each account as an account. The "stubborn Estonian" or "stubborn islander" image is a stereotype: record it as a thing people say, never as a finding.
- **Held for review:** none.
