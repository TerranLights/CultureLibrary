## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa against Harjumaa:** compare smoke-sauna and village-feast customs with urban spa and café habits.
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** check feast dishes and the place of cream, meat and beer in each region's festive table.
- **Pärnu, Haapsalu and Kuressaare:** check resort towns separately, since visitors and locals may use the same baths differently.
- **Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** check dress color, song and dance as island pleasures in their own museums.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Narva, Kohtla-Järve and Tallinn:** check *баня*, garden-plot and festive-table customs from Russian-language sources.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check fasting calendars and what is permitted on feast days, from congregational sources.
- **Lutherans, the two Orthodox churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare worship through incense, icons, song or plain rooms, and midsummer customs.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Estonian Swedes, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Ingrian Finns:** check Sabbath, festival and table customs each community keeps.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sauna, food and festival material and for alcohol statistics; mixed for touch and comfort; thin for Belarusian and Ingrian Finnish festive customs, where community societies are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German and Swedish for visitation records and early spa guides; Church Slavonic for fasting rules; Yiddish for Sabbath texts; Seto and Mulgi for food terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** alcohol harm data can feed national and language-group stereotypes; report regional and group variation without labeling a people. Some sauna and grove customs are private to their keepers.
- **Held for review:** none.
