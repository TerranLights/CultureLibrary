# Estonia: The domain: communication and learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia (with the Peipsi Old Believers' speech), the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, Estonian Sign Language, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech ways, neighbors, short journeys and early learning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Speakers of North Estonian, South Estonian and Mulgi:** check census dialect claims against EKI recordings; self-report and speech may differ.
- **Võro and Seto:** read language-nest and *leelo* material in each community's own institute.
- **The island communities (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** set each island's ferry and ice-road calendar beside its school trips.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Tallinn (Lasnamäe, Kopli) and Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare home-language and contact data by district, not as one block.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check how the street villages (*tänavkülad*) and prayer-house reading placed talk and teaching.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians (long settled and since 2022), Belarusians, Tatars, the Jewish community:** check each group's heritage school and its teaching language.
- **Deaf signers:** keep Estonian Sign Language sources apart from spoken-language surveys.
- **Lutherans, the two Orthodox churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare confirmation classes and teaching gatherings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language statistics, dialectology and school history; mixed for neighbor relations; thin for home talk among Tatars, Belarusians and Roma (community societies and oral history first).
- **Lands:** Estonia's tracker header lists no settled lands and no change to its pre-war lands, so the Gemini sheets describe Estonia as it stands, add no unit list and settle no border. Latvia, Lithuania, Finland, Belarus, Poland, Sweden and Russia's culture-nations (the Pechory district included) are comparison only; the diaspora takes the nation's findings.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian for surveys and corpora; Latin and German to read the 1648 and 1660 grammars; Seto and Võro for song and language-nest material; Swedish for the Aibolands holdings; Estonian Sign Language for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language policy, the move of Russian-language schools to Estonian teaching and the undetermined-citizenship "grey passport" are contested ground; let each community's account stand as its own. Small-group census cells may be suppressed.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Statistics Estonia database: for each municipality, set the 2021 counts of people speaking a second language beside the counts claiming a regional variety.
2. EKI Sõnaveeb and the dialect dictionary: look up *naaber*, *sinatama* and *jutt*, noting the parish of each record.
3. Estonian Literary Museum's archive catalog: search *mardisandid* and *kadrisandid* for door dialogues, one parish per region.
4. Integration Monitoring 2020: pull the tables on language of contact by region and age.
5. Transport Administration: list ice-road opening dates and ferry timetables for Kihnu and Vormsi over recent winters.
