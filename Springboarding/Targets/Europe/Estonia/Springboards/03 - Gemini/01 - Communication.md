# Estonia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, island speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Estonian Sign Language, Yiddish for the pre-war community. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what counts as good communication and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Estonian and South Estonian speakers:** check whether the recorded-talk corpora sample both, since campus-based corpora may lean toward Tartu and Tallinn speech.
- **Võro and Seto speakers:** check when a conversation moves from the home language into standard Estonian, using each community's own surveys.
- **Kihnu, Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Muhu islanders:** look for greeting and address forms in island recordings before assuming the mainland pattern holds.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians in Narva and in Tallinn:** set code-switching studies from each town side by side, and read each against that town's census language data.
- **Children of Russian-speaking and newly arrived Ukrainian families:** check studies of children interpreting for parents at offices and clinics.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check lay-led reading aloud in Church Slavonic as a speech setting of its own.
- **Estonian Swedes:** compare pre-1944 recordings with the speech of returnees and descendants.
- **Deaf signers:** look at interpreter access and Deaf club life separately from spoken data.
- **Generations schooled before and after 1991:** compare address-form studies by age group to see whether *sina* and *teie* use shifts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for linguistics and pragmatics of Estonian; mixed for gesture and body signals (few studies; observation needed); thin for conversation norms among Tatars, Belarusians and Ingrian Finns, where community recordings and interviews are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian for the pragmatics literature; German and early Estonian spelling to read Stahl; Swedish for the Isof recordings; Võro and Seto for home-language talk; Estonian Sign Language for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language-use rules for public offices and their enforcement are contested between Estonian- and Russian-speaking communities; record each side's account as its own. Recorded-conversation corpora carry consent limits.
- **Held for review:** none.
