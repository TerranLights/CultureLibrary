# Estonia: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with South Estonian, Võro and Seto local press), the Russian of Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war press; German for the Baltic German period. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how it is gathered, passed on, trusted, withheld and used: news, facts, data and records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Estonian-reading and Russian-reading audiences:** compare news-source and trust items in the Tartu survey by home language, and check how the gap is measured, not only its size.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Tallinn's Russian-speaking districts:** check regional press against national outlets for which stories reach each place.
- **Rural older readers in South Estonia and Mulgimaa:** look at county papers and the post as news channels where broadband is uneven.
- **Island readers (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu):** check island papers such as *Saarte Hääl* and *Hiiu Leht* for local information that national media skip.
- **Võro and Seto:** check *Uma Leht* and *Setomaa* as each community's own record of its news.
- **Ukrainians arrived since 2022:** check which channels and chat groups newcomers rely on, through refugee-support bodies.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check how parish and village notices are passed on.
- **Deaf viewers:** check sign-language news on the public broadcaster.
- **Estonian Swedes and the pre-war Jewish community:** look for their own periodicals before 1940, and say so if little survives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history, media-use surveys and the e-state; mixed for Soviet-era censorship (archive work); thin for Tatar, Belarusian and Ingrian Finnish information channels, where community bodies are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian for surveys and press; Middle High German for the Rhymed Chronicle; older Estonian spelling and German type for the calendars and Wilde; Russian for the Glavlit files; Finnish for the broadcasting side of the Finnish-TV story.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2007 Bronze Soldier events and the cyberattacks, limits placed on Russian state broadcasters, and the "information space" debate are contested ground; give each community's and each government's account as an account. Some Soviet security records stay restricted.
- **Held for review:** none.
