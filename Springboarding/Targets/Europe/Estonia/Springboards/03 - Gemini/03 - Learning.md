# Estonia: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with Mulgi and island speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, Church Slavonic for Old Believer reading, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish and Hebrew for the pre-war Jewish school, Ukrainian for newcomers. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution goes to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm families of North Estonia, South Estonia and Mulgimaa:** compare parish reading marks across regions and church districts.
- **Võro and Seto households:** check language-nest and home-teaching material to see what is learned in the regional language and what in standard Estonian.
- **Kihnu and the other islands:** check how craft, fishing and song skills are taught locally, through each island's heritage body.
- **Russian-speaking families of Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe and Tallinn:** gather parents', pupils' and teachers' own accounts of the teaching-language change.
- **Old Believers on the Peipsi shore:** check home and prayer-house teaching of Church Slavonic reading.
- **Ukrainian children arrived since 2022:** check how Estonian schooling and Ukrainian online classes are combined.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Belarusians, Tatars, the Jewish community:** look for Sunday or weekend schools run by each community.
- **Lutherans, the Orthodox of both churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare confirmation instruction (*leer*), church schools and seasonal teaching gatherings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literacy history, school statistics and international tests; mixed for learning inside the family (memoirs and folklore); thin for Belarusian, Tatar and Ingrian Finnish home learning, where community associations are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian for current data; Swedish and German for Forselius-era and parish records; older Estonian orthography for Masing; Church Slavonic for Old Believer primers; Hebrew and Yiddish for the Jewish school; Ukrainian for newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the move to Estonian-medium schooling is contested ground: collect what Russian-speaking parents, pupils, teachers and the ministry each say, and keep their accounts apart. Pupil-level data is protected.
- **Held for review:** none.
