# Estonia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with South Estonian and the island dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war community; German for Baltic German scholarship. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tartu and Tallinn audiences:** compare science-center and festival attendance with that of smaller towns, to see how far organized curiosity reaches.
- **South Estonia, Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check folktale and riddle records from these parishes separately from northern ones.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu and Ruhnu:** check how island museums present local explorers and seafarers.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** check Russian-language science outreach and the use of the two Russian words for curiosity in local media.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check what kinds of questions their teaching welcomes or limits, from their own congregations' writings.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians:** look for each group's own learned or hobby societies before 1940 and since 1991.
- **Children and teenagers:** check science-competition and *Rakett69* entries by school language.
- **Lutherans, the Orthodox of both churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare teachings on prying into sacred matters and on studying nature.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore corpora and the history of science; mixed for everyday curiosity about other people (forums and interviews needed); thin for minority groups' hobby science.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German for the learned-society proceedings and Baer's writings; Swedish for Ruhnu and Vormsi material; Finnish to follow Meri's films' kindred-peoples context.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Baltic German explorers and scientists served the Russian Empire, so their commemoration is read differently by Estonian- and Russian-speaking audiences; record each reading as an account. Sacred natural sites (*hiied*) may carry rules against entry and study, as Maausk communities state them.
- **Held for review:** none.
