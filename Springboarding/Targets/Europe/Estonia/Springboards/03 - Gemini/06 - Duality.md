# Estonia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, island speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the Peipsi Old Believers' Russian, Estonian Swedish; German for the Baltic German period. **Concept in one line:** how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once, dividing the world into pairs, and how those who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South Estonians, Võro and Mulgi speakers:** check how people describe using a home variety and standard Estonian, through regional surveys and the census dialect items.
- **Seto:** check whether "double faith" is a term the community accepts or rejects, before using it.
- **Russian-speaking Estonians of Narva, Sillamäe, Kohtla-Järve and Tallinn:** compare identity studies across towns and generations, and keep citizens, grey-passport holders and Russian citizens apart.
- **Children in immersion classes and their parents:** set immersion reports beside those of the Estonian-medium transition.
- **Old Believers of the Peipsi shore:** check how a Russian-speaking community with centuries in Estonia names its own belonging, in its own writings.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Tatars, the Jewish community:** check how each records life in two or more languages.
- **Orthodox parishes of both churches:** compare parish statements and members' accounts after the jurisdiction disputes.
- **Islanders and mainlanders:** check whether island heritage bodies speak of an island and a mainland self.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language history, life stories and identity research; mixed for paired worldviews in folklore; thin for how Tatars and Belarusians in Estonia describe dual belonging, where association records come first.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; German for Hupel; South Estonian of the 1600s for Rossihnius; Church Slavonic and Russian for church records; Swedish for Noarootsi and Vormsi; Seto for the community's own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the grey passport, the two Orthodox jurisdictions and dual loyalty claimed or denied after 2022 are contested ground; let each community and each church describe its own position, under its own name. Life stories carry privacy terms.
- **Held for review:** none.
