# Estonia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with Mulgi and the island dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German for Moravian and manor-era sources. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Estonian, South Estonian and Mulgi farm families:** check child-minding and inheritance-of-the-farm customs by region (the letter of inheritance law goes to Libra).
- **Seto and Võro families:** read their own song and lament collections before the all-Estonian ones.
- **Kihnu, Muhu and Saaremaa households:** check whether brothers' and sisters' tasks in fishing and farm work are recorded differently on the islands.
- **Russian-speaking families of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** compare sibling numbers by cohort and look for family studies in Russian.
- **Old Believer households of the Peipsi shore:** check family size and sibling roles through congregation and village histories.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Tatars:** look in each community's memoirs for siblings scattered by war, flight and resettlement.
- **Families of deportees and of those who fled West in 1944:** compare memoirs written in Estonia with those written abroad (the diaspora is comparison only).
- **Lutherans, the Orthodox of both churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare how each uses "brother" and "sister" for members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, demography and wartime memory; mixed for adult sibling relations today (survey modules vary); thin for Tatar, Belarusian and Ingrian Finnish family patterns, where community archives come first.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian and Russian; Seto for Hurt's collection; German for Moravian records in Herrnhut; Swedish for Estonian Swedish memoirs; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish family records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Forest Brothers, Estonians who served in opposing armies, and the deportations are contested ground: families and communities remember them differently, so set down each memory as that family's or community's account. Family-registry and birth data are privacy-protected.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Regilaul database: search *vend*, *õde* and *sõsar* in song titles; sort by parish.
2. Library catalog: search "Katus" or "Puur" with *perekonna suurus* for cohort family-size studies.
3. Statistics Estonia: open births by birth order and multiple births, earliest to latest years.
4. Folklore Archives: look up *pruudi vend* and *veli* together to find what the bride's brothers did at the table and the gate.
5. Estonian Memory Institute: search for oral histories mentioning *vennad* in the forest.
