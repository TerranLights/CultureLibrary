# Estonia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with the island, Mulgi and South Estonian dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German for manor-era plans. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Estonian and Mulgimaa farm owners after the 1860s purchases:** check how the move from rented to bought farms is described in each region's local histories.
- **Island households of Saaremaa, Muhu, Kihnu and Hiiumaa:** compare farm plans and walled yards with mainland ones, and look for what islanders say "home" covers.
- **Seto farms in Setomaa:** set the Seto Institute's own descriptions of the farmyard layout beside North Estonian ones.
- **Võrumaa smoke-sauna farms:** check what Võro writers include when they describe the home farm.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Lasnamäe, Kopli and Ida-Virumaa:** separate panel-block, barracks and private-house lives; check the *propiska* era's records against later memoirs.
- **Peipsi Old Believers and Orthodox households:** compare the place given to icons in the house.
- **Estonian Swedes of Vormsi, Noarootsi and Ruhnu; Ingrian Finns; Ukrainian families arriving since 2022:** look in each group's own accounts for homes left and homes remade.
- **Jewish families of pre-war Tallinn and Tartu; Tatar households:** check community records for addresses and house life before 1941.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing statistics, farm architecture and building custom; mixed for urban home life in Russian; thin for Tatar and Belarusian homes, and for Jewish homes before 1941 (community and city archives first).
- **Languages needed:** German for estate maps and Ränk's book; Russian for Peipsi, Kopli and Ida-Virumaa sources; Swedish for Vormsi records; Seto and Võro for each institute's descriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** restitution and the forced tenants, and the homes seized in the 1941 and 1949 deportations, are contested ground; set each side's account down as its own. Addresses of living people stay out.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Eurostat database: pull Estonia's tenure status and overcrowding rate, 2005 onward, by degree of urbanization.
2. Folklore Archives catalog: search *soolaleib* and *sarikapidu*; note the parish of each record.
3. DIGAR: search *sundüürnik* for 1993-2005 press in Estonian and Russian (*принудительный арендатор* *check*).
4. National Archives map portal: find one manor's farm plan from the 18th c. in Järvamaa and one in Võrumaa.
5. MuIS museum database: search *vanausuliste maja* for photographs from the Peipsi shore.
