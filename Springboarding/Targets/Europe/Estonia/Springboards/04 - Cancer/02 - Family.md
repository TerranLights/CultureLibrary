# Estonia: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects, Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia (and the Old Believers' church usage), Estonian Swedish, Finnish for the Ingrian Finns, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German for catechisms and church books. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South Estonian and North Estonian rural families:** check whether the side-specific uncle and aunt words lasted longer in one dialect area.
- **Kihnu and Saaremaa families:** check how far reunions and godparent ties reach on a small island.
- **Seto families:** read laments and Orthodox godparent customs in Seto sources before any general Estonian work.
- **Võro families:** look for Võro-language family memoirs in the Võro Institute's holdings.
- **Russian-speaking families of Narva, Sillamäe and Tallinn:** set ties to kin across the border beside ties inside Estonia; ask whether godparents (*krestnye*) matter in each.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check marriage within and outside the community in the metrical books.
- **Ingrian Finns, Estonian Swedes, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars and the Jewish community:** check each community's own records for families separated by war and deportation.
- **Lutherans, the two Orthodox churches, Catholics, Maausk and Taarausk:** compare what each teaches about godparents and the duties of kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy, church registers and demographic surveys; mixed for kin support today; thin for Tatar and Belarusian kin networks (community societies and interviews).
- **Languages needed:** German for older catechisms and church books; Church Slavonic and Russian for Old Believer registers; Finnish for Ingrian Finnish family papers; Seto for the laments; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who counts as family is a live public dispute; quote each organization's position as its own and leave the law to Libra. The deportations and the Narva border are remembered differently by different families. Biobank data are restricted by consent.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. EKI dialect dictionary: look up *lell* and *sõtse* and map the parishes where each is recorded.
2. Regilaul database: search *minia* in song texts; group the results by county.
3. National Library catalog (ESTER): search *suguvõsa* for family histories printed since 1990.
4. ERR archive: search *ENSV* and *Õnne* to list family series with their years.
5. Eesti Inkerisoomlaste Liit: ask what family-history collections it holds and in which language.
