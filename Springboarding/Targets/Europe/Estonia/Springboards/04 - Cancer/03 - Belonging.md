# Estonia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with island and South Estonian usage), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Ukrainian for recent arrivals, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; Latin for the medieval chronicle. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn, Tartu and small-town Estonians:** check whether surveys of local attachment differ by settlement size.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Muhu and Ruhnu people:** compare what each island's press counts as "local".
- **Seto and Võro communities:** read each one's own membership and festival records first.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Narva, Sillamäe, Kohtla-Järve and Lasnamäe:** break every belonging item down by passport (Estonian, Russian, grey), since felt membership and legal status may part ways.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check how long-settled communities frame their place against later Russian-speaking arrivals.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians (Soviet-era and since 2022), Tatars, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Estonian Swedes:** look in each community society's own publications for accounts of acceptance.
- **Congregations (Lutheran, both Orthodox churches, Catholic, Baptist and other Free Churches, Maausk, Taarausk):** set side by side the rites by which each admits a member and the rolls by which it counts them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for identity surveys, school data and press; mixed for felt belonging among recent arrivals; thin for Tatar and Belarusian community life (societies and interviews).
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the chronicle and its editions; Russian for LICHR reports, Ida-Virumaa press and Ivanov; Ukrainian for the newest arrivals; Seto and Võro for each society's records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language and citizenship policy, the grey passport and the 2007 Bronze Soldier events are contested ground; give each community's account in its own words and under its own name. Do not treat survey groups as speaking for whole communities.
- **Held for review:** none.
