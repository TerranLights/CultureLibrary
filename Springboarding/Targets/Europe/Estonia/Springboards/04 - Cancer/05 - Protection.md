# Estonia: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with island and South Estonian usage), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German for town and manor records. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North and South Estonian farm households:** check whether house-snake and guardian beliefs were recorded in some parishes far more than others.
- **Coastal and island people of Hiiumaa, Saaremaa, Kihnu and Ruhnu:** compare what guarded fishermen and sealers at sea.
- **Seto and Võro communities:** set protective customs in each community's own sources beside the general Estonian record.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Narva, Sillamäe and Tallinn:** compare will-to-defend and trust-in-protector answers by town, not by language alone.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check the protective place of icons and prayer in fishing villages.
- **Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Ingrian Finns, Estonian Swedes and the Jewish community:** look in each one's own records for charms, blessings and the ways it watched over its members.
- **Women and men:** check who is named as protector in the defense survey, the helpline data and folk records, without assuming roles.
- **Lutheran, both Orthodox, Catholic and Free Church faithful; Maausk and Taarausk:** compare protective blessings and sacred places.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, archaeology and defense surveys; mixed for household security; thin for Tatar and Belarusian protective customs.
- **Languages needed:** German for medieval Tallinn records; Russian for Sillamäe and Ida-Virumaa sources; Church Slavonic for Old Believer prayers; Seto for community material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases and helpline data are confidential; whom the state protects and from whom is argued over by different communities, so record each account as its own. The letter of protective law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Folklore Archives catalog: search *majauss* and *koduuss*; note parish and decade of each record.
2. MuIS: search *kaheksakand* and *kaitsemärk* for mittens and belts with collection notes.
3. Ministry of Defence: download the latest *Avalik arvamus ja riigikaitse* report and its tables by language.
4. Social Insurance Board: find yearly 116 111 contact counts and the Lastemaja reports.
5. Library catalog: search "Tõnisson" and *muinaslinnad*; follow the hillfort bibliography.
