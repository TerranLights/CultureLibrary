# Estonia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Ukrainian for arrivals since 2022, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; Low German and German for medieval and manor-era records. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn against Tartu, Narva and small towns:** set night-shelter languages and capacity side by side city by city.
- **Islanders and west-coast villages (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Vormsi, Läänemaa):** compare memories of 1944 crossings told on each shore.
- **Russian-speaking Ida-Virumaa:** separate dormitory-era arrivals from later residents in housing and shelter histories.
- **Peipsi Old Believers and the Orthodox of both churches:** check how refuge is told in each church's history.
- **Seto and Võro villages:** look in each community's own collections for lodging customs for pilgrims and wanderers.
- **Ukrainian arrivals since 2022 and the Ukrainians settled earlier:** compare how each group describes being housed.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Tatars, Belarusians, the Jewish community:** look for each one's own accounts of flight, hiding and return.
- **Lutheran, Catholic and Free Church charities:** compare which shelters each runs and whom they admit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval and wartime records and refugee reception; mixed for homelessness (counting methods vary *check*); thin for Tatar and Belarusian experience.
- **Languages needed:** Low German and Latin for Tallinn's medieval records; German for parish poor relief; Russian for dormitory memories and shelter clients; Ukrainian for recent arrivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** people in shelters are identifiable in small towns; keep personal data out. The Pühtitsa Convent belongs to a church whose standing is in dispute; give the convent's and the state's accounts separately. Asylum law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
