# Estonia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German for parish books and Baltic German burial records; Finnish for families working across the gulf. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mainland parishes of Harjumaa, Tartumaa and Viljandimaa:** check foundling counts and orphan songs by parish, since collections differ in density.
- **Islanders of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu and Vormsi:** look for families waiting on absent seamen and emigrants, and how waiting turned into being left.
- **Seto and Võro villages near the 1990s border line:** compare accounts of houses and graves left on the far side (the Pechory district itself is comparison only).
- **Russian-speaking residents of Paldiski, Narva and Kohtla-Järve:** set accounts of being left by a departed state or employer beside other accounts of the same years.
- **Peipsi Old Believer villages:** check how emptying villages are described by those who stayed.
- **Estonian Swedes:** look for the farms and churches left in 1944 in community records.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars, the Jewish community:** look in each group's records for orphans of war and deportation taken in by kin.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox and Catholic charities; Maausk and Taarausk:** compare who each took in when parents were gone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, church registers and child-welfare statistics; mixed for children of parents working abroad; thin for Tatar and Belarusian families.
- **Languages needed:** German for foundling entries and cemetery records; Finnish for cross-gulf family studies; Russian for children's-home and military-town memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare and adoption files are closed; deportation and the Soviet withdrawal are remembered differently by different communities, so set each account down as its own; never call removal by force "abandonment" when the families do not.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Regilaul database: search *vaeslaps* with *ema haud* (mother's grave) and list parishes.
2. Saaga: browse one Tallinn and one Tartu parish birth register for *Findelkind* entries, 1780-1850.
3. Social Insurance Board: download yearly counts of children in substitute care by type.
4. Library catalog: search *kadunud külad* and *mahajäetud talud* for local histories.
5. Press archives: search *suvekass* across Estonian- and Russian-language outlets.
