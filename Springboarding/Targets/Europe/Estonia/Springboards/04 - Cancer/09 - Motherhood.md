# Estonia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with South Estonian and island usage), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Ukrainian for mothers arriving since 2022, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; Latin and German for medieval and university records. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mothers in Tallinn and Harjumaa, Tartu, and rural Võrumaa and Järvamaa:** set age at first birth and use of leave side by side.
- **Kihnu, Muhu and Saaremaa women:** check how island records describe mothers running house and farm.
- **Seto mothers:** read laments and feast customs before general Estonian sources.
- **Russian-speaking mothers of Narva, Kohtla-Järve and Lasnamäe:** compare birth and leave figures, and the maternity wards chosen.
- **Peipsi Old Believer mothers:** check family size and birth customs in community sources.
- **Ukrainian mothers arriving with children since 2022; Belarusian, Tatar, Ingrian Finnish, Estonian Swedish and Jewish mothers:** look for each group's own groups and memoirs.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox (both churches), Catholic and Free Church women; Maausk and Taarausk:** compare the holy mothers and mothers' blessings each keeps.
- **Single, adoptive and foster mothers:** check how benefit and survey categories capture them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth statistics, folklore and the press; mixed for mothers' own voices in Russian; thin for Tatar and Belarusian mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Terra Mariana sources; German for the Tartu midwifery records; Seto for laments; Russian for Soviet award files and forums; Ukrainian for the newest families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportations, Soviet pronatal awards and debates over benefits and birth rates are remembered and argued differently by different communities and parties; keep each account as its own. Birth records are confidential. Abortion goes to Aries.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. DIGAR: search *emadepäev* in 1920-1940 newspapers and note the first years and organizers named.
2. Folklore Archives catalog: search *nekrutiitk* and *ämmaemand*, sorted by parish.
3. Statistics Estonia database: pull mean age of mother at first birth by county, 2000 onward.
4. Library catalog: search *Maarjamaa* with "Terra Mariana" for medieval and modern uses.
5. Press search: *emapalk* from 2003 to 2006 for the launch debate.
