## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Tallinn against small towns of Järvamaa, Mulgimaa and Läänemaa:** check whether amateur groups and attendance follow settlement size.
- **Tartu and South Estonia, Võrumaa:** compare student festivity with Võro-language music and theater.
- **Setomaa:** read the Seto calendar of feasts on its own terms, not as a branch of Estonian practice.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** check island festivals and costume use, and summer residents' effect.
- **Russian-speaking communities of Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Tallinn's Lasnamäe:** separate their holidays, stages and media before comparing.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check which feasts the congregations keep, and what they set apart from entertainment.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars, the Jewish community:** look for each society's own festivals, choirs and sports clubs.
- **Faiths (Lutheran, the two Orthodox churches, Free Churches, Maausk, Taarausk):** compare what each counts as fitting festivity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for amateur culture, sport membership and arts funding; mixed for courtship after 1944; thin for Tatar, Belarusian and Ingrian Finnish leisure (their societies first).
- **Lands:** there is no scope file, and the tracker header records no change to the pre-war lands: the Leo sheets take Estonia as it stands, list no units and settle no borders. Latvia, Lithuania, Finland, Belarus, Poland, Sweden and Russia's culture-nations (the Pechory district included) are comparisons only; the diaspora takes the nation's findings.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German for the guild books; dialect Estonian for runo songs; Russian for Narva's venues; Yiddish and Hebrew for the cultural autonomy's files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** memories of the Soviet-era and 1988 song celebrations differ by community; keep each account as its own. Gambling-addiction data are protected health records.
- **Held for review:** none.
