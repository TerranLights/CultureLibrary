# Estonia: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with Muhu, Kihnu and Haapsalu craft vocabularies), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi shore, Estonian Swedish; German for medieval commissions. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muhu, Kihnu, Saaremaa and Läänemaa (Haapsalu):** compare how each island or town names its own patterns and polices copying.
- **Tallinn and Tartu:** check the split between the capital's design and tech scene and the university town's painting and literary schools.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** read each people's makers (singers, potters, silversmiths, writers) in their own records, not as Estonian regional color.
- **Russian-speaking makers in Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** check which Russian-language art, theater and craft bodies exist, and how they are funded.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check icon painting and church singing for how invention is weighed against fidelity to the old forms.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Tatars, the Jewish community:** look for each community's craft or art society, and whether its makers are catalogued anywhere.
- **Generations:** compare makers trained in Soviet art schools with those trained after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fine art, craft revival and creative-industries statistics; mixed for everyday improvising (memoirs and museum questionnaires); thin for minority makers outside the Estonian and Russian communities.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German and Latin for Notke and Sittow commissions; Seto and Võro for singers and writers; Russian for *Siluett*'s Russian edition and *Vyshgorod*; Swedish for coastal craft.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet censorship of artists; ownership claims over regional patterns can be contested between makers and companies; keep each party's account as its own.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Estonian Literary Museum catalog: search *Hilana Taarka* and *laulik*; list collected repertoires by collector and year.
2. Konjunktuuriinstituut: find the latest *loomemajanduse kaardistamine* report and its regional tables.
3. DIGAR: search *Siluett* and browse issues from the 1960s to 1980s for home-sewing patterns.
4. Haapsalu Shawl Society and Muhu Museum: ask for pattern collections and any rules on naming a new design.
5. ERR archive: search *ERKI moeshow* for broadcast footage by decade.
