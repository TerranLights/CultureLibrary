# Estonia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (including Saaremaa and Mulgi usage), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia, Ukrainian, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; early modern German for 17th-c. sermons. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu and Kihnu people:** check what each island's festivals and press single out as its own pride, and how jokes about them are told.
- **Mulgimaa against Võrumaa and Setomaa:** compare which local symbols (farm wealth, language, silver, song) each region puts forward.
- **Tallinn and Tartu:** check rival city claims in press and sport.
- **Russian-speaking communities of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** separate pride in town, work, language and wartime memory; read each in its own media.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check what church teaching says on *gordynya*, and what the villages show visitors with pride.
- **Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Estonian Swedes, the Jewish community:** look for each community's own national days, flags and honors.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox (two churches) and Maausk communities:** compare teaching on pride and humility.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sermons, proverbs, symbols and surveys; mixed for everyday bragging norms (jokes and press); thin for Tatar and Belarusian community pride.
- **Languages needed:** early modern German and old Estonian for Stahl; Russian for 9 May and Narva sources; Ukrainian for the cultural center; Yiddish for pre-war Jewish youth movements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 9 May, the 2007 Bronze Soldier events and the deportations shape group pride on opposing sides; give each account as its community's own. Pride events stay contested; keep survey groups from speaking for whole communities.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. DIGAR or the University of Tartu digital library: find a scan of *Leyen Spiegel* and search the index for *kõrkus*.

4. Estonian Human Rights Centre: download all LGBT attitudes survey reports and their language-group tables.
5. ERR archive: list Independence Day reception broadcasts and the next day's press reviews.
