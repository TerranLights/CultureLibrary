# Estonia: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Russian-speaking residents of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** compare decoration and prize lists by home language, and check how Soviet-era titles are viewed in each community's press.
- **Võro and Seto speakers:** check how each campaign for recognition of its speech is argued, and by whom.
- **Ingrian Finns and Estonian Swedes:** read each cultural self-government's own records of what recognition was sought.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check which memorials, honors and acknowledgments each community has asked for.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check whether their customs appear in heritage registers and who nominated them.
- **Islanders (Kihnu, Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** compare whose island traditions are listed, and how islanders speak of outside acknowledgment.
- **Men and women; rural Järvamaa, Läänemaa and Mulgimaa against Tallinn:** compare who is decorated, and whether unsung farm and care work is ever recognized.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state honors, prizes and memorials; mixed for workplace praise; thin for recognition within the smaller communities.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Knighthood's rolls; Russian for Soviet title lists and Narva's records; Finnish and Swedish for the two self-governments; Hebrew and Yiddish for memorial inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportations, the Holocaust in Estonia, the 2007 Bronze Soldier events and the grey passport bear on who is recognized; give each community's account as its own. Language status and minority law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none.
