*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare how the region appears in its own press and in Tallinn media, in both languages.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** check how each people chose to be photographed, filmed and exhibited, against outsiders' images.
- **Islands (Ruhnu, Vormsi, Kihnu, Muhu):** check how small communities record visitors, researchers and tourists watching them.
- **Older people in rural Järvamaa, Läänemaa and the Peipsi shore:** compare loneliness and contact items with Tallinn and Tartu.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check rules on photographs, beards and dress in front of outsiders.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community:** look for each community's own media and whether national outlets cover it.
- **People with disabilities; newcomers since 2015:** separate their own advocacy materials from coverage about them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveillance history, media studies and census methods; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting customs; thin for Ruhnu, Tatar and Belarusian materials.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German for the ordinances; Russian for the KGB files and Ida-Virumaa's press; Swedish for the island archives; Ukrainian for recent arrivals' own media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** KGB files name informers and victims and carry access rules; the 2007 Bronze Soldier events and language and citizenship policy shape who feels seen or watched; give each account as its community's own. Health panel data are protected.
- **Held for review:** none.
