## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Island communities (Kihnu, Muhu, Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Ruhnu, Vormsi):** check how catch, seal and harvest shares were divided, in each island's own records.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** compare feast-day giving and gifts at weddings and funerals, read through each institute.
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa farm households:** check what records say about the wealthier farms' giving to the poor and to the parish.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** compare giving surveys by language, and look at Orthodox parish and mutual-aid networks.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check rules on alms and on shared village work.
- **Smaller communities (Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Estonian Swedes, the Jewish community):** trace each one's aid society, and whether help comes in from abroad or goes out to kin.
- **Tallinn's tech founders against older rural donors:** compare where large and small gifts go.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for donation surveys, registries and ethnographic gift customs; mixed for informal giving between kin; thin for the smallest communities' welfare.
- **Languages needed:** older Estonian and dialects for wedding records; Russian for Orthodox and Old Believer parish life; Ukrainian for aid networks; Yiddish and Hebrew for older Jewish welfare records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** donor data are private; charity disputes (the Slava Ukraini case) involve living people and may involve legal proceedings, so give each party's account as its own.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. ERM MuIS database: search *vakk* and *pruudi annid*; list objects by parish.
2. Rahvuskultuuri Fond website: list named funds by founding year and purpose.
3. Vabaühenduste Liit: find the latest *annetamise uuring* and its regional tables.
4. Blood Centre annual reports: tabulate donors by county.
5. ERR news archive: search *Slava Ukraini* and read Estonian- and Russian-language reports side by side.
