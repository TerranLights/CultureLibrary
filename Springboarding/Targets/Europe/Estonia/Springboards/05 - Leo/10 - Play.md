## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Children in Estonian- and Russian-medium schools:** compare collected rhymes and games, and check which crossed between the two.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa children:** look for each institute's records of games and songs in their own speech.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Muhu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** check island games tied to boats, ice and the sea, in local museum holdings.
- **Rural Järvamaa, Mulgimaa and Läänemaa against Tallinn and Tartu:** compare play spaces and travel distances to them.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check what congregations allow children and adults to play on feast days.
- **Families of Ukrainian, Belarusian, Tatar, Ingrian Finnish, Swedish and Jewish background:** check Sunday schools and community camps for the games each passes on to children.
- **Older people:** compare pensioners' clubs and table games by region and language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's folklore, curricula and online-play surveys; mixed for adult play; thin for minorities' own games.
- **Languages needed:** German for consistory records; Russian for courtyard-game memoirs; Võro and Seto for regional game names; Swedish for island childhood.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's survey data and folklore by named minors carry access rules; gaming-disorder records are health data.
- **Held for review:** none.
