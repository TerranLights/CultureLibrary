# Estonia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with South Estonian and island festive vocabulary), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi shore, Ukrainian, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; German for 17th-c. occasional verse. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn, Tartu and small towns of Järvamaa, Läänemaa and Mulgimaa:** compare life-satisfaction items by settlement type.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** read each people's own festive calendar and songs before comparing them with Estonian practice.
- **Islanders of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Muhu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** check which homecoming days and village feasts draw people back from the mainland, in island papers.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn:** compare survey items by home language and citizenship; look at which holidays each community marks.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check which church feasts call for celebration and what forms of merriment they rule out.
- **Tatars (Sabantuy), Ingrian Finns, Belarusians, Ukrainians, Estonian Swedes and the Jewish community (Purim, Hanukkah):** list each group's feast days from its own society's calendar.
- **Lutheran, Orthodox, Free Church, Maausk and Taarausk believers:** set side by side how each tradition's hymns, prayers or rites speak of gladness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, festivals and broadcast archives; mixed for everyday delight; thin for small communities' festive life.
- **Languages needed:** German and old Estonian for Brockmann; Russian for Narva and Peipsi celebrations; Ukrainian for the newest arrivals; Võro and Seto for regional song.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Victory Day (23 June) and 9 May carry different meanings for different communities; give each account as its own. Mental-health data are protected.
- **Held for review:** none.
