*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Tallinn's Russian-speaking districts:** check how older workers laid off after 1991 describe their standing, in Russian sources.
- **Võrumaa, Setomaa and Mulgimaa:** compare when farm children took on herding and field tasks; check Seto and Võro memoirs separately.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** check which tasks records assign to elders and children when working-age adults were away at sea or on the mainland.
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Läänemaa and Järvamaa:** compare employment past 65 and grandparent child care between the capital region and rural counties.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check the tasks given to each age in a household on the shore.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Tatars, Jews:** look for elders' roles as keepers of a language or a congregation; sources may be scarce.
- **Disabled people and their organizations:** read their own accounts before official ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor statistics and the work-ability reform; mixed for children's work before 1940 (folklore and memoirs); thin for Swedish and Tatar communities.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-c. Estonian print for Arvelius; German for the Enlightenment context; Russian and Ukrainian for Ida-Virumaa and refugee sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability and the "burden" theme need care; quote disabled people's groups in their own words. Benefits law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** in this usefulness scan, no source needed setting aside.
