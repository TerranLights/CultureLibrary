## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tartu and Tallinn:** compare the university town's humanities and natural sciences with the capital's engineering and IT; check which analytic traditions each claims.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check the Soviet-era technical workforce of the closed and industrial towns, and how its training is described in Russian-language memoirs.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check the lay scholar's argument from church books as its own mode of reasoning.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** compare folk reckoning and weather lore in Võro and Seto records with northern ones.
- **Islands and the west coast (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Pärnu, Läänemaa):** look at sailors' navigation and seamarks as practical measurement.
- **Estonian Swedes:** check whether rune-calendar sticks and Swedish measures appear in coastal records.
- **Jewish and Baltic German scholars at Tartu:** check their place in faculties before 1940, from university records.
- **Women in science and engineering:** compare figures across Soviet and post-1991 decades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science history, statistics and skills surveys; mixed for folk reckoning; thin for Old Believer textual practice (start with the congregations themselves).
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German for the ledgers; German for Anderson and Struve-era papers; Russian for Lotman and the Cybernetics Institute; Latin for early university material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reading test gaps by language is easily turned into a claim about a people; report variation and context. Counting by nationality in the interwar and Soviet statistics is itself contested ground; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** the analytic sources listed raised no standing-rule concern, so the line stays empty.
