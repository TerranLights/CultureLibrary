## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Võrumaa, Valgamaa and Setomaa:** compare life expectancy and distance to care; check Seto and Võro healers and remedies in their own institutes' records.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check health surveys and spa traditions from Russian-language sources, and how oil-shale work and HIV are discussed locally.
- **Tartu:** separate the university clinic's own history from the national story.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu) and Pärnu and Läänemaa:** check island access to doctors, and the coastal mud-cure towns as places of health.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** look at fasts and home remedies through what the congregations choose to share.
- **Maausk and Taarausk, Lutherans, the two Orthodox churches:** compare healing sites, prayer and church views of illness; sacred sites are shared only as their keepers choose.
- **Smaller communities:** check whether Jewish and Tatar doctors, Ingrian Finnish and Estonian Swedish remedy lore, and Ukrainian and Belarusian clinics or helplines have left any record of their own; say so where nothing is found.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for vital statistics, health surveys and e-health; mixed for folk medicine after 1945; thin for Tatar, Belarusian and Swedish-coast health customs.
- **Languages needed:** German and Latin for Pirogov-era and parish records; Russian for sanatorium and Ida-Virumaa sources; dialect Estonian, Võro and Seto for plant names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health gaps by nationality and the HIV epidemic must not become claims about a people; present each figure with its context. Patient data are protected. Sacred healing sites of Maausk followers are restricted knowledge. Health law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** under health, nothing met a standing rule that would hold it back.
