# Estonia: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with its South Estonian, Mulgi and island dialects), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; German and Swedish for court records before 1918. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered (punishment, repair, fair procedure, redress for history). Letter of the law only.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Tallinn against Tartu and South Estonian parishes:** compare parish court caseloads; check whether the same offenses went to village courts in north and south.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Viru Prison's catchment:** check recorded crime, court-language use and sentencing in Russian-language reporting.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check Seto and Võro folklore of wrongs and their settlement, and whether village elders heard quarrels.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu and Kihnu:** check how island parishes, far from the county courts, recorded theft and wreck-plunder cases.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check their congregations' own discipline beside the state's courts.
- **Communities touched by the 1941 and 1949 deportations:** compare survivors' associations' accounts with the commissions' and trials' records, keeping each as an account.
- **Estonian Swedes, the Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians:** check each one's records of wrongs suffered and redress sought, through its own society.
- **Faiths:** compare Lutheran, both Orthodox churches' and Maausk teaching on judgment and on forgiving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, court rulings and parish court records; mixed for restorative practice; thin for Seto, Old Believer and Tatar justice customs (interviews needed).
- **Languages needed:** German and Swedish for the Tartu appeal court; Middle Low German and Latin for Tallinn's town law; Russian for trial coverage in Ida-Virumaa; old Gothic-script Estonian for parish court books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportations, the Forest Brothers and wartime crimes are contested; the commission, survivors' groups and Russian-language voices tell them differently and stay separate accounts. Victim and juvenile files are closed. The spirit of the law and trust in courts go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. National Archives (AIS): search *vallakohus* with a parish name and pull one decade of minute books.
2. Ministry of Justice: download the latest *Kuritegevus Eestis* and the most recent *ohvriuuring*.
3. Riigikohus: search judgments for *karistuse kohasus* (fitness of punishment).
4. Library catalog: search "Uluots, Jüri" with *õiguse ajalugu*.
5. DIGAR: search "Rummu Jüri" in papers from 1910 to 1940.
