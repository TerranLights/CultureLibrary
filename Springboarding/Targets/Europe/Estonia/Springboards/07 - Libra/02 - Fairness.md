# Estonia: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else (equal shares, shares by merit or need, fair process, fair play, fair prices and wages).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Ida-Virumaa:** check whether the 1990s property handouts and today's pay data are read as fair in the same way in both places.
- **Mulgimaa against Võrumaa and Setomaa:** compare farm-inheritance customs where farms were bought early with areas of smaller holdings.
- **Kihnu, Ruhnu, Vormsi and the Saaremaa fishing villages:** check how a catch was shared among a boat crew and with widows.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check fishing-crew shares on the lake and the market terms of the onion growers.
- **Estonian Swedes:** compare 1919 allotments with their post-1991 restitution claims on the coast and islands.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars:** check voucher and residence rules as they touched people without Estonian citizenship in the 1990s.
- **The Jewish community:** check how property lost in 1940-1941 was or was not returned, through the community's records.
- **Lutherans, Orthodox of both churches, Maausk and Taarausk:** check how each speaks of fair shares and of church property returned after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tax, pay and survey data and for proverbs; mixed for 1990s privatization; thin for fishing-crew shares and for the smaller communities.
- **Languages needed:** dialect and old-orthography Estonian for runo songs; Russian for Ida-Virumaa labor studies; Swedish for coastal claims.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** citizenship rules in the 1990s handouts are contested: the state's account and the accounts of non-citizen residents differ and stay separate. Individual pay and inheritance files are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
