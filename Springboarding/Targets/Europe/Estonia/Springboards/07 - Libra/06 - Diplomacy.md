# Estonia: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, island speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-1941 Jewish community; German, Swedish and Middle Low German for older diplomatic papers. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life. (Third-party mediation is in Gemini's Mediation sheet.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and Võrumaa:** check whether address forms and indirect refusals differ by region and by age in the politeness studies.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how town officials handle cross-border contacts and how Russian-language press covered the 1994 and 2005-2014 talks.
- **Setomaa:** check how the Seto speak with officials on both sides of a border that runs through their land.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check how congregations dealt with each new authority (tsarist, interwar, Soviet, restored).
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Ruhnu:** check host-and-guest customs on islands where outsiders arrived by sea.
- **Estonian Swedes and Ingrian Finns:** check how their societies negotiated with Sweden and Finland for support and resettlement.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check how each community's leaders speak to the state through their own associations.
- **Faiths:** compare how Lutheran and both Orthodox church leaderships conduct talks with the state over property and status.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and treaties; mixed for everyday tact (some linguistic work); thin for the smaller communities' own dealings with authority.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German for the *Hanserecesse*; German and Russian for 1710; Russian and English for the 1994 and border talks; dialect Estonian for hosting customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the troop withdrawal, retired officers' residence and the border treaty are contested; the two delegations' records, and what Russian-speaking residents of Estonia said at the time, are three different stories to keep apart. Diplomatic files under seal stay closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
