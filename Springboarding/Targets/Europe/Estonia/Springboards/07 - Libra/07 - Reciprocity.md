# Estonia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind (gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm): the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them, including with the dead, the land and the saints. (Revenge proper is Scorpio's; work bees are covered in earlier signs.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Estonian parishes of Harjumaa and Järvamaa against Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** compare mumming records; check which side of the door sang and what payment was expected.
- **Setomaa:** check how the *praasnik* cycle and graveyard meals bind villages to each other and to the dead.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** compare memorial meals and favor networks in Russian-language memoirs with Estonian ones.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check what the community gives and receives at commemorations, and how outsiders are hosted.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** check gifts returned for help at sea and at the harbor, and offerings at island stones.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ingrian Finns, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check each one's mutual-aid and charity bodies, past and present.
- **The Jewish community:** check interwar loan and burial societies against today's community welfare.
- **Faiths:** compare Lutheran giving, Orthodox (both churches) offerings, and Maausk and Taarausk offerings to land and ancestors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for calendar customs, funerals and offering sites; mixed for Soviet favor networks (oral history); thin for survey data on reciprocity and for Tatar and Belarusian mutual aid.
- **Languages needed:** parish dialects in pre-1870s spelling for the mummers' blessing and curse verses; Russian for memorial customs and Soviet favor networks; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish society records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some offering sites are in active use; the practitioners decide what may be published about them. Favor networks of the Soviet era touch living people's reputations; keep accounts anonymous where asked.
- **Held for review:** none.
