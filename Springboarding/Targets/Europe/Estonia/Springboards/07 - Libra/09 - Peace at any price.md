# Estonia: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice; with principled pacifism as its neighbor, not its twin.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and South Estonia:** check whether Soviet-era accommodation is remembered differently by city intelligentsia and by farm families.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how Russian-speaking residents describe keeping quiet or speaking out since 2022, in their own media.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** compare Seto and Võro songs of a bride's endurance with North Estonian ones.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** compare the community's memory of withdrawal with records of the authorities who pressed it.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu and Muhu:** check how island communities settle quarrels quietly where everyone depends on the same ferry, harbor and shop.
- **Estonian Swedes:** check how the 1944 departure, and what went unsaid afterward about the homes left behind, is told in their society's histories.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars, Jehovah's Witnesses:** check each one's record of giving way or refusing under Soviet rule.
- **Faith communities:** set Lutheran sermons on turning the other cheek beside the canons of both Orthodox churches on military service, and ask Maausk and Taarausk groups where they draw the line between keeping quiet and speaking out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1939-1940 diplomacy and Soviet-era memory; mixed for family and workplace silence; thin for the smaller communities' accounts of yielding.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for 1939-1940 Soviet records and Ida-Virumaa media; Seto and dialect Estonian for daughter-in-law songs; Yiddish and Hebrew for *sholem-bayis* teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1939-1940, wartime collaboration, the deportations and the forest war are contested ground; Estonian, Russian-speaking and émigré accounts differ and stay separate. Abuse cases name victims; follow press-council rules and do not identify people.
- **Held for review:** none.
