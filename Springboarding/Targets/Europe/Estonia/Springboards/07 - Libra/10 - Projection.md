# Estonia: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with North and South Estonian, Mulgi, Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu speech), Võro, Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish for the pre-war Jewish community; Low German for the 16th-c. chronicle. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself, and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other"; with confession and owning one's faults as the counter-practice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mulgimaa, Hiiumaa and Saaremaa against Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check whose traits fill the regional jokes and who tells them; the teased region's own versions matter.
- **Setomaa:** check the older Estonian label *poolusulised* ("half-believers") for the Seto, and the Seto's own answers to it, in Seto Institute materials.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** compare how each language's media assigns blame for the same events.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check how outsiders, church and state described them over centuries, beside their own records.
- **Kihnu, Muhu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** check island and mainland images of each other in tales and press.
- **Estonian Swedes and Baltic Germans' descendants:** check how each appears in the "seven-hundred-year night" story and in their own histories.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Ingrian Finns:** check press accounts of blame placed on each in crises, and each community's own record.
- **Faiths:** compare Lutheran and both Orthodox churches' confession practice with Maausk and Taarausk teaching on fault and harm.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for propaganda, media and security documents; mixed for folk images of neighbors; thin for how the smaller communities describe being blamed.
- **Languages needed:** Low German for Russow; Russian for Soviet propaganda and Russian-language media; dialect Estonian for parish jokes and court books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** security yearbooks, the media spaces and the hate-speech bill are contested; the state's, the named groups' and each language community's accounts stay separate. Parish-court slander files name private families.
- **Held for review:** none.
