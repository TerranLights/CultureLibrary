# Estonia: The domain: death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; Latin and German for older records. **Concept in one line:** death and transformation: how a people meets death, power and what is kept hidden, across death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance and secrets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and South Estonia:** check cremation shares and funeral vocabulary in city and farm parishes.
- **Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** set mound cemeteries and village graveyards beside North Estonian churchyards; check farm-succession replies too.
- **Setomaa:** compare feast-day grave meals with Võro practice; Pechory district, which the Seto count as Setomaa, is comparison only.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** check which cemeteries Russian-speaking families use and how Russian-language papers word a death.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu) and Läänemaa:** check deaths at sea, Swedish churchyards, Kihnu funerals.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** look at burials and memorial days held without priests.
- **Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check each one's cemetery sections and burial rules; sources for the last three are thin, so start with their own community bodies.
- **Faiths:** set Lutheran, Orthodox (both churches), Catholic, Maausk and Taarausk burial forms beside civil funerals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for burial archaeology, folklore of the dead and vital statistics; mixed for sex ways; thin for couples' money and for smaller communities' death ways.
- **Lands:** Estonia's exact lands are yet to be settled, so this sign's sheets take Estonia as it stands, with no unit list and no border change. Neighbors serve as comparison; Estonians abroad take the nation's findings.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the chronicle; German for parish books before about 1890; Russian and Church Slavonic for memorial rites; Seto for feast-day graves; Hebrew and Yiddish for gravestones.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** excavated remains, graves of the deported and disputed war memorials: whose dead are honored is told differently by each community. Death and health registers are privacy-protected.
- **Held for review:** none; no domain pointer was set aside.
