# Estonia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood, with grief and mourning (funeral practice sits in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check where cross trees and Seto funeral laments survive, and which parishes still use them.
- **Tallinn and Tartu hospitals against farm parishes of Järvamaa and Mulgimaa:** compare where people die and who keeps vigil.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check ninth- and fortieth-day memorials in Orthodox families, and how suicide and violent-death figures are reported in Russian-language media.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu and Ruhnu:** look for drowning deaths, the missing at sea and how islanders mourn a body never found.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check how lay elders lead the dying and the funeral.
- **Jewish community and Tatars:** check rules on swift burial and on mourning days, and where each community's dead lie.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** look at war and deportation deaths in each one's own memoirs; for Ukrainians, check also how deaths in the war since 2022 are mourned here. Sources are thin; start with their societies.
- **Faiths and none:** compare Lutheran and Orthodox (both churches) teaching on the afterlife with Maausk and Taarausk accounts and with nonreligious respondents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore of the dead and mortality data; mixed for end-of-life care; thin for minority mourning customs and for grief support outside Tallinn and Tartu.
- **Languages needed:** dialect transcriptions in the death-omen files; German and Latin for funeral sermons; Russian for Orthodox memorial days; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Jewish dead.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide records are sensitive, so work from aggregates; war dead of opposing armies, deportees' graves and Holocaust sites carry rival accounts, which stay labeled by community; bereaved families decide what is told.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on death was set aside.
