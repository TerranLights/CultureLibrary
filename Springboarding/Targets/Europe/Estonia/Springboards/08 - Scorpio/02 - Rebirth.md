# Estonia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending (conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South Estonia (Tartumaa, Võrumaa, Mulgimaa):** check where the 1840s conversions clustered and where later reconversion followed.
- **Setomaa:** check what the revival rebuilt and who leads it; the Pechory side is comparison only.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare reclamation plans with residents' own talk of renewal, and Russian-language recovery groups with Estonian ones.
- **Vormsi, Noarootsi and Ruhnu Swedes:** check the 1870s revival and the return visits since 1991.
- **Kihnu, Saaremaa, Muhu and Hiiumaa:** check which old customs were revived for heritage and by whom.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check rebaptism of newcomers and the reopening of prayer houses after 1991.
- **Ingrian Finns, Jewish community, Tatars:** check each community's own account of reviving schools, parishes or societies after 1988.
- **Faiths:** compare Lutheran confirmation numbers, both Orthodox churches' growth or loss, free-church conversions, and the Maausk and Taarausk revival story.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revival movements and national awakening; mixed for language revival; thin for addiction recovery and prison reentry outside Tallinn.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Orthodox conversion files of the 1840s; German for Moravian diaries; Swedish for the Vormsi revival; Võro and Seto for revival media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1840s conversions and the Soviet rites are read as gain or loss by different churches and families; the Singing Revolution's story differs between communities; recovery accounts belong to their tellers.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist communities and teaching in Estonia, as a category only (the author's open question), set aside where reincarnation belief is traced.
