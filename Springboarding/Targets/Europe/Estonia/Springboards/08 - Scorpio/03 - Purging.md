# Estonia: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt in a body, mind, group, institution or place, so the whole can be clean again (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Läänemaa parishes against Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** check where the church's campaigns against groves were strongest and where offering sites survived.
- **Setomaa:** compare Seto fasting and cleansing days with Võro practice, and note what Soviet rule removed from Seto villages.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Sillamäe, Narva, Kohtla-Järve):** compare how residents tell the mine and tailings cleanup in Russian and in Estonian.
- **Tartu and Tallinn:** check who was purged from the university and the party in 1950, and how memoirs from each city tell it.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** check fumigation and sea-water cures, and what the Soviet border zone cleared off the coast.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check rules on dishes, food and contact with outsiders, from the congregations' own writings.
- **Jewish community and Tatars:** check purity practice (mikveh, food, washing of the dead) and where each was able to keep it in Soviet years.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check their own accounts of being removed from homes in the 1930s-40s; sources are thin, so begin with their societies.
- **Faiths:** set Lutheran confession, Orthodox (both churches) fasting, Maausk and Taarausk cleansing rites and secular detox side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church and Soviet-era records and for charm folklore; mixed for environmental cleanup; thin for purity practice among Tatars, Belarusians and Ukrainians here.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and German for visitation protocols; Russian for party files and deportation orders; Finnish for Oksanen; Church Slavonic for Old Believer rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1950 purge, the deportations and lustration are told in opposed ways; keep the regime's categories as its words, not as facts about people. Personal party files may carry access limits.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on purging was set aside.
