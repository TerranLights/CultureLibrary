## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and Järvamaa:** compare how memoirs from capital and countryside name the 1924 and 1940 actors.
- **Võrumaa, Setomaa and Mulgimaa:** check where accounts of betrayed Forest Brother bunkers are densest, and whose voice each archive holds.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** check who uses the word предатель of whom, and how residents' own interviews differ from what broadcasters say about them.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** check sea-escape stories and how the Soviet border zone shaped suspicion between neighbors.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check what counts as abandoning the faith, and how congregations speak of members who left.
- **Jewish community:** check testimonies of 1941 for who hid neighbors and who handed them over, with the survivors' and families' wording kept.
- **Baltic Germans (historical), Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** look at each one's own account of shifting loyalties in 1918-1919 and 1939-1945; little on the last three is published here, so ask their cultural societies first.
- **Faiths:** set Lutheran teaching on adultery beside both Orthodox churches' accounts of their split, and Maausk writing on the old faith's loss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 20th-c. political history and life stories; mixed for spy cases; thin for measured infidelity and for minority accounts outside Tallinn.
- **Languages needed:** the chronicle's Latin for each *perfidia* passage; German for Landeswehr sources; Russian for KGB files and media labels; Seto for song blame.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informer files and life stories name real neighbors; the 1940 actors, the Forest Brothers, the church split and the traitor labels are disputed, so each stays its source's account. Affair threads involve private people: cite patterns, not users.
