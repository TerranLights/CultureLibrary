## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn against Tartu and small towns of Järvamaa and Läänemaa:** check institutional trust by place size, not only by language.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** look for trust in local leaders against Tallinn institutions, and for credit and bargain customs in village records.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** compare trust answers by town and citizenship, including holders of the grey passport, rather than treating Russian speakers as one block.
- **Islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** check lending of boats, nets and tools, and how newcomers become trusted.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check credit and dealing inside the congregation and with outsiders, from community sources.
- **Jewish community:** check whom members turned to for guarantees and credit between the wars, and how the community's own press speaks of the state's reliability.
- **Ukrainians, Belarusians, Ingrian Finns, Tatars:** check whom each relies on, from their own societies; little is in print for Belarusians and Tatars here.
- **Faiths:** compare trust in the Lutheran and both Orthodox churches with Maausk and Taarausk bodies and the nonreligious majority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutional-trust surveys; mixed for credit history; thin for trust between strangers measured by experiment and for minority communities outside the large surveys.
- **Languages needed:** the grain-store files' German; Russian for 1990s banking memoirs and Russian-language survey reports; Ukrainian for refugees' own media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** citizenship status and trust in the state are disputed; report trust gaps as measured and as each community explains them. Vaccine data can be misread as group traits; keep to places and survey answers.
- **Held for review:** none; no trust pointer needed setting aside.
