# Estonia: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong, with forgiveness and amends as the alternatives. Private vengeance set against the state's punishment goes to Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa, Läänemaa and Järvamaa:** look at manor-era arson and 1941 reprisal sites, and how each parish marks them now.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check counter-magic records and postwar killings; Seto accounts across the Pechory side are comparison only.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** set 1990s crime reporting in Russian beside Estonian reports of the same cases.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** check island legends of paying back raiders and wreckers, and feuds over shore and fishing grounds.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** look at Forgiveness Sunday practice and how disputes are settled inside the congregation.
- **Jewish community:** check how 1941 killings and later trials are spoken of in community writing, as its own account.
- **Ingrian Finns, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check each one's own feud and reconciliation customs; little is in print here, so begin with community elders and societies.
- **Faiths:** set the Lutheran petition to forgive debtors, both Orthodox churches' Forgiveness Sunday, and what Maausk and Taarausk writers say of harm returned side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the epic, war legends and wartime memoirs offer much; mixed for crime reporting; thin for measured attitudes to revenge.
- **Languages needed:** dialect Estonian for war legends; German for manor-police files; Russian for 1941 files and 1990s crime press; Church Slavonic for Old Believer rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941 and postwar killings are claimed as resistance by some and as crimes by others; keep each telling tied to the community that tells it. Crime coverage names living people; online-harm cases involve victims, so cite patterns only.
- **Held for review:** none; no revenge pointer was set aside.
