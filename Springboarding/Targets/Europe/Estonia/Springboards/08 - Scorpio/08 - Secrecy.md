# Estonia: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how what may, must or must not be kept hidden is judged; privacy is one of its facets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Tartu against Mulgimaa and Järvamaa villages:** compare secret church rites in Soviet years and who could keep them hidden where.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** check which rites and sites Seto and Võro bodies keep closed; the Pechory side is comparison only.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Sillamäe, Narva, Kohtla-Järve):** check closed-town memories in Russian and in Estonian, and what workers' families were told about the plant.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** look at what islanders kept from the border guards, and what Kihnu bodies share with visitors.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** check hidden books and closed worship, in the congregations' own terms.
- **Jewish community and Tatars:** check what each hid of faith and origin in Soviet years, in community memoirs.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check hidden origins in family stories of the Soviet years; little is published here, so begin with community oral-history projects.
- **Faiths:** compare the seal of confession in Lutheran and both Orthodox churches with Maausk and Taarausk rules on what may be shown of rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era secrecy and folk-belief records; mixed for privacy attitudes; thin for minority secrecy practice and for anonymous online speech.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for closed-town files and the 1989 commission; Seto for rite descriptions; Church Slavonic for Old Believer books; German for 18th-c. lodge records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Peko rite, charm words and sacred-site locations are knowledge their keepers restrict; record only that it exists and who decides, never the content. Biobank and life-story data carry consent limits. The 1939 protocol is read differently by Estonian and Russian state accounts; keep both as accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on secrecy was set aside.
