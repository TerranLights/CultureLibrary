# Estonia: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pärnu, Läänemaa and the west coast:** check flood and storm memory, from 2005 back to older sea disasters.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu:** compare 1944 departures island by island and the Estonian Swedes' own accounts of leaving.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how the 1990s shock and later fire and job threats are told by residents in their own media.
- **Tallinn and Tartu against Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** compare how the 2008-2010 cuts and job losses fell on city and countryside.
- **Setomaa:** check what the control line changed for families and feast days.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** look at village shrinkage and fishing troubles as each congregation describes them.
- **Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns:** check each one's own record of the 1940s and 1990s upheavals.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians:** check how recent arrivals (Belarusians since 2020, Ukrainians since 2022) describe their own crisis and the help they met; their organizations are the place to start.
- **Faiths:** look at what Lutheran, Orthodox (both churches), Maausk and Taarausk bodies offered their people in the pandemic and the first war winter.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1990s and the 2008-2010 downturn; mixed for older disasters; thin for crisis experience in small communities and for helpline data by language.
- **Languages needed:** the plague legends' parish dialects; Swedish for 1944 crossings and the MS Estonia record; Russian for Ida-Virumaa's local press; Seto for border accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** MS Estonia involves the bereaved and an open inquiry; the 1944 flight and the 1990s transition are told very differently by communities; helpline and mental-health data are confidential. Crisis law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no crisis pointer was set aside.
