# Estonia: The domain: belief and philosophy (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; German, Latin and Church Slavonic for older records. **Concept in one line:** belief and philosophy: what is held true and where meaning is sought, across religion, philosophy, higher learning and long journeys, with religious ways and freedom ways as folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and South Estonia:** set census religion answers side by side, and see how a university town shapes the religious vocabulary.
- **Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** put grove and stone records beside parish histories, to find where older sites stayed in use.
- **Setomaa:** check the chapel calendar against Lutheran Võrumaa next door; Pechory district, claimed by the Seto as part of Setomaa, is comparison only.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** look at Orthodox parishes, Baptist and other free congregations, and Russian-speaking census answers.
- **Islands and the west coast (Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu; Pärnu, Läänemaa):** compare 19th-c. moves to Orthodoxy on Saaremaa and Muhu with the Swedish parishes of Vormsi and Ruhnu.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** see how the prayer-house elders keep the church year and the order of service.
- **Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** find each group's own registered congregations or cultural societies first; printed sources are few for the last three.
- **Lutherans, the Orthodox of two churches, Catholics, Maausk and Taarausk, the unaffiliated:** note who is allowed to speak for each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census religion, church history and folk religion; mixed for philosophy; thin for smaller faiths outside Tallinn.
- **Lands:** with no settled lands and no pre-war change in Estonia's tracker, the Sagittarius sheets work from present-day Estonia; they draw no unit list and fix no border. Neighbors are comparison only; Estonians abroad take the nation's findings.
- **Languages needed:** Low German and Latin for Reformation-era town records; Russian for the commissioner's files; Church Slavonic for Orthodox and Old Believer books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Maausk sites and rites are kept by their keepers, who decide what is shown; the two Orthodox churches' status and ties abroad are disputed, so each church's account stays labeled as its own.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist groups in Estonia's lands, as a category for the author; religions of South Asian origin and South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside from the census-category pointer.
