# Estonia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tartu and Tallinn congregations against Mulgimaa and Järvamaa farm parishes:** compare dues-paying members per parish with census answers.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** see whether chapel vows and Lutheran confirmation draw the same families, and how each is recorded.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** set Orthodox practice of the two churches beside Baptist and Pentecostal Russian-language congregations; ask how each counts its faithful.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa and Kihnu:** trace island Orthodox parishes from the 1840s conversions to now, beside Lutheran ones.
- **Vormsi, Ruhnu and Noarootsi:** look at Swedish-language worship before 1944 and its return since 1991.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** compare young and old members on fasting and reading the services.
- **Tatars and newer Muslims; the Jewish community; Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians and Belarusians:** check how each community's own leaders describe observance; studies of the last three are few, so start with their parishes.
- **Maausk and Taarausk:** check whether their members use the word *usk* at all, or prefer *maailmavaade* or *põlisusund*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Lutheran statistics and Soviet-era policy; mixed for Orthodox and Old Believer practice; thin for Muslim and smaller free-church life.
- **Languages needed:** Latin to read Henry's baptism chapters; Church Slavonic for the Old Believers' service books; Seto for vow customs; Tatar for the Islamic congregation's own papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the split between the two Orthodox churches is disputed ground, so each one's account is labeled; Soviet-era informers among clergy appear in archives and touch living families; some Maausk rites are shared only by their keepers.
- **Held for review:** nothing; every faith pointer above stands under the rules.
