# Estonia: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or the state, and freedom as a belief and political ideal (deciding for oneself and not depending on others stay with Aries; rights as written go to Libra and Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Tallinn against Tartu and South Estonia:** compare which freedom days and sites each city marks, and the stories told at them.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** check what *свобода* refers to in local memoirs of 1991 and 1993, and how grey-passport holders describe travel.
- **Võrumaa, Setomaa and Mulgimaa:** look at Forest Brothers' memory in the southern forests, and Seto accounts of the 1990s line through their villages; Pechory district stays comparison only.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu and Kihnu:** check island memory of Soviet border-zone permits, when moving to and from the coast needed papers.
- **Vormsi, Ruhnu and Noarootsi Swedes:** compare their older free status, their 1944 departure and their return.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** trace the search for freedom of worship that brought them, in their own village histories.
- **Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check what the 1925 cultural autonomy meant for each, and, for Ukrainians since 2022, what freedom means to those who fled.
- **Congregations under Soviet rule (Lutheran, both Orthodox churches, Baptist and Pentecostal, Maausk circles):** set each one's memory of closed churches and banned gatherings side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1918-1920 and 1987-1991 history; mixed for felt freedom; thin for minority voices outside Ida-Virumaa and Tallinn.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German and Latin for 1343 chronicles; German for emancipation-era manor records; Russian for Narva's 1993 press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language and citizenship policy, the grey passport, the Soviet deportations and the 2007 Bronze Soldier events are contested ground; Estonian, Russian-speaking and other communities' accounts stay labeled by community, and no bullet here states what a group is.
- **Held for review:** no freedom source was withheld from this sheet.
