# Estonia: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and the warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Tartu and South Estonia:** compare the market for seers and energy healers with church-linked belief.
- **Võrumaa and Mulgimaa:** look for older belief terms that survive in local speech and how younger speakers use them.
- **Setomaa:** check how Seto speakers sort what counts as Orthodox teaching and what as household custom; Pechory district only as comparison.
- **Läänemaa, Pärnu and the islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** set coastal and sea beliefs, as recorded, beside the island churches' teaching.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** in Russian-language sources, keep *приметы* and church teaching as separate threads and see where they meet.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** see how prayer-house elders draw the line between faith and *суеверие*.
- **Jewish community and Tatars; Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians and Belarusians:** look for creeds, teaching and debates each community has printed for its own members; the latter three have printed little, so their cultural societies are the door in.
- **Maausk and Taarausk members, Lutherans, Catholics, the Orthodox of two churches, the unaffiliated:** compare what each group means by "believing".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief and religious surveys; mixed for new spirituality; thin for political-belief history before 1991.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-c. German for Boecler; Russian dialect for Peipsi recordings; Hebrew and Yiddish for pre-war Jewish print.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** calling a belief *ebausk* is itself a verdict, so record who uses the word; Maausk members may object to their practice being filed as folk belief.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist groups in Estonia's lands, as a category, set aside from the new-spirituality pointer.
