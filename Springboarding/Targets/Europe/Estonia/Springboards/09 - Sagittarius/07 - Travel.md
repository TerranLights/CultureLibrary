# Estonia: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** going far from home (long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration) and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler; short trips and commuting stay with Gemini.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harbor-city Tallinn against inland Tartu:** one receives cruise passengers by the shipload, the other sends and hosts exchange students; compare how residents of each talk about visitors.
- **Hiiumaa, Vormsi, Ruhnu and Noarootsi:** set Estonian Swedes' removals (1781, 1944) beside their return visits since 1991.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu and Kihnu:** follow seafaring families and their long voyages, and today's ferry dependence, through island museums.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how travel to Russia, and visits from it, are described in Russian-language local press before and after 2022.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** look at how Seto pilgrims cross to Pechory and how the crossing has changed; Pechory itself is comparison only.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** trace fishermen's and traders' journeys across the lake in older times, from village histories.
- **Jewish community, Tatars, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** compare each group's arrival story as the community tells it, and its trips back; the Ingrian Finns' return migration of the 1990s needs its own sources.
- **Lutherans, Catholics and the Orthodox of two churches:** compare who organizes pilgrimages abroad and to which shrines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era travel memories, emigration and tourism statistics; mixed for pilgrimage; thin for Old Believer and Tatar travel histories.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and Ukrainian-Swedish sources for Gammalsvenskby; German for emigrant-era manor papers; Russian for Pühtitsa and border-crossing press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 flight and the deportations east were forced moves, not travel, and need each community's own account; Pühtitsa belongs to the church whose status is contested; refugees from Ukraine are a living group whose details stay private.
- **Held for review:** the pilgrimage, migration and tourism pointers all stand as written.
