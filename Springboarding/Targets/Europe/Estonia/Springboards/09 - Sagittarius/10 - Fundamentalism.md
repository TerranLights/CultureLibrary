# Estonia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against change; whether the word fits secular or political certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Capital and university town:** map the 1990s charismatic and strict congregations of Tallinn and Harjumaa against those of Tartu and the south, noting which closed and which grew.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** check how Russian-language Baptist, Pentecostal and Orthodox congregations describe their own strictness, and how outside reports describe them; keep the two apart.
- **Võrumaa, Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** look at the 19th-c. revivals and their 20th-c. heirs, and whether strict prayer-house groups survive.
- **Setomaa:** check whether any strictness debate exists between church Orthodoxy and Seto chapel custom; Pechory district only for comparison.
- **The islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** see how small island congregations handle newcomers' movements.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** ask members which outside labels they reject.
- **Jewish community and Tatar and newer Muslim communities:** check how each one's leaders describe strict observance, and whether outsiders' labels appear in security reports or the press.
- **Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians and Belarusians:** look at Lutheran and Orthodox congregations each community founded; sources are scarce, so start with the congregations.
- **Maausk and Taarausk:** check whether purity of tradition is argued among them, and in what words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for security reports and church disputes; mixed for strict Protestant groups; thin for Muslim and smaller Jewish voices on the label.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the crusade sources; Russian for the Orthodox dispute and Soviet campaigns; Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fundamentalist", "sect" and "extremist" are charges; record who uses each; the Orthodox dispute is contested and current; security yearbooks and radicalization cases involve living people and minors, so work from published, anonymized material.
- **Held for review:** the labels and movements listed here could all be named, so the line is empty.
