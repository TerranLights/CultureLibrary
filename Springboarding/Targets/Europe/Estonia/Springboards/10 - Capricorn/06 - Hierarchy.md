# Estonia: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; Middle Low German and German for guild and knighthood records. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Toompea against Tallinn's Lower Town; Harjumaa:** trace how separate jurisdictions (until 1877 *check*) shaped who lived above and below.
- **Tartu and South Estonia, Mulgimaa:** compare village ranks of farm masters, farmhands and cottagers in Livonian parishes with those in Estland.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** trace plant and mine job ladders by home language, before and after 1991.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** check how leelo choirs order lead singer and chorus, and how village feasts seat guests.
- **Kihnu, Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa:** check ranked roles at island weddings, as island museums record them.
- **Vormsi and Ruhnu Swedes:** compare free Swedish farmers' standing with Estonian serfs nearby under the same lords.
- **Lake Peipsi's Old Believer villages (Kolkja, Kasepää, Varnja, Mustvee, Raja):** ask how a priestless congregation ranks its elders and members.
- **Tatars, Ingrian Finns and the Jewish community:** look for internal ranks in its own records; for Ukrainians and Belarusians little is written.
- **Church and faith bodies (Lutheran, both Orthodox, Maausk, Taarausk):** set clergy grades beside the say given to lay members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval and manor ranks and for income data; mixed for Soviet cadre history; thin for minority internal ranks.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German (the guild rolls); German for knighthood registers and the resettlement; Russian for cadre files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *jeestlased*, the "700 years" story and the language gap in jobs are disputed; each account keeps its author's name. Some personal cadre files may still be closed to readers.
- **Held for review:** none.
