# Estonia: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; German for the Baltic codes. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Spirit of the law only; the letter is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check envelope-wage rates and trust in courts in the capital against rural counties.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and the Peipsi shore:** look at how border trade and smuggling are reported in local Russian- and Estonian-language papers.
- **Tartu and South Estonia:** set Tartu's legal scholars beside village memory of folk judgments in Võrumaa and Mulgimaa.
- **Setomaa:** check what is recorded of village settlement of disputes, and how the border since 1991 changed daily law.
- **North coast villages (Käsmu, Võsu) and Hiiumaa:** compare how smuggling is remembered in each.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** check old wreck-salvage and shoreline customs against state law.
- **Estonian Swedes:** look at how Swedish-law memory shaped coastal custom before 1944.
- **Holders of undetermined citizenship, and Ukrainians and Belarusians who arrived recently:** check how each describes its dealings with the law; keep each account its own.
- **The Jewish community and Tatars:** look at religious law kept beside state law in each community's own sources; little is published.
- **Lutherans, the Orthodox of both churches, Old Believers, Maausk:** compare teaching on obeying unjust laws.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional debate, trust surveys and tax-evasion studies; mixed for folk law; thin for minority religious law.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Baltic code; Russian for dissident files and border towns; Finnish for prohibition-era smuggling records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** legal continuity and citizenship, and the Forest Brothers as outlaws or fighters, are contested; give every account with its source. Spirit only: no reading of statutes as written.
- **Held for review:** none.
