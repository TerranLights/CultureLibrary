# Estonia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia and of the Peipsi Old Believers, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; German for early printed calendars. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** compare complaint data in Lasnamäe, Kalamaja and Nõmme, to see whose order each district's residents call for.
- **Ida-Virumaa's postwar towns (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** collect how people living in rebuilt and planned towns describe their streets, in Russian and Estonian.
- **Tartu and South Estonia, Võrumaa, Mulgimaa:** compare ERM answers on yard keeping between southern and northern counties.
- **Setomaa:** check the order of a *kirmas* (graves, church, meal, singing) in Seto accounts.
- **Old Believer street-villages on Lake Peipsi:** look at how the icon corner and the single-street plan order house and village.
- **The islands (Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu):** compare mumming and feast customs on each island against the mainland.
- **Pärnu, Läänemaa and Järvamaa:** look at summer-resort disorder in Pärnu and how the town regulates it, beside the farm order of Järvamaa.
- **Estonian Swedes:** check how Vormsi and Noarootsi village plans differ from Estonian villages.
- **Russian-speaking Tallinners, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Tatars and the Jewish community:** gather what each says of 2007 or of feeling safe in public, in its own words; for the smaller groups, little is published.
- **Lutheran, both Orthodox churches, Maausk:** compare views of the order of nature and the year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, enforcement data and the 2007 events; mixed for household order; thin for small communities' views of public safety.
- **Languages needed:** German for early calendars; Russian for 2007 press, Soviet patrol files and Narva sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bronze Night and the 1924 uprising are told in conflicting ways; keep each account with its source. Some 2007 case files are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
