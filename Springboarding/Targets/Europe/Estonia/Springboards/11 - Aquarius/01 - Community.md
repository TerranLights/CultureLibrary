# Estonia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (standard, plus South Estonian, Mulgi and island forms), Võro, Seto, Russian as used in Estonia and by the Peipsi Old Believers, Estonian Swedish, Ukrainian for post-2022 arrivals, Yiddish for the prewar Jewish community, German for 19th-c. ethnography. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (place, faith, trade, origin, interest, cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** map where neighborhood societies (*asumiseltsid*) exist, in which language they work, and which districts have none.
- **Tartu, South Estonia and Mulgimaa:** check whether parish days and farm-name use keep the parish as a membership line.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** read Seto Congress (*Seto Kongress*) resolutions beside Võro village plans, for how each names its own people.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare workplace-based community before and after plant closures, recording home language and citizenship.
- **Kihnu, Ruhnu, Vormsi, Muhu, Hiiumaa, Saaremaa:** test how and when summer people (*suvitajad*) and incomers come to count as islanders.
- **Peipsi shore:** check which institutions Old Believer and Estonian fishing villages share, and which they keep apart.
- **Tatars, Estonian Swedes, Ukrainians, Ingrian Finns, Belarusians:** count each group's registered bodies; for Ukrainians since 2022, look for new *hromada* self-help groups. Belarusian and Tatar sources may be scarce.
- **Lutheran and both Orthodox parishes, Maausk and Taarausk circles:** check which keep a membership roll and what membership asks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village life, proverbs and rural change; mixed for town neighborhoods; thin for the smaller minorities' community bodies.
- **Languages needed:** German for Wiedemann; Russian for Old Believer and Narva sources; Ukrainian for the newest groups; Võro and Seto for local plans.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** villages emptied by the March 1949 deportations and by collectivization are told in more than one way; a village's own memory, a family's and the state's are kept apart and labeled.
- **Held for review:** none.
