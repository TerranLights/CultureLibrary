# Estonia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (with island, Mulgi and southern speech), Võro, Seto, Russian (Estonian usage), Estonian Swedish, Ukrainian, Yiddish; Latin for the crusade chronicle; German and Russian for 19th-c. parish-community files. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how a culture weighs group against person, draws "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check whether workplace teams in the tech sector and in older industries report different group norms, recording working language.
- **Tartu, South Estonia, Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** compare 19th-c. recruit-selection records parish by parish, to see whether lots or votes were used.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check where "we" means the village, the Seto people or the parish, using local songs and society records.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare Soviet-era work-collective records with today's union and party membership, by citizenship status.
- **Kihnu, Ruhnu, Muhu, Vormsi, Hiiumaa and Saaremaa, with Läänemaa and the Pärnu shore:** check boat crews and fishing cooperatives for how shares and blame were split.
- **Peipsi shore:** see how Old Believer and Estonian fishermen formed crews, and whether crews crossed the faith line.
- **Ingrian Finns, Belarusians, the Jewish community, Estonian Swedes, Tatars:** check which group bodies each runs and who may join; written sources may be scant for Belarusians.
- **Faith bodies, from Lutheran parishes and the two Orthodox churches to Maausk and Taarausk circles:** look at membership changes between the two Orthodox churches since 2022, in each church's own statements.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, elections and Soviet-era archives; mixed for folk games; thin for group life inside the smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Henry; Russian for kolkhoz minutes and Narva-area sources; Ukrainian for post-2022 groups; Võro and Seto for local "we" words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collective blame is contested ground, in the Soviet deportations of whole families and in the 2007 Bronze Soldier events; set out each community's account as its own, never assigning a group a fault. The Orthodox church dispute is live.
- **Held for review:** none.
