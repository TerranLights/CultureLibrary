# Estonia: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian, Võro, Seto, Estonia's Russian, Ukrainian, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish (interwar Jewish press); Romani only as the Roma community publishes it. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harjumaa and Tallinn against Valga, Võru and Põlva counties:** set wage and service data side by side, to see whether place sorts people as much as sex or language does.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare citizenship status, home language and job outcomes in each town, keeping holders of Estonian, Russian and undetermined citizenship apart.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** check whether regional-language speakers report being treated as less than standard-language speakers in school and office.
- **Island people (Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Saaremaa, Ruhnu, Muhu, Vormsi):** test whether islanders' access to doctors, schools and ferries is framed as an equality question.
- **Peipsi shore and Mulgimaa, Järvamaa:** compare rural pay and pensions with town figures.
- **Women and men across regions and both main languages:** separate the pay gap by county and language before reading it as one national number.
- **Roma, Ukrainians, Tatars, Estonian Swedes, Belarusians, the Jewish community, Ingrian Finns:** see which bodies speaking for each group report unequal treatment, and on what grounds.
- **People with disabilities:** compare access data with what their own organizations say.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gender and regional statistics and policy debate; mixed for attitudes to sexual minorities; thin for Roma and other small groups.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for school-transition and citizenship sources; Ukrainian for newer arrivals; German for manor-era terms of rank; Võro and Seto for regional-language claims.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether the grey passport, the language rules and the school transition are equal treatment is itself disputed; quote each community's own claim, named as it names itself, and decide nothing. LGBT and Roma data concern small groups easily exposed.
- **Held for review:** none.
