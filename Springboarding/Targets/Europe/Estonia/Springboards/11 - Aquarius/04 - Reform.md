# Estonia: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its regional forms, Võro, Seto, Russian (including Old Believer usage on the Peipsi shore), Estonian Swedish, Yiddish and Hebrew for 19th-c. Jewish reform currents; Middle Low German, German and Swedish for Reformation and Swedish-era records. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check whether reform programs (pension, tax, digital state) drew different verdicts in the capital and in rural counties, using county-level polls.
- **Tartu and South Estonia:** trace the university town's part in the 1980s heritage and student movements.
- **Setomaa and Võrumaa:** set the Setomaa merger beside Võro towns merged against local wishes, in council minutes.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** read how each town's Russian-language press and Estonian national press reported plant closures and the oil-shale phase-out, as accounts of the same reform.
- **Hiiumaa, Saaremaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** see whether island councils accepted or fought mergers and school closures.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** their own histories of refusing Nikon's 17th-c. church reform, read for how a community remembers saying no to reform.
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** compare the 19th-c. farm-buying and society movement with Soviet farm amalgamation, as farm families tell them.
- **Estonian Swedes, Ukrainians, the Jewish community, Belarusians, Ingrian Finns, Tatars:** check which reforms each group's bodies called for or opposed, especially on schooling.
- **Lutherans and the Orthodox of both churches:** compare the 16th-c. Reformation's legacy with the 19th-c. conversions to Orthodoxy as rival reform stories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policy debate and 19th-c. movements; mixed for coercive Soviet programs; thin for the smaller groups' own reform aims.
- **Languages needed:** for the Reformation, Middle Low German and German; Swedish for the reduction; Russian for the LTP and Ida-Virumaa sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for *haskole* among Estonia's Jews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet collectivization was called a reform by its makers and a catastrophe by many families; the integration programs and school-language rules are argued from opposite sides. Give each as an account. LTP and medical files may be closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
