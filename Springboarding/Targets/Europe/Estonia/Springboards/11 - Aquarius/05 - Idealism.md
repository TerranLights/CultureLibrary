# Estonia: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (literary and regional), Võro, Seto, Russian as spoken and published in Estonia, Estonian Swedish, Ukrainian, Yiddish for prewar Zionist and socialist youth groups; German for the 1830s-1840s Estophile writings. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check how tech founders' talk of changing the world sits beside the capital's activist groups, using their own mission texts.
- **Tartu and South Estonia:** compare the university town's student idealism across the 1860s, 1960s and 1980s.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** see whether language revivalists and Seto leaders write of an ideal past or a future, in their own publications.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** read Narva's 2024 bid and local youth projects for the ideals they name, keeping Russian-language texts in Russian.
- **Islands and the west coast (Kihnu, Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Vormsi, Ruhnu; Pärnu, Läänemaa):** look at incomers who moved for an ideal of simple life, and how islanders describe them.
- **Peipsi shore:** see whether Old Believer writings set purity of the old faith as an ideal against later practice.
- **Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** check the farm as an ideal in interwar rhetoric against farmers' own letters.
- **Tatars, Ukrainians, Ingrian Finns, Belarusians, Estonian Swedes:** look for the ideals each group's societies wrote into founding statutes.
- **Maausk and Taarausk circles, Lutheran parishes, both Orthodox churches:** set side by side the ideal of a good life each teaches, from its own texts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the awakening and values surveys; mixed for present-day movements; thin for the smaller minorities' own ideals.
- **Languages needed:** German for the 1840s Estophile texts; Russian for Lotman, Soviet nostalgia and Narva's bid; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish youth groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kingissepp, *Eesti aeg* and Soviet nostalgia are remembered as ideals by some and as harms by others; label each memory with its holder. The Soviet deportations sit under several of these memories.
- **Held for review:** none.
