# Estonia: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (including dialect material from the islands, Mulgimaa and the south), Võro, Seto, Russian of Estonia (Peipsi Old Believer usage too), Estonian Swedish, Yiddish; Low German and German for 16th-17th-c. court records. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature, and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** compare the capital's tolerance of visible difference with small towns, using bullying and neighbor surveys by settlement size.
- **Tartu and South Estonia:** trace the student town's place in the 1960s-80s underground, through memoirs of artists and poets.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** check how Seto and Võro communities have spoken of eccentrics and of those who left the old ways.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** set Russian-language accounts of youth subcultures beside Estonian ones, without assuming either community is stricter.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Vormsi and Ruhnu; Läänemaa:** test the record of revivalist sects in the west against Lutheran parish complaints.
- **Old Believer villages by Lake Peipsi:** ask how a community founded on refusing reform treats its own members who leave or marry out.
- **Pärnu, Mulgimaa and Järvamaa:** check whether downshifters and incomers settle more in some rural districts than others.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses, Pentecostals, Maausk and Taarausk members:** gather each group's own account of being treated as odd or suspect.
- **Belarusians, Ingrian Finns, Ukrainians, Tatars, the Jewish community, Estonian Swedes:** compare what counts as nonconformity inside each group with what the majority sees.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era dissent and art, and for surveys; mixed for subcultures; thin for eccentrics in small groups and for gender nonconformity before 1991.
- **Languages needed:** Low German and German for trial records; Russian for Komsomol files and Old Believer lives; Church Slavonic for holy-fool texts; Yiddish for religious dissent in the old Jewish community.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** queer oral histories and psychiatric records hold private lives; follow archive and consent rules. Soviet labels of deviance were also political weapons; read them as the state's words, not as facts about the people named.
- **Held for review:** none.
