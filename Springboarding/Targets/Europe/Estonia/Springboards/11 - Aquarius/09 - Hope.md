# Estonia: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian (folk-song and dialect material included), Võro, Seto, Russian as used in Estonia and in Old Believer and Orthodox parish life, Ukrainian for refugees since 2022, Estonian Swedish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** compare consumer-confidence and life-expectation answers in the capital with rural counties, to see whether hope follows income or place.
- **Tartu and South Estonia, Mulgimaa:** count how often the *Kalevipoeg* return promise was quoted in the southern press during the awakening.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** look at Seto Easter and church-feast prayers for what is asked, and at Võro language revivalists' hopes, in their own texts.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** split future-expectation data by citizenship and home language, so worry about jobs is not mistaken for a view of the state.
- **Sea people of the islands and west coast (Kihnu, Ruhnu, Saaremaa, Vormsi, Muhu, Hiiumaa; Läänemaa, Pärnu):** check fishermen's and farmers' weather and luck customs for the hopes they guard.
- **Peipsi shore:** read Old Believer end-of-days writings for hope of salvation, beside Estonian fishing villages' secular hopes.
- **Järvamaa and rural central Estonia:** compare young people's plans to stay or leave with those in towns.
- **Minorities old and new (Ukrainian, Belarusian, Jewish, Ingrian Finnish, Tatar, Estonian Swedish):** set each group's hopes of return or of rootedness beside its own history of arrival.
- **Believers and non-believers alike:** ask Lutheran, Orthodox (each church), Maausk, Taarausk and unaffiliated respondents where they place hope (God, effort, family, the state), reading each group's own texts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, folklore and the epic; mixed for despair (sensitive health data); thin for the hopes of smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Orthodox practice, Old Believer texts and Ida-Virumaa sources; Ukrainian for refugee surveys; Seto for prayer texts; Church Slavonic for Old Believer writings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportations and the "white ship" carry rival memories; keep each memory tied to the person or community that holds it. Suicide and mortality data need care; deportee letters are private family papers.
- **Held for review:** none.
