# Estonia: The domain: the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community; German and Latin for older records. **Concept in one line:** the hidden and transcendence: how suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape are met, with confinement and institutions, and the magic ways (healing, charms, divination, witchcraft) as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa against Järvamaa farm parishes:** check whom people turn to first in distress (doctor, pastor, "wise one", no one), town against country.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** compare which healers and laments are still known by name; Pechory district, Setomaa to the Seto, is comparison only.
- **Saaremaa, Muhu, Hiiumaa:** test whether island witch-trial files and *puuk* legends reflect belief or only record survival.
- **Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** look for sea-danger customs in their own collections.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Russian-speaking Tallinn:** split help-seeking and institutional data by home language; read Russian-language coverage of healers and psychics.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** see how lay-led villages pray over the sick and guard against the evil eye, from their memoirs.
- **Mulgimaa, Läänemaa and the Pärnu coast:** check whether the folk calendar's dark-season customs are remembered alike.
- **Lutherans, both Orthodox churches, Maausk, Taarausk, Jewish, Tatar, Ingrian Finn, Ukrainian and Belarusian communities:** gather each one's own account of the unseen; the last three have few sources, so start with their societies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief, charms, the calendar and institutions; mixed for present help-seeking; thin for smaller communities' healing and for esoteric trades.
- **Lands:** for the last sign, as for the others, the sheets work from the republic's present map: Estonia's tracker settles no lands and records no shift in its pre-war ones, so no unit is named and no line redrawn. Beyond that map, for comparison: Finland, Sweden, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Belarus and Russia's culture-nations (with Pechory district, claimed by the Seto as Setomaa). Emigrant communities inherit what is found here.
- **Languages needed:** German and Swedish for trial and asylum files; dialect transcription for charms; Russian for Soviet psychiatry; Seto for laments.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patient files are closed; Soviet punitive psychiatry and the prisons of 1940-1991 carry rival accounts, each labeled by its teller; living healers decide what is told.
- **Held for review:** none.
