# Estonia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn against Tartu:** check whether food-bank and shelter users differ between the two cities, and which causes each city's givers favor.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** compare social-assistance uptake and harm-reduction work with Harjumaa, and how media in each language word one appeal.
- **Võrumaa, Setomaa and Mulgimaa:** look for farm-era customs of keeping the old and orphaned, and for Seto feast-day feeding of the poor in the *praasnik* records.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Kihnu and Ruhnu:** check how help for a drowned man's household was gathered on small islands.
- **Peipsi shore Old Believers:** study almsgiving rules kept by lay congregations.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians:** separate prewar residents from arrivals since 2022, as both givers and receivers of help; Belarusian sources are thin.
- **The Jewish community and the Tatars:** check each community's own welfare bodies and their records before and after 1940.
- **Estonian Swedes and Ingrian Finns:** look for help returned or refused across the 1944 flight and the postwar return, in each community's memoirs.
- **Lutheran diaconia, both Orthodox churches, Catholic charities, Maausk and Taarausk groups, secular NGOs:** compare whom each serves and the words each uses for the people it helps.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval town charity, parish poor relief and present NGOs; mixed for giving surveys; thin for smaller communities' mutual aid and for Swedish-coast poor relief.
- **Languages needed:** Low German for the almshouse books; German for 19th-century poor-relief files; Russian for Ida-Virumaa outreach; Ukrainian for the 2022 help networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** one large 2022 donation charity is disputed, so each account stays its teller's; welfare files are closed; drug users and sex workers speak through their own organizations.
- **Held for review:** none.
