# Estonia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Saaremaa and Muhu:** compare chronicle accounts of the 13th-century surrenders with what island museums and guides tell now.
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** check how the 1940 events and the 1994 withdrawal are retold in the capital's museums (Vabamu) and Russian-language media.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe) and Paldiski:** look at how former military families describe leaving or staying after 1991, in their own words.
- **Tartu, Võrumaa and Setomaa:** look for plague legends and saints'-day weather sayings on accepting a bad year; Seto prayer for the sick.
- **Läänemaa, Pärnu coast, Hiiumaa, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** check sea-loss sayings and how coastal families tell giving up a search; Estonian Swedes' accounts of losing land in 1944.
- **Järvamaa and Mulgimaa:** compare farm families' accounts of giving up the farm at collectivization (1949) and at the 1990s reform.
- **Peipsi Old Believers:** check what *smirenie* means in their own teaching and memoirs.
- **Ingrian Finns, the Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** look at each one's own telling of yielding and holding out in 1941-1945 and since 2022; sources are uneven.
- **Lutherans, both Orthodox churches, Maausk, Taarausk:** compare prayers and teachings on accepting fate, and whether Maausk sources speak of fate at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1939-1940 debate, proverbs and plague records; mixed for recovery and therapy; thin for small communities and for present attitudes to giving up.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for the rhymed chronicle; German for 1710 parish books; Russian for the 1994 withdrawal; Church Slavonic for Old Believer prayer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether 1939-1940 was surrender, betrayal or the only choice, and how 1944 and 1994 are read, are contested; give each account by its teller and community. Shelter and treatment records are private.
- **Held for review:** none.
