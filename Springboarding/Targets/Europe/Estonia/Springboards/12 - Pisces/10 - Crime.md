# Estonia: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law itself (statutes, courts, procedure) goes to Libra and Capricorn; this sheet cites them only.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** compare 1990s memories of street crime with the present tabloid map of "dangerous" districts (Kopli, Lasnamäe).
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how drug crime and smuggling are told locally in Russian and Estonian, and whether coverage links crime to the language community; record the claim, never adopt it.
- **Hiiumaa, Saaremaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu:** look at wrecking and smuggling legends and what islanders say was a crime and what was custom.
- **Võrumaa, Setomaa, the Peipsi shore:** check horse-theft and border-trade lore, and whether Old Believer villages keep their own ways of dealing with a wrongdoer.
- **Tartu and South Estonia, Mulgimaa, Järvamaa:** set parish penance records beside rural memories of theft from the collective farm.
- **Läänemaa and the Pärnu coast:** look at coastal Swedish accounts of shipwreck plunder and of the 1940s.
- **Ingrian Finns and Estonian Swedes:** see whether their memoirs name wrongs done to them in 1940-1950 as crimes, and by whom.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** look in each community's own press for complaints of being suspected, and for wrongs it wants named; little is collected.
- **Lutheran confession, Orthodox and Old Believer penance, Maausk and Taarausk teaching:** compare how each lets a wrongdoer back in, and what it asks of him first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, church records and press; mixed for surveys of attitudes; thin for smaller communities and for ex-prisoners' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** German for visitation protocols; Russian for chanson, Ida-Virumaa and 1990s sources; Swedish for coast legends; dialect Estonian for robber and charm records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who counts as a criminal in 1941-1953 (Forest Brothers, destruction battalions, deportation organizers) and the 2007 Bronze Soldier arrests are contested; label each account by its teller. Child-abuse and probation files are closed.
- **Held for review:** none.
