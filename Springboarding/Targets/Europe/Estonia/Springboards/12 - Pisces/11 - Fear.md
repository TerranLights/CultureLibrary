# Estonia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Estonia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Estonian and its dialects (North and South Estonian, Mulgi, the island speech of Saaremaa, Hiiumaa and Kihnu), Võro and Seto, the Russian of Estonia, the coastal Swedish of the Estonian Swedes, the Russian of the Peipsi Old Believers, Yiddish for the historical Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tallinn and Harjumaa:** compare fear of crime and of war in surveys with the city's ghost-tour and horror trade.
- **Ida-Virumaa (Narva, Kohtla-Järve, Sillamäe):** check how security fears since 2022 are voiced by Russian-speaking residents, each by their own account, and how Sillamäe talks of radiation.
- **Võrumaa and Setomaa:** look at fright cures and forest beings in local memory; Seto fears tied to the border closure.
- **Saaremaa, Hiiumaa, Muhu, Kihnu, Vormsi, Ruhnu, Läänemaa:** check sea fear after 1994 and island storm warnings.
- **Järvamaa, Mulgimaa and Tartu county:** compare wolf fears in farm areas with town views.
- **Pärnu and the west coast:** look at flood fear after the 2005 storm surge, as residents recall it.
- **The Peipsi shore and its Old Believers:** see how ice danger and the border are spoken of, in the villages' own words.
- **Estonian Swedes and Ingrian Finns:** look for fear of 1944 and of deportation in their own memoirs.
- **The Jewish community, Tatars, Ukrainians, Belarusians:** check each community's own statements on safety, then and now; Ukrainians since 2022 are a separate thread.
- **Pulpit, icon corner and grove:** set Lutheran and Orthodox sermons on the fear of God and hell beside what Maausk and Taarausk writers say a person should fear, if anything.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore of fright and for disaster and preparedness data; mixed for anxiety measures; thin for smaller communities' fears.
- **Languages needed:** German for Kelch and famine parish books; Russian for Ida-Virumaa and 2022 coverage; Swedish for MS *Estonia* and coastal sources; Seto for border fears.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the cause of the MS *Estonia* sinking, fear of Russia, and fear between language communities are contested; present each account as its teller's and never as what a group feels. Victims' families decide what is said of them.
- **Held for review:** none.
