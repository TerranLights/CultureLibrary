# Finland: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish; Finland-Swedish; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi; Karelian; Finnish Kale Romani; Russian. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ostrobothnia and Finland Proper:** check painted dowry chests and estate inventories side by side, since coastal trade may have filled some households with bought goods sooner than others.
- **Savo and Tavastia:** check inventories of inland farms for what was homemade, borrowed or held in common in the village.
- **Capital region and Uusimaa:** check small-flat living, storage cages and self-storage, where space limits what a household keeps.
- **North Karelia and the evacuated Karelian parishes:** check what was carried in 1940 and 1944 and what became keepsakes; objects left in houses now on the Russian side are part of the story.
- **Kainuu and Lapland:** check what the burning of Lapland in 1944-45 left people, and how households were refurnished.
- **Ålanders:** check island inventories and seafarers' souvenirs in Åland collections.
- **North, Inari and Skolt Sámi:** follow which museum-held objects each group has asked back, and how *duodji* pieces stay in families.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check object-handling rules only through Roma-authored sources, with the community's consent.
- **Orthodox Karelians, Old Believers and Ingrian Finns:** check icons and religious objects as family heirlooms brought across borders.
- **Tatars and Jews:** check family heirlooms and religious objects in community archives; sources are few and specific.
- **Russian-speakers and Somali-Finns:** check what was brought when moving, and what is sent back to relatives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inventories, grave finds and household durables; mixed for the felt bond with things; thin for minorities' keepsakes.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth- to nineteenth-century Swedish for most estate inventories; Finnish for later ones; Sámi for repatriation claims in Sámi words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi remains and sacred objects in collections; Roma purity rules are community knowledge; hoarding cases involve named people.
- **Held for review:** the estate inventories, kept here for what was owned; the rules of probate go to Libra.
