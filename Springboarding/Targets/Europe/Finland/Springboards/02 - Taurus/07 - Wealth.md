## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ostrobothnia (Finnish- and Swedish-speaking):** check tar, ship and farm fortunes against local poverty records, so the region's wealth is shown with its losers.
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check old merchant and industrial families against the newer tech wealth of Espoo and Helsinki.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check manor wealth and the merchant town against the crofts around them.
- **Tavastia and Savo:** check forest-industry towns such as Mänttä and the forest wealth of farmers selling timber.
- **North Karelia, Kainuu and Lapland:** check where income per head trails and how the gap is spoken of locally.
- **Ålanders:** check shipowning fortunes and island attitudes to display in Åland sources.
- **North, Inari and Skolt Sámi:** check whether herd size, fishing waters or craft skill brings standing, asking herders, fishers and makers themselves.
- **Karelian evacuees:** check property left behind and how families rebuilt wealth; the lost lands across the border bear on their accounts.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check livelihood history through Roma historians, since outside accounts of Roma trade often carry prejudice.
- **Tatars and Jews:** check merchant histories in each community's own publications.
- **Russian-speakers and Somali-Finns:** check business owners and remittance-sending households in their own studies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; public tax records, wealth surveys and business histories are plentiful; thin for minority fortunes and the hidden poor.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish above all for the older industrial families' papers, Finnish for tax and survey data; Sámi for herd wealth in its own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** named individuals' tax data; family fortunes from the war years; poverty studies naming people.
- **Held for review:** the rules on tax-data publicity, which belong to Libra; kept here only for what the publicity shows about wealth's standing.
