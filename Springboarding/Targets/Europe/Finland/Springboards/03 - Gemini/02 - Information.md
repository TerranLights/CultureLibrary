## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa against Kainuu and Lapland:** check broadband reach, distance to libraries and local-paper closures to see where information thins out.
- **Ostrobothnia, Finland Proper and Åland:** compare the Swedish-language and Finnish-language papers serving the same towns, since their agendas may differ.
- **Savo, Tavastia and North Karelia:** check regional papers (*Savon Sanomat*, *Karjalainen* *check*) as each region's own news record.
- **North, Inari and Skolt Sámi:** check which news reaches each language, and who decides what Sámi knowledge is recorded or kept back.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check Roma-run magazines (query *romanilehti*) and how Roma are reported in the general press.
- **Karelians and Ingrian Finns:** check evacuee papers and, for Karelia beyond the border, Karelian-language papers there (*Oma Mua* *check*), as part of the people's own press.
- **Russian-speakers:** check which news sources they use, Finnish, local Russian-language or from Russia, as a question of trust and reach.
- **Somali-Finns, Tatars, Jews, Old Believers:** check community newsletters, mosque and congregation bulletins, and group chats as the channels each uses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history, news-use surveys and statistics; mixed for word-of-mouth channels; thin for the smallest communities' own media.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish for the press; 1700s Swedish and old Finnish for the first papers; Russian and Somali for community channels; Sámi for Sámi news.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi records and some traditional knowledge are the community's to govern; Cold War self-restraint is still debated; tax-data publicity touches named people.
- **Held for review:** empty. The publicity principle of official documents, data protection and press statutes are legal texts and belong to Libra and Capricorn.
