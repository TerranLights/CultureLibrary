# Finland: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish; Finland-Swedish; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi; Karelian; Finnish Kale Romani; Russian; Somali. **Concept in one line:** living with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once, dividing the world into pairs, and how people who show two faces are treated (twins go to Siblings).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ostrobothnia and the Turku archipelago:** check two-language villages and mixed families, where Finnish and Swedish meet daily, against one-language areas.
- **Uusimaa and Helsinki:** check the city's long two-language history and its newer many-language youth talk.
- **Åland:** check whether a one-language Swedish island inside a two-language country holds its own kind of two-ness.
- **Tavastia, Savo and Finland Proper:** check where 1918 split villages most deeply, region by region.
- **Lapland, with North, Inari and Skolt Sámi:** check bilingual and trilingual life, two names for the same land, and how each Sámi group describes it.
- **North Karelia, Kainuu and Karelians:** look at households keeping both Orthodox and Lutheran feasts, and evacuees who speak of two homes; Karelians in lands now in Russia are counted with the people for this concept.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check Roma-authored accounts of moving between community and majority settings.
- **Ingrian Finns and Russian-speakers:** check life between Finnish and Russian, and how returnees were received.
- **Somali-Finns, Tatars, Jews, Old Believers:** check each community's own words for holding two belongings; Tatars and Jews have long histories to compare with newer arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for bilingualism, the civil war and literature; mixed for folk beliefs on doubles; thin for Old Believers.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish together for bilingual families; Russian for Ingrian and Russian-speaking families; Sámi for place names; old Finnish for runo metrics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1918 memory still divides families and towns; the two-faced label falls on real groups in politics; questions of who is "really" Finnish touch on racism.
- **Held for review:** none. Rules on holding two citizenships, and on two official languages, are legal matters for Libra and Capricorn; the lived two-ness stays here.
