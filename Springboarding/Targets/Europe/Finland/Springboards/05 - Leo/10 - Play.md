## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check playground parks and paid hobbies against country yard play.
- **North Karelia and evacuee communities:** check *kyykkä* and other Karelian games kept or lost after the moves.
- **Ostrobothnia and Tavastia:** check village and youth-society games, from the SKS and local museums.
- **Lapland and the three Sámi peoples:** compare *sáhkku*, lasso throwing and herding games, North, Inari and Skolt each separately.
- **Finland-Swedes and Ålanders:** check Swedish-language children's games and island summer play.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check children's games and play at summer gatherings, from Kale sources; likely thin in print.
- **Karelians in Russia and Ingrian Finns:** check village games recorded on the far side of the border.
- **Tatars, Jews, Old Believers, Russian-speakers, Somali-Finns:** check children's games brought from elsewhere and those taken up in Finnish schoolyards.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's lore, toys and board games; mixed for adult play outside sport; thin for Kale, Old Believer and Somali children's games.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish; North Sámi with Inari and Skolt for game names; Karelian; Latin and Swedish for Linnaeus; Russian and Somali.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's lore can include taunts and rude rhymes; Sámi game knowledge is shared by its holders.
- **Held for review:** none; age limits on gaming and betting are a Libra matter.
