## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check whether usefulness is spoken of through paid jobs and productivity more than through household and neighborly tasks.
- **Rural Savo, Kainuu and North Karelia:** check older people's roles on emptying farms, where children have moved to towns.
- **Ostrobothnia and Finland-Swedes:** check practical-skill culture (boat building, home repair, sewing circles) in both languages.
- **Åland:** check the island households' use and reuse of things where shops and repairmen are farther away.
- **Lapland and the Sámi peoples:** check children's and elders' roles in herding, fishing and crafts, each people separately.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check elders' standing and the family's help to them, from Kale organizations; little is in print.
- **Karelians and Ingrian Finns, in Finland and in Karelia now in Russia:** check evacuees' and returnees' accounts of being "of no use" after losing farms.
- **Russian-speakers, Somali-Finns, Tatars:** check grandparents' and children's tasks inside each community.
- **People with disabilities and older people:** check how each group speaks of being needed, in their own organizations' words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for working-conditions and pension surveys; mixed for elders' and children's roles; thin for Kale and Tatar views of the burden question.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish (*Strömsö* and SLS legends are Swedish-only); North Sámi for herding youth; Russian and Somali for older migrants in their own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** being a burden touches end of life, disability and suicide; treat accounts with care and never read a statistic as a verdict on a group.
- **Held for review:** the euthanasia pointer, kept for the "burden" language only; any statute on end-of-life care is for Libra and Capricorn.
