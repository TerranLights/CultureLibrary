## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check court caseloads and sentencing against small district courts; the largest courts set the pace for others.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check the appeal court's early files for how royal justice reached the parishes.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check the 19th-century knife-fighting cases and the sentences given, a regional run of violence before the courts.
- **Tavastia and Savo:** check the 1918 camps and field courts (Hämeenlinna, Lahti and others), where many Red prisoners were held.
- **North Karelia, Kainuu and Karelians from the ceded lands:** check evacuee compensation files and the courts' work on resettlement claims.
- **Lapland's Sámi:** check North, Inari and Skolt Sámi separately; roll rulings and the Skolt Act touch each group differently.
- **Åland:** check the island court's own records and language.
- **Finnish Kale Roma:** check how state courts and Kale custom meet after violence, from Kale-led sources.
- **Ingrian Finns, Russian-speakers, Somali-Finns and Tatars:** check legal-aid use and contact with courts in each, and what each group's own media report on trials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, prisons and court history; mixed for Kale custom, which is held within families; thin for Tatar and Old Believer dealings with courts.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish for codes and judgments; early modern Finnish for Ljungo's translations; the Sámi languages for council papers; Russian and Somali for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kale avoidance and feud customs touch living families; 1918 camp records name the dead and their judges.
- **Held for review:** none; why punishment exists and how far people trust courts are Capricorn's, so only the written rules and recorded rulings are kept here.
