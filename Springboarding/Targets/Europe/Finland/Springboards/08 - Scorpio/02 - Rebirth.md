## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Savo and Ostrobothnia:** check how the awakening and other revival movements describe the moment of change, movement by movement.
- **Finland Proper and Satakunta:** check sleep-preacher cases and the prayer movement (*rukoilevaisuus*), where trance and repentance were recorded.
- **Lapland, Rovaniemi and the Sámi:** check town rebuilding after 1944-45 beside Sámi language revival, two kinds of renewal in one region.
- **Inari and Skolt Sámi:** check each language's nest program separately; Skolt revival follows its own resettlement history.
- **North Karelia and Karelians from the ceded lands:** check language revival and Easter customs carried west.
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check recovery fellowships and re-entry programs, where most services sit.
- **Finland-Swedes, Ostrobothnian and Åland:** check Swedish-language revival and temperance societies as their own sources.
- **Kale Roma, Tatars, Somali-Finns, Russian-speakers:** check conversion and return to faith in each community, and how families receive it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revival history, folk poetry and name-change records; thinner for Skolt Sámi revival and for Kale conversion, where community sources and interviews are needed.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish, with older forms for revival letters; Swedish; Inari, Skolt and North Sámi; Karelian; Russian; Somali for community faith.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leaving a movement can break families, so ex-members' accounts need care; addiction and prison histories are personal; Sámi revival is the Sámi's own story to tell.
- **Held for review:** none; no conversion, recovery or revival source needed setting aside.
