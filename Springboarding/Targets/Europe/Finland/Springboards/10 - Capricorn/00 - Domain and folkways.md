# Finland: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish; Finland-Swedish; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi; Karelian; Finnish Kale Romani; Russian; Somali; Tatar. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain, authority and duty: who commands, who obeys and what is owed, through career, status, reputation, and the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** compare talk of officials and bosses near ministries and head offices with talk elsewhere.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check deference formed while Turku held bishop, academy and court.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check freeholding peasants and the Club War memory against the gentry; Swedish-speaking coastal parishes as their own series.
- **Tavastia:** check manor and croft as a school of masters and dependents.
- **Savo, North Karelia and Kainuu:** look at slash-and-burn settler households and the Orthodox village offices as contrasts with the western parish order.
- **Lapland:** check the *siida*, the herding cooperative and the Skolt village meeting as distinct seats of say over people and land, each Sámi group (North, Inari, Skolt) on its own.
- **Åland:** check how island self-government shapes whose authority islanders accept first.
- **Peoples:** for Somali-Finns, Tatars, Jews, Old Believers, Kale Roma, Finland-Swedes, Russian-speakers and Ingrian Finns, check who holds office inside each and how it meets Finnish officials; Finnic Karelians east of the border where needed. Sámi elsewhere, Estonians and emigrant Finns: outside comparisons only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state, church, labor and survey records; thin for Old Believer, Tatar and Kale internal authority, much of it oral.
- **Languages needed:** older Finnish and Swedish for estate-era records; the three Sámi languages for *siida* and herding authority; Russian and Karelian for Soviet Karelian papers; Somali and Tatar for community bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who speaks for the Sámi is a live dispute; 1918 still divides family memory of masters and servants. Which lands the nation will hold is not yet decided: the tracker carries Russia among its pre-war lands, read as Finnic and Karelian communities on the Russian side, named only where needed; no border is drawn. Law as written sits in the Libra sheets (`07 - Libra`); this sign takes only its spirit.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the author may settle which estate-era terms count as living vocabulary.
