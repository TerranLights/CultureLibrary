# Finland: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish; Finland-Swedish; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi; Russian; Somali; Karelian and Ingrian Finnish for the eastern record. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** see whether acceptance of police and officials' orders varies across Helsinki's districts, where contact with them is uneven.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** look in the cathedral city's clergy and gentry records for how church and crown authority were displayed.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check the revival preachers' authority against the parish priest's; Swedish-speaking parishes apart.
- **Tavastia and Savo:** check 1918 and the Civil Guard era for local memory of who gave orders and who refused them.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** check Orthodox priests and village elders as rival or paired authorities.
- **Lapland:** check reindeer-herding cooperative heads, Laestadian elders and state agencies as three kinds of command; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi separately.
- **Åland:** check islanders' reading of mainland agencies' reach.
- **Peoples:** check the Kale elders' standing as Kale writers describe it; Tatar and Jewish congregations' own boards; Russian-speakers' and Somali-Finns' encounters with police and teachers; Ingrian Finns and Karelians where Soviet authority shaped what they expect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, church and political history; mixed for workplaces; thin for Kale and Old Believer authority from the inside.
- **Languages needed:** early modern Finnish for the Bible versions, beside today's Finnish and Swedish; Russian for Ingrian and Orthodox church records; the Sámi languages for consultation papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** police-encounter studies touch minority complaints; 1932 and 1918 remain politically charged. The rules on police powers and obedience to orders as written sit in the Libra sheets.
