## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** see if classroom-calm complaints cluster in fast-growing school districts, to avoid reading a city issue as national.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check the cathedral school's long record of pupil discipline.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check Laestadian and Pietist households' rules on dress, media and conduct, and Swedish-language revival groups apart.
- **Tavastia:** check mill towns such as Tampere and Forssa for the arrival of clock discipline.
- **Savo and North Karelia:** check Orthodox fasting rhythms against Lutheran restraint in the same villages.
- **Kainuu and Lapland:** check boarding-school memories among North, Inari and Skolt Sámi, kept apart, and among Finnish pupils sent far from home.
- **Åland:** check seafaring training as a discipline learned young.
- **Peoples:** check Kale child-rearing as Kale writers describe it; Somali-Finnish and Russian-speaking parents in their meetings with schools; Tatar and Jewish congregations' religious-school routines; Ingrian Finns raised under Soviet schooling.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for schools, army and public health; mixed for home discipline before the ban; thin for minority child-rearing seen from inside.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Swedish for early school records; Finnish and the Sámi languages for boarding-school testimony; Russian and Somali for parents' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment of children touches living people and child-welfare files; reform-school accounts may name victims.
- **Held for review:** none; the author may decide whether reform-school history sits here or with a later sign's crime concepts.
