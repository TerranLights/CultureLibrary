## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** map the north-south split of Helsinki and the newer rifts between suburbs.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** set the academic and merchant gentry against the archipelago farmers.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check freeholders with few gentry against Swedish-speaking coastal towns with their own merchant families.
- **Tavastia:** check manor-dominated parishes, where crofters were many, as the sharpest rural ladder.
- **Savo and North Karelia:** check the eastern farm household's ranks, including Orthodox Karelian joint families.
- **Kainuu and Lapland:** check the herding economy's own differences of wealth, and North, Inari and Skolt Sámi separately in how herd size and kin standing count.
- **Åland:** check shipowner families as the islands' top rank.
- **Peoples:** check Finland-Swedes, where the old elite and fishing villages share a language but not a rank; Kale precedence by age and gender, as Kale writers describe it; Tatars and Jews as trading minorities; Russian-speakers before 1917 and after 1990; Ingrian Finns and Karelians from the eastern lands, whose standing in host towns shifted; Somali-Finns in a ranked labor market.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for estates, census and class research; mixed for ethnic ranking, where survey wording matters; thin for internal rank among Sámi herders and Kale families in print.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish, with older forms for registers; Russian for merchant records; Yiddish for early Jewish Helsinki; North Sámi for herding.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ranking of ethnic groups must be quoted as respondents' views, never as fact; segregation data can stigmatize districts.
- **Held for review:** none; keeping estate-era ranks and modern class as separate Stage 0 units is the author's call.
