*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** compare younger and older residents' talk of law as a tool (complaints, appeals, legal aid).
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** look for a lawyerly tradition in the court-of-appeal town's local life.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check the knife-fighter memory beside freeholders' pride in old law, and Swedish-speaking parishes apart.
- **Tavastia and Savo:** check how 1918 courts are remembered, as a test of whether law was seen as the people's own.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** check border smuggling and poaching lore as law bent locally.
- **Lapland:** check Sámi customary rules against state law, for North, Inari and Skolt Sámi separately; Finnish herders' views of the same rules.
- **Åland:** check islanders' sense of their own legislation as distinct from the mainland's.
- **Peoples:** check Kale customary rules and the state, from Kale-led sources; Russian-speakers and Ingrian Finns raised under Soviet law, for what they expect of officials; Somali-Finnish elders' mediation beside courts; Tatar and Jewish congregations' religious rules in daily life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal theory, penal policy and trust surveys; mixed for customary codes; thin for Kale and Old Believer rules in print, kept mainly within families.
- **Languages needed:** older Swedish and Latin for estate-era legal thought; the Sámi languages for customary rules; Russian for Soviet-era expectations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kale customary rules and Sámi land claims are sensitive; street-patrol groups require care in sourcing.
- **Held for review:** none; statute texts, court structure and rulings are left to `07 - Libra`.
