# Finland: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check complaint and safety data across districts, since perceived disorder clusters unevenly and can stigmatize places.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check the fire-replanned city and archipelago village by-laws.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check tidy-yard customs and church-village plans in both language groups.
- **Tavastia and Savo:** check farmyard order and the cleaning calendar in SKS replies, region by region.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** check Orthodox household order against Lutheran neighbors' habits.
- **Lapland:** check fences, herding districts and seasonal moves as land order, with North, Inari and Skolt Sámi apart.
- **Åland:** check island village order and Mariehamn's plan.
- **Peoples:** check Kale household purity and cleanliness rules, from Kale-led sources; Somali-Finnish and Russian-speaking residents' views of disorder in their own neighborhoods; Ingrian Finns' and Karelians' memories of Soviet "order"; Tatar and Jewish congregations' ordering of the year.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for planning, police and survey data; mixed for household order; thin for Old Believer household custom in print.
- **Languages needed:** medieval Swedish for the peace laws; Karelian and Russian for Orthodox homes; the Sámi languages for herding order.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disorder data can be used against districts and minorities; 1949 still divides labor and employer memory.
- **Held for review:** none; household tidiness as a habit leans on Virgo's sheets, and the author may draw that line.
