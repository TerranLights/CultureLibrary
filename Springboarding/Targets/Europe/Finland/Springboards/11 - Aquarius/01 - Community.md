# Finland: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish; Finland-Swedish (with Åland usage); North and Skolt Sámi; Karelian; Russian; Tatar; Hebrew and Yiddish for congregational records. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** set estate residents' talk of "our area" against older inner-city districts, to see if community follows tenure or length of stay.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** test whether islanders in the outer archipelago count the whole parish or only their own island as "the village".
- **Ostrobothnia:** compare Laestadian, Awakening and secular villages, and Swedish coastal *byar*, for what membership asks of a household.
- **Savo and Tavastia:** look at villages that lost their school against those that kept it.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** compare Orthodox feast villages with Lutheran ones, and villages that emptied with those that held.
- **Lapland:** check Skolt villages resettled after the war, North Sámi herding families and Inari Sámi lake villages each as a community of its own.
- **Åland:** check the *byalag* against municipal life.
- **Peoples:** check Kale Roma, Tatars, Jews, Old Believers, Russian-speakers and Somali-Finns for how each defines a member; Karelian evacuees and Ingrian return migrants for community carried without its place. Finland-Swedes in towns against fishing villages. Estonians and Finns abroad: outside comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village movement, revival and urban-policy material; thin for Old Believers and for Kale community life in print, where oral routes and Kale organizations are the way in.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish for village books and minutes; Karelian and Russian for Orthodox village sources; Skolt Sámi for resettled villages; Tatar and Hebrew for congregation papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ex-member testimony from revival communities is painful and contested; give both members' and leavers' voices. Kale family matters are not public by default.
- **Held for review:** whether revival congregations become Stage 0 units of their own waits for the author.
