# Finland: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Finland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Finnish (with student and army slang); Finland-Swedish; North Sámi; Russian; Finnish Kale Romani. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check student nations, tech-student guilds and supporters' groups, which gather people from the whole country into city-based crews.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** look at the Swedish- and Finnish-language universities as two student worlds.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check the Lapua movement's home parishes, and Swedish coastal towns that stayed outside it.
- **Tavastia and the Tampere area:** check factory towns where Red and White clubs stood side by side.
- **Savo, North Karelia and Kainuu:** compare where 1918 bit less deeply with where it bit most.
- **Lapland:** at roundup time, see who works beside whom: North, Inari and Skolt Sámi crews, and Finnish herders' crews, each its own team.
- **Åland:** check whether mainland party and union lines matter at all on the islands.
- **Peoples:** check Kale kin groups and their rules on conflict between families, as Kale writers state them; Finland-Swedes, where one language holds several classes; Russian-speakers, asking whether the Soviet-era word *kollektiv* appears when they describe work teams; Somali-Finns, where clan and nationwide associations both claim members; Tatars and Jews as small congregations. Sámi groups in Norway and Sweden as comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, sports, students and the 1918 split; thin for Kale and Old Believer group life in print.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish slang dictionaries for student and army words; Swedish for coastal and student sources; North Sámi for herding groups; Russian for *kollektiv* talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1918 and the Lapua abductions still divide families; Kale conflict rules must come from Kale sources, never from police files alone.
- **Held for review:** none; whether the 1918 sides count as groups or as a single divided unit is for the author.
