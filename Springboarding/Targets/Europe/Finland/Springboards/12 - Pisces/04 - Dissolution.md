## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check club culture, psychedelic circles and second-generation language loss, which may cluster in the city.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check archipelago parishes merged into larger units and how islanders speak of it.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check Swedish-speaking towns where Finnish is gaining, and the region's free-church revivals.
- **Savo and Tavastia:** check villages emptied by migration to towns and what their former residents write of them.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** check the mill closures and outmigration of Kainuu, and Karelian speakers whose language faded after evacuation.
- **Lapland:** check North, Inari and Skolt Sámi separately for assimilation and revival, and the mill town of Kemijärvi.
- **Åland:** check the municipal-reform quarrel as a test of how far island communities resist merging.
- **Peoples:** check Kale Roma, Ingrian Finns, Tatars, Russian-speakers, Somali-Finns and Jews for how each speaks of keeping or losing its language and ways; Old Believers through their surviving records, to see whether a separate community life lasted; Meänkieli and Kven speakers across the border only for comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language shift and closures; mixed for ecstatic experience; thin for the Ingrian jumpers and for Old Believers, where church and local archives are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish; Ingrian and Karelian dialects; North, Inari and Skolt Sámi; Kale Romani; Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dementia, drug use and forced assimilation of Sámi and Roma children are painful topics; survivors' and families' accounts come first. Language-rights and municipal-merger legislation go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the definition is proposed, and the author may decide whether assimilation belongs here or under identity.
