## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Uusimaa and the capital region:** check the alternative-spirituality market, silent chapels and migrant congregations, and how city seekers name the sacred.
- **Finland Proper and Turku:** check archipelago pilgrimage and the Turku synagogue as small sacred worlds near the old cathedral town.
- **Ostrobothnia:** check Swedish-speaking seekers in Åbo Akademi's Vaasa data beside Finnish-speaking ones.
- **Savo and Tavastia:** check lake and forest sites where offerings were recorded, and present-day nature retreats.
- **North Karelia and Kainuu:** check Orthodox chapel feasts and the forest prayers of Viena Karelian singers.
- **Lapland:** check North, Inari and Skolt Sámi devotion separately, in each language, and keep apart what Sámi share publicly from what they do not.
- **Åland:** check the pilgrim route and island chapels for spiritual practice outside Sunday worship.
- **Peoples:** check Finland-Swedes, Kale Roma, Tatars, Somali-Finns, Russian-speakers, Old Believers and Jews for practice at home and in small groups, each in its own terms; Estonia and Sámi beyond the border only as comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey items and church-run care; mixed for alternative spirituality; thin for native-faith groups and Old Believers, where self-published material and interviews are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Swedish; North Sámi (and Inari and Skolt for local practice); Karelian and Church Slavonic for chapel feasts; Russian; Somali and Arabic; Hebrew.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi sacred places and practice are partly restricted; offering-site locations should not be spread beyond the official register; healing claims touch health. Laws on religious communities go to Libra and Capricorn.
