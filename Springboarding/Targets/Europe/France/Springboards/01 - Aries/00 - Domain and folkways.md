## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France against the provinces:** check whether dress and naming trends start in the capital and spread, or arise separately.
- **Brittany, Alsace, Corsica, Occitania:** each has its own name stock and a regional-language revival; check how regional first names and old house names were used against official ones.
- **The Nord and Picardy, and Wallonia:** check carnival and giant figures (*géants*) as collective selves next to individual ones, across the border.
- **Luxembourg:** a three-language society; check which language a person uses to name and introduce themselves, and when.
- **Maghrebi-French, Kabyle-French and West African-French families:** check first-name choice across generations and the naming rules of each origin.
- **Antilleans and the overseas departments** (pointed to where needed): check Creole naming and nicknames against the civil record.
- **Manouche, Gitans and other Travelers:** check personal names kept inside the community against those on papers; sources are thin.
- **Armenian-French, Vietnamese-French, Portuguese-French, the Chinese of Paris, Jewish communities:** check surname change, double names and community records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for metropolitan France (statistics, museums, life writing); mixed for Wallonia and Luxembourg; thin for Travelers and some newer communities.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for France, French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. Flanders, the French Basque Country and Northern Catalonia are comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** French throughout; Luxembourgish and German for Luxembourg; regional languages for folklore; Arabic, Kabyle, Wolof and Creole for heritage communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** French official statistics rarely record ethnicity or religion; name-based studies of origin are contested and must be read with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose naming, dress and body customs would otherwise sit in section 3.

## 5. First queries
1. Download INSEE's *Fichier des prénoms* and chart three regional name stocks (Breton, Alsatian, Corsican) by department and decade.
2. Search the APA catalog for diaries by region and occupation, and note what is held for Wallonia and the Midi.
3. Read Mauss's essay, then the first volume of *Histoire du corps*, to set the French academic frame before the folk sources.
4. In van Gennep's *Manuel*, read the birth and baptism chapters and list the regions it covers least.
5. Query Statbel and STATEC for first-name tables and compare their categories with INSEE's.
