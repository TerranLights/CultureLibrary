# France: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Maghrebi Arabic, Kabyle and Wolof for heritage communities. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and the provinces:** check whether meeting and classroom speaking norms differ by city size, using the conversation studies above.
- **Gascony and Aquitaine:** check how the *gasconnade* label grew and whether people in the region use or reject it.
- **Corsica:** check *chjama è rispondi* singers as a formal arena for claiming the last word.
- **Occitania and Provence:** check the troubadour debate forms and their modern revival at Occitan festivals.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check bilingual workplaces for how claims are voiced in each language.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check regional manifestos and Brussels dialect humor as voice; point to Flanders only as comparison.
- **Luxembourg:** check language choice in meetings and complaints (Luxembourgish, French, German) as a claim in itself.
- **Maghrebi-French and Kabyle-French families:** check *nif* vocabulary across generations; never read it as a group trait.
- **West African-French associations:** check Wolof and Soninke terms in community talk; sources are thin.
- **Women, workers and the young:** check who is told to speak up or keep quiet, in advice columns and school reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on conversation and literature; mixed on everyday complaint and workplace voice; thin on heritage-community vocabulary.
- **Languages needed:** French, with Belgian and Luxembourg usage; Luxembourgish for spoken data there; Occitan and Corsican for the old verbal contests; Kabyle and Wolof for the community terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional labels (*gasconnade*, *râleur*) slide into stereotype; treat them as objects of study. Workplace and ethnic-relations studies carry personal data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose norms for speaking up and complaining would otherwise get a comparison bullet.
