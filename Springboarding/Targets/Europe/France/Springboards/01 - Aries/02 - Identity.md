# France: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Alsatian, Corsican, Picard, Walloon; Arabic, Kabyle, Antillean Creole, Romani, Armenian, Vietnamese and Chinese for communities. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bretons, Alsatians, Corsicans, Occitans:** check which comes first in self-description (region or nation), and whether this shifts by generation and language use.
- **Picards and people of the Nord:** check how the *Ch'ti* label moved from outsiders' joke to local badge.
- **Walloons and Brussels French-speakers:** check self-labels against Belgian, French and European; Flanders only as comparison.
- **Luxembourgers and resident foreigners:** check citizenship, language and the national motto in self-description.
- **Pieds-Noirs and Harkis:** check how memory associations define who they are since 1962.
- **Maghrebi-French, Kabyle-French, West African-French:** check first and second generation self-labels and the answer to "where are you from?".
- **Antilleans in the metropole:** check identity terms against those used in the overseas departments.
- **Jewish communities** (Alsatian, Comtadin, Bordeaux, North African-heritage): check how distinct histories shape self-naming.
- **Armenian-French, Portuguese-French, Vietnamese-French, the Chinese of Paris:** check community press and associations for self-labels.
- **Manouche, Gitans, Yéniche:** check self-names against the official term *gens du voyage*; sources thin, look to community associations and *Études tsiganes* (*check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national and regional identity; mixed for newer communities; thin for Travelers.
- **Languages needed:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton and Alsatian for older memoirs; Arabic, Kabyle, Armenian and Vietnamese for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** French statistics avoid ethnic categories; the *identité nationale* debate of 2009 and terms like *Français de souche* are politically charged. Colonial memory (Algeria, the Antilles) is contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and their self-labels and community press, held back from section 3.

## 5. First queries
1. Request the INSEE *Histoire de vie* microdata and tabulate the first-named identity item by region.
2. Read Renan's lecture, then Nora's introduction, to set the French frame.
3. Search TeO documentation for the "feel French" and "seen as French" questions and list origin groups covered.
4. Search the BnF catalog for *mémoires* by Bretons, Alsatians and Corsicans born before 1930.
5. Query the STATEC census tables for main language spoken, by nationality.
