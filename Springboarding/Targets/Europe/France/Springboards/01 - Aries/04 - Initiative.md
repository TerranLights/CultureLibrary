## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Auvergnats of Paris:** check café and coal trade histories for how migrants from the Massif Central set up in the capital.
- **The Chinese of Paris** (Wenzhou and Teochew origins): check separately, since their migrations and trades differ.
- **Portuguese-French and Maghrebi-French families:** check the routes into building firms and corner shops, and the role of kin credit.
- **Bretons and Alpine farmers:** check cooperative founding as group rather than solo initiative.
- **The Nord and Picardy:** check family textile and retail firms of Roubaix-Tourcoing, and miners' mutual societies.
- **Lyon area:** check silk-trade workshops and later industrial founders.
- **Wallonia, Brussels, Luxembourg:** check reconversion programs after heavy industry, and how far the state or private founders led.
- **Paris's Sentier garment district:** check the succession of communities who worked and founded firms there.
- **Women and the young:** check founders' data by sex and age, and school enterprise schemes such as *mini-entreprises* (*check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business statistics and associations; mixed for immigrant enterprise; thin for informal and Travelers' trades.
- **Languages needed:** French; Luxembourgish and German; Portuguese, Chinese and Vietnamese for community press; Breton for older cooperative papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** studies of ethnic enterprise can harden into stereotype; report them as practices to check, not group traits. Rules on business forms belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** studies of shops and trades run by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back from the immigrant-enterprise pointer.
