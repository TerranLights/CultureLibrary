# France: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Alsatian, Corsican, Walloon; Kabyle, Vietnamese, Chinese and Armenian for community new years. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bretons and Occitans:** check where the year begins in older rural calendars (1 November, Christmas Eve, the New Year) and what rites mark it.
- **Alsatians and Lorrainers:** check New Year and Epiphany customs against French national ones.
- **Provence and Corsica:** check Christmas-to-Epiphany rites and first-of-year visits.
- **The Nord, Picardy and Wallonia:** check carnival and *ducasse* openings as seasonal beginnings.
- **Luxembourgers:** check how the founding legend and the national day are taught and staged.
- **Kabyle-French and other Amazigh-heritage families:** check Yennayer celebrations in associations and how their scale has changed.
- **Vietnamese-French and the Chinese of Paris:** check Lunar New Year parades (13th arrondissement, Belleville) separately by origin.
- **Jewish communities:** check Rosh Hashanah alongside the civil New Year.
- **Pieds-Noirs, Harkis, Portuguese-French, Antilleans, West African-French:** check each arrival story as its own "beginning" and how it is told to grandchildren.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national origin myths and calendars; mixed on family rites; thin on smaller communities' first-year customs.
- **Languages needed:** French; Breton and Occitan for older calendar customs; Luxembourgish; Kabyle, Vietnamese, Chinese and Armenian for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin myths (the Gauls, the Franks) are used in present politics; the 1962 arrivals are contested memory; student initiations involve hazing.
- **Held for review:** new-year and birth rites of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back from the community new-year comparisons.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Beaune's book, then the opening chapters of a Lavisse primary-school history, to see the two founding stories side by side.
2. Search INA for "vœux du président" and list available years.

4. Search Retronews for "la rentrée" by decade.
5. Contact the Décines Armenian archive about arrival testimonies.
