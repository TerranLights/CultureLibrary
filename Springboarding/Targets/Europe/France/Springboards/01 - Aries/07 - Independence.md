## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Corsica:** check the independence movement's own publications against island opinion polls.
- **Brittany and Savoy:** check older claims to separate statehood (the duchy before 1532; Savoy before 1860) and how they are used now.
- **The Nord and Wallonia:** check allotment gardens and mutual societies as working-class self-provisioning.
- **The Massif Central and Occitania:** check farm households' self-supply records and the back-to-the-land newcomers separately.
- **Luxembourg and Brussels:** check national independence stories as taught in schools in each.
- **Maghrebi-French elders (*chibanis*):** check workers' hostels and isolation in old age, through their associations.
- **West African-French village associations:** check money sent home and collective projects as a form of self-help.
- **The Chinese of Paris and Vietnamese-French families:** check rotating credit and family firms.
- **Portuguese-French families:** check self-built houses in France and in Portugal as self-reliance projects.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check independent trades and caravan life in their own accounts; sources thin, start with community associations.
- **Disabled people and elders:** check how *maintien à domicile* is described by those it concerns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on household budgets, aid and rural history; mixed on community self-help; thin on Travelers.
- **Languages needed:** French; Corsican and Italian for Paoli-era sources; Breton; Luxembourgish and German; Chinese, Vietnamese and Wolof for savings groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *assistanat* is politically loaded; separatist movements include past armed groups; data on aid recipients is restricted.
- **Held for review:** savings groups and mutual help among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back from the rotating-credit pointer.
