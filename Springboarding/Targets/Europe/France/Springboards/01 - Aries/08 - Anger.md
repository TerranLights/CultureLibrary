*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with youth slang and Belgian usage); Luxembourgish; Corsican, Breton, Occitan, Alsatian; Maghrebi Arabic, Kabyle, Antillean Creole and Wolof for communities. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Corsica:** check feud records against folk songs and present-day mediation, keeping literary images apart.
- **Brittany:** check revolts from 1675 to the 2013 *Bonnets rouges* for continuity of symbols.
- **The Lyon area and the Nord:** check workers' anger in silk and mining towns through their own press.
- **Paris and Île-de-France suburbs:** check generational and neighborhood differences in how anger at police and schools is voiced.
- **Aquitaine and the Massif Central:** check rural cases like Hautefaye against tax and food riots.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check the 1960-61 strike and the 1996 *Marche blanche* as two kinds of public anger.
- **Luxembourg:** check anger words in Luxembourgish usage and broadcast talk shows; sources likely thin.
- **Maghrebi-French families:** check how *ḥogra* enters French youth speech, and what it names.
- **Antilleans:** check *move san* and other folk-illness ideas that tie anger to the body.
- **Harkis and Pieds-Noirs:** check anger over 1962 in association bulletins.
- **Women:** check how women's anger is named in press and law-court reporting across eras.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on collective anger (revolts, strikes, riots); mixed on everyday temper; thin on community folk-illness terms.
- **Languages needed:** French, including Old and Middle French; Corsican; Breton; Luxembourgish; Arabic, Kabyle and Creole for community terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *Gilets jaunes*, the 2005 riots, police violence and Corsican feuds are live political issues; criminal records have access limits; avoid tying temper to any people.
- **Held for review:** anger vocabulary and folk-illness ideas of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held back from the search terms.

1. Search Gallica for printed *cahiers de doléances* from Brittany, the Nord and Provence and compare their complaint words.
