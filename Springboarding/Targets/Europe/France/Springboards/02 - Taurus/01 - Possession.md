# France: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan; Maghrebi Arabic, Kabyle and Romani for heritage and Traveler communities. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brittany:** check marriage wardrobes, box-beds and their contents in probate lists, and what stayed in the family house.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check the 1939-1940 evacuations, what families carried south and what they found on return.
- **Occitania and the Rouergue:** check relic cults and village treasuries as shared, guarded possessions.
- **Paris against Wallonia and Brussels:** check lost-property and secondhand records on both sides of the border.
- **Pieds-Noirs and Harkis:** check what was packed in 1962, and the objects kept since as memory.
- **Jewish communities:** check family accounts of what was taken and what returned.
- **West African-French residents of workers' hostels:** check how shared rooms limit and shape what a person keeps.
- **Vietnamese-French arrivals after 1975 and Armenian-French families:** check objects carried out by refugees and handed down.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check what happens to the dead's caravan and goods, as families describe it; little is published.
- **Luxembourg:** check what its museums and attics record of rural households; sources may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theft, auctions, spoliation and literature; mixed for ordinary keeping; thin for Travelers and hostel residents.
- **Languages needed:** French; Middle French for Villon; Luxembourgish; Romani and Sinti varieties for Traveler accounts; Arabic and Kabyle for family objects of North African origin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spoliation and colonial-loss files name families; restitution debates are political; Traveler customs around the dead must come from the families themselves.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose heirlooms and gift customs would otherwise be compared here.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Villon's *Testament* in a modern edition, then Bonnot's book, to set an early and a recent frame.
2. Ask the Préfecture de police for yearly figures from the Service des objets trouvés.
3. Search the Mattéoli report for household-goods (*biens mobiliers*) chapters.
4. Search the ANOM catalog for 1962 compensation series, and note access rules.
5. Search Leboncoin's "Dons" section by region to see what is given away.
