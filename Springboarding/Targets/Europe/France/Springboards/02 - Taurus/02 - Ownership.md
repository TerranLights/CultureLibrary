# France: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Maghrebi Arabic and Kabyle. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brittany:** check *domaine congéable*, the old tenure in which a tenant owned the buildings but not the ground.
- **Béarn and the Pyrenean valleys:** check the single-heir house against equal division in neighboring regions.
- **The Massif Central and the Alps:** check hamlet commons and shared summer pastures, and who still uses them.
- **Corsica:** check undivided land, its effect on building and selling, and how families talk about it.
- **Normandy and the *bocage* west against the open-field north and east:** check whether hedged and open landscapes go with different ways of holding land.
- **Alsace and Moselle:** check how their separate land register shapes owners' records (its rules belong to Libra).
- **Paris, Brussels and Luxembourg City:** check renter majorities and housing prices as limits on owning.
- **Pieds-Noirs and Harkis:** check property claims after 1962 and the compensation laws' reception.
- **Maghrebi-French and Portuguese-French families:** check houses owned "back home" alongside renting in France.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check family-owned plots for caravans, and conflicts over where they may stay.
- **The overseas departments** (where needed): check undivided family land and who holds farmland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (cadastres, *compoix*, statistics); mixed for Wallonia's rural tenure; thin for Travelers' land and for Kabyle-French memory of land.
- **Languages needed:** French; Occitan for Béarn and Gascon house sources; Corsican for island land; Breton for tenure terms; German and Luxembourgish for Luxembourg's records; Arabic for Maghreb land categories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent land and property records name living owners; colonial land seizure in Algeria is a painful comparison and stays outside the core.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose property-holding patterns would otherwise be compared here.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Le Roy Ladurie's *Paysans de Languedoc*, then Rousseau's and Proudhon's texts, to set the old and the ideological frames.
2. Open one department's digitized Napoleonic cadastre (for example Finistère and Cantal) and compare parcel patterns.
3. Pull INSEE, Statbel and LISER tenure tables by region and age.
4. Download Eurostat COFOG totals for the three countries, and note how Belgium's regions are counted.
5. Search GIRTEC's reports for the number of untitled parcels by commune.
