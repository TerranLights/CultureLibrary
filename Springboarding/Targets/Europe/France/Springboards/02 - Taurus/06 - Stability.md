# France: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Occitan, Provençal, Alsatian, Breton, Corsican; Arabic for heritage families. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Occitania and the Pyrenees:** check the *ostal* as a continuing house and how long families stayed on one holding.
- **Provence:** check Félibrige memory and present festivals that repeat old forms.
- **The rural Center and the Massif Central:** check depopulation against families who stayed, in census series.
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check residential turnover against the provinces.
- **Lorraine and the Nord:** check how pit and mill closures broke multigenerational working lives.
- **Alsace:** check rural Jewish and Christian village continuity across the changes of 1871, 1918 and 1940-1945.
- **Luxembourg:** check how newcomers and long-settled families each use or ignore the national motto.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check whether federal instability touches daily life in residents' accounts.
- **Corsica:** check ties to the home village (*paese*) among those living on the mainland.
- **Portuguese-French and Maghrebi-French families:** check summer returns to a village house as a form of continuity.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check seasonal routes repeated across generations as their own form of continuity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (census, time use, demography, firm registers); mixed for continuity in heritage communities; thin for Travelers' routes.
- **Languages needed:** French; Occitan and Provençal for the *ostal* and Félibrige; Luxembourgish for the motto's uses; Alsatian for village studies; Corsican for the *paese*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "rootedness" language has been used by nationalist movements; read Barrès and similar sources as part of a debate, not as a neutral frame.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose settlement and continuity patterns would otherwise be compared here.
