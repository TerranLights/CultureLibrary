# France: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Walloon; Maghrebi Arabic and Kabyle. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris's western districts against the northern suburbs:** check where the wealth gap is widest and how each side describes the other.
- **Bordeaux and Burgundy wine estates:** check vineyard land as old wealth handed on in families.
- **The Lyon area:** check silk, banking and industrial fortunes and the old families' own histories.
- **The Nord:** check textile dynasties of Roubaix and Tourcoing and their workers' memory.
- **The Côte d'Azur and the Alps:** check second-home and resort wealth against local incomes.
- **Wallonia:** check the fading of industrial fortunes and the arrival of French residents.
- **Luxembourg:** check finance-sector incomes against cross-border workers' pay.
- **Corsica:** check villa and tourism wealth and how it is argued about locally.
- **Jewish, Armenian-French and Chinese of Paris communities:** check community histories of getting on, written from inside.
- **Maghrebi-French and Portuguese-French households:** check how "making it" is marked, including houses built in the home country.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check what families themselves count as wealth; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (tax series, wealth surveys, rich lists, business history); mixed for Luxembourg's finance wealth; thin for heritage communities' own accounts of wealth.
- **Languages needed:** French; Luxembourgish and German for Luxembourg; Kabyle and Arabic for remittance and status terms; Occitan and Breton for proverbs on rich and poor.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** writing on Jewish or other minority wealth has a long antisemitic and xenophobic history; read it only as evidence of prejudice. Tax data on individuals is closed.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, including traders whose family fortunes would otherwise be compared here.

## 5. First queries
1. Visit or read about the Palais Jacques-Cœur, then read Mollat's chapters on the confiscation.
2. Download WID top-shares series for France and the HFCS tables for the three countries.
3. Read *Les Ghettos du gotha* for method before searching rich-list archives.
4. Search Retronews for "Poujade" with "les gros" to see how the rich were named.
5. Search Belgian press for "exilés fiscaux Néchin" and note local voices.
