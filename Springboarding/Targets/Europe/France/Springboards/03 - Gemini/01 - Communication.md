# France: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French with Belgian and Luxembourg usage; Luxembourgish; Alsatian, Breton, Corsican; Maghrebi Arabic, Kabyle; Antillean Creole where the overseas departments are needed. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the standing of good communication.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Luxembourg:** check how speakers switch among Luxembourgish, French and German inside a single exchange, at work and at home.
- **Alsace:** check when shoppers and stallholders move between Alsatian and French, by age.
- **Brittany:** check Breton-language call-in radio as a record of talk among Breton speakers.
- **Corsica:** check when bilingual island broadcasters switch to Corsican, and for which topics.
- **The Nord and Wallonia:** check where *taiseux* is used and of whom, as a word to map.
- **Seine-Saint-Denis and other Paris suburbs:** check how eloquence contests and slam workshops frame speaking up for teenagers.
- **Maghrebi-French and Kabyle-French families:** check children's own accounts of interpreting for parents at the doctor's or the prefecture.
- **West African-French village associations:** check how meetings in workers' hostels give the floor, in members' own terms.
- **Vietnamese-French families:** check talk between parents and children who lack a fully shared language; sources thin.
- **Antilleans** (overseas, where needed): check storytelling with listener replies against everyday talk.
- **Deaf and hard-of-hearing people:** check interpreter access and LSF relay services in hospitals and offices.
- **Rural elders:** check who is cut off as services move online.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for conduct manuals, letters, telecom history and usage surveys; mixed for recorded conversation; thin for heritage-language family talk.
- **Languages needed:** French from the 17th century on, for civility literature; Luxembourgish and German for mixed-language exchange; Alsatian and Breton for broadcast talk; Kabyle and Darija for language-brokering studies; Creole for Martinican oral texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recorded conversations carry consent limits; studies of children as interpreters touch families' legal status; speech rights and limits belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose interpreting needs and family talk would otherwise join the language-broker comparison.
