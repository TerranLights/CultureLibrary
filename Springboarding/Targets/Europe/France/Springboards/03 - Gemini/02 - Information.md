## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alsace and Moselle:** check what readers moved to as German-language newspaper editions shrank.
- **Brittany and the west:** check how one dominant regional daily shapes local news choice.
- **Corsica:** check Corsican-language TV news against French-language bulletins on the island.
- **The Creuse and the rural Massif Central:** check broadband gaps and vanished local papers.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check whether viewers follow Belgian or French channels for news, by age.
- **Luxembourg:** check what Portuguese-Luxembourgers and cross-border workers read and watch.
- **Maghrebi-French households:** check satellite channels from the Maghreb against French news at home.
- **Kabyle-French listeners:** check Berber-language radio and television made in Paris as news channels.
- **Jewish communities:** check community radio as a news source and its reporting of antisemitic falsehoods.
- **The Chinese of Paris:** check Chinese-language weeklies and messaging-app groups.
- **Young and old:** check news avoidance and social-media news by age group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history, statistics and trust surveys; mixed for community media; thin for archives of small-community radio.
- **Languages needed:** 17th- and 18th-century French for the early press; German for the Alsatian editions; Portuguese and Luxembourgish for Luxembourg; Arabic and Kabyle for community broadcasting; Chinese for Paris weeklies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** official statistics carry no ethnic counts, so data on who reads or hears what by origin are scarce and disputed; intelligence files stay closed; data-protection and press law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose community press would otherwise sit with the diaspora-media checks.
