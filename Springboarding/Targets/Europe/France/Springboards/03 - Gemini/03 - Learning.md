## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brittany:** check memoirs of children who came to school with only Breton, against Diwan pupils' accounts.
- **Alsace:** check how parents describe choosing a bilingual school.
- **Corsica:** check how families add Corsican at home beside school lessons.
- **Rural Auvergne and the Massif Central:** check single-class schools and what children learned when they closed.
- **The Nord and Lorraine:** check trade schools tied to mines and mills and what replaced them.
- **Brussels and Walloon cities:** check who attends homework schools, by commune.
- **Luxembourg:** check results for pupils whose home language is Portuguese, French or another, under trilingual teaching.
- **Maghrebi-French and West African-French parents:** check their own account of helping with homework, beside teachers' accounts.
- **Families of the Chinese of Paris and Vietnamese-French families:** check children learning a trade in family shops and restaurants, in their own telling.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check how families weigh trades learned from parents against school; sources thin.
- **Deaf children:** check bilingual LSF classes against oral teaching.
- **Older adults:** check third-age course enrollment by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history, assessment and literacy; mixed for learning at home and at work; thin for Travelers and for heritage families' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Middle French for La Tour Landry; Breton, Occitan and Alsatian for immersion-school sources; Luxembourgish and German for test reports; Arabic and Kabyle for parent studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pupil-level data is restricted and attainment by origin is a disputed measure; memories of school punishment can be painful.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose homework help and after-school classes would otherwise be compared here.
