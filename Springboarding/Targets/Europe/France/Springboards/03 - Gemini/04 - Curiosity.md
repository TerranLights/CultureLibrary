# France: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Arabic; Armenian for genealogy groups. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris:** check memoirs of apartment-building life for how watching neighbors is described and resented.
- **Brittany:** check legends that punish looking or asking, in Breton-language collections.
- **Provence and the Alps:** check public nights at observatories and amateur clubs, by town.
- **The Massif Central:** check what local people and visitors each ask at volcano sites and museums.
- **Corsica:** check which questions newcomers and returning islanders say they are asked in villages.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check how children read explorer and reporter heroes in comic albums, against readers in France.
- **Luxembourg:** check science-center visitors by language group.
- **Maghrebi-French families:** check which questions elders count as *fuḍūl* (prying), in their own words.
- **West African-French associations:** check greetings that ask after family and news, and how they meet French small talk.
- **Jewish communities:** check study circles where questioning is the method, as participants describe them.
- **Armenian-French families:** check genealogy groups searching for origins lost in 1915.
- **Children and retirees:** check science-festival and museum visitors by age.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science popularization, museums and collecting; mixed for nosiness and etiquette; thin for heritage families' rules on asking.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-century French for Perrault and Fontenelle; Breton for tale collections; Luxembourgish for visitor studies; Arabic for the *fuḍūl* question; Armenian for genealogy groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human exhibitions, colonial collecting and ethnographic consent are painful topics; true crime touches victims' families; medical and genetic testing rules belong to Libra.
