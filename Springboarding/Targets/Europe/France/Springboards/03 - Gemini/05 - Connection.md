# France: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish, German, Portuguese; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Arabic, Soninke and Bambara for West African associations; Armenian. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bretons and Auvergnats of Paris:** check whether their friendly societies still link people to home, and what took their place.
- **Provence and the Var:** check whether village *cercles* survive as meeting places, beside newer associations.
- **The Nord and Wallonia:** check what replaced closed cafés in former mining towns as places to meet.
- **Alsace:** check village clubs (*Vereine*) against town associations, by members' age.
- **Corsicans on the mainland:** check island associations' help with housing and jobs, in members' accounts.
- **Luxembourg:** check cross-border workers' ties at work against ties where they live.
- **Pieds-Noirs and Harkis:** check reunions and associations as ways of keeping scattered families in touch.
- **Malian and Senegalese residents:** check how hostels and village associations connect new arrivals, in members' own accounts.
- **Portuguese-French families:** check parish and folk-dance associations as links among families in France.
- **Armenian-French communities:** check compatriotic unions organized by town of origin.
- **Older people living alone:** check isolation by region and town size.
- **Students and young adults:** check loneliness reports, and which ties they say they lack.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for loneliness surveys, sociability history and network studies; mixed for elite networks (studied mostly from outside); thin for Travelers' and newer communities' ties.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-century French and Latin for patronage letters and Mersenne; Soninke and Bambara for association records; Portuguese and Luxembourgish for Luxembourg; Armenian for compatriotic unions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Freemasonry and elite clubs attract conspiracy writing, to be read only as evidence of suspicion; heat-wave death data names no one but concerns families; nepotism rules belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose associations and family networks would otherwise sit with the hometown comparisons.
