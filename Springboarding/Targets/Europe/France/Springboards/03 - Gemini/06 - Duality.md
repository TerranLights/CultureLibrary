# France: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Alsatian and German; Luxembourgish; Occitan; Arabic; Antillean Creole where the overseas departments are needed. **Concept in one line:** living with two-ness: two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and the treatment of people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Alsace and Moselle:** check whether "between two countries" is told as loss or gain, by generation, in songs and memoirs.
- **Occitania and Brittany:** check whether speakers take up the *diglossia* term or describe the split in their own words.
- **The Catholic west against secular areas:** check whether paired public and private school networks still divide villages socially.
- **Brussels:** check how French-speaking residents describe life in a city with two official languages; Flanders only as comparison.
- **Wallonia:** check *belgitude* among readers and writers, beside the Walloon movement's own claims.
- **Luxembourg:** check whether residents call the country Germanic, Romance or both, by home language.
- **Strasbourg and the Rhine border:** check shopping, work and schooling across the river at Kehl.
- **Corsicans in Marseille and Paris:** check island and mainland selves in community associations.
- **Maghrebi-French dual nationals:** check their own media on the 2015-2016 nationality debate.
- **Pieds-Noirs:** check how two homelands appear in memoirs, and whether children born in France take them up.
- **Antilleans** (overseas, where needed): check lives split between an island and the metropole.
- **Families of mixed faith:** check which holidays of two traditions are kept, and how children describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, philosophy, electoral geography and language surveys; mixed for everyday double lives; thin for mixed-faith families and smaller binational groups.
- **Languages needed:** Old French for Renart; Latin for Descartes's first edition; German and Alsatian for Schickele and the song; Luxembourgish for Fehlen; Arabic for heritage memoirs; Creole for island sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** loyalty suspicion of binationals and colonial memory are charged; private lives of public figures involve living people; citizenship law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose two-country lives would otherwise be compared here.
