# France: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish and German; Breton, Occitan, Corsican; Darija and Kabyle; Portuguese. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and the Île-de-France hospices:** check foundling admissions against rural placement records.
- **The Massif Central and the Limousin:** check emptied hamlets and how remaining residents speak of them.
- **Corsica's interior:** check villages full only in summer and the elders who stay year-round.
- **Lorraine's steel valleys:** check Florange and Gandrange workers' memoirs.
- **The Charleroi and Liège basins:** check the language of "abandoned" districts in Walloon press.
- **The Ubaye valley:** check letters between emigrants and wives left behind.
- **Luxembourg:** check emigrant families' letters home, held in national and parish archives.
- **Harki families:** check camp-era children's accounts of being left by the state.
- **The Belgian *métis*:** check survivors' searches for mothers in Central Africa.
- **Portuguese-French families:** check children left with grandparents in Portugal while parents worked in France.
- **Brittany:** check elders left in villages by children who moved to Paris after 1950.
- **People born *sous X*:** check their associations' accounts against CNAOP figures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundling history and wards; mixed for absent fathers and left-behind families; thin for abandoned elders before the 1990s.
- **Languages needed:** 17th- to 19th-century French for hospice registers; Luxembourgish and German for emigration files; Portuguese for left-behind children; Kabyle and Darija for Harki testimony; Lingala and French for *métis* accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foundling and ward files and anonymous-birth records are closed or restricted; Harki and *métis* histories are open wounds; the law on anonymous birth and adoption belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose children raised apart from migrant parents would otherwise join the Portuguese check.
