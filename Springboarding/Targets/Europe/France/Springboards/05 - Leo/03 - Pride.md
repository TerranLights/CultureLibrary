# France: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Picard, Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Walloon; Arabic, Kabyle. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brittany:** check the BZH sticker and the *Gwenn ha Du* for who displays them and since when.
- **The Nord and Picardy:** check *ch'ti* self-naming in songs and festivals before and after 2008.
- **Alsace:** check the red-and-white *Rot un Wiss* flag against the regional merger of 2016.
- **Occitania and the Southwest:** check *Se canto* sung at rugby grounds as a show of pride.
- **Corsica:** check the Moor's head flag on houses, cars and goods.
- **Lorraine:** check steel and mining pride in Longwy and the Fensch valley after closures.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check which emblems residents choose: Walloon, Belgian or city.
- **Luxembourg:** check whether Portuguese and cross-border residents join the *Nationalfeierdag*.
- **Pieds-Noirs:** check how pride in the old towns of Algeria is shown at gatherings and in bulletins.
- **Maghrebi-French:** check flags of origin shown at football victories, and how the press reports it.
- **West African-French and Antilleans:** check *négritude* and later *créolité* claims in schools and associations.
- **Armenian-French:** check pride in the diaspora's figures, as shown at the Lyon area's commemorations.
- **Portuguese-French:** check pride in builders' work and in the 2016 European title.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emblems, survey items and the history of nationalism; mixed for pride in work; thin for personal pride in heritage families' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for cathedral inscriptions; 17th-century French for Bossuet; Breton and Alsatian for flag movements; Luxembourgish for national-day speeches; Armenian and Portuguese for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional flags are tied to autonomist politics, some once armed; foreign flags shown in French streets set off heated reporting; national pride slides into nationalism, so read such sources as part of an argument.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose street celebrations and home-country flags would otherwise join the flags-of-origin check.
