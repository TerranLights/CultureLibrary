# France: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Occitan, Breton, Corsican; Romani varieties; Darija; Antillean Creole. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Limousin and Occitania:** check whether troubadour *jòi* is taught or sung in Occitan schools today.
- **Brittany:** check Lorient's parade and village *pardons* as two kinds of gladness.
- **Provence and the Midi:** check summer *fêtes votives* as the year's high point, in villagers' words.
- **The Nord:** check *ducasse* and fanfares as moments of joy in mining towns, from local memoirs.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check Christmas markets for gladness against commerce, in residents' accounts.
- **Corsica:** check sung polyphony at village feasts as shared joy, from singers' own descriptions.
- **Brussels:** check *Meyboom* groups against newer residents' joining in.
- **Luxembourg:** check *Buergbrennen* across villages and residents of foreign birth.
- **Maghrebi-French families:** check *zgharīt* and wedding joy, and the end of Ramadan, as families describe them.
- **Antilleans:** check carnival weeks and *zouk* dances as the measure of a good year.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check *Vie et Lumière* gatherings in their own accounts.
- **Portuguese-French:** check summer returns to home villages as the year's joy.
- **Vietnamese-French and Chinese of Paris:** check the Lunar New Year as family gladness, apart from the street parade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, music and life-satisfaction surveys; mixed for everyday gladness (few studies outside surveys); thin for heritage communities' family feasts.
- **Languages needed:** Old Occitan for troubadour lyric; Middle French for Charles d'Orléans; Breton and Corsican for festival songs; Romani varieties for worship gatherings; Darija and Creole for wedding and carnival songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** happiness rankings invite talk of a national temperament, to be avoided; Travelers' worship gatherings face local hostility, so use their own sources; noise and public-order rules for festivals belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose family feasts and public festivals would otherwise join the community-feasts checks.
