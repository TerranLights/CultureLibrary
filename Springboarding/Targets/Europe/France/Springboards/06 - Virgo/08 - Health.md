# France: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Alsatian; Maghrebi Arabic and Kabyle; Antillean Creole; Chinese and Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Nord and Hainaut:** check how residents explain their shorter lives against national figures.
- **Brittany:** check visits to healing fountains and saints at *pardons*.
- **Rural Massif Central:** check how people cope where doctors are few.
- **Marseille's northern and southern districts:** check health gaps inside one city.
- **Wallonia against Brussels:** check health inequalities in sickness-fund data.
- **Luxembourg's cross-border workers:** check where they seek care, in Luxembourg or at home, against residents.
- **Muslims of Maghrebi heritage:** check when *ruqya* is sought alongside a doctor.
- **West African-French families:** check use of ethnopsychiatric consultations and of healers in Paris.
- **Antilleans in Île-de-France:** check remedies brought from the islands.
- **Chinese and Vietnamese of Paris:** check use of Chinese medicine practitioners in the 13th arrondissement.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check access to care on halting sites; sources are thin.
- **Men:** check doctor avoidance by age and job.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, public-health history and epidemics; mixed for folk and religious healing; thin for health among Travelers and recent arrivals.
- **Languages needed:** 16th-century French for Paré; Breton for healing-saint hymns; Arabic for *ruqya* manuals; Creole for remedy names; Wolof and Bambara for ethnopsychiatric case work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health records are confidential; some exorcism and *ruqya* cases ended in harm or death; ethnopsychiatry is sharply criticized by some anthropologists; HIV still carries stigma; assisted dying is Scorpio's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose healing systems would otherwise join the Chinese-medicine check.
