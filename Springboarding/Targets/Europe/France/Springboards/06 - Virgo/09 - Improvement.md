# France: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Walloon, Breton, Occitan; Portuguese; Arabic and Judeo-Arabic. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former tenants of demolished Paris blocks (*îlots insalubres*, 1940s-1970s):** check their accounts of being moved out for improvement.
- **Breton farm families:** check memories of field merging and lost hedges.
- **Savoie (Tignes):** check how the drowned village is remembered by families who left it.
- **Lorraine's steel towns:** check how "reconversion" schemes are judged by those they were meant for.
- **Charleroi against Liège:** check how each city judges regional recovery plans.
- **Luxembourg:** check who lives in and who only works at Belval.
- **Portuguese-French families:** check how a child's diploma or a trade certificate is spoken of as the family's step up.
- **First-generation graduates of Maghrebi heritage:** check how they tell class-crossing stories, and to whom.
- **Harkis and their children:** check what the camps' "integration" programs meant to those placed in them.
- **West African-French communities:** check the trade in skin-lightening products and campaigns against it, in community media.
- **Jewish families from North Africa:** check how former pupils of the Alliance israélite universelle schools tell their "regeneration" schooling.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check settlement programs offered as improvement, in their own words; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban renewal, mobility tables and colonial history; mixed for self-help reading; thin for what home DIY means to those who do it.
- **Languages needed:** late-18th-century French and English for Condorcet and Young; Arabic and Judeo-Arabic for Alliance pupils' letters; Portuguese for family accounts of getting on; Breton for farm memories; Luxembourgish for Belval residents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the civilizing mission and assimilation did harm and must be told first by those it was done to; skin lightening touches colorism; demolitions displaced poor and immigrant tenants; class-crossing memoirs are used in political fights.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose accounts of rising through schooling would otherwise join the class-crossing check.
