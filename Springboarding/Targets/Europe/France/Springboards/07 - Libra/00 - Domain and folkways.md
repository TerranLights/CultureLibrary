## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The northern customary lands against the southern written-law lands (*pays de coutumes*, *pays de droit écrit*):** check dowry and shared-property contracts in notarial records on both sides of the old line.
- **Alsace and the Moselle:** check religious weddings and courts under local law beside the general French rules.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check where Belgium's reformed Civil Code books and *cohabitation légale* part from French texts.
- **Luxembourg:** check its 2004 partnership law and the 2023 constitution on equality of the sexes.
- **Brittany:** check wedding-feast songs and the *gwerz* on ill-matched marriages, in Breton collections.
- **Occitania and Provence:** check wedding songs and dowry contracts in the Occitan archives of Toulouse and Montpellier.
- **Maghrebi-French couples:** check the civil ceremony against the religious one and the consular conventions with Morocco and Algeria.
- **West African-French families:** check how marriages made abroad are copied into French records by the central civil-status service in Nantes.
- **Jewish communities:** check how the civil-first rule and the *get* meet.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check betrothal and wedding customs in their own accounts and in evangelical missions; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, marriage and divorce; mixed for how daily bargains are sealed; thin for Travelers.
- **Territory in this sign:** Libra's sheets take metropolitan France, Wallonia, Brussels and Luxembourg as the core while the author decides the final map; Flanders, the French Basque Country and Northern Catalonia serve as comparison, and the overseas departments are named only where a sheet needs them. Law is cited per land.
- **Languages needed:** Old French with Picard features for Beaumanoir; Middle French for the customs; Dutch for the Belgian gazette's other half (comparison); Hebrew for rabbinical rulings; Arabic for consular conventions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced marriage and polygamy are crimes or legal bars, never traits of a community; divorce and custody files are closed; the Évian promise is grieved differently on each side.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose weddings abroad and their registration would otherwise join the Nantes check.
