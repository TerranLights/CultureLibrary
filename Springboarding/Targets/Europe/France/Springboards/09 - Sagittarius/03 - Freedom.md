# France: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish, German; Latin (medieval chronicles); Breton, Corsican, Walloon; Antillean Creole; Kabyle; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Picardy (Laon and the northern towns):** check how medieval communes are remembered locally, in civic festivals and town histories, as freedom stories.
- **Brussels and Wallonia:** check how 1830 is taught and celebrated in each, and the place of the Liège volunteers.
- **Luxembourg:** check which language carries which sense of freedom in the trilingual press, and how independence is spoken of.
- **Corsica:** check whether songs and speeches since the 1970s set a people's freedom against a person's.
- **Brittany:** check whether Breton-language campaigners frame their cause as freedom or as rights.
- **Antilleans and the overseas departments:** check abolition-day rites and *mawonaj* memory, at home and in Île-de-France.
- **Vietnamese-French and Cambodian-French:** check refugee memoirs for what freedom meant on arrival.
- **Kabyle-French:** check associations and song for freedom of language and belief.
- **Jewish communities:** check how emancipation in the Revolution is remembered in synagogue and community history.
- **Armenian-French:** check survivors' memoirs for France as a place of refuge and of freedom after 1915.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the vocabulary of *liberté* is deeply documented in philosophy, art and political history; how ordinary people weigh freedom day to day is mostly in polls and protest studies; Luxembourg has little written on the concept itself.
- **Languages needed:** medieval Latin for Guibert; 19th-century French for Constant; Kabyle for song lyrics; Creole for abolition songs; Vietnamese for refugee memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the health-pass protests and the security debate divide families and parties; abolition memory touches descendants of the enslaved and of slaveholders; the legal list of liberties belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** refugee and exile accounts from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
