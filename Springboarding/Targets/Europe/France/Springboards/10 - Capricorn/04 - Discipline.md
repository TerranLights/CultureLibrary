# France: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Breton, Corsican, Occitan; Maghrebi Arabic; Hebrew; Armenian; Romani (Manouche). **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Burgundy:** check how monastic hours are kept today at Cîteaux, and how visitors at Taizé take on a day's rule.
- **Brittany:** check Belle-Île inmates' files against islanders' memory of the 1934 revolt.
- **Alsace and the Moselle:** check school memoirs from 1871-1918 for German classroom discipline set against French schooling after 1918.
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check exclusion and detention figures by school district (Paris, Créteil, Versailles).
- **Corsica:** check the Foreign Legion parachute regiment at Calvi as a discipline institution within island life.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check memoirs of Catholic boarding schools (*pensionnats*) in Walloon towns.
- **Luxembourg:** check how its volunteer army recruits and trains young residents of many nationalities.
- **Maghrebi-French and West African-French Muslims:** check fasting reports by age and how schools and firms adjust to them.
- **Jewish communities:** check how observance of Shabbat and kashrut is taught to children as self-rule, in Strasbourg and Paris day schools.
- **Armenian-French:** check Saturday schools and dance troupes as training regimes, in community bulletins.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check how Manouche guitar is taught and practiced in families, in musicians' interviews; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions (schools, armies, penal colonies); mixed for discipline at home; thin for religious self-discipline as lived.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the Cluny customs; Old French for the *Bataille*; Dutch for half of the Brussels academy's records; Arabic for fasting guides.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** penal-colony files name minors, and a cadet's death is still grieved by his family; physical punishment at home is a charged debate.
- **Held for review:** fasts and other religious self-disciplines kept by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, left out of the fasting check.
