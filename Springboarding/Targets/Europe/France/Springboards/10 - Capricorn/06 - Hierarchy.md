# France: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French; Luxembourgish; Corsican, Walloon, Occitan; Antillean Creole; Maghrebi Arabic; Soninke; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check how the western and eastern parts of the region rank each other, in speech and in housing data.
- **Aquitaine:** check how the 1855 ranking shapes the standing of estate owners, managers and vineyard workers in the Médoc.
- **Brittany:** check the place of minor noble families in parish life, in local histories.
- **Alsace:** check the textile dynasties of Mulhouse and the rank they held in town life.
- **The Nord:** check Roubaix and Tourcoing as two-tier towns of employer families and workers' districts.
- **Corsica:** check how *i sgiò* and shepherds appear in village memoirs.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check how French as a marker of rank in old Brussels society is remembered (Flanders as comparison only).
- **Luxembourg:** check society-page coverage of the court and old families against the new financial elite.
- **West African-French:** check whether inherited status groups (nobles, craftsmen, descendants of slaves) still affect association offices, in West African researchers' own work.
- **Vietnamese-French:** check how pre-1975 elites and later refugees place each other in community life.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check how Manouches, Gitans and Yéniches describe one another; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for occupations, segregation and elites; mixed for noble and club life, which is semi-private; thin for ranks inside migrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Adalbéron; Martinican Creole for responses to the documentary; Soninke for association records; Vietnamese for community bulletins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inherited status among West African-French families touches descent from slavery and must be handled with the community's own scholars; color words in the Antilles carry pain.
- **Held for review:** any ranking within or about South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, excluded from the comparison list.
