# France: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian, Walloon; Maghrebi Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law only; what codes, statutes and courts say as written is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brittany and Normandy coasts:** check wrecking legends against the court records Cabantous used.
- **The Alps and the Lyon area:** check how Mandrin is kept alive in Savoy and Dauphiné festivals and songs.
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check fare-evasion surveys and fines on the Paris transport network as everyday rule-bending.
- **The Nord and the Belgian border:** check tobacco and fuel runs across the border as a tolerated habit, in customs reports and local press.
- **Corsica:** check the *paillotes* affair (1999), when state agents burned an illegal beach restaurant, as a case of law upheld by lawless means.
- **Alsace and the Moselle:** check how residents defend their local rules as part of who they are (the rules themselves: Libra).
- **Luxembourg:** check Luxembourgish-language commentary on tax planning that drew foreign criticism.
- **Maghrebi-French:** check how *ḥogra* is used in rap and testimony to name unequal treatment by police and courts.
- **West African-French:** check the strikers' own statements on work allowed but residence refused.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check how Travelers speak of eviction orders when promised halting sites are missing; sources are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trust in justice, scandals and legal sociology; mixed for tolerated evasion; thin for settlement outside the courts.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-century French for the Mandrin ballads; Dutch for the full Belgian barometer; Arabic for any mediation records; Luxembourgish for local commentary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims about religious tribunals are often unfounded and inflammatory, so handle with evidence first; undocumented people's names must not be exposed; corruption cases name living people.
