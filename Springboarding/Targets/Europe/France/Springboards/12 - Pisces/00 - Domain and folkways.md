# France: The domain: the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Alsatian, Corsican, Walloon; Maghrebi Arabic and Kabyle; Romani (Manouche). **Concept in one line:** how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape: the unconscious, madness and its institutions, sleep and dreams, spirits and the dead, transcendence outside religion, and the magic ways (folk healing, charms, divination, witchcraft).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check where people in distress turn (hospital, therapist, psychic) by district and income.
- **Brittany:** check whether unwitchers and burn-charmers still work beside hospitals, and who sends patients to them.
- **The Nord and Picardy:** check mining-basin spirit lore against the farm country of the Somme.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check Lutheran, Catholic and Jewish ways of speaking of the dead in one region.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check Ardennes *nuton* legends against Brussels's psychoanalytic scene.
- **Luxembourg:** check how white-lady legends are now told to schoolchildren and tourists.
- **Corsica:** check which villages still tell of dream-hunters and death-omens, and who records them now.
- **Provence and Occitania:** check *masca* lore in the Alps of Provence against Cévennes Protestant accounts of the unseen.
- **Maghrebi-French:** check when families turn to a *raqi* rather than a psychiatrist, and how clinicians answer.
- **West African-French:** check the networks of healers who serve Malian and Senegalese households in Paris and Montreuil.
- **Antilleans:** check how distress is named in Creole among Antilleans of Île-de-France (overseas departments named because the concept needs them).
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check customs around the belongings of the dead; sources are few, so start with families' own writing.

## 4. Flags
- **Ground covered:** Pisces keeps to metropolitan France with Corsica, Wallonia, Brussels and Luxembourg while the post-war frontier waits on the author; Flanders, Northern Catalonia and the French Basque Country serve only as foils, and the overseas departments come in only where a concept calls for them.
- **Data:** asylum history and spirit folklore are rich; present-day healers leave little in print; distress statistics are smaller for Luxembourg.
- **Languages needed:** Breton for death lore; Walloon for witch tales; Maghrebi Arabic for healing recitations; 17th-century French for trial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asylum patients' files stay closed for long periods; clients of unwitchers and *marabouts* are often in crisis, so fieldwork needs consent.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose healing, shrine and spirit customs would otherwise join the healer comparisons.
