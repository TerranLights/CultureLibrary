# France: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Arabic; Hebrew (for Jewish religious terms). **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check who gives to people begging in the Métro, and how givers decide (observational studies, interviews).
- **The Nord and the Pas-de-Calais coast:** check which townspeople bring food and showers to exiles waiting to cross, and which protest it.
- **The Lyon area:** check whether Catholic and secular aid networks serve the same poor or different ones.
- **Alsace:** check the deaconess houses of Strasbourg against Catholic care orders as two traditions of mercy.
- **Brittany:** check how fishing ports collect for the families of crews lost at sea.
- **Corsica:** check what neighbors bring to a bereaved household, and who is expected to bring it.
- **Wallonia:** check how food aid is organized in the former mining towns of Hainaut.
- **Luxembourg:** check whether residents and cross-border workers give through Caritas, the Red Cross or neither.
- **Maghrebi-French:** check whether *zakât* is spent locally or sent to home villages, and who decides.
- **Jewish communities:** check how *bikour ḥolim* groups in Paris and Strasbourg organize visits to the sick.
- **Vietnamese-French:** check how pagoda lay groups helped the boat-people arrivals of 1979-1980.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check the work of the Catholic chaplaincy to Travelers; sources are thin, so start with its newsletters.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** giving and food-aid figures are rich; everyday mercy to beggars and offenders lives in ethnography and fiction; Luxembourg data is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Old French for the saint's life; Hebrew and Arabic for the religious words of mercy and alms; Breton and Corsican for village customs of help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pictures of people receiving aid can shame them, so look for consent; mercy to offenders touches victims' families; the treatment of offenders by courts is for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose temple kitchens and charity drives would otherwise join the faith-alms checks.
