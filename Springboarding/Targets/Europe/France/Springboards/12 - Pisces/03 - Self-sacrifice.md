# France: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Alsatian; Arabic; Portuguese (for Portuguese-French families). **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check what grown children of migrant parents say they owe for what was given up for them.
- **Brittany:** check how fishing towns remember men lost at sea, and what widows were expected to give up.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check how families of men forced into German service speak of sacrifice that was not chosen.
- **The Massif Central:** check the memory of emigrants who left for Paris (cafés, coal) to send money home.
- **Provence and the Midi:** check what members of the penitent confraternities of Nice say their penance gives others.
- **Corsica:** check how colonial and military service is remembered in village memorials and family stories.
- **Wallonia:** check what retired miners and their wives say they gave up, as told in oral-history projects.
- **Luxembourg:** check whether Portuguese-Luxembourgish families tell the same story of parental sacrifice as Portuguese-French ones.
- **Harkis:** check how their descendants weigh their fathers' service against what followed.
- **West African-French:** check the memory of the *tirailleurs* among their grandchildren in Paris and Marseille.
- **Jewish communities:** check how the Resistance and the rescue of children are told as sacrifice in community memorials.
- **Muslims of France:** check how the Feast of the Sacrifice is kept in cities, and what families say it teaches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** memorials and war dead are rich; the private sacrifices of mothers, eldest daughters and carers are thinner.
- **Languages needed:** Greek or Latin for the Lyon letter; Portuguese for migrant families' own accounts; Luxembourgish for local memorial speeches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deaths of hostages, gendarmes and monks touch living kin; hunger strikes can end in harm; colonial soldiers' sacrifice is tied to unpaid pensions and grievance.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose vows and fasts would otherwise join the penitent and Lenten comparisons.
