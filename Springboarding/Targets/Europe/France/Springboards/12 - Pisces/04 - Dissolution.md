# France: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage); Luxembourgish; Breton, Corsican, Occitan, Alsatian; Middle French and Latin for medieval mystics; Romani. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger (a person, a crowd, nature, the divine), and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check who goes to all-night clubs and why, and how they speak of "losing themselves" there.
- **Brittany:** check the *fest-noz* as a dance in which a line of dancers moves as one, and what dancers say of it.
- **Corsica:** check who now learns *paghjella* and whether the merging of voices is described in religious or secular words.
- **Normandy:** check how the former Moulinex towns describe the years after the plants closed.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check how families speak of a parent with dementia who returns to Alsatian or Lorraine Franconian and is no longer understood.
- **Burgundy:** check the Taizé night prayers as a practice of silence and song shared by many young people at once.
- **The Massif Central:** check hamlets emptied by departure and how their last residents tell the end.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check the mystic women of the Liège region against modern Walloon writing on industrial towns coming apart.
- **Luxembourg:** check whether families who speak four languages describe a loss of self in migration, and in which language.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check what evangelical converts say about trance and the Spirit at large gatherings; written sources are few.
- **Antilleans:** check *veillées* and drum gatherings (*gwoka*) in Île-de-France as places of losing oneself in the group (overseas departments named because the concept needs them).
- **Pieds-Noirs:** check whether the 1962 exodus is told as the dissolution of a whole society, and by whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mystical texts, crowd theory and language-shift data are rich; first-person accounts of trance, raves and dementia are scattered and need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Middle French and Latin for Porete and the Liège lives; Breton and Corsican for song and language loss; Creole for *gwoka* gatherings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families of people in groups called cults are in pain, and the "cult" label is disputed by the groups themselves; drug-use accounts can expose people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose ecstatic devotion and trance practices would otherwise join the Traveler and Antillean comparisons.
