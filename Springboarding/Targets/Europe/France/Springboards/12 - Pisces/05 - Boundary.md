# France: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check how flat dwellers handle shared walls, stairwells and courtyards, and when a neighbor is felt to intrude.
- **Normandy and Brittany:** check hedged farm country against open-field Beauce for how neighbors mark and talk about property lines.
- **The Nord and Wallonia:** check what border-town families remember of crossing for goods, work and dances across the customs line.
- **Alsace:** check daily crossings to Germany and Switzerland, and whether residents speak of the border as a line or a seam.
- **The Lyon area and the Alps:** check how Alpine villages divided summer pastures and marked them.
- **Provence and the Midi:** check walled villas and gated estates on the coast against old village squares open to all.
- **Corsica:** check how dry-stone walls in the hills are read as marks of whose land begins where.
- **Brussels:** check what residents of the bilingual capital say about the language line around the city (Flanders named as comparison only).
- **Luxembourg:** check how residents and the many daily cross-border commuters describe the country's edges.
- **Maghrebi-French:** check how families speak of the *ḥurma* of the home and who may enter which rooms.
- **Jewish communities:** check the *érouv* (eruv) boundaries in Paris and Strasbourg and how they are drawn and kept.
- **Chinese of Paris:** check how shopkeepers in Belleville and the 13th arrondissement separate shop, home and street.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** private-life history and housing studies are rich; emotional boundaries in today's talk live mostly in self-help and online.
- **Languages needed:** Gascon and Latin for sanctuary charters; Luxembourgish for border-village accounts; Hebrew for eruv practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2018 consent debate still divides; food rules in school canteens are a live political issue; property quarrels between neighbors belong in law to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose household thresholds and purity rules would otherwise sit beside the *ḥurma* and eruv checks.
