# France: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** France, region Europe, with French-speaking Belgium (Wallonia, Brussels) and Luxembourg. **Language(s) for searching:** French (with Belgian and Luxembourg usage, and slang: *argot*, *verlan*); Luxembourgish; Corsican; Maghrebi Arabic loans in street French; Romani loans. **Concept in one line** (named by the author: what it means for something to be a crime): what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Here wrongdoing is a moral and social category; law is researched in Libra and Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Paris and Île-de-France:** check how *banlieue* residents and city-center residents name "real" crime in the same surveys.
- **The Nord and Picardy:** check how former mining towns speak of drug selling as a job or as betrayal of the neighborhood.
- **Normandy:** check rural sayings and press on poaching and theft from farms, and how harshly they are judged.
- **Alsace and Lorraine:** check cross-border smuggling memories as remembered mischief or as wrongdoing.
- **The Lyon area:** check the Lacassagne heritage in local memory of famous cases.
- **Provence:** check how Marseille residents speak of the *milieu* of the past against today's drug violence.
- **Corsica:** check the *bandit d'honneur* in village memory, and whether residents separate it from organized crime today.
- **Wallonia and Brussels:** check how the 1980s Brabant killings are remembered as a national trauma, beside the 1996 march.
- **Luxembourg:** check what residents count as serious wrongs in a small country where offenders and victims may know each other.
- **Maghrebi-French and West African-French:** check how young people describe being seen as suspects, in their own words.
- **Manouche and other Travelers:** check how families answer theft accusations, and where their own writing exists.
- **Pieds-Noirs and Harkis:** check how wartime wrongs in Algeria are named, by whom, and whether "crime" is the word.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** crime news, fiction and criminology history are rich; how ordinary people rank wrongs is thinner.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-century French for Cartouche chapbooks; *argot* and *verlan* for inside accounts; Luxembourgish and German for Grand Duchy crime fiction.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child victims of the 1990s Belgian cases are still remembered by living families; prisoners' letters need consent; naming a district or a people as "criminal" stigmatizes. Law, courts and penalties are for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose accounts of being suspected or targeted would otherwise join the suspect-in-their-own-words checks.
