## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** coming-of-age rites, nudity norms and church ties split here; post-1990 life stories add a second break.
- **The Low German north coast:** naming and self-introduction where Low German is still spoken.
- **North Frisians and Saterland Frisians:** island naming and a small-language identity.
- **Danish minority, Southern Schleswig:** a self held across two languages and school systems.
- **Sorbs, Upper and Lower Lusatia:** dress and rites differ by confession and district.
- **Catholic and Protestant West (Rhineland, Münsterland, Hesse, Baden, Württemberg):** saints' names and confirmation customs.
- **Resettlers from the former USSR:** name changes on arrival.
- **Turkish- and Kurdish-heritage Germans, Alevis separate:** naming between languages and faiths.
- **German Sinti, Roma and Yeniche:** names a community may keep inward.
- **Afro-Germans; Vietnamese Germans in the east:** self-naming movements; contract-worker histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for naming, census forms, diaries, youth movements; mixed for the body; thin for the smaller minorities' own sources.
- **Languages needed:** German; Low German, Frisian, Sorbian, Danish for minority archives; Turkish, Russian, Arabic for heritage communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era ancestry and folklore sources must be read critically, never as identity data; Sinti and Roma registration records carry persecution history; ancestry data may not be collected at all.
- **Scope:** Germany's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author. These sheets are written for the core German lands; Bavarian material appears only as a neighboring comparison (Austro-Bavaria is a separate target).
- **Held for review:** none.
