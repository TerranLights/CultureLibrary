## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** crèche-based upbringing and full-time work for mothers in the GDR against the western family model; check how each shaped later ideas of who decides.
- **The 1968 generation and their children:** the anti-authoritarian turn in parenting, and its reversal.
- **Farm families (Lower Saxony, Westphalia, Württemberg):** who chooses a career when a farm needs an heir.
- **Families of Turkish, Kurdish, Arab and Balkan origin:** the family's part in spouse and career choice, which varies by origin, generation and piety; avoid generalizing.
- **Free-church Germans from Russia (Baptists, Mennonites):** conflicts between community rules and state schooling.
- **Disabled adults in sheltered workshops against those on personal budgets:** two ways of being looked after.
- **Danish and Sorbian school communities:** collective self-rule in education.
- **Urban alternative scenes (Berlin-Kreuzberg, Leipzig-Connewitz, Hamburg's Schanze):** autonomy as a neighborhood project.
- **Older people at home against those in care homes:** control over daily routine.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for philosophy, youth, aging and work; mixed for family decision-making among heritage communities; thin for regional detail.
- **Languages needed:** German; Danish and Sorbian for minority schools; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for family studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abortion, assisted dying, compulsory schooling and data protection are matters of law (Libra for the letter, Capricorn for the spirit); here only the lived practice. Family-control topics need care not to stereotype any community.
