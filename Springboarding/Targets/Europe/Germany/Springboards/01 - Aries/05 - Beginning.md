*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Frisians and Low German coast people:** season-opening fires and door-to-door New Year customs.
- **Saxony and Thuringia:** the first-school-day cone and its local names.
- **East Germans after 1989:** biographies with a forced new start, by age at the turn.
- **The 1945 generation and expellee arrivals:** starting from rubble or from a camp.
- **Christian, unaffiliated and secular families:** baptism against the GDR's socialist name-giving ceremony and its traces.
- **Catholic Sorbs:** spring riding as a renewal rite.
- **Kurds and Alevis (Newroz), Vietnamese (Tết), Russian-speaking resettlers (Novy god), Jews (Rosh Hashanah):** where the year begins.
- **Refugees since 2015:** first years in a new country.
- **Founders and people who failed:** how a second start is received.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for customs and postwar history; mixed for starting over after failure.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older printed forms; Low German, Frisian and Sorbian for customs; Kurdish, Vietnamese and Russian for heritage calendars.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the folk-belief encyclopedia and the Arminius story were bent to Nazi uses; read critically. "Zero hour" is a contested label.
- **Related signs:** rebirth (Scorpio), tradition (Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Grimm's *Deutsche Sagen* for founding legends of towns in the north, the Rhineland and Thuringia.

3. Search library catalogs for "Schultüte Geschichte" and "Richtfest Brauch".
4. Read Museum Friedland's exhibition guide.
5. Find the latest DAK resolutions survey and its regional tables.
