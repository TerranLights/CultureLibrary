## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stuttgart and Württemberg:** prosperous citizens' protest anger around a rail project.
- **Dresden and Saxony:** grievance movements and their counter-protests, with very different readings of the same anger.
- **East and West:** post-1990 grievance should be measured, not assumed.
- **Ruhr football towns:** rivalry as a weekly outlet.
- **Farmers (tractor protests) against city climate activists:** two collective angers that met head-on.
- **Men and women:** how anger is allowed and judged; domestic violence data.
- **Young men in youth-justice programs:** anger as a target of treatment.
- **Turkish-, Kurdish- and Arab-heritage communities:** anger at racism, and conflicts carried over from the homelands.
- **The 1968 generation and today's young activists:** protest anger across fifty years.
- **Online and offline:** anger that lives mainly in comment sections.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest, policing, history of emotions and psychology; mixed for anger in families and among minorities.
- **Languages needed:** German, including medieval forms; Turkish, Kurdish and Arabic for heritage communities.
