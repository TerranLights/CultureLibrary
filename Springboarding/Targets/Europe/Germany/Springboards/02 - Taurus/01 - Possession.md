# Germany: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German and Low German; Sorbian, Danish and Frisian for the recognized minorities; Turkish, Russian, Polish and Arabic for large migrant communities. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Swabian and Baden villages:** partible inheritance meant belongings were listed and divided often; a different archive trail from single-heir areas.
- **Westphalian and Lower Saxon farm households:** dowry chests and the farm as a unit; compare with the southwest.
- **East Germans born before 1975:** shortage-era keeping, swapping and mending, then the 1990s flood of goods.
- **Expellee families (Silesian, East Prussian, Pomeranian, Danube Swabian descent):** belongings lost or carried; heirlooms as the only link to a place.
- **Turkish-German families of the recruitment era:** suitcases, goods taken home on summer trips, return plans.
- **Russian-German resettlers (*Spätaussiedler*):** a second move with few things, from the late 1980s on.
- **Sorbs and North Frisians:** heirloom dress and objects tied to minority identity.
- **Urban sharing milieus (Berlin, Leipzig, Hamburg, Cologne):** where giving away and lending are most organized.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, for its own household-inventory and heirloom traditions. **Diaspora comparison only:** German-American families' heirloom traditions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for archives and material culture; mixed for present-day attitudes to belongings, which surveys rarely ask about directly.
- **Languages needed:** German, including Kurrent script for inventories; Low German; minority and migrant languages for family-object stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** property looted from Jews and others in 1933-1945, now in homes and museums (provenance research); expellees' lost goods remain politically charged; hoarding is stigmatized; probate files may carry privacy limits.
- **Held for review:** none.
