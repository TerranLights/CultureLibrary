# Germany: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Old and Middle High German for early tenure terms); Low German; Upper and Lower Sorbian; Danish; Turkish, Russian, Polish, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Northwest single-heir farm regions (Westphalia, Lower Saxony, Schleswig-Holstein):** the farm held whole across generations.
- **Southwest partible-inheritance regions (Württemberg, Baden, parts of Hesse and the Palatinate):** small, divided plots; different land-holding lore.
- **The former GDR:** land reform, collectives, restitution claims and Treuhand sales; how ownership was learned or relearned after 1990.
- **Berlin and Hamburg:** cities where renting and tenant movements are central; compare with small towns and villages.
- **Lusatian Sorbs:** land and villages lost to mining; a minority's tie to place.
- **North Frisians and Danes in Schleswig:** reclaimed land (*Köge*) and minority institutions holding property.
- **Jewish families and heirs:** property seized in 1933-1945 and restitution afterward.
- **Turkish-German families:** owning in Germany against owning in Turkey; *tapu* and houses built "back home".
- **Russian-German resettlers:** arrival from the late 1980s and the path to owning afterward, in towns of Westphalia and Lower Saxony (to be checked).
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, whose farm-tenure history is studied on its own. **Diaspora comparison only:** Amish and Hutterite communal or church-guided ownership in North America.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; statistics, GDR records and restitution files are extensive.
- **Languages needed:** German, including Kurrent for older deeds; Sorbian for Lusatian village sources; Turkish for family property abroad.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era seizures; GDR expropriations and restitution claims; mining relocations; heirs' privacy in land registers. Property law goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here.
- **Held for review:** none.
