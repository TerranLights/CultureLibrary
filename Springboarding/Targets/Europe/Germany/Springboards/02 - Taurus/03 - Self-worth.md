## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West Germans:** worth after 1990; pay and leadership-position gaps to measure; change across generations.
- **Ruhr and Saarland mining and steel communities:** worth from heavy labor and its end.
- **The educated middle class (*Bildungsbürgertum*) against skilled workers:** titles and schooling against craft pride (*Meister*).
- **Protestant north and Pietist Württemberg against Catholic Rhineland and Westphalia:** grace, work and worth.
- **People on basic income support:** stigma under *Hartz IV* and its successor.
- **Turkish-, Arab- and Kurdish-heritage young men:** respect, honor, discrimination.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** status lost in migration, degrees not recognized.
- **German Sinti and Roma; Sorbs:** minority dignity after persecution or contempt.
- **Women in East and West:** paid work and worth under two different postwar systems.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, for how regional pride and standing are framed next door. **Diaspora comparison only:** German-heritage communities in Namibia, on worth and standing in a postcolonial state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theory and East-West debates; mixed for measured self-esteem by region; thin for Sorbian and Frisian sources.
- **Languages needed:** German; Middle High German for courtly sources; Turkish and Russian for migrant voices; Romani for Sinti and Roma accounts where published.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "honor" framings of Turkish and Arab families risk stereotype and need their own voices; the Nazi idea of "unworthy life" (euthanasia murders) is a source of later dignity debates; East-West resentment is politicized; suicide data need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
