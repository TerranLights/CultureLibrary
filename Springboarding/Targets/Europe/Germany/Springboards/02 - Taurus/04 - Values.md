# Germany: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Middle High German for medieval moral writing); Sorbian and Danish for minority institutions; Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Polish for migrant communities. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** church membership and secularity to compare; *Jugendweihe* against confirmation.
- **Protestant north and center against Catholic Rhineland, Westphalian Münsterland and the Eichsfeld enclave:** confession as a dividing line.
- **Pietist Württemberg:** a distinct strand of Protestant moral life.
- **Hanseatic cities:** a merchant and civic value tradition to test.
- **Catholic Sorbs (Upper Lusatia) against Protestant Lower Sorbs:** confession inside one minority.
- **Danish minority in South Schleswig:** Nordic-style schools and associations.
- **Muslim communities (Turkish, Arab, Kurdish, Bosnian heritage):** distinct religious and secular currents, not one bloc.
- **Jewish communities:** older German-Jewish and post-1990 Russian-speaking members.
- **Generations:** postwar, 1968, post-unification, and the young.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, a Catholic-majority neighbor for confession-linked value comparisons. **Diaspora comparison only:** Mennonite and Amish communities abroad, whose values texts descend from German-speaking origins.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; values surveys, philosophy and public debate are dense.
- **Languages needed:** German; Middle High German; Turkish and Arabic for Muslim organizations' own texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era value language (*Volksgemeinschaft*, "German virtues") colors later debate; the *Leitkultur* debate is polarized; claims about Muslim or East German "values" easily turn into stereotypes, so keep the groups' own voices. Law is not researched here.
- **Held for review:** none.

1. GESIS: search "European Values Study Germany" and download the integrated file's codebook; note items on ranking life domains.
