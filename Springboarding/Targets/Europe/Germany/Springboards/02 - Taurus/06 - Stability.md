# Germany: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (Middle High German for *staete*), Low German, North Frisian, Danish and Sorbian; Turkish, Russian, Polish, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Hanseatic families and firms:** dynastic continuity to examine, and how it is spoken of.
- **Ruhr and Saar:** stable mining and steel careers, then structural change.
- **The former GDR:** job and housing continuity before 1990, upheaval after; *Ostalgie* and its meanings.
- **Rural Westphalia, Lower Saxony and the Hunsrück:** single-heir farms, villages, club life.
- **Württemberg and Baden:** small firms (*Mittelstand*) and household routines.
- **Catholic enclaves (Eichsfeld, Münsterland):** parish continuity.
- **Sorbs, Danes, North and Saterland Frisians:** minority continuity through border and regime changes.
- **Recruitment-era migrants and their children:** stability built across two countries.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** rebuilt routines after relocation.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, for continuity customs measured with the same panel data. **Diaspora comparison only:** Old Order Amish and Mennonites, whose continuity practices have German-speaking roots.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; panels, museums and oral-history projects are extensive.
- **Languages needed:** German; Middle High German; Low German and Frisian for coastal customs; Sorbian for Lusatian sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Germans love order" is a stereotype to examine, not a finding; *Ostalgie* is politically charged; the Nazi era used *Bodenständigkeit* and rootedness in a racial sense, so older uses need care.
- **Held for review:** none.
