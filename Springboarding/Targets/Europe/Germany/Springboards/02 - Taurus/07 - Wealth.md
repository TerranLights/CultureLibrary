## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hamburg and Bremen merchant families:** old commercial wealth and the "honorable merchant" idiom.
- **Rhineland and Ruhr industrial dynasties:** heavy-industry fortunes and their reputations.
- **Württemberg, Baden, Sauerland and other small-firm regions:** quiet provincial fortunes in family companies.
- **Affluent districts (the Taunus near Frankfurt, the Elbe suburbs of Hamburg, Düsseldorf-Oberkassel):** high-income belts, to compare with quiet provincial wealth.
- **The former GDR:** socialist privilege, then late entry to private wealth and inheritance.
- **Saxon court heritage (Dresden):** princely wealth as regional pride and tourism.
- **Turkish-German entrepreneurs:** shop, restaurant and trade wealth; display at weddings as a question to research, not assume.
- **Russian-German and post-Soviet Jewish arrivals:** starting over with little.
- **German Sinti and Roma; long-term unemployed:** the other end of the scale.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, the neighboring case for wealth-gap and rich-list comparisons. **Diaspora comparison only:** German-American brewing and industrial fortunes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; wealth statistics and literary sources are deep, though top wealth is underreported in surveys.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older German for the word's history; Turkish and Russian for migrant entrepreneurs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antisemitic stereotypes of Jewish wealth in older sources (Gustav Freytag's *Soll und Haben*, 1855, is the classic case; read critically); fortunes built under Nazi rule or with forced labor; East-West resentment over who owns what.
- **Held for review:** none.
