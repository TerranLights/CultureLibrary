## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Frisians and East Frisians:** island and coastal refusal legends; check how outsiders' "stubborn Frisian" jokes compare with Frisians' own accounts.
- **Wendland (Lower Saxony):** a rural district's protest history and how residents describe it.
- **Württemberg:** Pietist conscience and Stuttgart's protest movement.
- **Saxony and Thuringia:** 1989 steadfastness against later pandemic and anti-government protests; the same streets, different meanings.
- **Westphalia:** a regional reputation for obstinacy to test against data, not repeat.
- **Lusatian Sorbs:** the persistence of language and villages under pressure, as Sorbian sources tell it.
- **Turkish-German families:** *inat* as a word in family and public speech.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** holding to faith and customs through two migrations.
- **Generations:** postwar parents raised under "will-breaking" norms against their children and grandchildren.
- **Neighbor comparison only:** Bavaria, whose own "obstinate" image in jokes needs the same testing. **Diaspora comparison only:** Old Order Amish, who refused many modern changes in German-speaking communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature and protest archives; mixed for psychological measures by region.
- **Languages needed:** German; Low German and North Frisian for coastal legends; Turkish and Russian for migrant family speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the cliché of German stubbornness, or of particular regions, must be tested, not repeated; *Reichsbürger* and some pandemic protests are extremist and under official observation; the Nazi idea of "fanatical" will colors older praise of firmness; Brandt's gesture touches war guilt.
- **Held for review:** none.
