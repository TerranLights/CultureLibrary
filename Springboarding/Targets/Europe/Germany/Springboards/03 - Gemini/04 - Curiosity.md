# Germany: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Low German; Danish; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for immigrant communities. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Württemberg and Baden:** the inventor-workshop image (*Tüftler*), worth testing against data instead of repeating.
- **The GDR generation:** state-run popular science, and Sigmund Jähn of the Vogtland, the first German in space (1978), as a curiosity hero.
- **Hamburg and Bremen:** port cities with overseas collections and the colonial questions they raise.
- **Village and city:** watching the street from the window against urban anonymity.
- **Turkish-, Arabic- and Russian-speaking families:** which personal questions are welcome or rude, which differ by home language.
- **Muslim, Christian and unaffiliated households:** limits on questioning set by faith or none.
- **Genealogists:** family research, with its fraught Nazi-era history (the *Ahnenpass*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Science engagement, media and museums are well recorded; nosiness and everyday asking are thinner, mostly in folklore and comedy.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for questions of politeness in asking.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial collections and human remains in museums; Nazi racial science as curiosity's crime; genealogy's link to Nazi ancestry certificates. Penalties for photographing accident victims are law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** none.
