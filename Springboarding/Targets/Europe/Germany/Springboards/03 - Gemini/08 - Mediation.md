*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic regions** (Rhineland, Münsterland, Eichsfeld, Catholic Upper Lusatia): saints and the Virgin as intercessors.
- **Protestant north and the largely unchurched east:** direct prayer, and in the GDR, churches as civic go-betweens.
- **The Ruhr and other heavy-industry areas:** coal-and-steel co-determination (1951 *check*) and works councils as standing go-betweens.
- **Württemberg:** Stuttgart 21 as a test case of public conciliation.
- **Turkish-, Kurdish- and Arabic-speaking communities:** elders, imams and family heads as informal mediators.
- **Russian-German communities:** church elders in Free Church congregations.
- **Rural Westphalia and Lower Saxony:** wedding inviters and village go-betweens.
- **Professional urban milieus:** paid mediators for divorce and workplace disputes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Labor conciliation and professional mediation are documented; informal go-betweens are thin and need interviews.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish, Arabic and Kurdish for community mediators; Russian for Aussiedler congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the debate over informal "peace judges" in some immigrant communities is polarized and touches law, so read critically and send the legal side to Libra; the Mediation Act and court-linked mediation belong to Libra.
