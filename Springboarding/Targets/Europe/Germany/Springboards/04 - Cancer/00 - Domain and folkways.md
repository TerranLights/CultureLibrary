# Germany: Home and family, the domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, Low German, Frisian, Sorbian, Danish, Romanes; Turkish, Kurmanji, Russian, Polish, Arabic, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the domain question, what is home and who is family, across household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** four decades of separate housing and child-care policy before 1990; split every table by old and new Länder.
- **North-west impartible against south-west partible inheritance areas** (Westphalia and Lower Saxony against Württemberg, Baden, Hesse, the Palatinate): how farm succession shaped co-residence (property is Taurus's).
- **Sorbs (Upper and Lower Lusatia):** a Slavic-speaking people with their own village house and family customs.
- **North Frisians, Saterland Frisians and the Danish minority:** island and marsh households and minority-language family life.
- **Russian-German resettlers, many in East Westphalia-Lippe:** free-church congregations and household size to test.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Alevi, Arab and Vietnamese families:** co-residence and how far kin ties reach.
- **German Sinti and Yeniche:** family ways with their own norms; see the flags.
- **City-states (Berlin, Hamburg, Bremen) against rural districts:** living alone and household size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: dense statistics, a strong *Volkskunde* tradition (now *Europäische Ethnologie*) and a large family-history literature. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled: this scouting is written for the core lands of pre-war Germany outside Bavaria, which belongs to Austro-Bavaria. Federal tables include Bavaria; use Land-level breakdowns to set it aside.
- **Languages needed:** German; older German handwriting (Kurrent, Sütterlin) for archives; Low German, Frisian, Sorbian and Danish for minority sources; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for the largest immigrant groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nazi state's racialized "family" vocabulary and genealogy (*Ahnenpass*, *Sippe*) and its persecution of Sinti, Roma and Jewish families; Sinti family knowledge is not for outsiders by default; approach through the Zentralrat Deutscher Sinti und Roma *check*. Family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
