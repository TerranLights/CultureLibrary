# Germany: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, Low German, Upper and Lower Sorbian, North Frisian; Turkish, Russian, Polish, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hunsrück, Palatinate and other western rural districts:** the classic settings of Heimat writing and film.
- **Lusatia's lost villages (Sorbs and Germans):** home erased by mining, and resettlement.
- **Expellee families settled in Lower Saxony and Schleswig-Holstein after 1945:** a home place that no longer exists.
- **East Germans across 1990:** a home that changed around those who stayed.
- **Württemberg's homeowners against Berlin's and Hamburg's renters:** home as owned property or as a lease; ownership itself is Taurus's.
- **Ruhr mining colonies:** home tied to the pit and the company house.
- **Turkish-German and Kurdish families:** *sıla* and the summer journey; the old village house.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** a "homeland" reached that was not the home left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Heimat history and housing statistics; thinner on home as daily feeling, for which diaries, interviews and media will be needed.
- **Languages needed:** German; Low German and Sorbian for regional writing; Turkish and Russian for migrants' "back home".
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Heimat* was used by Nazi blood-and-soil writing and is contested again in current politics (a federal ministry took the word in 2018 *check*); keep partisan uses as data, not as a voice for all. Expellee organizations' "right to a homeland" claims are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
