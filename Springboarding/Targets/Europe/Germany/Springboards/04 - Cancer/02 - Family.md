# Germany: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, Low German, Upper and Lower Sorbian, North Frisian; Turkish, Kurmanji, Russian, Arabic, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Westphalian and Lower Saxon farm families:** farm names (*Hofnamen*, "genannt" names *check*) that could outlast surnames; the house as lineage.
- **North Frisian island families:** seafaring absences and older naming customs *check*.
- **Catholic Upper Lusatian Sorbs against Protestant Lower Sorbs:** godparenthood and wedding customs by confession.
- **East and West:** two family-policy systems; kin ties across the Wall.
- **Turkish and Kurdish families:** kin reach and in-law ties; compare by generation.
- **Yazidi families in Lower Saxony (Celle, Oldenburg):** marriage within the community, as the community explains it.
- **Russian-German Mennonite and Baptist congregations:** large families and congregation as kin.
- **Vietnamese families in Berlin and the eastern Länder:** separated former contract workers.
- **Same-sex-parent and blended families:** chosen ties beside blood.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: registers, family books, sociology and policy reports. Thin on everyday kin talk outside the majority.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older script for registers; Low German and Sorbian; Turkish, Kurmanji, Russian, Arabic and Vietnamese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogy carries the shadow of Nazi-era pedigree proofs; Sinti family genealogies were used for persecution, so Sinti kin knowledge stays with the community; Yazidi marriage rules are internal matters for the community to describe; privacy of living persons in registers.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: "Rosenbaum Formen der Familie" and the latest *Familienbericht*; list the family types each uses.
2. GenWiki: "Ortsfamilienbuch" plus a sample of villages from the Rhineland, Westphalia, Saxony and Schleswig-Holstein.
3. AdA site: search the maps for "Oma", "Großmutter" and other kin words.
4. Matricula: test which northern and western dioceses are online; note godparent entries in a sample village.
5. Detmold museum catalog and Russian-German association archives: search "Familienzusammenführung" for reunited families' accounts.
