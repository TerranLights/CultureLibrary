# Germany: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cologne, Hamburg and Swabian Württemberg:** each has its own word for the incomer; compare how long the label lasts.
- **The Danish minority in South Schleswig:** belonging chosen by declaration.
- **Sorbs:** a people inside the nation with its own federation and language.
- **East Germans:** belonging to the East, to the Land and to the united country.
- **Expellee families and their descendants:** once the newcomers, now the locals.
- **Black Germans:** German-born and still asked where they are from.
- **Turkish Germans:** called *Almancı* in Turkey and foreigners in Germany: belonging nowhere fully, to test.
- **Post-Soviet Jewish immigrants and the older Jewish communities:** belonging inside congregations as well as in the country.
- **Refugees from 2015 on (Syrian, Afghan, Eritrean):** the welcome and its limits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and history; mixed for local, informal belonging, which sits in club records and dialect media.
- **Languages needed:** German and regional speech; Sorbian and Danish; Turkish, Russian, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** belonging and exclusion touch racism, antisemitism and antiziganism; the far right's "who is German" claims are data, not a voice for the nation; Sinti and Roma belonging should be approached through their own bodies (Zentralrat Deutscher Sinti und Roma *check*). Citizenship law is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** none.
