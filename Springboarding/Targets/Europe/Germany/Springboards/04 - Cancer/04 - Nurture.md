## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** crèche from infancy against care at home, and how far that has converged.
- **Württemberg Pietist and Halle Pietist traditions:** their child-rearing writings, against today's practice in the same regions.
- **Anthroposophical families (the first Waldorf school opened in Stuttgart, 1919 *check*):** a whole alternative theory of nurture.
- **Academic and working-class households:** schooling ambitions, lessons and free time.
- **Sorbian families:** passing on a language at home and in preschool.
- **Turkish-German and Arab families:** grandmothers' role and the *terbiye* ideal against German preschool norms.
- **Vietnamese-German families:** school expectations, checked against the "model minority" framing.
- **Russian-German families:** *vospitanie* as taught in the Soviet Union, carried into Lippe and Württemberg towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: a long German advice literature, strong developmental psychology, good child-care statistics.
- **Languages needed:** German (older print in Fraktur); Sorbian; Turkish, Russian, Arabic, Vietnamese for parent interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era infant-care advice still debated as a family legacy (see Motherhood); harsh-pedagogy history touches child abuse; avoid one national "German parent" voice.
- **Held for review:** none.
