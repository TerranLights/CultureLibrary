*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West mothers:** paid work as the norm against the home as the ideal; the *Rabenmutter* charge by region and generation.
- **Lower Rhine Catholics (the Kevelaer Marian pilgrimage *check*) and Catholic Upper Lusatian Sorbs (the Rosenthal pilgrimage *check*):** Marian devotion beside lived motherhood.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab mothers:** religious honor of the mother, and daughters' changing choices.
- **Russian-German free-church families:** many children as a calling.
- **Single mothers and lesbian mothers:** motherhood outside the married couple.
- **Late first-time mothers in Berlin, Hamburg and Frankfurt against younger mothers in rural districts.**
- **Jewish mothers, historical and present:** from Glikl to post-Soviet immigrant families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: early printed books, Nazi-era and postwar policy history, magazine archives, good birth statistics.
- **Languages needed:** German, Early New High German, Fraktur print; Sorbian; Yiddish for Glikl's original; Turkish, Arabic, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nazi motherhood cult, *Lebensborn* and forced sterilization; abortion is a fierce debate (Aries); maternity-protection law goes to Libra. Do not reduce mothers to one "German mother".
- **Held for review:** none.
