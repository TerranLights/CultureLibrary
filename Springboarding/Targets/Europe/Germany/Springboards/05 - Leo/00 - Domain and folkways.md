## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rhineland Catholics** (Cologne, Düsseldorf, Mainz): carnival as a civic institution with its own court, speeches and ranks.
- **The Swabian-Alemannic southwest:** masked guild carnival with older roots than the Rhineland's reformed one.
- **The Protestant north and the Hanseatic cities:** a thinner carnival calendar; check what takes its place.
- **East Germans, by generation:** the GDR festival calendar, *Jugendweihe* and club life rebuilt after 1990.
- **Sorbs of Lusatia:** Catholic Upper Sorbs and largely Protestant Lower Sorbs keep different festive years.
- **Frisians and the Danish minority** in Schleswig-Holstein: their own feasts and annual meetings.
- **Turkish-German and Kurdish families:** wedding halls and henna nights as the main occasions of display.
- **Germans from the former Soviet Union:** a celebration repertoire carried from the Soviet decades.
- **Village and big city:** the parish fair and shooting feast against Berlin's club culture.

## 4. Flags

- **Lands:** Germany's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; the sheets for this sign are written for the German core lands. Bavaria belongs to the separate Austro-Bavaria target, so the Munich Oktoberfest and Bavarian *Fasching* serve here only as outside comparisons.
