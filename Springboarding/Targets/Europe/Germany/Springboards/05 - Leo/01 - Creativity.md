# Germany: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German and Low German; Upper and Lower Sorbian; Danish and Frisian in Schleswig-Holstein; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for the largest immigrant communities. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Württemberg and Baden:** the "tinkerer" self-image and dense small engineering firms; test the self-image against patent data.
- **Saxony and Thuringia:** a long chain of craft and precision work (Erzgebirge wood, Meissen porcelain, Jena optics).
- **Berlin:** the artists who came for cheap space after 1990, and the rents that pushed many out.
- **The Ruhr:** industrial sites turned into culture venues; craft and art in former mining towns.
- **East and West Germans:** state-commissioned art and writing circles against the western gallery market.
- **The Low German north:** a literature and stage of its own (Fritz Reuter, Klaus Groth *check*).
- **Sorbs:** a craft and literary tradition carried in two small languages.
- **Writers and filmmakers from immigrant families** (Turkish-German, Russian-German, Arab-German): who is counted as a German artist.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the arts economy and institutions; mixed for everyday making and DIY, where trade surveys and hobby magazines will do most of the work.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older German for pre-1800 texts; Sorbian for the minority's crafts; Turkish and Russian for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nazi "degenerate art" campaign and looted art; the genius idea's political misuse; copyright and AI-made art belong to Libra's letter-of-the-law research.
- **Held for review:** none.
