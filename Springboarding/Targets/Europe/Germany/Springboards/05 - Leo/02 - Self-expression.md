# Germany: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, including Low German; Upper and Lower Sorbian; Danish and Frisian; Turkish, Kurdish, Russian and Arabic for the largest immigrant communities. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The north coast and the Rhineland:** local self-descriptions of reserve and of openness, to be tested, not assumed.
- **Carnival towns:** the mask as permission to say and wear what is held back the rest of the year.
- **East Germans who grew up in the GDR:** the split between public and private speech (Günter Gaus's "niche society" *check*), and how it shifted after 1990.
- **Generations:** the 1968 cohort, the post-reunification youth, and today's digital natives.
- **Sorbian women who wear the costume daily, and younger Sorbs who wear it for feasts:** dress as identity, by age and parish.
- **Young people in mixed city districts:** *Kiezdeutsch* (Heike Wiese, 2012 *check*) as a style of speaking.
- **Muslim women who wear a headscarf:** faith shown in dress, and the arguments around it.
- **Germans from the former Soviet Union:** pressure to blend in after arrival, and how their children answer it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for values surveys and youth research; mixed for everyday emotional display, where qualitative studies and media will carry the work.
- **Languages needed:** German, older German for 18th-century sources; Turkish, Kurdish, Russian and Arabic for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Stasi files hold third parties' personal data and have access rules; free-speech law, and the history of the anti-gay penal clause, go to Libra and Capricorn; headscarf debates are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none.
