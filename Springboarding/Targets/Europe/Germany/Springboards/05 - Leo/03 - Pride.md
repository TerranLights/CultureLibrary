# Germany: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, with Middle High German for medieval texts and the Cologne and Low German dialects; Upper and Lower Sorbian; Danish; North Frisian; Turkish, Russian and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cologne and the Rhineland:** loud civic pride in dialect and song, to set beside the hesitancy around national pride.
- **The Ruhr:** pride rooted in hard work underground, tested against deindustrialization.
- **Hamburg and Bremen merchants:** a self-image of understatement; check whether it reads as modesty or as a quieter pride.
- **East Germans:** pride in a region and in lives lived in the GDR, against the feeling of being looked down on.
- **Sorbs, Danes and Frisians:** minority pride with flags and anthems of its own.
- **Turkish-Germans:** pride in origin and in the new country, and the pressure to choose.
- **Germans from the former Soviet Union:** pride in German descent kept through the Soviet years.
- **Tradespeople and engineers:** pride in work and in "quality", as a self-description to test.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in surveys and political debate; mixed for everyday bragging and modesty norms.
- **Languages needed:** German (and Middle High German), Kölsch and Low German, Sorbian, Danish, Turkish, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride after 1945 is politically loaded, and far-right groups use the word; avoid reading any one camp as the nation's voice. Research on pride and ethnicity must not slide into stereotypes of "the German character".
- **Held for review:** none.
