# Germany: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German and its regional greetings and dialects; Upper and Lower Sorbian; North Frisian; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for evil-eye and visibility vocabulary in immigrant families. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages and small towns:** neighbors who see everything; social watching as care and as control.
- **Berlin, Hamburg and other big cities:** anonymity chosen, and loneliness not chosen.
- **The north and the rest:** the greeting habits that mark strangers as seen, mapped by region.
- **East Germans:** watched by the state before 1990, then underrepresented in national media and leading posts.
- **Black Germans:** questioned for looking "foreign" while being invisible as Germans.
- **Turkish- and Arab-German families:** evil-eye beliefs, and the headscarf as visible faith.
- **Sinti and Roma:** stereotyped visibility against everyday invisibility as citizens.
- **Sorbs and Frisians:** visibility through language on screens and signs.
- **Disabled and older people living alone:** the people most often looked past.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for media counts, privacy debates and loneliness surveys; thin for everyday gaze and greeting norms, where fieldwork will be needed.
