# Germany: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Middle High German for medieval mystics); Sorbian; Danish; Romanes, through Sinti and Roma institutions; Turkish, Arabic, Russian and Polish, the last for live-in care workers. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Catholic west (Rhineland, Westphalia):** dignity framed by Catholic social teaching and its associations.
- **The Protestant north and east:** dignity framed by work and duty; check how this shows in welfare debates.
- **Industrial workers in the Ruhr and Saarland:** dignity bound up with work that has disappeared.
- **People on basic income support:** the stigma of the benefit office and the food bank.
- **Care-home residents and the live-in carers from Poland and other eastern countries:** two sides of the same dignity question.
- **Refugees in shared housing:** privacy, cooking for oneself, waiting.
- **Sinti and Roma:** dignity claimed against a long record of degradation.
- **Disabled people:** self-advocacy against institutional care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for care, discrimination and institutional history; mixed for everyday courtesy.
- **Languages needed:** German, Polish, Turkish, Arabic, Russian; Romanes through community bodies only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony from homes and camps; the constitution's dignity clause and the rulings on assisted dying are law, for Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the *Runder Tisch Heimerziehung* final report and list the regions and church bodies named.
2. Search the NaDiRa reports for items on being treated with less respect.
3. Search library catalogs for "Anrede Duzen Siezen Wandel" to find address studies beyond Besch.
4. Ask Tafel Deutschland for its user surveys or published studies of food-bank users.
5. Search municipal sites for "Sozialbestattung" and "ordnungsbehördliche Bestattung" to see how the unclaimed poor are buried.
