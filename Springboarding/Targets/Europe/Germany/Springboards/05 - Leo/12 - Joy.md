# Germany: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German, with 17th-century hymn German and the carnival dialects of Cologne and Mainz; Upper Sorbian; Danish; Turkish, Arabic and Russian. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cologne, Mainz and Düsseldorf:** loud, seasonal joy with its own cheers, songs and calendar.
- **Moravian Brethren at Herrnhut and other Pietist communities:** inward joy in faith; compare with carnival exuberance.
- **Saxony and the Erzgebirge:** Christmas lights, candle arches and markets as the region's joyful season.
- **Berlin clubgoers:** joy at night, in dance, by choice of scene.
- **East Germans:** the joy of 1989 against the later disappointments, by age.
- **Turkish- and Kurdish-German families:** weddings with *halay* dancing as the big shared joy.
- **Sorbs:** the Bird Wedding and Easter customs as children's joy.
- **The Danish minority:** *hygge* as a calm form of gladness.
- **Generations:** reported happiness by age in the well-being surveys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey well-being and festivals; thinner for how joy is felt and shown day to day, where language corpora and interviews help.
- **Languages needed:** German (older hymn German; Kölsch and Mainz dialect), Sorbian, Danish, Turkish, Arabic, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Love Parade disaster and its victims; depression data; the Nazi leisure body's use of "joy" in its name belongs to the domain sheet's history.
- **Held for review:** none.
