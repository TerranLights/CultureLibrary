# Germany: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (standard and regional), Low German, Upper and Lower Sorbian, Danish, North and Saterland Frisian, Romani (Sinti), and the main immigrant languages (Turkish, Kurmanji, Arabic, Russian, Polish). **Concept in one line:** what is a fair bargain and a good partnership? ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** two family codes, two church histories and two labor regimes before 1990; marriage, divorce and gender patterns need separating.
- **Catholic Rhineland and Westphalia against the Protestant north:** church weddings, marriage preparation and divorce attitudes followed confession.
- **Hanseatic city-states (Hamburg, Bremen):** merchant traditions of the handshake deal and of the honorable merchant.
- **Sorbs in Lusatia:** their own wedding customs and their own language of kinship.
- **Danish and North Frisian minorities in Schleswig:** bilingual weddings and a minority's own associations.
- **Sinti:** family and marriage customs held privately.
- **Turkish-, Kurdish- and Arab-origin communities:** several stages of marriage (promise, engagement, religious and civil vows) and informal mediators.
- **Russian Germans (*Spätaussiedler*):** Soviet-era family norms meeting German law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, marriage statistics and panel surveys; mixed for regional folk custom; thin and sensitive for informal dispute settlement in immigrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** German (including old forms and dialects), Sorbian, Danish, Frisian, Turkish, Arabic, Russian.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core lands. In this setting Bavaria belongs to a separate nation, so Bavarian material is a neighbor comparison only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sinti and Roma family customs (keep to what the communities publish); "parallel justice" in immigrant communities (stigma risk); Nazi-era race laws on marriage.
- **Held for review:** none.
