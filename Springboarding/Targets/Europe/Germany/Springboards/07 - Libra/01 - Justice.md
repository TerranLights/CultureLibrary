# Germany: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Turkish, Arabic, Russian and Polish for the largest immigrant communities; Sorbian and Danish for the old minorities. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former GDR citizens:** the Stasi files, the Wall trials and the "victor's justice" complaint made justice after 1990 a lived question there.
- **Jewish communities:** restitution and the long delay in prosecuting Nazi crimes.
- **Sinti and Roma:** recognition of the genocide came late; their own justice bodies are not for outsiders.
- **Turkish- and Kurdish-origin Germans:** the arson attacks of Mölln (1992) and Solingen (1993) and the NSU murders, and how families judged the investigations.
- **Russian Germans:** Soviet-era courts and German courts as two frames of reference.
- **Ruhr and Saxon industrial towns:** social justice framed by the labor movement and, later, by the end of mining.
- **Hamburg and Berlin against rural Mecklenburg-Vorpommern or Thuringia:** city and country on punishment and policing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (statistics, case law, history of justice after dictatorship); thinner for informal justice in immigrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** German, including legal German and older forms; Turkish, Arabic, Russian for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi crimes and the victims' families; racist attacks; the "clan" crime label (stigmatizing); Stasi files (privacy rules on third parties).
- **Held for review:** none.
