# Germany: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Turkish, Russian, Arabic and Vietnamese for large immigrant communities; Danish in South Schleswig. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Westphalia and Lower Saxony against Baden, Württemberg and the Palatinate:** single-heir farms against equal division shaped what "fair to the siblings" meant.
- **East against West:** wage levels, pensions and the solidarity surcharge as running arguments about a fair share since 1990.
- **Hamburg and Bremen:** merchant codes of the fair price and the honest dealer.
- **Former Gastarbeiter and their children:** fair wages and fair treatment as recruits and later as citizens.
- **Vietnamese Germans in the East:** a history of GDR labor contracts and later self-employment.
- **Generations:** pensions and debt argued as fairness to the young.
- **Public-sector and private-sector workers:** different pay rules and ideas of desert.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; Germany is a center of experimental economics and of survey research on justice and fairness.
- **Languages needed:** German; the English loan *fair* and the native *gerecht* overlap, so search both.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** none special; *Neid* talk can serve as a political weapon, so read sources for their side.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Cologne experimental-economics literature for "Ultimatumspiel" and the follow-up studies with German subjects.
2. Map *Anerbenrecht* and *Realteilung* areas from a regional history atlas, then read one Land's *Höfeordnung*.
3. Search DWDS for *fair* and *gerecht* to see how usage has shifted since 1950.
4. Pull the Federal Anti-Discrimination Agency's annual complaint figures by ground.
5. Search Wander's lexicon for *teilen*, *mahlen* and *Betrug*.
