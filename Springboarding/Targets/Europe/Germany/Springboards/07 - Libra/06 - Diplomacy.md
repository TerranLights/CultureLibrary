# Germany: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Low German for the coast); Danish and Polish for the border regions; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for immigrant families' go-betweens and child interpreters. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hamburg and Bremen:** a reputation for Hanseatic reserve, to test against how business talk is actually done.
- **Berlin:** the "Berliner Schnauze" as a local name for bluntness, worth studying as self-image.
- **East and West:** misunderstandings in tone after 1990, studied in their own right.
- **Schleswig's Danish minority:** daily diplomacy between two languages and two states.
- **Oder-Neisse twin towns (Görlitz-Zgorzelec, Frankfurt an der Oder-Słubice):** cross-border negotiation in daily life.
- **Sorbs:** bargaining with two Land governments over language and school rights.
- **Children of Turkish-, Arab- and Russian-speaking families:** language brokers at offices and doctors.
- **Rhineland:** reputations of easy sociability, to test against the measures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and labor mediation; mixed for everyday tact; the "direct German" image needs care.
- **Languages needed:** German; Danish and Polish for borders; immigrant languages for brokering.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Second World War and its aftermath frame many talks with neighbors; national-character claims in business guides must be tested, not repeated.
- **Held for review:** none.
