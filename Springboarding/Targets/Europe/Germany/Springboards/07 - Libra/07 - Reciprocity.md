# Germany: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Low German for the northwest and Middle High German for the courtly tradition); Turkish, Vietnamese, Russian, Arabic, Polish. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Münsterland, Emsland and Grafschaft Bentheim:** codified neighbors' duties that may be stronger or weaker than elsewhere.
- **Pomerania and Mecklenburg:** coastal and Baltic gift customs (the *Julklapp*) shared with Scandinavia.
- **Former GDR citizens:** the favor-swapping of a shortage economy and what replaced it after 1990.
- **Ruhr and Ore Mountains mining towns:** miners' mutual funds and comradeship underground.
- **Turkish-origin women's networks:** the "gold day" as savings and sociability.
- **Vietnamese Germans:** rotating credit in the East's market trade.
- **Russian Germans:** large kin networks that share housing, labor and care.
- **Big cities against villages:** anonymous giving (food banks, online swaps) against known neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in survey and experimental measures; mixed for regional customs, which sit in local and dialect sources.
- **Languages needed:** German, Low German, Middle High German; Turkish and Vietnamese for informal savings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Thurnwald's later career under National Socialism; informal savings groups may operate outside banking rules, so approach with care.
- **Held for review:** none.
