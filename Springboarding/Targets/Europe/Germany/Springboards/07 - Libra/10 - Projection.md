# Germany: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Kölsch for Carnival customs and Early New High German for the witch-trial era); Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Polish. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West:** each side's image of the other since 1990, as a projection question in itself.
- **The Moselle and Rhineland witch-trial areas against the Protestant north:** whether accusations differed in intensity, and why.
- **Cologne, Mainz and Düsseldorf Carnival:** a ritual outlet that places blame on a figure.
- **Jewish communities:** the targets of the oldest projections, and their own reporting networks.
- **Sinti and Roma:** long-standing scapegoating and their own representative council.
- **Muslims and Arab-origin Germans:** blamed in moral panics since 2015.
- **Turkish-origin Germans:** evil-eye beliefs and amulets as a folk account of misfortune.
- **Russian Germans:** blamed as "Russians" and themselves divided over enemy images since 2022.
- **Saxony:** often cast in national media as the problem region, worth studying as a case of projection in both directions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; German scholarship on projection, prejudice and authoritarianism is extensive, with long survey series.
- **Languages needed:** German; Early New High German and Latin for witch trials; Turkish and Arabic for folk beliefs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** antisemitism, antiziganism and racism; Nazi and GDR propaganda; victims' testimony. Present every group as a target or holder of images to study, never as the problem.
- **Held for review:** none.
