## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholic Rhineland and Münsterland against the Protestant north and Saxony:** All Souls' grave visits against Eternity Sunday.
- **The former GDR states against the former West:** state atheism and anonymous urn fields set the east's death ways apart.
- **Westphalia and Lower Saxony (single heir) against Württemberg, Baden and Hesse (divided farms):** inheritance custom split the landscape.
- **Hanseatic cities (Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck):** merchant inheritance and old civic burial customs.
- **Sorbs of Upper and Lower Lusatia; Danes and North Frisians of Schleswig; Saterland Frisians:** minority churches, languages and customs.
- **Sinti and Roma:** distinct death and purity customs, and the memory of genocide.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab Muslim communities:** burial without coffin, repatriation of the dead, separate grave fields.
- **Russian-German resettlers, Jewish communities, Vietnamese Germans:** each keeps its own mourning calendar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, folklore studies and memory scholarship; sexuality data are newer and thinner.
- **Scope:** Germany's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core German lands, and Bavaria, a separate nation in this setting, appears only as a neighboring comparison (for example for Catholic death customs).
- **Languages needed:** German, incl. reading Fraktur print and Kurrent or Sütterlin handwriting for archives; Low German; Sorbian; Danish; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era Volkskunde (incl. the *Handwörterbuch*) must be read critically; the Holocaust and the genocide of Sinti and Roma; human remains in museum collections and their return; Sinti and Roma customs on the dead are not for outsiders to publish without consent. The legal side of burial, inheritance and sex goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
