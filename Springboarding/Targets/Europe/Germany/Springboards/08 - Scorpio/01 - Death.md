# Germany: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German and Low German; Sorbian, Danish and Frisian; Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Vietnamese and Yiddish or Hebrew for community sources. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood, incl. grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The unchurched east (Saxony, Thuringia, Brandenburg, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Saxony-Anhalt):** death without clergy; secular funeral speakers and how meaning is spoken.
- **The Catholic Rhineland, Eifel and Münsterland:** grave lights on All Saints, requiem masses, the six-week mass.
- **Lutheran north and the Hanseatic cities:** Eternity Sunday (Totensonntag) and the Protestant funeral liturgy.
- **North Frisians and coastal Danes:** deaths at sea and graves for drowned strangers (query: "Friedhof der Namenlosen Amrum").
- **Ruhr and Erzgebirge mining families:** pit disasters and miners' funerals.
- **Sorbs of Lusatia:** Catholic Upper Lusatia against Protestant Lower Lusatia.
- **Jewish communities:** Kaddish, the Weißensee cemetery in Berlin and the vast loss of 1933-45.
- **Sinti and Roma:** mourning customs and genocide memory, as their own bodies share them.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab Muslims:** quick burial, washing rites, repatriation.
- **Vietnamese Germans and Russian-German resettlers:** ancestor altars and the ninth- and fortieth-day rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, scholarship and memorial culture; thin on what ordinary families say at deathbeds.
- **Languages needed:** German (Fraktur for older consolation literature), Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Vietnamese, Hebrew and Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Holocaust, the genocide of Sinti and Roma, the T4 murders and war dead; suicide; child death. The rules on assisted dying and burial go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit). Funeral practice belongs to the domain sheet.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (their own mourning ways).
