## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Württemberg's Pietist heartland (Korntal and the Swabian fellowships):** the place to test how far conversion accounts reach everyday life.
- **Siegerland, Wuppertal and Minden-Ravensberg revival belts:** 19th-c. awakening cultures, each with its own flavor.
- **Herrnhut and Upper Lusatia:** Moravian renewal and its burial-ground Easter.
- **The secular east:** Jugendweihe in place of confirmation; the post-1990 remaking of careers and selves.
- **Catholic Rhineland:** gradual sacramental renewal (confirmation, confession) against sudden awakening.
- **Ruhr cities:** the rebirth of a region after mining ended.
- **Sorbs, North Frisians and Low German speakers:** language revival on their own terms.
- **Russian-German free churches in eastern Westphalia and Lippe:** congregations founded through migration.
- **Jewish communities:** renewed by post-1990 immigration from the former Soviet Union.
- **German converts to Islam and to evangelical churches; Turkish and Arab Muslim communities:** repentance and return in another vocabulary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church statistics, Pietist archives and postwar reconstruction; mixed for addiction recovery and extremist exits; thin for private "second chance" stories.
- **Languages needed:** German (Fraktur and Kurrent for Pietist sources), Sorbian, Frisian, Low German, Russian, Turkish, Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** extremist exits (protect identities); addiction; postwar "rebirth" stories can hide Nazi pasts; Jewish renewal after the Holocaust. Penal reintegration goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.
