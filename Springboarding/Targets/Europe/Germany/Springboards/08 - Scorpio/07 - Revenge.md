## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brandenburg and Saxony:** home ground of the Kohlhase story; also of postwar and 2015-era vigilante "citizens' patrols", sensitive to research.
- **Lower Saxony:** Hameln's legend and Busch's prank tales, both about getting even.
- **The Ruhr:** the Schalke-Dortmund derby as ritual payback between neighboring cities.
- **Expellee families (from Silesia, East Prussia, the Sudetenland) now spread over all Länder:** how the public renunciation of revenge sits beside remembered grievance, as a question.
- **The West German left and the RAF generation:** political retaliation and its memory.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab families:** blood-feud and honor vocabularies, as the communities' own women's and youth groups discuss them; never assume them.
- **Russian-German resettlers and Polish Germans:** wartime grievance across generations.
- **Jewish communities:** the debate over vengeance after 1945 (the Nakam group), from Jewish sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, legend and political violence; mixed for everyday revenge; thin and politicized for family feuds in migrant milieus.
- **Languages needed:** German (Early New High German for the Götz memoir), Turkish, Kurdish, Arabic, Russian, Polish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "honor killing" and "clan" talk are easily turned into stereotypes of whole peoples; vigilantism is tied to the far right; the air war as "retribution" is a revisionist talking point. Private vengeance against state punishment is Capricorn's (spirit of the law).
- **Held for review:** none.
