## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The former East against the West:** two histories of leaving religion; secular rites (Jugendweihe) with no Western twin.
- **Exceptions inside the East:** the Catholic Eichsfeld and the Erzgebirge's Pietists test whether "secular East" holds everywhere.
- **The Western confessional map:** Catholic Rhineland and Münsterland; the Lutheran north; Reformed East Frisia and Lippe; Württemberg Pietism beside Catholic Baden.
- **Sorbs:** one small people split by confession.
- **North Frisians and South Schleswig's Danes:** their own languages of worship.
- **Sinti:** a long-settled minority whose religious life is rarely studied in its own terms.
- **Muslims by origin and branch** (Turkish Sunni, Alevi, Kurdish, Arab, Bosnian) and the Yazidis: not one community.
- **Jewish communities:** older families and the post-1990 immigrants from the former Soviet Union.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** their own Baptist and Mennonite congregations.
- **Vietnamese Germans:** GDR contract workers and Western boat refugees took different religious paths.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for large-church membership; mixed for belief content; thin for small minorities in their own words.
- **Lands:** Germany's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; the sign's sheets are written for the core lands. Bavaria is a separate target nation and stays outside; where Franconia and Bavarian Swabia fall is part of that settlement.
- **Languages needed:** German, with Fraktur and Kurrent for archives; Latin and Old Saxon; Low German; Sorbian; Danish; Turkish, Kurdish, Arabic, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era church history (the "gottgläubig" category of the 1939 census *check*); far-right misuse of Germanic pagan material; state monitoring of some religious bodies (contested). Church tax and church-state law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
