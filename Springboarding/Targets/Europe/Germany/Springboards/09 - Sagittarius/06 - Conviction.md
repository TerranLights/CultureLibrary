## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **GDR church-based opposition (Leipzig, East Berlin, Jena) against Western movements:** to compare stands taken at the risk of prison with stands taken at lower cost.
- **The Wendland:** to ask how a long protest shaped a rural district's sense of itself.
- **The Rhineland lignite area and Lusatia:** climate activists against mining families; two convictions, two livelihoods.
- **Stuttgart 21's middle-class protesters:** conviction outside the usual activist milieus.
- **Peace churches (Mennonites of the Palatinate and Lower Rhine) and Jehovah's Witnesses:** conviction as a religious duty to refuse.
- **Alevi, Kurdish and Turkish activists:** stands tied to conflicts in Turkey, carried out in German cities.
- **Generations:** 1968ers, the 1980s peace movement and climate strikers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for movements and memory; mixed for ordinary people's stands; thin for minority-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** German, early New High German for 1521; Turkish, Kurmanji, Arabic, Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political violence (RAF; attacks from all sides) is described, not endorsed; some Kurdish organizations are banned (legal side to Libra); climate protest is politically charged; objector records hold personal data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
