## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former East and former West:** forty years of different schooling, work and state; most surveys split them.
- **Hanseatic north (Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck):** standing rooted in city self-government, to separate from court and army rank.
- **Prussian Brandenburg and Berlin:** the old seat of state, army and civil-service ranks.
- **Catholic Rhineland and Westphalia:** church, guild and club hierarchies, and the carnival societies that parody them.
- **Pietist Württemberg:** a separate Protestant strand on work and self-examination, to keep apart from Lutheran Prussia.
- **Sorbs of Lusatia:** a Slavic minority with its own church life and its own organizations.
- **Danish minority and North Frisians:** a different language of duty and authority within the same state.
- **Turkish-German and Kurdish families:** what is owed to elders and kin, by generation.
- **Russian-German Aussiedler:** Soviet working lives carried into German ones.
- **Sinti and Roma:** the state as persecutor in living memory; authority seen from that side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the work is choosing among surveys, archives and dictionaries, not finding them.
- **Languages needed:** German first (including older print and Fraktur for pre-1945 sources); Danish, Sorbian and Frisian for minorities; Turkish, Kurdish, Russian and Arabic for immigrant voices.
- **Lands:** Germany's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author in Stage 0; Austro-Bavaria is a separate nation in this setting, so this sheet and the sign's sheets are written for the remaining core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi-era obedience and the Stasi state are heavily written about; treat them as periods, not the whole story. Stasi files carry access rules; Sinti and Roma records include persecution files.
- **Held for review:** none.
