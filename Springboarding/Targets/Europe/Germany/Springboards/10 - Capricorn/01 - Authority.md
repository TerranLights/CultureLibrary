*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Low German, Danish, Frisian, Sorbian; Turkish, Kurdish, Russian, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West Germans:** different relations to police, party and employer authority before 1990; the trust gap is a research question.
- **The 1968 generation and its children:** the revolt against fathers' authority, then the "anti-authoritarian" nurseries; see what followed.
- **Brandenburg and Berlin:** the Prussian army and court as an older seat of command.
- **East Frisians and Dithmarschers:** a remembered tradition of rule without lords.
- **Catholic and Protestant regions:** bishops' teaching authority against the Protestant synod.
- **Sorbs:** authority split between the Catholic parish (Upper Lusatia), the Protestant church (Lower Lusatia) and the Domowina.
- **Turkish-German Sunni communities, Alevis and Kurds:** mosque associations tied to a foreign state's religious office, and those who refuse that tie.
- **Recent Syrian and Afghan arrivals:** police and officials met after very different states at home.
- **Sinti and Roma:** police registration and persecution in living memory; authority seen from the receiving end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the risk is reading everything through the Nazi years. Keep earlier and later periods in view.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older print for Luther and Prussian sources; Turkish and Arabic for mosque and refugee communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Stasi files (personal data rules); abuse studies (survivors' testimony first); police violence data is contested between police unions and researchers.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Luther's 1523 treatise and Kant's 1784 essay side by side; list the limits each puts on obedience.
2. Search "Leipziger Autoritarismus-Studie" and download the latest report and its East-West tables.
3. Search "Erziehungsziele Gehorsam Selbständigkeit Trend" in the Allensbach and GESIS catalogs.
4. Search the Bundeswehr site for "Innere Führung Grenzen des Gehorsams" and the Zentrum Innere Führung's publications.
5. Search the Bundesarchiv for the Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv research series on everyday compliance and refusal.
