# Germany: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Danish, Sorbian, Low German; Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Vietnamese, Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former GDR and West:** state-organized voluntary work (*Subbotnik*-style labor days, the Volkssolidarität) against church-based and club-based service.
- **Rural villages and big cities:** where the fire brigade, the club and the parish still depend on volunteers.
- **Württemberg:** house-rule duties written down and enforced among neighbors.
- **Schleswig-Holstein's coast and Danish minority:** Danish-language associations with their own expectations of members.
- **Catholic Upper Sorbs:** vows and processions kept by families over generations.
- **Turkish, Kurdish, Arab and Kosovar families:** care of parents and money sent home, by generation.
- **Vietnamese families in the East:** former contract workers and their children, between two ideas of filial duty.
- **Jewish communities:** religious obligation, and the post-1990 Russian-speaking members.
- **Conscientious objectors and military families:** two sides of the same national service.
- **Women and men:** who carries care duties, to research, not assume.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering, care and service; thinner for duties inside immigrant families, which need community sources and fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish, Arabic and Vietnamese for family duties; Hebrew and Russian for Jewish community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** duty is tied to the Nazi defense of "only doing one's duty"; handle it as one strand, not the frame for every period. Care and family-conflict data involve private lives.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the *Hildebrandslied* in a parallel edition and Kant's *Grundlegung*, first section; note what each says is owed and to whom.
2. Download the latest Freiwilligensurvey report; pull the East-West and age tables.
3. Search "Pflichtfeuerwehr" with each state's name to see where compulsory fire service still exists and has been used.
4. Search DEAS publications for "intergenerationale Unterstützung" and "Pflege durch Angehörige".
5. Search the Volksbund site and Deutsches Tagebucharchiv catalog for "Pflicht" in letters and memorial texts.
