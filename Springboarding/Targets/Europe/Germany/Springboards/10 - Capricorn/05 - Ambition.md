## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East Germans:** careers broken or rebuilt after 1990, and children raised on that experience.
- **Ruhr working-class families:** the first child at university, and the miner's son as a regional story.
- **Small-town Mittelstand regions (Württemberg, Westphalia):** family firms where ambition is tied to a place and a name.
- **Berlin's founder scene:** venture-funded striving set against the older family-firm model.
- **Danish minority in South Schleswig:** a Jante code to compare with German attitudes to standing out.
- **Turkish-German second and third generations:** from shop owner to professional; the family's ambition and one's own.
- **Russian-German Aussiedler and Syrian arrivals:** degrees not recognized; starting again lower down.
- **Vietnamese families in the East:** school aspirations, to research, not assume.
- **Women:** part-time work and the tax treatment of married couples as limits on ambition, as women themselves describe them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mobility, education and founders; thinner on how ambition is talked about in private, which needs interviews, forums and fiction.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish, Russian, Arabic and Vietnamese for family aspirations; Danish for the minority.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Freytag's novel and other success literature carry antisemitic stereotypes; quote nothing without context. Avoid model-minority framing of any group.
- **Held for review:** none.
