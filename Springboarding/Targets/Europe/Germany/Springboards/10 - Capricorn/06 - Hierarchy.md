# Germany: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German; Sorbian, Danish; Polish, Turkish, Russian, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brandenburg, Mecklenburg and Western Pomerania:** Junker estates, then the 1945 land reform; what remains of manor and village rank.
- **Hamburg and Bremen:** senate families who long refused princely orders and titles; a town elite's own ladder.
- **Cologne and the Rhineland:** the yearly mock court of the carnival; who gets to wear the prince's costume, and at what cost.
- **Danish minority in South Schleswig:** whether its schools and clubs keep Denmark's near-universal *du* beside German *Sie*.
- **Vietnamese families in the East:** age-ranked pronouns at home, German address at work and school.
- **Women and men:** who reaches the top floors of firms, universities and churches; to research, not assume.
- **East Germans:** a self-described "second-class" position after 1990, to test against the elite data.
- **Sorbs:** centuries as a rural people under German lords and burghers.
- **Polish and Eastern European care workers:** in German homes, often missing from the statistics.
- **Turkish-German families:** from guest-worker jobs at the bottom of the factory ladder to later generations.
- **Sinti and Roma:** placed outside the ranks altogether in past policy.
- **Universities and the professions:** titles kept in address and on doors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for stratification and schooling; thinner for everyday rank in speech and seating, which needs ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** German, older print for Prussian and medieval sources; Polish for carers; Turkish and Vietnamese for kin-rank address.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nazi racial hierarchy is a separate, grave subject; keep it distinct from ordinary social rank, and use victims' sources. Carers' work arrangements may be informal; protect identities.
- **Held for review:** none.
