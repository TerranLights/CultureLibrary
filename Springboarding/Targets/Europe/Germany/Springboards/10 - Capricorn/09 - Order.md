## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Karlsruhe and Mannheim:** whether living inside a ruler's geometry shapes local talk of order.
- **The Swabian-Alemannic southwest:** guilds that regulate the disorder of carnival itself.
- **Berlin-Kreuzberg and Hamburg's Schanzenviertel:** some residents defend disorder as freedom, others ask for policing.
- **East Germans:** the ordered GDR estate, then the "baseball-bat years" (*Baseballschlägerjahre*) of the 1990s, as people now name them.
- **Vietnamese Germans in Rostock and Berlin:** targets in 1992; later, late-night shops that stretch closing-time rules.
- **Lower Sorbs:** the *Zapust* Shrovetide custom, with its own fixed order of costume and dance.
- **North Sea coast:** order as dikes and storm warnings; the 1962 flood in family memory.
- **People sleeping rough, squatters and Roma street musicians:** whose presence is reported as disorder, in their own words first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for planning, policing and safety; thin for household order (forums, cleaning influencers, ethnography).
- **Languages needed:** German, early-modern print for the ordinances; Vietnamese for 1992 survivors; Turkish and Arabic for residents of reported "hot spots".
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nazi state's "order" is a grave, separate subject, not part of everyday tidiness. Lichtenhagen survivors first. Riot accounts are contested.
- **Held for review:** none.
