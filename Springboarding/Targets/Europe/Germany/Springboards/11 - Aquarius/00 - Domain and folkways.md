## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hanseatic cities** (Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck): merchant guilds and civic foundations as an older model of joining.
- **Rhineland and Westphalia:** Catholic confraternities, marksmen's clubs and carnival societies as dense associational networks.
- **Swabia and Baden:** Pietist circles and Fastnacht guilds; a different route from faith to group.
- **The former GDR Länder** (Saxony, Thuringia, Brandenburg, Saxony-Anhalt, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania): collectives by decree, then their dissolution after 1990.
- **Ruhr:** mining-town solidarity and a long immigrant mix (Polish from the 1870s, Turkish from the 1960s).
- **Sorbs in Lusatia; Danes and North Frisians in Schleswig; Saterland Frisians:** recognized minorities with their own associations.
- **Sinti and Roma:** family and community structures, documented on their own terms.
- **Turkish-, Kurdish- and Arab-heritage communities; Russian-German resettlers; Jewish communities** (old and post-1990): parallel associational worlds.
- **Church members against the non-religious majority of the east:** different starting points for organized belonging.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in surveys and association counts; thin for the small minorities and immigrant groups' inner life.
- **Languages needed:** German first; Danish, Frisian and Sorbian for minority sources; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for immigrant ones.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Gemeinschaft* and *Volksgemeinschaft* carry Nazi-era use; the folk-custom atlas and older Volkskunde were shaped by that era. Stasi files carry privacy limits. Sinti and Roma history includes genocide; use their own organizations' framing. Association law belongs to Libra and Capricorn. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for Germany's core lands. Bavarian and Austrian material belongs to the separate Austro-Bavaria target and appears only as a neighboring comparison.
- **Held for review:** none.
