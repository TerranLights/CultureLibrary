# Germany: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Latin for early reform writing); Upper and Lower Sorbian; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for immigrant communities; Hebrew for Jewish religious reform. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Prussian core** (Brandenburg, Berlin): the home of reform by officials.
- **Baden and Württemberg:** liberal reform traditions of the 19th century; Wyhl (1975) as a start of rural environmental protest.
- **Saxony and Thuringia:** Reformation heartland, later the 1989 reform movement and 2004 protests against welfare cuts.
- **Wendland and other rural districts:** long fights against projects imposed from outside.
- **Lusatian Sorbs:** reform and "progress" done to them, from schooling policy to mining.
- **Jewish communities:** Reform, Liberal and Orthodox congregations side by side.
- **Muslims of many origins:** reform debates inside Islam (liberal mosques; the state's Islam Conference, 2006 on).
- **Russian-German resettlers:** memories of perestroika shaping views of reform.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; reform is a central word in German history-writing and politics.
- **Languages needed:** German; Latin for early tracts; Hebrew for Jewish reform; Turkish and Arabic for Muslim reform debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reform-school abuse (survivors' accounts first); the Nazi era's misuse of the Lebensreform and youth reform currents; Sorbian village relocation. Law reform as text is for Libra.
