# Germany: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Middle High German, Low German and Kiezdeutsch); Upper and Lower Sorbian, Danish; Turkish, Russian, Polish and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ruhr:** friendships formed underground and in the colliery estates, and what remains after the pits.
- **Cologne and the Rhineland:** pub and carnival-club sociability and networks of favors (Klüngel); how deep such ties run is a question to test.
- **Hamburg and the coast:** test the claim of slow-to-open but lasting friendships against data, without taking it as given.
- **Former GDR:** trusted private circles under surveillance, and the betrayal some later found in their files.
- **Turkish-, Arab- and Kurdish-heritage youth:** friendship talk in mixed neighborhoods; brotherhood words used for friends.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** close-friend norms from the Soviet past meeting German usage.
- **Sorbs and Danes:** friendships inside and across minority lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and literature; thinner for immigrant friendship in its own languages.
- **Languages needed:** German, older German for albums and the friendship cult; Turkish, Russian and Arabic for youth and immigrant sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Stasi files show friends reporting on friends; access is restricted and personal. Expatriate surveys carry outsiders' bias. Favor networks shading into corruption touch law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the RAA database by place (Wittenberg, Jena, Göttingen) to see who signed albums and when.
2. Pull DEAS tables on number of friends by age, sex and East/West.
3. Search DWDS for "Freundeskreis", "Kumpel" and "Klüngel"; compare their regional and period spread.
4. Read the SVR barometer items on cross-group friendship and note the groups compared.
5. Survey expatriate forums by city (Berlin, Hamburg, Cologne, Leipzig) for newcomers' friendship complaints, as data on outsiders' views only.
