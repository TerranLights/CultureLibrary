# Germany: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Berlin districts** (Kreuzberg, Friedrichshain): squats, queer life and subcultures, and their gentrification.
- **Hamburg St. Pauli:** a harbor quarter with its own countercultural football club and nightlife.
- **Leipzig Connewitz and other eastern scenes:** GDR punks under surveillance, then left scenes facing the far right.
- **Münsterland:** Catholic norms and the Anabaptist memory side by side.
- **Württemberg Pietist towns:** strict religious norms and the outsiders they produced (Hesse among them).
- **Turkish- and Arab-heritage youth:** pressure to conform from family and from wider society at once.
- **Russian-German resettlers in free churches:** a conservative faith that departs from the secular majority.
- **Queer people of immigrant origin:** two sets of norms to depart from.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in archives and youth studies; mixed for rural nonconformity and for immigrant scenes.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish, Arabic and Russian for immigrant youth and congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBTQ history includes Nazi persecution; GDR files on punks and dissidents carry personal data. The Querdenker milieu overlaps with conspiracy theories and the far right; do not treat it as one voice. Laws against dissent and on assembly are for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask the Archiv der Jugendkulturen for its finding aids on GDR punk and on Turkish-German hip-hop.
2. Search DWDS for "Querdenker" by decade to trace its turn from praise to protest label.
3. Pull fowid tables on the non-religious by Land and year.
4. Search the Federal Archives and Stasi Records Archive guides for "Bausoldaten" and for files on youth subcultures.
5. Read Lüdtke's *Eigen-Sinn* introduction and list the forms of small refusal it names, as categories for field research.
