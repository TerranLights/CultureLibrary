# Germany: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Germany, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** German (with Middle High German and Low German); Upper and Lower Sorbian, Danish; Turkish, Arabic, Kurdish, Russian and Polish for immigrant communities. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East against West:** the hopes of 1989 and the disappointment after unification, against the West's postwar "economic miracle" story.
- **Lusatia and the Ruhr:** hope for life after coal, set beside job loss; Sorbian villages among those affected.
- **Churchgoers against the non-religious east:** hope with and without a religious frame.
- **Turkish-heritage families by generation:** the guest workers' hope of return against their children's hopes here.
- **Recent refugees** (Syrian, Afghan, Ukrainian): hope on arrival, in their own languages.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** a hope of homecoming and what arrival brought.
- **Jewish communities:** hope after the Shoah and after the post-1990 arrivals.
- **Young people:** climate hope and fear.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; long poll series, theology and philosophy, and archives of 1989.
- **Languages needed:** German, including older German for hymns; Arabic, Turkish, Kurdish, Russian and Ukrainian for newer arrivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2015 "we can do this" refugee slogan is politically divisive; present all sides. Disappointment in the east is often caricatured; let eastern voices speak. Hopelessness and suicide need care.
- **Held for review:** none.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask Allensbach for the New Year "hopes or fears" series, split east/west, from 1990.
2. Read Bloch and Jonas side by side; list their key terms as search words for later press research.
3. Search DWDS for "Zuversicht" and "Zukunftsangst" by decade.
4. Contact the Archiv Bürgerbewegung Leipzig for peace-prayer and Monday-march records.
5. Search the Gotha letter collection for letters from the core German lands, by region of origin.
