## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East and West Germans:** after 1990, each side's sense of being understood by the other is itself an empathy question, measurable in surveys and memoirs.
- **Turkish-German generations:** the second and third generation as go-betweens who read two sets of feelings; compare with first-generation workers' accounts.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** perspective-taking across a gap where they were "Germans" in Kazakhstan and "Russians" in Germany.
- **The Deaf community (DGS users):** reading faces and bodies as a first language; their accounts of hearing people's empathy.
- **Autistic self-advocates:** their own writing against outsiders' claims that they lack empathy.
- **Men and women, by generation:** how men are taught to show or hide feeling-with; test any regional "reserved northerner" image against data rather than assuming it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Intellectual history and lab research are rich; everyday practice of feeling-with needs ethnography, media study and corpora. Keep the history of the word apart from how ordinary people use it.
- **Languages needed:** German; Turkish and Russian for migrant life writing; DGS for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** empathy with perpetrators (the debate over the film *Der Untergang*, 2004); suicide contagion research; testimony of victims' families.
- **Held for review:** none.
