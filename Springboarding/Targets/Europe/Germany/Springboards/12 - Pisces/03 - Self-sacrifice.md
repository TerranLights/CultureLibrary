# Germany: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former GDR vs. the old West:** the GDR's antifascist martyrs (Buchenwald's memorial, Thälmann) against the West's 20 July; two state cults of sacrifice to compare in schoolbooks and monuments.
- **Coastal Frisian, Low German and island communities:** sea rescue and storm-flood dead as their own record of giving one's life for strangers.
- **Catholic and Protestant regions:** saints, vows and Lenten penance against Lutheran wariness of merit; compare the EKD's *7 Wochen Ohne* fast with Catholic practice.
- **Jewish communities:** the Rhineland martyrs of 1096 as their own memory, distinct from national war remembrance.
- **Turkish and Kurdish Germans:** *şehit* and *kurban* in mosque and home; the guest-worker parents' sacrifice in children's accounts.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** Soviet-era *podvig* and Victory Day memory meeting German May 8 remembrance (the Soviet memorial at Treptow).
- **Women and men as caregivers:** whose sacrifice is expected at home and whose in uniform; test any assumed pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for memory, war and caregiving; thinner for everyday parental sacrifice, which needs interviews and life writing.
- **Languages needed:** German; Middle High German and Hebrew for the pre-modern sources; Turkish, Arabic and Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Nazi sacrifice cult (Langemarck, the *Mutterkreuz*, "Blutzeugen"); martyr language in extremist propaganda; families of soldiers killed abroad.
- **Held for review:** none.
