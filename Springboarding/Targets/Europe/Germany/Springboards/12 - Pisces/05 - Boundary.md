## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former GDR and old West:** the Wall, the Stasi and FKK may have drawn different lines on privacy and the body on each side; the tightness data measure them separately.
- **Oder-Neisse divided towns (Görlitz, Frankfurt (Oder), Guben):** a national border running through one urban fabric.
- **Saarland and the Palatinate:** daily commuters into France and Luxembourg; the border as routine.
- **Schleswig's Danish minority and North Frisians:** identity lines kept by schools and clubs, not walls.
- **Sorbian villages in Lusatia:** the line between Sorbian and German speech within families and parishes.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab families:** *mahremiyet*, gendered space and the inner house; compare generations.
- **Observant Jews and Muslims:** purity boundaries in food and ritual (kosher and halal butchers, the *mikveh*).
- **City tenants vs. rural homeowners:** shared stairwells and quiet hours against hedges and property lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Border history and privacy debates are deep; everyday micro-boundaries need ethnography. Old outsider claims of German "privacy" or "reserve" must be tested, not repeated.
- **Languages needed:** German; Danish; Polish; French; Sorbian; Turkish; Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wall deaths and their families; Stasi files of third parties; segregation and "parallel society" rhetoric. Data-protection, tenancy and neighbor law go to Libra; order in the stairwell to Capricorn.
