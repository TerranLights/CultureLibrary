# Germany: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern lands after 1990:** the wave of right-wing violence (Rostock-Lichtenhagen, 1992) and attitudes to GDR state wrongs, against Western crime debates.
- **Neighborhoods labeled "dangerous" (Duisburg-Marxloh, Berlin-Neukölln):** residents' own view against the label.
- **Turkish, Kurdish and Arab families:** how families speak of a son's offense, in their own terms; the contested "clan crime" label as outsiders apply it.
- **Russian-German resettler youth:** prison and street subcultures studied since the 1990s, in their own accounts.
- **Sinti and Roma:** police registration as presumed criminals, before and after 1945; their organizations' accounts of that stigma.
- **Hamburg's St. Pauli and port milieu:** the underworld romanticized in film and song.
- **Rural Hunsrück and Eifel:** bandit legends and village shaming against city anonymity.
- **Business and political elites:** white-collar wrongs and how far the public condemns them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Victimization surveys, criminology and crime fiction are deep; the line between this concept and law needs care.
- **Languages needed:** German; Rotwelsch for historical sources; Turkish; Arabic; Russian.
