# Germany: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Sea and Elbe coasts:** storm-flood memory (Hamburg, 1962) and dike work; check how they shape what coastal people fear.
- **Ahr valley and other flood districts:** fear after 2021, and trust in warnings.
- **Former GDR and old West:** fear of the state before 1989 and fear of decline after it; survey splits on fear of strangers.
- **Wendland and other anti-nuclear districts:** a local culture of dread and protest.
- **Turkish Germans after Solingen (1993) and the NSU killings:** fear of being targeted, in community sources.
- **Jewish communities:** security at synagogues and fear after attacks.
- **Russian-German resettlers:** worries about status and belonging, in their own surveys and press.
- **Children and women:** fear of public space at night; child-scaring figures such as Knecht Ruprecht, set beside the Austro-Bavarian Krampus as a neighboring comparison.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich. Fear surveys, histories of fear and disaster records are deep; everyday fear talk needs corpora and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** German; Sorbian; Turkish; Arabic; Russian; Hebrew.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "German Angst" as a national stereotype (record it as a claim only); far-right exploitation of fear; victims of racist and antisemitic attacks.
- **Held for review:** none.
