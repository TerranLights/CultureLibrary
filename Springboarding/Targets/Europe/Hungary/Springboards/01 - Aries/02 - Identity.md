# Hungary: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian and its dialects; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac, Burgenland Croatian); Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Székelys against Hungarians of Kolozsvár and the Partium (Nagyvárad):** check whether "Székely" stands before or beside "Hungarian" and how city and village answers differ.
- **Jász and Kun descendants (Jászberény, Karcag, Kiskunhalas):** check whether Jász or Cuman heritage is claimed as a layer of identity and in which settings.
- **Ormánság and Őrség villages:** check local identity labels in small regions against county or national ones.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** check which self-description each tradition used and how it shifted after 1944 and 1989.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check census answers against in-group naming, group by group.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna against Buda-hill Germans:** check whether dialect or region carries more of the self-label.
- **Greek Catholics of the Nyírség and Rusyns:** check where rite and descent overlap or part.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** check how emigrant press frames "Hungarian" for contrast; findings apply from the nation outward.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: identity is a heavily studied theme in Hungarian sociology and minority studies.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian; German, Croatian, Serbian and Slovak for each community's press; Romani and Beash for Roma self-labels.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Trianon, 1944 and origin claims (Hun descent, contested fringe theories) are contested; the Romanian majority's sources on Transylvania are comparison only.
- **Held for review:** the dual-citizenship and kin-state status provisions (2001, 2010) and the 2011 Fundamental Law's preamble, deferred to Libra.
