# Hungary: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian and its dialects; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** how starting is treated: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Székelys against Great Plain Hungarians:** check which origin legend each tells first (Hun descent through Csaba, or Árpád's conquest) and where it is taught.
- **Jász and Kun communities:** check their own arrival stories (13th century and later) beside the national conquest story.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and the Buda hills:** check 18th-century arrival memory and the second "beginning" after 1946.
- **Serbs and Croats of the south:** check migration-founding memory (1690 for Serbs; Bunjevac and Šokac arrival stories) as each tells it.
- **Hungarians of the Partium and Kolozsvár:** check what 1920 and 1990 mean as endings or new starts in local memory, read as each community's claim.
- **Roma (Romungro, Vlach Roma, Boyash):** check each group's own arrival and settlement stories in the European record, without speculating on remoter origins.
- **Jewish communities:** check how 1867 emancipation and the 1868–69 split are dated as beginnings by Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo writers.
- **Catholic and Reformed families:** check New Year and christening customs in parish and church-district records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: origin and calendar customs are among the best-documented fields of Hungarian ethnography and historiography.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the chronicles and charters; Hungarian; Serbian for the Migration records; German for settlement memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin theories (Hun, Scythian, "Sumerian") range from mainstream to fringe and are politically charged; the Romanian and Hungarian accounts of Transylvania's settlement are rival claims, to be presented as claims.
- **Held for review:** the Holy Crown doctrine as constitutional theory, deferred to Capricorn.
