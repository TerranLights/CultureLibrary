# Hungary: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian and its dialects; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Great Plain herding and farming villages against Transdanubian vine-growing villages (Balaton, Sopron):** check feud and reconciliation customs and who stepped in to calm a quarrel.
- **Székely and Mezőség villages:** check curse ballads and curse formulas as sung and spoken, and whether women's and men's forms differ.
- **Budapest against Miskolc and Ózd:** check how deindustrialization is linked to anger in local press and surveys.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash:** check how quarrels are settled and who mediates, group by group; check also the communities' responses to the 2008–09 attacks, as they tell them.
- **Reformed, Catholic and Lutheran preaching:** check sermons and catechisms on wrath and forgiveness, each tradition separately.
- **Danube Swabians and Slovaks:** check dialect anger words and swearing as markers of the home language.
- **Jewish communities:** check rabbinic responsa and community press on quarrels within congregations, Neolog and Orthodox apart.
- **Hungarians of Kolozsvár and Marosvásárhely:** check how inter-community anger (for example March 1990) is told by each side, presented as accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in literature, linguistics and folklore; thinner in adult survey data on anger.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian; Latin for the 1514 sources; Romani for in-group mediation; German and Slovak for dialect swearing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2006, the 2008–09 anti-Roma attacks and the Marosvásárhely clashes are raw and contested; football violence links to extremist groups; victims are private persons.
- **Held for review:** criminal law on incitement and hate speech, and the legal side of dueling, deferred to Libra.
