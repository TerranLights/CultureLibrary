# Hungary: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian, Romani, Beash, German, Slovak, Croatian, Serbian, Romanian, Rusyn, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest flat-dwellers (prewar blocks, *lakótelep* estates) and village homes with a *tisztaszoba*:** check whether a room kept for show survives, and in which places it has gone.
- **Göcsej and Őrség against the Hortobágy and Kiskunság:** compare chests, shelves and stores, since the open-air museums sort houses by region.
- **Kalotaszeg and the Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely villages):** check the piled bed (*vetett ágy*) and wall-plate displays in each, and who keeps them up.
- **Palóc villages (Hollókő, Balassagyarmat area):** check dowry chests and their contents in local museum inventories.
- **Jewish households (historical, Budapest and the provinces):** separate ritual objects, family keepsakes and what 1944 records list, and read survivors' own accounts beside them.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash families:** check which belongings carry family memory, from community-made oral history rather than outside reports.
- **Danube Swabians, Békés Slovaks, Šokac Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Méhkerék, Rusyns of Komlóska:** compare the dowry chest's contents and the house icon or saint's picture by group.
- **Reformed and Catholic villages of the Nyírség and Szatmár:** check whether religion changes what is displayed in the best room.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village interiors and dowry goods; mixed for present-day belongings; online resale is searchable but unsorted.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian for most; German for Swabian museum labels; older Hungarian and Latin for Transylvanian inventories; Romani and Beash for in-group terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1944 asset forms name real families and their losses; *lomtalanítás* coverage often targets Roma collectors; hoarding cases involve living people's health.
- **Held for review:** who may keep a found object, and the rules of pawn, are legal matters for Libra.
