## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest renters against owner-occupiers in Szabolcs and Borsod villages:** check whether self-provisioning and debt change the measure of want.
- **The Kiskunság and the old Ottoman-era market towns:** compare town memory of protection bought collectively with later family strategies.
- **Former mining and steel towns (Komló, Ózd, Tatabánya):** check how pensions and early retirement figure in local accounts after closures.
- **Kolontár and Devecser residents:** check survivors' accounts against official ones, separately.
- **Székely villages and Kalotaszeg:** check how remittances and seasonal work abroad enter household plans, using Transylvanian Hungarian studies.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash households:** check access to credit and the role of kin lending, from studies that let residents speak.
- **Jewish communities (Neolog, Orthodox) before and after 1944:** compare mutual-aid institutions, from community records.
- **Germans of Baranya, Croats of the Drava region, Serbs of Battonya, Slovaks of Szarvas:** check preserving, stores and burial societies village by village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics (SILC, pensions, ESS) and economic history; mixed for household money talk; thin for Rusyn and Beash households specifically.
- **Languages needed:** burial-society minute books may be in Hebrew or Yiddish; the market towns' early protocols mix Latin and Hungarian, with Ottoman Turkish on the occupiers' side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** usury studies and civil-guard patrols touch Roma communities and have fed stereotype: name residents' own accounts and avoid group verdicts; red-mud victims are living people with claims.
- **Held for review:** pension and social-insurance statutes, to Libra; national security and police are outside this concept.
