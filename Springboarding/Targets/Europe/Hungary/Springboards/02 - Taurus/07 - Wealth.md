## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's Buda hills (Rózsadomb) against Józsefváros and the Szabolcs villages:** check wealth survey and census data at the finest level available.
- **The Sárköz and Matyó lands against the Ormánság:** check how local studies date and explain periods of peasant prosperity and decline.
- **Hortobágy and Hajdúság herding towns:** check how herds counted as wealth in town records, and what replaced them.
- **Tokaj, Villány and Badacsony wine families:** compare old and new vineyard fortunes after 1990.
- **Transylvanian magnate families and Székely free peasants:** check estate records against commons-based household wealth.
- **Greek-descended and Armenian-descended families:** check how merchant histories are kept in family and church archives.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jewish communities:** check philanthropy records and community wealth separately, using community archives; avoid outside generalizations.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check each group's own reckoning of what counts as wealth and how display is read inside the group.
- **Danube Swabian, Slovak, Croatian, Serbian and Romanian villages:** compare village landholding sizes in the landowner registers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for aristocratic and landed wealth, literature and present-day surveys; mixed for merchant diasporas; thin for informal wealth.
- **Languages needed:** German for Esterházy and Swabian records; Greek and Armenian (with Latin) for merchant company papers; Yiddish and Hebrew for community philanthropy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Jewish wealth was the target of antisemitic tropes and of 1938–44 seizures: study it from community sources and name no group trait; Roma and poverty likewise; "oligarch" lists are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** taxation and inheritance law, to Libra; the left-right spending figure is under Ownership.
