## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against the Great Plain towns (Kecskemét, Hódmezővásárhely, Szeged):** check whether the atlases and BUSZI treat capital and plains speech as separate norms.
- **Palóc villages of Nógrád and Heves:** compare village speech recordings with the standard taught in their schools.
- **Székely Land (Csík, Udvarhely, Háromszék) and Kalotaszeg:** separate dialect from the effects of schooling in a Romanian-majority state.
- **Ormánság and the Őrség:** check small-village sources on visiting and schooling.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash communities:** take each group's home language and schooling record separately; Romani and Beash are distinct languages.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks of Békés and Pilis, Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac), Serbs, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns of Komlóska:** compare home language against the nationality school's language.
- **Reformed, Catholic, Lutheran, Unitarian and Greek Catholic schooling traditions:** check each church's school network as a separate route of literacy.
- **Jewish learning traditions (Neolog, Orthodox, Status Quo):** compare the Budapest Rabbinical Seminary with yeshiva and *heder* records in the northeast.
- **Jász and Kun towns; Armenian-descended Hungarians of Szamosújvár:** check local histories for speech remnants and community schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialectology, school statistics and folk oratory; mixed for neighbor contact; thin for daily talk in Rusyn and the smaller nationalities.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the early grammars; Romanian for contact studies; German, Slovak, Croatian and Serbian for nationality media; Romani and Beash for home speech.
- **Lands:** no scope file yet; the tracker carries Romania, read here as Transylvania's Hungarians and the Székely Land (Moldavian Csángó as comparison only), a working reading the author still confirms. The exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** segregated Roma schooling; Hungarian-language education in Romania; the *rovás* revival, which some tie to contested origin theories; the 1944 deportations, which ended many Jewish learning traditions.
- **Held for review:** language-rights statutes (the nationalities act, the sign-language act) to Libra; what those statutes are for, to Capricorn.
