# Hungary: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, plus the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian and its regional speech; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest offices against small-town Northern Hungary (Miskolc, Eger, Ózd):** check whether workplace-meeting and feedback norms recorded in management studies hold outside the capital.
- **Catholic and Reformed congregations (Eger, Debrecen, Sárospatak):** compare preaching manuals and greeting formulas tied to each church.
- **Greek Catholics of Hajdúdorog and the Nyírség:** check when liturgy moved into Hungarian and what that changed in parish talk.
- **Székely villages and the Mezőség:** compare dance-floor rhymes and verbal sparring as recorded by Kolozsvár folklorists.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash speakers:** check which language each uses with outsiders, at home and in church, from in-group sources.
- **Deaf communities in Budapest, Vác and Debrecen:** check regional sign variants before treating Hungarian Sign Language as one.
- **Swabians of the Buda hills and Baranya, Slovaks of Tótkomlós, Croats of the Drava, Romanians of Méhkerék:** compare when each switches to Hungarian in public.
- **Historical Jewish letter-writers (Neolog Pest; Orthodox Sátoraljaújhely and Mátészalka):** compare German, Yiddish and Hungarian choice by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corpora, pragmatics and censorship history; mixed for gesture and silence; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Deaf regional variants.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century Hungarian for the letters; Romani for community radio; Romanian for the linguistic-landscape studies; German and Yiddish for Jewish family correspondence.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** oral histories of 1956 and of informing (*besúgás*) touch living people; hate speech against Roma and Jews in public debate.
- **Held for review:** free-speech and press statutes to Libra; what speech limits are for, to Capricorn.
