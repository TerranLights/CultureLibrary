## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buda and Pest districts:** check which side's local histories claim which traits for themselves, and treat each as a claim.
- **Worker-peasant villages of the Nyírség and Borsod:** compare factory commuters' testimony with that of farm-only households in the same village.
- **Kolozsvár and the Székely towns:** separate Hungarian-majority Székely settings from minority Kolozsvár when reading dual-citizenship studies.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and the Buda hills:** check German and Hungarian identity answers across the census years.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash respondents:** check how each answered two-nationality census questions, and whether forms let them.
- **Romanians of Gyula and Méhkerék, Serbs of Battonya, Slovaks of Békéscsaba:** compare church language with home language in each.
- **Jewish Hungarians of several traditions:** compare dual-belonging answers by Neolog, Orthodox and secular respondents.
- **Unitarians and Reformed of Transylvania:** check how each church's press handles the two-country tie.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literary doubles, minority identity surveys and the dualism era; mixed for folk paired-opposites; thin for Roma and Rusyn dual-belonging in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish and Latin for dual-taxation records; German for Swabian identity studies; Romanian for mixed-marriage and Transylvanian press sources; Yiddish for historical Jewish identity writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** dual citizenship and the 1920 border are politically charged in both countries; informer files and disclosures involve named families; Jewish and Roma identity questions after 1944.
