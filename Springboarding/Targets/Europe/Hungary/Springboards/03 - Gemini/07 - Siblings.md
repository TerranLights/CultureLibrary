# Hungary: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, read with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc and Székely kin terms included); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ormánság and Sárköz villages:** compare parish registers on family size with what the press and sermons said about them.
- **Palóc and Matyó joint households:** check when brothers' shared households broke up, and what records date it.
- **Croatian Šokac of Mohács and Serbs of the Danube villages:** compare *zadruga* splitting with Hungarian household partition.
- **Swabian villages of Tolna and Baranya:** check the fate of non-inheriting siblings, from emigration to trades.
- **Székely Land and Kalotaszeg families:** check which child stayed with the parents, from local family studies.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash households:** check older sisters' care of younger children through each group's own testimony.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jewish families (historical):** compare surviving siblings' accounts after 1944 by region.
- **Budapest only children and step-families:** check recent survey data on half- and step-siblings by settlement size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kinship terms, household history and one-child demography; mixed for adult sibling ties today; thin for Rusyn and Beash sibling customs.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Árpád-age chronicles; German for Swabian inheritance studies; Croatian and Serbian for *zadruga* sources; Hungarian and Yiddish for survivor testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the one-child debate carried moral and ethnic blame, so keep it as claims; survivor testimonies involve living families; Roma family size is a stigmatized theme in public talk.
- **Held for review:** inheritance rules to Libra; family care to Cancer; loyalty and inheritance quarrels to Scorpio.
