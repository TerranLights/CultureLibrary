# Hungary: Home and family, the domain and its folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, together with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land (a working reading; see Flags). **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest flats against Great Plain farmsteads (*tanya*) near Kecskemét and Szeged:** check household size and the distance between kin in each settlement form.
- **Palóc villages (Hollókő, Nógrád) and the Őrség's scattered *szer* hamlets:** compare joint-family records with the layout of separate house clusters.
- **Székely Land (Csík, Udvarhely, Háromszék), Kalotaszeg and the Mezőség:** check how the *had* and the gate frame kin, apart from the Romanian-majority setting around them.
- **Nyírség *bokortanya* clusters and Szatmár villages:** check settlement studies for kin-grouped farmsteads.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash families:** take each group's words for house and kin from its own speakers, not one "Roma family" label.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna, Slovaks of Békés, Croats of Baja, Serbs of Szentendre, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns of Komlóska:** compare house forms and family feasts (the Serbian *slava*) village by village.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran, Unitarian and Greek Catholic households:** read each church's teaching on the home separately.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jewish families:** separate Budapest from northeastern small towns in pre-1944 records.
- **Jász and Kun towns; Armenian-descended families of Szamosújvár and Erzsébetváros:** check local histories for distinct house and family records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk architecture, census households and historical demography; mixed for home life in minority languages; thin for Rusyn and Boyash family records.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian throughout; German for Swabian and Hajnal-line debates; Romanian for Transylvanian census and village studies; Latin for early modern household lists.
- **Lands:** no scope file and no settled lands yet; the carried Romania is read here as the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land (Moldavian Csángó for comparison only), a working reading the author still confirms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homes emptied by the 1944 deportations and the 1946–48 expulsion of Germans; Roma housing segregation; property restitution after 1989.
- **Held for review:** law on family and housing goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); nothing else on the home was set aside here.
