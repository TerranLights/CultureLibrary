# Hungary: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Great Plain market towns (Kecskemét, Nagykőrös):** check kin contact and household size in TÁRKI data by settlement type.
- **Palóc villages and the Matyó lands:** compare joint-household accounts with later single-family records.
- **Székely Land (Székelyudvarhely, Csík) against Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad families:** check where *nem* and *ág* survive as names and where only the urban household is used.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check each group's kin terms and naming customs from its own speakers.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks of Tótkomlós, Croats (Bunjevac of Baja, Šokac of Mohács), Serbs, Romanians of Méhkerék, Rusyns:** compare godparent and patron-feast customs village by village.
- **Reformed, Lutheran, Unitarian, Catholic and Greek Catholic families:** check each church's registers for how godparents and step-kin are recorded.
- **Jewish families (Neolog, Orthodox, Status Quo):** separate pre-1944 genealogies from postwar family reconstruction.
- **Jász and Kun towns; the Armenian-descended families of Szamosújvár:** check whether family histories carry a distinct descent story.
- **Families split between Hungary and Transylvania:** check letters and visits across the border as one kin network.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy, noble lines and household surveys; mixed for in-law and step-family ties; thin for Rusyn and Beash kin terms.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for medieval clan records; German for Swabian family books; Serbian for *slava* studies; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** state family policy is politically charged, so official and independent sources should be read side by side; Holocaust gaps in family records; adoption records closed by privacy rules.
- **Held for review:** family-law texts on adoption and inheritance belong to Libra; what kin owe one another under law, to Capricorn.
