# Hungary: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against small towns of the Great Plain:** check how long a newcomer stays a *gyüttment* in local usage.
- **Székelys of Csík and Háromszék against Hungarians of Kolozsvár and the Partium:** check which symbols and words each uses for "us".
- **Kalotaszeg and the Mezőség:** compare villages in Hungarian-majority and mixed settings for who counts as local.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check each group's own word for "our people" and what it covers.
- **Danube Swabians, Bukovina Székelys and Hungarians moved from southern Slovakia (*felvidéki áttelepítettek*) in the same Tolna and Baranya villages:** compare the postwar settlers' and the remaining residents' accounts.
- **Slovaks, Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac), Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check the nationality institutes' surveys for double belonging.
- **Greek Catholics of Hajdúdorog and the Nyírség:** separate rite from language as the marker in church records.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews; Unitarians of Kolozsvár:** check how each community's press framed membership.
- **Jász and Kun towns:** check how town festivals use the old settler identity.
- **Moldavian Csángó (comparison only):** use as a contrast case for belonging argued from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for minority-identity surveys and Transylvanian demography; mixed for village newcomers; thin for Rusyn and Beash self-description.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for Transylvanian surveys; Slovak, Croatian and Romanian for the institutes' own work; Latin for *hospes* charters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who belongs to the nation is political in Hungary and Romania alike; Roma exclusion; Jewish belonging before and after 1944; Székely symbols in Romanian public space.
- **Held for review:** naturalization and citizenship rules go to Libra; the dispute over what citizenship is for, to Capricorn.
