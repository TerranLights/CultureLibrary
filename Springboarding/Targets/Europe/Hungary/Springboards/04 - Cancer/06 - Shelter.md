# Hungary: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's night shelters against small Great Plain towns:** check where services exist at all and where kin take people in instead.
- **Sopron and the western border in 1989 against Szeged and the South in 1991–95:** compare how border towns received two waves of refugees.
- **Székely Land and Kolozsvár families who fled in 1987–89:** check their later accounts of reception in Hungary.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash families in Bihar and Borsod:** check each group's account of overcrowded housing and doubling up.
- **Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac) and Serbs of the South:** check parish and church records of kin taken in from across the border in 1991–95.
- **Danube Swabians:** check whether postwar expellees' memoirs name who sheltered them on the way.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Greek Catholic charities:** compare which church runs which shelters.
- **Jewish community welfare (Neolog, Orthodox) in 1944 and today:** check children's homes and soup kitchens in the records.
- **Ormánság and Nyírség villages:** check how homeless people in small villages are counted, or missed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1989, the siege and Budapest homelessness services; mixed for 1991–95 and 2015; thin for small-village homelessness.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Habans and East Germans of 1989; Polish for 1939–44; Croatian and Serbian for 1991–95; Romanian for Transylvanian flight.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asylum since 2015 is politically charged in Hungary, so state and NGO accounts should be read side by side; homelessness and public-space rules; women's shelter locations are confidential.
- **Held for review:** asylum and anti-rough-sleeping statutes belong to Libra; nothing on giving shelter itself was set aside.
