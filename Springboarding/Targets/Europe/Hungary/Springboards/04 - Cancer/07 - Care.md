# Hungary: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest care homes against Great Plain farmstead elders:** check where *tanyagondnok* service replaces family visits.
- **Small villages of the Őrség and Göcsej:** compare ageing-village care networks with church-run homes.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy) and the Mezőség:** check who cares for elders when daughters work in Hungary or Austria.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash households:** check each group's own account of caring for the sick, apart from health-service reports.
- **Danube Swabians and Slovaks:** check deaconess, sister and parish nursing in German and Slovak villages.
- **Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check whether church-linked charities serve them in their own language.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian church-run social services:** compare growth of church-run homes since 2010 with public ones.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jewish welfare (*Chevra Kadisha* hospitals, old-age homes):** check pre-1944 and present-day records separately.
- **Families with disabled children in Pécs, Győr and Debrecen:** compare access to Pető-style services outside Budapest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health statistics, nursing orders and folk medicine; mixed for family carers; thin for Rusyn and Beash care practices.
- **Languages needed:** German for live-in care in Austria and Swabian nursing; Latin for medieval hagiography; Romanian for Transylvanian training records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informal payments to medical staff; conditions in residential institutions for disabled people; patient records are closed.
- **Held for review:** the carer's allowance as legislation goes to Libra; what duty adult children owe parents, to Capricorn.
