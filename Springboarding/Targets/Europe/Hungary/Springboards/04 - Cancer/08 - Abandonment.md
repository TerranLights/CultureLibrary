# Hungary: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; the Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Cserehát, Zemplén and the Ormánság:** check whether village depopulation is described as being left or as choosing to leave.
- **Great Plain emigrant villages (Szabolcs, Bereg) of 1880–1914:** compare letters home with parish notes on families left behind.
- **Székely Land and the Mezőség:** separate seasonal work in Hungary from longer stays in Western Europe in children's accounts.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash families:** check each group's account of children taken into care, alongside agency figures.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna:** compare *málenkij robot* families with families of the 1946–48 expulsions.
- **Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check emigration-era parish records for spouses left behind.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jewish families:** check child survivors' testimonies and postwar children's homes separately.
- **Catholic and Reformed children's homes:** compare how each church recorded children it took in.
- **Jász and Kun towns:** check local museums for foundling and foster records from the asylum system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emigration history, 1944–46 testimony and state-care statistics; mixed for left-behind children; thin for elders' own accounts of being left.
- **Languages needed:** English for American emigrant records; German and Russian for the deportations; Yiddish for *agune* cases; Romanian for Transylvanian migration research.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma children in care; adoption and child-welfare files are closed; the 2004 referendum and Trianon memory are contested ground; ÁBTL files name living people.
- **Held for review:** custody, child-support and adoption statutes go to Libra; whether leaving aging parents breaks a duty, to Capricorn.
