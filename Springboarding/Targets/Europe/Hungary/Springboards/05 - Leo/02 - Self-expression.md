# Hungary: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, plus the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian and its dialects (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (incl. Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian as spoken in Hungary; Rusyn. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Great Plain small towns (Jászberény, Karcag, Makó):** check how "what people will say" is named in each in interview studies.
- **Matyó (Mezőkövesd), Kalocsa and Sárköz costume villages:** compare dress as personal choice and as village code in costume studies.
- **Palóc villages of Nógrád and Heves:** check lament and song recordings for who may voice grief and how.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely) and Kolozsvár:** separate public display of Hungarian symbols in Romanian space from private expression.
- **Nagyvárad and the Partium:** check samizdat and church records for the room for dissent there.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare song, dress and speech as self-statement within each group, from each group's own writers.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks, Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac), Serbs, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** check minority-language press for personal voice; Rusyn material is thin.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian congregations; Orthodox and Neolog Jews:** compare teaching on modest dress and plain speech.
- **Kádár-era youth against post-1989 cohorts:** read subculture studies by decade.
- **LGBTQ Hungarians in Budapest and in villages:** check sources separately; rural material is scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for samizdat, pop culture and folk song; mixed for everyday emotional display; thin for Rusyn and Beash self-writing.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for Partium and Székely records; German for Swabian literature; Lovari for Roma poetry.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** irredentist symbols, Pride events and Roma self-display are all politically charged, so record each side's words; state-security files name private people.
- **Held for review:** the law on speech, assembly and media goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); nothing on expression was set aside beyond that.
