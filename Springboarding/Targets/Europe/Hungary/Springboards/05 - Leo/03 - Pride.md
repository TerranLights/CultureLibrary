# Hungary: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, and the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani and Beash; German of the Danube Swabians; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish for the older Jewish record. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Jász, Kun and Hajdú towns (Jászberény, Karcag, Hajdúböszörmény):** check how each town's founding status is retold as local pride.
- **Debrecen's *cívis* burghers against Budapest:** compare which achievements each city puts on its walls and in its festivals.
- **Szeged and the South:** check paprika, flood recovery and university as civic pride themes in local press.
- **Palóc lands (Hollókő) and the Őrség:** separate heritage-tourism pride from residents' own accounts.
- **Székely Land (Sepsiszentgyörgy, Marosvásárhely), Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad:** compare minority pride in mixed and Hungarian-majority towns.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check which symbols each group adopts, and whether pan-Roma ones are used at all.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** compare nationality festivals; Rusyn sources are sparse, so start with the Rusyn self-government.
- **Neolog Jews before 1944 and today:** check how pre-1944 patriotic pride and the persecution that followed are treated in the community's press.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian clergy:** compare sermons on pride as sin against national-church pride.
- **LGBTQ Hungarians:** separate Budapest organizations from small-town experience.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, literature and sport; mixed for pride in work; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended community pride.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and early Hungarian for the codices; Romanian for Kolozsvár festival coverage; Romani for Roma organizations' own texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride overlaps with irredentism and Trianon grievance; complaints of anti-Roma and antisemitic chants at matches are a source to log, not a trait; the counting of "Hungarian" laureates is contested.
- **Held for review:** law on national symbols and on LGBTQ content goes to Libra and Capricorn; no other pointer on pride was put aside.
