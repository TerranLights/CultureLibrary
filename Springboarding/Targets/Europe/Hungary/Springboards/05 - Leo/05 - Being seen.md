# Hungary: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe; the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land are included. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely varieties); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian of Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish (pre-1944). **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest housing estates against small Great Plain farmsteads (*tanya*):** check how isolation and being watched by neighbors are each described.
- **Szeged, Eger and Sopron promenades against Kecskemét's:** compare when each town's *korzó* rose and faded, from local press.
- **Northern Hungary (Miskolc, the Palóc villages) and the Ormánság:** check how depopulated villages describe being forgotten by the state.
- **Székely Land (Marosvásárhely, Csíkszereda) and the Partium:** compare visible Hungarian signs in mixed and Hungarian-majority towns.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check each group's count gap and media presence separately; Boyash data are thin.
- **Danube Swabians after 1946 and Rusyns after 1945:** check memoirs for keeping a minority identity out of sight.
- **Slovaks, Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac), Serbs and Romanians of Hungary:** compare nationality broadcasting hours and audiences.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** separate 1944 forced visibility from later choices about being visible.
- **Unitarians and Greek Catholics:** check whether national histories mention them at all.
- **Older people living alone and the unhoused:** check who visits them and how they are counted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveillance, media and photography; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting; thin for Rusyn and Boyash visibility.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the 1664 portrait book; Romanian for Székely signage disputes; Romani for Roma media's own output.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ÁBTL files name living people and have access rules; census ethnicity counts are disputed; *rovás* signs and Roma media portrayals are politically charged.
- **Held for review:** privacy and data-protection law go to Libra; secrecy and surveillance as themes are shared with Scorpio; nothing more on being seen was set aside.
