# Hungary: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, taken with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (with Palóc, Great Plain and Székely usage); Romani and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian in Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish for the record before 1944. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's eighth district (Józsefváros) against Szabolcs-Szatmár villages:** check how poverty and respect are spoken about by residents in each.
- **Borsod (Miskolc, Ózd) and the Ormánság:** compare public-works participants' own accounts with official reports.
- **Szeged, Pécs and Győr care homes:** check inspection reports by region.
- **Székely Land (Székelyudvarhely, Csík) and Kalotaszeg:** compare elder care in villages left by the young.
- **Nagyvárad and the Partium:** check church-run care and charity records.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** separate experiences of segregated housing, schooling and services by group and by county.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** check nationality press for dignity claims tied to language rights; Rusyn sources are scarce.
- **Jewish forced labor servicemen and deportees:** compare testimonies from Budapest and the provinces, and across Neolog and Orthodox families.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian charities:** compare how each frames help without shaming.
- **Disabled people in institutions against those living at home:** check whose voices appear in the studies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for social policy, Holocaust testimony and institutional reports; mixed for everyday courtesy; thin for Rusyn and Beash experience.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for Transylvanian elder-care records; German for Swabian memoirs of expulsion; Yiddish for some pre-1944 community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** institutional abuse cases involve living people; Roma segregation and public-works policy are politically charged; Holocaust testimony carries family claims.
- **Held for review:** dignity in the constitution and the courts goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); assisted dying and the law, likewise. No other dignity pointer was held.
