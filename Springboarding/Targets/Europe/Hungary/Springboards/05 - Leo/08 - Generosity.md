# Hungary: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, alongside the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely forms); Romani and Beash; the Swabians' German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian (in Hungary); Rusyn; Yiddish for Jewish charity before 1944. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest donors and foundations against village churches in the Great Plain (Hajdúság, Kiskunság):** check formal against informal giving in each.
- **Transdanubian wine villages (Balaton uplands, Villány) and Swabian Baranya:** compare harvest and Martinmas sharing customs.
- **Northern Hungary (Eger, Miskolc, Tokaj) and Szatmár:** check where aid flows go in poorer counties and who runs them.
- **Székely Land (Csík, Háromszék) and Kalotaszeg:** compare *kaláka* records and church-building collections.
- **Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad:** check how urban Hungarian charities in Romania are funded.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare feast-giving, lending and sharing within each group.
- **Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check church-community charity and feast customs per nationality; Rusyn material is scarce.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** compare charity societies and postwar aid.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran, Unitarian and Greek Catholic congregations:** compare collections and tithing practices.
- **Hungarians in Hungary against those in Transylvania:** check giving across the border in both directions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for charity organizations, folk hospitality and wedding customs; mixed for giving amounts; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended giving.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and older Hungarian for wills; Romanian for Transylvanian work-bee studies; Yiddish and English for JDC files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** aid to Roma communities can be framed as shaming; state grants across the border are politically disputed; Holocaust-era property and aid claims are tied to restitution.
- **Held for review:** tax law on donations goes to Libra; reciprocity as obligation belongs to Libra too; no other generosity pointer was set aside.
