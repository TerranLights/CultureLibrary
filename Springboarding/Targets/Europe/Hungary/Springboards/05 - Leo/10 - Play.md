## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest housing-estate playgrounds against Great Plain farmstead children (Kiskunság, Szeged area):** check the time and space children have for unsupervised play.
- **Palóc and Matyó villages:** compare game-song collections for local variants.
- **Transdanubia (Göcsej, Őrség, Balaton shore):** check summer play by the lake against inland villages.
- **Székely Land (Csík, Udvarhely) and the Mezőség:** compare children's game collections across Hungarian and mixed villages.
- **Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad schools:** check school sport and play for Hungarian-language pupils.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash children:** sources on their games are scarce; look for school ethnographies and Romani-language teaching materials, not general surveys.
- **Danube Swabian skittle alleys, Slovak and Croatian (Šokac) carnival games, Serbian and Romanian children's rhymes, Rusyn village play:** read each in its own collections.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jewish families:** compare Purim and Hanukkah play across communities.
- **Catholic, Reformed and Lutheran church youth groups:** check what games each allowed on Sundays.
- **Older adults:** check card clubs and pensioners' clubs for adult play.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk games, toys and sport; mixed for adult play and gaming; thin for Roma, Rusyn and Beash children's games.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and older Hungarian for school drama; German for Swabian games; Romanian for Transylvanian village collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's records need care; gaming and gambling overlap; "folk" collections may omit Roma play.
- **Held for review:** gambling law and school-curriculum law go to Libra; competition as such stays with Aries; no other play pointer was set aside.
