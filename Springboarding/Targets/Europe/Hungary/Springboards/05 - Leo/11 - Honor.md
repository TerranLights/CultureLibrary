*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, read with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely usage); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian in Hungary; Rusyn; Yiddish for the community record before the war. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest officer and gentry families against Great Plain farmers (Hódmezővásárhely, Kecskemét):** check whose honor codes appear in memoirs and court records.
- **Jász, Kun and Hajdú towns:** compare honor tied to collective privileges with that of serf villages.
- **Transdanubia (Győr, Sopron, Zala's Göcsej):** check nobility registers against village honor customs.
- **Northern Hungary (Eger, Tokaj, Palóc villages):** compare the siege-of-Eger tradition with everyday honor talk.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely, Sepsiszentgyörgy) and Marosvásárhely:** separate rank-based honor from village reputation.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and Arad:** check how commemorations are organized by Hungarian bodies under Romanian rule.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare honor and respect vocabularies; do not carry one group's terms to another.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks, Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac), Serbs, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** check each community's war memorials and church honors; Rusyn records are few.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** compare how war service and community office were honored.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran, Unitarian and Greek Catholic communities:** check what each church honors in sermons and memorials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nobility, military and commemoration; mixed for everyday honor; thin for Rusyn and Beash codes.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor tied to women's conduct, honor-based violence, and the 1944 persecution of decorated Jewish veterans are sensitive; nobility nostalgia carries politics.
