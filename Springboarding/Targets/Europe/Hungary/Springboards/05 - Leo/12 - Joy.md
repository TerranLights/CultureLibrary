# Hungary: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, together with the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (with Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian of the Gyula area; Rusyn; Yiddish (pre-war). **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's seventh and eighth districts against Great Plain towns (Kecskemét, Szolnok):** check how nightlife and town festivals each frame a good time.
- **Szeged and the Tisza South:** compare open-air theater audiences with village feasts nearby.
- **Transdanubia (Balaton uplands, Villány, Sopron's wine hills):** check harvest and summer festivals by village.
- **Tokaj, Eger and the Palóc lands:** separate wine-trade festivals from parish feasts.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy) and the Mezőség village of Szék:** compare dance and song gatherings, using local collections.
- **Kalotaszeg and Kolozsvár:** check urban dance houses against village dances.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare celebration music within each community, from each group's own performers.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks of Békés, Šokac and Bunjevac Croats, Serbs of Szentendre and Battonya, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns:** list each community's main feasts; Rusyn material is thin.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Hasidic Jewish communities:** compare Simchat Torah and Purim joy in pre-war and present records.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian congregations:** check how each speaks of joy in worship.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk song, festivals and surveys; mixed for private joy; thin for Boyash, Rusyn and Armenian-descended celebration.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and older Hungarian for the Kájoni and Bornemisza texts; Romanian for Székely and Kalotaszeg festival coverage; German for Swabian feasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "joy in sorrow" saying works as a national stereotype, so trace it, do not repeat it; nightlife quarrels and Roma music's stage use are charged.
- **Held for review:** alcohol and noise law go to Libra; sensual pleasure to Taurus, escape to Pisces. No other joy pointer was held.
