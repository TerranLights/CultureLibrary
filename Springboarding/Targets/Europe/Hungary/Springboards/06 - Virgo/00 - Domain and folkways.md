# Hungary: Work and service, the domain and its folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, taken here with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land (a working reading the author still confirms; see Flags). **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian, with Palóc, Great Plain and Székely usage; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; the German of the Danube Swabians; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Hungary's Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish for the pre-1944 Jewish trades. **Concept in one line:** what is good work and a well-kept life?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest office and factory districts (Csepel, Angyalföld) against Great Plain farmsteads (*tanya*) around Kecskemét and Szeged:** check whether the clock or the season sets the day.
- **Transdanubian wine hills (Balaton, Sopron) and the Ormánság:** compare small-holding routines with estate-servant villages.
- **Northern Hungary (Miskolc, Ózd) and the Palóc villages:** check how industrial shifts and farm work fit into one family.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely) and Kalotaszeg:** look at forest, sheep and timber work and the shared labor that went with it.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** separate the trades each group is recorded in; check sources written by outsiders against Roma accounts.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna, Slovaks of Békés, Rusyns of Komlóska:** compare village work calendars from each nationality's own museum or *Heimatbuch*.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jewish craftsmen and shopkeepers before 1944:** check Sabbath rest against market days in town records.
- **Reformed Debrecen, Catholic Szeged, Unitarian Székely villages:** compare what each church preached on work and idleness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for peasant work, socialist labor and official statistics; mixed for housework and domestic service; thin for Rusyn and Beash working lives and for Armenian-descended merchant families.
- **Languages needed:** German for Swabian village books and the Sopron mining records; Romanian for Transylvanian labor statistics; Latin for early estate accounts.
- **Lands:** no `00 - Scope.md`, and the tracker settles no lands; the Romania carried into the row is read as the Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land (the Moldavian Csángó for comparison only), a working reading the author still confirms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma public works and segregated jobs; socialist forced labor and the 1951 removals; employment of minors in seasonal gangs.
- **Held for review:** labor law and the codes of guilds go to Libra or Capricorn; the clock and public order to Capricorn; nothing else in the domain was set aside.
