# Hungary: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, plus Palóc, Great Plain and Székely words for tasks); Lovari Romani and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Bunjevac and Šokac Croatian; Serbian; the Romanian of Gyula and Méhkerék; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Miskolc and Ózd steel workers against Budapest office workers:** check how each group's memoirs speak of a good day's work.
- **Great Plain smallholders of the Jászság and Kiskunság:** compare farm work with the *tanya* family's own accounts of rest.
- **Győr and Sopron factory towns in Transdanubia:** check how commuting from villages shaped hours.
- **Szatmár and the Nyírség:** look at seasonal migration for harvest and building work.
- **Székely Land (Sepsiszentgyörgy, Csíkszereda) and the Mezőség:** compare forest, farm and migrant work across villages.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** look for each group's own work histories; check who wrote each source.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks of Tótkomlós, Croats of the Drava, Serbs of Szentendre:** compare which trades each nationality held.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jews before 1944, Armenian-descended merchants of Szamosújvár:** check trade and craft records.
- **Catholic, Reformed and Lutheran parishes:** compare sermon collections on idleness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for socialist labor and post-1989 labor statistics; mixed for unpaid home work; thin for Rusyn and Beash work histories, where local nationality self-governments and Pécs Romology are the first doors.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for some socialist-era labor studies; Romanian for Bucharest migration records; Yiddish and German for Jewish trade associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma joblessness and labels of idleness; forced labor under the 1950s regime; claims about who "works hard" between nationalities.
- **Held for review:** labor law and the Labor Code go to Libra; nothing else on work was set aside.
