*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (school and scientific registers, plus folk reckoning words of the Palóc, Plain and Székely areas); Lovari and Beash; German, including Danube Swabian; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew for Talmudic learning. **Concept in one line:** taking a whole apart to understand it: reasoning from parts, evidence, measurement and detail.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's elite gymnasiums against schools in Borsod and Szabolcs:** check where analytic training is offered and to whom.
- **Szeged and Debrecen university towns:** compare their science traditions with Pécs and Győr technical schools.
- **Great Plain herders (Hortobágy) and Kiskunság farmers:** check folk reckoning and tallies against school arithmetic.
- **Palóc and Northern Hungary villages:** look at riddles and weather lore as local reasoning.
- **Kolozsvár, Marosvásárhely and Csíkszereda:** compare Hungarian-language science teaching with Romanian-language schools as a separate check.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** look for each group's own accounts of schooling and reckoning, not test gaps alone.
- **Orthodox and Neolog Jewish learning before 1944:** compare Talmudic argument with secular science careers.
- **Swabian, Slovak, Croatian and Serbian school networks:** check each nationality's science textbooks; Rusyn material is scarce, so start with Komlóska's records.
- **Reformed Debrecen and Sárospatak colleges, Catholic Piarist and Benedictine schools, Unitarian Kolozsvár:** compare how each taught reasoning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mathematics, statistics and science history; mixed for everyday reckoning; thin for Rusyn and Beash learning and for Armenian-descended merchants' accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for early conscriptions; English for the émigré philosophers; Hebrew and Yiddish for Talmud study; Romanian for Transylvanian school data.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** test gaps read as group traits; the "Martians" genius story and its use in national-pride talk; the politics of audits.
- **Held for review:** courts' and auditors' legal powers go to Libra; nothing on analysis was set aside.
