# Hungary: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (everyday, schoolyard and market speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; the German of the Danube Swabians and of medieval Buda; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest and the county towns (Győr, Debrecen):** check whether "connections" or "merit" dominate in admissions and hiring complaints.
- **Great Plain farm towns (Hódmezővásárhely, the Jászság):** compare how the compensation auctions of the 1990s divided land.
- **Ormánság and the Nyírség:** look at how inherited land was split among siblings in village studies.
- **Kalotaszeg, the Mezőség and the Székely Land:** check church-property and forest-commons claims village by village.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash families in Borsod and Baranya:** separate school, housing and work cases by group and place.
- **Swabians of Tolna and Baranya:** look at the 1945 land reform and the shares taken from them and given to settlers.
- **Slovaks of Békés, Croats of Pécs, Serbs, Romanians and Rusyns:** check nationality-school funding disputes in their own press.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo communities:** compare memoirs on the 1920 quota and on postwar restitution.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey items, statutes and court cases; mixed for everyday splitting and turn-taking; thin for Beash and Rusyn children's rules and for Armenian-descended families' inheritance custom.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for the Buda town law; Romanian for restitution rulings; Romani for Roma families' own accounts of school cases.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1920 quota and later anti-Jewish laws; Roma segregation cases; restitution quarrels across the border.
- **Held for review:** procedural trust in courts goes to Capricorn; no pointer on fair shares was set aside under the other rules.
