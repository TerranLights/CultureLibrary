# Hungary: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (with Palóc, Great Plain and Székely usage); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish; Latin for the medieval compacts. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest districts set beside Jászberény and Kiskunfélegyháza:** check where cohabitation and registered partnership are more common.
- **Transdanubia (Pécs, the Őrség, Göcsej):** compare betrothal customs and the role of go-betweens in village records.
- **Palóc villages and Szatmár:** look at *mátkázás* and other sworn bonds of young people.
- **Székely Land (Sepsiszentgyörgy, Székelyudvarhely) and Kalotaszeg:** check how families paired land with matches.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** separate the partnership forms of each (musicians' bands, trading partners, couples) from their own accounts.
- **Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac) and Serbs:** compare how each community remembers the 1868 settlement and its own place in Hungary.
- **Danube Swabians and Slovaks:** check family firms and farm partnerships in nationality archives.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo families:** compare matchmaking and business partnership across the three.
- **Unitarian, Reformed and Catholic couples in Transylvania:** look at mixed-marriage pledges in church registers.
