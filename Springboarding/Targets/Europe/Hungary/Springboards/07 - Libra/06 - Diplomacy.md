# Hungary: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (court, diplomatic and everyday speech); Latin and Ottoman Turkish for early modern treaties; German and French for Habsburg-era diplomacy; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest officialdom against provincial towns (Szombathely, Nyíregyháza):** check how meetings with foreign partners and sister towns are staged.
- **Szeged and the South, along the Serbian border:** compare cross-border town ties and the Serbian community's role in them.
- **Sopron and the Burgenland border:** look at the 1921 plebiscite and how talks with Austria are remembered locally.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Székely Land:** check what Transylvanian Hungarian leaders asked of Budapest and of Bucharest, in their own records.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** look at the go-betweens each group sends to councils and offices, in Roma accounts.
- **Danube Swabians, Slovaks and Croats:** check how nationality leaders negotiated schools and resettlement with the state after 1945.
- **Neolog and Orthodox leaders in 1944:** compare the Budapest Relief and Rescue Committee's dealings (contested) with the Jewish Council's.
- **Reformed and Catholic church diplomacy:** compare the churches' talks with the socialist state (the 1950 and 1964 agreements).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state diplomacy and published papers; mixed for everyday tact; thin for Beash and Rusyn go-betweens and for Armenian-descended merchants' trade diplomacy.
- **Languages needed:** Ottoman Turkish and Latin for Porte papers; French for the 1920 record; Romanian and Serbian for the neighbors' versions of the same talks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Trianon and the Basic Treaty are read in opposite ways in Budapest and Bucharest; the 1944 rescue negotiations are disputed; name each side's claim.
- **Held for review:** treaties' legal force is pointed to in this sign's Domain sheet; no envoy, treaty or politeness source had to be held back.
