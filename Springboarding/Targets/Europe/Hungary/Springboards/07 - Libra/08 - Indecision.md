# Hungary: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (literary, political and everyday speech); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest commuters against Great Plain small towns (Mezőtúr, Kisújszállás):** check undecided-voter shares and late deciding in each.
- **Transdanubia (Győr, Zala villages):** compare how families choose a child's school track at fourteen.
- **Northern Hungary and the Palóc villages:** look at St. Andrew's and Luca customs for choosing a spouse.
- **Kolozsvár, the Mezőség and the Székely Land:** check memoirs of the choice to emigrate or stay, decade by decade.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** see who in the family has the last word on schooling and marriage, from Roma researchers' work.
- **Danube Swabians in 1946:** compare accounts of families who had to choose between expulsion and staying.
- **Slovaks of Békés during the 1946–48 exchange:** check how families decided whether to resettle to Czechoslovakia.
- **Jewish families in 1938–44:** compare memoirs of deciding to leave, convert or stay, across Neolog and Orthodox communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, wartime diplomacy and polling; mixed for everyday deciding; little is likely in print on how Rusyn, Beash or Armenian-descended households make choices.
- **Languages needed:** German and English for wartime papers; Slovak for the 1946 population exchange; Romanian for emigration records from Transylvania.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941–44 choices carry blame for the Holocaust and the war; expulsion and resettlement choices were often forced; keep each account as its writer's.
- **Held for review:** chance-based choosing is noted as folk custom, not as a method; rashness belongs to Aries; nothing else was held back.
