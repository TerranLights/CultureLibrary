# Hungary: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely usage); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest hospitals and hospices against farmstead and village deaths in the Kiskunság, Szatmár and the Palóc lands:** check where people die, and who sits with them.
- **Catholic Transdanubia and Eger against Reformed Debrecen and the Nyírség:** compare prayer for the dead and teaching on purgatory.
- **The Székely Land and Kalotaszeg:** check whether the dead-bride and dead-groom funeral survives, and where.
- **Transylvanian Unitarians and Lutherans of Sopron and Békés:** compare funeral sermons and hymns.
- **Greek Catholics and Rusyns (Hajdúdorog, Komlóska):** look at memorial services and the Slavonic or Hungarian used in them.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** compare *kaddish*, *yortsayt* and the remembering of those with no grave.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** separate what each says about the dead's return and about their names.
- **Swabians of Tolna and Baranya, Šokac Croats of Mohács:** check how each marks war dead and the dead left behind at expulsion.

## 4. Flags

- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide (follow reporting guidance); the Holocaust dead; the assisted-dying debate; children's deaths.
