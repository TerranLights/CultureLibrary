# Hungary: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely usage); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again (proposed).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest ministries and the officer corps against village notaries and teachers on the Great Plain:** check whose names the 1945–46 lists carried.
- **Swabian villages of Budaörs, the Völgység and Baranya:** compare who was removed, who stayed and who came back.
- **Békés Slovaks of Békéscsaba, Tótkomlós and Pitvaros:** check family accounts of leaving or staying in 1947.
- **Bukovina Székely settlers in Tolna:** look at how the newcomers describe moving into emptied houses.
- **Reformed congregations of Kalotaszeg and the Székely Land against Catholic Csík:** check which churches covered their paintings and which kept them.
- **Hajdúság and Nyírség farm towns:** check how kulak lists were drawn and later remembered.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash:** ask each group whether purity rules apply and in what words.
- **Orthodox and Neolog Jews:** compare mikveh use and the washing of the dead.
- **Szatmár and the Upper Tisza:** check fishermen's and towns' accounts of the 2000 spill.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1945–56 purges and the expulsions; mixed for folk cleansing; thin for Roma purity rules in the families' own words.
- **Languages needed:** German for expulsion lists and Swabian memoirs; Slovak for the exchange records; Romanian for Baia Mare's mine records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the expulsions and exchange (living memory and property claims); the 1919 speech's antisemitic readings, which are contested; names on vetting lists; Roma purity rules misread by outsiders.
- **Held for review:** the legal side of screening officials after 1990 goes to Capricorn, and detox health claims to Virgo; nothing on purging itself was set aside.
