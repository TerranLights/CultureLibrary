# Hungary: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely usage); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish; Arabic in translation for the earliest outside account; Latin and German for princely letters. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will; and how a culture judges who holds it, how it is held and how it is used.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest ministries and party headquarters against county seats (Miskolc, Pécs, Győr):** check how far power reached and who filled the gap locally.
- **The Great Plain manors against the peasant towns of the Jászság and Kiskunság:** compare power under lords with towns that bought their own rights.
- **Transylvanian Hungarians and the Székely Land:** read princely and church power separately from the kingdom's story.
- **Catholic hierarchy against Reformed and Unitarian synods:** compare how each church gives and checks power.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo leaders:** check who held communal power and how the state dealt with each.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare traditional leaders with elected self-governments.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians and Rusyns:** check how much power the minority self-governments hold in practice.
- **Tolna and Paks against Budapest:** look at who decides about the plant, and who lives beside it.
- **Women in public life:** check from the women's own memoirs and studies how far they reached into power.

## 4. Flags
