# Hungary: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Šokac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew; Latin for early hymnals. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's districts against Szabolcs and Borsod villages:** check whether a change of faith in one generation shows up in survey files by settlement size.
- **Szeged and the Southern Great Plain against Debrecen:** compare Catholic vows and pilgrim habits with Reformed psalm-singing as the voice of faith.
- **Lutherans of Sopron, the Őrség and the Slovak towns of Békés:** test whether their faith literature follows the Ordass line or local pastors.
- **Székely Catholics of Csík against Unitarians of Udvarhely and the Homoród:** set catechisms side by side, village by village.
- **Kalotaszeg and the Mezőség Reformed parishes:** check what keeps church life going where Hungarians are few.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews and Hasidic pilgrims from abroad:** separate local observance from visitors' practice at the graves.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare Catholic shrines such as Csatka with Pentecostal congregations.
- **Swabians of Baranya, Šokac and Bunjevac Croats, Serbs of Szentendre, Romanians of Békés, Rusyns of Komlóska:** look at pilgrim groups and parish feasts in their own languages.
- **Greek Catholics:** read Hungarian-language liturgy as a choice of faith and belonging, in parish sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and church history; mixed for private devotion; thin for Rusyn and Boyash parish life.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and early Hungarian for the 1607 psalter; Serbian Church Slavonic for icons and service books; Yiddish and Hebrew for pilgrim prayers; Romanian for Unitarian records under Romanian rule.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clergy informer files; the Holocaust; church restitution; pilgrim sites as contested symbols.
- **Held for review:** the legal standing of churches goes to Libra; no faith pointer apart from that was set aside.
