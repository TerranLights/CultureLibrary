# Hungary: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish; Latin for the devotional classics. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Southern Great Plain (Kiskunság, Csongrád, Szeged's hinterland) against Transdanubia:** map suicide and hopelessness items by county before any reading of despair.
- **Budapest professionals against small-town factory workers in Miskolc and Ózd:** compare calling and pointless work in their own words.
- **Wine country (Tokaj, Eger, Villány, Balaton) against grain farms of the Hortobágy:** check whether work and leisure are spoken of as meaning in the same way.
- **Reformed and Catholic villages of the Ormánság and Göcsej:** look for sermons and parish books on vanity and endurance.
- **Hungarians of Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Mezőség against the Székely Land:** read minority meaning (church, school, language) where Hungarians are a majority and where they are few.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna:** separate deportation memory from expulsion memory, village by village.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo families:** check survivors' memoirs for how each tradition answered loss.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare life-story collections rather than outsiders' reports.
- **Slovaks of Pilis and Békés, Croats of the Drava, Serbs, Romanians, Rusyns:** look for local memory books that state what the community is for.
