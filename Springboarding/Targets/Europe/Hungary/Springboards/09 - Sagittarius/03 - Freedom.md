# Hungary: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish; Latin for early charters and chronicles. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The hajdú towns (Hajdúböszörmény, Hajdúnánás) and the Jász and Kun districts:** test whether old collective freedoms still shape local speeches and festivals.
- **Budapest dissident circles against Szeged students and Győr's 1956 workers:** compare what freedom each demanded.
- **Sopron and the Austrian border villages:** check memory of 1989 against memory of the earlier closed border.
- **Székelys of Csíkszereda and Sepsiszentgyörgy against Hungarians of Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad:** read freedom as self-rule against freedom as minority rights, in their own press.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo communities:** separate emancipation memory from the losses of 1920–44.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check how each tells the end of travel and the years of settlement.
- **Serbs of Szentendre and Ráckeve, Croats of the Drava, Swabians, Slovaks, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns:** look for each community's own freedom days and charters.
- **Lutheran and Reformed villages of the Őrség and the Nyírség:** look for sermons on freedom of conscience during the Counter-Reformation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1848 and 1956 memory; mixed for everyday freedom talk; thin for Beash and Rusyn sources.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for charters and decrees; Serbian and Church Slavonic for the 1690 privileges; Romanian for Székely-march coverage; Yiddish for emancipation-era press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partisan use of 1848 and 1956; rating disputes between camps; Székely self-rule claims; Roma settlement history.
- **Held for review:** the Golden Bull (1222) and the noble liberties of Werbőczy's *Tripartitum* are legal texts, held for Libra and Capricorn.
