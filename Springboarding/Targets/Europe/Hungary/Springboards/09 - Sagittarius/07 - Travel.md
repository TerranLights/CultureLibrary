# Hungary: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari), Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew; Ottoman Turkish (in translation) for Evliya. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Szabolcs, Szatmár and the Nyírség against Győr and Sopron:** compare who left for America before 1914 and who later commuted to Austria.
- **Balaton resort towns against Budapest:** check how hosts speak of summer guests, local and foreign.
- **Székely villages of Csík and Udvarhely:** look at labor travel to Budapest and abroad, and at pilgrims arriving for Pentecost.
- **Kolozsvár and Kalotaszeg:** follow the movement of Transylvanian Hungarians to Hungary after 1989, in their own memoirs.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash:** separate itinerant trades, settlement, and recent labor migration, each in its own sources.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo families:** compare who emigrated when, and who returned.
- **Danube Swabians:** trace journeymen's routes and the later expulsions as two different kinds of travel.
- **Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians and Rusyns:** look at travel to kin states and pilgrimages to shrines across the border.
- **Jász and Kun towns and the Palóc lands:** test which local sayings mark the traveler as admirable or suspect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emigration and tourism statistics; mixed for travel attitudes; thin for Rusyn and Beash migration records.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Hungarian; German for Höhnel and journeymen's books; Ottoman Turkish only through translation; Hebrew and Yiddish for emigrant letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced settlement of Roma; postwar Jewish departures; recent emigration as a political theme.
- **Held for review:** passport and border law go to Libra; the diaspora abroad is comparison only; nothing else on the road was set aside.
