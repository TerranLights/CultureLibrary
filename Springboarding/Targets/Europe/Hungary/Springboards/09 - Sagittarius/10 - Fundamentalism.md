# Hungary: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain, Székely); Romani (Lovari), Beash; German (including the Habans' old German); Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Hebrew and Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest free churches against Reformed parishes of the Tiszántúl:** compare literal Bible reading in large new congregations and in old village churches.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo communities, with the northeast's Hasidic courts before 1944:** separate strictness of rite from political stances, each in its own records.
- **Székely Sabbatarians, Unitarians and Catholics of Csík:** check how each community tells restoration of an original faith.
- **Greek Catholics of Hajdúdorog:** look at disputes over keeping the old rite unchanged.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash Pentecostals against Catholic Roma:** compare congregational rules and leaving.
- **Swabian Lutherans and Catholics, Slovak Lutherans of Békés:** look for pietist or revival currents in parish history.
- **Serbian Orthodox of Szentendre and Romanian Orthodox of Gyula:** check calendar and rite disputes in their dioceses.
- **Muslim communities in Hungary:** read each body's own statements before any outside label.
- **Kalotaszeg and the Mezőség:** test whether small Reformed communities see strictness as survival.
