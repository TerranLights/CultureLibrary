# Hungary: Authority and duty, the domain and its folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, read here with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land (a working reading the author still confirms; see Flags). **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Palóc, Great Plain and Székely speech); Latin for early modern records; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Bunjevac, Šokac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed: career, status, reputation, authority and responsibility, with the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against county seats and small villages:** compare address forms and office titles before reading any one as national.
- **Great Plain market towns (Kecskemét, Hódmezővásárhely, the Jászság, the Kiskunság) against Transdanubian estate villages (Somogy, Tolna, the Ormánság):** check what records of rule each left.
- **Northern Hungary (Miskolc, Ózd, Eger, the Palóc villages):** set factory ranks against village elders in the same valleys.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium, Kalotaszeg, the Mezőség, and the Székely seats (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely, Sepsiszentgyörgy, Marosvásárhely):** check where church and civic leaders stood in for state offices.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** record each group's own terms for leaders and elders, kept separate.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Bunjevac and Šokac Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** compare nationality self-governments as a ladder of local office.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jewish communities:** compare community office records (president, rabbi, burial society).
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran, Greek Catholic and Unitarian churches:** separate episcopal, presbyterian and synodal government first.
- **Armenian-descended Hungarians of Szamosújvár and Gyergyószentmiklós:** look for the old merchant-town magistracies; sources likely thin.

## 4. Flags

- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for Habsburg-era rank records; Romanian for Transylvania's state records after 1920 (comparison); Yiddish and Hebrew for communal minute books.
- **Lands:** Romania is the one pre-war land carried, with no borders drawn and no Stage 0 file yet; this sign reads the nation as Hungary with the Transylvanian and Székely Hungarian communities, pending the author's word. The Romanian majority of Transylvania and the Csángó of Moldavia are comparison only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Trianon (1920), 1944, 1956 and the state-security files; Roma segregation; rival Hungarian and Romanian accounts of Transylvania's past, handled as claims.
