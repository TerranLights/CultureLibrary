# Hungary: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (with Székely and Palóc usage); Latin for early modern proclamations; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against small Great Plain and Ormánság villages:** test whether the mayor, priest or doctor is the first one called, place by place.
- **Catholic Eger and Szeged against Reformed Debrecen and Lutheran Sopron:** check whether bishops or elected presbyteries carry church authority.
- **Unitarians of Kolozsvár and the Homoród valley:** read how their synods elect a bishop.
- **Kalotaszeg, the Mezőség and the Székely Land:** compare authority split between Hungarian local leaders and state offices held by others.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare whom each group's own writers name as a voice for the community.
- **Danube Swabians of Baranya and Tolna:** read memory of the Volksbund and the Treuebewegung for who could command loyalty.
- **Slovaks of Békéscsaba, Serbs of Szentendre and Battonya, Croats of Baja, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns of Komlóska:** check the priest's role beside the nationality self-government's.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo congregations:** compare how a rabbi's ruling binds members in each.
- **Greek Catholics of Hajdúdorog and Nyíregyháza:** check how the bishop's authority was rebuilt after 1990.
- **Jász and Kun towns:** look at how redeemed-town self-rule shaped regard for county officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cults of leaders, church-state history and attitude surveys; mixed for workplace and family authority; thin for Beash and Rusyn.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the 1703 manifesto; Romanian for prefect and court records in Transylvania (comparison only); Yiddish and Hebrew for rabbinic rulings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1944 Jewish Council and the Horthy era are fiercely disputed; 1956 reprisals; state-security files under access rules; present-day political polarization over authority.
