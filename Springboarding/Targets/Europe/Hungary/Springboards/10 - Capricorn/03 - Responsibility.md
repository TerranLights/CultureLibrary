# Hungary: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, Székely and Great Plain usage); Latin and old Hungarian for early histories; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Szabolcs-Szatmár and Borsod villages:** compare how poverty is explained, by whom, before reading any single answer.
- **Transdanubian towns (Pécs, Győr, Sopron):** check how Swabian expulsion memory frames collective guilt in local commemorations.
- **Szeged and the South:** look at how Serbs, Bunjevac and Šokac Croats remember the reprisals of 1944–45 and the earlier killings of 1942.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium:** check how Hungarian institutions lost after 1959 are blamed and mourned.
- **Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy and Marosvásárhely:** compare how local leaders and the Bucharest government are each held to answer, keeping Romanian accounts as comparison.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check how each is spoken of in debates on parents' responsibility, and what their own writers answer.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo survivors' communities:** compare how each names those responsible for 1944.
- **Reformed, Catholic, Lutheran and Greek Catholic churches:** check whether and when each issued its own statement of guilt for the 1940s.
- **Slovaks of Békéscsaba and the Pilis villages:** check memories of the 1946–48 population exchange for who is blamed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1944, the 1946 expulsions and disaster inquiries; mixed for household responsibility; thin for Beash and Rusyn views.
- **Languages needed:** old Hungarian and Latin for Cserei and early sources; German for expellee literature; Hebrew and Yiddish for survivors' memorial books; Romanian for the 1959 university records (comparison).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blame for 1944 is a live political dispute; the expulsion of Germans and the Slovak exchange; statements about Roma parents easily turn into stereotypes, so keep sources' own claims marked.
- **Held for review:** the letter of the Criminal Code goes to Libra; no source on blame or apology was dropped under the other rules.
