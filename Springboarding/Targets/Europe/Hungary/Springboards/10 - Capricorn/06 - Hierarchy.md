# Hungary: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, Palóc, Great Plain and Székely usage); medieval Latin; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Great Plain market towns against Transdanubian estate villages (Somogy, the Ormánság):** compare peasant ranks where freeholders ruled with those under great estates.
- **Szatmár and the Nyírség, with their petty nobility:** check whether noble descent still marks standing in villages.
- **Budapest's districts (the Buda hills, Józsefváros, Csepel):** compare address, income and housing as signs of rank.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and Kalotaszeg:** test how the old aristocracy, the Reformed clergy and the urban middle class ranked one another after 1920.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy, Székelyudvarhely):** compare the memory of free Székely ranks with village wealth today.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** look at each group's own accounts of standing, never one group's description of another taken as fact.
- **Danube Swabian villages:** check the ladder from *Großbauer* to *Knecht* in village chronicles.
- **Neolog and Orthodox congregations:** compare seat prices and synagogue honors.
- **Serbs of Szentendre, Croats of the Drava region, Romanians of Gyula, Rusyns of the northeast:** check whether priests and teachers headed their local orders.
- **Armenian-descended Hungarians:** look for the ennobled merchant families of Szamosújvár and Erzsébetváros; sparse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the estate order, interwar sociography and party records; mixed for workplace rank; thin for Roma internal rank and Rusyn villages.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the 1351 decree; German for Swabian and civil-service sources; Romanian for land-reform records (comparison); Yiddish and Hebrew for synagogue registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "kulak" lists and class-enemy files of the 1950s; ranking between Roma groups risks stereotype; the land reforms are told differently by Hungarian and Romanian historians, so keep each as a claim.
- **Held for review:** no rank pointer was set aside under the standing rules; the power-distance indexes sit in earlier signs' sheets and are not repeated here.
