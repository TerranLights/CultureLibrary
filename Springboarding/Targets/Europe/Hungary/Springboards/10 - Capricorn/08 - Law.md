*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (legal, colloquial and Székely usage); medieval Latin; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it (here, the spirit of the law).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Borsod and Szabolcs villages:** compare reporting of crimes and trust in local police.
- **Former market towns of the Great Plain (Kecskemét, Nagykőrös) and Jász and Kun towns:** check their old self-governing courts for how custom met county law.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium:** test how Hungarians use Romanian courts and ombudsmen for language and property claims.
- **Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy, Marosvásárhely):** compare collective claims (autonomy, symbols) with everyday dealings with state law.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** separate the *kris*, used by Vlach Roma, from the routes other groups take, and check whom each turns to first.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo congregations:** compare how each placed state law beside religious law.
- **Danube Swabians and Serbs:** check memories of collective measures in 1944–48 for how each group came to regard state law.
- **Reformed, Catholic, Lutheran, Unitarian and Greek Catholic clergy:** compare church teaching on obeying unjust laws.
- **Slovaks, Croats, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** look at how nationality self-governments use their legal rights in practice.
