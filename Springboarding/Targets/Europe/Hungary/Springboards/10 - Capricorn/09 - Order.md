## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's inner districts against its outer housing estates:** compare complaint data and perceived safety.
- **Great Plain farmsteads (the Kiskunság, Szeged's surroundings) against compact Transdanubian villages (Őrség, Göcsej):** check how scattered and clustered settlement shaped who kept order.
- **Northern Hungary (Miskolc, the Palóc villages) and the Nyírség:** look at crime-victim data and how neighbors describe disorder.
- **Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad against the Székely Land (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely, Sepsiszentgyörgy):** compare Hungarians' trust in local police and village self-help.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare how each group records being policed, apart from how they are described by others.
- **Danube Swabian, Slovak and Serbian villages:** check whether planned layouts and village rules differ by founding group.
- **Reformed and Catholic parishes:** compare church rules for Sunday quiet and feast-day order.
- **Neolog and Orthodox Jewish quarters before 1944:** look at how community order (Sabbath, market days) met town order.
- **Croats (Bunjevac, Šokac) and Romanians of Gyula and Méhkerék:** check their own festivals of permitted disorder.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policing, crime and settlement history; mixed for household and street order; thin for Rusyn and Beash village rules.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the 11th-century decrees; German for resettlement records; Romanian for police data in Transylvania (comparison); Romani for Roma testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the gendarmerie's role in 1944; special registration of Roma; the criminalization of homelessness; football violence with racist or antisemitic chants.
- **Held for review:** public-order statutes as written go to Libra; no pointer on order was set aside under the standing rules.
