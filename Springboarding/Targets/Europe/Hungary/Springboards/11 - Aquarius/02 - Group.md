# Hungary: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard and Great Plain usage); Latin for the Jagiellonian diets; Romani (Lovari, Kelderash) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian (Šokac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** people who see themselves, or are seen, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against the Great Plain's farmstead belt and the Northern Hungarian industrial towns (Ózd, Miskolc):** compare party sorting and union membership by settlement.
- **Transdanubian Swabian villages (Baranya, Tolna) and Mohács's Šokac:** check where a village's minority group and the village itself coincide, and where not.
- **The Nyírség, Szatmár and the Jász and Kun towns:** check settler and old-district groups that still name themselves.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad, Kalotaszeg and the Székely seats:** separate the political, student and church groups in which Hungarians organize.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma (Lovari, Kelderash and others) and Boyash:** check each group's own names and lines.
- **Bunjevac and Šokac Croats, Serbs, Slovaks, Romanians of Hungary, Rusyns:** compare how each is counted and how it counts itself.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** compare group lines drawn after 1869 and again after 1945.
- **Reformed, Lutheran, Catholic, Greek Catholic and Unitarian:** check whether confession still marks groups in mixed towns.
- **Armenian-descended families of Gyergyószentmiklós and Szamosújvár:** whether a group identity persists; data likely thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for party and union data and for 1944–46; mixed for student and festival groups; thin for Rusyn and Beash group lines.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the diets; Hebrew and Yiddish for youth-movement papers; Croatian for Bunjevac and Šokac debates; Romanian for Transylvanian student life (comparison).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1946 pogroms, Roma subgroups and Bunjevac identity are contested; name each group by its own name and present claims as claims.
