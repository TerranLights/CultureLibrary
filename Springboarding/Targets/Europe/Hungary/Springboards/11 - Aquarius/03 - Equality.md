# Hungary: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, Great Plain and Székely usage); Latin for late-medieval sermons; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's hill districts, outer estates and the Józsefváros:** compare residential gaps within one city before national figures.
- **The Ormánság and Szatmár villages against Győr and Sopron:** check how regional poverty and wealth shape equality talk.
- **Hortobágy, Kiskunság and Szeged:** check land-reform memory where the great estates and the market towns met.
- **Palóc villages and the Miskolc industrial belt:** compare peasant and worker children's routes into schooling after 1945.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium; Csíkszereda and Sepsiszentgyörgy:** compare equal-standing claims in mixed cities and in Hungarian-majority towns.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check testing studies and Roma activists' own writing for each group, not one "Roma" figure.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** compare what each nationality advocate raises.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** check how equal citizenship was argued before and after 1920.
- **Reformed and Unitarian congregations (lay rule) against Catholic and Greek Catholic parishes (clerical rank):** compare equality inside church life.
- **Women and men across classes:** check household and wage data side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income data, land reform and minority-rights writing; mixed for testing studies; thin for Rusyn and Beash.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the sermons; German for *Sonntagsblatt*; Romanian for state records on Transylvania (comparison only).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma segregation and discrimination; the 1920 *numerus clausus*; rival Hungarian and Romanian accounts of minority treatment; all held as claims.
- **Held for review:** anti-discrimination statutes and court cases go to Libra; nothing about equal standing was set aside under the other rules.
