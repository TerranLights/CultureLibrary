# Hungary: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (including Old Hungarian for codices); Latin; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian (Cyrillic); Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and German for Jewish communal debates. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest's reform-minded intelligentsia against county gentry and village voters:** check who welcomed each reform and who paid for it.
- **Debrecen and the Reformed Tiszántúl against Catholic Transdanubia (Győr, Pécs):** compare how church tradition colors the word "reform".
- **The Great Plain cooperatives (Jászság, Kiskunság) after 1968:** check whether village reform experience differs from the factory towns of the North.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad, Kalotaszeg and the Mezőség:** compare reform carried by Hungarian schools and churches with reforms imposed by the Romanian state (comparison).
- **Székelyudvarhely, Csíkszereda and Marosvásárhely:** check Unitarian, Reformed and Catholic reform traditions side by side.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check which programs addressed which group, and their own leaders' assessments.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Serbs, Croats, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** compare each nationality's renewal societies and schools.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** the reform question split them; compare each one's sources on it.
- **Lutheran and Greek Catholic communities:** check renewal movements in liturgy and language.
