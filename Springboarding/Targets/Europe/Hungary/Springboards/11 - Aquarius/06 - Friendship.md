# Hungary: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (standard, Budapest slang, Székely usage); Latin and Old Hungarian for 16th-century verse; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Danube Swabian German dialects; Slovak; Croatian (Šokac, Bunjevac); Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Debrecen, Szeged and the Great Plain farmsteads:** check whether friend networks run through school and work or through neighbors and kin.
- **Tokaj and Villány wine towns:** compare friendship kept in cellars with that kept in clubs and cafés.
- **The Palóc villages and the Nyírség:** check friendship and *koma* ties side by side.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium; Marosvásárhely and Sepsiszentgyörgy:** compare friendships inside and across the Hungarian-Romanian line.
- **Székely villages:** check the reach of *koma* and *cimbora* against kin.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** look for each group's own friendship terms and occasions.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Croats, Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** compare ritual-friendship forms and dialect words.
- **Jewish Budapest (Neolog, Orthodox, Status Quo):** check how Yiddish words of comradeship spread into city slang.
- **Men and women, young and old:** compare network data by sex and age before assuming one pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for letters, literature and network surveys; mixed for women's and minority friendship; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended families.
- **Languages needed:** Old Hungarian for Rimay; Swabian dialect German; Serbian and Croatian for *pobratimstvo*; Yiddish for loanwords.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cronyism talk is politically charged; Roma–non-Roma friendship studies touch segregation; forum posts are by private persons, so cite patterns only.
- **Held for review:** no friendship source was held back under the South Asian or other standing rules.
