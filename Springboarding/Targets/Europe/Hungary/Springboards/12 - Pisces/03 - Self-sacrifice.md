# Hungary: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (Székely ballad usage); medieval Latin; Romani and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Győr and Sopron against Szeged and the South:** check whose war dead are honored by local churches and whose by the state.
- **Hortobágy and Jászság farm families:** compare which child stayed on the land, and what was said of it.
- **The Palóc villages around Eger:** check ballad and lament versions of the sacrificed wife or mother.
- **Kalotaszeg, Nagyvárad and the Partium; Csíkszereda and Sepsiszentgyörgy:** compare the poets', bishops' and soldiers' martyr cults of the Transylvanian Hungarians.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** check how giving up for family is described in each group's own life stories, not only in outsiders' studies.
- **Swabian and Slovak villages:** compare war memorials after 1945, and whether the expelled and deported are named on them.
- **Orthodox Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check martyr saints and memorial services in each church calendar.
- **Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Unitarian martyrologies:** compare whom each church calls a martyr, from the galley-slave preachers to the 1950s.
- **The Jász and Kun towns:** check the 1745 redemption, the districts' buying back of their freedom, as shared sacrifice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for martyr cults, ballads and war memory; mixed for caregiving; thin for Rusyn and Beash sources.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Saint Gerard; Hebrew and Yiddish for memorial books; Croatian for Bunjevac and Šokac parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Don, 1944 and 1956 are contested ground; martyr claims are politically used; suicide as protest needs care in wording.
