*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (village and field vocabulary of the Great Plain and Transdanubia); Latin for chronicles and charters; Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest flat-dwellers against Great Plain farmstead families (the Kiskunság, the Hortobágy):** compare where privacy is a wall and where it is distance.
- **The Őrség, Sopron and Győr:** check how decades beside a sealed border shaped ideas of the line.
- **Szeged and the South (Röszke, Ásotthalom):** compare memories of the 1950s border with talk of the 2015 fence.
- **The Palóc lands and Tokaj:** check courtyard and gate customs in village houses.
- **Marosvásárhely, Kolozsvár and Nagyvárad, then the Székely seats:** compare where the Hungarian–Romanian line runs through institutions and where it softens.
- **Vlach Roma, Romungro and Boyash:** check each group's own rules of clean and unclean, kept separate.
- **Swabians, Slovaks, Croats and Serbs of the border villages:** compare which borders cut their families apart, and when.
- **Romanians of Hungary (Gyula, Méhkerék) and Rusyns:** check how a small minority marks its edge from both majorities.
- **Orthodox, Neolog and Status Quo Jews:** compare kosher and Sabbath lines with the ghetto boundary forced on them.
