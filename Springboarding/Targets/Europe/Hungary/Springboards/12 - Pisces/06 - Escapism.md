# Hungary: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (with slang of the 1970s–2010s); Romani (Lovari) and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest nightlife against Borsod and Szabolcs villages:** compare which escapes are sold and which are home-made.
- **Lake Balaton and the Tokaj wine villages:** check how places sold as escapes see their visitors.
- **The Hortobágy and the Kiskunság:** compare herders' and farmstead families' leisure with that of market towns.
- **The Székely seats (Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely) and Kalotaszeg:** check how hosts of Hungarian tourists describe the trade.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare who provides escape (musicians) and who is blamed for it in the press.
- **Swabian wine villages, Slovak and Croatian parishes:** compare feast-day and wine-cellar customs as release.
- **Serbs, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** check fasting seasons and the feasts that end them.
- **Reformed and Lutheran temperance against Catholic feast customs:** compare church teaching on drink.
- **Orthodox, Neolog and Status Quo Jewish families:** check Purim's sanctioned drinking beside daily sobriety norms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for alcohol statistics and socialist leisure; mixed for drugs and gambling; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended Hungarians.
- **Languages needed:** slang dictionaries for drug terms; Romani for musicians' accounts; Yiddish for Purim and temperance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug and drink talk is often aimed at Roma and the rural poor; *Sírva vigad a magyar* is a stereotype to study, not repeat.
- **Held for review:** drug and gambling law goes to Libra; none of the escapes on this sheet was set aside under the other rules.
