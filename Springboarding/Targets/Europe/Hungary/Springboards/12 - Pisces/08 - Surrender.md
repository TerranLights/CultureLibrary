# Hungary: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (17th-century devotional Hungarian; 1849 military usage); Romani and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Hebrew and Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against Szabolcs and Borsod villages:** check where letting go is a therapy word and where older religious words stand in.
- **Komárom, Győr and Transdanubia against the Great Plain (Debrecen, the Hortobágy):** compare how 1849's surrenders are remembered in each.
- **Kolozsvár, Nagyvárad and the Partium:** compare staying-and-accepting with leaving, in families' own accounts after 1920 and 1945.
- **The Székely seats (Csíkszereda, Sepsiszentgyörgy):** check Catholic and Unitarian words for resting in God's will.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare tales, songs and sayings of fate within each group.
- **Swabians deported in 1946 and those who stayed:** check farewell customs and songs; sources are scarce, start with village chronicles.
- **Croats, Serbs, Slovaks, Romanians of Hungary and Rusyns:** compare deathbed customs in Catholic, Orthodox and Greek Catholic parishes.
- **Reformed (Providence) and Catholic (abandonment) teaching:** compare sermons on accepting suffering.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo mourners:** compare funeral prayers of acceptance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1849, interwar culture and death statistics; mixed for therapy talk; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended Hungarians.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-century Hungarian and Latin for *ars moriendi*; Hebrew for funeral prayers; Russian for POW-camp records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1849, Trianon and 1945 are contested; "fatalism" claims about a nation or group are to be recorded, never adopted.
- **Held for review:** medical aid in dying and treatment refusal are law and go to Libra and Capricorn; no letting-go pointer was set aside under the other rules.
