# Hungary: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hungary, region Europe, with the Hungarian communities of Transylvania and the Székely Land. **Language(s) for searching:** Hungarian (children's folklore and healers' vocabulary; Székely usage); Latin and German for 18th-century plague and earthquake records; Romani and Beash; Swabian German; Slovak; Croatian; Serbian; Romanian; Rusyn; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Budapest against small Great Plain towns (the Kiskunság, the Jászság):** compare what people fear at night, and the words they use for it.
- **Szeged, Sopron and Buda's plague monuments:** check how each town tells its epidemic past.
- **Komárom and Győr:** compare earthquake memory with flood memory along the Danube.
- **The Rétköz, Szatmár and the Nyírség:** check Ottoman-era flight legends against later war fears.
- **Csíkszereda, Székelyudvarhely and the Gyergyó villages:** compare wildlife fear with older fears of the forest's spirits.
- **Romungro, Vlach Roma and Boyash:** compare fears named inside each community with fears others hold about them.
- **Fright cures and bogeymen by minority:** check Swabian, Slovak, Croatian, Serbian, Romanian and Rusyn villages, each in its own language.
- **Neolog, Orthodox and Status Quo Jews:** compare *yires-shomayim* in teaching with present safety fears.
- **Catholic, Reformed and Unitarian preaching:** check sermons on the fear of God after plagues and quakes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for plagues, folklore and political campaigns; mixed for anxiety data; thin for Rusyn, Beash and Armenian-descended Hungarians.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for 18th-century records; Yiddish and Hebrew for fear of Heaven; Romani for families' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** migration campaigns, antisemitism and child removal from Roma families are contested and painful; bogeymen that name groups must be recorded critically.
- **Held for review:** fear of crime as law and policing goes to Libra and Capricorn; nothing on fright or dread was set aside under the other rules.
