## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lutheran, Catholic and Orthodox Latvians:** check christening and naming practice by confession, since parish records differ.
- **Latgalians (Latgale, Rēzekne, Daugavpils):** separate Latgalian self-naming and the Latgalian language question from the national picture.
- **Livonians of the Livonian Coast:** a very small people; check what self-names and personal names their own sources record.
- **The Suiti (Alsunga, Gudenieki, Jūrkalne):** a Catholic enclave in Kurzeme; check costume and song as marks of a distinct self.
- **Russian speakers and the Old Believers of Latgale and Riga:** check how self-identification is narrated, apart from language policy.
- **Belarusians, Poles, Ukrainians and Lithuanians:** check community bodies' records for naming.
- **Latvian Roma:** check naming and community sources; likely few written ones.
- **The Jewish community:** compare personal papers from before 1941 with survivor testimony and today's naming.
- **Dievturība followers:** check naming and rite-of-passage practice in this revived faith.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the song and belief corpora, oral history and naming; mixed for minorities; thin for the Roma and the Livonians (look to community bodies and the Livonian Institute, never pad from neighbors).
- **Languages needed:** Latvian for the core corpora; Latgalian for eastern sources; Russian for the Russian-speaking press and the Old Believers; German for Baltic German-era church and manor records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census ethnicity and the status of non-citizens (*nepilsoņi*) are contested; present each account as an account. Deportation and Holocaust testimony needs care with living families. Latvia's exact lands in the post-war order are still to be settled; this sign is written for Latvia as it stands.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on naming, personhood or the body had to be set aside.
