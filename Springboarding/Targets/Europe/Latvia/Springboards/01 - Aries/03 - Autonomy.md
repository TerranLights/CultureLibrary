## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm households of Vidzeme and Kurzeme against Latgale's village communities:** check household-head and inheritance patterns, since settlement forms differ.
- **Riga and Pierīga against small towns:** check the age of leaving home and renting, where housing costs may set the pace.
- **Lutheran, Catholic and Orthodox families:** check who decides marriage and schooling, using parish and survey data.
- **Russian speakers and the Old Believers of Latgale and Riga:** check congregational self-rule and family decision-making separately.
- **Poles, Belarusians and Lithuanians:** check the history of their interwar schools against their present-day Sunday and weekend schools.
- **The Livonian Coast villages and the Suiti of Alsunga:** see who decides on festivals and heritage work in each small community.
- **The Jewish community's self-administration:** compare its interwar school and welfare bodies with the bodies it runs now.
- **Latvian Roma:** check family decision-making sources; likely few, so start with community bodies and the folklore archive.
- **Soviet-era and post-1991 generations:** check how each tells the story of choosing a job, a flat and a spouse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for historical household records and EU surveys; mixed for present-day family decision-making; thin for the Roma and Livonians.
- **Languages needed:** Latvian; Russian; German for guild, estate and church records before 1918.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet-era surveillance files and deportation records name living families; bodily-autonomy debates (abortion, euthanasia, gender) are contested; give each side's account. Legal questions go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on self-rule, households or bodily choice was withheld.

1. Pull Eurostat's leaving-home ages and EU-SILC 2010 module tables for Latvia.

4. Ask the folklore archive for *Jurģi* songs and customs by region.
5. Request the HBSC Latvia national report and note its family-autonomy items.
