## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The four historic regions and Sēlija:** see which battles and heroes each regional museum puts first.
- **Latgalians and Poles of Daugavpils:** check how the 1920 campaign is told in Latvian, Latgalian and Polish sources.
- **Russian speakers:** check their own commemorations and memoirs of the war, as accounts beside the national museums.
- **Old Believer congregations:** gather their own memory of wartime and Soviet-era persecution, which may not match either main narrative.
- **Holocaust survivors and their rescuers:** treat survivor testimony and rescuers' records as two source sets, then read them together.
- **Latvian Roma:** check testimony of the Roma genocide; sources are few, so start with Holocaust archives and Roma organizations.
- **Livonians:** check wartime displacement from the coast and the Soviet border zone in their own records.
- **Women and men:** check whether honors lists and memoirs credit endurance differently by gender.
- **Generations of 1918-20, 1941-45, 1991:** check how each generation's courage is told by the next.
- **Dissidents and conformists:** check Soviet-era files and memoirs for how quiet refusal is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and national heroes, the barricades and dissent; mixed for everyday and civilian courage; thin for Roma and Livonian wartime experience.
- **Languages needed:** Latvian; Russian for Soviet files and veterans' memoirs; German for First World War and Baltic German sources; Polish for 1920 sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Legion and 16 March, the national partisans, the Red Riflemen, the Holocaust and the deportations are contested; keep each community's telling distinct and call each group by the name it uses. Soviet-era files name informers and victims with living families.
- **Held for review:** none; military honors and dissent sources raised no standing-rule issue.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the national library for the Lāčplēsis Order recipients' dictionary and sample citations by region.
2. Read the barricades museum's published testimonies and note who is named.
3. Search Yad Vashem's Righteous database for Latvia and compare with the Lipke memorial's records.
4. Read the Historians' Commission volumes on 1941-45 before any partisan or Legion source.
5. Search *periodika.lv* for Lāčplēsis Day coverage, 1920s-30s and 1990s.
