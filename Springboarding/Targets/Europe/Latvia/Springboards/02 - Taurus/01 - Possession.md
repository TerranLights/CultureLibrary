# Latvia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian, Livonian, the Russian of Latvia; Polish, Belarusian and Yiddish for particular communities. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Deportee families of 1941 and 1949:** check memoirs for what was carried, hidden or reclaimed.
- **Baltic German heirs and the manors they left:** check how manor contents passed into museums, homes or loss.
- **Latgale's Catholic households against Lutheran Vidzeme and Kurzeme:** compare household objects of devotion and their handing-on.
- **Old Believers of Latgale and Riga:** check icons and books as kept objects through Soviet campaigns.
- **Russian speakers who arrived after 1945:** check Soviet-era shortage goods and flat contents in their own memoirs.
- **Livonians and coast fishing families of Kurzeme (Kolka, Mazirbe, Ventspils):** check boats and nets as family property.
- **Suiti households of Alsunga:** check costume pieces kept as heirlooms.
- **Jewish families:** check what was left in ghetto homes in 1941 and what was recovered, if anything, after 1991.
- **Polish and Belarusian families of Daugavpils and Krāslava:** check their community bodies for keepsakes brought across borders.
- **Roma families:** check how possessions are kept or given away after a death; sources likely thin, so ask community organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for words, theft data, deportation records and household-goods tables; mixed for heirloom practice; thin for Roma and Livonian household objects.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Riga probate inventories and the 1939 resettlement files; Russian for Soviet-shortage memoirs and Old Believer sources; Latgalian for eastern household terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** what was confiscated in the deportations, abandoned in the ghetto or left in 1939 touches living families and open claims; present each account as an account. Ownership claims and restitution belong under Ownership, and their law under Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the heirloom, theft and deportee-belongings sources named here raised nothing under the standing rules.
