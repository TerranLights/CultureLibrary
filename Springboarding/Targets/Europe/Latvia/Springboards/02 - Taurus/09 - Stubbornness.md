## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale and Kurzeme:** compare how local histories praise holding out (the press ban, the Semigallian wars) and which events each region claims.
- **Old Believers:** check community accounts of keeping their rite, in Russian and Latvian.
- **Russian speakers and Latvian speakers on public disputes:** read each group's statements in its own media; never let a bullet say who yields.
- **Veterans' and anti-fascist groups on 16 March:** read each group's own words and keep them apart.
- **The Jewish community:** check refusenik memoirs and community histories.
- **Rural farmstead families of Vidzeme and Sēlija:** check memoirs of the Soviet resettlement for who stayed and who moved.
- **Livonians:** check how community histories tell the keeping of identity under the border zone.
- **Belarusians and Poles of Latgale:** check parish and school histories under Soviet rule.
- **Catholic, Lutheran and Orthodox congregations:** compare closure and reopening stories.
- **Roma:** check community accounts of keeping language and custom; thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for idioms, history and press coverage; mixed for psychology; thin for Roma and Livonian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Old Believer and refusenik sources; Latgalian for the manuscript books; German for the Order's side of the Semigallian wars; Yiddish and Hebrew for some refusenik writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 16 March, Soviet resettlement and Jewish emigration are contested or painful; present each account as an account and never call any group stubborn. Labeling a people "stubborn" is a stereotype to study as a label, not a trait.
- **Held for review:** none; idiom, resistance and holdout sources raised nothing under the standing rules.
