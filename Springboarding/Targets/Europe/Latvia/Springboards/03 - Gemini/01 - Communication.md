# Latvia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Polish, Belarusian, Latvian Romani and Yiddish for particular communities. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Vidzeme and Kurzeme against Riga:** check greeting and small-talk norms in recorded speech by setting, not by assumption.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check their recorded speech and song as a separate western case.
- **Latgalians in Rēzekne and Ludza:** check when Latgalian is chosen over Latvian, and with whom.
- **Livonians:** check how revival learners use Livonian among themselves today.
- **Russian speakers in Daugavpils and Riga:** check bilingual code-switching in recorded talk rather than survey answers alone.
- **Old Believers of Latgale:** check speech in prayer and at home in community sources.
- **Belarusians and Poles of Daugavpils and Krāslava:** check which language serves at home, at church and at market.
- **Ukrainians, including those arriving since 2022:** check new interpreting needs in schools and clinics.
- **Latvian Roma:** check Romani radio or community recordings; likely thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check how Yiddish, Russian, German and Latvian divided the community's talk before 1941.
- **Dievturība followers:** check spoken ritual forms against everyday speech.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dictionaries, press and usage advice; mixed for recorded conversation; thin for Romani and Old Believer everyday speech.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for Elger; Estonian for the Livonian dictionary; Yiddish for the LCAAJ; Russian for code-switching studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** which language is used in public service, and the 'non-citizen' question, are contested; present each side's account. Silences about deportation and the Holocaust are painful ground; approach family testimony with care.
- **Held for review:** none; language-use rules as written are Libra's, and no source on talk needed setting aside.
