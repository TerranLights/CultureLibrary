## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latvian- and Russian-language audiences in Riga:** check which news channels each audience uses, from audience data, not from assumption.
- **Daugavpils and Rēzekne:** check local press and radio in Latvian, Latgalian and Russian side by side for what reaches eastern towns.
- **Rural Vidzeme, Sēlija and Kurzeme parishes:** check library and broadband data for who is cut off.
- **Livonians of the Livonian Coast:** check how a scattered community passes news.
- **Old Believers:** check community channels for news, given their own church structure.
- **Belarusians, Poles and Ukrainians:** check community websites and newsletters in their own languages.
- **Latvian Roma:** check whether any Romani-language media exist; likely very thin.
- **The Jewish community:** compare the interwar Yiddish, Russian and Hebrew press with today's community bulletins.
- **Deportees and their families:** check how news of the 1941 and 1949 deportations moved in letters and hearsay.
- **Dievturība followers:** check their own publications for what they choose to publish.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press history and recent media surveys; mixed for Soviet listening habits (memoirs); thin for minority-language media outside Russian.
- **Languages needed:** German and Polish for the early Riga press; Russian for *Segodnya* and today's Russian-language outlets; Yiddish for interwar Jewish news.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the KGB files, the blocking of Russian channels and the TV Rain case are contested; present each side's account. Some security-service records are restricted.
- **Held for review:** none; broadcast and privacy rules as written are Libra's, and no media source here needed holding.
