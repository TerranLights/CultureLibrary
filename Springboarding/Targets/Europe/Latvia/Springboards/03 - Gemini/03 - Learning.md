# Latvia: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Polish, Belarusian, Latvian Romani and Yiddish for particular communities; German and Latin for the early schools. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution and higher learning sit under Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zemgale and Vidzeme farm childhoods:** check memoirs region by region for what children were set to do and at what age.
- **Kurzeme fishing villages and the Livonian Coast:** check whether boys and girls learned the sea and the nets apart.
- **Latgale's Catholic villages:** check church and family teaching through the press-ban years.
- **Riga and Daugavpils apartment childhoods:** compare kindergarten and after-school data with rural parishes.
- **Russian-language school families:** check how the switch to Latvian-language teaching is described by parents and teachers themselves.
- **Old Believers:** check church reading as a learning path apart from state school.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Latgale:** check their own schools and Sunday classes.
- **Ukrainian children arriving since 2022:** check reception classes and remote study in Ukrainian.
- **Latvian Roma:** check attendance and support projects; sources likely thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check the interwar *kheyder*, Hebrew and Yiddish schools and the community school today as separate learning paths.
- **The Suiti:** check whether drone singing is passed on in families or in organized groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history, surveys and childhood memoirs; mixed for apprenticeship; thin for Roma and Old Believer learning.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the Cathedral and Jesuit schools; Russian for two-stream schools and Old Believer classes; Latgalian for the post-1904 primers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the move of minority schools to Latvian-language teaching is contested; give each account. Children's records are personal; use published sources.
- **Held for review:** none; school law as written belongs to Libra, and every learning source here could be named.
