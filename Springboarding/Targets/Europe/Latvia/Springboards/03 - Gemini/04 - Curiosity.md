# Latvia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; German for the 18th- and 19th-century sources; Polish, Belarusian and Yiddish for particular communities. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga's Baltic German learned circles against Latvian peasant readers:** check who had access to natural science in the 18th and 19th centuries.
- **Kurzeme coast and Liepāja:** check birding and seaside nature clubs as a regional pastime.
- **Latgale (Daugavpils, Rēzekne, Krāslava):** check science-center and library access, and folk legends of lakes, for eastern sources.
- **Livonians:** check the community's own view of a century of outside study.
- **Russian speakers:** check Russian-language popular science read in Latvia, then and now.
- **Old Believers:** check whether community sources frame questioning in religious terms.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Latgale:** check their parish and community libraries.
- **Latvian Roma:** check any community writing on outsiders' questions; likely thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check the interwar scientists and scholars of Riga and Daugavpils, and their fate in 1941.
- **The Suiti:** check whether the 2009 UNESCO listing changed outsiders' interest, and how the Suiti speak of it.
- **Dievturība followers:** check what they invite visitors to learn and what they keep private.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science history, museums and birding; mixed for nosiness (forums only); thin for minority views of being studied.
- **Languages needed:** German for Stender's world and the Riga naturalists; Russian for Soviet science magazines; Estonian and German for Sjögren and later Livonian research.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet secret sites and the KGB past are touchy; give former prisoners' and staff accounts as accounts. The Holocaust and deportations should not be treated as spectacle when surveying dark tourism.
- **Held for review:** none; no curiosity, science or tourism source here needed setting aside.
