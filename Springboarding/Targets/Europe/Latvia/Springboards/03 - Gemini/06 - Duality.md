*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian for Latgale's borderland identities; German for the Baltic German layer; Yiddish for the Jewish community before 1941. **Concept in one line:** how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale against Kurzeme and Vidzeme:** check the Catholic-Lutheran split in parish and school sources before calling either half typical.
- **Latgalians:** check whether people describe Latgalian identity as regional, ethnic or both, in their own sources.
- **Russian speakers in Riga's Moscow Suburb (*Maskavas forštate*) and Daugavpils:** check how two-language lives are described from inside, not inferred.
- **Mixed Latvian-Russian families:** check children's and parents' own accounts.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Latgale:** check the "people from here" self-name in older records.
- **Old Believers:** check how a community settled since the late 17th century describes its double belonging.
- **Livonians:** check reclaimed identity against Soviet-era records.
- **Latvians who switched to German in 19th-century towns:** check town and guild records for who switched and when.
- **The Jewish community:** check how interwar Jews of Riga and Daugavpils spoke of being Latvian citizens and Jewish at once.
- **Latvian Roma:** check how Roma organizations describe holding two identities; likely thin.
- **Valka residents:** check daily cross-border life on the Latvian side.
