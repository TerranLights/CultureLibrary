# Latvia: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian (the Middle dialect of Vidzeme and Zemgale; the Tamian speech of the northern Kurzeme coast; High Latvian of the east), Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Belarusian and Polish for Latgale and Daugavpils, Latvian Romani, and Yiddish for the prewar Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what home is and who is family (household forms, kin, roots, hospitality, house types, child-rearing).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme and Kurzeme farmsteads against Latgale's villages:** check household size and the grandparents' place in each settlement form.
- **Riga and Pierīga against small towns:** check microdistrict flats against private houses and *dachas*.
- **Zemgale and Sēlija:** check regional house types on the farming plain.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check the Catholic enclave's household records as a separate Kurzeme line.
- **Livonians of the coast:** check fishing households and who ran the home while boats were out.
- **Russian speakers in Riga, Daugavpils and Liepāja:** check Russian-language sources on household form, not Latvian-language inference.
- **Old Believers of Latgale:** check family size and the place of the elder in community records.
- **Belarusians, Poles, Ukrainians and Lithuanians:** check community sources on mixed-language homes.
- **Latvian Roma:** check how households are counted; likely thin and may need community contact.
- **The Jewish community:** compare prewar household records with today's community, after the Holocaust.
- **Dievturība followers:** check the faith's household rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for censuses, architecture and folk custom; mixed for Russian-speaking households; thin for Roma and Livonian homes today.
- **Languages needed:** German for Baltic German-era house records; Russian for Soviet planning documents and today's Russian-language family press; Latgalian for village studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Soviet deportations, the Holocaust and the 'non-citizen' status of some Russian-speaking residents bear on household records; present each contested account as an account. Latvia's tracker lists no settled lands: its exact lands in the post-war order are still to be settled, and this sign is written for Latvia as it now stands.
- **Held for review:** none; every household, house-type and family-memory source in this domain could be named, and family law as written is left to Libra.
