# Latvia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian (with the coastal and eastern subdialects), Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian of Latgale, Latvian Romani, Yiddish for the prewar Jewish home. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Vidzeme and Kurzeme farm families:** check how the farm name and the family home overlap in usage.
- **Latgale's villages and towns:** check the emptying of homes after emigration, by parish.
- **Riga's wooden suburbs against its microdistricts:** check what owners and tenants say about each kind of home.
- **Jūrmala:** check summer homes and year-round residents as two kinds of home in one town.
- **Liepāja and Ventspils port families:** check whether home talk shifts with work at sea and in port.
- **Livonian descendants:** check summer returns to coastal villages as homecoming.
- **The Suiti:** check whether Alsunga's home ties hold among those who moved to Riga.
- **Russian speakers who settled in the Soviet decades:** check how they name Latvia as home in their own press.
- **Old Believer families:** check the domestic place of icons and prayer books.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Daugavpils:** check family-home terms in community sources.
- **Roma:** check housing and home terms; likely thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check how prewar homes are remembered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing statistics and architecture; mixed for the meaning of home; thin for Roma and Livonian homes today.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Soviet housing memoirs and Old Believer domestic religion; Latgalian for eastern home terms; Yiddish and German for prewar Riga homes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet housing allocation, the 1941 ghetto, and the post-1991 return of buildings to prewar owners are told differently by each community; present each as an account. Housing rights as written are Libra's.
- **Held for review:** none; no housing, district or homecoming source needed setting aside.
