# Latvia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian (including the orphan songs' diminutives), Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Latvian Romani; Yiddish for prewar and wartime Jewish sources. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale's emptied parishes against Pierīga's growing suburbs:** check how those who stayed describe those who left.
- **Vidzeme and Sēlija farms:** check abandoned homesteads and how heirs speak of them.
- **Kurzeme's Soviet-era sites (Skrunda, Irbene, Liepāja's Karosta):** check how residents tell the army's departure.
- **Livonians:** check the story of emptied villages from the families' side.
- **Families split by the 1941 and 1949 deportations:** check survivors' accounts alongside the record.
- **Families of workers in Ireland, the UK and elsewhere:** check children's and grandparents' accounts inside Latvia.
- **Russian speakers:** check accounts of 1991 as told in Russian.
- **Old Believers:** check whether community care absorbs orphans and the old.
- **Belarusian, Polish and Ukrainian families:** check kin left across borders.
- **Roma:** check community accounts of children taken into care.
- **The Jewish community:** check survivors' accounts of families left behind in 1941.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk song and child-welfare data; mixed for emigrant families; thin for Roma and for the unclaimed dead.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for camp letters and Russian-language memoirs; Latgalian for parish depopulation; Livonian and Estonian for village histories; English for diaspora-side research on families left behind.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportations, Soviet withdrawal, the 'non-citizen' question, Roma children in care and emigrant parents are contested ground; present each account as an account and blame no group. Child-maintenance and custody law are Libra's.
- **Held for review:** none; the orphan, foster-care and emigration sources raised nothing under the standing rules.
