# Latvia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Belarusian and Polish in Latgale; Yiddish for the prewar Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale:** check Latgalian pride in language and Catholic heritage against pride in Latvia as a whole.
- **Kurzeme and the Suiti:** check whether pride gathers at the town, the parish or the ethnic group.
- **Livonians of the coast:** check how a people of very few speakers marks its worth in public.
- **Vidzeme and Cēsis:** check the town's claim as the flag's birthplace, traced to a banner in the *Livonian Rhymed Chronicle*.
- **Zemgale and Sēlija:** check farm and grain pride, and Sēlija's newer regional claims.
- **Riga and Pierīga:** check city pride against national pride in a city with many Russian speakers.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check pride in Russian-language culture, faith and history on their own sources.
- **Belarusians, Poles, Ukrainians and Lithuanians:** check community days and schools as places of pride.
- **Roma:** check community events and Romani-language projects; sources likely thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check how pride in rebuilt community life sits beside Holocaust memory.
- **Dievturība followers:** check pride in an ancestral faith revived.
- **LGBTIQ people:** check how the loanword "praids" is used and argued over.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, surveys and monuments; mixed for personal and work pride; thin for Roma and small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for 9 May coverage and Russian-language community sources; Latgalian for regional identity writing; German and Latin-script Old Latvian for Glück.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 9 May, the Victory Monument, the 16 March Legion commemoration and Pride events are contested; present each account as an account and name each community by its own name.
- **Held for review:** none; the monument, congress and sports-triumph sources needed no setting aside, and 9 May and 16 March stay in the flags as contested ground.
