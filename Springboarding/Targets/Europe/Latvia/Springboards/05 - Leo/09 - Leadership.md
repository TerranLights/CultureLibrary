*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian (*latgalīšu volūda*), Livonian (*līvõ kēļ*), the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian in Latgale; Latin and German for medieval and ducal sources; Yiddish for the prewar Jewish community. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale:** check priests and teachers as leaders, and how that changed after 1991.
- **Kurzeme and the Suiti:** check the Catholic enclave's parish leadership against Lutheran neighbors.
- **Livonians:** check how the Livonian Union (*Līvõd Īt*) chooses leaders for a small people.
- **Vidzeme:** check the farmstead master (*saimnieks*) and parish elder in memoirs.
- **Zemgale and Sēlija:** check memories of kolkhoz chairmen against interwar farm leaders.
- **Riga and Pierīga:** check business and city leadership; who rises and through which networks.
- **Russian speakers:** check parties, unions and community bodies that speak for them, in their own sources.
- **Old Believers:** check lay leadership and its succession.
- **Belarusians, Poles, Ukrainians and Lithuanians:** check how community associations choose heads.
- **Roma:** check whether family elders or registered associations act as leaders; sources thin.
- **The Jewish community:** check rabbis and lay heads before 1941 and today.
- **Dievturība followers:** check Ernests Brastiņš's founding role and how later heads were chosen.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for chronicles, national history and politics; mixed for workplace and village leadership; thin for Roma and Livonian leadership.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Middle High German for the chronicles; German for guild and ducal records; Russian for kolkhoz and Old Believer sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for interwar Jewish politics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Ulmanis regime, Soviet-era leaders and the 1940s are contested; present each account as an account. Formal authority goes to Capricorn, the law of office to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the chronicle, guild, regime and kolkhoz-chairman sources could all be named.
