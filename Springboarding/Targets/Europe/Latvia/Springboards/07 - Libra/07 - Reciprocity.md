# Latvia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian, Livonian, the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian in Latgale; Ukrainian for the newest arrivals; Latvian Romani; Yiddish for the prewar loan societies. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga and Pierīga against the farm districts of Vidzeme, Zemgale and Sēlija:** check whether help between households is counted, returned or left open differently in town and country.
- **Latgale (Daugavpils, Rēzekne):** check church-based giving, cross-family help and loan customs in Latgalian, Russian and Polish sources.
- **Kurzeme, Liepāja and the Livonian Coast:** check fishing-village customs of helping widows and crews, from Livonian and coastal records.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check gift and hospitality customs tied to their wedding and feast days.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check alms and memorial-meal customs in their own publications, and favor-for-favor talk in the Russian-language press.
- **Ukrainians, Belarusians, Poles, Lithuanians:** check community mutual-aid groups, the Ukrainian ones especially since 2022.
- **The Jewish community:** check how prewar loan and welfare societies are remembered and whether any successor exists.
- **Roma:** check community-led accounts of help between families; few written sources exist.
- **Dievturība followers:** check whether their festivals include gifts or offerings framed as an exchange.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk song, tales and the associational press; mixed for present-day favors, which leave few records; thin for Livonian and Roma practice.
- **Languages needed:** German for the early mutual-aid statutes; Yiddish and Hebrew for the loan societies; Ukrainian for hosting accounts from the guests' side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** favor-for-favor practices can shade into the bribery that KNAB pursues, so keep reciprocity and corruption apart in the wording; refugees' accounts are personal and need consent.
- **Held for review:** none; gift, aid and loan sources here are folk, civic and statistical, with nothing to set aside.
