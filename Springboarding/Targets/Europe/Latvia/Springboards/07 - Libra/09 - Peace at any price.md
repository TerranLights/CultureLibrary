# Latvia: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian, Latgalian, Livonian, the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian in Latgale; Latvian Romani; Yiddish; German for the 1939-41 resettlement and occupation records. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga and Pierīga against rural Vidzeme, Zemgale and Sēlija:** check whether reporting of family violence and workplace complaints differs where neighbors know each other.
- **Latgale (Daugavpils, Rēzekne):** check how parish clergy and Orthodox priests counsel couples in conflict, in Latgalian and Russian sources.
- **Kurzeme, Liepāja and the Livonian Coast:** check how coastal families recall living under the Soviet border regime and what they kept quiet about.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check whether the community's closeness shapes how quarrels are handled.
- **Russian speakers:** check how 1940 and 1991 are framed in their own press, since the meaning of "peace" in those years is disputed.
- **Old Believers:** check whether a tradition of withdrawing from state conflict shows in their own histories of Latgale.
- **Poles, Belarusians, Lithuanians, Ukrainians:** check how each community's organizations handled moments of tension with the state.
- **The Jewish community:** check survivors' and historians' accounts of 1940-41 and of postwar silence about the Holocaust.
- **Roma:** check community-led accounts of reporting violence and discrimination; such sources are scarce.
- **Women's and men's crisis services:** check what callers say about why they stayed silent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1939-40 decisions, the statutes and the surveys; mixed for family and workplace silence; thin for Livonian, Suiti and Roma accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for the Soviet-era memoirs and saying; German for the 1939 resettlement and occupation-era records; Latgalian for parish sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1940, the KGB files, the Istanbul Convention and domestic violence touch grief, honor and current politics; give each account as an account. Abuse testimony is personal data.
- **Held for review:** none; the sources on giving way, silence and settlement are Latvian public records and folklore, with nothing that needed holding.
