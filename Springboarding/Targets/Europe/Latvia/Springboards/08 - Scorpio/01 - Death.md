# Latvia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian, Livonian, the Russian of Latvia; Polish, Belarusian and Ukrainian in Latgale and Riga; Latvian Romani; Yiddish for the Jewish towns of Courland and Latgale. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life: how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale's Catholic villages against Kurzeme's Lutheran farms:** check deathbed rites (the priest's last visit versus a pastor's or none) and the prayer cycle after burial.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check their singing at wakes and graves against neighboring parishes' practice.
- **The Livonian Coast and Liepāja and Ventspils fishing towns:** check how the sea's dead, often unburied, are remembered.
- **Russian speakers, Orthodox and Old Believer:** check the ninth- and fortieth-day memorials and the priestless funeral in their own sources.
- **Ukrainians arrived since 2022:** check how deaths at the front are mourned from Latvia.
- **Jewish community:** check how *kaddish* and *yortsayt* are kept for victims with no known grave.
- **Roma:** check the 1941-44 killings and present mourning from Roma-led sources; sources are scarce, start with community organizations.
- **Dievturība followers and secular Latvians:** check whether death is framed as a passage to the souls' world or as an end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tales, war memory and statistics; mixed for end-of-life care; thin for children's deaths and Roma grief in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** German for the 1710 plague sources, Russian for exile deaths recorded in Soviet files, Livonian for any grave inscriptions and coastal lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data need care with small-area numbers; the Legion cemetery, the Victory monument and Holocaust collaboration are contested ground, so give each as an account; exile memoirs name the dead's families.
- **Held for review:** none; no death source here came from a restricted tradition or needed setting aside.
