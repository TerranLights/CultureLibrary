# Latvia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects, Latgalian, Livonian, the Russian of Latvia; Ukrainian for recent arrivals; Polish and Belarusian in Latgale; Yiddish for prewar religious life; German for the Moravian and Lutheran church records. **Concept in one line:** a new life through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and belief that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme's Moravian heartlands against Kurzeme's Baptist districts (Liepāja, Ventspils):** check which revivals took hold where, and the adult baptism stories each kept.
- **Latgale against the rest:** check whether revival is told through the press ban and Rēzekne, or through Riga's Awakening.
- **Livonian descendants of the coast:** check who now counts as Livonian and how relearning the language is described.
- **The Suiti:** check the UNESCO-backed safeguarding of their singing as a revival with its own narrators.
- **Russian speakers:** check charismatic churches such as *Jaunā Paaudze* and Orthodox revival after 1991 in Russian-language sources.
- **Old Believers:** check whether renewal is a theme at all in priestless communities, or continuity is stressed instead.
- **Ukrainians since 2022 and Belarusians since 2020:** check how exiles describe a new start in Latvia.
- **The Jewish community:** check the rebuilding of congregations after 1991 and memory of *tshuve* movements.
- **Dievturība followers and folklore singers:** check how each tells its relation to the pre-Christian past.

## 4. Flags

- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1840s conversions and the 1980s Awakening are told differently by Latvian, Russian-language and Orthodox writers; give each as an account. Recovery testimonies are personal.
