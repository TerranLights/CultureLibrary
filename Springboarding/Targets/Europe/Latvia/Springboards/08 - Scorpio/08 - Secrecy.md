## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Livonian Coast villages:** check how the closed border zone shaped what Livonian families could keep, record or say.
- **Kurzeme (Skrunda, Irbene, Liepāja's Karosta) against Riga:** check how life beside a secret installation is told by Latvian and Russian-speaking residents.
- **Latgale's Catholics:** check secret religious instruction and hidden priests under Soviet rule, from parish histories.
- **Old Believers of Latgale:** check how centuries of persecution shaped what is shown to outsiders, in their own published histories.
- **The Suiti:** check whether their Catholic customs were practiced in hiding in Soviet years.
- **The Jewish community:** check survivors who lived in hiding (1941-44) and later kept their past from children, in testimony.
- **Russian speakers:** check how former Soviet officers' families speak of the closed sites they worked in.
- **Dievturība followers:** check how the movement survived in exile and in quiet at home, and what it kept from view.
- **Roma:** check what Roma families keep within the community, but only as Roma organizations choose to describe it; little is published.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet secret sites and leaks; mixed for family secrets, held in memoirs and novels; thin for privacy views in surveys specific to Latvia.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for site and border-zone files; German for the old Riga lodges; Yiddish and Hebrew for hidden survivors' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gay and lesbian life histories, adoption searches and genetic data concern living people; consent first. Leaked recordings name living figures; treat contents as allegations. Restricted knowledge of any community is recorded only as that community allows.
- **Held for review:** none; secret sites, lodges, leaks and novels make up the list, and no source here met a holding rule.
