*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zemgale's manor belt against Latgale:** check how German noble power and Polish magnate power left different traces in land, churches and memory.
- **Riga against Ventspils and Liepāja:** check how port money and city halls shape local power outside the capital.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check the role of the Alsunga manor's Catholic convert lords (the Schwerin family, *check*) in making the enclave, and how Suiti tell it.
- **Livonians:** check who spoke for the coast under Baltic German, Soviet and present governments.
- **Russian speakers:** check how political exclusion and representation are described in Russian-language outlets, as one account beside others.
- **Old Believers:** check the power of lay congregations without priests, and their dealings with the state.
- **The Jewish community:** check prewar communal bodies (*kehile*) and their leaders' standing in Riga and Daugavpils politics.
- **Roma:** check representation in municipal and state bodies; sources are thin, start with Roma advisory councils.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians:** check whether exile groups have gained any voice in local or national politics.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for noble, guild and Soviet power; mixed for informal influence today; thin for power within small communities.
- **Languages needed:** German for the knighthoods and guilds; Polish for Inflanty and the Plater papers; Russian for nomenklatura files and Russian-speaking parties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Ulmanis years, Harmony's exclusion and named local leaders are contested; give each account as its holders' own, and treat court cases against living figures as reported. Authority and hierarchy belong to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; nobles, guilds, parties and unions filled the list without any item to set aside.
