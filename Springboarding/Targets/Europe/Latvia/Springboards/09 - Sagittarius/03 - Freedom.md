# Latvia: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia; Belarusian and Polish in Latgale and Daugavpils; Latvian Romani; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Jewish community; German for the manor era. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme and Kurzeme manor parishes against Latgale's village communes:** check how the end of serfdom (1817-19 in the west, 1861 in Latgale) is told locally, since the dates differ.
- **Riga against the rural east:** check whether freedom is spoken of as speech and movement in the capital and as land and work in the countryside, through local sources rather than assumption.
- **Russian-speaking citizens beside non-citizens:** check how the organizations of each describe freedom of movement and of language; present each as an account.
- **Belarusians and Ukrainians who arrived after 2020 and 2022:** check their exile organizations' writing on freedom apart from older Belarusian and Ukrainian residents'.
- **The Jewish community:** check emigration-era memory against those who stayed, through the community's own publications.
- **Livonians:** check how the Soviet closing of the coast and its reopening appear in Livonian testimony.
- **The Suiti and the Latgalian Catholics:** check how freedom of worship under Lutheran lords or Russian rule is remembered in each.
- **Roma:** check Roma-led accounts of wartime persecution and Soviet settlement rules; sources are thin.
- **Old Believers:** check their own histories for freedom of conscience as a reason for settling and staying.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in political history, song and the press; mixed for how ordinary people define freedom today; thin for Roma and Livonian voices.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Soviet religious-affairs files and refusenik memoirs; German for manor-era sources; Belarusian for exile writing; Hebrew for Israeli memoirs of Riga emigrants.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** non-citizens' status, the Soviet years and the Legion commemoration are contested; present each account as an account. Rights as written law belong to Libra; liberty as a legal culture to Capricorn.
