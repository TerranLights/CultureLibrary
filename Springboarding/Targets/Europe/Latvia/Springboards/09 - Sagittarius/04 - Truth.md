# Latvia: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian in Latgale; Latvian Romani; Yiddish, Hebrew and Russian for Jewish historiography. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latvian-language and Russian-language audiences:** check which outlets each reads and trusts, through audience surveys, without assuming one stance per language.
- **Latgale's border towns (Daugavpils, Krāslava, Zilupe):** check media reach across the border and how locals rate it.
- **Riga and Pierīga against Kurzeme and Vidzeme towns:** check whether local newspapers survive as sources of record.
- **The Jewish community:** check how its own historians and memorial bodies handle Holocaust evidence and the history of 1941.
- **Livonians:** check whether Livonian accounts of coastal history differ from standard Latvian ones, in their own publications.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Latgale:** check where they get news (Polish, Belarusian, Latvian or Russian outlets).
- **Old Believers:** check how their communities regard church history and the old books as the truth of the faith.
- **Roma:** check whose word is reported in coverage of Roma communities; Roma-authored media is scarce, so note the gap.
- **Generations:** check which sources are trusted by those who came of age with the Soviet press and by those who came of age online.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for history disputes and the press; mixed for everyday trust; thin for Roma, Livonian and Polish-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for the Soviet press and today's Russian-language outlets; German and Yiddish for Holocaust testimony; Livonian grammar terms for the mood comparison.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1940, the Holocaust, the deportations and media restrictions are contested; present every account as an account. Courtroom truth belongs to Libra.
