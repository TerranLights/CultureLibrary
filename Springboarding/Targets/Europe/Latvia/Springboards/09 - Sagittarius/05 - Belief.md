*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Latgale against Vidzeme and Kurzeme:** check whether folk-belief collections are denser in Catholic or Lutheran parishes, and why collectors went where they did.
- **The Tamian coast and the Livonian villages:** check sea and weather beliefs in Livonian and Tamian speech separately.
- **Riga's suburbs against Zemgale farms:** check whether present-day surveys of folk belief differ by town size.
- **Russian speakers:** check Orthodox, Old Believer and unaffiliated belief separately, through each community's own writing.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Daugavpils and Krāslava:** check folk-belief vocabulary in Polish and Belarusian, not only in Latvian translation.
- **The Suiti:** check belief records from Alsunga for Catholic and older layers together.
- **Dievturi:** check how they draw belief from the *dainas* in their own texts, against folklorists' readings.
- **The Jewish community:** check religious and secular belief in its own historians' work.
- **Roma:** check for belief material gathered with Roma consent; treat outsiders' lists of Roma "superstitions" as a record of the outsiders, not as evidence.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in the folklore archive and in European surveys; mixed for present-day folk belief; thin for Roma and for Livonian material held in Latvia.
- **Languages needed:** Estonian for the Livonian folklore held in Tartu; Russian for minority sociology; Yiddish and Hebrew for interwar Jewish debates; German for older collectors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outsiders' records of minority "superstitions" carry prejudice and must be read critically; folk healing and charms overlap with Pisces's magic ways.
- **Held for review:** none; belief material here came from the folklore archive, the press and survey sources outside the holding rules.
