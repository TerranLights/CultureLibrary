*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects; Latgalian; Livonian; Russian as used in Latvia; Polish and Belarusian of the southeast; Latvian Romani; Yiddish for pre-1941 sources; German and Latin for the medieval and early modern layers. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lutherans, Catholics and Orthodox:** check how each church's own structure (synod, bishop, patriarchate) shapes what members hear about obeying rulers.
- **Latgale against Kurzeme and Vidzeme:** check the weight of priestly and landlord authority in Latgalian memoirs against Lutheran parish sources.
- **Russian speakers:** check Soviet-era migrants, their children born in Latvia and older Russian families separately, since the state they grew up obeying differs.
- **Old Believers:** check how lay-led congregations name and limit leaders' authority.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Daugavpils and Krāslava:** check parish and school sources in their languages for whom they cite as an authority.
- **The Livonian Coast and the Suiti:** check whether village or kin heads appear in their sources, and say so where nothing survives.
- **The Jewish community:** check rabbinic authority before 1941 against the lay-led community rebuilt after 1989.
- **Roma:** check kin and community leadership in Roma-led sources first, and weigh outsiders' talk of "chiefs" or "barons" with care.
- **Dievturi:** check how a revived faith names its leaders (*dižvadonis*, *check*) and on what grounds.
- **Generations:** check people schooled before 1940, in Soviet schools and after 1991 apart, since each met a different state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church and state; mixed for families and workplaces; thin for Livonian and Roma internal authority.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the 1561 charters; Russian for OMON and Soviet press material; Polish for Latgale's parish layer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** January 1991 and the OMON are told differently by the two language communities: give each as an account. The 1993 ordination decision is still argued within Lutheranism. The authoritarian-libertarian measures (permission share, correctional control) are scored at Stage 2 from the law; this sheet points only to attitudes.
- **Held for review:** nothing; authority's pointers here are churches, surveys and press, none falling under a standing rule.
