# Latvia: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian (High Latvian forms for the east); Latgalian; Livonian; Russian of Latvia (dominant for the Soviet school, army and sport records); Polish and Belarusian of Latgale; Latvian Romani; Yiddish and Hebrew; German for the military-order layer. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others or by oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former Latvian-medium and Russian-medium schools:** check discipline policy and pupils' accounts in each, since the two systems ran side by side until 2023.
- **Lutherans, Catholics, Orthodox and Old Believers:** check fasting rules and how strictly each community reports keeping them.
- **Latgale (Daugavpils, Rēzekne):** check garrison, factory and parish discipline in Russian, Polish and Latgalian sources.
- **Riga and the Kurzeme ports (Liepāja, Ventspils):** check Soviet naval and port discipline against farm work rhythms inland.
- **The Livonian Coast and the Suiti:** check fishing-crew and church discipline in their own song and memoir.
- **The Jewish community:** check yeshiva and secular-school discipline before 1941 apart from each other.
- **Roma:** check how Roma families describe school discipline and exclusion, through Roma organizations.
- **Men and women:** check army, sport and household discipline by gender in memoirs and survey data, without assuming the split.
- **Generations:** check accounts of the Soviet conduct mark against today's pupils' accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school and military discipline; mixed for families, where reports are self-reported; thin for Livonian and Roma practice.
- **Languages needed:** German and Latin for the Order's rule; Russian for Soviet sport, army and labor records; Hebrew and Yiddish for *muser* writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment and abuse in institutions; survivors' accounts come first and personal records are confidential. Soviet discipline campaigns are remembered differently by the two language communities.
- **Held for review:** nothing; every discipline source here is a school, army, church or survey body clear of the standing rules.
