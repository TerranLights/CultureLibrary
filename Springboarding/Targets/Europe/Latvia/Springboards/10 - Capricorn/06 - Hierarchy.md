# Latvia: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian and its dialects; Latgalian; Livonian; Russian of Latvia; the Polish and Belarusian of Latgale's gentry and peasant villages; Latvian Romani; Yiddish and German (for the German-speaking Jews of Courland as well as the Baltic Germans). **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme, Kurzeme and Zemgale (manor Latvia) against Latgale (gentry and Catholic clergy):** check whether the ladder had different rungs in the east.
- **Riga:** check the old town ranks (*Literaten*, guild burghers, *Undeutsche*) separately from the countryside.
- **Russian speakers:** check how Soviet-era rank and post-1991 status in citizenship and language are described in their own press, as accounts.
- **Old Believers:** check merchant and peasant families as distinct strata in Latgale and Riga.
- **Poles and Belarusians:** check the gentry and peasant layers among Latgale's Poles, and Belarusian village sources.
- **The Jewish community:** check Courland, Riga and Latgale separately.
- **Livonians and the Suiti:** check whether their sources rank within the community or only against outsiders.
- **Roma:** check how Roma groups in Latvia describe their own internal standing, through Roma-led sources only.
- **Men and women:** check rank at work and in church by gender as a question, using statistics and interviews.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the manor, the Soviet elite and state protocol; mixed for present-day class; thin for internal rank among Roma and Livonians.
- **Languages needed:** German for the knighthoods and *Literaten*; Russian for Soviet privilege; Polish for the Latgale gentry; Yiddish and German for Courland's Jews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the end of the Baltic German manor, Soviet privilege and the citizenship divide after 1991 are argued over: present each as an account. Avoid ranking groups.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the rank sources found are estates, churches, armies and surveys, which the standing rules leave untouched.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the National Library for studies of *vaļinieki* and *pusgraudnieki* in the 19th c.
2. Find the 1920 Agrarian Reform Law on *likumi.lv* or in the 1920 gazette, then search the League of Nations archive for the landowners' petitions.
3. Search *periodika.lv* for "slēgtie veikali" in 1988-1991 issues.
4. Read the Ministry of Foreign Affairs page on state protocol and copy the precedence list.
5. Search the Rēzekne-Aglona diocese site for its founding documents and parish list.
