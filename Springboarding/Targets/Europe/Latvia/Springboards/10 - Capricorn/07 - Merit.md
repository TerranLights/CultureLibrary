# Latvia: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; Livonian; Russian of Latvia (for Soviet titles and the professional-exam debates); Polish and Belarusian of Latgale; Latvian Romani; Yiddish and Hebrew for interwar Jewish schooling; German for the guild era. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga against regional towns (Liepāja, Ventspils, Rēzekne, Cēsis):** check whether admissions and hiring are described as more open or more connection-bound in smaller labor markets.
- **Latvian- and Russian-speaking graduates:** check how each group explains success and failure in exams and hiring, as accounts.
- **Latgalians:** check whether Latgalian-language merit (writing, song) is honored inside and outside Latgale.
- **Old Believers and Orthodox parishes:** check how religious standing is earned (by learning, age or election) in their own sources.
- **Poles, Belarusians and Lithuanians:** check minority schools' graduates in admissions data and community media.
- **The Jewish community:** check interwar and post-1991 routes into professions apart.
- **Livonians and the Suiti:** check whether skill in heritage crafts and song is the route to standing in their own groups.
- **Roma:** check Roma students' and workers' accounts of selection, through Roma organizations.
- **Generations:** check people who earned Soviet titles and degrees against those trained after 1991.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exams, admissions and prizes; mixed for hiring; thin for minority experience of selection.
- **Languages needed:** German for guild statutes; Russian for Soviet titles and the language-exam debate; Latgalian for regional honors; Yiddish and Hebrew for interwar Jewish schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language requirements for professions are disputed; present each side's case as an account. Hiring discrimination is a measured question, not a finding.
- **Held for review:** nothing; merit's pointers here are guilds, exams, prizes and surveys that no standing rule sets aside.
