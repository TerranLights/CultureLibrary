# Latvia: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia (needed for Soviet legal culture and prison speech); Polish and Belarusian of the eastern border districts; Latvian Romani; Yiddish and Hebrew; German for the older legal history. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them; here the spirit only (what law is for, obeying, bending or avoiding it, discretion, trust); the letter of the law is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga against Latgale's border districts (Ludza, Krāslava, Zilupe):** check how cross-border trade and customs rules are described in each place.
- **Latvian and Russian speakers:** check trust in courts and police by language in surveys and in each language's press, as a measured question.
- **People schooled in Soviet law and those trained after 1991:** check lawyers' and judges' memoirs from each generation for what they say law is for.
- **Old Believers:** check whether disputes are settled inside the community before reaching the state, in Pomor sources.
- **Poles and Belarusians:** check border-village sources on customs and smuggling in their own languages.
- **Livonians:** check Soviet border-zone rules on the coast as the fishing families recall living under them.
- **The Jewish community:** check the Kalējs case and earlier war-crimes cases as the community recorded them.
- **Roma:** check whether any internal ways of settling disputes are described in Roma-led sources; do not assume one.
- **Dievturi and the main churches:** check what each teaches on obeying unjust law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the legal profession and enforcement debates; mixed for everyday bending of rules; thin for internal community justice.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for comrades' courts and prison speech; German and Latin for Švābe's sources; Polish and Belarusian for the border.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war-crimes cases and Soviet repression trials are contested; present each account as an account. Prison sources concern living people. Law here is its spirit; anything about what statutes say goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing; legal-culture pointers here are professional, folk and press sources, none touched by a standing rule.
