# Latvia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian (folk-belief terms vary by dialect); Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia (dominant in Soviet patrol records and the Interfront press); Polish and Belarusian of Daugavpils; Latvian Romani; Yiddish; Swedish and German for the fortress and town-planning records. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga's Old Town and microdistricts against small towns (Kuldīga, Cēsis, Sigulda):** check complaints, noise rules and tidiness contests in each.
- **Rural Vidzeme and Kurzeme against Latgale:** check whether farmstead and village layouts and tidiness contests differ by region.
- **Latvian and Russian speakers:** check how 1989-91 is told as order kept or order broken in each language's press, as accounts.
- **Old Believers:** check village layout and prayer-house order in Pomor community sources.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Daugavpils:** check how the planned city appears in their memoirs and parish histories.
- **The Livonian Coast and the Suiti:** check how coastal and Alsunga villages describe a well-kept place, in their own sources.
- **The Jewish community:** check prewar Riga and Dvinsk neighborhoods in Yiddish memoirs.
- **Roma:** check experiences of being policed for "public order" through Roma-led sources.
- **Generations:** check memories of Soviet patrols against views of today's municipal police.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policing, planning and protest; mixed for household order; thin for Livonian and Roma sources.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and German for fortress and town plans; Russian for *družinas* and Interfront; Yiddish for prewar neighborhoods.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Interfront and the events of 1989-91 are contested; present each account as an account. Do not treat any community as a source of disorder.
- **Held for review:** nothing; order's pointers here are folk, planning, police and survey sources that the standing rules do not reach.
