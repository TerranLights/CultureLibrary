# Latvia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia; Polish and Belarusian of Latgale; Ukrainian; Latvian Romani; Yiddish; German. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Soviet-built districts (Imanta, Purvciems, Ķengarags; Daugavpils's Ķīmiķu ciemats):** check whether neighbors in large blocks organize as readily as owners of older houses.
- **Vidzeme's Gauja valley towns (Cēsis, Valmiera):** check small-town civic life against Riga's.
- **Zemgale's grain parishes and Sēlija's thinly settled ones:** check how depopulation changes who is left to keep a parish going.
- **The Livonian Coast:** check descendants' own accounts of the villages before and after the border zone.
- **The Suiti of Alsunga:** check community life tied to the parish and its feasts, beside Lutheran neighbors.
- **Latgalians in Rēzekne and Ludza districts:** check Catholic parish life against secular rural associations.
- **Russian speakers in Daugavpils and Riga:** check community ties in Russian-language organizations apart from Latvian-language ones.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Latgale:** check each association's own record of members and parish ties.
- **Ukrainians:** check pre-2022 residents and new arrivals separately.
- **The Jewish community and Roma:** check each through its own organizations and records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Riga housing, rural development and survey data; mixed for minorities; thin for Roma and Livonian descendants.
- **Languages needed:** German for Mancelius; Russian for Daugavpils and Soviet housing records; Ukrainian for the newest community; Yiddish for burial-society books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** loss of community under Soviet border rule and in 1941 is part of contested history: give each account as an account. Private details of host families and Roma households stay out of the research.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the community sources here are housing, rural and minority bodies whose names raised no standing rule.
