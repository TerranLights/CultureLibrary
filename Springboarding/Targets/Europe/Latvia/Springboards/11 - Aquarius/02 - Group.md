# Latvia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; the Russian of Latvia; Livonian; Polish and Belarusian of Daugavpils; Latvian Romani; German for the Brethren's records. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and what members are expected to do together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme's former Brethren parishes against Kurzeme:** check whether the 18th-c. lay bands left a different habit of forming groups.
- **Riga's universities:** check student corporations, student unions and informal circles as three distinct ways to join.
- **Latgale (Daugavpils, Rēzekne):** check which groups (parish, language association, party) people report belonging to.
- **Russian speakers:** check the school-protest generation of 2004 against older Soviet-era residents and against Old Believers.
- **Latgalians:** check Latgalian student and music groups against Latvian-wide bodies.
- **The Suiti and the Livonian Coast:** check ensembles and associations as the forms each uses to act together.
- **Poles, Belarusians and Lithuanians:** check how school and parish groups overlap in each.
- **The Jewish community:** check interwar political youth groups apart from present-day community bodies.
- **Roma:** check working and kin groups only through Roma-led sources.
- **Dievturība followers:** check how a congregation decides and admits members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest, deportation and survey records; mixed for clubs and student bodies; thin for Roma and for informal groups.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Brethren's archives; Russian for the 2004 movement, the 1949 files and 1990s crime reporting; Yiddish for interwar party papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1949 deportations, the 2004 school protests and the 2009 riot are contested: present each as an account. Deportation files name living families; use published memorial lists.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the group sources here (congregations, student bodies, protests, surveys) raised no standing rule.
