# Latvia: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; the Russian of Latvia (also for Old Believer sources); German and Latin for the Reformation; Yiddish and German for Jewish modernizers; Polish; Livonian. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga's German-speaking burghers and the Latvian countryside, 16th c.:** check how far the Reformation reached rural parishes and when.
- **Vidzeme and Kurzeme farmers, 1880s:** check whether petitions to the Manasein inspection asked for the same changes in each province.
- **Latgale's Old Believers:** check congregations that took *edinoverie* against those that refused.
- **Latgalians:** check language reformers against those who opposed new spellings.
- **Riga intelligentsia of 1988 against Popular Front branches in small towns:** check whether reform meant the same in each.
- **Russian speakers:** check Interfront supporters, Russian-speaking Popular Front members and the undecided as three separate groups.
- **Small towns merged in 2021 (in Zemgale, Sēlija and Latgale):** check each place's objections in its own record.
- **The Jewish community:** check the modernizing and traditional congregations of the 19th c. separately.
- **Livonians and the Suiti:** check revival work since 1988 as reform from inside a community.
- **Former LTP inmates and their families:** check their own accounts against official ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Atmoda years and for recent state reforms; mixed for the 19th c.; thin for Old Believer and Livonian sides.
- **Languages needed:** German and Latin for the Reformation; Russian for the Manasein files, *edinoverie*, Interfront and the LTP; Latgalian for spelling debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Interfront and the reforms of 1988-91 are contested; present each side as an account. LTP case files concern named, often living, people; use published sources. The legal text of the 2021 reform is Libra's; here only the arguments and the public record.
- **Held for review:** nothing; every reformer, movement and institution named here falls within the rules.
