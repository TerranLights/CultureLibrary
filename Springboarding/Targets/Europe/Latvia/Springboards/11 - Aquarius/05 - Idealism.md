# Latvia: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; German for the late-Enlightenment layer; Latgalian; the Russian of Latvia, including Church Slavonic terms for Old Believer sources; Yiddish and Hebrew; Polish; Livonian. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Riga's students and young professionals:** check whether idealistic programs draw mainly from the capital or from regional universities (Daugavpils, Rēzekne, Liepāja, Valmiera).
- **The 1988-91 generation and those born after 1991:** check stated ideals and disillusion in each cohort's own words.
- **Latgale:** check Catholic, Old Believer and secular ideals of right living as three separate strands.
- **Russian speakers:** check Soviet-era idealists, post-1991 civic activists and religious communities apart.
- **The Jewish community:** check interwar Zionist, Bundist and religious ideals apart from present-day community aims.
- **Dievturība followers:** check how today's congregations relate to the 1930s founders' writings.
- **Livonian descendants and the Suiti:** check revival work as ideals each community states for itself.
- **Rural incomers and long-settled farmers in Vidzeme and the Gauja valley:** check whether back-to-the-land newcomers and older residents describe the countryside differently.
- **Roma and Poles:** check their own organizations for stated aims; likely thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and Soviet youth press; mixed for NGOs and youth surveys; thin for ecovillages, Roma and Livonian sources.
- **Languages needed:** German for Merkel; Russian and Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts; Hebrew and Yiddish for the training farms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Dievturi movement's interwar politics and Soviet ideals are contested; read each in its own words and beside its critics. Old Believer religious life is partly private; rely on what congregations publish.
- **Held for review:** nothing; no idealist, movement or program found for this concept fell under a standing rule.
