# Latvia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia; Polish; Yiddish and Hebrew; Latin and Middle High German for the crusade chronicles. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vidzeme and Kurzeme:** check whose fallen are named on parish memorials, and which wars each region's memorials cover.
- **Latgale (Rēzekne, Aglona, Daugavpils):** check Catholic martyr and vow language against the Lutheran regions' war memory.
- **Riga:** check the barricades generation's memory against older war memory.
- **Russian speakers:** check family memories of the Second World War told in Russian as a separate account, alongside the Old Believers' own memory of persecution.
- **Legion veterans' families and their critics:** check the 16 March accounts side by side, each as an account.
- **The Jewish community:** check how *kiddush ha-Shem* and the Rumbula dead are spoken of within the community, in its own words.
- **Poles of Daugavpils:** check the memory of Polish soldiers who fought for the city in 1920.
- **Women carers in small towns:** check whether care falls on daughters, wives or sons, by municipality.
- **The Livonian Coast:** check fishermen's sea rescues as a local record of risk for others.
- **Roma:** check Roma-led memory of the war years, which is rarely written down.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war memory and church history; mixed for caregiving; thin for Livonian, Roma and Polish sources on this concept.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the account of Bishop Berthold's death; Russian for the Second World War family memories; Yiddish and Hebrew for martyr literature; Latgalian for Catholic devotions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whose sacrifice counts (Legion, Red Army, national partisans, Holocaust victims, deportees) is contested ground: give each as an account, never set them against each other, and name each community by its own name.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the chronicle, war memory, church and Jewish sources here sit inside the rules.
