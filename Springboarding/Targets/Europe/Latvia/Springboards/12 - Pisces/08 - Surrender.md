# Latvia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Latvia, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Latvian; Latgalian; Livonian; the Russian of Latvia; Polish; Yiddish; German and Latin for capitulation records. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kurzeme coast (Liepāja, Ventspils, Talsi):** check memories of May 1945 and the boats, by family and village.
- **Latgale (Preiļi, Rēzekne, Ludza):** check Catholic resignation in prayer books against holdouts like Pīnups from the same district.
- **Riga:** check how the city's 1710 and 1940 surrenders are taught and remembered.
- **Vidzeme and Zemgale:** check fate sayings in the *dainas* by region.
- **Russian speakers:** check how 1940, 1945 and 1991 are told as surrender or liberation in their own accounts, each as an account.
- **Old Believers:** check humility teaching apart from Orthodox neighbors.
- **Legion veterans' families and national partisans' families:** check how each tells the end of its war.
- **The Jewish community:** check *bitokhn* in pre-1941 religious writing, apart from Holocaust memory.
- **The Livonian Coast and the Suiti lands:** check local sea and war endings in their own records.
- **Families of the dying:** check whether home deaths differ between rural Latgale and Riga.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and political surrender; mixed for end-of-life data; thin for Livonian and Roma sources and for therapy practice.
- **Languages needed:** German and Latin for the 1230 and 1710 documents; Russian for Old Believer teaching and the Russian-language war memory; Latgalian for the press-ban prayer books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1940, 1945, the Legion and the partisans are contested ground: each memory is one community's account, told under that community's own name. Assisted dying law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing; treaties, prayer books, war memoirs and health data needed no holding.
