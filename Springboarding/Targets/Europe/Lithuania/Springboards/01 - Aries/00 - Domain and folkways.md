## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitians, Samogitians, Dzūkians, Suvalkians:** compare christening, naming and dress customs region by region; the ethnographic regions are the usual frame of the folklore archives.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Klaipėda region (*lietuvininkai*):** check Lutheran naming and Prussian-era records of the person; a different church and state history.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region (Šalčininkai, Vilnius district):** check how self-labels (*tutejszy*, Polish, Lithuanian Pole) are chosen in census and speech.
- **Russians and Old Believers (Visaginas, Zarasai, Vilnius):** compare patronymics and Orthodox or Old Believer name-giving with Catholic practice.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check the census answers against home language; the categories may not fit.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check Muslim and civil names side by side.
- **Karaim of Trakai:** a very small community; check its own publications first.
- **The Jewish community, and Litvak history:** separate the present community from the prewar Litvak world.
- **Roma:** check naming and self-description in community sources, not only in outsiders' reports.
- **Ukrainians and recent arrivals:** check how newcomers' names are adapted to Lithuanian endings.
- **Romuva:** check pre-Christian revival names and name-giving rites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names, costume and folklore; mixed for psychology of the self; thin in Lithuanian-language scholarship on the smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian and its dialects, Polish, Russian, Belarusian, Yiddish; Karaim and the Arabic-script *kitab* writing for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** name spelling for the Polish minority (letters *w*, *ł*) is a live public dispute; present each side's account. Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this set is written for Lithuania as it stands.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; every naming, costume and personhood pointer here falls inside the standing rules.
