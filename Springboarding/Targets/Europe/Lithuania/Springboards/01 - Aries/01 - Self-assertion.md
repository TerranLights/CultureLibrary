# Lithuania: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with Samogitian, Aukštaitian and Dzūkian speech), Vilnius-region Polish, Lithuanian Russian, Belarusian, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitians and Aukštaitians:** check whether proverb and wedding-song collections from each region license sharp speech in the same settings.
- **Suvalkians (Sūduva):** check market and bargaining records from the region separately (Marijampolė's used-car market is one modern site to sample), so one town does not stand for the region.
- **Vilnius and Kaunas against villages:** check workplace and service encounters in the cities apart from rural community meetings.
- **Poles of the Šalčininkai and Vilnius districts:** check minority-rights campaigns as collective assertion, separately from personal speech norms.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas:** check how the closing of the Ignalina plant was contested; a test case of a town pressing claims.
- **Old Believers:** check community records for how disputes with clergy and outsiders were voiced.
- **Litvak yeshiva culture:** check Musar texts against memoirs; the ideal of humility and the practice may differ.
- **Lutherans of Lithuania Minor:** check Prussian parish and school records for how farmhands and tenants spoke to masters.
- **Roma:** check whether community sources and outside reports describe the same disputes.
- **Women and men:** check assertion norms by gender within each group, not one national rule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and literature; mixed for modern measured data; thin for the Karaim and Tatars on this concept (look in their community press first).
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Polish and Russian for modern media; Yiddish for the Musar material; Ruthenian chancery language and Latin for the Metrica.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** minority-rights disputes (Polish schools, name spelling) are contested; present each party's account. Soviet-era "complaints" may be denunciations; read them critically.
- **Held for review:** no speaking-up or complaint pointer was set aside under the standing rules.
