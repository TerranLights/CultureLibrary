## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia:** check how the Saulė battle (1236) and Tadas Blinda appear in regional museum displays (the Alka museum in Telšiai) against national ones.
- **Dzūkija:** check partisan memory in local museums and bunker sites; the postwar fighting there left many local sources.
- **Aukštaitija and Suvalkija:** check which courage stories local schools and museums tell, to see whether the same ones recur.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check memory of the Home Army (*Armia Krajowa*) and of wartime clashes with Lithuanians; both sides' accounts conflict, so present each.
- **Russian speakers:** check Soviet war memory and Victory Day observances, kept apart from Lithuanian state commemoration.
- **The Jewish community:** check ghetto fighters, partisans and survivors, and how rescuers are remembered; Holocaust memory is contested ground.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check accounts of Tatar soldiers in Grand Duchy armies as the community tells them.
- **Deportees and their families:** check endurance stories in memoirs from different decades.
- **Women and men:** check whether endurance, rescue and armed fighting are honored differently by sex.
- **Belarusians and Old Believers:** check whether their own wartime stories are recorded at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war, resistance and rescue; mixed for everyday courage; thin for minorities' own courage narratives.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Polish, Russian, Yiddish, German; Latin for the chronicles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the postwar partisans, 1941, the Holocaust and Polish-Lithuanian wartime clashes are contested; some honored figures are accused of wartime crimes. Present each account and name each community by its own name.
- **Held for review:** no courage or rescue pointer is held; contested figures stay in, flagged above, rather than being dropped.
