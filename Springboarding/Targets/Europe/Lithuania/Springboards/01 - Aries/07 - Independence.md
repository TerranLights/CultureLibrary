## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dzūkija:** check foraging and smallholding as provision on poor sandy soils; local museums and festival records hold it.
- **Suvalkija and Samogitia:** check homestead dispersal records region by region; the reform reached areas at different times.
- **Aukštaitija:** check flax and craft households as a source of cash income alongside the farm.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check fishermen's and farmers' self-provisioning in Prussian-era and postwar sources separately.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check land restitution after 1991 and its disputes, which shaped who could farm independently; present each side's account.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas:** check how the town's dependence on a single employer was discussed before and after closure.
- **Old Believer villages:** check community self-help and farming records in the northeast.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check trades and market gardening as each community records them; little is published, so start with community museums.
- **The Jewish community:** check prewar self-help networks against today's community welfare.
- **Roma:** check how dependence and self-support are described in Roma-led sources rather than in outsiders' reports.
- **Rural and urban elders:** check who lives alone, with children, or in care, by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national independence and agrarian history; mixed for household self-reliance; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma economic life.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Polish, Russian, Yiddish, Hebrew (Yizkor books); Ruthenian and Polish for the 16th-century reform.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** benefit stigma touches Roma and poor households; avoid group labels. Land restitution in the Vilnius region is contested.
- **Held for review:** no self-reliance or statehood pointer was set aside under the standing rules.
