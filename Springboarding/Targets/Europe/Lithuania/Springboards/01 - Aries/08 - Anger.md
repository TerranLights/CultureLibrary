# Lithuania: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitians, Aukštaitians, Dzūkians, Suvalkians:** check curse formulas and charm texts in the folklore archives region by region; dialect sources may hold words absent from the standard.
- **Kaunas and Vilnius:** check street protest records in each city; 1972 and 2009 happened in different places with different crowds.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check how grievances over schools and spelling are voiced in the community's own media, beside Lithuanian coverage; accounts conflict.
- **Russian speakers:** check how Russian obscenity is used and judged inside Russian-speaking and mixed families.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check whether quarrel and curse terms in local speech blend Belarusian, Polish and Lithuanian forms.
- **The Jewish community:** check Musar teaching against memoir and fiction (for example the yeshiva novels) for how anger was handled in practice.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check religious teaching on anger in each community's prayer and moral texts; few are translated.
- **Women and men:** check how anger is recorded in domestic-violence data, crisis-center reports and surveys by sex.
- **Generations:** check Soviet-raised and post-1990 cohorts for differences in swearing and in protest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and protest history; mixed for psychology; thin for minority religious teaching on anger.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian (including dialect transcriptions), Polish, Russian, Hebrew and Yiddish, Latin (Łasicki).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalanta's death is a suicide; handle with care. Domestic-violence records protect victims' identities. Hate-speech rulings name groups; do not repeat slurs.
- **Held for review:** none of the anger, curse or protest pointers needed holding.
