# Lithuania: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** standard Lithuanian with Samogitian, Aukštaitian and Dzūkian dialect forms; Polish as spoken around Vilnius; Lithuania's Russian; southeastern Belarusian; Yiddish for Litvak history; Karaim and the Tatar *kitab* tradition for those two communities. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, food ways, dress ways and wealth ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ethnographic regions:** check Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija one by one on dowry goods, holiday tables and costume; the folklore archives sort material this way.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** compare the Lutheran farm and fishing households of Klaipėda, Šilutė and Nida with the Catholic inland.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check manor, farm and town households around Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district in Polish-language sources.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** compare fasting tables and household goods in Visaginas and Zarasai.
- **Southeastern Belarusian speakers:** check which language their household and food words are recorded in.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check feast foods and alms at Kurban Bayram, and household teaching in the *kitabs*.
- **Karaim of Trakai:** check the community's own food and household publications.
- **Litvak history:** separate the prewar livelihoods of the Jewish towns from today's community.
- **Roma:** check trade and livelihood in community-made sources; scarce, so note where nothing is found.
- **Ukrainians:** check how recent arrivals set up households.
- **Romuva:** check harvest and offering customs as the movement itself describes them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, dress, food and post-1990 statistics; mixed for Soviet-era household life; thin for the smaller minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with its dialects; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for the council records; Karaim and Arabic-script Slavic for those two communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** property taken in 1940-1953 (nationalization, deportations) and the property of murdered Jews after 1941 are contested ground; present each community's account by its own name. Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled: the Taurus sheets are written for the country as it now stands, with no border decided here.
