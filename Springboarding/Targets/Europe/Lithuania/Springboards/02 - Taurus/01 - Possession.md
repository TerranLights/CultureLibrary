# Lithuania: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with dialect dictionaries), Polish, Russian, Belarusian; Yiddish for the Litvak past; Karaim; the Tatars' Arabic-script Slavic. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** compare dowry-chest motifs and owner's marks; museums file the chests by region.
- **Dzūkija:** check forest-gathering rights and the keeping of bee trees in the Varėna woods.
- **Curonian Spit families:** check how gear and boats passed down in the *kuršininkai* fishing villages.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check heirlooms carried or left at the 1944-1947 departures to Poland, and what stayed with families who remained.
- **Old Believers:** check which icons and books families kept apart from church buildings.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check which manuscripts families still hold privately.
- **Karaim:** check whether household objects went to the museum or stayed in homes.
- **Litvak history:** check prewar household inventories against what survivors and later donors brought back.
- **Roma:** check community-made sources on movable goods; outsiders' reports dominate, so note the gap.
- **Romuva:** check objects the movement uses at its rites, as its members describe them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in museums and folklore; rich but painful in deportation and Holocaust archives; thin for present-day household goods beyond the statistics.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Russian for Soviet files, Polish, Yiddish, Old Church Slavonic for Old Believer books, Arabic-script Belarusian and Polish for the *kitabs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation inventories name families still living; looted Jewish property and the 1941 events are contested ground, so give each community's account as an account.
- **Held for review:** none for possession.
