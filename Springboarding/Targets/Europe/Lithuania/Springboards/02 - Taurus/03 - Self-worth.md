# Lithuania: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian and its regional speech, the Polish and Russian spoken in Lithuania, Belarusian, Yiddish, and Latin for one 16th-century source. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Village and city:** compare rural districts with Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda on what people say their worth rests on (land, work, schooling, income).
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija:** check whether regional speech is treated as a mark of low or high standing in schools and media.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check how recognition of the community and its schools figures in its own press.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas:** check how workers spoke of worth after the Ignalina plant closed.
- **Deportees' families:** check memoirs and associations for how worth was kept or rebuilt after return.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check the community's own writing on its noble past and its present standing.
- **Karaim:** check how a very small community writes about its worth as a people.
- **Litvak history:** compare learning, piety and livelihood as grounds of standing in Yiddish memoirs.
- **Roma:** check community-made testimony on being looked down on; outsiders' material dominates, so note gaps.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians who arrived after 2020-2022:** check how loss of profession and status is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and helplines since 2000; mixed for history; Tatar, Karaim and Roma voices on standing are scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with an ear for regional speech; Polish and Russian for minority press; Yiddish for Vilna memoirs; Latin for Michalo Lituanus; Belarusian for recent arrivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and bullying data touch living people; cite aggregates only. Public talk of a "small nation" inferiority is a discourse to study, never a finding.
- **Held for review:** none at this sheet.
