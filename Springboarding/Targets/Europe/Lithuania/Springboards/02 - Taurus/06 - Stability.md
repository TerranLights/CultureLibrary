# Lithuania: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (the Dzūkian and northeastern Aukštaitian material especially), Polish of Vilnius, Russian (including Old Believer usage), Belarusian, German for the Curonian Spit's Prussian records, Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dzūkija and Aukštaitija:** compare surviving old villages and the families who stayed against districts emptied by melioration.
- **Samogitia:** check farm continuity in families that kept or regained homesteads after 1991.
- **Klaipėda and the Spit:** check how resettlement after 1945 broke and restarted community life in Lithuania Minor.
- **Vilnius district Poles:** check family continuity on the land through 1944-1947 departures and Soviet rule.
- **Visaginas:** check how a young planned town built its routines, and what the plant closure did to them.
- **Old Believers:** check how communities near Zarasai and Utena kept rites unchanged.
- **Karaim and Tatars:** check six centuries of residence as each community narrates it, against archival breaks.
- **Litvak history:** check the destruction of continuity in 1941-1944 through survivors' and community accounts.
- **Roma:** check settledness and movement in community testimony; outside records may mislabel them.
- **Emigrant families:** check households split by work migration since 2004.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official statistics and heritage; mixed for Soviet rural upheaval; few studies of continuity among small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Polish, Russian, Belarusian, German (Prussian records), Yiddish and Hebrew (cemetery inscriptions).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** nostalgia for Soviet times is contested; treat it as an account. The 1941-1944 destruction of Jewish life and the deportations need survivors' and communities' own framing.
- **Held for review:** nothing for stability was held back.
