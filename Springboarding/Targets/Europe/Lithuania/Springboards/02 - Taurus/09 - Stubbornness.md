# Lithuania: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (Samogitian sources for the parish and Constance material), Polish and Russian for minority and Soviet records, Belarusian, Hebrew and Yiddish for the Vilna disputes, Latin for medieval petitions. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia:** check how parish histories and local museums tell resistance stories, and test any "regional temperament" talk against sources.
- **Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** compare partisan memory and its local commemorations district by district.
- **Klaipėda region:** check Lutheran communities' refusals and accommodations under German, Lithuanian and Soviet rule.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check how the community's press frames its stands on language and schools.
- **Old Believers:** check community writing on keeping old rites under three empires.
- **Litvak history:** compare the Misnagdim of Vilna with Hasidic communities in Lithuania, and beyond it as comparison only.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check how each community narrates keeping its faith over six centuries.
- **Russian speakers:** check how Visaginas and Vilnius Russian-language media describe holding out against or adapting to change after 1990.
- **Romuva:** compare the movement's own story of persistence with Soviet records.
- **Families:** check estrangement and reconciliation in advice columns and forums.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective resistance and church history; mixed for everyday stubbornness; thin for psychological measures.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, Latin (Constance), Russian (tsarist and KGB files), Polish, Hebrew and Yiddish (Wilensky's documents).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partisan memory, the 1941 events and the Polish-Lithuanian language disputes are contested ground; give each account as an account and each community by its own name. Claims of a stubborn regional or national character are folklore to study, never findings.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld on stubbornness.
