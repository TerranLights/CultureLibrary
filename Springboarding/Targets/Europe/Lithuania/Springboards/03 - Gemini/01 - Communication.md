# Lithuania: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (standard and Samogitian, Aukštaitian, Dzūkian speech); Polish of the Vilnius lands; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian of the southeast; Yiddish for Litvak history; Karaim; Lithuanian Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitians:** check when Samogitian is spoken and when the standard takes over (school, office, church), since that switch shapes who sounds formal.
- **Vilnius-region Poles in Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** check code-switching between the local Polish, standard Polish, Lithuanian and Russian by listener and place.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas and Klaipėda:** compare everyday talk in a mostly Russian-speaking town with a mixed port city.
- **Generations:** check language choice among people schooled in the Soviet system against those schooled after 1990, since schooling set which languages each learned.
- **Lithuania Minor:** check the Lutheran letter and sermon tradition of the Klaipėda region as its own written line, beside the Catholic one.
- **Deaf Lithuanians:** check Deaf community sources on communication with hearing family and officials.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check which language each setting uses (prayer, home, community meeting), as the community describes it.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** check how Karaim is used beside Lithuanian and Russian in a very small community.
- **Litvak history:** separate Yiddish, Hebrew, Russian and Polish use in prewar Vilna, Kaunas and the small towns.
- **Roma:** check community-run sources on Romani use and on talk with officials; likely scarce.
- **Ukrainian newcomers since 2022:** check language-brokering by children for parents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for standard-language debate and broadcasts; mixed for recorded everyday talk; thin for minority-language talk today.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian and its dialects; Polish; Russian; Yiddish; Latin and Chancery Slavonic for the pre-modern pointers; Lithuanian Sign Language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** language choice in the Vilnius region and for Russian speakers can be politically charged; present each community's account in its own words. Language-law enforcement goes to Libra.
