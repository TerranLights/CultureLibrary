# Lithuania: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with dialect folklore); Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish for Litvak sources; Karaim; Latin and German for early outsider accounts. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **City and village:** check museum, library and science-center use in Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda against small towns of Dzūkija and Aukštaitija.
- **Samogitia:** check how the outsider accounts of Samogitian religion are now read and answered by Samogitian writers and museums.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** compare the pastors' ethnographies with what the Klaipėda region's own museums collect.
- **Generations:** separate Soviet-era popular science and the post-1990 science centers and online sources.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check which popular-science media and museums they use, Polish or Lithuanian.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas:** check the town's technical clubs and libraries as settings for inquiry.
- **Karaim of Trakai and Lithuanian Tatars:** check what each community says about visitors' questions and which questions it welcomes.
- **Litvak descendants and today's Jewish community:** compare the genealogists' questions with what local archives and museums can answer.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement handles public curiosity about Baltic faith, in its own publications.
- **Old Believers and Roma:** check how each community describes outsiders' interest in it; data likely scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for museums, folklore and outsider accounts; mixed for everyday nosiness and children's questions; thin for minorities' own views of being studied.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Latin and German for Łasicki and Prätorius; Polish; Russian; Yiddish and Hebrew for genealogy; Karaim.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ask the smallest communities (Karaim, Tatars, Roma) how they want outside interest handled before studying them; the Teutonic-era and pastoral accounts of Baltic religion are adversaries' and outsiders' views; Gimbutas's theories are disputed. Mark each as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; no museum, riddle or outsider-account pointer was set aside.
