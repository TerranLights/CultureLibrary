# Lithuania: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish (Vilnius lands); Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; German for Rhesa's edition. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija villages:** check isolation of older residents and population change in districts such as Ignalina, Zarasai and Anykščiai against regional towns.
- **Samogitians and Dzūkians in the cities:** check how home-district ties are kept up through associations, festivals and visits.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check family and school ties across the Polish border, and community centers at home.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check kin ties across eastern borders and the strain on them after 2014 and 2022, in community voices.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** compare port-city networks and seasonal workers with the inland.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check ties between mosque communities and with Tatars in Poland and Belarus.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** check links with Karaim communities elsewhere and how a very small group keeps ties.
- **Litvak descendants and today's community:** check visits, restitution of ties to towns, and the community's own networks.
- **Roma:** check kin and community networks in Roma-run sources; note gaps.
- **Ukrainian and Belarusian newcomers:** check how arrivals since 2020 form ties with locals and among themselves.
- **Romuva communities:** check gatherings at midsummer (*Rasos*) as ties among scattered members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for songs, Soviet-era networks and emigration ties; mixed for loneliness; thin for the smallest communities' networks.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; German (Rhesa); Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for the society records; Karaim.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet favor networks touch on informers and complicity; kin ties to Russia and Belarus are politically charged; let each family and community describe those ties itself.
- **Held for review:** none; no tie, network or loneliness pointer was set aside.
