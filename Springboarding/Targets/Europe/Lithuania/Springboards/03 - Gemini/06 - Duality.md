# Lithuania: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish (the Vilnius lands' identity debate); Russian; Belarusian; German (Lithuania Minor); Yiddish (Litvak identity); Karaim and the Tatars' Arabic-script Slavic; Latin for early-modern formulas. **Concept in one line:** living with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once, dividing the world into pairs, and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Vilnius region (Šalčininkai, Vilnius district):** check self-naming as Polish, Lithuanian or "local", and switching between languages, village by village.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** compare families who named themselves *lietuvininkai* or *Memelländer*, and what happened to each label after 1945.
- **Samogitians:** check how regional and national belonging are held together in Samogitian cultural groups.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check how residents of Visaginas and the northeast describe being Russian-speaking and Lithuanian citizens.
- **Belarusian speakers of the southeast:** check self-naming between Belarusian, Polish and "local".
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check how the community describes being Muslim and long settled, in its own publications.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** check the community's own statements on its identity; weigh outside accounts against them.
- **Litvak history:** separate Litvak, Lithuanian-Jewish and Lithuanian labels as used before and after 1941.
- **Mixed families:** check children of Lithuanian-Polish and Lithuanian-Russian marriages on language and belonging.
- **Returning emigrants:** check people back from the UK and Ireland on living between two countries.
- **Romuva and Catholics:** check where old and new religious practice meet in the same family or village.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Polish-Lithuanian and Lithuania Minor identity record; mixed for Soviet double lives; thin for twins and paired symbols.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian and Polish for the Vilnius-identity debates; German for Sudermann's Lithuania Minor; Latin for the Jesuit reports on Samogitia; Russian and Belarusian for Soviet-era double lives and the southeast; Yiddish for the Mitnagdic writings behind Nadler's study; Karaim for that community's own self-descriptions.
