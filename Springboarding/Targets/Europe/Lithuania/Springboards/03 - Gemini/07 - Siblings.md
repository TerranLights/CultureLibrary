# Lithuania: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with the diminutives of the *dainos*); Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; the Tatars' Polish-language genealogies. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check inheritance and payout customs among brothers and sisters, since the ethnographic studies sort them by region.
- **City and village:** check sibling numbers and contact in Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda against farm districts.
- **Emigrant families:** check households where older siblings stayed in charge while parents worked abroad.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check kin terms and sibling duties in Polish-language family memoirs of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** check household and sibling patterns in Old Believer villages against Russian speakers in Visaginas.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check family lines in the armorials and the community's own genealogies.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** check how a very small community keeps track of kin across generations.
- **Litvak history:** check siblings scattered by emigration and the Holocaust in memoirs and testimony collections.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lutheran farm inheritance in the Klaipėda region with the Catholic inland.
- **Roma:** check community-made accounts of sibling roles; say where data stop.
- **Deported and partisan families:** check how siblings were split by deportation and war, in each family's words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk songs, tales and family statistics; mixed for inheritance practice; thin for sibling life among the smallest minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, including dialect kin words, for the Juškas' songs and the tale variants; Polish for the Mackiewicz brothers and the Tatar armorial; Russian for Old Believer family histories; Belarusian for southeastern kin terms; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak family memoirs; Karaim for that community's genealogies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partisan and deportation memoirs belong to contested ground; present them as accounts and name whose. Children in care and emigrants' children are private matters: aggregate sources only.
- **Held for review:** none; no song, tale or family-data pointer for siblings was set aside.
