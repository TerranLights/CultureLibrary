# Lithuania: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian with Samogitian, Aukštaitian and Dzūkian house and kin words; Vilnius-lands Polish; Lithuania's Russian; southeastern Belarusian; Yiddish (Litvak households); Trakai Karaim; Tatar *kitab* texts. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family: household forms, house and settlement building, how kin are reckoned, and how children are raised.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia against Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** check house plans and the hearth's place, which the farmstead surveys record region by region.
- **Suvalkija and Lithuania Minor:** compare Suvalkija farm households with Lutheran homesteads around Klaipėda, which grew under another state.
- **Panel-block cities against villages:** check three-generation living in Vilnius, Kaunas, Šiauliai and Panevėžys flats against rural homesteads.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check how 1897, interwar and Soviet sources counted their households, since each state drew categories differently.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** check Old Believer village households separately from Russian-speaking families who came in the Soviet decades, as in Visaginas.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check whether the sources count them apart or fold them into Poles or Russians.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check household and settlement forms in their own publications first.
- **The Litvak community:** separate the destroyed prewar household world from today's small community.
- **Roma:** check housing and household records, and note where only official sources exist.
- **Ukrainians:** separate the older Soviet-era community from families who arrived after 2022.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census, house types and demography; mixed for child-rearing practice; thin for Karaim, Tatar and Roma households today.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect house and kin terms; Polish; Russian (imperial and Soviet records); Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew; Karaim; Arabic-script Slavic for Tatar books; German for Lithuania Minor records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled; the Cancer sheets are written for the country as it is today. Homes lost to deportation, the Holocaust and homestead clearance are contested ground: give each account as whose it is. Any family-law question goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no household or house-type pointer was set aside.
