# Lithuania: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (standard and regional words for house, hearth and birthplace); Polish as used around Vilnius; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish for the lost Litvak homes; the Polish and *kitab* writings of the Tatars. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija and Samogitia:** check whether the house's honored corner, hearth and threshold customs are recorded the same way, since the house-belief material is regional.
- **Dzūkija:** compare forest-village homes with homes on farmland, to see whether "home" reaches past the yard into the forest.
- **Suvalkija:** check how farm families there speak of the *tėviškė* in memoirs written after leaving for town.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** compare fishing houses and Lutheran homes around Klaipėda with inland Catholic farms.
- **Kaunas, Vilnius and Šiauliai flats:** check how block residents make a Soviet-planned flat their own.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check family manor and village homes in Polish memoir and fiction against Lithuanian accounts of the same places.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** check whether Visaginas families, who came for the plant, call the town home, and how Old Believer families speak of long-held villages.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check what each community calls its home place in Lithuania across six centuries.
- **The Litvak community:** check how survivors and descendants speak of homes they lost, and what return visits record.
- **Roma:** check housing records and the Roma Community Centre's material on the Kirtimai rehousing, noting how sparse they are.
- **Romuva:** check whether household fire rites appear in its own publications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, literature and housing statistics; mixed for how people describe home; thin for Old Believer, Tatar and Roma homes.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect house words; Latin for Łasicki; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; German for Curonian Spit records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homes taken over after the Holocaust and after deportation, and property return, are contested; give each account as whose it is and send property law to Libra.
