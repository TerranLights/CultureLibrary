*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with the full in-law vocabulary and the dialects' kin words); Polish of the Vilnius district and Šalčininkai; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak family records; Karaim; Polish and Arabic-script records of the Tatars. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija, Suvalkija:** check whether in-law terms, godparent duties and who lived with whom after marriage are recorded differently region by region.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lutheran family records and confirmation customs around Klaipėda with Catholic godparent customs inland.
- **Vilnius and the Vilnius region:** check mixed Polish-Lithuanian-Russian marriages in Vilnius, Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district, and how such families name and raise their children.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** check priestless Old Believer marriage and godparent practice apart from Orthodox and secular Russian-speaking families.
- **Belarusians:** check which church records and which language a southeastern family's records sit in.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check how these very small communities keep track of kin and whom they marry, in their own records.
- **The Litvak community:** check what family records survived the Holocaust and how descendants rebuild trees from them.
- **Roma:** check community-made family accounts, and note that official records are the main written source.
- **Romuva:** check its naming and wedding rites as the community describes them.
- **Ukrainians:** check families split by the war since 2022, with members in Lithuania and Ukraine.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kin terms, parish records and family sociology; mixed for godparent practice today; thin for Karaim and Roma family records.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect kin words; Latin and Polish for parish registers; Russian for imperial records; Yiddish and Hebrew; Karaim; Belarusian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who counts as a family is contested in public debate; the constitutional and statutory definitions belong to Libra and Capricorn. Living people's vital records are restricted; family trees of Holocaust victims need care with descendants.
- **Held for review:** none; no kin-term, record or family-sociology pointer was set aside.
