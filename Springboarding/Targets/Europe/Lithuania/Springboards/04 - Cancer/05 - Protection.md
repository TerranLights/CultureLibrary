## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check which saints and objects guarded homesteads in each, since the folk-sculpture surveys map them by district.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check lightning, fire and wolf charms in forest villages against open-field farms.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** check how fishing households guarded against storm and sea, and what the Lutheran tradition put in place of patron saints.
- **Vilnius and Kaunas against small towns:** check whether households rely on alarms and guards or on neighbors and kin.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check which Marian shrines and patron saints Polish parishes invoke, and how the war years are told.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Old Believer icon practice from the Orthodox and from secular Russian speakers in Visaginas.
- **Belarusians:** check southeastern charm vocabulary, which may sit between Lithuanian and Belarusian traditions.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check amulet use and the community's tradition of military service to the grand dukes, as it tells it.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** check the community's own account of its guard duty at Trakai Castle.
- **The Litvak community:** check ghetto self-protection, partisans and rescuers in survivor sources.
- **Roma:** check how Roma families seek protection and from whom, in their own words.
- **Men and women:** check who is expected to defend the household, in surveys and in Riflemen's Union material.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief, war memory and service data; mixed for felt safety; thin for Tatar and Karaim protective practice today.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect charm words; Latin and Polish for the Trakai shrine books; Russian and Belarusian for *obereg* vocabulary; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Kaunas ghetto record; Arabic-script Slavic to read Tatar amulets; Karaim.
