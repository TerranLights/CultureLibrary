# Lithuania: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (roof, refuge, lodging and homelessness words); Polish; Russian (for Soviet housing and for many shelter users); Belarusian; Ukrainian for arrivals since 2022; Yiddish and Hebrew for the old Jewish poorhouses and for hiding; German for Lithuania Minor. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda against small towns:** check where night shelters exist at all, and where people without a roof go when there is none.
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check the custom of lodging beggars and pilgrims overnight in farmhouses, which ethnographers recorded by parish.
- **Dzūkija:** check forest hiding places used in wartime, and how villages remember sheltering.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lepner's lodging customs with Lutheran parish charity houses around Klaipėda and Šilutė.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check Polish charities and parishes that house the needy in Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district.
- **Russian speakers:** check how many night-shelter users speak Russian, and whether services are offered in it.
- **Old Believers:** check the community's own accounts of arriving in the Grand Duchy as people seeking refuge.
- **The Litvak community:** check those who were sheltered and those who sheltered them, in survivor testimony first.
- **Roma:** check rehousing after the Kirtimai settlement's clearance, from residents' accounts and municipal reports.
- **Ukrainians and Belarusians:** compare hosting in homes after 2022 with exiles who arrived after 2020.
- **Asylum seekers of 2021:** check how reception centers were run, from both official and NGO reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for social-housing and Eurostat figures and wartime testimony; mixed for homelessness counts; thin for rural homelessness and for Roma housing.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; German for Lepner; Polish; Russian for shelter users and Soviet housing files; Ukrainian for hosted families; Yiddish and Hebrew for the hekdesh records and testimonies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hiding in 1941-1944, partisan bunkers and the 2021 border crisis are contested; give each account as whose it is. Shelter residents are vulnerable: aggregate data only. Asylum and housing law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no lodging, homelessness or refuge pointer was set aside.
