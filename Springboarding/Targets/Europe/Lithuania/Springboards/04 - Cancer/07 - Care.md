*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (nursing, tending and worry words, and herbal names); Polish, for Vilnius hospice and parish care; Russian, for Soviet health records and Russian-speaking carers; Belarusian; Yiddish and Polish for the interwar Jewish health societies; the Old Believers' Church Slavonic and Russian. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** check folk remedies and who knew them, since herbal and charm collections give the village of each record.
- **Suvalkija:** check how farm households arranged care of retired parents within the household, in memoirs and ethnography.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lutheran deaconess and parish nursing around Klaipėda with Catholic sisters' work inland.
- **Big cities against depopulated districts:** check how home care reaches the old in emptied villages of Dzūkija and northeastern Aukštaitija compared with Vilnius and Kaunas.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check Polish-language parish and hospice care in the Vilnius district and Šalčininkai.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check Russian-language services for older people in Visaginas and Klaipėda, and Old Believer community care.
- **Belarusians:** check how southeastern families care for elders where services are thin.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check how each tiny community looks after its old members.
- **The Litvak community:** separate interwar health societies from the care given to elderly survivors today.
- **Roma:** check access to care services through mediators' and NGO reports.
- **Men as carers:** check survey data on sons and husbands who care, and whether the sources count them at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for care statistics, nursing history and folk medicine; mixed for family care at home; thin for minority communities' care.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with herb names; Polish for hospice and parish records; Russian for Soviet health files and Old Believer records; Yiddish and Polish for OZE; Belarusian; Latin for older church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** care-home residents and patients are vulnerable: aggregate data only. Care-home scandals should be read from inspectors' and residents' sides. Health-care law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no nursing, folk-remedy or care-service pointer was set aside.
