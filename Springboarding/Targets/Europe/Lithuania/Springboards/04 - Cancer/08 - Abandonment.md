# Lithuania: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (orphan, foundling and emptied-farm vocabulary, and the songs' diminutives); Polish, for families who left the Vilnius region after 1944; German, for the East Prussian children of 1945-1948; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish graves and towns left without their people. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija, Suvalkija:** check how orphan-song variants differ, since the song edition records the parish of each singer.
- **Northeastern Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** check where depopulation is deepest and which households are left alone in emptying villages.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check the families who took in East Prussian orphans, and Lutheran parish records of children left after 1945.
- **City and village:** check where baby boxes stand and where children are taken into care, to see whether cities and rural districts differ.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check family memories of relatives who left for Poland, and of houses left behind, against Lithuanian accounts.
- **Russians and Visaginas:** check how residents speak of their town since the plant closed.
- **Belarusians:** check southeastern villages losing their young, and whether sources treat them separately.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check how communities shrinking through emigration speak of members who left.
- **The Litvak community:** check graves and synagogues left with no community to keep them, and the descendants abroad who return to them.
- **Roma:** check children in care and family separations, with NGO and community sources.
- **Families of the deported:** check children left with relatives or in children's homes when parents were deported.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, care statistics and depopulation data; mixed for elders left alone; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma experience of being left.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with song dialects; German for the *Wolfskinder* and East Prussian records; Polish for repatriation memoirs; Russian for Soviet-era children's-home files; Yiddish and Hebrew for cemetery records; Belarusian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relinquished babies, children in care and adoptees are private: aggregates and published accounts only. Postwar repatriation, deportation and the Holocaust are contested: present each account as an account. Adoption and child-relinquishment law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no song, care-data or emptied-village pointer was set aside.
