# Lithuania: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with the many affectionate forms of "mother" in songs, and birth-custom words); Polish for Vilnius-region memory and the Rasos tomb; Russian for Soviet awards and records; Belarusian; Yiddish for women's prayer books; Karaim; Tatar community sources. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija, Suvalkija:** check birth customs and the new mother's rest period, since the ethnological material is gathered by region.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lutheran churching and confirmation records around Klaipėda with Catholic customs, and check Prussian-Lithuanian song images of the mother.
- **City and village:** check age at first birth and maternity-leave use in Vilnius and Kaunas against rural districts.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check Marian devotion and Mother's Day customs in Polish parishes, and the Rasos memorial's place in them.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** check Soviet-era motherhood ideals among Russian-speaking families and Old Believer birth customs, separately.
- **Belarusians:** check birth and christening customs of southeastern villages where Belarusian speech survives.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check the community's own accounts of naming and birth rites in Muslim families.
- **The Karaim:** check birth and naming customs as the Trakai community describes them.
- **The Litvak community:** check women's prayer books and memoirs for mothers' religious lives before the Holocaust.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement speaks of the earth-mother Žemyna and of Laima at births, in its own publications.
- **Roma:** check maternal-health access through mediators' and NGO reports, noting scarce data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth statistics, ethnology and literature; mixed for mothers' own voices; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with song diminutives; Polish for Rasos and parish sources; Russian for Soviet award files; Yiddish and Hebrew for *tkhines*; Belarusian; Karaim.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth and medical records are confidential: aggregates only. The Rasos memorial touches the contested Vilnius question; give each community's view as its own. Abortion and parental-leave law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no birth-custom, health or memorial pointer was set aside.
