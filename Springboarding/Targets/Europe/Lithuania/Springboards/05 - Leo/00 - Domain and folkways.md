## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check which feast days, dances and song forms each region's archives hold.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** compare Lutheran festivity records in German and Lithuanian with Catholic parish feasts across the old border.
- **Vilnius, Kaunas, Šiauliai and Panevėžys against villages:** check theater-going and sport attendance by place size.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region (Šalčininkai, Vilnius district):** check their ensembles, parish feasts and Polish-language press calendars.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** separate Old Believer feast days from Soviet-era Russian-speaking leisure.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check whether their feasts are recorded apart from Polish material.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Trakai Karaim:** start from community publications and festivals.
- **The Litvak community:** separate the prewar world from today's community events.
- **Roma, Ukrainians and Romuva:** check what each group records of its own celebrations; sources may be few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for song festivals, sport and surveys; mixed for courtship and pleasure; thin for Karaim, Roma and Belarusian festivity.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian and its dialects; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; Arabic-script Slavic for Tatar texts; German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the country as it stands. Soviet-era festivity records passed through censorship. Gambling law and alcohol law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no festivity, leisure or sport pointer had to be set aside.
