## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia against Aukštaitija:** check carvers' workshops and roadside shrine making in each, which the museum inventories record by district.
- **Dzūkija:** check weaving and woodwork collections from the Varėna and Alytus districts for local patterns.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** compare Lutheran grave-marker carving and Prussian-era craft records with Catholic sacral carving.
- **Vilnius and Kaunas art schools:** separate the Vilnius academic line from the interwar Kaunas Art School, since each trained different generations.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish-language folk-art and amateur theater records in Šalčininkai and Vilnius district.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Old Believer copying traditions from Soviet-era Russian-language artists in Vilnius and Visaginas.
- **Belarusians:** check how southeastern needlework is catalogued and by whom.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check each community's own collections first; the Trakai Karaim material sits partly in museum hands.
- **The Litvak community:** look at Vilna-trained Jewish artists and the Yiddish avant-garde as their own stream.
- **Roma, Ukrainians, Romuva:** check Roma musicians' and Romuva craftspeople's own accounts; sources may be few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fine art, folk art and music; mixed for invention and everyday making; thin for Karaim, Roma and Belarusian craft.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Polish; Russian (imperial and Soviet sources); Belarusian; Yiddish; Church Slavonic for Old Believer manuscripts; Arabic-script Slavic for Tatar texts; German for Klaipėda-region records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** works taken from Jewish owners in the Holocaust and from manors and churches in the Soviet years have contested provenance; copyright questions go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no art, craft or invention pointer had to be withheld.
