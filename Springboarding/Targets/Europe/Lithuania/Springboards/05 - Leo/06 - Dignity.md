# Lithuania: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Vilnius-region Polish; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; Latin for Grand Duchy political writing. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check care-home and eldership reports for differences in how the rural old are looked after.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check Lutheran deaconry and charity records from the Prussian era against Catholic parish charity.
- **Cities against countryside:** compare the use of formal address and courtesy in Vilnius and Kaunas services with district towns.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish-language accounts of how officials and services treat Polish speakers.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** check Visaginas workers' accounts of the plant's closure in terms of respect and work.
- **Belarusians:** check how southeastern villagers describe treatment in Soviet and present institutions.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check burial and care customs each community records as its own.
- **The Litvak community:** read ghetto diaries and survivor testimony together; each sets down its own account.
- **Roma:** check Roma organizations' statements on treatment by police, schools and landlords, and note how few there are.
- **Ukrainians and Romuva:** check refugee and faith-community accounts of how they are received.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for testimony and institutional reports; mixed for everyday courtesy; thin for Roma and Belarusian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian for care-home, welfare and address-form studies; Polish and Latin for Volanas's treatise and its readers; Russian for prison and deportation accounts; Belarusian for southeastern elders' care; Yiddish for Kruk's ghetto diary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deportation, partisan reburials and the Holocaust are contested ground: present each account as an account, naming whose it is. Institutional reports may identify vulnerable people. Euthanasia and anti-discrimination law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no dignity pointer was withheld.
