# Lithuania: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Vilnius-region Polish; Lithuania's Russian (Soviet and Old Believer usage); Belarusian; Yiddish, Hebrew and German for ghetto records; Karaim; Chancery Slavonic and Latin for Grand Duchy sources. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check parish and village records for who led communal work and festivals, and whether priests or lay elders took the lead.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare cooperative and farm-association leaders of the interwar years in each region.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check the Klaipėda Directorate's leaders (1923-1939) and how Prussian Lithuanian and German-speaking residents followed them.
- **Vilnius, Kaunas, Šiauliai and Panevėžys:** compare how mayors and city councils are spoken of, since municipal leadership varies by city.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish-language accounts of community leaders in Šalčininkai and Vilnius district, and who speaks for whom.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Old Believer lay leadership from Soviet-era factory and plant managers in Visaginas.
- **Belarusians:** check who organizes the Belarusian community in Vilnius, older and post-2020.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check how each community chooses its religious and lay heads, from its own publications.
- **The Litvak community:** read prewar rabbinic and communal leadership apart from the ghetto years and from today's community.
- **Roma, Ukrainians and Romuva:** check who represents each group to the state, and whether members recognize them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political leaders and the Soviet and independence years; mixed for village and workplace leadership; thin for Belarusian, Roma and Karaim leadership.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian for Smetona's press and kolkhoz memoirs; Polish for Vilnius-region community leaders; Russian for Sniečkus's Party archive and the Old Believers' council; Belarusian for southeastern village heads; Yiddish, Hebrew and German for the ghetto councils' records; Karaim for the community's own heads; Chancery Slavonic and Latin for the Council of Lords.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Smetona, Sniečkus, the partisan commanders and the ghetto councils are all contested: present each account as an account, naming its source. Formal authority and constitutional powers go to Capricorn and Libra.
