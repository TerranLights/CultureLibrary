## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check regional names and rules for the same games in the folklore archives, since names differ by dialect.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check village evening games (*vakaruškų žaidimai*) recorded in each region.
- **Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** check games of fishers' children and Prussian-era school recreation for a separate line.
- **Apartment-block cities against villages:** compare courtyard play in Vilnius, Kaunas, Šiauliai and Panevėžys with farm childhoods.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish-language children's rhymes and games recorded in Šalčininkai and Vilnius district.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** check what Old Believer congregations' own rules say about games and festivities, apart from secular Russian-speaking yard culture.
- **Belarusians:** check southeastern children's games for Belarusian-language rhymes.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check holiday games each community records as its own.
- **The Litvak community:** look at *kheyder* children's play and Purim customs before 1941, and today's community events.
- **Roma, Ukrainians and Romuva:** check whether any games are recorded for Roma children, and what Romuva festivals include as play.
