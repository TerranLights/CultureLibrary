# Lithuania: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Vilnius-region Polish (gentry vocabulary); Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew; Karaim; German and Latin for the Teutonic Order's records. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check local legends of hillfort defenders and burned strongholds for how each region tells honor in defeat.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare interwar district press on fights, feuds and their settlements between the two regions.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check Prussian military tradition among Lutheran Lithuanians against Catholic Lithuanian tradition.
- **Officers, partisans and veterans:** separate interwar army, postwar partisan and present-day army codes; each is its own source set.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check gentry and veterans' writing in Polish for what honor is said to require.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Soviet military honors kept by veterans from Old Believer communities' own codes of conduct.
- **Belarusians:** check whether southeastern villages' gentry and peasant memories are told in Belarusian or Polish sources.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check each community's accounts of service and loyalty to the grand dukes.
- **The Litvak community:** read prewar honor of learning and charity apart from Holocaust testimony.
- **Roma, Ukrainians and Romuva:** check each group's own statements on honor and good name; Roma sources may be few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medieval, noble and military sources; mixed for everyday honor and insult; thin for Roma, Belarusian and Karaim material.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian for officers' courts and village fight reports; Polish for Niesiecki's armorial and gentry memoirs; Russian and Belarusian for veterans' and southeastern accounts; Yiddish and Hebrew for burial-society records; Karaim for the community's service traditions; German and Latin for Paravicini's knights and the Order's sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor tied to women's conduct concerns living families: aggregate sources only. Wartime honor is contested ground between Lithuanian, Polish, Jewish and Russian-speaking accounts; present each as an account. The Statutes of Lithuania's rules on insult and defamation law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no honor-related pointer was set aside.
