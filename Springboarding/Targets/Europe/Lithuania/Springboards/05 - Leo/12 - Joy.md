# Lithuania: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian, with the Prussian Lithuanian of Lithuania Minor for older texts; Vilnius-region Polish; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; Tatar religious terms. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check how merry-song repertoires and festival dances differ between the regions' song volumes.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare village feast-day (*atlaidai*) merrymaking after Mass in each, from local museum and folklore records.
- **Lithuania Minor:** compare Lutheran festivity in Donelaitis's world with today's Klaipėda festivals.
- **Cities against villages:** compare where joy is sought (concerts, sport, nature) in Vilnius and Kaunas against Šiauliai, Panevėžys and the countryside.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish-language parish and harvest festivities.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Old Believer feast days from Soviet-era holidays still kept by some Russian speakers.
- **Belarusians:** check southeastern midsummer and harvest customs recorded in Belarusian.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check each community's festival calendars and who keeps them.
- **The Litvak community:** read prewar festival joy in Yiddish sources, apart from present-day community events.
- **Roma, Ukrainians and Romuva:** check Roma music and weddings in Roma accounts, and Ukrainian festivals after 2022.
