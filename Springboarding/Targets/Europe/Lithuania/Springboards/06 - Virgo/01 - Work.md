# Lithuania: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with Samogitian, Aukštaitian and Dzūkian forms); Vilnius-region Polish; Lithuania's Russian; southeastern Belarusian; Yiddish for the Litvak past; Karaim; German for Lithuania Minor. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda against the former Grand Duchy lands:** check whether Prussian Lutheran sources such as *Metai* frame sloth differently from Catholic sermons and catechisms.
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** map the proverb and tale editions by place of record to see whether sayings on work cluster by region.
- **Kaunas, Šiauliai and Panevėžys:** check factory and workshop histories for how industrial work in each town differed from Vilnius's administrative economy.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare employment and commuting statistics for Šalčininkai and Vilnius district with their own farm press.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** separate Visaginas plant workers from the older Old Believer villages of the Zarasai and Utena districts, whose work records are another body.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check memoirs of seasonal and cross-border work; sources may be few.
- **The Litvak community:** use Yiddish and economic-history sources for artisans and traders, and keep forced labor in the ghettos as its own subject.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim of Trakai:** look for community histories of trades and occupations before drawing anything from general sources.
- **Roma and Ukrainians:** look for NGO and community reports on employment barriers; little may exist.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor statistics, Soviet honors and folklore of work; mixed for informal work; thin for Karaim, Tatar and Roma occupations.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, including archaic Prussian Lithuanian for *Metai*; Russian for Soviet labor records; Polish; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak economic sources; German for Prussian-era material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet labor heroes are a propaganda record and should be read as one; forced labor under Soviet and Nazi rule (camps, deportations, the ghettos) needs care, with each account given as an account. Wage and labor law belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no work pointer was set aside.
