## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lithuanian Catholics by region:** check parish records of altar servers, sodalities and pilgrim services in Samogitia, Aukštaitija and Dzūkija, since parish life organizes much unpaid service.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda Lutherans:** compare deaconess and parish service records with the Catholic material.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare scouting, parish and municipal volunteering in Šalčininkai and Vilnius district with Lithuanian-language bodies.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** separate Orthodox, Old Believer and secular Russian-speaking service bodies; each keeps its own records.
- **Belarusians:** check whether Orthodox and Catholic Belarusians in the southeast have their own service bodies; sources may be few.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** treat their service traditions in each community's own words before reading outside histories.
- **The Litvak community:** separate the prewar kehilla's societies from the present Jewish Community of Lithuania's welfare work.
- **Romuva and Roma:** check what each group says of its own roles of service; little may be published.
- **Vilnius and Kaunas against smaller towns:** compare hospitality employment and volunteering rates by municipality.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military service, Tatar history and volunteering; mixed for domestic service; thin for Karaim and Roma service roles.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Old Ruthenian (chancery Slavic) and Latin for the Metrica; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim; Arabic-script Slavic for Tatar texts; German for Lutheran parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the postwar partisans (*miško broliai*) and Soviet army service are contested; present each account as an account. Service in Nazi-era police battalions is part of the Holocaust record and needs care. Conscription law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no service pointer was set aside.
