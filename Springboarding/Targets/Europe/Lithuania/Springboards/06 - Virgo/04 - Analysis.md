## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Litvak yeshiva towns (Telšiai, Kaunas-Slabodka, Panevėžys) against Vilnius:** check whether study methods recorded by alumni differ by school, since the Lithuanian yeshivas argued over method.
- **Lithuanian Catholics and the Jesuit academy:** compare Latin school logic with later Lithuanian-language teaching to see how analytic schooling passed into the vernacular.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare exam and PISA results by school language and municipality (Šalčininkai, Vilnius district) before reading anything into them.
- **Russian speakers and Visaginas:** check engineering education and technical careers in the power-plant town against national data.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** read Shapshal's work alongside other Karaim accounts and outside scholarship, since his theory of origins is disputed.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check whether *kitab* scholarship and community schooling have their own tradition of commentary; sources may be few.
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija:** compare riddle and measure records by region for village reckoning.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement relates folklore analysis (Greimas, Gimbutas) to its own practice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Jewish learning, statistics and education testing; mixed for Soviet computing; thin for Tatar and Belarusian traditions of reasoning.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Aramaic with Yiddish for the yeshivas; Latin for Śmiglecki; Polish; Russian for Soviet industry; Karaim and Turkic studies for Shapshal.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the destruction of the Lithuanian yeshivas in the Holocaust; the wartime record of Shapshal's statements on Karaim origins is contested, so present it as an account. Census categories of nationality are politically charged.
