## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Dzūkija, Aukštaitija and Suvalkija:** use the charm edition's place-of-record notes to map which illnesses and remedies are recorded where.
- **Vilnius against rural districts:** compare life expectancy, doctor density and travel time to care, since the gap is the main health question by place.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region (Šalčininkai, Vilnius district):** check municipal health data and Polish-language health information.
- **Russian speakers, Visaginas and Old Believers:** separate nuclear-plant occupational health and Chernobyl cases from Old Believer home remedies, and read each from its own sources.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check the whispering healers recorded in Belarusian-speaking villages; few sources may exist.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** look for healing prayers and community records in their own publications.
- **The Litvak community:** separate prewar folk medicine and health societies from the ghetto's health work under the Holocaust.
- **Romuva and Catholic herb customs:** compare the movement's midsummer practice with the parish blessing of herbs.
- **Roma and Ukrainians:** look for NGO reports on access to care; data may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, surveys and folk-medicine editions; mixed for informal payments; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma health practice.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect plant names; Latin and Polish for the early medical society; Russian for Soviet health records; Belarusian; Yiddish; Arabic-script Slavic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, alcohol harm and men's early death need care and no character claims; Soviet psychiatric abuse and the ghetto's medical history are restricted and painful subjects.
