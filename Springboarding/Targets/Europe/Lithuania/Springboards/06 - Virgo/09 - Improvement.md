*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda against the Grand Duchy lands:** compare the timing and form of agrarian reform, since improvement of the farm came under two different states.
- **Aukštaitija, Samogitia, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** check model-farm and contest records for where interwar improvement drives found most uptake.
- **Kaunas and the reservoir villages:** set the official record of the dam beside displaced families' memoirs.
- **Vilnius districts:** compare Lazdynai and other Soviet estates with the Old Town's restoration to see who gained and who moved out.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check community accounts of school reform debates and rural development funds in Šalčininkai and Vilnius district.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** compare Old Believer refusal of church reform, in their own telling, with Soviet-era migrants' accounts of modernization.
- **The Litvak community:** separate the Haskalah's own aims from the state's schooling program, and read both against traditionalist critics.
- **Lithuanian Tatars, the Karaim and Romuva:** look at each as a revival on its own terms, through its own organizations.
- **Belarusians, Roma and Ukrainians:** check integration and development programs as their communities describe them; sources may be thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for agrarian reform, Haskalah history and post-1990 surveys; mixed for self-help culture; thin for Belarusian and Roma accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Polish for the Pavlovas records; German for Prussian reform; Hebrew and Yiddish; Russian for imperial and Soviet files; Tatar community sources in Lithuanian and Polish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** imposed "improvement" (Russification, Soviet resettlement, the flooding of villages) should be told first by those it was imposed on. Soviet deportations sit near collectivization and must be handled as their own subject. Land and education law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no improvement pointer had to be set aside.
