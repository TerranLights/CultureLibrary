*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check whether village-court and volost-court records show regional differences in how theft and violence were answered.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** compare Polish-language press accounts of minority-rights cases with Lithuanian ones, to separate group-justice claims from individual cases.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas and Vilnius:** check which justice-themed media they read, since the 1991 trials were covered from Moscow too.
- **Litvak communities:** treat rabbinic justice before 1941 and the postwar search for accountability as two separate inquiries.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check whether their own communal judging survived into the 20th century; sources are scarce, so start with community histories.
- **Families of deportees and of partisans:** compare memoir collections on what each counts as redress.
- **Roma:** check NGO reports on policing and courts; little else may exist.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, trials and surveys; mixed for village justice; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma views.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Russian for Soviet trial files; Hebrew for rabbinic law; Latin for Wigand's chronicle; Polish for the minority press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941, the partisans' courts and the Soviet deportations are contested; present each as an account. Living defendants and victims in recent trials need care. The spirit of the law belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no justice pointer was set aside.
