# Lithuania: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with folk-song forms); Polish of the Vilnius region; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak marriage papers; Russian-language Muslim registers for the Tatars; Karaim. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija, Samogitia, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare unmarried-birth shares and wedding dances by county, to see whether the old regional lines still show.
- **Vilnius-region Poles and Lithuanians:** check parish and registry records for mixed couples, since mixed marriage is a test of the 1994 partnership at home.
- **Russian speakers, Old Believers and Visaginas:** separate Old Believer marriage rules from Orthodox and Soviet registry practice; Visaginas was built by incoming workers.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Trakai Karaim:** check community records on marrying inside and outside the group; both communities are small, so ask the communities first.
- **The Litvak community:** read marriage papers for the prewar order, and separately the postwar fate of survivors' families.
- **Romuva:** check how its wedding rite defines the couple, in its own publications.
- **Roma:** look for NGO and community accounts; published sources are few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography, registers and folklore; mixed for business partnerships; thin for Tatar, Karaim, Belarusian and Roma couples.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian folk-song language; Polish for the treaty debate; Russian for Muslim and Soviet registers; Hebrew and Yiddish for *ketubot*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partisan memoirs and Soviet files tell opposed stories; present each as an account. Registry records under a hundred years old are restricted. The same-sex partnership law is covered in section 0.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing about couples, firms or alliances needed holding.
