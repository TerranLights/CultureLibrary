# Lithuania: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish and Russian of Lithuania; Belarusian; Hebrew and Yiddish for rabbinic courts; Latin, Polish and chancery Ruthenian for the Grand Duchy's courts. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vilnius against small municipalities:** check ethics-commission cases by municipality, since recusal is harder where everyone knows everyone.
- **Šalčininkai and Vilnius district (Polish majorities):** compare how Polish-language and Lithuanian-language press frame municipal hiring cases, without assuming either frame.
- **Russian speakers and Visaginas:** check how residents of a single-employer town report fair treatment in hiring and public services.
- **Grand Duchy nobility and townspeople:** compare Tribunal and town-court records, since each estate had its own courts.
- **The Litvak community:** use communal court records for the prewar period.
- **Tatars and Karaim:** check whether their own communities named neutral arbiters; sources are scarce, so ask community historians.
- **Roma:** look for NGO and ombudsperson reports on equal treatment by police and schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ethics rulings, courts and indices; mixed for everyday favoritism; thin for minority communal arbiters.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Polish and Russian for minority press; Hebrew for rabbinic rules; Latin and Polish for Tribunal records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pending and recent corruption cases involve living people; cite only rulings and published reporting. Minority-municipality disputes are contested; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; no impartiality pointer was set aside.
