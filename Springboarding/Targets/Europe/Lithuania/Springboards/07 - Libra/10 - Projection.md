## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare witch-belief records and regional jokes by region to see where blame of neighbors is recorded most.
- **Vilnius-region Poles and Lithuanians:** read each language's press on the other before drawing any conclusion, and keep the Vilnius question as its own topic.
- **Russian speakers and Visaginas:** check how residents describe being cast as a security risk, and how they cast others, in Russian-language media.
- **The Litvak community:** use Jewish sources on accusations under the tsars and in 1941 alongside Lithuanian ones; the Holocaust is its own subject.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check how their own writings answer outsider images of them, and how chronicles name "Tatars" as enemies; sources are scarce.
- **Roma:** look at social-distance results and NGO reports; Roma-authored sources may be few.
- **Recent migrants and refugees:** separate 2021 border arrivals from Ukrainian and Belarusian newcomers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for propaganda, surveys and court records; mixed for folk blame; thin for minority self-accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Russian for Soviet propaganda; Polish for the press studies and old courts; Yiddish; Latin for witch-trial records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941, antisemitic myths, the migrant crisis and minority stereotypes are contested and painful; quote propaganda only as an object of study, present each account as an account, and never let a pointer state what a group is.
