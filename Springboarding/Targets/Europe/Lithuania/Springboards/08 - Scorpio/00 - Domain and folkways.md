## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The four ethnographic regions (Aukštaitija, Samogitia, Dzūkija, Suvalkija):** test wake hymns, laments and grave markers in each, since the folklore archive files its material by region.
- **Lithuania Minor, Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** set Lutheran burial orders and carved grave boards beside inland Catholic practice.
- **Poles and Belarusians around Vilnius, Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** look at *Dziady*, *Zaduszki* and cemetery visits in their own parish and press records beside *Vėlinės*.
- **Russian speakers:** keep three strands apart: priestless Old Believer burial rules, Orthodox *Radonitsa* visits, and the civil funerals of Visaginas, a town built for the nuclear plant.
- **Lithuanian Tatars; the Trakai Karaim:** each has its own cemeteries and funeral prayers; little is published, so begin with each community's own periodicals and cemetery surveys.
- **The Litvak community:** prewar burial-society (*khevre kadishe*) records and the postwar memorial books are separate sources from the Holocaust record itself; keep them apart.
- **Romuva; Roma:** Romuva publishes its own rite for the dead; Roma funerals appear mainly in NGO and ethnographic reports, which is where to start.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on funeral folklore, cemeteries and mortality; middling on inheritance and couples' money; sparse on the death ways of the smallest communities.
- **Languages needed:** dialect Lithuanian for laments; Latin and German for the chronicles; Polish, Russian and Belarusian for the southeast and Visaginas; Yiddish and Hebrew for memorial books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Holocaust, the deportations and the postwar partisan war are disputed; give each community's telling as a telling. Security files name living people. Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled; the Scorpio sheets cover the present-day state only.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on death, rebirth or secrecy had to be set aside.
