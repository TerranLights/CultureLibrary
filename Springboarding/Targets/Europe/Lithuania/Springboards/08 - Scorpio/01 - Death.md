# Lithuania: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian and its regional speech; the Polish of Vilnius and its district; Russian (with Old Believer church usage); Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew (ghetto and rabbinic writing); Latin for early modern texts. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and what death means are understood; grief and mourning belong here, funeral practice to the domain sheet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija beside Aukštaitija:** look for regional versions of death omens and of the Giltinė tales in the archive's village files.
- **Poles of Vilnius and the Vilnius district:** read the Polish-language parish press and hospice writing on dying well beside the Lithuanian-language material.
- **Old Believers and Orthodox Russians (Visaginas, Zarasai, Vilnius):** check each church's teaching on the soul's journey and the memorial days, rather than one "Russian" view.
- **Belarusian-speaking villages near the border (Šalčininkai, Švenčionys districts):** look in dialect and ethnographic field notes for laments and mourning terms; published material is scarce.
- **The Litvak community:** keep prewar teaching on death and mourning apart from the Holocaust record; Yiddish diaries are the main source for both.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Trakai Karaim:** each faith's teaching on death is in its own prayer books and community writing; start with the communities' own publications.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement draws on Łasicki and folk material for its view of the dead.
- **Kin of the deported and of the partisans:** check how families who lost people in exile or in the forest mourn and are mourned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on folk belief, Holocaust deaths and mortality data; mixed on hospice and grief; thin on Karaim, Tatar and Roma views of death.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Łasicki; old Lithuanian orthography for Daukša; Yiddish for ghetto diaries; Russian for Old Believer texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide; euthanasia; the Holocaust, where who killed whom is disputed in memory; the deportations. Present each account as one side's account.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on death or mourning had to be set aside.
