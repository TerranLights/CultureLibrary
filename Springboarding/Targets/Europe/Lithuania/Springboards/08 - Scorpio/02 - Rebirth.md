# Lithuania: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Vilnius Polish; Russian, Belarusian and Karaim; Hebrew-script Yiddish; Latin for the conversion chronicles; German for Lithuania Minor's press. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead in resurrection or reincarnation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The two conversions, Aukštaitija (1387) and Samogitia (1413 on):** check whether each region tells its baptism and its later Catholic revival differently.
- **Klaipėda's and the old Prussian parishes' Lutherans:** a Pietist awakening tradition sits beside Catholic sacramental renewal; start from Donelaitis and the Prussian hymnals.
- **Poles of Vilnius and Šalčininkai:** look for *odrodzenie* in their press after 1989: their own revival as a minority, told in Polish.
- **Old Believers and Orthodox in Visaginas, Zarasai and Utena districts:** check how a closed nuclear-plant town and old priestless parishes speak of a new start.
- **Lithuanian Tatars (Raižiai, Nemėžis) and the Karaim:** check mosque and *kenesa* reopenings after 1990 in each community's own periodicals.
- **Romuva and Catholic charismatic groups:** set the two "rebirth" claims side by side without ranking them.
- **The Litvak community:** use its own accounts of rebuilding; kept apart from outside heritage tourism.
- **Roma of Vilnius (the Kirtimai settlement):** look for Pentecostal conversion among Roma families in NGO and church reports; sources are scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plentiful on the two national revivals and on Easter folklore; patchy on conversion and recovery figures; very little on Karaim and Roma renewal.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Długosz and Constance; German and old Lithuanian orthography for Lithuania Minor; Yiddish; Karaim.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Romuva's state recognition, refused by the Seimas, is disputed; the Christianization is told as triumph by some and as loss by others. Recovery records are confidential.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on rebirth had to be set aside.
