## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** the partisan war was fierce here; compare village memory of informers and of the *stribai* with the files.
- **Samogitia:** check whether local legends of betrayed partisans and of Krėva differ from Aukštaitian tellings.
- **Polish speakers of Vilnius, Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** check how the 1920 seizure of Vilnius, the 1944-1945 Home Army (*Armia Krajowa*) fighting and 1990-1991 are told as betrayal by or of whom, in Polish-language sources.
- **Russian speakers and the *Jedinstvo* (Edinstvo) movement of 1990-1991:** look at how loyalty to Moscow or to Vilnius was argued inside this community's own press.
- **Klaipėda's prewar Germans and Lithuanians:** check memoirs on both sides of the 1923 takeover and the 1939 annexation.
- **The Litvak community:** use survivors' testimony on 1941 and the communal concept of the *moyser*; keep rescue and betrayal accounts together.
- **Old Believers:** their own histories of the church schism and flight into Lithuania; check how the old split is still framed.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Karaim:** check community histories for accusations of disloyalty under each regime; little is published.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** abundant on the partisan war, the chronicles and the Holocaust; middling on private infidelity; scarce for the Tatars and Karaim.
- **Languages needed:** chancery Ruthenian and Latin; Russian for MGB files; German for Klaipėda; Yiddish testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** naming informers can harm living people and their families; KGB files are partial and were partly removed to Moscow. Who betrayed whom in 1941, in the forest war and over Vilnius is disputed: attribute every version to the community that tells it.
- **Held for review:** none; no betrayal pointer was set aside.
