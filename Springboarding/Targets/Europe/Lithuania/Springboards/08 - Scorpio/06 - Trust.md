*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small towns of Aukštaitija and Dzūkija beside Vilnius:** check whether trust in strangers and in institutions shows a city-country gap in the survey microdata.
- **Samogitia and Suvalkija:** the fair and horse-trading customs were recorded in both; compare how a deal was sealed and witnessed in each.
- **Polish speakers of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** compare their press and survey answers on trust in Vilnius-run institutions with their trust in local ones.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas and Klaipėda:** look at media trust by language and by city; the two towns had different Soviet histories.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Trakai Karaim:** the communities' own histories make much of loyal service to the grand dukes; check what that story does for trust today.
- **The Litvak community:** use interwar loan-society and cooperative records; postwar trust between Jews and Lithuanians is a separate, contested subject.
- **Belarusian newcomers after 2020:** check NGO and press reports on how arrivals from Belarus are trusted and how they trust the state.
- **Ukrainians and Roma:** look for surveys or NGO reports on trust in both directions; data are sparse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** strong on surveys and bank crises; mixed on folk ritual kinship; thin on Tatar, Karaim and Roma trust networks.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** distrust between communities touches the Vilnius question, 1941 and Russia's information war; attribute each claim to its source. Bank-failure lawsuits are ongoing in some cases.
