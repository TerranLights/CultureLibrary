# Lithuania: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with coastal Samogitian legend material); Polish of Vilnius and the Švenčionys-Molėtai area; Russian; Yiddish and Hebrew for the ghetto fighters; Middle High German for Livonian sources. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore honor or standing, or deter the next wrong; private vengeance set against the state's punishment goes to Capricorn.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages of Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** set local memory of who struck back at whom after 1944 against what the files and LGGRTC lists record.
- **Samogitian coast and Klaipėda:** test how the Jūratė legend and coastal tales of punishment are told by Lutheran and Catholic families.
- **Poles of the Švenčionys, Molėtai and Vilnius districts:** read Polish-language memory of 1944 beside Lithuanian; check each community's commemorations of the two massacres.
- **Russian speakers:** check how the 1990s gang wars and Soviet reprisals are framed in Russian-language press and memoir in Vilnius and Visaginas.
- **The Litvak community:** use Yiddish and Hebrew testimony on the ghetto fighters and on *Nakam*; keep the debate over vengeance in the survivors' own terms.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Trakai Karaim:** look for any recorded feud or reprisal in community histories; little is likely, so say so rather than borrow.
- **Roma:** check NGO and court-press reports on disputes settled inside families; avoid general claims; sources are few.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement retells the Order's crusade and its reprisals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** heavy for war reprisals and the 1940s; moderate for crime and school data; light for private feuds and minority customs.
- **Languages needed:** Middle High German for the chronicle; Polish for 1944; Yiddish and Hebrew for the ghetto fighters; Russian for files and gang press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941, the partisan war and 1944 are disputed between communities; frame every killing in the words of the source that records it. Crime files can name victims' families.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on revenge was set aside.
