## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suvalkija and Samogitia:** the clandestine book networks ran strongest in each; compare parish and family memories of hiding books and teachers.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** Rhesa's songs come from here; check whether Lutheran parish records show different courtship secrecy than the Catholic north.
- **Poles of Vilnius:** the Philomaths and the wartime Polish underground are their founding secret societies; check Polish-language memoirs for how secrecy is honored or questioned.
- **Russian speakers and former closed military towns:** check how residents of Soviet garrison and nuclear sites recall what they could not say.
- **The Litvak community:** use hidden children's own testimony and that of rescuer families; the two may differ on what was kept silent afterward.
- **The Trakai Karaim:** check the community's own account of how its identity was classified during the occupation; a delicate subject, sources limited.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check whether private prayer books and family manuscripts were kept from outsiders; consult community scholars first.
- **Catholics, dissident and conformist:** compare underground clergy and lay networks with parishes that kept quiet; avoid ranking either.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on underground movements and state secrets; mixed on private and family secrecy; thin on Karaim and Tatar private knowledge.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for the student societies; Russian for NKVD and KGB files; German for Rhesa; Yiddish for testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burial sites and testimony involve living kin; the CIA inquiry touches classified matters; the Karaim wartime classification and hidden children's stories need the communities' consent before close study. Anything a community keeps to itself stays with it.
