## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lithuania Minor and the Klaipėda coast:** the plague and the 1999 storm struck here; compare Prussian-era records with coastal memory.
- **Samogitia and northern Aukštaitija:** check whether famine memory and emigration lore cluster where the 1867-1868 hunger was worst.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija's small farms:** see how swine fever and drought were borne by small keepers against larger farms.
- **Visaginas and Russian speakers:** look at how this community lived the early-1990s collapse and later crises, in Russian-language media.
- **Polish speakers of the Vilnius district:** check rural household strategies in 1992-1993 and 2009 through Polish-language press and local studies.
- **The Litvak community:** the 1915 expulsion and the relief effort are documented in Yiddish and Russian; keep them apart from the Holocaust.
- **Lithuanian Tatars; Trakai Karaim:** look in each community's periodicals for wartime flight, evacuation and return; little has been printed.
- **Roma and recent Ukrainian arrivals:** check NGO reports on how each group met 2020-2022 crises; data are scarce and recent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** strong for modern statistics and preparedness; moderate for 19th- and 20th-century crises; very weak for minority experiences of crisis.
- **Languages needed:** German for Prussian records; Russian for 1915 and Soviet-era files; Yiddish for relief committees; Polish for local studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crisis-line data are anonymized and aggregate only; war-preparedness talk touches present security fears; give each community's account of 1915 and the early 1990s as its own.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on crisis was set aside.
