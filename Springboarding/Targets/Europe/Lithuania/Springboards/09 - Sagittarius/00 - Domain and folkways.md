## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholics by diocese (Vilnius, Kaunas, Telšiai, Šiauliai, Panevėžys, Vilkaviškis, Kaišiadorys):** compare parish life and pilgrimage feasts diocese by diocese, since each has its own bishops' records.
- **Poles and Belarusians of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** check Polish-language parishes and the *tutejsi* label, to see where faith, language and nationality are separated or joined in each community's own words.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** set Lutheran prayer-book reading beside the Catholic inland.
- **Russian speakers:** keep Orthodox parishes (now under two jurisdictions *check*), priestless Old Believers and the unaffiliated of Visaginas as separate cases.
- **The Karaim of Trakai and the Lithuanian Tatars:** each has its own scripture, prayer house and calendar; sources are few, so begin with the communities' own bodies.
- **The Litvak community:** the prewar learning world and its destruction need separate treatment; survivors' and descendants' accounts are the starting point.
- **Romuva and the unaffiliated:** both are counted from the same census question; compare the two answers municipality by municipality.
- **Roma and Ukrainians:** check their own congregations through parish and NGO sources; little else exists.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Catholic history, folklore and census religion; thin on Karaim, Tatar and Roma religious life.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the medieval and Lutheran layers; Polish and Russian for the southeast and the Old Believers; Hebrew and Yiddish for Vilna sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Holocaust, Soviet repression of clergy and Romuva's status are contested; report each community's version under its own name. The lands Lithuania will hold are not yet settled; these sheets cover the present-day state.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist groups in Lithuania (category only, an open question for the author); South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
