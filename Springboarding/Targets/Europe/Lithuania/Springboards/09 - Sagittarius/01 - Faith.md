# Lithuania: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** standard and dialect Lithuanian; Vilnius Polish; Russian (Orthodox and Old Believer church usage, with Church Slavonic); Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew; Karaim; Lithuanian Tatar sources in Arabic script. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia against Aukštaitija, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare parish feasts (*atlaidai*) and their attendance, since each region keeps its own pilgrimage map.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check Polish-language parishes and the Gate of Dawn devotions for how faith is spoken of in Polish, kept separate from Lithuanian-language sources.
- **Belarusian Catholics and Orthodox of the southeast:** trace which church and which language each family used across the 20th century, since the census categories changed.
- **Old Believers and Orthodox Russians:** compare a priestless prayer house with a parish of the Moscow line and the newer Constantinople exarchate *check*.
- **Lutherans of Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda; the Reformed of Biržai:** check what a Protestant faith in a mostly Catholic state rests on, in their own church papers.
- **Lithuanian Tatars, the Karaim, the Litvak community:** each has its own scripture and prayer tradition; sources are few for the first two, so begin with their own editions.
- **Romuva; young urban Catholics; the unaffiliated:** read each group's own statements on what faith is before any survey.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Catholic devotion and Soviet-era religious dissent; middling on Orthodox and Old Believer practice; thin on Tatar and Karaim devotion in print.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Old Believer books; Polish for the Gate of Dawn and Divine Mercy literature; Arabic script for Tatar manuscripts; Hebrew and Aramaic for Litvak texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Orthodox jurisdiction split is current and disputed; abuse in churches and its handling is a live issue; the disputed origins of the Divine Mercy cult belong to each church's own account.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer for faith had to be set aside.
