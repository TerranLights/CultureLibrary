# Lithuania: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Latin (16th-century political writing); Polish of the old Commonwealth and of today's Vilnius region; Belarusian (the 1863 rising's peasant press); Russian; Yiddish (the labor movement); Karaim and Tatar community sources. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Descendants of serfs and of the small gentry (*bajorai*), especially in Samogitia:** compare family memory and memoirs, since freedom had a different history for each estate.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** set the Prussian path to peasant freedom and the region's interwar standing beside the inland story.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** read their own accounts of 1920-1939, 1944 and 1990 for what freedom meant to them, apart from Lithuanian accounts of the same dates.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check the Belarusian side of 1863 and of Vilnius as a center of Belarusian print.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** compare a community that came for freedom of worship with Soviet-era Russian arrivals; both are counted together in the census.
- **The Litvak community:** separate Bundist, Zionist and religious understandings of freedom; the Holocaust ended all three in Lithuania, and survivors' accounts come first.
- **The Karaim and the Lithuanian Tatars:** start with each community's own commemorations of its old privileges; outside sources are few.
- **Roma:** look for Roma organizations' own statements on freedom of movement and settlement (Kirtimai in Vilnius) before any outside report.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on national liberation and dissent; middling on peasant freedom; thin on how minorities other than Poles and Jews used the word.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Wolan; Polish for the noble tradition; Belarusian and Russian for 1863 and the Old Believers; Yiddish for the Bund.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1863, 1920, 1944, 1941 and the partisan war are each told differently by Lithuanians, Poles, Belarusians and the Litvak community; label every telling with its teller. Rules on speech, broadcasting and assembly belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing was set aside for freedom.
