## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Dzūkija, Suvalkija and Aukštaitija:** compare which local legends are told as true and by whom, since the folklore archive files them by parish.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** follow the Polish-language press on 1920-1944, including the Armia Krajowa, and set it beside Lithuanian school accounts.
- **Russian speakers in Vilnius, Klaipėda and Visaginas:** check which news sources they rely on and how far Lithuanian fact-checking reaches them.
- **Belarusians, including recent arrivals from Belarus:** look at Belarusian-language outlets based in Vilnius for their own handling of disputed facts.
- **The Litvak community:** survivors' testimony, the Yiddish memorial books and the state's history bodies are three sources; compare where they meet and part.
- **The Karaim and the Lithuanian Tatars:** each has a contested origin account; read the community's own version first, then the scholarly debate.
- **Lithuania Minor and Klaipėda:** check how the region's German past is presented in local museums and schools.
- **Romuva:** read its claims about the antiquity of Baltic faith beside archaeologists' views; label each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on memory politics and propaganda; good on science-trust surveys; thin on minority-language media audiences.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian and Polish for 16th-17th-century polemic; Russian for Soviet sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak memory; French for Greimas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1941, the Holocaust, the 1944 killings, the partisan war and January 1991 are each told in rival versions; present every one as an account and name its teller. Court rulings on these events belong to Libra.
