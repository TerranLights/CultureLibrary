# Lithuania: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian, with village belief terms in Aukštaitian, Samogitian and Dzūkian; Latin (Jesuit reports); Polish (19th-century antiquarians and Vilnius-area folklore); Russian (Old Believer texts, Soviet lecture series); Yiddish; Tatar and Karaim community sources. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija, Samogitia, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare which spirits and omens appear in each region's belief files, since collectors worked unevenly across them.
- **The Curonian Spit and Lithuania Minor:** test Lutheran pastors' records of folk belief against Catholic priests' records inland.
- **Poles and Belarusians of the southeast:** separate Polish- and Belarusian-language belief material, and ask which language each village used for its legends.
- **Old Believers:** look at how belief is warranted by the old books and the old rite, in their own writing, not in outsiders' summaries.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas and Klaipėda:** check survey answers on God and science against those of Lithuanian speakers in the same towns.
- **The Litvak community:** keep the folk beliefs collected by *zamlers* apart from rabbinic teaching and from Haskalah skepticism.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** their own statements of creed are the place to start; few outside studies exist.
- **Romuva and the freethinker tradition:** compare two groups that left Catholic belief in opposite directions, each in its own texts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on folk belief and Soviet atheism; middling on present-day political belief; thin on Karaim and Tatar statements of belief.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Jesuit letters; Polish for Narbutt and Kolberg; Church Slavonic for Old Believer texts; Yiddish for the *zamlers*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some 19th-century sources mix record and invention; label each. Soviet atheist material names believers who were pressured.
- **Held for review:** none; no belief pointer had to be set aside.
