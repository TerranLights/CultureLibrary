## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suvalkija and Samogitia:** compare emigration before 1914, and test whether the land border and the Baltic ports gave each region its own routes.
- **Dzūkija and Aukštaitija:** set present-day departure and return rates side by side, municipality by municipality.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** separate the port's sea-going workers and the spit's summer visitors from inland patterns.
- **Vilnius-region Poles and Belarusians:** look at who travels to Poland or Belarus and why; the two neighbors draw different groups.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas:** read memoirs of the journeys that brought builders and engineers there from across the Soviet Union, and of later departures.
- **The Litvak community:** prewar emigration, wartime flight and postwar departures to Israel are three separate journeys with three sets of records.
- **The Karaim and the Lithuanian Tatars:** begin with the stories each community tells of its ancestors' arrival from Crimea and the steppe; scholarship on this is thin and should be read beside the communities' versions.
- **Ukrainians:** keep earlier Ukrainian residents apart from those who arrived after 2022.
- **Former deportees and their families:** compare the forced journey east with the return journey and with later trips back to the sites.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on emigration and official migration counts; good on pilgrimage; thin on minorities' own travel accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for Radziwiłł; German for Hamburg and Prussian records; Russian for Soviet files; Yiddish for emigrants' letters; Ukrainian for recent arrivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2021 border events are disputed between government, NGOs and migrants; label each account. Deportation journeys and refugee records concern living people. The Lithuanian diaspora abroad is for comparison only. Entry and asylum law: Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no travel pointer was set aside.
