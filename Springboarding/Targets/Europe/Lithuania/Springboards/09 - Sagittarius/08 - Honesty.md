# Lithuania: Honesty (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish and Latin (Vilnius town books); Russian (tsarist and Soviet officialdom, today's Russian-language press); Belarusian; Yiddish; Karaim and Tatar community sources. **Concept in one line:** truthfulness in word and deed: not lying, cheating or stealing; candor; keeping one's word.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Border districts (Šalčininkai, Varėna, Lazdijai, Pagėgiai):** compare smuggling records with local press coverage and residents' own accounts, without reading one district's data as a trait of its people.
- **The capital and the other cities (Kaunas, Šiauliai, Panevėžys, Klaipėda):** see whether bribe-experience answers can be split by city, and if so, set them side by side.
- **Samogitia, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare folktale variants on liars and honest men, since the tale collections give the collecting district.
- **Vilnius-region Poles and Russian speakers:** read Polish- and Russian-language press on corruption cases for how each community frames honesty in public life.
- **The Litvak community:** look at prewar merchants' and tradesmen's own records and Yiddish moral writing separately from outsiders' portrayals, which may be hostile and must be read as such.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** sources on honesty in trade or speech are scarce; begin with community histories and elders' memoirs.
- **Roma:** check NGO and Roma organizations' own writing, and treat the press with care, since stereotyped reporting is a known problem.
- **Generations:** compare those schooled in Soviet times with those schooled after 1990 on cheating at school and on small gifts to officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on corruption surveys and official statistics; middling on everyday lying and white lies; thin for minority communities.
- **Languages needed:** Polish, Latin and Ruthenian for magistrate books; Russian for tsarist and Soviet material; Yiddish for Litvak sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honesty is a common ground for ethnic stereotypes; name variation, never a group's character. Fraud and bribery rulings belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing was held under the standing rules.
