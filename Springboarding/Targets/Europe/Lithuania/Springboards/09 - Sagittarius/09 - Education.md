## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Suvalkija and Samogitia:** compare where each region's 19th-century educated class studied (gymnasium, seminary, Moscow or Saint Petersburg) using biographical dictionaries.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** follow the path from Polish-language school to university, in Lithuania or in Poland, through the community's own press and data.
- **Russian speakers:** check admission and attainment figures by school language, and the place of Russian-language higher learning since 1990.
- **Belarusians:** separate long-settled southeastern Belarusians from students and staff who came for the exile university.
- **The Litvak community:** keep yeshiva learning, the state-run seminary and secular Yiddish and Hebrew schools apart; each defined the educated person differently.
- **The Karaim and the Lithuanian Tatars:** look for community schools, religious teachers and scholars in each group's histories; published material is limited.
- **Roma:** check attainment data and the work of Roma organizations on school completion, not press accounts alone.
- **Rural Dzūkija and Aukštaitija against Vilnius and Kaunas:** compare who goes on to university and who stays.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on university history and present-day statistics; good on yeshivas; middling on Belarusian, Tatar and Karaim education; thin on Roma beyond NGO reports.
