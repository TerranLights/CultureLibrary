# Lithuania: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish and Latin (Counter-Reformation records); Russian (Old Believer writing, security reports' Russian-language sources); Hebrew and Yiddish (yeshiva disputes); Tatar community sources; English (scholarship on Islam and new religions in the Baltic). **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Catholics of different dioceses and movements:** separate traditionalist, charismatic and mainstream parishes in Vilnius, Kaunas, Telšiai and Šiauliai, each in its own words, before applying any label.
- **Old Believers of Zarasai, Utena and Visaginas:** check how they describe faithfulness to the old rite, and whether outsiders' labels fit or distort.
- **Lithuanian Tatars, Muslim converts and newer Muslim residents:** keep the three apart in every source, and check whether security and press material runs them together.
- **The Karaim of Trakai:** a scripture-centered tradition with its own reading rules; begin with the community's own explanation, as outside work is limited.
- **The Litvak community:** read the prewar yeshiva disputes and the Haskalah critiques side by side; later Haredi institutions abroad are for comparison only.
- **Protestants of Lithuania Minor and Biržai; Pentecostal and charismatic congregations:** compare how older and newer Protestant bodies treat scripture's authority.
- **Romuva and other Baltic-faith groups:** check whether and by whom the label is applied to them, and how they answer.
- **Russian-speaking evangelical and Orthodox congregations:** look at their own publications, since Lithuanian-language coverage is limited.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** middling; the word is used more by outsiders than claimed by anyone, so sources are mostly labels; thin on small groups' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for 1658; Hebrew for the Musar dispute; Russian for Old Believer and Russian-speaking congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "sect", "fundamentalist" and "radical" stigmatize; quote who applies them and never adopt them as findings. Security reports concern living people. Rules on recognizing religious bodies are law: Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing for this concept needed holding.
