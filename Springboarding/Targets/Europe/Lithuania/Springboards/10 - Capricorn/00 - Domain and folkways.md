# Lithuania: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian in all three dialect areas; Vilnius-region Polish; Russian as spoken in Lithuania and by Old Believers; Belarusian of the southeast; Karaim; Tatar *kitab* texts; Yiddish and Hebrew; chancery Ruthenian, Latin and Prussian German for older rule. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed: career, status, reputation, the ages of life, the ordering of time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** check whether parish and manor records show different lord-and-serf arrangements, since the two lands had separate administrations in the Grand Duchy.
- **Suvalkija and Dzūkija:** compare farm-owning and forest-village records, to test whether tenure changed who was obeyed.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** set Prussian state records and Lutheran parish books beside the Catholic inland.
- **Poles of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** check Polish-language municipal and party sources for how local authority is described in their own terms.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers, including Visaginas:** separate the Soviet industrial town from the older priestless villages.
- **Belarusians, Ukrainians and Roma:** check community associations first for who speaks for each; little else is likely.
- **Trakai Karaim; Lithuanian Tatars:** look for each community's own lists of its hazzans or imams, to learn who held office and by what choice.
- **The Litvak community:** prewar self-government and the ghettos' councils need separate, careful treatment.
- **Romuva:** check how the movement titles its own leaders, for its account of authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Grand Duchy, serfdom, Soviet rule and survey data; thin for Karaim, Tatar, Roma and Belarusian views of authority.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian and Latin for the Metrica; Hebrew for the Council's minutes; German for Lithuania Minor; Russian for Soviet files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the partisans, 1941 and the Holocaust, the deportations and the Vilnius question are contested; give each as an account, naming each community by its own name. Which lands Lithuania will hold is not yet settled, so all ten Capricorn sheets are written for the present-day state.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing in this domain overview was withheld under the standing rules.
