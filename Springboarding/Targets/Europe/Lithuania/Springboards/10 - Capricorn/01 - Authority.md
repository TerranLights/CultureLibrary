# Lithuania: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish and Russian as used in Vilnius, Šalčininkai and Visaginas; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for rabbinic and ghetto authority; Karaim; Latin for the Order's chronicles. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia:** check parish records of Valančius's diocese against Aukštaitija's, to see whether the bishop's reach differed by diocese.
- **Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare partisan-district records (Dainava, Tauras) for how command was organized in each.
- **Klaipėda and Lithuania Minor:** test how Prussian civil authority and the Lutheran consistory appear in local memoirs, against the inland Catholic pattern.
- **Poles of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** look at how Polish-language papers (*Kurier Wileński*) cover municipal and national authorities, to find whose authority is accepted locally.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas:** check how the former nuclear plant's management served as local authority, and what replaced it after closure.
- **Litvak communities:** separate rabbinic authority before 1941 from the ghetto councils, which need their own careful study.
- **Lithuanian Tatars, Belarusians, Roma, Ukrainians:** start with each group's own association, to learn who speaks for it and how that is accepted; sources are scarce.
- **Romuva:** compare how the movement chooses its *krivis* with how the Catholic hierarchy names bishops.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the interwar and Soviet state, the Church and the partisans; thin for minority communities' internal authority.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Dusburg; Hebrew and Yiddish for rabbinic writing; Russian for party files; Polish for the southeast's press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Smetona's rule, the partisans and Soviet party careers are read very differently by different families; the ghetto councils are among the most painful questions of the Holocaust. Present every account as one account.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on obedience or command had to be withheld.
