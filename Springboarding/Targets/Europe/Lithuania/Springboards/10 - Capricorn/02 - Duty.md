# Lithuania: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian and its regional speech; Polish of the Vilnius lands; Russian, including Old Believer church usage; Belarusian; Yiddish for communal societies; Karaim and Tatar community writing. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia, Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** compare *talka* customs and harvest songs by district, to see whether the reciprocal duty was organized alike.
- **Suvalkija:** check farm memoirs for how inheriting sons and their siblings settled duties to parents after the farm passed on.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** look at Lutheran confirmation books and fishermen's guild rules for duties owed in a Prussian frame.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare *Zaduszki* grave visits (Rasos Cemetery) with Lithuanian *Vėlinės*, keeping each community's own description.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** separate Soviet-memory commemorations from the Old Believers' own days for the dead.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check each community's cemetery customs and charitable funds for duties to the dead and to the poor.
- **The Litvak community:** prewar mutual-aid societies first; the duty of postwar remembrance as a separate question.
- **Roma and Ukrainians:** look to community associations for family obligations; scholarship is scarce.
- **Romuva:** compare its autumn rites for ancestors with Catholic *Vėlinės*, in its own published calendar.

## 4. Flags
