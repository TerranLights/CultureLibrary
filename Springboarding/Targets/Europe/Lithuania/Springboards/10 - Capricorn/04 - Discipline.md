## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Suvalkija:** check ethnographers' notes on child-rearing and correction district by district, rather than assuming one village pattern.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** compare the revivalists' records with Catholic parish practice, since the two were trained in different church orders.
- **Panevėžys and Kaunas:** set the yeshiva and the theater beside each other as two local schools of rigor with separate sources.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** look at Polish-language school and scouting (*harcerstwo*) materials for their own vocabulary of discipline.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** separate Soviet army and factory memories from the Old Believers' fasting and prayer rules, recorded by their own councils.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check how the community describes keeping the Ramadan fast in a Catholic-majority calendar.
- **The Karaim:** look for their own fasting days and youth instruction in community publications; sources are few.
- **Roma, Belarusians and Ukrainians:** start with school-integration reports and community groups; do not borrow material from neighboring countries.
- **Romuva:** check whether the movement prescribes any personal practice of restraint, in its own texts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, school, army and Mussar history; mixed for parenting surveys; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Yiddish for Mussar writing; Latin for Jesuit rules; German for the revivalists; Russian for Soviet army memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hazing, abuse in schools and seminaries, and child punishment involve living people; use published accounts and survivors' own words. Discipline enforced by courts belongs to the Law sheet.
- **Held for review:** none; no discipline source was withheld under the rules.
