# Lithuania: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish (minority schools and "acquaintance" idioms); Russian (Soviet labor honors, *blat*); Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for yeshiva learning; Latin and Polish for patents of nobility. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vilnius, Kaunas and the regions:** compare matura and admission data with municipal poverty data, to test how far place shapes earned results.
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija:** check whether rural schools' olympiad and exam records differ, and how local papers report them.
- **Klaipėda:** look at maritime academy admission and seafarers' certification as a separate ladder of tested skill.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare Polish-language school results and university paths in Lithuania and Poland, from the community's own sources.
- **Russian speakers, including Visaginas:** check how Soviet-era engineering credentials were valued after independence, in workers' accounts.
- **The Litvak community:** read the yeshiva's rule of learning apart from family standing, then compare with secular schooling in prewar Kaunas.
- **Tatar, Karaim, Belarusian and Ukrainian communities:** check whether any keeps its own prize or stipend for able pupils, and whom it names; little is published.
- **Roma:** check education NGO reports on how Roma pupils fare in exams, in community voices where possible.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for exams, civil service and corruption surveys; mixed for Soviet honors; thin for small minorities.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for ennoblement; Hebrew and Yiddish for yeshiva sources; Russian for Soviet awards.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** nepotism cases name living officials; use published findings only. The minority exam and land restitution are disputed; give each side's account.
