# Lithuania: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish and Ruthenian for the Statutes and Tribunal; Russian for imperial and Soviet law in practice; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for rabbinic views of state law; Polish of today's southeast. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law; its letter belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** compare 19th-century volost-court and parish records to see whether villagers in each turned to the state's courts at the same rate.
- **Suvalkija:** check how the Napoleonic code's legacy there *check* differed in daily legal life from the lands under Russian law.
- **Klaipėda and Lithuania Minor:** look at Prussian legal habits in local memoirs, as a separate inheritance from the inland's.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** compare attitudes to courts and police in Polish-language polls and press, where disputes over names and signs have gone to court *check*.
- **Russian speakers and Visaginas:** check trust in courts and police in Russian-language surveys, and whether underworld "concepts" talk appears in 1990s memoirs.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** look for how each community handled marriage and inheritance between its own rules and the state's.
- **The Litvak community:** prewar use of rabbinic courts and the state's as two inquiries; the Holocaust trials belong to Libra's Justice.
- **Roma:** check NGO reports on policing and court contact, never outsiders' claims.
- **Border districts:** compare Lazdijai, Varėna and Šalčininkai press on smuggling for how locals describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Statutes, the press ban and trust surveys; mixed for customary law; thin for Tatar, Karaim and Roma views of the law.
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian, Polish and Latin for the Tribunal; Russian for tsarist and Soviet sources; Hebrew for rabbinic rulings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** smuggling and bribe surveys touch living people; report aggregates. Soviet legality, the partisans and 1941 are contested; present each account as an account.
