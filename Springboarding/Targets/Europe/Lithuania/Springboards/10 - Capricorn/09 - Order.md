## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Aukštaitija:** compare how lone-farmstead and street-village layouts survived Soviet resettlement, using district maps and memoirs.
- **Dzūkija:** check forest-village accounts of the postwar years for when ordinary order broke down and how villagers kept it.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** separate Prussian coastal rules and fishing-village order from inland patterns.
- **Vilnius and Kaunas:** compare Soviet planned districts with interwar Kaunas, through residents' own accounts.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** look at Polish-language local press on village and municipal upkeep, in the community's terms.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas:** check how the planned Soviet town and its upkeep are described before and after the plant closed.
- **Old Believers:** look for their own rules on prayer-house order and village layout in Zarasai and Utena districts.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Karaim:** check cemetery and prayer-house rules in community publications for each group's own order.
- **The Litvak community:** read Vilna's prewar communal rules for public space apart from the ghetto, which needs its own treatment.
- **Roma:** check reports on the demolished Kirtimai settlement in Vilnius *check*, in residents' words where possible.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk custom, planning and crime; mixed for the 1990s; thin for minority settlement order.
- **Languages needed:** German for Prussian coastal rules; Russian for 1990s crime and Visaginas; Hebrew for eruv sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2021 border response and Roma settlement removals are disputed; give each side's account. Crime figures must not be read as traits of any group.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on order or disorder fell under a standing rule.
