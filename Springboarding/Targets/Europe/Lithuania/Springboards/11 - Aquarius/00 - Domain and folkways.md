# Lithuania: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (Aukštaitian, Samogitian and Dzūkian speech); the Polish of the Vilnius region; Lithuania's Russian; southeastern Belarusian; Karaim; the Tatars' *kitab* writing; Yiddish for the Litvak past; German for Lithuania Minor. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for: friends, associations, humanitarian ideals, networks, entering society, progress, upheaval, causes, tolerance and the ways of settling, neighboring and gathering.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija, Samogitia, Dzūkija and Suvalkija:** compare village plans and *talka* records, since farmstead resettlement reached each land at a different time.
- **Klaipėda, Lithuania Minor and the Curonian Spit:** separate Lutheran parish and fishing-village records from the Catholic interior.
- **Poles of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** check Polish-language associations and the *tutejsi* self-name in their own sources.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** compare the priestless *obshchiny* with the Soviet-built town's clubs.
- **Belarusians and Ukrainians, including arrivals since 2022:** look for their own community bodies before any outside count.
- **Lithuanian Tatars; Trakai Karaim:** each congregation's own records, read separately.
- **The Litvak community:** the prewar *khevres* and their destruction need their own careful line of inquiry.
- **Roma:** check Roma community centers and advocacy groups in Vilnius first, for sources in their own voice.
- **Romuva:** check its congregations' own accounts of joining.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** World Lithuanian Community bodies, as a check on what migrants rebuilt abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations, song festivals, Sąjūdis and surveys; mixed for village customs (much is Soviet-era ethnography); thin for Roma, Belarusian and Karaim community life.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect help for archive texts; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Litvak records; German for Lithuania Minor; Arabic script for *kitabs*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Vilnius question, 1941 and the Holocaust, the deportations and the postwar partisans are contested: give each as an account and name each community by its own name. Lithuania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the present-day state.
- **Held for review:** none; this overview of groups and ideals set nothing aside.

## 5. First queries
