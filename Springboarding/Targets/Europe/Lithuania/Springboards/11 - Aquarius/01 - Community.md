# Lithuania: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (with Samogitian and Dzūkian forms in archive texts), Vilnius-region Polish, Russian, Belarusian, Yiddish; German for Klaipėda's past. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitian parishes and Aukštaitian villages:** compare feast attendance and lay singing groups, to see how far a parish sets the edge of "us".
- **Dzūkija's forest villages and Suvalkija's farmsteads:** check settlement records to separate the clustered village from the lone farm.
- **Vilnius, Kaunas and the smaller cities:** compare block associations and renovation votes for differences between newer and older housing.
- **Klaipėda region Lutherans:** look in the pietist and parish records for a church-based community distinct from the Catholic parish.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** check Polish parish and association sources for their own word for "community" and who it covers.
- **Visaginas and Old Believer villages near Zarasai:** keep the planned town apart from the old congregations.
- **Belarusians and Ukrainians:** start from their associations' rolls before any survey.
- **Litvak survivors and descendants:** read yizkor books as their own record of lost communities.
- **Roma of Vilnius and other towns:** compare city documents with community statements.
- **Romuva members:** use their own publications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for parish life, cooperatives and Litvak memorial writing; mixed for present-day neighborhood life; thin for Belarusian, Ukrainian and Roma community records.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian (Samogitian forms for hymn and folklore texts); Polish for Daukša's preface and for the Vilnius region; Yiddish and Hebrew for yizkor books; German for the pietists; Russian for Visaginas.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Holocaust-era losses and the Kirtimai dispersal need each community's own account first; the Romuva decision is contested and should be presented as each party states it.
- **Held for review:** none; no community pointer was set aside under the standing rules.
