# Lithuania: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish (Vilnius region); Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish for the Litvak labor and youth movements; German and Latin for the Order's chronicles. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the person is weighed against the group, where the line between "us" and "them" falls, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitians and Aukštaitians:** check whether regional clubs and fan rivalries draw a group line inside the Lithuanian majority.
- **Dzūkija's partisan districts and Suvalkija's towns:** compare memoirs from each side of the postwar conflict, village by village.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** read their party and associations' own statements on voting as a bloc.
- **Russians and Russian speakers in Klaipėda and Visaginas:** separate union and workplace collectives from church groups.
- **Old Believer congregations:** look in their councils' decisions for rules on marrying or worshipping outside the group.
- **Belarusians and Ukrainians:** check their associations for whom they admit as members.
- **Tatars and Karaim:** each community's own histories of serving in one unit or living as one quarter.
- **Litvak movements:** compare the Bund, the Zionist youth groups and the yeshivas as separate groups.
- **Roma:** check community statements on being blamed as a whole.
- **Romuva and other new faiths:** their entry rites, in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the partisans, the 1972 and 1991 crowds and the Bund; mixed for fan and online groups; thin for minority groups' own inner rules.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the chronicles; Russian for KGB files; Yiddish for Bund papers; Polish for the Vilnius region's party press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "traitor" and "collaborator" labels around the partisans, the *stribai* and 1941 are disputed; quote each side as its own account and avoid any label for a whole people.
- **Held for review:** none; the group pointers raised nothing under the standing rules.
