# Lithuania: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish (the gentry's and the Vilnius region's); Russian; Belarusian (for the 1863 leaflets); Yiddish and Hebrew for interwar Jewish autonomy. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ. (What the law says about discrimination belongs to Libra.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitian and Vilnius-region gentry descendants and former serf villages:** check whether family histories and village records remember the old line between nobles and peasants.
- **Aukštaitija's farm families:** compare inheritance customs with Suvalkija's, where single heirs and dowries were recorded differently.
- **Vilnius against the eastern and southern districts:** check income and service data for urban-rural gaps.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** read their own press on spelling and signage before any outside summary.
- **Russian speakers in Visaginas and Klaipėda:** check surveys for whether language shapes perceived treatment at work.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** look at histories of their nobles' rights in the Grand Duchy for a minority's standing in the old order.
- **Trakai Karaim:** start from the 1441 privilege to Trakai and the community's later petitions.
- **Litvak autonomy and its end:** the interwar record calls for Hebrew and Yiddish sources first.
- **Roma communities:** compare the state's plans with what Roma organizations say.
- **Belarusians, Ukrainians and Old Believers:** check whether any equality claims appear in their associations' papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income data, the women's movement and the Soviet period; mixed for gentry history; thin for Roma, Belarusian and Old Believer voices on equal treatment.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for gentry records; Belarusian for Kalinowski; Hebrew and Yiddish for autonomy papers; Russian for tsarist-era party files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Polish-name dispute and Kalinowski's legacy are contested between communities; same-sex partnership is a live political fight; present each as its parties state it.
- **Held for review:** none; no equality pointer here needed holding.
