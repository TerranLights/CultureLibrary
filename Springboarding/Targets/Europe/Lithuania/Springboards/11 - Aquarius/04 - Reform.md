*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Biržai and northern Aukštaitija:** compare the Reformed parishes with Catholic neighbors for a long local memory of religious reform.
- **Samogitia:** check temperance records against parish books to see where the pledge took hold.
- **Suvalkija:** look at the revival press's readers and writers, since many revival figures came from there.
- **Klaipėda region:** compare Prussian school and church reforms with those across the old border.
- **Poles of Šalčininkai and the Vilnius district:** read their school associations' statements on the 2011 changes.
- **Visaginas and other Russian-speaking towns:** check how plant closure and post-1990 reform are told locally.
- **Old Believers near Zarasai and Utena:** their own histories of refusing reform.
- **Litvak Musar and Haskalah circles:** separate the two movements and their rival programs.
- **Tatars and Karaim:** each community's records of modernizing its religious bodies.
- **Romuva:** check how it presents its revival as renewal rather than reform.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the revival era, Sąjūdis and post-1990 reforms; mixed for the Reformation in the Grand Duchy; thin for Tatar and Karaim institutional reform.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for the 16th century; Hebrew and Yiddish for Musar; Russian for Old Believer writing and Soviet files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform and restitution touched Polish-speaking owners; school reform in the Vilnius region is disputed; present each community's own account.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on reform was set aside.
