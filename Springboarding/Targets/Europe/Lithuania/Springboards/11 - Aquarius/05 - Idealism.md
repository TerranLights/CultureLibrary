## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia:** compare Daukantas's picture of the Samogitian past with local histories written later, to see whose golden age is meant.
- **Vilnius and its Polish-speaking region:** check Polish-language school readings of Mickiewicz for which ideals are passed on.
- **Kaunas:** separate the interwar capital's Catholic, liberal and Zionist youth movements in their own papers.
- **Dzūkija and Druskininkai:** look at Čiurlionis's local memory against the national cult.
- **Klaipėda and Lithuania Minor:** check Lutheran and Prussian-Lithuanian writers for a separate set of ideals.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** compare Soviet idealism's local afterlife with Old Believer devotion to "the old faith".
- **Belarusians and Ukrainians:** check their associations' programs for any stated ideals.
- **Karaim and Tatars:** each community's own account of its origin and calling.
- **Litvak movements:** Yiddishist, Zionist and Musar ideals separately.
- **Romuva:** its writings on an ideal pre-Christian past, read as its own account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Romanticism, the revival, the Catholic and Zionist youth movements and dissent; mixed for present-day volunteering; thin for intentional communities and minorities' stated ideals.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for the Philomaths and Mickiewicz; Ruthenian for the chronicles; Hebrew and Yiddish for Zionist and YIVO papers; Russian for trial and KGB files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mickiewicz is claimed by more than one people; the Karaim origin debate touches wartime survival; present each as its own account.
