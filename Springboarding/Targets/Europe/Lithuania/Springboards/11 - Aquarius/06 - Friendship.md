# Lithuania: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** check whether the bee-sharing custom and its vocabulary were recorded in both, and where it lasted.
- **Samogitia and Suvalkija:** compare wedding records for how friends were chosen for formal roles.
- **Klaipėda and Lithuania Minor:** set Rhesa's songs beside later Lutheran youth clubs, to test a separate tradition.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** check the *przyjaciel*-*kolega* distinction in their own speech and press.
- **Russian speakers, Old Believers and Visaginas:** gather oral history on co-godparents and workplace friendships.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check whether *siabar* and its customs appear in local recordings.
- **Litvak yeshiva and youth-movement friendships:** read memoirs, many written abroad after the Holocaust.
- **Tatars and Karaim:** look for community memoirs before assuming any custom; sources are scarce.
- **Ukrainians arriving since 2022:** compare host and guest accounts.
- **Roma:** check whether Roma organizations have recorded friendships across communities; little is likely.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wedding and godparent customs and survey data; mixed for Soviet-era networks; thin for minority friendship customs, where oral history is the main route.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian with dialect help for the Juškas' texts; German for Rhesa's notes; Hebrew and Yiddish for yeshiva memoirs; Polish; Russian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partisan comradeship and betrayals within units are contested memory; *blatas* touches on corruption accusations; treat each as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; no friendship pointer was withheld.
