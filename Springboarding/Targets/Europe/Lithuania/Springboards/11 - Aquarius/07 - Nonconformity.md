## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia:** compare missionary reports with later folklore to see how the "old ways" were named over time.
- **Kaunas and Šiauliai:** check how the two cities' music scenes were policed and remembered in the 1980s.
- **Vilnius:** separate Užupis's artists from the older Old Town bohemia and the Soviet-era underground.
- **Kėdainiai and Biržai:** compare the Calvinist towns with Catholic neighbors for how a minority faith was treated.
- **Klaipėda region:** check whether Lithuania Minor's Lutheran pietists were counted as dissenters by their own church.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** look in their press for which youth styles or faiths drew objection.
- **Russian speakers and Old Believers:** separate the Old Believers' long dissent from Soviet-era informal youth groups in Visaginas and Klaipėda.
- **Litvak Hasidim and *mitnagdim*:** read each side's documents for the boundary between them.
- **Tatars and Karaim:** check community writings for how they describe keeping apart.
- **Roma and Romuva:** each has its own account of being called outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Hasidic dispute, Soviet-era rock and Pride coverage; mixed for new religions; thin for hermits and for minority accounts of their own nonconformists.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Jesuit reports; Hebrew for the bans; Russian for Soviet press and Old Believer histories; Arabic script for *kitabs*; Polish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Pride events and new religions draw hostility; refusal of military service is touchy in wartime politics; present each party's account without labeling any group.
