## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Vilnius-region Poles and Lithuanian parishes:** compare how each language group frames the Divine Mercy devotion and its charity work.
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija:** check parish Caritas records for differences in who is helped where farms are poor and populations old.
- **Lithuania Minor Lutherans:** read Donelaitis beside Lutheran diaconal records to see mercy in a non-Catholic idiom.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** look for congregation alms at memorial meals in Visaginas and the Zarasai district.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** separate *sadaka* and festival sharing from Christian almsgiving.
- **Litvak community:** prewar *tsedoke* institutions need Yiddish records; their destruction in 1941 changes every source after.
- **Roma:** check whether charity reports speak about them or with them; seek Roma NGO voices.
- **Ukrainians and new arrivals since 2022:** compare host and arrival accounts of the same aid.
- **Vilnius against small towns:** volunteering and giving may track age and wealth; test with survey breakdowns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church charity, shrine devotion and refugee aid; mixed for social-policy history; thin for Roma and for Tatar and Karaim almsgiving in writing.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Polish (devotional and Caritas material in the Vilnius region); Russian; Yiddish and Hebrew; German for older Lithuania Minor records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** help to migrants at the Belarus frontier since 2021 is argued over by officials, volunteers and outside monitors; quote each as the account of whoever gives it. Rescue during the Holocaust is entangled with collaboration questions; let survivors' testimony lead. Mercy to offenders through pardons is law: Capricorn.
