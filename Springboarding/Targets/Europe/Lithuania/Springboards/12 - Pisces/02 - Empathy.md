# Lithuania: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; the Polish of the Vilnius region; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish for Litvak sources. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Poles of the Vilnius region and Lithuanians of Vilnius:** check how each side's press narrates the other's grievances, to find where perspective-taking holds or breaks.
- **Samogitia against Aukštaitija:** compare how regional writers and archive tellers voice outsiders, since dialect literature carries its own narrators.
- **Russian speakers of Visaginas and Klaipėda:** look for interviews and studies on being understood after 1990, from their own side.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check for Belarusian-language accounts of how neighbors read them; sources are thin.
- **Litvak survivors and their descendants:** testimony collections are the first ground for whether neighbors' empathy was felt.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and Trakai Karaim:** check whether minority memoirs describe being understood by the majority, or misread.
- **Roma:** test how Shrovetide masks and media coverage are received in Roma organizations' own statements.
- **Deaf Lithuanians:** sign-language users' accounts could show empathy across a sensory line *check*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for literature and memory activism, thin for measured empathy and for minority voices.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Polish for the Vilnius debate; Russian for Visaginas and Klaipėda; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew for testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Mūsiškiai* and the 1941 events are bitterly disputed; read every account as its author's and let survivors speak first. The Polish-Lithuanian Vilnius question has two histories: keep both in their own words. Shrovetide masks depicting minorities need care.
- **Held for review:** none; every empathy pointer above could be named.
