*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Dzūkija:** compare partisan memory and recruit songs by district, since resistance and conscription fell unevenly.
- **Poles of the Vilnius region:** read how Polish-language sources remember the *Armia Krajowa* and its dead, which overlaps the Lithuanian story in contested ways.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** look for how Soviet war memorials and *podvig* language are kept or dropped in their own communities.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** check community histories for military service across centuries as told in their own sources.
- **Karaim of Trakai:** look for Karaim accounts of the 20th century, which differ from the Litvak record.
- **The Litvak community:** *kidush hashem* and ghetto resistance need Yiddish and Hebrew testimony first.
- **Lutherans of Lithuania Minor:** test whether sacrifice in their sermons and hymns differs from Catholic martyr devotion.
- **Women and men:** caregiving and remittance data split by sex; check who is praised and who is expected.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for martyrs, partisans and memorial days; good for donation and time-use statistics; thin for minority voices on sacrifice.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Russian for KGB files; Polish for the Home Army; Yiddish and Hebrew; Latin and German for the medieval record.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the partisans (*miško broliai*) are honored by some communities and accused by others: present each account as an account. Kalanta's case touches suicide and psychiatric labeling by the regime; treat with care. Ghetto testimony leads on Wittenberg.
- **Held for review:** none; no sacrifice pointer had a reason to be withheld.
