## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia and Suvalkija:** compare farmstead layout and boundary-quarrel records, since single farmsteads and street villages set neighbors at different distances.
- **Dzūkija:** check forest and border-village memory along the Belarus line, before and after the fence.
- **Poles of the Šalčininkai and Vilnius districts:** follow the quarrels over bilingual signs as a contest about public lines (law belongs to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check how kin split by the state border speak of crossing it now.
- **Russians, Old Believers and Visaginas:** check Old Believer congregations' own accounts of keeping apart from outsiders, and apartment life in Visaginas.
- **Lithuanian Tatars and the Trakai Karaim:** compare marriage and burial lines as each community records them.
- **The Litvak community:** the *eruv* and the ghetto wall are opposite kinds of line; keep them distinct.
- **Klaipėda and Lithuania Minor:** compare a port's open edge with the Spit's guarded past.
- **Roma:** check settlement histories (Kirtimai in Vilnius) for how the edge of a neighborhood is drawn by outsiders and by residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land history, folklore and surveys; mixed for privacy and personal space; thin where Belarusian and Roma people speak of their own lines.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian; Old Ruthenian (chancery Slavic), Latin and Polish for the Metrica; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Belarus border since 2021, the bilingual-sign question and the ghetto are contested or painful: give each account as its community's own. The demolition of Roma homes at Kirtimai is disputed.
- **Held for review:** none; no boundary source had to be held back.
