# Lithuania: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian; Polish of the Vilnius region; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Samogitia against Dzūkija:** set district memories of *legalizacija* side by side, since armed resistance ended at different times in each.
- **Aukštaitija and Suvalkija:** check Laima and fate songs by district for different endings.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** the 1939-1940 changes of rule over Vilnius are told differently in Polish sources; find those tellings first.
- **Russians and Old Believers:** look for *smirenie* (humility) in congregations' own teaching and in Visaginas life stories after 2009.
- **Belarusians of the southeast:** check fate vocabulary in Belarusian folk material from Šalčininkai and Varėna districts.
- **Lithuanian Tatars:** compare how their writings speak of yielding to God's will with Catholic sources.
- **The Litvak community:** *bitokhn* and ghetto testimony belong in Yiddish and Hebrew first; never fold them into national narratives.
- **Lutherans of Lithuania Minor:** check Pietist hymns of submission against Catholic deathbed customs.
- **The old and the dying:** hospice data by municipality could show where letting go is supported.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1940 decision, partisan amnesties and folklore of fate; mixed for end-of-life practice; thin for minority teaching on resignation.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian, including 17th-century print; Russian for amnesty files; Polish; Yiddish and Hebrew for the ghetto diary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1940 and *legalizacija* are argued over as shame or prudence; give each as its source's account. Ghetto councils are deeply painful; survivors' voices lead. Euthanasia is a legal question for Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on yielding was withheld.
