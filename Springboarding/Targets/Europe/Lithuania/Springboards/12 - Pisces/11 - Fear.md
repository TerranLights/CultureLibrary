# Lithuania: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Lithuania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Lithuanian (dialect forms for bogeymen and fright cures); Vilnius-region Polish; Lithuania's Russian; Belarusian of the southeast and of recent arrivals; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Aukštaitija and Dzūkija:** compare bogeyman names and fright cures in archive files, since forest and lake districts tell different warnings.
- **Samogitia:** look at plague graves and legends against the 1709-1711 records, which bore hardest in the west and in Lithuania Minor.
- **Klaipėda and the Curonian Spit:** check storm, sea and drowning lore among fishing families.
- **Vilnius-region Poles:** gather, from their own media, Polish-language voices on anxieties about language and school policy.
- **Russians and Visaginas:** compare fears over the plant's closure and over suspicion of loyalty since 2022, in Russian-language interviews.
- **Belarusians, long-settled and newly arrived:** separate southeastern villagers' concerns from those of exiles since 2020.
- **The Litvak community:** ghetto diaries and testimony come first; Lithuanian sources do not stand in for them.
- **Karaim and Lithuanian Tatars:** wartime and Soviet-era dread may surface in memoirs published by each community; expect few.
- **Ukrainians since 2022:** look for their own groups' accounts of war fear carried into Lithuania.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, Soviet-era memory and preparedness material; mixed for clinical anxiety; thin for Karaim, Tatar and Roma accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Lithuanian (old orthography for the 1547 catechism); Latin for its preface; Polish; Russian; Belarusian; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fear of Russia, of Belarus and of minorities' loyalty is politically charged; quote each group's own voice and never assign a fear to a people. Ghetto diaries need care; use published editions.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing about fear or dread was withheld.
