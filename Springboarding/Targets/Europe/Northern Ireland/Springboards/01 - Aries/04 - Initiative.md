## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East Belfast's engineering trades and west Belfast's community economy:** check how initiative from below is described in shipyard records and in community-business histories.
- **Derry / Londonderry:** check whose initiative is credited in the founding stories of the credit union and the Inner City Trust.
- **North Down and the Ards:** check the Plantation founders' accounts against later local histories that retell them.
- **Mid-Ulster (Dungannon, Cookstown, Magherafelt):** check firm density and family ownership in the engineering cluster against Belfast.
- **South Armagh and the border:** check how cross-border trading is recorded in local histories, staying clear of reputation-based framings.
- **Irish speakers and integrated-school parents:** check who started each school, and with what support or opposition.
- **Irish Travellers:** check trades such as tinsmithing and horse dealing as self-employment in Traveller-led sources.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese, Syrian and Filipino residents:** check which first ventures each group starts and through which networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business statistics and founding biographies; thin for informal enterprise and for newer communities' firms, which need interviews and trade directories.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for monastic lives; early modern English and Scots for Plantation papers; Irish for Gaeltacht projects; Cantonese and Polish for community business media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Plantation founding accounts and border trade are contested topics; give each as an account. Business regulation is law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Northern Ireland's lands (one pointer).
