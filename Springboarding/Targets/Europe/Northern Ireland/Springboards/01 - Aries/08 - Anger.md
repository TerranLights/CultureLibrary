## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Belfast interfaces (Ardoyne, Glenbryn, the Limestone Road):** check when in the year tensions rise and who steps in to cool them.
- **Derry / Londonderry:** check how anniversaries (Bloody Sunday in January, the Apprentice Boys' parade in August) rekindle or channel anger on each side.
- **Portadown and Drumcree:** check the rival accounts of the 1995 to 2000 standoffs and how local people describe their anger.
- **Border districts of south Armagh and Fermanagh:** check land quarrels and family grudges in folklore apart from political violence.
- **Men and women:** check health-survey and helpline data for how each expresses anger and seeks help.
- **Football supporters (Linfield, Glentoran, Cliftonville, Derry City):** check club codes of conduct and the incidents that led to them.
- **Roma families in south Belfast, Polish, Filipino and Chinese households:** check each community's own media for how it describes anger and fear after attacks, beside police framing.
- **Irish Travellers:** check grievance accounts in Traveller-led sources against settled accounts of disputes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective anger (riots, inquiries, the Troubles); thin for everyday temper at home and work, which needs ethnographic and survey work.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish and Latin for saga and penitential vocabulary; Ulster Scots for temper words; Cantonese, Polish and Arabic for victims' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** riots, 1641, parades and interface violence are contested; quote each source as an account and keep victims' testimony within its consent terms. Riot law goes to Libra, revenge to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** none; the minority-anger pointers came from a Belfast research institute whose reports needed no item withheld.
