## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Glens of Antrim and the Ards (Portaferry):** check how hurling rivalries are told in the two Ulster districts where the game has deep roots.
- **The north-west (Derry, Strabane, Donemana):** check why cricket draws teams from both communities and how its rivalries are framed.
- **County Armagh:** check the road-bowling scene and orchard-country fair contests, and who bets.
- **Grammar and non-grammar schools:** check how pupils from each describe themselves after the transfer test.
- **Mid-Ulster and Tyrone:** check Lambeg drumming matches and Gaelic football rivalry as two separate contest worlds.
- **Marching and pipe bands; feiseanna and fleadhanna:** check whether competitive music is shared across communities or held apart.
- **Women and girls:** check camogie, ladies' Gaelic football, hockey and netball records against the better-documented men's games.
- **Irish Travellers:** check horse fairs and sport in Traveller-led sources; data likely thin.
- **Filipino, Lithuanian, Polish, Hong Kong Chinese and Syrian residents:** check which contests newcomers enter and which they build themselves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for sport and school selection; thin for workplace rivalry and envy, which need interviews and local press.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for the saga contests; Irish for feis and GAA records; Tagalog, Lithuanian and Polish for community-league sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sport has carried sectarian rivalry (Belfast Celtic, chanting, flags at grounds); give each as an account. Selection rules are policy, so their legal side goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; community-league pointers were left at the query stage because the clubs themselves are still to be identified.
