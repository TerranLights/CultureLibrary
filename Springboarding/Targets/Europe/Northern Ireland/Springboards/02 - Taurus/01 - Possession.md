# Northern Ireland: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Irish (Ulster dialect); Old and Middle Irish for the saga record; Traveller Cant; Polish, Arabic, Cantonese and Portuguese for newer families. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Belfast and the interface streets:** check households burned or moved out in 1969 to 1972, and how both Catholic and Protestant families tell what they lost.
- **The Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** check wreck-salvage and beachcombing lore, and who claims what the sea brings in.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check pawnbroking and the "good coat" in shirt-factory households, on both the cityside and the Waterside.
- **South Armagh and the Mournes:** check how Murphy's informants talk about crocks of gold and taking stones or timber from forts.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands and the Sperrins:** check heirlooms kept on small farms and what leaves with emigrant children.
- **Congregations:** check Presbyterian, Brethren and Elim sermons on treasure in heaven beside Catholic and Church of Ireland teaching on goods, and what "others" say.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check how Traveller families themselves describe a dead relative's trailer and belongings, and what evictions took; start with Traveller-led groups.
- **Jewish Belfast, Hong Kong Chinese, Syrian, Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese and Filipino families, Roma:** check which objects come from the old home and which are sent back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; museum records are good, household evidence is scattered in oral history; thin for Roma and Syrian families.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Irish for the saga; Ulster Scots for gear and moving words; Arabic and Polish for newer families' own accounts of what they carried.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burnings and forced moves are contested, with accounts from each side; give each as an account. Hoards and the Broighter case touch north-south claims; treat them as claims.
- **Held for review:** treasure-finding pointers were weighed against the law-only rule; the legal side of treasure trove is left for Libra, nothing else was set aside.
