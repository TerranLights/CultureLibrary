# Northern Ireland: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Irish (Ulster dialect; nineteenth-century Ulster Irish for proverbs); seventeenth-century Scots and English for catechism and covenant texts; Polish, Arabic, Tetum and Portuguese for newer congregations. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ballymena, mid-Antrim and the Free Presbyterian heartland:** check Sabbath-keeping and dancing or drink rules against what surveys show for the same areas.
- **West Belfast and the Gaeltacht Quarter:** check how Irish-language schools and clubs state their aims.
- **Derry / Londonderry:** check the civil-rights generation against their children, using the EVS age bands where samples allow.
- **The Glens of Antrim and Rathlin:** check how small Catholic and Protestant communities share values in a place where both are few.
- **Fermanagh and south Tyrone:** check border parishes and Methodist circuits for ranking family, land and faith.
- **The Ards and Strangford Lough:** check fishing and farming villages with Brethren and Elim congregations.
- **"Others" and the secular young in south Belfast:** check whether "no religion" respondents rank values differently from either bloc.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics, East Timorese families in Dungannon, Filipino congregations:** check how they meet local Catholic practice.
- **Belfast Jewish community, Muslim communities, Roma, Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check each group's own statements of what must be kept; Traveller and Roma sources are thin, start with their own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and church texts; mixed for everyday ranking; thin for the newer faiths.
- **Languages needed:** nineteenth-century Ulster Irish for the proverbs; early-modern Scots and English for the catechism and covenant; Polish and Arabic for new congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slogans, the Covenant, the civil-rights platform and Rule 21 are contested and politically used; present each as one tradition's statement.
- **Held for review:** newer-faith pointers were limited to bodies with a known Belfast base; none had to be set aside under the standing rules.
