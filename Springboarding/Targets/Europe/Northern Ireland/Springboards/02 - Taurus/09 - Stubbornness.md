## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Derry / Londonderry:** check how the siege story and the Bloody Sunday campaign each frame holding out, among the Apprentice Boys and among the Bogside families.
- **Portadown and the Garvaghy Road:** check each side's own explanation of why it would not yield in the 1990s.
- **West Belfast and the Shaw's Road families:** check Irish-language revival told as perseverance.
- **East Belfast and the shipyard:** check how workers describe the 2019 occupation and earlier strikes.
- **North Antrim and Ballymena:** check Free Presbyterian, Reformed Presbyterian and Brethren accounts of standing firm on doctrine.
- **South Armagh and Tyrone:** check memory of the Nine Years' War and later holdouts in local history and song.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands and the Sperrins:** check farm families refusing to sell land, in their own words.
- **"Others" and the young:** check how people outside both blocs describe changing their minds, using survey items where they exist.
- **Irish Travellers, Roma, Hong Kong Chinese, Polish, Lithuanian, Filipino and Syrian families:** check persistence in keeping language and customs, from each community's own sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political standoffs and commemorations; mixed for everyday stubbornness; thin for newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century English and Scots for siege and covenant texts; early-modern Irish for O'Neill-era sources; Irish and Ulster Scots for the everyday words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the siege, hunger strikes, Drumcree, Bloody Sunday and apologies are contested; give each as an account and pair accounts from both traditions; victims' groups must be heard on prisoner narratives.
- **Held for review:** inquiry and inquest findings came up as possible law pointers; only their public apology and campaign side is used here, the rest left to Libra and Capricorn.
