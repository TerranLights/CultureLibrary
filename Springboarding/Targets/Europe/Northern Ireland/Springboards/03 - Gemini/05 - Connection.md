## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast's interfaces (the Shankill and the Falls; north Belfast):** check how residents keep ties across a wall, and with whom.
- **East Belfast and the shipyard streets:** check whether "speaking for" still decides jobs in any trade.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check family ties stretched across the border to Donegal.
- **Mid-Ulster and Dungannon:** check how East Timorese, Portuguese, Polish and Lithuanian workers found their first contacts.
- **The Glens of Antrim, Rathlin and the Ards:** check ties to Scotland by sea, past and present.
- **South Armagh, the Mournes and south Down:** check wakes and funerals as gatherings that renew kin ties.
- **The Sperrins, Tyrone and Fermanagh:** check rural isolation and the role of church, GAA club and Orange hall in keeping people linked.
- **The Lough Neagh shore:** check fishing families' networks along the lough.
- **Catholic, Protestant and "other" respondents:** check whether loneliness and contact figures differ by community background and how.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check how kin are gathered for weddings, funerals and fairs; few sources, start with Traveller groups.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Filipino, Roma, Syrian, Muslim and Jewish families:** check ties to home countries and to each other inside Northern Ireland.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for contact studies and loneliness figures; mixed for job networks; thin for Roma and East Timorese ties.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Irish for Magh Rath; Cantonese for lineage materials; Portuguese and Tetum for Dungannon.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recruitment by family or lodge, football ties and interface life are contested; present each as an account and avoid tying a trait to a community.
- **Held for review:** networks of the newer communities were the likeliest place for a rule-bound pointer; on review none was named, so nothing is held.
