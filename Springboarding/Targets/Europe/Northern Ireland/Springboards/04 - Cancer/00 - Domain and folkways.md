## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (east, west, north; the Shankill and the Falls):** compare terraces, redeveloped estates and flats, and how near parents grown children settle.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** look at Creggan and the Waterside for estate-building and family size over time.
- **The Sperrins, Tyrone and Fermanagh lakelands:** check how farm succession decides who stays in the homeplace and who leaves.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** check kin ties across the sea to Scotland and seasonal returns home.
- **The Ards and Strangford Lough; the Mournes and south Down:** compare Ulster-Scots and Irish house names and farmstead plans.
- **South Armagh, the Armagh orchards and the Lough Neagh shore:** check border-straddling families and dispersed farmhouses.
- **Catholic, Protestant (Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren, Elim) and "other" homes:** set each church's teaching on the household beside measured household data.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check trailers, sites and group housing as homes; sources are few, so start with Traveller organizations.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Belfast Jewish, Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese, Filipino, Roma, Syrian and Muslim households:** check who shares a roof here and who stayed behind, through each group's own bodies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing history and household counts; mixed for family memory and hosting; thin for Cant-speaking, Roma and Tetum-speaking homes.
- **Scope:** the tracker lists only "UK" as pre-war lands and the post-war lands are still to be settled; this Cancer set writes for the six counties, with the Republic (Donegal, Cavan and Monaghan included) as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Scots and Irish for house and kin words; seventeenth-century hands for the hearth rolls; Cantonese and Tetum for some households.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** housing allocation and conflict-era forced moves are contested; give each account as an account. Family papers may touch living people.
- **Held for review:** the household and housing sources here were checked against the standing rules; nothing set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the 1937 census housing volume; note persons per room by county borough and rural district.

3. Find Pobal Feirste or Shaws Road oral histories on how the houses were built.
4. Request the NICOLA variables on household members and care given or received.
5. Read McDowell and Deane side by side for who holds the family's memory.
