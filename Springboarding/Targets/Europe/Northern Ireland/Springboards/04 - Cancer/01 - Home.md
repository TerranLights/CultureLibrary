# Northern Ireland: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast's Shankill, Falls, east and north:** compare which streets were cleared, which were kept, and where displaced families say home now is.
- **Derry / Londonderry, Cityside and Waterside:** set the Bogside flats beside the Waterside estates for how home was rebuilt after clearance.
- **The Glens of Antrim and Rathlin:** check emigrants' "home" visits and houses kept for returning kin.
- **The Mournes and south Down; south Armagh:** compare stone farmhouses and the border townland as home place.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Fermanagh's lakeland islands:** check abandoned farmhouses left standing beside new bungalows.
- **The Ards, Strangford Lough and the Lough Neagh shore:** compare fishing households and chalets turned year-round dwellings.
- **Churchgoing households (Catholic; Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren, Elim) and those of no faith:** check house blessings, wall texts and Sabbath rules on household work.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check what trailers, sites and houses each mean as home; Traveller-led bodies first.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese and East Timorese workers in Dungannon, Portadown and Ballymena:** check shared private rentals and the "home" kept abroad.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Filipino, Syrian, Roma, Muslim and Belfast Jewish families:** check flats above businesses, resettlement housing and homes rebuilt after attack, through each group's own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public housing, valuation and memoir; mixed for the feeling of home; thin for Cant speakers, Roma and the newest arrivals.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century Ulster Irish for Mac Cumhaigh; Ulster Scots for fireside vocabulary; Polish and Portuguese for workers' housing forums.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homes lost to intimidation, burning and clearance are contested ground; each account stays an account; never publish attacked families' addresses.
- **Held for review:** housing studies of migrant workers were weighed; no pointer had to be set aside.
