## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West and north Belfast; the Shankill:** check how near married children live to their mothers, and how clearance changed it.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** look at cross-border kin with Donegal and how families crossed for weddings and wakes.
- **The Sperrins and Mid-Ulster:** check the shared surname clusters in one townland and nicknames used to tell them apart.
- **South Armagh and the Mournes:** compare kin networks across the border and the role of the eldest son on the farm.
- **The Ards Peninsula and north Antrim:** check Ulster-Scots naming patterns and links with kin in Scotland.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands and the Lough Neagh shore:** check fishing and farming families where kin share boats, land or rights.
- **Catholic, Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren and Elim households, and "others":** compare godparents or sponsors, mixed marriages and who is invited to which rite.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check extended-family networks and travel between related families; sparse sources, so begin with Traveller bodies.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Filipino and Muslim families:** check relatives brought over in sequence and kin left abroad.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese, Roma, Syrian and Belfast Jewish families:** check godparents chosen at home or here, and transnational family calls.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy and registers; mixed for present-day kin contact; thin for Traveller and Roma kin, where outsiders' records dominate.
- **Languages needed:** Early Irish for the genealogies; Ulster Irish for kin words; Tetum for alliance terms in Dungannon families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** killings within families and mixed marriages under threat are painful ground; give accounts as accounts and keep living people anonymous.
- **Held for review:** genealogy and kin-network studies were checked against the standing rules; no family-history source was held back.
