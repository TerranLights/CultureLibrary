## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Belfast and the Mater catchment; east Belfast:** compare hospital, hospice and home-care use, and the role of neighbors in daily checks.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check daughters' and sons' care of parents, including care given to or by kin across the border.
- **The Sperrins, Tyrone and Fermanagh lakelands:** check how distance to services shapes care of the old on farms.
- **South Armagh and the Mournes:** check whether and where folk cures and "charmers" are consulted, and how families talk about them.
- **The Glens of Antrim, Rathlin and the Causeway Coast:** check how islanders and glen families arrange care when services are far.
- **The Ards and the Lough Neagh shore:** check fishing and farming households where carers juggle seasonal work.
- **Churches (Catholic orders, Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren, Elim) and secular providers:** compare who runs homes and visiting schemes.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check family care of the sick and old on sites; sources few, so start with Traveller health workers.
- **Hong Kong Chinese elders:** check who cares for older people who came in the 1960s to 1980s.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese, Roma, Syrian, Muslim and Jewish families:** check care given across borders and care work done for others here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, inspection and inquiry evidence; mixed for family practice; thin for folk cures, which are often kept within families.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for *Bretha Crólige*; Tagalog, Polish and Portuguese for care-worker accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some families treat "the cure" as private and not to be recorded; care-home deaths and abuse cases involve grieving families.
- **Held for review:** one pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule; category only: South Asian-heritage communities in Northern Ireland's lands.
