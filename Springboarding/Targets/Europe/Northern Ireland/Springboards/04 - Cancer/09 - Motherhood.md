## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West and north Belfast; the Shankill:** check mothers' groups formed in the conflict years, from each community's own records.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check how households split paid and home work when mothers were shirt-factory workers.
- **South Armagh, the Mournes and south Down:** check home births, handywomen and the move to hospital births.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and the Fermanagh lakes:** compare family size by area, from census data, and the distance to maternity units.
- **The Glens of Antrim, Rathlin and the Causeway Coast:** check where island mothers give birth and how they travel.
- **The Ards, Strangford Lough and the Lough Neagh shore:** check mothers in fishing and farming households.
- **Catholic, Church of Ireland, Presbyterian, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren and Elim women, and women of no faith:** compare church teaching on mothers with women's own accounts.
- **Women who gave birth in mother and baby homes, and their adult children:** check the language each group uses now.
- **Irish Traveller mothers (Mincéirí):** check birth, health and child-rearing in Travellers' own health projects.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, East Timorese, Filipino, Roma, Syrian, Muslim and Jewish mothers:** check postpartum customs kept or dropped, and grandmothers brought over.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for maternity statistics and the institutions inquiry; mixed for everyday mothering; thin for Roma and Tetum-speaking mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Irish for the Macha tale; Cantonese, Arabic and Tagalog for postpartum customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mother and baby homes, forced separations and adoptions are painful and partly restricted; prisoners' mothers' campaigns are contested, so present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** maternity and mothers' movement sources were reviewed against the standing rules; no pointer was withheld.
