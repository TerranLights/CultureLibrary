## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (east and west; the Shankill and the Falls):** compare mural studios, band workshops and community arts centers for who trains makers.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check the shirt-factory sewing skill against the city's later arts boom.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands:** compare Belleek's factory craft with farm and lake crafts nearby.
- **The Lough Neagh shore, Tyrone and the Sperrins:** look at eel-net making, stone carving and fiddle making as local skills.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin; the Ards and Strangford Lough:** check boatbuilding and sea crafts on both coasts.
- **South Armagh, the Armagh orchards, the Mournes and south Down:** check drystone walling, basketry and Irish-language song making.
- **Ulster-Scots speakers and Irish speakers:** set the rhymer and the song maker side by side for how a new verse is judged.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check tin, horse and trading skills from Travellers' own groups; written sources are thin.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and East Timorese weekend schools and cultural groups:** see which crafts (*wycinanki* paper-cutting, straw ornaments, *tais* weaving) are taught to children here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fine art, poetry and museum craft; mixed for invention and everyday fixes; thin for Traveller, Roma and Tetum-speaking makers.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Irish for the Armagh manuscript; Irish for song making; Ulster Scots for workshop and rhyme words; Polish and Tetum for community crafts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** murals and drum and banner designs carry contested symbols; give each maker's account as an account. Traveller craft knowledge belongs to families; ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** craft-guild and creative-industry sources were weighed for named makers the standing rules cover; nothing in this creativity set was set aside.
