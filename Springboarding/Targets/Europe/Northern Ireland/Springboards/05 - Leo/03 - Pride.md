## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (east and west; the Shankill and the Falls):** compare band names, murals and club crests for whose pride is shown where.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check city pride from the walls to the Bogside, as each side tells it.
- **South Armagh and south Down; the Mournes:** look at county football pride and border-parish identity.
- **The Ards Peninsula and Strangford Lough:** check Ulster-Scots heritage events and how they are received locally.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Mid-Ulster:** compare GAA and band pride in neighboring villages.
- **The Glens, the Causeway Coast, Rathlin, the Fermanagh lakelands, the Lough Neagh shore and the Armagh orchards:** look at pride in produce and place (apples, eels, coast).
- **Catholic, Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren and Elim congregations; "others":** compare teaching on pride as sin against pride in one's people.
- **LGBT people:** check Pride's spread beyond Belfast to county towns.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check whether Traveller pride events are held in the North; sources likely thin.
- **Portuguese, East Timorese and Polish communities of Mid-Ulster (Dungannon, Cookstown):** check pride in origin against pride in the new home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for group and sporting pride; mixed for personal pride and arrogance; thin for Traveller and Roma pride.
- **Languages needed:** Classical Modern Irish for the bardic texts; modern Irish for *An tUltach*; Ulster Scots for heritage writing; Polish and Tagalog for community events.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** group pride here is tied to flags, bands and sport and is contested; give each account as its own. The 1970s campaign records name living people.
- **Held for review:** pride sources here meant national days and heritage bodies; among those weighed, none fell under the standing rules, so this sheet sets nothing aside.
