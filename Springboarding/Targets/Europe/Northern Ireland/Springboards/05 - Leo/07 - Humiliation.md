## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Belfast (Ardoyne and Glenbryn) and the Shankill and the Falls:** set each side's telling of school-run and interface disputes beside the other.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check how slagging and comedy work as a release, and when they turn to bullying.
- **Mid-Ulster, Tyrone and the Sperrins:** look at session discipline and Catholic confraternity rules in rural congregations.
- **South Down, the Mournes and the Ards:** compare penance at holy wells with public rebuke in session minutes.
- **South Armagh, the Lough Neagh shore and the Armagh orchards:** check accounts of searches, shaming and mockery at fairs.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast, Rathlin and the Fermanagh lakelands:** look at mocking songs and nicknames in small places.
- **Women in both traditions:** check who was shamed for "fraternizing" or "informing," in their own accounts.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** ask Traveller groups how they describe media portrayals and refusals of service.
- **Roma, Polish, Hong Kong Chinese and Muslim households:** check workplace and street experiences; sources often sit with advice centers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for conflict-era events and inquiries; mixed for everyday slagging and school shaming; thin for Roma and Tetum-speaking workers.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for the saga; Irish and Ulster Scots for shame words; Polish and Tagalog for workers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tarring, strip-searches and Holy Cross are contested and involve living people; present each account as an account and avoid naming private persons.
- **Held for review:** workplace-treatment reports were the likeliest place for a community covered by the rules to appear; the row is left as a search, not a named study, so nothing had to be held.
