## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (the Falls, the Shankill; north and east):** compare street play, adventure playgrounds and bonfire building across districts.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check how the Halloween carnival grew from children's street custom.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** look at hurling clubs and seaside play.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Mid-Ulster; the Lough Neagh shore:** check road bowling, card nights and football in villages.
- **South Armagh and the Armagh orchards; south Down and the Mournes:** compare camogie, racing and hill walking as play.
- **The Ards and Strangford Lough; the Fermanagh lakelands:** check sailing and boating clubs and who joins them.
- **Presbyterian, Free Presbyterian, Brethren, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Elim and Catholic families, and "others":** compare rules on Sunday games and card playing.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check horses, sulkies and children's games through Traveller organizations; written sources are few.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Syrian and Filipino children:** see which games come from home and which children pick up at school.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for organized sport and festivals; mixed for children's street play; thin for Traveller and Roma play and for newcomers' games.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for early hurling texts; Ulster Scots for rhymes; Cantonese and Arabic for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bonfire building and some youth "play" near interfaces are contested; give each account as its own. Children's play recordings need consent.
- **Held for review:** a stage comedian first weighed here for verbal play went to the Joy sheet instead; that was a question of fit, not of the standing rules, so this line holds nothing.
