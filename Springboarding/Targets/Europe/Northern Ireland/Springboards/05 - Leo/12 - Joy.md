## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (east, west, north and south; the Shankill and the Falls):** compare where people celebrate wins and festivals, and whether crowds mix.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check music, comedy and Halloween as the city's own sources of delight.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Mid-Ulster (Coalisland, Cookstown):** look at homecomings for sporting heroes in small towns.
- **The Causeway Coast, the Glens of Antrim and Rathlin:** check seaside fun and summer festivals.
- **The Ards, Strangford Lough, the Mournes and south Down:** compare charismatic worship, regattas and New Year customs.
- **South Armagh, the Armagh orchards, the Lough Neagh shore and the Fermanagh lakelands:** check apple-blossom, music and lake festivals.
- **Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Methodist, Free Presbyterian, Brethren, Elim and Catholic worshippers, and "others":** compare joy in worship and in festivity.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** ask how weddings and christenings are celebrated; published sources are few and often outsiders'.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, Portuguese and Filipino communities:** check which feasts are kept here and with whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, sport and well-being surveys; mixed for private delight; thin for Traveller, Roma and Tetum-speaking celebrations.
- **Languages needed:** Irish for song titles and harp tunes; Ulster Scots for New Year customs; Tagalog, Polish and Arabic for feasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some celebrations (the Twelfth, Easter, football chants) are contested and are covered in other sheets; give each account as its own.
- **Held for review:** two joy pointers set aside, category only: South Asian-heritage communities in Northern Ireland's lands.
