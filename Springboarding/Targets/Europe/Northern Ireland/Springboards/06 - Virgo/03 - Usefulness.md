## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast mill districts (the Falls, the Shankill, Ballymacarrett):** compare how children's earnings were spoken of in each.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check whether men's joblessness changed talk of who was "useful" at home.
- **The Glens of Antrim and Rathlin:** check the roles kept by island and glen elders: boat, story, tradition.
- **The Ards and Strangford Lough; the Mournes and south Down:** compare handiness praised in fishing and stone-working families.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Mid-Ulster:** check farm handover and the place of the retired farmer on the land.
- **South Armagh and the orchards; the Lough Neagh shore:** check which seasonal tasks were left to the old and the young.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands:** check how emigration left elders alone and how neighbors filled the gap.
- **Irish speakers and Ulster-Scots speakers:** compare arguments over whether each tongue is "of use".
- **Disabled people and their organizations:** check their own accounts against the "burden" language in policy.
- **Polish families and Hong Kong Chinese families:** keep apart; check grandparents' and children's roles separately.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check how trades once valued (tinwork, horse dealing) lost or kept their use, as Travellers tell it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mills and pensions; mixed for elders and disability; thin for migrant grandparents and for Travellers' own view.
- **Languages needed:** Irish for the language debate; Polish and Cantonese for family accounts; Ulster Scots for old farm words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "burden" talk near end-of-life care and disability is painful; quote people's own words, and handle care records with consent.
- **Held for review:** the flax lists, pension files and childcare survey carry no single author, so the question did not arise for usefulness.
