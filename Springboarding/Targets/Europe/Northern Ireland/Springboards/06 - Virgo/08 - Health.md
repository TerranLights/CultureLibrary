## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North and west Belfast; the Shankill and the Falls:** compare health gaps and how residents tie them to the Troubles, poverty or habits, each as told.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check cross-border use of doctors and hospitals with Donegal (comparison only).
- **The Fermanagh lakelands:** check the hereditary-physician memory and rural access to care.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** check island health care and journeys for treatment.
- **The Ards, Strangford Lough, the Mournes and south Down:** check spa, sea-bathing and fishing-injury records.
- **South Armagh, the orchards and the Lough Neagh shore:** check what wells and cures remain in use, and with whom.
- **Tyrone, the Sperrins and Mid-Ulster:** check farmers' health and the stress talk in farm papers.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check access to doctors and the health workers Travellers trust.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese-speaking patients:** keep apart; check where each turns first for care.
- **Filipino families:** check whether *hilot* and other home healing are used, as families describe them.
- **Hong Kong Chinese and Arabic-speaking families:** separate them; check interpreters, diet and remedies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health statistics and hospital history; mixed for folk healing; thin for newer communities' own remedies.
- **Languages needed:** Early Modern Irish for medical manuscripts; Polish, Cantonese and Tagalog for patients' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asylum case books are closed for privacy; mental-health and addiction data need care; some well and healing customs are kept within families.
- **Held for review:** migrant-health and hospital-staff studies were the likeliest place for a rule-covered name in this concept; the pointers kept are public bodies, records and sites, so nothing had to be set aside.
