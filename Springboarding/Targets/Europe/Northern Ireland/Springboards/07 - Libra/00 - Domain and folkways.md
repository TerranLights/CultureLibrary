## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (the Shankill, the Falls, east and north) and Derry / Londonderry:** check interface areas for both mixed marriages and neighbor quarrels; note which city name each record uses.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** check Lammas Fair courting lore and marriages across the Moyle.
- **The Ards Peninsula and Strangford Lough:** test whether *infare* and *broose* survive in Down records more than elsewhere.
- **South Armagh, the Mournes and south Down:** check how civil disputes reached courts in the Troubles years against published claims of informal justice.
- **The Lough Neagh shore, the Sperrins, Tyrone, Mid-Ulster and the Fermanagh lakelands:** check sibling quarrels over small-farm inheritance, and cross-border marriages (Cavan and Monaghan as comparison only).
- **Marrying bodies (the Catholic Church; Presbyterian, Free Presbyterian, Church of Ireland and Methodist churches; Brethren and Elim; humanist celebrants):** compare written rules on mixed marriage and remarriage one body at a time.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** ask Traveller-led groups how matches and quarrels are settled; court and press accounts are outside claims.
- **Muslim, East Timorese and Hong Kong Chinese families; the Jewish community of Belfast:** keep apart: a registration question, a marriage exchange, a banquet custom, a congregation's records (likely thin).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law and church records; mixed for wedding custom; thin for Cant, Tetum and Roma material.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries only "UK" for this row; the Libra sheets take the six counties as the core, use the Republic (Donegal, Cavan and Monaghan included) for comparison alone, and leave the lands for the author to settle.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for the tracts; Law French for Davies; Ulster Scots; Tetum and Arabic for marriage terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mixed marriage, the McCann case and informal "justice" are contested; give each account as an account. Family-court reports carry reporting restrictions.
- **Held for review:** a UK-wide forced-marriage casework series was weighed and dropped because it does not separate Northern Ireland, not under the standing rules; nothing is held.
