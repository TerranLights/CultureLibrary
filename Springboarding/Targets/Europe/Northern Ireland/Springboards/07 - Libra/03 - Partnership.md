## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Belfast and the Gaeltacht Quarter; east Belfast's shipyard districts:** compare Irish-speaking households and shipyard squads for how shared work was organized.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check the Irish Society's and the companies' estates against tenants' own records.
- **Mid-Antrim (Gracehill and Ballymena):** check Moravian marriage and trade records against neighboring Presbyterian ones.
- **The Ards Peninsula and Strangford Lough:** check joint farm and boat ownership among Ulster Scots families.
- **The Sperrins, Tyrone, Fermanagh and the Lough Neagh shore:** check creamery cooperatives and joint holdings.
- **South Armagh, the Mournes and the Glens of Antrim:** check cross-border and across-the-Moyle marriages for where couples settle.
- **Same-sex couples in Belfast and in rural districts:** check civil-partnership and marriage registrations by council area.
- **Hong Kong Chinese catering families:** check how restaurants are held among relatives and partners.
- **Filipino and Polish couples:** check who migrates first and how sponsors (*ninong*, *świadek*) are chosen at a distance.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** ask Traveller-led groups about family trading partnerships; official business registers may miss them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plantation and estate papers are plentiful; partnership deeds and Moravian records need archive visits; newer couples' sources are few.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for sureties; German for early Moravian records; Tagalog and Polish for sponsorship customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mixed marriage and power-sharing are contested; family-court material on separating partners is restricted.
- **Held for review:** the couples' sources weighed were the 1996 mixed-marriage study, church liturgies and census tables; none touches a rule-covered name, so nothing is held.
