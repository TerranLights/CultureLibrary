# Northern Ireland: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Irish (Ulster usage; Early Modern Irish for the lordship era); Ulster Scots; Early Modern English for Tudor state papers; Cantonese, Arabic, Tetum, Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese for go-betweens in newer communities. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** check the talks between Bogside residents and the Apprentice Boys over parades for how local accommodation was reached.
- **West and north Belfast:** compare church-run contacts in the Clonard area with the interface phone networks between community workers.
- **South Armagh and the Fermanagh border:** check cross-border councils' joint projects for who spoke for whom.
- **The Glens of Antrim, the Causeway Coast and Rathlin:** check island and coastal links to Scotland for informal diplomacy over fishing and ferries.
- **The Ards Peninsula, Strangford Lough and the Mournes:** check how fishing fleets negotiate with regulators and with fleets across the sea.
- **Tyrone and the Sperrins; the Lough Neagh shore:** check how eel fishers and the estate's owners kept talking over the fishery.
- **Women's groups across the divide:** check minutes and memoirs for back-room work not credited in official records.
- **Hong Kong Chinese elders' associations:** check how disputes with neighbors are raised through community leaders or the council.
- **East Timorese families in Dungannon:** check whether customary sit-down settlements are used among them; sources may exist only in Timor.
- **Syrian refugee families:** check how resettlement officers and interpreters broker first contact; reports are few.
- **Irish Travellers (Mincéirí):** check Traveller-led groups' accounts of negotiating sites with councils.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the peace process is heavily documented; everyday tact and fair-day brokering live mostly in oral sources.
- **Languages needed:** Early Modern English and Irish for the O'Neill era; Cantonese, Arabic and Tetum for community go-betweens.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** back-channel files and some government papers stay closed; contacts with armed groups are contested, so each side's version stands as a version.
- **Held for review:** the consulates row was limited to missions of communities named on this sheet; none of them falls under the standing rules, so nothing is held.
