## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Six Mile Water valley, Ballymena and the Braid:** compare revival memory in 1625, 1859 and later missions across the same parishes.
- **East Belfast and the shipyard streets:** test the 1920s mission stories against labor and riot records.
- **West Belfast's Gaeltacht Quarter:** separate prison-learned Irish from school and family routes into the language.
- **Newry, south Down and south Armagh:** look at recovery houses and how recovery is talked of across the border.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** set the restored civic buildings against residents' accounts of what was rebuilt and what was not.
- **Fermanagh lakelands and Cuilcagh:** ask farmers, rangers and walkers how a restored bog is spoken of.
- **Evangelical, Catholic charismatic and Pentecostal congregations (Elim, Free Presbyterian, Brethren among them):** compare what counts as conversion, sudden or gradual, in each body's own teaching.
- **Former prisoners, loyalist and republican:** compare redemption stories in each group's own media, keeping every account its own.
- **Mincéirí and Roma:** set Traveller and Roma Pentecostal growth apart; their sources differ.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** compare Easter renewal customs, parish by parish.
- **Muslim converts and Hong Kong Chinese Christians:** keep apart, since one rests on converts' own accounts and the other on a migrant congregation's records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Protestant revival history and language revival; mixed for recovery and prison conversion; thin for Traveller, Roma and convert Muslim testimony.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Irish (check whether prison Irish coined its own words); Ulster Scots; Polish; Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison conversions of men convicted of killings are contested by victims' families; recovery meetings are anonymous by rule, so only published lists and memoirs are fair game.
