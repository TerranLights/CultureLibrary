*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Belfast and the Ballymurphy estates:** compare how informing is spoken of in republican memoirs and in families' accounts.
- **The Shankill and east Belfast loyalist districts:** check loyalist publications for their own vocabulary of the traitor and agent.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** set the 1602 defection story beside modern informer cases in the city.
- **South Armagh:** check how informing is described in the district's own local histories and press; read accounts, not reputations.
- **North Down and the Ards:** look at unionist "sell-out" rhetoric in constituencies that backed and opposed the 1998 Agreement.
- **Mid-Ulster and the Sperrins:** compare how republican splits are described in local papers.
- **Couples in mixed marriages:** check personal accounts of being called traitors by either side.
- **Hong Kong arrivals since 2021 and longer-settled Hong Kong Chinese families:** separate the two; their worries and their sources differ.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese workers:** treat each language's informer words as its own question.
- **Mincéirí and Roma:** ask community groups whether there are own words for informers; outsiders' reports are not a substitute.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for informers and agents (memoirs, inquiries, press); mixed for infidelity; thin for newer communities, where forums and community workers are the route.
- **Languages needed:** early modern English for Docwra; Irish; Cantonese slang; Polish and Portuguese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informer cases name people who were killed, or are still at risk; Kenova and Kincora carry contested claims; each party's account stays its own, and no unconfirmed name is repeated.
- **Held for review:** affair websites' user leaks were considered and dropped as a privacy risk to individuals; nothing fell under the standing rules.
