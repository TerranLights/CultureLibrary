## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast (the Shankill, the Falls, east and north):** compare church closures and new congregations, area by area.
- **Ballymena, the Braid and mid-Antrim:** check how many Protestant denominations each parish holds, against census rows.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** set the Colmcille (Columba) tradition beside both cathedrals' histories.
- **South Armagh and the Armagh orchards:** compare the two Armagh cathedrals' public roles with rural parish life.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands, the Glens and Rathlin:** check island sites, Irish-language devotion and Scottish links, place by place.
- **'Others' (neither bloc):** follow "no religion" rows and humanist ceremonies as their own category.
- **Hong Kong Chinese and Filipino residents:** check which congregations each joined or formed; their records sit apart.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** check whether chaplaincies run their own parish life or merge into local parishes.
- **Muslim communities, the Jewish community and Syrian refugee families:** check where each worships and who speaks for each in interfaith bodies.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** seek Traveller groups' own accounts of blessings and pilgrimages before outside descriptions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for denominational counts and church history; mixed for secular and newer-faith groups; thin for Cant religious words and Tetum-speaking worship (ask community workers).
- **Borders:** nothing beyond "UK" is recorded as the row's pre-war holding, so where the nation ends is for the author to fix; these Sagittarius sheets work from the six counties outward and consult Donegal, Cavan, Monaghan and the rest of the Republic only for contrast.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for church sources; Ulster Scots; Polish, Lithuanian, Cantonese and Arabic for congregation papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious labels double as community labels; census and church statements stay each body's own account. Law on religion belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Northern Ireland's lands; Buddhist practice in Northern Ireland's lands (an open question for the author). Both held as categories, no group named.
