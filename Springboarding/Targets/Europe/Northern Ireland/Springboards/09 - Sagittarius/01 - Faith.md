# Northern Ireland: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Irish (Ulster dialect); Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Tetum, Tagalog, Cantonese and Arabic for congregations of newer communities. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ballymena, Antrim and the Coleraine coast:** set gospel-mission conversion accounts beside charismatic healing ministries in the same towns.
- **West Belfast and north Belfast:** compare Catholic practice data with what parish clergy report of falling Mass numbers.
- **Mid-Ulster and the Lough Neagh shore:** look at votive customs at old crosses and wells against parish records.
- **The Fermanagh lakelands and south Armagh:** check pilgrimage parties and their organizers for border-crossing patterns.
- **Methodist, Church of Ireland, Free Presbyterian, Brethren and Elim congregations:** separate them; conversion, assurance and leaving are framed differently in each body's own literature.
- **'Others' and people who left a church:** seek accounts of loss of faith that are not framed by either community.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** compare chaplaincy attendance with local-parish attendance among the second generation.
- **Filipino and East Timorese Catholics:** treat separately; their devotions, languages and parish ties differ.
- **Muslim communities:** check Ramadan and Friday-prayer arrangements with mosque committees before outside reporting.
- **Mincéirí:** ask Traveller-led groups about blessings and pilgrimages, keeping their framing first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for denominational history and hymnody; mixed for practice data (each church counts differently); thin for Tetum, Tagalog and Traveller practice, needing interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Irish for well-round words; Ulster Scots; Polish, Lithuanian, Tagalog and Cantonese for congregation material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clerical-abuse inquiries bear on trust in churches (Scorpio's trust sheet); prisoners' testimonies involve living victims; each account stands as its teller's.
- **Held for review:** devotional publications from other Irish dioceses were considered and kept for comparison; no faith-practice source fell under the standing rules.
