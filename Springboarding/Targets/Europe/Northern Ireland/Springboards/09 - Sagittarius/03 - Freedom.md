# Northern Ireland: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Irish (Ulster dialect); French for Huguenot records; Polish, Lithuanian, Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic for newcomers' independence days and exile accounts. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast's Shankill and Sandy Row:** check Orange "civil and religious liberty" usage in lodge and banner records.
- **The Falls and the Bogside:** compare "freedom" in republican murals and commemorations with civil-rights-era leaflets.
- **The Ards Peninsula and north Down:** follow the 1798 radical-Presbyterian memory through Dickson and Porter country.
- **Lisburn and the Lagan valley:** set Huguenot refugee memory beside later arrivals' accounts.
- **South Armagh, Fermanagh and the border roads:** compare freedom of movement as experienced at crossings, before and after 1998 and after 2016.
- **The Mournes and the Sperrins:** check walkers' and landowners' accounts of access separately.
- **Women's groups across the divide:** compare definitions of freedom in their founding papers.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and East Timorese residents:** check how each community's independence memory is passed to children born here.
- **Hong Kong arrivals and earlier Hong Kong Chinese families:** compare how each speaks of 自由; they left under different conditions.
- **Syrian refugee families and Muslim communities:** check asylum-group records for what freedom means after flight.
- **Mincéirí:** seek Traveller accounts of the freedom to travel and stop, before official reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1790s, 1912-1914, civil rights and the 1981 hunger strike; mixed for walking access; thin for Timorese and Syrian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** French for Huguenot records; Ulster Scots; Irish for slogans and murals; Cantonese and Arabic for exile accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hunger strike, gun-running and border protests are contested; present each side's account as an account; prisoner and victim families are living.
- **Held for review:** Dublin and Westminster freedom writings were weighed and kept as comparison; nothing set aside under the rules.
