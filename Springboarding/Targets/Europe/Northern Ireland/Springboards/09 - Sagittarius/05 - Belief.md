## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Islandmagee, Carrickfergus and the Larne coast:** compare charm-and-cure belief records across two centuries of court and folklore sources.
- **The Lough Neagh shore and Mid-Ulster:** check origin tales of the lough against what shore families tell now.
- **Belfast's south and east:** trace spiritualist and skeptic meetings; both cluster in the city.
- **The Sperrins and Tyrone uplands:** set luck and omen beliefs in farm families beside church teaching.
- **South Down and the Mournes:** look for belief stories about named rocks and passes.
- **Catholic, Church of Ireland and Presbyterian schools:** compare how the shared RE syllabus is taught in each sector.
- **'Others' and nonbelievers:** follow atheist and humanist groups as their own subjects.
- **Lithuanian and Polish households:** keep apart; their calendars and church ties differ.
- **Hong Kong Chinese and Filipino families:** check siting and luck practices separately; their origins and religions differ.
- **Muslim communities:** start from what mosque teachers say, not from outsiders' labels.
- **Mincéirí:** ask Traveller groups which beliefs they consider theirs to share.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and religious-education material; mixed for belief surveys (small samples for minorities); thin for Filipino, Lithuanian and Muslim household belief.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Irish for the Lough Neagh tale; Ulster Scots for *freet* and *trow*; Lithuanian, Tagalog and Cantonese for household material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** belief and community label overlap; "superstition" is an outsider's word, so record each group's own term. Folk cures and spirit contact touch living practitioners.
- **Held for review:** new-religious-movement sources from Britain were weighed but were not about the six counties, so left aside; nothing was held under the standing rules.
