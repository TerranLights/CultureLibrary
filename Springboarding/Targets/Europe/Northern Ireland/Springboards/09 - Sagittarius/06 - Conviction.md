*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Antrim and Down Presbyterian districts:** check how the 1639 refusals are remembered in congregational histories.
- **South Armagh (Bessbrook and around):** compare Quaker-founded village memory with later accounts of the same place.
- **Ballymena, Kilkeel and Portadown:** follow tent-mission records for where "conviction" preaching drew crowds.
- **Ballymurphy and west Belfast:** set Des Wilson's writings beside parish and diocesan accounts.
- **Belfast city center and east Belfast:** check the 1985 protest's leaflets and later unionist reflections.
- **Integrated-school parents and 'others':** follow their papers separately from church-run sector records.
- **Republican and loyalist ex-prisoners, and prison staff:** keep each group's accounts in its own frame.
- **Polish and Lithuanian communities:** separate their commemorations; the events and dates differ.
- **East Timorese in Dungannon and Portuguese workers:** keep apart; histories and organizations differ.
- **Muslim communities and recent converts:** check mosque and convert networks separately.
- **Mincéirí:** ask Traveller organizations about their own campaigners and how they are remembered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church history and the 1980s protests; mixed for integrated-school archives; thin for Timorese and convert testimony.
- **Languages needed:** early modern Scots and English for the 1639 material; Polish, Lithuanian, Tetum and Portuguese for commemorations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison protests and solidarity campaigns are contested and touch living people; each account stays its teller's. Hong Kong activists here may face risk; name no individual.
- **Held for review:** conscientious objection from the world wars was weighed and found to concern Britain more than the six counties; nothing set aside under the standing rules.
