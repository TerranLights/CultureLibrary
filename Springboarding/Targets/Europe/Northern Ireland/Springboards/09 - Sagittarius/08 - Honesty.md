## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Fermanagh, Tyrone and south Armagh border parishes:** check how smuggling stories are told, and by whom, across generations.
- **Belfast's Shankill, Falls and east Belfast:** compare "doing the double" accounts from the 1980s with later welfare-reform reporting.
- **Mid-Ulster and the Lough Neagh shore:** look at dealing customs in eel, sand and farm trades as told by those in them.
- **Derry / Londonderry and the Foyle:** set Assembly-standards complaints beside council-level ones.
- **Catholic and Protestant children's instruction:** compare First Confession and Sunday-school material for when and how lying is named.
- **Polish and Lithuanian workers:** check labor-abuse cases by nationality; their agencies and contracts differ.
- **Portuguese- and Tetum-speaking agri-food workers of Mid-Ulster:** check how an agency's or employer's word is talked about in each language's own media, read separately, since each community has its own press and advice centers.
- **Hong Kong arrivals and earlier Hong Kong Chinese families:** compare which scams each meets.
- **Muslim communities:** start from teachers' own framing.
- **Mincéirí:** ask Traveller groups how trading honesty is judged inside the community before any outside account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official fraud estimates and folklore; mixed for informal-economy and smuggling accounts; thin for Timorese and Muslim community material.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for Mac Da Thó; Ulster Scots; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese and Tetum for labor cases; Cantonese for scam notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** smuggling and benefit-fraud talk can stigmatize whole districts or groups; record each teller's account, never a group trait. Prosecutions belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** UK-wide honesty experiments without a Northern Ireland sample were weighed and left for later; nothing set aside under the standing rules.
