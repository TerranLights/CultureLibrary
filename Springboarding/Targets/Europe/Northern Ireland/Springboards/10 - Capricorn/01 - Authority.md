# Northern Ireland: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belfast English and the rural speech of Tyrone and Antrim (Ulster Scots spoken there too); Irish of Ulster (and Old Irish for the saga material); Gammon; Polish and Lithuanian for parish life; Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic for community bodies. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Divis, the Falls and Ballymurphy:** compare armed-group, church and state claims to authority in residents' own accounts.
- **The Shankill and east Belfast:** check loyalist groups' and churches' claims to speak for residents, recorded separately.
- **The Bogside, the Creggan and the Waterside:** set the barricade period beside the city's later civic arrangements; both city names in use.
- **Crossmaglen, Newtownhamilton and the Fermanagh frontier parishes:** look in published local accounts for whom residents approached in place of police.
- **Munterloney and the Sperrins:** read native-speaker tales for landlord and priest figures, against estate records.
- **Mid-Antrim, Ballymena and the Braid:** check where church office and political office overlap, congregation by congregation.
- **Elected elders, bishops and gospel-hall assemblies:** compare Presbyterian, Catholic, Church of Ireland, Brethren and Elim ways of conferring and withdrawing authority.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** check whether homeland clergy or local parishes are turned to; their papers differ.
- **Muslim and Arabic-speaking families, Syrian arrivals among them:** check how mosque committees and resettlement officials share authority.
- **Roma households around the Lisburn Road and the Village:** look for records of dealings with police and social workers; little published, so begin with charities' reports.
- **Mincéirí:** check Traveller organizations' own accounts of family heads and elders before outside studies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policing and church government; mixed for household authority; thin for Cant terms and for authority inside newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** Tyrone Irish to read Ó Tuathail's tellers; Old Irish where the Cathbad motif is traced; Polish, Lithuanian and Arabic for congregation papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the authority of the state, of armed groups and of police is contested; Motorman, "no-go" areas and policing reform stay as each side tells them. Legal culture goes to the Law sheet.
- **Held for review:** of the pointers on clergy and congregational authority, none touched a restricted community or work, so the sheet holds none back.
