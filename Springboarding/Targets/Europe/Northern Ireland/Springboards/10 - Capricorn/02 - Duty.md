# Northern Ireland: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English in town and country; Ulster Scots of Antrim and Down; Ulster Irish, and Old Irish where the early law on parents is read; Tagalog, Polish, Lithuanian and Tetum for family obligation abroad; Cantonese and Arabic for filial and religious duty. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Protestant villages of the Ards and mid-Antrim with Somme memorials:** compare whose names were carved and who tends the memorials now.
- **Catholic parishes of west Belfast and Newry:** check memorials to Catholic war dead and to republican dead, and who keeps each.
- **Farm families of the Sperrins and Fermanagh:** check what the son or daughter who stays on the land is expected to give up.
- **Lough Neagh shore and Mourne fishing families:** check obligations within crews and between generations in co-operative and harbor records.
- **Presbyterian, Catholic and Methodist teaching:** set the catechisms' lists of duties side by side, then sermons that apply them.
- **Filipino families:** check how *utang na loob* and money sent home appear in their own community writing.
- **Polish and Lithuanian households:** check obligations to parents left at home, using homeland-language forums and Saturday schools.
- **East Timorese in Dungannon:** check whether customary obligations to the sacred house persist; few written sources.
- **Muslim households and Belfast's synagogue congregation:** compare religious duties (*zakat*; keeping a *minyan* with a small congregation) as each describes them.
- **Mincéirí:** check duties to extended family and at funerals in Traveller-written accounts first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military memory and religious teaching; mixed for care duties; thin for newer communities' obligations abroad and for the Cant vocabulary of duty (none confident enough to list).
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish to read the *goire* provisions; Ulster Irish for song texts; Tagalog and Tetum for family obligation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** service in the Crown forces, in republican and in loyalist organizations is each framed as duty by its own side; memorials and commemorations stay each side's own account.
- **Held for review:** filial-duty sources for the newer communities were the likeliest to need setting aside; the ones listed name no restricted group, so nothing is held.
