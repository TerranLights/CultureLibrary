*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ministers, advisers and civil servants at Stormont:** compare how each group describes its share in RHI and other failures.
- **Bogside families and veterans' associations:** set the reception of the 2010 statement side by side; accounts differ.
- **Loyalist and republican ex-prisoner groups:** compare what each says it owes the families of its victims.
- **Lough Neagh's eel fishers and lakeside farms:** check what fishermen, farmers and residents say about who must answer for the water.
- **Farm households in Fermanagh, south Tyrone and the Armagh orchards:** check who is held answerable for land, debt and safety when the farm passes on.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese families:** check children's interpreting load in school and service records.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check how responsibility for elders is shared between generations, using Cantonese community sources.
- **Arabic-speaking and Syrian households:** check how families and mosques talk about answering for young people's conduct.
- **Mincéirí:** check who answers for a family member's actions in Traveller-written accounts, not settled commentary.
- **The Jewish community of north Belfast:** check how a small congregation shares charge of its synagogue, cemetery and records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official inquiries and statements; mixed for household responsibility; thin for Ulster Scots and Irish-language writing on owning up, so start with dictionaries.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish to follow the ruler's-truth passages; Cantonese for the "wok" idiom and family sources; Portuguese and Lithuanian for school material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** apologies and statements on the Troubles are contested; each is an account. Institutional abuse testimony is protected. Where responsibility becomes criminal law, see the Law sheet and Libra.
- **Held for review:** survivor testimony and inquiry evidence were weighed for this concept; they are protected records rather than set-aside pointers, so the line stays empty.
