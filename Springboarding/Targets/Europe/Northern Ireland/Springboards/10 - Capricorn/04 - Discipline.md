# Northern Ireland: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** schoolroom and shop-floor English of Ulster; Ulster Scots of the bands and Bible classes; Irish, including early-modern devotional Irish; Polish and Lithuanian for fasting and parish customs; Cantonese and Arabic for study and religious practice. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Flute, pipe and accordion bands in mid-Ulster, the Ards and east Belfast:** compare band rules and practice demands, band by band.
- **Boxing and Gaelic games clubs in north and west Belfast and south Armagh:** check how trainers describe what they demand.
- **Former pupils of Christian Brothers and grammar schools:** compare memories of the strap by school type and decade.
- **Free Presbyterian, Brethren and Reformed Presbyterian families:** check Sabbath and abstinence rules as the congregations state them.
- **Catholic pilgrims to Lough Derg (in Donegal, comparison only):** check parish records of who goes from the six counties to fast there.
- **Muslim families in Belfast and Craigavon:** check how Ramadan is kept around school and shift hours.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** check Lenten customs kept in homeland-language Masses.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check study expectations through the Saturday schools and their parents.
- **Mincéirí:** check Traveller accounts of how children are corrected and schooled, before welfare reports.
- **Sperrins and Fermanagh farm households:** check the routines that farm work imposes on children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school, band and religious practice; mixed for home discipline; thin for Tetum and Cant terms (none listed for that reason).
- **Languages needed:** early-modern Irish for Mac Aingil; Cantonese for Saturday-school material; Arabic for Ramadan guidance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** training-school and institutional records hold abuse testimony; survivors' accounts come first. The "Green Book" and *Hunger* are contested; each is an account. Prison regimes go partly to Pisces.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist meditation in Northern Ireland, as a category only: a candidate under spiritual discipline, kept back while the author's question on Buddhism stays open.
