# Northern Ireland: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** courtroom, police and street English; Ullans; Irish (Early Modern Irish for annal entries on brehons); Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese on trust in police; Tetum for custom; Cantonese; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Capricorn takes the spirit of the law; the letter goes to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Republican and loyalist working-class districts of Belfast:** compare attitudes to police and courts, and to community restorative schemes, area by area.
- **South Armagh and the Fews:** check how outlaw memory and present-day views of the law are told locally, without assuming either.
- **Derry / Londonderry:** check how the city's civil-rights history shapes talk of the law's purpose; both names in use.
- **Mid-Antrim and the Ards:** check church teaching on rulers and conscience, congregation by congregation.
- **Fermanagh's brehon country and its later estates:** trace native law's memory against estate courts.
- **Former prisoners and prison officers:** compare what each says prison was for.
- **Polish and Lithuanian residents:** check trust in police against trust carried from home, using their own-language media.
- **East Timorese residents:** check whether *tara bandu* or other customary means are used among families here; few sources.
- **Hong Kong Chinese residents, recent and long-settled:** compare how "rule of law" is spoken of before and after 2020.
- **Muslim and Arabic-speaking residents:** check where religious and civil law are each turned to (marriage, inheritance).
- **Mincéirí:** look for how Traveller organizations themselves describe settling disputes and meeting the police.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emergency law, policing reform and rights debates; mixed for everyday legal culture; thin for Ulster Scots and Cant legal vocabulary.
- **Languages needed:** annal Irish to find the brehon entries; Cantonese for Hong Kong residents' sources; Tetum for customary law.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** emergency law, informers, prisons and killings of judges are contested and sometimes dangerous; give every account as an account and never apply a label such as "tout" to a person.
- **Held for review:** sources on religious law among newer communities were weighed; the pointers here are general advice-body reports, so nothing needs holding back.
