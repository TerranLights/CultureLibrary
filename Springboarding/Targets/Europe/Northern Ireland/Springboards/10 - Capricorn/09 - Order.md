# Northern Ireland: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** kitchen, street and newsroom English of Ulster; Ulster Scots; Irish (the source of the local word for "untidy"); Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese on neighborhood safety; Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic on peace and chaos. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Belfast's interface districts (north Belfast, Short Strand, Suffolk and Lenadoon):** compare residents' own accounts of what makes a street feel ordered or not.
- **Downpatrick, Armagh and Belfast:** check how each runs St Patrick's Day and who stays away.
- **Derry / Londonderry:** compare Halloween's carnival with the August and December parades; both names in use.
- **Portadown, Drumcree and the Garvaghy Road:** check the parade disputes from each community's records, without blending them.
- **Ballycastle, Rathlin and the northern glens:** look at how lanes, harbors and fair days are kept in order, the Ould Lammas Fair included.
- **Lisburn, Craigavon and the Lough Neagh shore:** check anti-social behaviour reports against residents' own sense of safety.
- **Hong Kong Chinese residents arriving after 2020:** check how they speak of public order and protest, in Cantonese sources.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese residents:** check experiences of intimidation and of feeling safe, through their own-language media.
- **Roma households in south Belfast:** check NGO records of displacement; few published sources.
- **Mincéirí:** check how Traveller organizations describe site rules and evictions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, parades and policing; mixed for household order; thin for newer communities' sense of public order.
- **Languages needed:** Irish for the *trína chéile* link; Cantonese for Hong Kong residents; Polish and Lithuanian for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** riots, interfaces, intimidation and parade disputes are contested; each account stays its own. Policing law goes to the Law sheet and Libra.
- **Held for review:** the intimidation data might identify households; it is pointed to in aggregate only, and nothing under the standing rules was set aside.
