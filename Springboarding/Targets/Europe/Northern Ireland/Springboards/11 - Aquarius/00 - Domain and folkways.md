# Northern Ireland: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of Ulster (Belfast speech, Mid-Ulster English); Ulster Scots (Ullans); Irish of the Ulster dialect, with Old Irish for early terms; the Travellers' Cant; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic for the associations of newer residents. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain: what draws people into groups and what they hope for (friends, associations, hopes, ideals toward humanity); settlement, visiting, neighboring and gathering are its folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shankill and Falls; east and north Belfast:** check which lodges, clubs and community groups each district's own histories list, and whether any membership crosses the interface.
- **Derry / Londonderry, Cityside and Waterside:** both names in use; check how associations on each bank of the Foyle describe their reach.
- **Glens of Antrim and Rathlin; Fermanagh lakelands and south Armagh:** compare glen and island visiting records with those of dispersed border farms.
- **Lough Neagh shore and Mid-Ulster:** check co-operative membership rolls for who joined with whom.
- **Church of Ireland, Presbyterian, Methodist, Free Presbyterian and Elim congregations; "others" of neither tradition:** compare halls, youth groups, aid appeals and integrated-school parent groups as separate associational worlds.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** begin with what Traveller-led groups themselves publish about fairs and big family occasions.
- **Hong Kong Chinese associations; Syrian families resettled after 2015:** different arrival routes, so read their association records apart.
- **Polish and Lithuanian residents:** check Saturday schools and parish groups in Dungannon, Portadown and Ballymena.
- **Belfast's Jewish congregation:** check minute books and refugee committee papers of the 1930s.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations, segregation and church life; mixed for visiting, which sits in folklore archives; thin for hopes about science and the future, which surveys mostly report for the UK only.
- **Lands:** "UK" is the tracker's only pre-war holding and the territory is not yet fixed; this sign takes the six counties as they stand, with the Republic (Donegal, Cavan and Monaghan included) as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Scots and Ulster Irish for visiting words and folklore; Cantonese, Arabic and Tetum for association papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parades, flags, the Covenant and Easter commemorations are contested; each is an account. Refugee files name living people.
- **Held for review:** association registers and newcomer studies were the likeliest sources to meet a restricted community; none named here required setting aside.
