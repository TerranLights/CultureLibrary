# Northern Ireland: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English, including the political "two communities" usage; Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish, and Old Irish for early farming terms; Cantonese, Arabic, Polish, Lithuanian and Portuguese for neighborhood life among newer residents. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), who know one another as members and owe one another something; and how that bond feels.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Townland communities of the Sperrins and Tyrone:** check how far "our townland" still names a working community, using parish and GAA-club histories.
- **The Ards fishing villages and Strangford farms:** compare church-centered and harbor-centered ties in local histories.
- **Interface streets of north Belfast against new-build estates outside Belfast:** check how residents of each define "the community" in tenants' associations.
- **Derry / Londonderry's Waterside and Cityside:** both names in use; check flood and regeneration reports for who organized on each side.
- **Rathlin and the Glens of Antrim:** check island and glen sources for neighbor work and who counts as local.
- **Congregations as communities (Church of Ireland, Methodist, Brethren, Catholic parishes):** compare how each marks a new member and a lapsed one.
- **East Timorese and Portuguese residents of Dungannon:** check Tetum and Portuguese community groups' records separately; sources are likely scarce.
- **Hong Kong Chinese across generations:** compare the long-settled families with arrivals since 2021.
- **Roma families in south Belfast:** check what happened after the 2009 attacks from Roma-led or advocacy sources.
- **Syrian households in council areas outside Belfast:** check council resettlement reports for how they were placed.
- **Belfast's Jewish community:** check its own publications for how a small congregation holds together.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on "community relations", which can crowd out other meanings of community; mixed on neighbor help and loneliness; thin on townland-level ties.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Irish for *meitheal* and visiting; Old Irish for *comar*; Cantonese for 街坊 associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "community" often stands for a side; murals and the 2009 attacks are contested or painful, so each source is an account. Session minutes name people; check access rules.
- **Held for review:** for community the risk lay in newcomer and faith-group directories; none of the groups chosen fell under a standing rule.
