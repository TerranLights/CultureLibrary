# Northern Ireland: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English (pulpit, platform and press); Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish, with Old Irish and Latin for the early monastic reform; Lithuanian, Polish, Cantonese and Arabic for newcomers' memories of reform at home. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Covenanters (Reformed Presbyterians) and Non-Subscribing Presbyterians:** compare what each tradition holds needs reforming, in church or state, from their own magazines.
- **Catholic parish social action in west Belfast and Derry / Londonderry:** both city names in use; check credit unions, housing associations and parish committees as reform from below.
- **Civil rights marchers and unionist critics of 1968-69:** read each account of what reform was asked for and what it would cost.
- **Republican and loyalist ex-prisoners:** compare how each network describes personal change and reintegration.
- **The Sperrins and the Lough Neagh shore:** compare two environmental campaigns, one over a mine and one over a lough's water.
- **Women's groups of the Shankill and Falls:** check cross-community women's centers for reform agendas set by women.
- **Lithuanian residents:** check whether *Sąjūdis* memory appears in their associations' commemorations.
- **Hong Kong Chinese arrivals since 2021 and Syrian families since 2015:** check their own groups with care; talk of reform or protest at home can put relatives at risk, so sources may be few.
- **Mincéirí:** check Traveller-led groups' campaigns on site provision as reform asked for by those affected.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1960s civil rights, temperance and revivals; mixed for land reform in the six counties; thin for the Céli Dé's northern houses.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Irish for monastic rules; Lithuanian for *Sąjūdis* memory; Arabic and Cantonese where newcomers' own groups write.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** civil rights, policing reform and ex-prisoners are contested; each account stays its own. Reform of the law and of policing powers goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** reform movements abroad that newcomers carry with them were the likeliest to raise a standing rule here; none of the movements or groups named needed to be set aside.
