# Northern Ireland: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots (rural verse of Tyrone, Antrim and Down); Ulster Irish, with Early Modern Irish for eighteenth-century poetry and Old Irish for the voyage tales; Polish, Lithuanian, Arabic and Cantonese for newcomers' writing. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South Armagh and the Creggan district:** check how the *aisling* tradition is kept in local festivals and schools.
- **Moravian Gracehill and Quaker Bessbrook:** compare a church-built and an employer-built ideal, from their own records.
- **The Glens of Antrim and the Ards:** check which local writers hold up a lost rural ideal, and in which language.
- **Republican and loyalist former activists:** compare how each describes youthful ideals and later revision, using their own publications.
- **Evangelical mission families of Down and Antrim:** check mission magazines for how they framed a calling.
- **"Others" in the Alliance and Green tradition:** check party literature for ideals that refuse both blocs.
- **Polish residents:** check whether memory of *Solidarność* appears in their parish and community events.
- **Lithuanian residents:** check commemorations of 1990 in their associations.
- **Syrian families resettled since 2015:** check, with care, their own groups' writing about the hopes of 2011; few may wish to speak.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** check how outsiders' romantic pictures of the road compare with Traveller-written accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1790s, missions and the post-1998 debates; mixed for survey measures; thin for newer residents' own ideals.
- **Languages needed:** Early Modern Irish for the *aisling* poets; Old Irish for the voyage tale; Arabic and Polish for community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** republican and loyalist ideals, "no surrender" and the 1998 settlement are contested; present each as an account. Missionary records describe other peoples, so read them critically.
- **Held for review:** the mission magazines were the item most open to question, because they describe other peoples from outside; they stay as pointers to be read critically rather than being set aside, so nothing is held.
