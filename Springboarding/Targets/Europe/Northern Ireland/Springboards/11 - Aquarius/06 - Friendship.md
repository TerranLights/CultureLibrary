# Northern Ireland: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belfast and rural Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish, with Latin and Old Irish for the early texts; Polish, Portuguese, Tetum, Tagalog, Cantonese and Arabic for friendship words among newer residents. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men in rural Fermanagh and the Sperrins against men in Belfast:** check shed and club records for where friendships form after school ends.
- **Pupils of integrated, Catholic-maintained and controlled schools:** compare survey answers on friends across the line by school type.
- **Derry / Londonderry's young people:** both names in use; check youth-work reports for friend groups crossing the river.
- **GAA clubs and rugby clubs:** compare the social side of each as a place adults keep friends.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** check Traveller-written sources on whether close friends come mainly from kin or from outside it.
- **Filipino residents:** check *barkada* groups and church fellowships in their own community media.
- **Portuguese and East Timorese families in Dungannon:** check whether co-parenthood at baptism still binds friends there; sources likely few.
- **Polish residents:** check how *przyjaciel* and *kolega* are used in their community press.
- **Hong Kong Chinese, earlier and recent arrivals:** check Cantonese-language church and association events for where friends are made, wave by wave.
- **Syrian and other Arabic-speaking newcomers:** check Belfast Friendship Club and mosque reports for how new friends were made.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for friendships across the religious line; mixed for men's friendship and loneliness; thin for friendship words in Ulster Scots and among newer groups.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the *Confessio*; Tagalog and Tetum for community friendship words; Polish for the press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendships across the line during the Troubles could be dangerous, and testimony may be guarded; the Masonic order and school networks draw claims of favoritism, so treat each as an account.
- **Held for review:** friendship sources among newer residents were weighed most closely, since club and church records name members; no named item met a standing rule.
