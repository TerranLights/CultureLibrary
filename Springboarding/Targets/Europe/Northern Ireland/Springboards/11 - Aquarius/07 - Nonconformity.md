# Northern Ireland: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English (including seventeenth-century church English); Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Cantonese and Arabic for newer residents' words for the odd one out. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far that departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Down's Bangor and Holywood, with the Ards:** set the Presbyterian dissent of the 1600s beside today's "no religion" returns, parish by parish.
- **Lisburn and Lurgan Quakers; Covenanter congregations in Antrim:** compare how each small body records refusal and its cost.
- **Free Presbyterian congregations and LGBT groups:** read each one's own statements on Pride; do not merge them into one story.
- **Punks of the late 1970s in Belfast and Derry / Londonderry:** both city names in use; check fanzines for how each city's scene saw itself.
- **Protestant Irish learners in east Belfast and Catholic Ulster Scots enthusiasts:** check press and project reports for how each was received.
- **Mid-Ulster villages and rural Fermanagh:** check LGBT oral histories for whether difference meant leaving home.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** check Traveller-written accounts of pressure to settle and to conform at school.
- **Polish and Lithuanian young people:** check youth-work reports for how far they keep or drop home-country norms.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check Cantonese church and community writing on children choosing paths outside family expectation.
- **Muslim communities in Belfast and Craigavon:** check how their own organizations discuss members who leave or change practice; sources may be guarded.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church dissent and LGBT history; mixed for subcultures; thin for eccentricity as such, which lives in local anecdote.
- **Languages needed:** the older Scots-flavored English of Blair's memoir and the Secession minutes; Ulster dialect dictionaries for words that mark the odd one out; Cantonese and Arabic where faith communities discuss those who leave.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Pride, the counter-protests, leaving a faith and crossing language lines are contested or personal; names in oral histories may be protected. Laws once applied to gay men belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for nonconformity the delicate items were testimony from people who left a faith; they are personal, not rule-bound, so none had to be set aside.
