# Northern Ireland: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English (everyday reticence and its idioms); Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish, with Old and Middle Irish for the monastic and Sweeney texts; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Cantonese and Arabic for newcomers' words for coldness and calm. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity), others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North and west Belfast against the north Down suburbs:** check survey and oral-history material for how each speaks about keeping out of trouble.
- **Farmers living alone in the Sperrins, Fermanagh and south Armagh:** check rural isolation studies and farm-support charities' reports.
- **Derry / Londonderry after Bloody Sunday:** both names in use; check survivors' testimony for composure and its cost, each account as its own.
- **Doctors, nurses and ambulance crews of the Troubles:** compare clinical writing with later oral histories.
- **Contemplatives in south Down and Armagh:** compare monastic writing on silence with Presbyterian and Methodist teaching on calm in trial.
- **Men in their middle years:** check health-service and helpline reports on whether, when and how they seek help.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** check Traveller-written sources on keeping distance from settled institutions.
- **Polish and Lithuanian workers in Craigavon and Dungannon:** check community reports on isolation and contact with neighbors.
- **Hong Kong Chinese elders:** check Cantonese-language services for isolation among older residents.
- **Syrian and other Arabic-speaking families:** check resettlement evaluations for withdrawal or engagement in new towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for conflict-related coping and mental health; mixed for reserve as an everyday manner; thin for contemplative practice outside church records.
- **Languages needed:** early Irish to read white martyrdom and the *geilt* in their own terms; Cantonese where isolation among older Hong Kong residents is written up; Polish and Lithuanian for reports on workers living apart from family.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trauma testimony and mental-health data are personal; survivors' accounts and inquiry evidence are contested and some are protected. Neutrality required of officials belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** health surveys and helpline reports were weighed for detachment, as they touch distress; they need care, not exclusion, and nothing named was set aside.
