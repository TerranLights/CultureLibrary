# Northern Ireland: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English (church, campaign and everyday usage); Ulster Scots; Ulster Irish, with older Irish for prophecy texts; Cantonese, Arabic, Tetum, Portuguese, Polish and Lithuanian for the hopes newer residents carried with them. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Derry / Londonderry:** both names in use; check how the "hope and history" line was received on each side of the Foyle after 1995.
- **Peace-wall streets in north and west Belfast:** compare residents' hopes and fears about removal by district, from survey breakdowns.
- **Families of the Disappeared:** check their own statements and the commission's reports, noting where each family lived and searched.
- **Emigrant districts of Fermanagh and Tyrone:** check letters and local histories for what leavers and stayers hoped for.
- **Catholic pilgrims at Struell and Presbyterian revival converts:** compare how each tradition frames hope of healing and salvation.
- **Young people deciding whether to stay or leave:** check youth surveys for hopes about work and a future at home.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** check Traveller-led groups for what families say they hope for their children's schooling and health.
- **Hong Kong arrivals since 2021:** check community groups for hopes in Cantonese and English, kept apart from older Chinese residents' accounts.
- **Syrian families resettled since 2015:** check evaluations and their own groups for hopes of return or of staying.
- **East Timorese and Portuguese households in Dungannon:** check church and community notices for how hope and waiting are spoken of.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for expectations about community relations and the peace process; mixed for religious hope and emigration; thin for newer arrivals' stated hopes.
- **Languages needed:** older Irish and Latin behind the prophecy collection; Cantonese for Hong Kong groups; Arabic for resettled families; Tetum for Dungannon parish material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1998 referendum, the Disappeared and suicide are painful or contested; each account stays its own and bereaved families' privacy comes first. Promises written into the Agreement as law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for hope, the near-miss was a commercial prediction almanac, left out because it mixes forecasts with material the method does not use; nothing restricted was named.
