# Northern Ireland: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English; Ulster Scots; Irish, including the Old Irish of early homilies; Polish, Lithuanian, Filipino (Tagalog), Cantonese and Arabic for families who live apart or fast. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shankill and east Belfast:** check Somme commemorations and lodge banners for which dead are named.
- **West Belfast and Derry / Londonderry (both names in use):** read hunger-strike murals, gardens of remembrance and annual marches as one community's account, beside other accounts of 1981.
- **Fermanagh lakelands:** compare how Enniskillen's Remembrance Day dead of 1987 are honored by each tradition.
- **South Down and the Mournes coast:** check lifeboat and mountain-rescue volunteers' own histories.
- **Lurgan, Portadown and the mill towns:** compare mill families' memoirs on wages handed over.
- **Armagh and its archdiocese:** check Plunkett devotion beside Church of Ireland Armagh's own martyr calendar.
- **Catholic families of ex-servicemen:** look for family papers that were kept private.
- **Presbyterian, Free Presbyterian and Reformed Presbyterian congregations:** compare fast days and self-denial teaching.
- **Filipino and Polish households:** separate remittance and family-separation accounts by community.
- **Muslim communities:** check mosque and center Ramadan guidance as their own record of fasting.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war dead, rescue and Famine-era emigration; mixed for carers; thin for newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for homilies; Tagalog and Polish for families abroad.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the hunger strikes, the Somme, Easter commemorations and the dead of the Troubles are contested; "martyr" is a disputed word, so record whose usage each source carries.
- **Held for review:** the hunger-strike and paramilitary-memorial material could tip into glorifying or condemning; each pointer is kept as an account, and no source had to be withdrawn.
