# Northern Ireland: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English of the farms and the city estates; Ulster Scots field and fence words; Irish place-name and territory terms; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Cantonese and Arabic for privacy and shared housing. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ardoyne and Glenbryn, north Belfast; Short Strand and east Belfast:** compare residents' groups on each side of the same wall.
- **The Fountain estate, Derry / Londonderry (both names in use):** check how a small Protestant district on the Cityside records its own edge.
- **South Armagh and the Fermanagh border:** compare farmers whose land the border crosses with town traders in Newry and Enniskillen.
- **Strabane and the Foyle crossings:** check daily crossers' accounts beside customs history.
- **Ulster Scots districts of the Ards and mid-Antrim:** check Gregg's lines against speakers' own sense of where "our talk" ends.
- **The Gaeltacht Quarter, west Belfast:** check how Irish-language signage marks a district.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** start from An Munia Tober and other Traveller-led groups.
- **Polish and Lithuanian tenants in shared houses:** separate their housing complaints by community.
- **Muslim women's groups:** ask the groups themselves about modesty and space before any outside source.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check Cantonese-language media on privacy and family visits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the border, interfaces and territory marking; mixed for family privacy and touch; thin for newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** Irish for place-names and territory; Ulster Scots for field words; Arabic for privacy terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peace walls, flags, kerbstones and the border are contested; abuse reviews concern survivors; the post-Brexit arrangements appear only as a social event (their text is Libra's).
- **Held for review:** clergy-abuse material was weighed hardest; the reviews named are public, and survivors' own groups are left for the researcher to approach on their terms.
