# Northern Ireland: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English as used in prayer meetings and at holy wells; Ulster Scots of the metrical psalms; Irish devotional terms, with Old Irish for early prayer; Polish, Portuguese, Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic for the devotions of newer congregations. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; this sheet takes experience, practice and the sacred outside the church (institutions belong to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Fermanagh lakelands and Lough Erne islands:** compare holy-well rounds and island devotions with parish practice.
- **Downpatrick, Saul and Strangford Lough:** check Patrick sites as Catholic, Church of Ireland and ecumenical places of prayer.
- **Mid-Antrim and the Ards:** check evangelical missions and gospel halls.
- **Derry / Londonderry (both names in use):** compare Colmcille devotion with Church of Ireland St Columb's Cathedral practice.
- **People of no religion and humanists:** look at Census 2021 tables and humanist groups' own accounts.
- **Brethren and Elim assemblies:** check their published testimonies separately.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** separate the two communities' chaplaincies.
- **East Timorese Catholics in Dungannon:** printed material is probably scant; begin with the parish's notices and any Tetum-language prayer group.
- **Chinese Christian congregations in Belfast:** check for Cantonese-language services.
- **Muslim communities:** compare the Belfast Islamic Centre with smaller prayer rooms in Craigavon, Derry and Newry.
- **Modern pagans and Druids:** check their own websites for gatherings at Navan Fort (Emain Macha) and stone circles.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for evangelical and Catholic practice; mixed for holy wells and seekers; thin for small faiths.
- **Languages needed:** the *Lorica* and its glosses are in Old Irish; newsletters of the Polish chaplaincy, Chinese churches and the Islamic Centre are partly in their own languages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** faith in Northern Ireland is tied to community identity; record each source's tradition and do not merge them. Closed or private rites stay private.
- **Held for review:** two pointers on this sheet were set aside and stand as bare categories: Buddhist practice (left open for the author), and South Asian-heritage communities in Northern Ireland's lands.
