# Northern Ireland: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ulster English (Belfast street speech and farm talk); Ulster Scots of Antrim, Down and the fishing ports; Irish, with Old Irish glossary terms for poetic insight; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Cantonese and Arabic for hunch and premonition words. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North and west Belfast; the Shankill:** check how residents' own accounts describe "telling" strangers, and how youth projects try to unlearn it.
- **Rathlin, the Glens and the Causeway Coast:** look for second-sight lore with Scottish links.
- **The Ards Peninsula and the Mourne coast:** compare Portavogie and Kilkeel weather lore.
- **The Sperrins, Tyrone and Fermanagh:** check water divining and death tokens in farm communities.
- **South Armagh and the Lough Neagh shore:** check omen lore beside parish records.
- **Surgeons, nurses and ambulance crews of the Troubles years:** start with their own memoirs and journal papers.
- **Presbyterian and Catholic clergy:** compare how each tradition writes about discerning a vocation.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** ask Traveller-led groups whether judging a horse by eye is a skill they want written about, and in whose words.
- **Polish and Lithuanian residents:** check community media for dream-books and premonition talk, one language at a time.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check Cantonese-language community groups for the words members themselves use for hunches and luck.
- **Muslim communities:** check whether *firāsa* or dream-reading appears in sermons or study circles at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: folklore for omens and divining, clinical and farm records for expert judgment; thin for second sight and newer communities.
- **Languages needed:** Old Irish for the glossary; Ulster Scots for coastal speech; Cantonese and Polish for community material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "telling" is sectarian profiling, so the topic needs care; Troubles casualty records concern living patients.
- **Held for review:** fortune-selling and dream-reading pointers were checked against the rule that such material is a record of belief, never a source of fact; all are framed that way, and none needed holding.
