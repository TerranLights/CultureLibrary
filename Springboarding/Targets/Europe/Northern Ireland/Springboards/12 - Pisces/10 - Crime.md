# Northern Ireland: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Belfast and border English, with the slang of informers, joyriders and "hoods"; Ulster Scots; Irish, including early modern words for outlaws; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Cantonese and Arabic for newer residents' reporting and victim accounts. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it (law itself is researched in Libra and Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Belfast and Derry's Creggan:** check residents' own accounts of joyriding and of punishment attacks in the 1980s and 1990s.
- **Shankill and east Belfast:** compare loyalist districts' accounts of drug dealing, exiling and restorative schemes.
- **South Armagh and the Fermanagh border:** look at how smuggling and fuel laundering are spoken of locally, beside official reports.
- **Rural Tyrone and the Sperrins:** check rural crime groups and farm-theft reporting.
- **Prisoners and ex-prisoners, republican and loyalist:** check their own groups on the criminal label.
- **Families of informers and of those killed as alleged informers:** sources are sensitive and scarce; begin with families' published statements.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** start from Traveller-led groups on being suspected and refused service.
- **Roma and Romanian residents:** check hate-crime reporting and support groups' accounts.
- **Polish and Lithuanian residents:** compare victim-support and community police contact, community by community.
- **Hong Kong Chinese and Muslim communities:** check each community's own reports on racist attacks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** paramilitary violence and official counts are heavily documented; the everyday sense of which wrongs matter, and newer residents' own views of crime, will need oral and community sources.
- **Languages needed:** Irish for outlaw terms; Polish, Romanian and Arabic for victim material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether paramilitary acts are "crime" is the central contested question; informers, punishment attacks and victims' status touch living people; law and courts go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** informer memoirs could expose living people; McGartland's is already public and is paired with the communities' answers, while unpublished informer material is left untouched.
