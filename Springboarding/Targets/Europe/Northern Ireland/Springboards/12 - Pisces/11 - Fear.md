# Northern Ireland: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Northern Ireland, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of Belfast, Derry and the counties, with its words for alerts, dread and nerves; Ulster Scots fright words; Irish names for the dreaded and the uncanny; Polish, Lithuanian, Portuguese, Tetum, Cantonese and Arabic for newcomers' worry and safety talk. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety are its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **North Belfast's interfaces (Ardoyne, the New Lodge, Tigers Bay):** check residents' own accounts of fear of attack, district by district.
- **Rural Fermanagh and the border:** compare border Protestant families' accounts of fear with nationalist accounts of the same years.
- **Derry / Londonderry (both names in use):** check Waterside and Cityside memories of moving house out of fear.
- **Islandmagee, Carrickfergus and east Antrim:** look at how the witch trial and Riddell's stories feed local ghost tours.
- **Free Presbyterian, Brethren and other evangelical assemblies:** compare how each speaks of the fear of God in its own preaching.
- **Members of the security forces and their families:** check their associations' publications on living with threat.
- **Roma and Romanian families:** check community advocates' reports from the 2009 attacks onward.
- **Polish and Lithuanian residents:** compare each community's own reports on hate incidents.
- **Hong Kong Chinese families:** check Cantonese-language groups for racist-harassment reports.
- **Muslim communities, Syrian families among them:** look at mosque and support-group reports on fear after attacks elsewhere.
- **Mincéirí (Irish Travellers):** what Traveller women's and youth groups publish on feeling unsafe at sites and in towns is the place to begin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** heavy on Troubles-era fear and trauma; folklore of dread is scattered; newcomers' fear is reported mainly by support groups.
- **Languages needed:** Ulster Scots and Irish for fright words; Polish, Romanian, Cantonese and Arabic for community reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** trauma research concerns living people; Troubles fear is contested ground, so record each community's account separately.
- **Held for review:** trauma studies that name individual participants would be off limits; the two named here report aggregates, so they stand.
