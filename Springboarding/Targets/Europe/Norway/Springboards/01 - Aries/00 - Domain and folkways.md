# Norway: Self and identity, the domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Norwegian (Bokmål, Nynorsk and the dialects); North, Lule, Pite and South Sámi; Kven; Romani (*tatere*) and Romanes (Rom); Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Arabic, Tigrinya, Kurdish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the self: what a person is taken to be, when personhood starts and ends, the body and how it is shown, and the naming ways that set one person apart from another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo and Østlandet, Vestlandet, Trøndelag, Northern Norway:** check first-name rarity and the spoken "I" by county, two cheap markers of setting oneself apart.
- **Bunad wearers of Hardanger, Setesdal, Telemark and Gudbrandsdalen:** check which local rules on wearing a district's dress are written down, and who sets them.
- **Coastal, reindeer-herding and South Sámi families:** check how *gákti* details and Sámi names are read across language areas; Sámi institutions decide what they share.
- **Kvens, Norwegian Finns and the Forest Finns of Finnskogen:** check name changes under Norwegianization, a record of a person made to fit an official form.
- **Romani people (*tatere*) and the Rom:** check naming and dress through their own organizations, not through old welfare files alone.
- **The Jewish community of Oslo and Trondheim:** check Hebrew and civil names side by side.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi, Kurdish, Syrian and Vietnamese Norwegians:** check name order, spelling and surname adoption at registration.
- **Laestadians and *bedehus* congregations:** check christening and confirmation against civil naming parties.
- **Longyearbyen on Svalbard:** check how a mostly transient population records home place and belonging.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names, bunad and literature; thinner for how a self is spoken of in Kven, Pite Sámi and Romani. The tracker records no change to Norway's pre-war lands and its exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for Norway as it stands, with Svalbard.
- **Languages needed:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Old Norse for runic and medieval texts; North and South Sámi for dress; Kven and Finnish for the north and Finnskogen; Somali and Tigrinya for newer naming systems.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi *luohti* and dress details belong to their bearers; race-science measurements taken from Sámi people are a painful record (check repatriation history before using any of it).
- **Held for review:** foreign "national character" travel books were weighed for this overview and left out as outsiders' generalizations; no South Asian pointer arose here.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull SSB's first-name tables by county for 1950, 1990 and the latest year, and mark which counties have the most rare names.
2. Open Digitalarkivet's 1801 census for one Vestlandet and one Finnmark parish; note how persons and farms are entered.
3. Search NEG's questionnaire list for *navnegiving*, *dåp* and *konfirmasjon*.
