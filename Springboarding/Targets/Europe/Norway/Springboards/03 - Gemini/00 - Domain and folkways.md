# Norway: Communication and learning, the domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Norwegian (Bokmål, Nynorsk and the spoken dialects); North, Lule, Pite and South Sámi; Kven; Norwegian Romani (*tatere*) and Romanes (Rom); Norwegian Sign Language; Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Arabic, Tigrinya, Kurdish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** the domain of communication and learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home (speech ways, languages, neighbors, short journeys, early learning and media habits).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo's east and west ends, with its multiethnolectal youth speech:** check address, slang and code-switching as speakers describe them.
- **Vestlandet (Bergen, Sogn, Hardanger) against Østlandet:** check where Nynorsk is the school language and what that does to reading habits.
- **Trøndelag and Northern Norway (Lofoten, Vesterålen, Tromsø):** check dialect recordings and local radio for regional speech ways.
- **Telemark and Setesdal:** check the *stev* and ballad as a learning route kept in families.
- **North Sámi of Kautokeino and Karasjok, coastal Sámi, Lule and South Sámi:** check each language area's own school and media history, since revival work differs.
- **Kven speakers and Norwegian Finns in Troms and Finnmark; Forest Finns of Finnskogen:** check home use and recorded speech (scarce; start with Kven associations and folklore archives).
- **The *tatere* and the Rom; the Deaf community:** check each language through the group's own organizations.
- **Newer communities (Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi, Kurdish, Syrian, Vietnamese):** check the Saturday and mother-tongue schools each runs.
- **Longyearbyen and Barentsburg:** check which languages a Svalbard settlement uses at work and school.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialects, media use and schooling; mixed for neighborliness (surveys and local histories); thin wherever the speech of Kven, Forest Finn and Romani homes is the subject.
- **Languages needed:** Nynorsk and dialect transcriptions for the corpora; Danish-Norwegian for the 18th-century catechism material; North, Lule and South Sámi; Norwegian Sign Language for Deaf-community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi, Kven and Romani schooling histories touch Norwegianization (*fornorskning*), which is contested: present every account as one account, in each people's own name; Romani words collected by police or missions need the community's consent before use.
- **Held for review:** older dialect-joke books were weighed as a speech-ways source and dropped as caricature, not record; nothing in this overview had to be set aside under the South Asian rule.
