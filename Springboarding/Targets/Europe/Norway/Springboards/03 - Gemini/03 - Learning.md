# Norway: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Danish-Norwegian and Middle Low German for older Bergen records; North, Lule and South Sámi; Kven; the languages of adult learners of Norwegian (Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Arabic, Tigrinya, Kurdish, Vietnamese). **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution goes to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coastal families of Lofoten, Vesterålen and Finnmark against inland farm families of Gudbrandsdalen and Valdres:** check what children are given to do at sea or on the farm, and at what age.
- **Vestlandet's boatbuilding and Hardanger's crafts districts:** check master-learner chains in local craft records.
- **Trøndelag:** check apprenticeship take-up in industry and farming against Oslo's school-heavy routes.
- **Reindeer-herding Sámi families:** check how herding is learned on the land, as Sámi researchers describe it.
- **Coastal and South Sámi:** check language-nest and kindergarten access in each area.
- **Kven and Forest Finn descendants:** check adult relearning of a grandparent's language.
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check each group's account of schooling and of learning trades at home, apart from official reports.
- **Adult immigrants (Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Eritrean, Syrian, Vietnamese):** check second-language learning routes by group, and children's role in teaching parents.
- **Laestadian and *bedehus* families:** check Sunday school and home Bible reading as learning.
- **Svalbard:** check how children in Longyearbyen learn about polar-bear safety and the land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kindergartens, apprenticeships and adult Norwegian tests; mixed for learning by pitching in (ethnography more than figures); thin for Kven early learning and for Romani home learning.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Low German and Danish-Norwegian for Hanseatic and older school records; Sámi for language-nest reports; learners' first languages for their own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi and Kven learning histories carry the boarding-school years (Norwegianization), contested ground; let each people's own account lead. Studies with children need consent care.
- **Held for review:** tutoring-company marketing was considered for the self-teaching facet and dropped as advertising; no learning pointer called for the South Asian rule.
