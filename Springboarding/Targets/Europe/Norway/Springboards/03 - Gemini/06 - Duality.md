*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk (both, since the pair is itself a subject); Old Norse for sagas and legend terms; North and Lule Sámi; Kven; Romani (*tatere*); Somali, Arabic, Kurdish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nynorsk districts of Vestlandet and inland valleys against Bokmål-majority Østlandet and the north:** check how a pupil's second standard is felt, by district.
- **Coastal Sámi of Finnmark and Troms, Marka Sámi (*markasamer*) of Ofoten, and families who hid a Sámi or Kven past:** check accounts of reclaiming an identity, in each group's words.
- **Reindeer-herding Sámi in Kautokeino and Karasjok:** check how Sámi and Norwegian schooling and work lives sit together.
- **Lule and South Sámi:** check bilingual life where the Sámi language is small.
- **Kvens and Norwegian Finns:** check use of each name across generations.
- **Forest Finns:** check how Finnskogen families describe a Finnish past under Norwegian names (scarce; local museums).
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check living between a group's own language and Norwegian, through their own organizations.
- **War children and their families:** check their accounts, published and oral.
- **Children of Somali, Iraqi, Kurdish, Syrian and Vietnamese parents:** check how they describe holding two belongings, from their own writing and studies.
- **Svalbard:** check the Norwegian and Russian-speaking settlements side by side.
