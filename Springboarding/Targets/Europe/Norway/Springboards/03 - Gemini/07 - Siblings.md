# Norway: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk with dialect kin words; Old Norse; North and South Sámi; Kven; Vietnamese, Arabic, Polish, Somali and Tigrinya where apt. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm families of Gudbrandsdalen, Østerdalen and Trøndelag:** check how the farm passes to one sibling and what the others receive, from local histories.
- **Fishing families of Lofoten and the Finnmark coast:** check brothers sharing boats and quotas, from fishery registers and memoirs.
- **Vestlandet's *bedehus* congregations and the Laestadians of the north:** check the size of families and the use of "brother" and "sister" for fellow believers.
- **Reindeer-herding Sámi families:** check how siblings share herding work and earmarks, as Sámi sources describe it.
- **Coastal and South Sámi:** check sibling and cousin terms in each language.
- **Kvens:** check kin terms and family size in Kven farming communities (scarce; Kven associations).
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check sibling ties in each group's own family histories.
- **Vietnamese, Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi and Syrian families:** check older siblings' load of interpreting and care, from studies that ask them.
- **Polish and Lithuanian families:** check siblings who migrated in turn, one after another.
- **Families in Oslo with only children against larger rural families:** check survey data on contact between adult siblings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in register-based studies of birth order and family size; mixed for adult sibling relations; thin for Kven and Romani sibling customs.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for the king's histories; North and South Sámi for kin terms; Vietnamese, Somali and Arabic for families' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *Arv og miljø* debate touched abuse claims inside a real family; treat it as a public debate, not as fact. Child-welfare files and violence studies involve minors.
- **Held for review:** popular birth-order personality books were considered and set aside as unsupported; no sibling pointer needed the South Asian rule.
