# Norway: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Latin and Old Norse for medieval texts; North Sámi; Kven; Somali, Arabic, Kurdish, Polish and Tigrinya where apt. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision, and more broadly the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural districts of Hedmark and Oppland against Oslo:** check whether neighbors still go to the *forliksråd*, a lay person, or straight to a lawyer.
- **Fishing communities of Lofoten and Vesterålen:** check how disputes over berths, gear and fishing grounds are settled, from local histories and fishers' associations.
- **Industrial towns (Rjukan, Odda, Mo i Rana):** check how local labor disputes were talked down or escalated.
- **Reindeer-herding Sámi families and their farming neighbors:** check who brokers grazing disputes and in which language.
- **Kvens:** check whether the church, kin or local officials settled quarrels (scarce; Kven local histories).
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check whether, and how, disputes are settled inside each community, only from its own accounts.
- **The Jewish and Catholic communities:** check religious go-betweens in family matters.
- **Polish and Lithuanian workers:** check who stands between them and employers: unions, agencies or compatriots.
- **Longyearbyen:** check how a small town with residents from many countries handles quarrels without kin to step in.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor mediation and family-counseling figures; mixed for informal go-betweens; thin for Sámi, Kven and Romani practices, which are mostly oral.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the *Passio Olavi*; North Sámi for herding disputes; Somali, Arabic and Kurdish for faith communities' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family mediation touches domestic violence; Norway's peace-facilitation record is contested abroad; minority communities' internal ways of settling disputes are easily stereotyped, so rely on their own accounts.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Norway's lands (one pointer set aside under the rule; category only).
