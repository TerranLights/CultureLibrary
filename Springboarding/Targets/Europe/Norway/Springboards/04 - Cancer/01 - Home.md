# Norway: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; North and Lule Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Tigrinya, Polish and Somali where apt. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |

| Bokmål / Nynorsk | *hjembygd* / *heimbygd*, *heimstad* | home district; home place | *heimstad* runs through bygdebok titles and Nynorsk verse |
| Bokmål / Nynorsk | *hjemlengsel* / *heimlengt* | homesickness | emigrant letters and soldiers' letters |
| Bokmål | *eie eller leie*, *leieboer* | to own or to rent; lodger, renter | the phrase heads decades of press debate on whether renting is a real home |
| Bokmål | *bostedsløs*, *husløs* | homeless | *bostedsløs* is the term in the national counts |
| Bokmål | *gjenreisningshus* | reconstruction house | Finnmark and Nord-Troms after the 1944-1945 burning |
| North Sámi | *ruovttus*, *ruovttuguovlu* | at home; home area | *check* forms; search with the spring and autumn sites of Kautokeino herders |

| Polish / Somali | *dom*; *guri*, *dal* | home; house, homeland | Polish parishes in Oslo and Stavanger; Somali associations in Oslo and Drammen |

English keywords: Norwegian home ownership, coziness at home, housing cooperatives, homelessness count, reconstruction housing, homesickness, remote farms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo's west end against Grønland and Groruddalen:** check tenure and crowding by city district.
- **Bergen estates studied by Gullestad against Sørlandet villa towns (Kristiansand, Arendal):** check how the decorated home is shown to guests.
- **Finnmark's rebuilt towns against Østlandet's older settled farms:** check how a burned and rebuilt home is remembered.
- **Lofoten and Vesterålen *rorbu* and fishing homes:** check the line between work cabin and home across the season.
- **Kautokeino and Karasjok herding families, coastal Sámi of the fjords, Lule Sámi of Hamarøy:** check where each says "home" is across the year.
- **Kvens of Nordreisa and Vadsø:** check which side of the border *kotimaa* names in older and younger speakers.
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check how each group's organization speaks of settled housing offered by the state.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Syrian, Polish and Lithuanian households:** check sharing, crowding and the pull of a homeland in SSB's immigrant tables.
- **Longyearbyen residents:** check what home means where the stay is temporary by rule.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tenure, housing standards and homelessness; mixed for home as a feeling (magazines, ethnography); thin for Kven and Romani speech about home.
- **Languages needed:** Nynorsk for bygdebøker and Aasen; Dano-Norwegian for older emigrant letters; North and Lule Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Tigrinya and Somali.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi relocation, the burning of Finnmark, the confiscation of Jewish homes in 1942 and the settling of the *tatere* by the mission are contested: name each community by its own name and treat each telling as an account. Tenancy law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** estate-agent listings were weighed as a source for the words of home and kept for a later, critical pass; no pointer on home was held under the South Asian rule.
