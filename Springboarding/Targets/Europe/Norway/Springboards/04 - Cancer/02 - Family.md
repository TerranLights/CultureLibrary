# Norway: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk with dialect kin words; Old Norse; North and South Sámi; Kven; Somali, Arabic, Tigrinya, Vietnamese and Polish where apt. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm families of Hallingdal and Gudbrandsdalen against town families of Oslo and Stavanger:** check how far kin are counted where a farm name ties them.
- **Fishing families of Lofoten and the Helgeland coast:** check how crew and kin overlap in local histories.
- **North Sámi herding families of Kautokeino and Karasjok; South Sámi families of Snåsa and Røros:** check how each traces kin across districts and the border.
- **Kvens and Norwegian Finns of Vadsø and Porsanger; Forest Finns of Grue and Åsnes:** check what surname changes did to the paper trail of kin.
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check whether family records exist outside the mission's and the state's files, through the groups' own organizations.
- **Laestadian families of Nord-Troms and Alta:** check family size and kin gatherings through the movement's own publications.
- **Jewish families of Oslo and Trondheim:** check family lines broken in 1942 and rebuilt after.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi, Kurdish, Polish and Vietnamese families; adoptive families:** check how duties to kin abroad are spoken of.
- **Barentsburg and Longyearbyen:** check how two Svalbard settlements house families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy, family statistics and kin contact; mixed for ritual kin; thin for Kven, Forest Finn and Romani family lines.
- **Languages needed:** Nynorsk and dialect for bygdebøker; Old Norse for the laws and runes; Danish-Norwegian for church books; North and South Sámi; Kven; Somali, Arabic and Tigrinya.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the removal of *tatere* children, Sámi and Kven boarding-school separations, the 1942 deportation, and child-welfare (*barnevern*) disputes with immigrant families are contested: present each as an account and name each community by its own name. Reindeer marks and herding rights may be private family matters; ask before use.
- **Held for review:** commercial DNA-match services were weighed as a kinship source and left aside, since their data are private and their categories are not Norway's own; no family pointer was held under the South Asian rule.
