## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coastal families of Lofoten, Helgeland and Sunnmøre:** check how protection at sea is spoken of in lighthouse and rescue histories.
- **Mountain farms of Telemark, Setesdal and Valdres:** check the *nisse* and protective charms in each valley's folklore collection.
- **Reindeer-herding families of Finnmark and South Sámi herders of Trøndelag:** check guarding of herds and pasture through their own associations and the press.
- **Kvens and Norwegian Finns:** check protective customs at sea and at home; sources are few, so start with Kvensk institutt and Varanger museum.
- **The *tatere*:** check how the people describe the mission's "protection", using their own accounts first.
- **Jewish and Muslim congregations of Oslo and Bærum:** check how each speaks of being guarded at worship.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Somali and Eritrean families:** check what they say about child protection in their own media.
- **Longyearbyen and the field stations:** check the gun and flare rules as everyday safety.
- **Oslo against rural districts:** check children's freedom to roam versus being kept close.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for safety institutions and violence surveys; mixed for folk protection; thin for Kven and Forest Finn protective customs.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the *Passio*; Danish-Norwegian for Bang's charms; North Sámi; Kven; Polish and Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *tatere* history, child-welfare disputes and the attacks on places of worship are contested and painful; give each account in its tellers' own words. Some charm texts may be held as private by families; ask before use. Law on child protection and weapons goes to Libra or Capricorn.
