# Norway: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Old Norse; North Sámi; Kven; Polish and Lithuanian where apt. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Finnmark and Nordland coastal villages against Oslo's growth:** check how people who stayed speak of places emptied by policy.
- **Inland valleys with *ødegårder* (Valdres, Hallingdal):** check how deserted farms are remembered in bygdebøker.
- **Sámi families of Kautokeino and Karasjok; coastal Sámi of Porsanger:** check boarding-school memories of being left, in the people's own accounts.
- **Kvens of Vadsø and Porsanger:** check family memory of kin who emigrated; sources are scarce, so start with Kven associations.
- **The *tatere*:** check children taken from families, from the families' side.
- **War children and their mothers:** check what each says about being left by father and by country.
- **Polish and Lithuanian workers in Norway:** check how parents and the children left at home speak of the arrangement.
- **Somali and Eritrean families split across borders:** check family reunification as a long wait.
- **Svalbard:** check Pyramiden and the short stays that leave few lasting bonds.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for depopulation statistics and children's-home inquiries; mixed for folk legend; thin for Kven and Forest Finn abandonment memory.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for the laws; Nynorsk and dialect for legends; North Sámi for Qvigstad's sources; Kven; Polish and Lithuanian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war children, children's-home survivors, the *tatere* and Sámi boarding-school pupils speak of being abandoned by the state, and these are contested accounts: name each group by its own name and treat each telling as one account. Avoid naming any person found dead alone. Child-welfare law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** online "urban exploration" photo sites of abandoned houses were weighed as a source on deserted places and held back, since they often reveal private property; no abandonment pointer fell under the South Asian rule.
