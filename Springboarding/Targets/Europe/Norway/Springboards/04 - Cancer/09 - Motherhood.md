# Norway: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Old Norse and Latin for medieval material; North Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Polish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mothers in Oslo and Bergen against mothers in inland farming districts:** check part-time work and leave use by municipality.
- **Fishing-coast mothers of Lofoten and Finnmark:** check how mothers ran households while men were at sea, through local histories.
- **North Sámi mothers of Kautokeino and Karasjok; South Sámi mothers of Snåsa:** check how each speaks of birth and mothering away from maternity wards.
- **Kven mothers of Vadsø and Børselv:** check language passed on at home; sources are thin.
- **Romani mothers (*tatere*) and Rom mothers:** check what each says of the state's dealings with their children.
- **Laestadian and *bedehus* mothers:** check family size and mothering in their own publications.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi, Syrian, Kurdish, Polish and Vietnamese mothers:** check birth and early mothering in the health service's reports and mothers' groups.
- **Unmarried mothers of the 1950s-1960s against today's single mothers:** check how the two cohorts tell their stories.
- **Longyearbyen mothers:** check birth away from home and returning with a newborn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth registry, leave and work statistics; mixed for mothering ideals; thin for Kven, Forest Finn and Romani mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Norse for medieval material; Danish-Norwegian for the missionaries; North Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Polish and Vietnamese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi religious material recorded by missionaries may hold knowledge Sámi communities wish to handle themselves; ask Sámi institutions first. Unmarried mothers' homes, *tyskerjenter* and the taking of Romani children are contested; give each account in its tellers' words. Abortion belongs with Aries.
- **Held for review:** mothers' blogs were weighed as a voice of current mothering and kept for the research stage, since many are private and short-lived; no motherhood pointer fell under the South Asian rule.
