# Norway: Creativity and self-expression, the domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, with dialect feast and game words; Old Norse; North and South Sámi; Kven; Romani (*tater*); Polish, Somali and Kurdish where apt. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure (art, play, festivity, romance, children as joy, fame, sport and games of chance).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hardanger and Setesdal fiddle districts against Oslo's club scene:** check festival programs for which kinds of display each puts on stage.
- **Trøndelag and Northern Norway against Østlandet:** check local revue (*revy*) scripts for how mock-contests and boasting humor are staged.
- **Lofoten and Vesterålen fishing villages:** check where feasts fell in the winter fishery's calendar.
- **North Sámi of Kautokeino and Karasjok, coastal Sámi, Lule Sámi of Tysfjord and South Sámi of Snåsa:** check each group's own stages for joik, *duodji* and contests.
- **Kven dance evenings in Varanger and Forest Finn feasts in Grue and Åsnes:** check what is still danced and sung, since print says little.
- **Laestadians of Nord-Troms; *bedehus* congregations of Sørlandet:** check how each congregation states its own rules on dance and sport.
- **Romani (*tater*) singers and Rom families:** check who performs for whom at weddings and gatherings, as each group tells it.
- **Polish, Kurdish, Somali, Eritrean, Syrian and Vietnamese communities:** check where Newroz, *Tết* and Eid are held in Oslo, Bergen and Stavanger.
- **Longyearbyen:** check festivity among short-stay residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for arts funding, sport and gaming figures; mixed for courtship history; thin for Forest Finn, Kven and Romani festivity.
- **Languages needed:** Danish-Norwegian for Sundt and Lid; Old Norse for skaldic verse; North and South Sámi for joik; Kven; Polish and Kurdish for festival programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** a joik may belong to a person; Norwegianization (*fornorskning*) and the treatment of the *tatere* bear on which songs survived: present each account in each people's own name. Prohibition law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** gaming-company marketing was weighed for the pastime facet and left out as advertising.
