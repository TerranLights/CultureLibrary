# Norway: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål, Nynorsk and the valley dialects' names for tools and styles; Old Norse; North, Lule and South Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Somali and Polish where apt. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Telemark, Hallingdal and Rogaland rose painters:** check how each valley's makers speak of copying a master against making their own.
- **Hardanger fiddle makers of Vestlandet against Østlandet violin makers:** check guild and course records for who teaches whom.
- **North Sámi *duodji* makers of Karasjok and Kautokeino against Lule Sámi makers in Tysfjord and South Sámi makers in Snåsa:** check which designs each holds to be a family's.
- **Kven makers of Porsanger; Forest Finn builders of Grue and Åsnes:** check what craft survives, since written sources are few.
- **Romani tinsmiths and horse dealers (*tatere*):** check craft as the people describe it, and the later loss of the trades.
- **Oslo's design and music scenes against Trondheim's and Bergen's:** check where grants and studios cluster.
- **Laestadian and *bedehus* homes:** check which crafts these households count as fitting.
- **Polish, Eritrean, Kurdish, Vietnamese and Syrian makers:** check crafts and music carried into Norway, through community associations.
- **Lofoten and Vesterålen fishermen:** check boat and gear making as creative work, through local museums.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** museum catalogs, design history and income surveys give plenty; invention is patchy beyond patents; Kven, Forest Finn and Romani making has left little in print.
- **Languages needed:** Nynorsk and dialect for craft terms; Old Norse; North, Lule and South Sámi; Kven; Arabic and Somali for community arts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some *duodji* patterns and joik belong to families or persons; Norwegianization (*fornorskning*) bears on lost Sámi and Kven crafts: present each account in its people's own name. Copyright law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** souvenir "troll" and Viking trinkets were weighed as tourist craft and left out as mass-made goods.
