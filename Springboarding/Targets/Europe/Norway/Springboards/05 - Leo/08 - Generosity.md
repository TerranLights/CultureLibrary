# Norway: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål, Nynorsk and the valley and coastal names for feast gifts; Old Norse skaldic praise words; North Sámi; Kven; for kin and religious giving, Somali, Polish and Arabic. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sports clubs and housing co-ops in Oslo against villages in Sogn and Hardanger:** check how *dugnad* duty is asked for and refused.
- **Setesdal and Telemark against Trøndelag:** check *føring* customs in local histories.
- **North Sámi families of Kautokeino and Karasjok against coastal Sámi of Porsanger:** check gifts at weddings and births, as each describes them.
- **Kvens of Porsanger and Forest Finns of Grue:** check whether a work-party custom survives, since sources are few.
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check hospitality and gifts in each group's own accounts, set against older outsider claims.
- **Haugean *bedehus* congregations of Sørlandet and Laestadian congregations of Nord-Troms:** check how each collects and what it gives to.
- **The Jewish community and Muslim congregations:** check *tzedakah* and *zakat* as each organizes them.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Polish and Vietnamese households:** check giving to kin abroad and collective funds.
- **Fishing families of Lofoten:** check how the catch was shared with crews, widows and neighbors.
- **Longyearbyen:** check how a transient town raises money for local causes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** collection totals, volunteering accounts and foundation registers are public; private and kin giving is mixed; Kven, Forest Finn and Romani customs are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for skaldic praise; dialect for *føring* terms; North Sámi; Kven; Somali, Polish and Arabic.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** aid politics and the oil fund's ethics are contested; outsiders' claims about Romani or immigrant giving must be read as claims. Tax rules on gifts go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** "round up your purchase" schemes in shops and banks were considered as everyday giving; they were not listed, since they show what firms ask for rather than what people choose to give.
