*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo apartment children against children in Setesdal and Sogn villages:** check *Barnetråkk* maps for where each plays and how far from home.
- **Northern Norway's dark season against Sørlandet's summer:** check outdoor-time figures and kindergarten routines by season.
- **North Sámi children of Kautokeino and Karasjok against South Sámi children of Snåsa:** check which traditional games each school or kindergarten teaches.
- **Kven and Forest Finn descendants:** check whether game names survive in Porsanger or Finnskogen, since records are few.
- **Romani (*tater*) and Rom children:** check play in each people's own accounts, apart from school reports.
- **Laestadian and *bedehus* families:** check what congregations say about cards, dance, television and video games.
- **Polish, Arabic-speaking, Somali and Vietnamese families:** check games brought from home and played in Oslo and Bergen courtyards.
- **Bergen and Trondheim boys' and girls' play:** check how studies split toys and games by gender.
- **Longyearbyen:** check winter play rules near polar-bear country in school and kindergarten guidance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** kindergarten research, risky-play studies and game-industry figures are rich; children's own game lore is mixed; Kven, Forest Finn and Romani play is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for game terms; dialects for game names; North Sámi; Kven; Polish, Arabic and Vietnamese for home-language material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some research on children's play involves identifiable children; *russ* dares raise alcohol and harassment debates. Gambling and gaming regulation go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** app-store and toy-shop bestseller lists were looked at as measures of play; they were not listed, since they track sales, not play.
