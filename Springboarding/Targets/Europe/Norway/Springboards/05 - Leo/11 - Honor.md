# Norway: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Old Norwegian of the court translations; North Sámi; Kven; Arabic and Polish where apt. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hallingdal, Valdres and Telemark against Vestlandet's coast:** check *slåsskjempe* and wedding-fight legends by valley.
- **Trøndelag, the Falstad district and Northern Norway:** check how occupation-era honor and dishonor are told locally.
- **Sámi of Kautokeino, Karasjok, the coast and Snåsa:** check honoring of elders and the dead in each group's own media.
- **Kvens and Forest Finns:** check church and memorial records for honors paid, since other sources are few.
- **The *tatere* and the Rom:** check promise-keeping and family standing as each describes it, never from police files alone.
- **Muslim, Kurdish, Somali, Eritrean and Iraqi Norwegians:** check each community's own words for honor and family standing in its associations and media, and how its members took part in the national debate on *æresrelatert vold*, rather than reading them through that debate alone.
- **Polish and Lithuanian workers:** check trust in a given word in labor disputes, through union material.
- **Veterans of missions abroad and their families:** check how they speak of honor, through veterans' associations.
- **Fishing communities of Lofoten and Finnmark:** check handshake deals in fish buying, in local histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** policy documents, memorials and sports honors are easy to reach; older honor customs are scattered in local histories; Kven, Forest Finn and Romani honor is thin and must not be filled from neighbors' sources.
- **Languages needed:** reading Old Norwegian to compare the romances with their French originals; Danish-Norwegian for 19th-century parish and local sources on illegitimacy; North Sámi for prize and obituary texts; Kven; Arabic and Polish for community statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor-related violence is contested ground: give state, scholar and community accounts as accounts and name no group as a type; the occupation, Quisling and the treason settlement also need care. Defamation and family law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** tabloid case stories of honor violence were weighed and left out, since they report single cases as patterns.
