# Norway: Work and service, the domain and folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, with dialect farm and fishing words; Old Norse; North, Lule and South Sámi; Kven and Forest Finnish; Romani (*tater*); Polish, Somali and Tigrinya where apt. **Concept in one line:** the domain of work and service: what good work and a well-kept life are taken to be, across the working day, the yearly round, the kept house, chores, colleagues, craft, rest and health.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sogn and Hardanger fjord farms against Gudbrandsdalen summer farms:** check how the *onn* calendar and the summer-farm move differed.
- **Lofoten and Vesterålen fishing-farmers against Oslo office workers:** check rest patterns where the fishing season, not July, sets the break.
- **Herding Sámi of Finnmark, coastal Sámi of the fjords, Lule Sámi of Tysfjord, South Sámi of Snåsa and Røros:** check each one's own account of the seasonal round.
- **Kvens of Porsanger and Nord-Troms; Forest Finns of Finnskogen:** check what survives of the work party, through local museums.
- **Romani (*tater*) families and the Rom:** check the history of their trades in their own associations' words.
- **Laestadian households and *bedehus* congregations:** check which rest days and work-free hours each keeps, in congregation papers.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Eritrean, Somali and Vietnamese workers in Oslo, Bergen and Stavanger:** check how community media explain the Norwegian working day to newcomers.
- **Longyearbyen against Tromsø:** check how shift work and the polar night reshape the day.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** time-use, sick-leave and labor tables are plentiful; house-keeping history is patchier; Kven, Forest Finn and Romani work customs have left little in print.
- **Languages needed:** Danish-Norwegian to read Sundt and Leem in the original; dialect for the farm-season words; Old Norse for *iðn* and *starf*; North, Lule and South Sámi for the herding round; Kven; Polish and Somali for guides to newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sundt and Leem judge cleanliness and industry from outside, and Norwegianization (*fornorskning*) shaped how such texts were used: present each as an account. Holiday and working-hours law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** expatriate "work culture in Norway" guides were weighed for the domain question and left out in favor of Norwegian and minority sources.
