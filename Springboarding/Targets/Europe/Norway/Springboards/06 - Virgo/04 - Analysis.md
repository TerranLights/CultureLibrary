## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bergen and Trondheim research milieus against Oslo's:** check how each city's histories present the Bergen School, NTNU's engineering and Oslo's statistics as its own.
- **Lofoten and Finnmark fishers:** check fishing-mark knowledge in interviews, against the echo sounder and GPS.
- **North Sámi herders of Kautokeino and Karasjok against coastal Sámi fishers:** check whose knowledge is cited in herding and fishery reports, and in which language.
- **Lule Sámi of Tysfjord and South Sámi of Snåsa:** check whether studies of herders' knowledge reach these areas at all.
- **Kvens of Porsanger and the Forest Finns:** check how survey and census categories counted them over time, against their own associations' counts.
- **Romani (*tater*) people and the Rom:** check how registers and research once classified them, kept apart from their own accounts.
- **Haugean and *bedehus* communities, and the Christian press:** check how *Vårt Land* and *Dagen* report on science and its limits.
- **Polish, Vietnamese, Iraqi, Kurdish and Somali families:** check how parents' groups discuss Norwegian math teaching and homework.
- **Longyearbyen researchers against its permanent residents:** check how the town's newspaper treats the research stations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** very rich for science history, statistics and school tests; mixed for fishers' and herders' methods; thin for Kven, Forest Finn and Romani perspectives.
- **Languages needed:** medieval Norwegian and Latin for the land register; Danish-Norwegian for Kiær's era; North Sámi for herding studies; Kven; Polish and Arabic for parents' groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race-biology research at the Oslo university (the Schreiner collection) and the old registers of the *tatere* are contested ground: name each community by its own name and present each account as an account. Some herding knowledge belongs to families and siida.
- **Held for review:** the commercial polling firms' trust-in-science reports were considered and left out, since Norwegian public surveys cover the same ground with open methods.
