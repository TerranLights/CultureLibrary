# Norway: Death and transformation, the domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, with Telemark and Setesdal dialect forms; Old Norse; North and South Sámi; Kven; Polish, Somali, Arabic and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** the domain of death and transformation: how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden, with its death ways, sex ways, inheritance and shared resources.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Telemark and Setesdal against Oslo and Østlandet:** check legends of the dead against city cremation figures.
- **Vestlandet *bedehus* congregations and Sørlandet:** check funeral sermons and hymn choices in their own papers.
- **Fishing families of Lofoten, Vesterålen and the Finnmark coast:** check how deaths at sea and graves without bodies appear in memorials.
- **Gudbrandsdalen and the inland valleys:** check *odel* and *åsete* in bygdebøker for who stayed on the farm.
- **North Sámi of Kautokeino and Karasjok, coastal Sámi, South Sámi of Snåsa and Røros:** check burial and the dead as each group's own institutions record them.
- **Laestadians in Nord-Troms and Alta:** check funeral practice in the movement's sermons and songbooks.
- **Kvens of Porsanger and Varanger; Forest Finns of Finnskogen:** check parish books and local museums; print is scarce.
- **Jewish community; Somali, Iraqi, Kurdish and Syrian Muslims; Polish and Lithuanian Catholics:** check burial at home against sending the body abroad, in each community's guidance.
- **Romani people (*tatere*) and Rom:** check grave-tending and mourning in each people's own publications and interviews.
- **Longyearbyen:** check how residents describe ending life away from Svalbard.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registers, probate and folklore archives; mixed for sex ways before 1987; thin for Kven, Forest Finn and Rom death ways.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for grave and inheritance words; Danish-Norwegian for 1880s print and probate; Telemark dialect for legends; North and South Sámi; Kven; Somali, Arabic, Polish and Vietnamese for burial guidance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi drums and grave sites are not open data: follow Sámi institutions' rules. Sámi remains in the University of Oslo's anatomical collection, and their return, need care and the Sámi Parliament's guidance. Inheritance law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** private funeral-home price lists were weighed for the cost facet and left out until a neutral price survey is found.
