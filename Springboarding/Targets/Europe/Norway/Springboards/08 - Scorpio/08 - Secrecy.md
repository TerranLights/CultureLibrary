# Norway: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Danish-Norwegian and Latin for the black books; Old Norse; German for Lebensborn files; North Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Somali and Polish where apt. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is one facet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Telemark and Setesdal against Trøndelag:** check where black books turn up and how their owners were described.
- **Oslo and Bergen against small coastal towns:** check coming-out accounts in Skeivt arkiv by place.
- **War children and their mothers:** check what each generation was told, from their own association's material.
- **North Sámi of Kautokeino and Karasjok; Lule Sámi of Tysfjord; South Sámi:** check how each speaks of sacred places and what is kept from outsiders, in Sámi-run sources.
- **Kvens of Vadsø and Børselv; Forest Finns:** check accounts of hiding a language or an origin.
- **Laestadians and *bedehus* congregations:** check how each handles confession and kept matters.
- **Freemasons and Odd Fellows:** check membership by town against public debate in local papers.
- **Jewish community:** check its own record of hiding and flight in 1942.
- **Somali, Iraqi, Kurdish and Polish communities:** check each community's own media rules on naming and privacy.
- **Longyearbyen:** check whether a small, shifting town keeps or spreads private matters, in *Svalbardposten*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state secrecy and openness debates; mixed for family secrets; thin for Kven and Forest Finn accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Danish-Norwegian and Latin for the black books; Old Norse; German for Lebensborn; North, Lule and South Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Somali and Polish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi sacred places and restricted knowledge must not be located or retold. Lebensborn and church-book records name living people's parents. Queer archive material may identify people: follow the archive's rules.
- **Held for review:** leaked-document sites were weighed for the state-secrets facet and left out, since their holdings cannot be cited responsibly at this stage.
