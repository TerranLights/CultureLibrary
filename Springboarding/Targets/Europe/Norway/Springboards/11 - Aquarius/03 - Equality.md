# Norway: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, with Oslo east-end and Vestlandet usage; Old Norse; North Sámi; Kven; Polish, Somali, Arabic, Tigrinya and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects. Which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

English keywords: equal worth, gender equality, income gap, hiring discrimination, national minorities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo's west-end districts against Groruddalen and Søndre Nordstrand:** check how each side's local press names the gap.
- **Vestlandet's freeholding farm valleys and Østlandet's former large estates (Hedmark, Østfold):** check *bygdebøker* for the cotter (*husmann*) and the farmer, and how memory of the difference is kept.
- **Sørlandet's *bedehus* districts and Oslo's secular milieu:** check how each speaks of women preachers and leaders.
- **Northern Norway's fishing districts:** check whether the old share system of the boats is remembered as a leveler.
- **Herding families of inner Finnmark, fjord and coastal Sámi, South Sámi of Trøndelag:** check each one's writing on equal standing with Norwegians and among themselves; Sáráhkká's papers carry Sámi women's voices.
- **Kvens and Forest Finns:** check the minority reports and their own associations on recognition next to the Sámi.
- **Tatere and Rom:** check each people's own case for equal treatment, from their own organizations.
- **Jewish community:** check the community's own history of exclusion (the 1814 ban, lifted 1851) and later standing.
- **Polish, Lithuanian and Vietnamese workers and families; Somali, Eritrean and Iraqi communities:** check each community's own media on pay, housing and school.
- **Longyearbyen's residents of many nationalities:** check their responses to the Svalbard white paper.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (income, gender and survey data are deep and open); mixed for how equality is felt in daily manners; thin for Kven and Forest Finn claims outside the minority reports.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse for rank and match words; Nynorsk for cotter memories in farm histories; North Sámi for Sáráhkká and Sámediggi texts; Kven; Somali, Tigrinya and Polish for workers' and parents' media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Norwegianization, the treatment of the tatere and the Rom, and the 1814 Jewish ban carry competing accounts: let each community's version stand as its own. Anti-discrimination statutes go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** work on the "Jante law" was weighed and left out: earlier sheets lean on it, and leveling here is better opened through Gullestad and the tax lists.
