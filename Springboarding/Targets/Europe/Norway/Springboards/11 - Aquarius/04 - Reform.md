# Norway: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, and the dialect debates behind the spelling reforms; Danish-Norwegian; North Sámi; Kven; Arabic, Polish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Temperance-lodge parishes of Vestlandet and Sørlandet and the port and mining towns:** check lodge membership counts against the towns' licensing fights.
- **Nynorsk heartlands (Sogn, Sunnmøre, Telemark, Setesdal) and Bokmål towns:** check local press on each spelling reform.
- **Merged and split municipalities (Indre Østfold, Ålesund and Haram, Tromsø's county):** check how residents told the story of the merger in local papers.
- **Kautokeino and Karasjok Sámi; Alta's coastal Sámi; South Sámi:** check whose reform claims each group's organizations pressed after Alta.
- **Kven villages of Nord-Troms and Porsanger:** check Kven associations on language revival as reform.
- **Finnskogen's Forest Finns:** check the revival of names, crafts and the smoke cabin in their own association's writing; printed work is scarce.
- **Tatere; Rom:** two peoples, two bodies of evidence: check each one's testimony on what was done to it in reform's name.
- **Laestadians and the Church of Norway:** check revival writing against church reform debates (women priests, liturgy).
- **Polish, Arabic-speaking and Vietnamese communities:** check their own media on reforms back home and in Norway.
- **Oslo's and Trondheim's Jewish congregations:** check their histories of the 1851 repeal of the ban on Jews.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the people's movements, language reform and labor; mixed for the reform of persons (treatment and mission records); thin for Kven and Forest Finn renewal outside their own associations.
- **Languages needed:** Danish-Norwegian for Hauge and early lodge books; both written standards side by side for the spelling fights; North Sámi for Alta-era writing; Kven; Arabic and Vietnamese for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Svanviken, the removal of tatere children and the Alta controversy are disputed and painful: put survivors' and each people's own accounts first, and name the Romani people and the Rom as two peoples. Drug-policy reform and the treatment laws go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** eugenic sterilization under the 1934 act was considered; it is law and medicine, so it is held for Libra and the Health sheet.
