# Norway: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk, with local dialect words for odd folk; Old Norse; North Sámi; Kven; the Romani of the tatere; Arabic, Polish and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kristiania-era Oslo and today's Grünerløkka against inland valleys (Gudbrandsdalen, Valdres):** check local press on odd dress and ways of life.
- **Sørlandet and Rogaland prayer-house districts:** check how members who leave are spoken of, in leavers' and members' memoirs.
- **Bergen's and Trondheim's music scenes:** check fanzines for local subcultures (punk, black metal, hip-hop).
- **Inner Finnmark's North Sámi, the Lule Sámi of Divtasvuodna and the South Sámi:** check queer Sámi writing and Sápmi Pride reporting, each area apart.
- **Kvens and Norwegian Finns in Vadsø; Forest Finns of Finnskogen:** check memoirs of being made the odd ones by school and church.
- **Tatere; Rom:** check, in memoirs by members of each people, whose norms schools and missions pressed on them.
- **Laestadians of Alta and Nord-Troms:** check the movement's own writing and leavers' memoirs on what counts as departing from its ways.
- **Jewish, Muslim and Catholic Norwegians:** check how each describes keeping its own customs in a Lutheran-shaped school year.
- **Vietnamese, Polish and Arabic-speaking youth:** check their own media for standing out at home and at school.
- **Svalbard trappers and Longyearbyen residents:** check memoirs of living outside mainland norms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for bohemians, religious dissent, gay history and music scenes; mixed for local eccentrics (scattered through parish books); scant for Kven, Forest Finn and Rom voices.
- **Languages needed:** Danish-Norwegian for the 1880s; Old Norse for saga terms; North and Lule Sámi; Kven; Norwegian Romani; Arabic and Vietnamese for youth media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the black metal arsons and killings, the pressure put on Romani and Rom families, and the pre-1972 ban touch living people: cite published accounts. Leaving strict congregations is told differently by members and leavers.
- **Held for review:** Sandemose's "Jante law" came up first and was kept off on purpose: seven earlier sheets already rest on it, and local records of the odd ones open the question from below.
