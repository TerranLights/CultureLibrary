# Norway: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Norway, with Svalbard, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Bokmål and Nynorsk; Old Norse; North Sámi; Kven; Polish, Lithuanian, Arabic, Somali and Vietnamese where apt. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. This sheet takes the moral and social side; the law itself is Libra's (letter) and Capricorn's (spirit).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oslo's east and west sides:** check press and survey sources for which wrongs each side fears and which it shrugs off.
- **Bergen and Vestlandet:** check local press for how small-town wrongdoing is named when everyone knows the family.
- **Gudbrandsdalen, Hedmark and Telemark:** check outlaw ballads and *bygdebøker* for which thieves were admired.
- **Coastal and reindeer-herding Sámi:** check Sámi-language sources for which wrongs get Sámi words and how they are judged within the community.
- **Kvens and Forest Finns:** check their own histories and museums for the words each uses for a wrong and for being wronged.
- **Tatere and Rom:** check each people's own organizations for how they name wrongdoing and the wrongs done to them.
- **Jewish community:** check community sources for how wartime confiscation of property is remembered as a wrong.
- **Laestadians and *bedehus* congregations:** check how sin, confession and public wrongdoing are told apart.
- **Polish, Lithuanian, Somali, Eritrean, Iraqi, Kurdish, Syrian and Vietnamese communities:** check each community's own media for how wrongdoing and shame are named and judged among neighbors.
- **Longyearbyen:** check local papers for how a small, rotating town handles a wrongdoer it cannot avoid.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press, fiction and victimization surveys; mixed for neighbors' moral judgment; thin for Kven and Romani words of wrongdoing.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norwegian; Dano-Norwegian for 1800s sources; North Sámi; Kven; Polish, Lithuanian, Arabic, Somali and Vietnamese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the treatment of the tatere, the 1942 deportation and the postwar reckoning are contested; give each account as an account. Codes, courts and sentencing go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** registered-crime tables by immigration background were weighed and set aside; if used later they belong in a contested public debate, read from more than one side and beside each community's own sources.
