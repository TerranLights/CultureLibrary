*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani; Transylvanian Saxon and Banat Swabian German; Ukrainian (incl. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and Țara Oașului against Bucharest:** whether village nicknames and dress still mark who someone is in the city as well as the village.
- **Bukovina (Suceava) and Moldavia (Iași) against Oltenia and Muntenia:** whether naming after saints and godparents follows the same pattern in each.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** what a long mix of Romanians, Serbs, Swabians, Bulgarians and Slovaks did to the categories people use for themselves.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari speaking Boyash Romanian, settled Romanian-speaking Roma):** which name each uses for itself against names others gave.
- **Transylvanian Saxons and Banat Swabians:** how belonging to a *Nachbarschaft* tied into personhood, and what emigration left.
- **Lipovans of the Danube Delta (Tulcea), and Ukrainians and Hutsuls of Maramureș and Bukovina:** how rite and church mark a person in two Slavic communities.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja (Constanța):** how Muslim naming and life-cycle rites sit within a mostly Orthodox state.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land (Hungary's row); Moldova; the Aromanians; the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Romanian village ethnography and onomastics; mixed for minorities; thin for the Rudari and small Roma groups (start with ISPMN and Romano Kher).
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for the census, Marian and the atlas; Latin for Cantemir; Church Slavonic for votive inscriptions; Saxon German for the dictionary; Romani, Ukrainian, Russian and Turkish for each group's self-naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the national-character essays include interwar nationalist and antisemitic strands; study them as objects. Roma self-naming touches *robie*. Csángó identity is contested: present accounts as claims. **Lands:** Romania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for Romania as it stands, with Transylvania's Hungarians carved out to Hungary's row.
- **Held for review:** for the domain, birth-and-naming pointers came from Romanian, Romani, German and Slavic sources alone, so none had to be set aside.

## 5. First queries

4. Search "Cimitirul Vesel epitafuri" for a transcription of the Săpânța epitaphs.
