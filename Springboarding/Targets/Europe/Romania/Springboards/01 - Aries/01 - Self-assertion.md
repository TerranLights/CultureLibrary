# Romania: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German (Saxon, Swabian); Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Țara Oașului and Maramureș against Muntenia:** whether *strigături* still give a public channel for calling someone out, and who gets to use it.
- **Bucharest against Moldavian towns (Iași, Suceava):** whether complaint channels (ANPC, ombudsman) are used at the same rates; the ombudsman reports by county are the test.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Brașov:** worth separating because of 1989 and 1987; check whether that history shows up in later petitions and protests.
- **Kalderash and other Vlax Roma groups:** how *pakiv* governs who may speak in a gathering; any internal dispute body (the *kris*) is a law topic for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Transylvanian Saxons:** what the *Richttag* allowed a neighbor to say, and whether the remaining communities keep it.
- **Lipovans (Tulcea, Brăila):** how a priestless or priestly Old Believer community decides who speaks for it; check *Zorile*, the community paper.
- **Dobruja Turks and Tatars:** how claims are put through the Muftiate and the community unions.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarians of Transylvania and the Székely Land; Moldova; the diaspora, whose protest votes are a separate story.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dissent under communism and for proverbs; mixed for everyday talk (one main corpus); thin for minority-language speech norms.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian with Cyrillic-script 16th-century Romanian for Neacșu; Latin and German for the *Supplex*; Saxon German for the *Nachbarschaft* books; Romani for the Roma rights material; Yiddish for some Filderman-era community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Securitate files hold informers' reports on living people; CNSAS rules limit access. The Holocaust material is grave and must be handled as testimony, not color. Petitions edge into law: point to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** proverbs from neighboring peoples were considered for the comparison and left out as outside the row; nothing went to the held list.
