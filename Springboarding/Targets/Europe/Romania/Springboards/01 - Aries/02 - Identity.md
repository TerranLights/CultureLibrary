# Romania: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Armenian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Transylvania's Romanians (Cluj, Alba Iulia, Sibiu) against Muntenia and Oltenia:** whether the Latin-origin story and the Greek Catholic past weigh the same in each.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** check how Moldavian chronicles and the painted monasteries are used in local identity against a Bucharest-made national story.
- **The Moți of the Apuseni and the shepherds of the Mărginimea Sibiului:** whether highland labels are worn as self-names or given by others.
- **Roma groups:** which group names are used inside, which outside; the Rudari and Băieși need separating because self-naming is disputed.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; record each account as a claim, with the people's own answers first.
- **Lipovans, Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** how faith and rite mark the line between Slavic groups living side by side.
- **Dobruja Turks and Tatars; Banat Serbs, Bulgarians and Slovaks:** how small communities hold an identity across a border from a kin state.
- **Armenians and the Jewish community:** check how much of the record is memory after emigration.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians and the Székely Land; Moldova and the Moldovan identity debate; the Aromanians; the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national identity and the origin debate; mixed for minorities; thin for Rudari and Csángó self-description (search ISPMN and Iași diocesan sources).
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for chronicle originals; Latin for some Transylvanian School texts; German for Roesler, Müller and Schlattner; Romani for Grigore's school texts; Armenian for *Ararat*; Ukrainian for the union's papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the continuity and settlement accounts, the Csángó question and the Moldovan identity debate are contested; present each claim as a claim. Interwar texts carry exclusion; Sebastian's diary records it.
- **Held for review:** the Csángó pointer was kept in the body on its contested footing rather than held; no other identity source needed holding.
