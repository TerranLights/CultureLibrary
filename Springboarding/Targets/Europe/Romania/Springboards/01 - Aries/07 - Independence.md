## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Maramureș and Bukovina against the Bărăgan plain of Muntenia:** check the farm census for how far households feed themselves in each, since land and farm sizes differ.
- **Mărginimea Sibiului shepherds and the Moți woodworkers of the Apuseni:** separate livelihoods built on moving and trading from those built on fixed farms.
- **The Jiu Valley and other single-industry towns:** check how households managed after mine and plant closures, and who they turned to.
- **Roma groups:** compare welfare use with Roma-authored accounts of work and trade, to keep outside stereotypes out of the record.
- **Lipovan and Ukrainian fishing households in the Delta:** check how fishing quotas and the reserve's rules affect self-provisioning.
- **Saxon and Swabian villages after emigration:** who now keeps the houses and land, and on what terms.
- **Banat and Bihor Slovaks, and Dobruja Turks and Tatars:** check community unions' reports for small-farm life and mutual help.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians; Moldova; migrants abroad (their remittances count here only on the receiving side).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for farm structure, remittances and elder living arrangements; mixed for mutual aid; thin for minority household economies (start with community unions and ISPMN).
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for INS, Neculce and the novels; German for Saxon village records; Russian and Ukrainian for Delta households; Romani for Roma-authored sources on work; Slovak for Nădlac-area material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** welfare and Roma is a stereotype minefield: name sources, never traits. Disability institutions involve abuse cases. The 1980s rationing memory is political. Welfare law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for the national side of independence, pointers on the 1918 unions with Bessarabia and Bukovina were weighed; Bessarabia belongs to the Moldova comparison, so it was moved there, not held.
