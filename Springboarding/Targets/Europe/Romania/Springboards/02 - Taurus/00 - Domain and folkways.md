# Romania: Domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani (Kalderash, Ursari) and the Boyash Romanian of the Rudari; Saxon and Swabian German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having and how it is held, with food, dress and wealth ways as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and the Apuseni (Moți) against the Bărăgan plain of Muntenia:** check how highland smallholdings and lowland estates, later state farms, shaped which goods a family keeps.
- **Oltenia, Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** compare dowry-chest contents in museum catalogs, as written inventories of household value.
- **Saxon and Swabian villages (Sibiu, Brașov, Timiș):** check church and household inventories before and after 1945 and the 1990s emigration.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Rudari, Ursari, settled Roma):** seek each group's own account of trade and money, not outsiders'.
- **Lipovans of the Delta (Tulcea):** check boats, nets and fishing rights as household goods.
- **Dobruja Turks and Tatars (Constanța, Medgidia):** compare *çeyiz* and feast food with the Orthodox fasting calendar next door.
- **Bucharest against the villages:** check where display money goes (flats, cars, weddings, migrants' houses).
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose church and estate restitution belongs to Hungary's row; Moldova, co-holder of the blouse file.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village ethnography, dowry and food; mixed for present household wealth; thin for Rudari and Lipovan household economies.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the Brâncoveanu ledger and older dowry lists; Saxon German for storeroom records; Romani for Kalderash money words; Turkish for Dobruja *çeyiz* accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collectivization and the seizures of Jewish (1940–44) and German (1945) property are contested; present each account as a claim. Older dowry lists and deeds can list Roma held in *robie* (until 1856): read them as owners' papers and seek Roma accounts alongside. Property law goes to Libra and Capricorn. **Lands:** Romania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign covers Romania as it stands, minus Transylvania's Hungarians (Hungary's row).
- **Held for review:** the cross-cultural coding databases in the source types were weighed and left out to keep the domain on Romania's own record; nothing was held.
