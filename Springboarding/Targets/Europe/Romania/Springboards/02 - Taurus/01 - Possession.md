# Romania: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian and Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Banat Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gabor Roma, Kalderash and Rudari:** check which objects each group treats as never for sale, and who in the family may pledge them.
- **Țara Oașului and Maramureș against Moldavian villages (Iași, Vaslui):** check whether money from abroad ends as kept goods or shown goods in each.
- **Saxons and Swabians who left against those who stayed (Sibiu, Timiș):** compare the objects carried out with those left in the house or church.
- **Lipovans of Tulcea and Brăila:** check how household rules on icons, books and shared dishes mark what stays inside the family.
- **The Jewish community of Iași and Bucharest:** check what seized goods came back, and what sits in museum or community collections.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja:** compare what happens to a dead person's clothes and goods with the Orthodox *pomană* of clothing.
- **Banat Serbs and Banat Bulgarians (Dudeștii Vechi):** check which heirloom dress and church objects each community keeps.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose household-object studies in Hungarian sit with Hungary's row even in mixed villages; Moldova, whose shortage-era objects follow a Soviet timeline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for death customs and communist-era objects; mixed for lending and lost property; thin for Lipovan and Tatar household goods.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for Ghinoiu, Creangă and the wills; Cyrillic-script Romanian for Antim's originals; Romani terms inside Berta's English text; German for the *Heimatbücher*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *robie* documents treat people as possessions; the *românizare* seizures and later restitution are contested (restitution law goes to Libra). The Gabor beaker trade is a community's own practice: record it as theirs, never as a Roma trait.
- **Held for review:** a world photo survey of families posed with all their goods was weighed as a method model and dropped for carrying no Romanian material; nothing was held.
