# Romania: Domain and folkways (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech (*graiuri*: Moldavian, Muntenian, Oltenian, Banat, Crișana, Maramureș); Romani (Kalderash, Ursari) and the Boyash speech of the Rudari; Saxon and Swabian German; Ukrainian, including Hutsul; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian, Banat Bulgarian, Slovak; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the domain of communication and learning: how people talk, learn and connect close to home, with speech ways and learning ways as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moldavian, Muntenian, Oltenian, Banat, Crișana and Maramureș speakers:** check which regional features speakers keep with outsiders and which they drop at school.
- **Bucharest blocks against Apuseni (Moți) or Mărginimea Sibiului villages:** check where neighbors meet (stairwell, gate, church, shop).
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari speaking Boyash, Romanian-speaking Roma):** check which speak Romani at home and which do not, before any Romani-language claim.
- **Saxons and Swabians against Romanian pupils in German schools:** check who carries German-language schooling today.
- **Ukrainians and Hutsuls of Maramureș and Bukovina; Lipovans of Tulcea:** check where home speech and school language part ways.
- **Dobruja Turks and Tatars; Serbs, Banat Bulgarians and Slovaks of the Banat:** check the minority-school networks and church language in each.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose school network is Hungary's row; Moldova, whose standard and dialects must be kept apart from Romania's; the Aromanians, whose language status is disputed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialectology and school history; mixed for everyday conversation and neighbor life; thin for Boyash, Banat Bulgarian and Hutsul speech in print.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Micu and Șincai; Cyrillic-script Romanian for Antim's sermons; German for the Sibiu and Timișoara school sources; Turkish and Tatar for Medgidia; Romani for Sarău's materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether Moldovan and Romanian are one language, and the status of Aromanian and Boyash, are contested; present each view as a view. Csángó speech is contested ground: present it as claims. **Lands:** Romania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign covers Romania as it stands, without Transylvania's Hungarians (Hungary's row).
- **Held for review:** cross-cultural coding sheets listed among the source types were weighed for kin terms and left aside, since the Romanian dialect atlases give finer, local coverage.
