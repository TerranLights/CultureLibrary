# Romania: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the ideas people hold about what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moldavia, Oltenia and the Banat:** check the address pronouns each region uses with elders and strangers in the spoken corpora, before claiming one national pattern.
- **Bucharest offices against Maramureș villages:** check how requests and refusals are phrased in each, using recorded speech, not hearsay.
- **Romani-speaking Roma (Kalderash, Ursari) and the Boyash-speaking Rudari:** check who interprets for whom at school, clinic and town hall.
- **Banat Swabians and Saxons:** check who still listens to German-language broadcasts, and whether Romanian listeners do too.
- **Lipovans, Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** check when each switches out of Russian or Ukrainian, and for whom.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja:** keep the two languages apart when tracing which one a household speaks with elders.
- **The Jewish community, past and present:** check the move from Yiddish to Romanian in Iași, Botoșani and Bucharest through memoirs.
- **Deaf Romanians:** check how signed talk crosses into spoken settings (interpreters, television).
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose bilingual exchanges with Romanian speakers are traced from Hungary's row; Moldova, where the Russian-Romanian language choice follows its own history.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public speech and broadcast archives; mixed for everyday conversation (few spoken corpora); thin for Boyash and Tatar talk in print.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the Bistrița letters; German for the Radio Timișoara hours; Russian for *Zorile*; Yiddish for the Iași stage history; Tatar and Turkish for the language-shift studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1989 and 1990 events are contested and caused deaths; give each account as a claim. Wiretapping and informers belong to the Securitate files, a sensitive record. Rules on insult and speech go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** global business-culture scales that rank countries on directness were weighed and not used, since they speak for a whole country in one voice.
