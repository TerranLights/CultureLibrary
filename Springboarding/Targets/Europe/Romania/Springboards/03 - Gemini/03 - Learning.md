## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia's potters and Maramureș woodcarvers against Bucharest and Iași apartments:** check whether children still learn a craft by sitting in on adult work, and at what age.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash coppersmiths, Rudari woodworkers, Ursari, settled Roma):** check each group's own account of how trades and Romani are passed on.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check how kindergarten and German schooling have changed now that most pupils are not German.
- **Ukrainians and Hutsuls; Lipovans:** check the age at which children start Slavonic or chant classes, and who teaches them.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja:** check which language mosque classes use with children.
- **Serbs, Banat Bulgarians and Slovaks:** check how many children still learn the home language before Romanian.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose children learn in a separate school network within Hungary's row; Moldova, whose primers and alphabet changes need their own study; diaspora children's weekend Romanian classes, a learning setting outside these lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school-centered learning and literacy history; mixed for learning by watching in families; thin for Lipovan chant schools and Tatar religious classes.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the *bucoavne*; Church Slavonic and Russian for the chant teaching; Turkish and Tatar for the mosque classes; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Jewish school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment in homes and schools is a live debate; record views as views. Chant and religious teaching belong to their communities: ask before recording. School law and rights go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** global child-development indexes were looked at for the early years and passed over in favor of the Romanian family and craft sources above.
