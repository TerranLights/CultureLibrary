## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Muntenia (Bucharest):** check which popular-science clubs and magazines each city produced, and who read them.
- **The Apuseni (Moți) and the Banat's karst:** check how local cave guides and visiting scientists differ in what they look for underground.
- **Villages of Oltenia and Maramureș:** check the questions neighbors may ask a returning migrant, using memoirs and forums.
- **Roma groups:** check how Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari describe being studied and photographed, as the subject rather than the observer.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check the science societies and museum collections of Sibiu, Brașov and Timișoara for who joined.
- **Lipovans and Ukrainians, including Hutsuls:** check what each community lets visitors see in churches and homes.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja:** check how mosques in Constanța and Mangalia receive curious visitors.
- **The Jewish community:** check how scholars such as Gaster were received, and what was lost when they left.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose science societies (for example in Cluj) belong to Hungary's row; diaspora scientists abroad, whose careers answer a different question about curiosity and opportunity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science history and popular science; mixed for everyday nosiness, which lives in forums and fiction; thin for minority communities' own words on being looked at.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for older *Alexandria* manuscripts and Milescu; German for the Sibiu society; Russian for Lipovan community views on visitors; Romani for Roma responses to being studied.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chelcea's study belongs to the era of racial policy that led to the 1942 deportation of Roma to Transnistria; read it as evidence of that gaze. Crime coverage can expose victims, especially minors. Church interiors and rites may be closed to visitors' cameras.
- **Held for review:** country-level personality and avoidance-of-uncertainty scores were weighed and kept out of this sheet because they stand in one number for many groups.
