# Romania: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Serbian; Ukrainian and Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moldavia and Oltenia against Bucharest and Cluj:** check emigration figures by county first, then how ties to absent kin are kept in each: calls, vans, holidays home.
- **Maramureș and Țara Oașului against the Banat:** check the number of godchildren a couple may take on and what the tie obliges, in each.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, settled Roma):** check whether *kirvo* ties cross group lines, and how Pentecostal congregations add new ties.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check who organizes reunions and who now cares for empty churches and graves.
- **Serbs of the Banat and Banat Bulgarians:** compare *kumstvo* and *slava* hosting with Romanian *nășie* next door.
- **Lipovans and Ukrainians, including Hutsuls:** check ties to kin and churches across the borders to the north and east.
- **Turks and Tatars:** check whether ties to kin in Turkey shape gatherings at home.
- **The Jewish community:** check how small communities in Iași, Suceava and Timișoara keep in touch with members abroad.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose networks across the Hungarian border belong to Hungary's row; Moldova, where godparenthood ties cross into Romania and need separate sorting; the diaspora's own associations in Italy and Spain, studied from the migrants' side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration ties and ritual kinship; mixed for loneliness (few local surveys); thin for Tatar and Lipovan networks.
- **Languages needed:** Slavonic and Cyrillic-script Romanian for the *înfrățire* charters; Serbian for *slava* customs; Romani for *kirvo* ties; Russian for the Lipovan festival record; Turkish for *Hıdırellez*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *pile* shades into corruption claims; record them as claims. Families split by migration and children left behind are a painful subject. Nepotism rules go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** global social-capital indexes were considered and dropped for this concept; Romanian village and migration sources come closer to how ties are made.
