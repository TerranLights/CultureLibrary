# Romania: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani and Boyash; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Bulgarian (Banat); Yiddish and French (for bilingual writers). **Concept in one line:** how people live with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholic and Orthodox Romanians of Transylvania (Blaj, Cluj, Maramureș):** check how each tells the 1700 union, and how mixed families handle it today.
- **Moldavia and Muntenia against Transylvania's Romanian villages:** compare the folk creation legends recorded in each for how the paired makers are portrayed.
- **Rudari, Ursari and Kalderash:** check how each group draws its own line between Roma and others, without imposing one.
- **Saxons and Swabians in villages now mostly Romanian or Roma:** check who wears the carnival masks and who watches.
- **Turks and Tatars; Lipovans; Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** check which side of a double belonging each foregrounds at school, at prayer and abroad.
- **The Jewish community:** check memoirs of writers who left Romanian for French, Yiddish or Hebrew, and what they said of it.
- **The Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; record each side's claims about their language and belonging as claims, and let Csángó voices speak first.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose Hungarian-Romanian two-ness is studied from Hungary's row; Moldova, whose Romanian passports for its citizens raise a separate two-country question; the Aromanians, whose standing between peoples is disputed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church union history and literary bilingualism; mixed for everyday two-faced talk; thin for Banat Bulgarian and Rudari self-description.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Cyrillic-script Romanian for the union documents; French for Tzara and Voronca; Boyash and Romani for the Rudari studies; Turkish and Tatar for the citizenship studies; Banat Bulgarian for Dudeștii Vechi.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the church union and the 1948 suppression of the Greek Catholic church remain disputed; Rudari and Csángó identity are contested; Eliade's interwar far-right ties need noting. Slurs for mixed people are recorded as terms only.
- **Held for review:** the Russian "double faith" framework is glossed above as a term only; no pointer to that scholarship was given, so a framework built on neighboring Slavic peoples does not frame Romania's own sources first.
