# Romania: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and Bukovina (Suceava) against the Bărăgan and Dobruja plains:** check which sibling inherits the house in each, using village studies.
- **Oltenia and Moldavia (Iași):** check the regional words for an older brother or sister and when each is used.
- **Romanians born 1967–89 against those born after 1990:** check how sibling numbers and shared rooms changed, from census and memoirs.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check how each group describes the duties of brothers to sisters, in its own accounts.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check single-heir farm customs against Romanian neighbors' sharing among all children.
- **Ukrainians and Hutsuls; Lipovans:** check family-size figures by locality against the national ones before any claim.
- **Turks and Tatars of Constanța:** check whether elder-sibling titles are kept by children schooled in Romanian.
- **The Jewish community:** check sibling testimony for separations caused by deportation and emigration.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose inheritance customs are studied from Hungary's row; siblings split between Romania and the diaspora, compared to see how distance reshapes care for parents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and institutional childhood; mixed for adult sibling ties; thin for Lipovan, Hutsul and Tatar families.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian regional speech for the house-inheritance customs; Romani for Roma family accounts; Turkish for Dobruja kin terms; Yiddish and Hebrew for parts of the testimony collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Decree 770 and its births are contested ground; give each account as a claim. Institutional childhood and international adoption involve living people; avoid naming children. Inheritance rules go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** popular birth-order personality books were looked at and left aside: they would sort siblings by a theory before Romanian families describe themselves.
