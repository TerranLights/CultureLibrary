*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Greek and Ottoman Turkish (for the dragomans); Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision; and, more broadly, the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava) against Oltenia:** check whether village quarrels go first to the priest, the godparent or the mayor, using local studies.
- **The Jiu Valley and Hunedoara:** check who stood between miners and governments in 1977, 1990 and 1999, and how miners' families tell it.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, settled Roma):** check which keep a *kris* and which turn to a church, a leader or the state.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic and neo-Protestant believers:** check how intercession by saints is taught or rejected in each.
- **Saxons and Swabians; Banat Serbs and Bulgarians:** check which clergy act as go-betweens in mixed villages.
- **Turks and Tatars:** compare imams' and elders' roles in family quarrels.
- **The Jewish community:** check the *shtadlan* role from the 19th century to 1944 through community records.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose Romanian-Hungarian interethnic mediation is traced from Hungary's row; Moldova, where Orthodox intercession runs through a different church hierarchy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church intercession and labor crises; mixed for family go-betweens; thin for Lipovan and Tatar mediation.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the boundary charters; Greek and Ottoman Turkish for the dragomans; Romani for the *kris*; Russian and Church Slavonic for the Old Believer split; Turkish for Dobruja imams.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *kris* is internal Roma knowledge: rely on Roma authors and ask before recording. The 1990s miners' marches caused deaths and remain disputed. Formal mediation law and courts go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** conflict-style instruments that place whole countries on one scale were considered; none fit village and church go-betweens, so they stay out.
