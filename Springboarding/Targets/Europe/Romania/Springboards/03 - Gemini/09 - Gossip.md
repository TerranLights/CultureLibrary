## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and Oltenia villages against Bucharest and Timișoara blocks:** check where talk about neighbors happens (gate, church, stairwell, chat group) and how fast it travels.
- **Women and men:** check the claim that one sex gossips more against recorded talk, before repeating it.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** check how the 1941 rumors are remembered by Jewish and Romanian families today.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, settled Roma):** check how each describes talk that damages a family, and who settles it.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check emigrants' memoirs for gossip about who left and who stayed.
- **Lipovans, Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** check whether church communities police talk, and how.
- **Turks and Tatars; Banat Serbs:** check talk across faith lines in mixed towns.
- **Neo-Protestant congregations:** check teaching on speech and how gossip is handled inside a church.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose village talk and tabloids sit in Hungary's row; migrant communities in Italy and Spain, whose gossip about home is studied only to see how it travels back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tabloid and broadcast material; mixed for village gossip (ethnology, fiction); thin for Lipovan, Hutsul and Tatar talk.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for early copies of Cantemir's novel; Turkish and Tatar for *dedikodu* studies; Yiddish for Iași testimony; Romani for Roma reputation studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Iași pogrom and its rumors are contested in public memory; give each claim as a claim, survivors' accounts first. Denunciation letters name living people and their families. Defamation law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** psychology experiments on gossip and cooperation run abroad were weighed but not listed, because they would substitute lab findings for Romanian talk.
