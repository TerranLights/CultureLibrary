# Romania: Domain and folkways (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani and Boyash; Saxon and Swabian German; Ukrainian and Hutsul; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what home is and who counts as family, with building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and Bukovina against Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare the census share of three-generation homes, and whether plans keep a guest room.
- **Oltenia and the Bărăgan against the Banat:** check how settlement history (half-buried *bordei* houses, planned Swabian villages) shaped house types.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** set the 1770s counts beside today's.
- **Kalderash, Ursari, Gabori and Rudari Roma:** check how each group's own writers define who belongs to a household.
- **Saxons, Swabians, Lipovans, Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Turks and Tatars, Serbs, Banat Bulgarians, Slovaks:** check community museums for house forms before using regional averages.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic, Baptist, Pentecostal and Adventist families:** compare household size and house-blessing practice church by church.
- **Csángó of Bacău county:** contested ground; compare how Romanian- and Hungarian-language sources describe Catholic households, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians and the Székely (Hungary's row), as a contrast to Saxon and Romanian village plans; Moldova, whose household censuses part ways after 1940; the Aromanians' pastoral households; diaspora homes in Italy and Spain, for what migrants rebuild.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village houses and life-cycle rites; mixed for fathering and hospitality; thin for Hutsul, Banat Bulgarian and Tatar homes.
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Cyrillic-script Romanian for the 1770s counts; French for Olivera; German for Saxon and Swabian house histories; Romani for Kalderash kin words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma household figures rest on disputed self-identification; Csángó identity is contested. **Lands:** Romania's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for Romania as it stands, with Transylvania's Hungarians left to Hungary's row.
- **Held for review:** Todd's maps of European family systems were weighed and kept back, since they sort Romania from outside before its own household counts are read.
