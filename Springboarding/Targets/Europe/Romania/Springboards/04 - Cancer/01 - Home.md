# Romania: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (Muntenian, Moldavian, Maramureș and Banat speech); Romani; Swabian and Saxon German; Hutsul Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Tatar and Turkish; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Iași block districts against Muntenian and Moldavian villages:** check what "home" names for each, the flat or the parents' village house.
- **Maramureș, Țara Oașului and Bukovina:** check how migrant earnings rebuild houses there, and what the old house becomes.
- **Oltenia and the Banat (Timișoara):** compare yard layouts and street fronts, where house type follows colonization history.
- **Dobruja and the Danube Delta (Constanța, Tulcea):** check Lipovan, Turkish, Tatar and Romanian homes side by side in the same villages.
- **Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and Romanian-speaking Roma:** check housing by group (settled, informal, evicted) rather than one "Roma housing" figure.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check who lives in their houses now, after the 1990 emigration.
- **Moți of the Apuseni and shepherds of the Mărginimea Sibiului:** compare the village house with summer houses on the pastures.
- **Comparison only:** Székely and Transylvanian Hungarian houses (Hungary's row) where Saxon, Romanian and Hungarian homes share a village; diaspora houses built in Spain and Italy, to set against the ones migrants build back home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village houses and block history; mixed for how home feels; thin for Hutsul and Tatar homes.
- **Languages needed:** German for Bartók's carol volume and the Saxon return press; Yiddish and Hebrew for the memorial books; Ukrainian for Hutsul house studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** evictions and informal settlements involve living families; avoid naming individuals. The 1980s demolitions are told differently by different sides; keep each account as an account. Property and housing law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Western theory of dwelling (Bachelard, Heidegger) was considered as a frame, but Romanian carols and building rites come first, so it stays out of this sheet.
