# Romania: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (Crișana, Oltenia and Moldavian kin words included); Romani of the Kalderash; Swabian German; Serbian; Hutsul Ukrainian; Turkish; Yiddish and Hebrew (historical). **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hunedoara villages and the Jiu Valley mining towns:** check how kin ties change when families move from farm to shaft.
- **Oltenia and Crișana (Oradea):** compare the words used for in-laws and the place a new couple lives after marriage.
- **Iași and Suceava against Constanța:** check census family types for regions with high and low birth numbers.
- **Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari families:** check each group's own marriage and kin accounts separately; one "Roma family" picture would hide the differences.
- **Swabians and Saxons; Banat Serbs and Bulgarians; Slovaks of Bihor and the Banat:** compare surviving family registers to see which records reach furthest back.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja; Lipovans of Tulcea:** check whose kin now live mostly abroad, in Turkey or elsewhere, and how families keep up.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic and neo-Protestant families:** check family size and in-law roles by church, not by region alone.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian Hungarian families, whose kin networks are studied in Hungary's row, set against mixed Hungarian-Romanian households; families split between Romania and Italy or Spain, to see who keeps the kin network running.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy and wedding orations; mixed for current kin contact (one survey wave); thin for Hutsul and Tatar families.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and Cyrillic-script Romanian for the Cantacuzino genealogy; German for the Swabian family books; Hebrew and Yiddish for some JewishGen records; Romani for Kalderash accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma marriage customs are a target of stereotype and court cases; keep each account in its author's words. Parish records include living people. Adoption and family law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Banfield's "amoral familism" was looked at as a lens and left out: it was written about another country and would color Romanian kin ties before they are described.
