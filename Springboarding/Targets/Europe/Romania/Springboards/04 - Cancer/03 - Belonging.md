# Romania: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian, with Banat, Năsăud and Apuseni usage; Romani; Saxon and Landler German; Ukrainian; Crimean Tatar and Turkish; Yiddish and Romanian for Jewish writers. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Brașov and Hunedoara born residents against families who came from Moldavia after 1950:** check when the newcomer label stopped being used, if it did.
- **The Banat against Oltenia and Muntenia:** check regional "we" talk in local press and how each speaks of Bucharest.
- **Moți of the Apuseni and shepherds of the Mărginimea Sibiului:** compare how each ties belonging to land, pasture and memory.
- **Roma in Baia Mare, Cluj and Bucharest; Kalderash and Rudari villages:** check where exclusion is spatial (walls, edges of town) and where it is social.
- **Saxons and Landler; Banat Swabians:** compare how each small group tells who has stayed and who has left.
- **Ukrainians and Hutsuls of Maramureș and Suceava; Lipovans:** check whether belonging is framed by language, church or village.
- **Turks and Tatars of Constanța:** check how each speaks of Turkey and Crimea as places of belonging.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; compare the claims made about which community they belong to, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians and the Székely, whose sense of belonging is studied in Hungary's row, set beside the Saxons and Landler as other non-Romanian groups in the same region; Moldova, where the same question of who belongs is fought over; Romanians abroad, for the belonging they keep or lose.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for internal migration and minority writing; mixed for measured belonging; thin for Landler and Hutsul communities.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Landler histories; Romanian and Hebrew editions for Manea; Crimean Tatar and Turkish for Dobruja community writing; Ukrainian for Hutsul events and the refugee reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** exclusion of Roma is current and named in decisions of the equality body; keep the parties' statements separate. Csángó belonging is contested ground. Citizenship law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Romania's lands; one pointer on how villages receive recently recruited foreign workers was set aside under that rule.
