# Romania: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (with Moldavian and Muntenian nursery words); Romani; Ukrainian of Maramureș; Crimean Tatar and Turkish; Lipovan Russian; German; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia and Moldavia (Iași):** compare lullaby texts and nursery words to see which regional features survive in what parents sing today.
- **Bucharest and Cluj parents against Oltenian and Bărăgan villages:** check who does the daily feeding and minding (mother, grandmother, nanny, crèche).
- **People born 1965–89, who went through weekly crèches, against their children:** check how each generation describes its own early care.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check how child-minding is shared in each group, from Roma authors and mediators' reports.
- **Saxons and Swabians; Lipovans; Ukrainians of Maramureș:** check whether minority-language kindergartens or church groups carry nurture outside the home.
- **Turks and Tatars of Dobruja:** check who teaches small children the family language, mothers or grandparents.
- **Greek Catholic, Orthodox and neo-Protestant families:** compare church teaching for parents (sermons, parish groups, Sunday schools).
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian Hungarian kindergartens, studied in Hungary's row, beside Romanian and German ones in the same towns; diaspora children raised by grandparents during summers in Romania, for what each side calls good care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and childhood memoir; mixed for measured parenting; thin for Tatar, Lipovan and Ukrainian home practice.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and old Cyrillic-script Romanian for the bestiary; Romani for the Roma cradle songs; Crimean Tatar and Ukrainian for the Dobruja and Maramureș collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** weekly crèches and the institutions linked to them involve living people; take memoir as memoir. Physical punishment is a live public argument; discipline goes to Capricorn, child-protection law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** a Western nature-against-nurture frame was set aside, since Romanian sayings on how a child "turns out" should be gathered first and in their own wording.
