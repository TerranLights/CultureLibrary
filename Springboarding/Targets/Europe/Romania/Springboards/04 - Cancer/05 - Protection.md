## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia (Vâlcea, Gorj) and Muntenia:** check how *cule* and walled courtyards relate to raids remembered in local history.
- **Bukovina (Suceava) and Moldavia (Iași):** compare which saints and icons are asked for protection, at home and on pilgrimage.
- **Shepherds of the Mărginimea Sibiului, Maramureș and the Apuseni:** check how guarding flocks differs where bears are counted most.
- **Bucharest block districts and small towns of the Jiu Valley:** check who people call first when in danger: police, neighbors or kin.
- **Roma groups in Cluj, Bucharest and Bihor:** check reports from Roma organizations on protection and policing, group by group.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check how village fire and night-watch duty was shared and when it ended.
- **Turks and Tatars; Lipovans; Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** compare protective objects in homes, and whose religious figure may make them.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Pentecostal and Adventist households:** check attitudes to amulets, since some churches reject them.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian Hungarian protective customs (Hungary's row), set beside Saxon and Romanian ones in shared villages; Moldova, whose child-protection reforms ran in parallel; diaspora parents abroad, whose fears about leaving children are covered under Abandonment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church art, folklore and child-protection statistics; mixed for everyday self-protection; thin for Tatar and Hutsul protective practice.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for *Varlaam și Ioasaf*; Turkish and Crimean Tatar for the *muska* sources; German for Saxon fire-brigade records; Romani for Roma community statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Caracal case involves victims' families; keep names out of notes. Policing of Roma is contested; give each side's statement. Domestic-violence and child-protection law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** gun-ownership surveys from the concept block were weighed; whether any such figures exist for Romania is a question for later, with hunters' associations as the place to start.
