# Romania: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (with Muntenian and Moldavian healing vocabulary); Old Romanian for hospital charters; Romani; Transylvanian Saxon German; Turkish and Tatar; Hebrew and Yiddish for Jewish institutions. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villages of Moldavia, Oltenia and Maramureș with few young adults left:** check who now cares for the old (neighbors, paid carers, a returning child).
- **Bucharest and Brașov against small towns:** compare access to hospice and home care, using provider maps.
- **Families with a member paid as personal assistant:** check how carers describe being both kin and employee.
- **Roma communities in Muntenia and Crișana:** check health mediators' and Roma NGOs' reports separately by group.
- **Saxons, Swabians and the Jewish community:** check how care homes run by small, aging communities cope.
- **Turks and Tatars; Lipovans of Tulcea; Ukrainians and Hutsuls of Suceava:** compare who washes and sits with the dying, by faith.
- **Orthodox parishes, Catholic orders, Baptist and Adventist congregations:** check which run care homes or home visits, and for whom.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian Hungarian church care homes, studied in Hungary's row, to set beside Saxon ones; Romanian women working as live-in carers in Italy and Spain, whose own parents' care at home is the point to compare.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hospital history and folk remedies; mixed for informal care (one EU survey wave); thin for Tatar, Lipovan and Hutsul care practices.
- **Languages needed:** old Cyrillic-script Romanian and Greek for the early hospital charters; German for Carl Wolff reports; Turkish for Dobruja practice; Hebrew for Jewish community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2020 care-home cases involve victims and families; avoid names. Health data on Roma must not be read as a trait of a people. Disability-assistance law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** care-ethics theory from the concept block was put aside here; nursing orders' and healers' own words should frame the first pass.
