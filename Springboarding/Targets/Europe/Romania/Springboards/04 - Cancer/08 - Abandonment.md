# Romania: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (Mărginimea Sibiului and Banat speech where the sources come from there); Romani; Banat Swabian German; Hebrew and Romanian for the Transnistria orphans; English and French for foreign press of 1990. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Maramureș and Moldavia (Iași, Suceava), where many parents work abroad:** check how grandparents and relatives raising children describe their situation, in their own words.
- **Bihor, Caraș-Severin and Hunedoara towns after mine and factory closures:** compare how each speaks of being "left" by the state.
- **Bucharest and Constanța street populations:** check whether street life is reported differently in a capital and a port.
- **Roma children in residential care:** check Roma organizations' figures and arguments against official ones before any claim.
- **Swabians and Saxons left in the villages; Lipovans of the Delta:** compare who stayed, by age, after each group's emigration wave.
- **Jewish survivors of Transnistria and the Iași pogrom:** check the testimonies of those orphaned, kept separate from rescue narratives.
- **Orthodox and neo-Protestant foster and adoptive families:** check how church communities speak of taking in left children.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian Hungarian children's homes run by churches (Hungary's row), as another network working in the same counties; Moldova, whose "children left behind" debate ran in parallel; diaspora parents in Italy and Spain, to hear the leaver's side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1990 institutions and child-abandonment studies; mixed for elders left in hospitals; thin for 18th-c. flight records.
- **Languages needed:** English and French for the 1990 foreign coverage; Hebrew for Transnistria orphan testimonies; German for Swabian church reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors of institutions and adoptees are living people; avoid identifying details. Transnistria and the 1990 institutions are painful and contested; give each account as an account. Child-protection and adoption law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** attachment-theory scales were weighed for measuring the fear of being left, and kept for later because they were built outside Romania and need validation here first.
