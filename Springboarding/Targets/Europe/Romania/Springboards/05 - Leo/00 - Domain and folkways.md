# Romania: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani (Kalderash, Ursari) and Boyash; Saxon and Swabian German; Ukrainian and Hutsul; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure, with sport ways as the sign's folkway (dress and child-rearing ways as runners-up).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia against Oltenia:** check the *Barometrul* gap in festival-going between the capital and Dolj or Gorj.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** compare village year-end masking with town New Year parties.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** check which bands and dances Romanians, Serbs and Swabians shared at village feasts.
- **Maramureș and Transylvania's Romanian villages (Mărginimea Sibiului, Țara Făgărașului, the Apuseni):** compare village dance days with city clubs.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** set Tatar and Turkish festival calendars beside the resort season and Constanța's football.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** read music, dance and feast-giving in Roma writers' own accounts first.
- **Lipovans, Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Serbs, Banat Bulgarians, Slovaks and Czechs, Armenians:** find each community's own festival and ensemble lists.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic and neo-Protestant households:** check which dances, drink and music each church allows at feasts.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; compare dances as Romanian and Hungarian sources record them, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians and the Székely, whose dance-house movement belongs to Hungary's row, read only beside Romanian village dances; Moldova and the Aromanians, for shared song festivals; diaspora festivals in Italy and Spain, for what play travels abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, folk dance and sport; mixed for leisure and spending; thin for Banat Bulgarian, Czech and Armenian play.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian Cyrillic for early copies of the *Condica*; Ukrainian for *Malanca* reports; German for the Saxon band histories; Turkish and Tatar for *Tepreș* coverage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bear-mask customs touch quarrels over live bears; Roma music is often written about from outside, so Roma voices come first. **Lands:** the exact lands of Romania are still to be settled; until then these Leo sheets cover the country as it stands today, and the Hungarians of Transylvania stay on Hungary's side of the line.
- **Held for review:** the concept block's game-and-feast coding files were looked at for the domain; Romania's own folklore archives cover the same ground in more detail, so no pointer was taken from them.
