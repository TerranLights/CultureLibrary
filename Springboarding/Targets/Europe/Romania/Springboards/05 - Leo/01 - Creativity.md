*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cluj and Bucharest studios against village workshops:** check who is called an "artist" and who a *meșter* in each setting.
- **Oltenia's woven *scoarțe* and Maramureș carving:** compare how a weaver or carver signs, varies or repeats a family motif.
- **The Moți of the Apuseni:** check coopers' and woodworkers' records for whether a maker's mark mattered when wares were sold in the plains.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** check monastery workshops and lay craftspeople for who trains whom.
- **Timișoara and the Banat:** look at the 2023 European Capital of Culture program for which makers it showed, Serbian and Swabian among them.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** look for studies of Tatar embroidery and of Lipovan icon painting, and how each is taught.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check whether Roma makers are named as authors in museum catalogs or filed as a group.
- **Saxons and Swabians, Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Serbs, Slovaks, Armenians:** check community museums for named makers.
- **Women and men:** check which crafts are filed as women's "handwork" and which as men's "art" in exhibition records.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; check how embroidery is claimed in Romanian and in Hungarian sources, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** the Kalotaszeg embroidery of Transylvania's Hungarians, to tell shared motifs from Romanian ones; Moldova's craft revival; Paris-based Romanian artists, only to see how fame abroad fed back home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk art, music and literature debates; mixed for invention and DIY; thin for Tatar, Armenian and Slovak makers.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and Church Slavonic for older *Erminia* copies; German for Saxon furniture studies; Tatar for embroidery terms; English for ERIAC's material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "who owns a motif" touches disputes between villages, museums and fashion firms; give each side as a claim.
- **Held for review:** Cucuteni pottery came up as a candidate and stayed out, because popular claims about prehistoric "national" creativity feed the contested continuity debate rather than this concept.
