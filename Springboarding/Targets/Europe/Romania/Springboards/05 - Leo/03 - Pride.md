# Romania: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani; Saxon German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia (Craiova) and Bucharest:** compare fan chants and local press for how each side words its pride against the other.
- **Timișoara in the Banat and Oradea in Crișana:** check town museums and tourist offices for which "firsts" each city claims.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** check how the memory of Stephen the Great and the painted monasteries is used in regional pride.
- **Transylvania's Romanian towns (Alba Iulia, Sibiu, Brașov) and the Moți:** check how the 1848 and 1918 memories shape local pride.
- **Hunedoara and the Jiu Valley:** set miners' feast-day speeches beside the closure years.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** check Tatar, Turkish and Lipovan events for which emblems each displays.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check April 8 events for which groups take part, and in whose words.
- **Saxons and Swabians, Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Serbs, Banat Bulgarians, Armenians, the Jewish community:** look for each community's songs and emblems of its own.
- **Neo-Protestant and Orthodox congregations:** check sermons for how each treats pride as sin and pride in one's people.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; check whose pride each camp claims to speak for, giving each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Székely flags and Hungarian national days in Transylvania, which belong to Hungary's row, set beside December 1 only to see the contrast; Moldova's language-day pride; the diaspora's flag displays in Italy and Spain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols and ideology; mixed for polling (firm surveys vary in method); thin for Tatar, Hutsul and Banat Bulgarian symbols.
- **Languages needed:** old Romanian Cyrillic for early *Hronicul* copies; German for the Saxon song's history; Romani for "Gelem, Gelem" variants; Tatar for *tamga* use.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin claims (continuity and settlement) and protochronism are contested; give each as a claim. Minority emblem disputes can become legal; that part goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** online forums that boast of an ancient Dacian past were looked at as a window on hubristic pride; since they spread contested origin claims as fact, they belong in a later, careful query and are not listed as sources here.
