## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia against Oltenia and Moldavia (Iași):** check honorary-citizen lists for how far local sons and daughters crowd out national names.
- **Bukovina (Suceava) and Maramureș:** check which village priests, teachers and craftspeople are named on plaques and in local monographs.
- **Banat and Crișana towns (Timișoara, Lugoj, Oradea):** look at street names after 1990 for whether Serbian, Swabian and Jewish figures were added.
- **Transylvania's Romanian towns (Blaj, Alba Iulia, Sibiu, Cluj) and the Moți:** set the Micu-Klein and 1918 memorials beside each other for whom they credit.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** check the Tatar, Turkish and Lipovan seats in parliament and what each group says the seat gives it.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check August 2 and February 20 events for which Roma voices are invited to speak.
- **Saxons and Swabians, Ukrainians and Hutsuls, Serbs, Banat Bulgarians, Slovaks and Czechs, Armenians, the Jewish community:** check which have a reserved seat and which share or lack one.
- **Women and men:** compare Academy membership and award lists by sex across decades.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; check claims about recognition as a separate group, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarian party and its demands, which sit in Hungary's row, read only to see how recognition is asked for; Moldova's academy and prizes; diaspora figures honored at home after success abroad.
