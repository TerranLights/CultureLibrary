## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia and Maramureș:** compare how outlaw ballads tell the giving robber, and which local figure each region prefers.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** compare donor names on new churches in migrant-sending villages with older founder lists.
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** check the city's share of crowdfunding and tax redirection against rural counties.
- **Transylvania's Romanian towns (Cluj, Sibiu, Brașov) and the Banat (Timișoara):** check community-foundation grants for who gives and to what.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** compare Muslim alms at the feasts with Lipovan and Orthodox alms at Easter.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** check memorial gift-feasts and giving at Pentecostal services in Roma writers' own accounts.
- **Ukrainian villages of Maramureș and the Hutsuls of Bukovina:** check New Year carols of plenty for what the household gives back.
- **Saxons and Swabians, Serbs, Slovaks, Armenians, the Jewish community:** check each community's charity funds for who gives to whom.
- **The four church families (Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic, neo-Protestant):** compare collections, tithes and alms side by side.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; check church giving in Catholic villages as each side records it.
- **Comparison only:** church collections among Transylvania's Hungarians, which belong to Hungary's row, read only as a contrast in giving to a minority church; Moldova's charity appeals; diaspora associations abroad, only for the money they send home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church giving, ballads and tax redirection; mixed for informal giving between kin; thin for Lipovan, Serbian and Armenian charity.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and Church Slavonic for the Athos charters; Ukrainian for the carols of plenty; Turkish and Tatar for *Kurban* sharing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** donors and recipients named in parish and charity records are living people; aggregate first.
