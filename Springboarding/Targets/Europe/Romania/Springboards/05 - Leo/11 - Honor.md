# Romania: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia and Muntenia against Moldavia (Iași):** compare *Toma Alimoș* variants for how the insult and the revenge are told.
- **Maramureș and Bukovina (Suceava):** check wedding-custom studies for whether honor displays are still held, and how younger brides talk of them.
- **Transylvania's Romanian villages (Mărginimea Sibiului, Țara Făgărașului, the Apuseni):** check fair and sheepfold customs for how a man's word is tested.
- **Mixed villages of the Banat and Crișana:** check Romanian, Serbian and Swabian memoirs for how insults between neighbors were settled.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** compare *namus* talk among Tatars and Turks with Lipovan church rules on family conduct.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, Boyash):** compare honor terms group by group, in Roma authors' own words.
- **Saxon, Ukrainian, Hutsul, Banat Bulgarian and Slovak communities:** check local histories for how a dishonored member was treated.
- **The Jewish community and Armenians:** check memoirs for how family standing and a good name were kept in trade and marriage.
- **Women and men:** test, without assuming, whether a woman's and a man's honor rest on different conduct.
- **Comparison only:** the honor codes of Transylvania's Hungarian gentry, which belong to Hungary's row, read only to see what Romanian boyar and peasant codes were set against; Moldova's ballads of the same heroes; Aromanian shepherd codes, for neighboring pastoral honor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ballads, novels and military rites; mixed for wedding and fair customs; thin for Tatar, Banat Bulgarian and Slovak honor terms.
- **Languages needed:** old Romanian Cyrillic for oath documents; Russian and English for the Pacepa debate; Turkish and Tatar for *namus*; Romani for *patjiv*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wedding-night customs and family honor touch women's bodies and possible violence; women's accounts first, no individuals named. Defamation and insult law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** a pointer on killings in the name of honor was considered and not written; no Romanian case series could be named with confidence, and a heading without data would suggest a pattern, so the question stays inside the bride's-shirt row.
