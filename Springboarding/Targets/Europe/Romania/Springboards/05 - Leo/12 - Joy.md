## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** compare *Drăgaica* and *Sânziene* customs for who takes part, girls or whole villages.
- **Moldavia (Iași, Vrancea) and Bukovina (Suceava):** check wake games and Easter customs for where laughter is allowed beside grief.
- **Cluj and Transylvania's Romanian villages (the Apuseni, the Mărginimea Sibiului):** compare festival-goers with village dance days.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Maramureș:** compare folk-music dedications by region on the channels' request lines.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** check Turkish and Tatar *Bayram* festivities and Lipovan Easter for how each shows gladness.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** compare joy at Pentecostal services and at family feasts, in Roma accounts.
- **Saxons and Swabians, Serbs, Banat Bulgarians, Slovaks and Czechs, Armenians:** check community calendars for their merriest days.
- **Ukrainians and Hutsuls:** check how Easter dances differ between Maramureș and Bukovina villages.
- **Neo-Protestant and Orthodox households:** compare where joy is expected (worship, Easter) and where restraint is (Lent, the Sabbath).
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** contested ground; for joy, compare the Romanian-language and Hungarian-language records of their feast days and dances, and log who made each recording and to what end, each account as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian village festivals of Transylvania, which belong to Hungary's row, read only to tell shared feast days from Romanian ones; Moldova's *Mărțișor* festival; diaspora Easter and New Year parties abroad.

## 4. Flags

- **Sensitive or restricted:** wake customs touch grieving families; mental-health data are sensitive; aggregate only.
