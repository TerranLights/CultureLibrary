# Romania: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (with Moldavian, Oltenian and Banat usage); Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Armenian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest against Mioveni and Ploiești:** set the capital's office hours beside Muntenian car-plant and refinery shifts.
- **Oltenia (Gorj, Dolj):** check lignite towns such as Motru and Rovinari for what replaced mine wages.
- **Moldavia (Iași, Vaslui, Botoșani):** check how much household income comes from work elsewhere, in INS and remittance data.
- **Bukovina and Maramureș:** follow forestry jobs and Ukrainian and Hutsul earning trips across seasons.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** compare dock and shipyard work with Turkish and Tatar farm households and Delta fishing.
- **Banat and Crișana:** check Timișoara's foreign-owned plants, Reșița's steel past and Banat Bulgarian and Serb farming for how wage work reached villages.
- **Transylvania's Romanians:** separate Hunedoara steelworkers, Jiu Valley miners, Cluj programmers and the woodworking villages of the Apuseni.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Rudari, Ursari, Boyash and others):** check labor statistics for whose work goes uncounted, beside Roma researchers' accounts.
- **Baptist, Pentecostal and Adventist congregations:** check whether church ties are used to find jobs at home and abroad.
- **The Jewish community's history:** read artisan records of Moldavian towns apart from the 1940–44 labor detachments.
- **The Csángó:** seasonal work studies exist in Romanian and in Hungarian; contested ground, every account a claim.
- **Comparison only:** commuting from the Székely counties to jobs in Hungary, a route on Hungary's row; Moldova, for a second labor-migration pattern; Romanian pickers in Huelva and carers in Italy, for how earnings abroad are talked about at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** abundant on labor statistics, strikes and mining; patchy for informal and household work; scarce on the working lives of Banat Bulgarians, Czechs and Armenians.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the old *muncă* texts; English for Kideckel; Ukrainian for *zarobitky* talk in Maramureș; Hebrew and Yiddish for some labor-detachment testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Canal and Holocaust-era forced labor name victims and guards; the 1933 strike's memory is contested, since the later regime retold it. Labor law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Hungarian-language field studies of Csángó day labor were considered and kept for the comparison side only, until the author rules on how Csángó ground is handled.
