*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** set the shop clerk, the office cleaner and the live-in carer side by side.
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** trace which villages sent daughters into town service before 1940 and after.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina (Iași, Suceava):** check lay helpers at Putna, Neamț and Sucevița next to the monks' obedience.
- **Dobruja (Constanța):** compare resort service work with imams' and muezzins' service in Tatar and Turkish mosques.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** look at Catholic and Serbian Orthodox charities for how each staffs its service.
- **Maramureș:** check the Greek Catholic and Ukrainian cantors who lead village worship when no priest is present.
- **Transylvania's Romanians (Sibiu, Alba Iulia, the Moți):** read the *Serviciul Social* reports on the villages served.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** after the 1990 emigration, check who now keeps and opens the fortified churches.
- **Roma:** read the *robie* duty lists beside Roma historians' work, and look at Roma mediators in schools and clinics as a present role.
- **Lipovans, Armenians, the Jewish community:** compare which offices each still fills with volunteers.
- **The Csángó:** the language in which Catholic priests serve Csángó parishes is disputed, so every account of it stays a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarian Catholic and Reformed charities (Hungary's row), to see how far churches split service by language; Moldova's military service, against Romania's draft suspended in 2007 *check*; Romanian *badante* in Italy, for service done abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** good on court offices, monasteries and volunteering surveys; uneven on domestic service; slight for Banat Bulgarian, Slovak and Czech service roles.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for older Lipovan service books; Greek for some court-office records; Hebrew and Yiddish for older Jewish welfare records; Romani for mediators' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *robie* inventories come only from owners and the church, so Roma voices must be sought elsewhere; soldiers' labor memories name units and officers. Public-service rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Turkish-language schools tied to a religious movement that calls itself by the word *hizmet* were weighed as a lead for Dobruja and put aside as politically contested, to be raised with the author first.
