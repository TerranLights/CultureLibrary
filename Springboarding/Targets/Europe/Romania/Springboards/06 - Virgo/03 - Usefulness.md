## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dolj and the wider south:** check whether the elders' tasks in Sorescu's village match what SHARE records for Oltenia and Muntenia.
- **Iași, Suceava and the Moldavian villages:** see how families count grandparents' child-minding when parents work in another town.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** look at how Tatar, Turkish and Lipovan households hand down boat, net and kitchen skills.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** set the dual-school firms beside what Swabian, Serb and Slovak families taught at home.
- **Maramureș and Bukovina's uplands:** follow the working horse and the woodcutter's tools among Romanians, Ukrainians and Hutsuls.
- **Transylvania's Romanians (Brașov, Cluj, the Apuseni, Hunedoara):** compare what ASTRA's booklets taught with what Moți and Jiu Valley families say a young man must know.
- **The Rudari and other Roma groups:** check which wooden and metal household goods are still made, bought and used, asking makers first.
- **Saxons and Swabians who stayed after 1990:** check the roles older people took on when the young emigrated.
- **Adventist, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** check what tasks each gives to older and disabled members.
- **Armenians and the Jewish community:** look at what roles elders hold in shrinking communities.
- **Csángó villages:** contested ground; Romanian and Hungarian accounts differ on which skills mark these households, and both stay claims.
- **Comparison only:** trade schooling in Hungarian in Transylvania (Hungary's row), as a test of the dual-school model; Moldova's pensioners, under a different pension system; parents working in Italy or Spain, whose view of the grandparents at home is a diaspora question.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** good for folklore and surveys of older people; uneven for disability and work; little for Lipovan and Banat Bulgarian household skills.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian Cyrillic for Șincai's manuscript; Ukrainian for the Hutsul stud's local writing; Romani and Boyash for the Rudari makers' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability and the old touch dignity and care; the socialist sorting of children as "irrecoverable" belongs to the history but must be handled with survivors' voices.
- **Held for review:** Roma recycling and scrap work was considered as a pointer for thrift and set aside, since naming it here could pin a trade on a people; the author may want it approached through Roma-led studies.
