# Romania: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Ukrainian (with Hutsul song forms); Crimean Tatar and Turkish; German (Banat and Saxon press); Serbian; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, and the forms this takes: feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The capital set beside Muntenian and Oltenian county towns:** check who owns the local papers and whether they criticize their own mayors.
- **Iași and Moldavia:** follow the literary reviews published there for how harsh a review may be, decade by decade.
- **Bukovina and Maramureș:** collect Ukrainian and Hutsul *kolomyiky* that tease neighbors, beside Romanian teasing verse in the same villages.
- **Dobruja:** compare Tatar *mane* with what Lipovan elders say about correcting the young.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana:** read German-language and Romanian papers on the same city hall for what each dares to say.
- **Transylvania's Romanians (Cluj, Sibiu, Alba Iulia, Hunedoara):** check student-press and Jiu Valley union statements for criticism aimed upward.
- **Saxon parishes:** check whether church-council minutes still record rebukes now that congregations are small.
- **Roma:** check how Roma activists answer press criticism of Roma, in their own outlets and statements.
- **Orthodox and Greek Catholic parishes:** compare confession manuals with sermons for how each church teaches self-examination.
- **The Jewish community:** look for satire in Romanian Jewish periodicals before 1940.
- **The Csángó:** both Romanian and Hungarian actors criticize each other's policy toward Csángó villages; contested, every charge a claim.
- **Comparison only:** satire written in Hungarian in Cluj and the Székely counties, which falls to Hungary's row, useful only for spotting politicians mocked in both languages; Moldova's press, for a neighbor under other pressures; diaspora Facebook groups, for criticism of the home country from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for satire, press and party ritual; mixed for school and family correction; thin for Tatar, Serbian and Armenian forms.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian Cyrillic for Varlaam's 1645 text; Crimean Tatar for *mane*; Ukrainian for *kolomyiky*; German for the Banat press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** party self-criticism minutes name living people; journalists named in *FreeEx* cases may still be under threat. Libel and insult statutes belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Ion Budai-Deleanu's *Țiganiada* was weighed as the early classic of Romanian satire and held, because it satirizes through Roma figures; the author decides whether it enters a sheet at all.
