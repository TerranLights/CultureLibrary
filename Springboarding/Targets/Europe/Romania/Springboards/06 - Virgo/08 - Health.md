## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oltenia (Dolj, Olt) and Muntenia:** check whether *Căluș* healing survives anywhere beyond the stage, and how villagers explain it.
- **Bucharest:** set private-clinic users beside state-hospital patients for how each describes good care.
- **Moldavia (Iași, Vaslui) and Bukovina (Suceava):** compare distances to a doctor with the use of *vraci* and charms, county by county.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** weigh Constanța's HIV cohort, Tatar and Turkish breathing prayers and Lipovan villages reached only by boat.
- **The Banat and Crișana:** ask how Swabian, Serb and Banat Bulgarian households remember their village doctors.
- **Maramureș:** follow Ukrainian and Romanian herb gatherers and what they sell to Plafar and to private firms.
- **Transylvania's Romanians (Cluj, Sibiu, Hunedoara, the Apuseni):** check Turda's salt mine, Jiu Valley miners' lung disease and Apuseni villages' distance from care.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, Boyash and others):** read health mediators' own reports and Roma-led studies before any outside survey.
- **Adventists and Pentecostals:** check how health teaching (diet, abstinence, prayer for healing) shapes doctor visits, in their own words.
- **The Jewish community:** trace Jewish hospitals in Iași and Bucharest, and the 1940–44 bars on Jewish doctors.
- **The Csángó:** healing charms among them have been recorded in two languages by rival schools; contested, each record a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Sovata and Praid in the Székely counties (Hungary's row), as a check on salt and spa cures; Moldova's TB and vaccine figures, for a neighbor's system; Romanian doctors working in Germany, France and the UK, for what their leaving means at home.

## 4. Flags
