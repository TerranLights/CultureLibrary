*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest, Muntenia, Oltenia:** check from county registries whether civil and church weddings fall on one day or apart.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** look for dowry lists in family papers and court files.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** set Muslim, Lipovan and Orthodox marriage records side by side for the same towns.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** find Serb, Swabian, Bulgarian and Slovak registers that log mixed marriages.
- **Maramureș:** compare Romanian and Ukrainian wedding orders recorded in neighboring valleys.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Cluj, Sibiu's Mărginime, Alba Iulia, the Moți, Făgăraș, the Jiu Valley):** trace customary and Saxon-law survivals zone by zone.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** seek each group's own account of how a match is agreed.
- **Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic and neo-Protestant churches:** compare rules on mixed marriage and remarriage.
- **Jewish and Armenian communities:** look for marriage registers in Iași, Suceava and Gherla.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** treat Romanian and Hungarian wedding studies as rival claims.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose marriage law goes to Hungary's row; Moldova, which shares the old Moldavian codes but not the modern ones; diaspora couples in Italy and Spain, where two legal systems meet in one divorce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes and wedding folklore; mixed for disputes settled outside courts; thin for Czech, Slovak and Banat Bulgarian marriage material.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian and Slavonic for the 1640 and 1652 codes; Latin and early modern German for the Saxon statute book; Turkish for the Muftiat; Church Slavonic for Lipovan wedding books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** early marriage among some Roma groups is argued over inside and outside the communities; let Roma women speak first. Law's spirit, obedience and trust in courts belong to Capricorn.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for present-day Romania, with the Hungarians of Transylvania and Székely Land kept for Hungary's row.
- **Held for review:** cross-national economic-game studies of fairness were weighed, but no study that sampled Romanian players could be named with confidence.
