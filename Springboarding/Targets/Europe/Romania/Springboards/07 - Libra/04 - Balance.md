## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia against Oltenia:** check whether Gorj and Dolj weavers kept the same symmetry rules as Muntenian workshops.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** look at how fasting days are kept in towns against villages, from church and press surveys.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** set Ramadan, Orthodox and Lipovan fasting calendars side by side for one town, such as Tulcea or Medgidia.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** find where the minority-language threshold brings Serbian, Slovak or German signs and where it falls short.
- **Maramureș and Bukovina's Ukrainian villages:** check the same threshold from the Ukrainian side and in Hutsul villages.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Brașov, Sibiu's Mărginime, Făgăraș, the Apuseni):** compare villages that ask for bear culls with those that profit from bear-watching.
- **The Jiu Valley and Hunedoara:** see how shift rotas in the mines set the balance of work and rest in miners' accounts.
- **Roma groups:** look for what Roma families say about balancing seasonal work away with home, in their own testimony.
- **Greek Catholics, Roman Catholics and Adventists:** compare their fasting and rest rules with the Orthodox calendar.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check whether field-rotation rules outlasted collectivization in their former villages.
- **Csángó:** treat claims about their fasting and festival year as contested between Romanian and Hungarian authors.
- **Comparison only:** Székely Land, where the language threshold mostly concerns Hungarian and so falls to Hungary's row; Moldova, which has its own separation-of-powers crises; Romanians in Italy, whose working hours abroad bear on balance at home only as context.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional rulings and statutes; mixed for folk ideas of bodily balance; thin for Tatar and Romani words for moderation.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian and Turkish for the old *oca* and *dram* (Ottoman-era units); German for Saxon field rules; Ukrainian and Serbian for bilingual-sign disputes; Russian for Lipovan fast rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2012 referendum and the bear laws are still fought over; give each side its own source. What the separation of powers is for, beyond its text, is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** for bodily balance, an import of the hot-and-cold scheme known from other folk medicines was tempting; it is not set as a frame, since Romanian sources should name their own pairs first.
