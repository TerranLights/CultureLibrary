# Romania: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Latin (Transylvanian estates' law); German; Romani; Ukrainian; Turkish; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest against county towns:** check ANI findings per county for how often a mayor's or council's relatives appear in hiring cases.
- **Oltenia and Muntenia:** look at court-of-appeal statistics for recusal requests granted.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** look for the interwar jury verdicts that drew charges of bias in political or antisemitic trials.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** check how Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan applicants fare in local civil-service exams, from their associations' own reports.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** compare recusal practice in courts where judges and parties may share a minority language.
- **Maramureș and Bukovina's Ukrainians:** find whether exams in Ukrainian-language schools are graded in the same centers as the rest.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Alba Iulia, Cluj, Sibiu):** trace how the *Approbatae* status is taught locally and how the 1791 petition answered it.
- **Roma groups:** let Roma organizations report how situation tests were run and who conducted them.
- **Greek Catholic Romanians:** follow the 1948 abolition of their church through the legal acts that ended its recognition.
- **The Jewish community:** set the 1940 decree-law beside the 1866 and 1923 constitutional texts for how equal treatment was written and unwritten.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** look at the Saxon *Universitas* and its own officials' elections as a separate legal estate before 1876.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarian estates and Székely seats, which are Hungary's row, matter here only as the other side of the *Unio*; Moldova's courts, for how a neighbor wrote its own rules on assigning judges.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for modern statutes, ANI decisions and the 1989 trial; mixed for interwar jury records; thin for minority-language exam grading.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for the *Unio* and the *Approbatae*; French for the Berlin treaty text; German for the Saxon *Universitas* records; Romani for the testers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Transylvanian estates' order, the 1940 laws, the 1989 trial and the 2024 annulment are contested; give each claim its source. Whether officials are trusted to be fair is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** for bias in judging, a well-known foreign study of judges' rulings came up and is not used here, since it says nothing about Romanian courts.
