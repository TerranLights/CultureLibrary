*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** count reconciliation-ended cases in Bucharest courts against county courts, where court statistics allow.
- **Oltenia (Craiova, Gorj):** look for county police figures on withdrawn family-violence complaints, where published.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** read the June 1940 cession through Bukovina's Romanian, Ukrainian and Jewish families' memoirs, each on its own.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** find how Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan community bodies settle quarrels without courts, from their own records.
- **The Banat and Crișana:** check Serb and Slovak village memory of the 1951 Bărăgan deportations and what was kept unsaid afterward.
- **Maramureș:** compare Romanian and Ukrainian church records of conscientious objectors.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Cluj, Alba Iulia, Brașov, the Jiu Valley):** gather how Greek Catholic families describe the choices the 1948 forced union put to them.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** look at how complaints against police are withdrawn or pursued, with Roma organizations' documentation first.
- **Neo-Protestants (Baptists, Pentecostals, Adventists):** check how each church taught members to answer communist pressure, in their own histories.
- **The Jewish community:** set the community's 1940s petitions to the regime beside survivors' accounts.
- **The Csángó:** treat accounts of their schooling quarrels as contested between Romanian and Hungarian claims.
- **Comparison only:** the 1940 Vienna Award belongs to Hungary's row and is read only for Bucharest's choice not to fight; Moldova's memory of June 1940; diaspora workers who report keeping quiet about abuse abroad, as a pattern outside these lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, the 1940 record and church persecution histories; mixed for family-violence practice; thin for how Dobruja's communities settle quarrels.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Romanian for Neagoe's counsel; Ottoman Turkish for tribute registers; Ukrainian and Yiddish for Bukovina memoirs; Serbian and Slovak for Bărăgan memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1940 cessions, the 1948 forced union of the Greek Catholic Church with the Orthodox, family violence and harassment are painful and contested; give each claim its source and protect victims' names. What peace and order are for is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** essays that read a national character into *Miorița* were passed over; they make claims about a nation rather than point to sources for this concept.
