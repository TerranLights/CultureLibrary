*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** read capital newspapers in 1990 for whom they blamed for each street clash, set against later court findings.
- **Oltenia:** check local lists of "class enemies" in Dolj and Gorj against families' memoirs.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** read the 1941 pogrom's official blame of the victims against survivors' testimony.
- **Dobruja (Constanța, Tulcea):** look for how Turkish and Tatar community papers respond when the national press blames Muslims.
- **The Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana:** compare Swabian and Serb accounts of the 1951 deportation lists that named them a risk.
- **Maramureș and Bukovina's Ukrainians:** check whether 1940s security files called Ukrainian villages disloyal, and on what grounds.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities and the Jiu Valley:** follow how miners were blamed and how they blamed others after 1990.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and others):** start with Roma authors on collective blame, and on how their own communities explain misfortune.
- **Lipovans:** check how the Russian state's old label of schismatic appears in their own histories.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** read the 1945 deportation orders through their community's records.
- **The Jewish community:** set the press of the late 1930s beside community archives of the same years.
- **Comparison only:** Romanian-Hungarian clashes at Târgu Mureș (1990), whose Hungarian side is Hungary's row, read only for each side's charge against the other; Moldova's media on Romania; the diaspora as a target of blame in Romanian politics.
