# Romania: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Lipovan Russian; Ukrainian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish and Hebrew (historical); Ancient Greek for the classical source. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending, where an old self, place or way dies and something renewed takes its place: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia, Oltenia and Dobruja:** map *Caloian* records by village to see where the burial-and-return rite survived longest.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** compare painted *Anastasis* scenes between monasteries, and ask Suceava's Ukrainian parishes for their Easter order.
- **Sibiu, the Mărginime and the Apuseni:** follow *Oastea Domnului* from its Sibiu start into villages, testing whether members kept apart from parish life.
- **Banat (Timișoara) and Crișana (Oradea):** read Baptist and Pentecostal church rolls in Oradea, Arad and Timiș to see where adult baptisms cluster.
- **Roma groups:** compare converts' accounts among Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari families with those who stayed Orthodox.
- **Lipovans of the Delta:** compare newcomer rules in priestly and priestless communities.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** set 1980s confirmation records against today's.
- **Jewish community:** look for *tshuve* and Jewish revival since 1989 in Bucharest's community records.
- **Csángó:** treat Catholic renewal stories from Romanian and Hungarian church authors as competing claims.
- **Comparison only:** Székely Land's Unitarian and Reformed revivals go to Hungary's row; Moldova's evangelical churches are a parallel only; returnees from Spain and Italy who call their move home a fresh start are diaspora context.
