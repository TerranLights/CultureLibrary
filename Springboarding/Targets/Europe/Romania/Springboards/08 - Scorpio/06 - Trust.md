## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare who lost savings in Bancorex and other 1990s failures with who kept trust in state banks.
- **Oltenia:** study village shop notebooks in Dolj and Gorj for credit given to neighbors and kin.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** ask Ukrainian and Hutsul villages how deals are sealed, with or without paper.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** compare how Turkish, Tatar, Lipovan and Romanian traders in Constanța and Tulcea rely on a given word; sources are thin, so start with community unions.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** set Swabian and Serbian credit traditions beside Romanian ones in the same villages.
- **Crișana (Arad and Oradea):** look for local readings of Slavici's novella in its county.
- **Maramureș:** check whether handshake deals at livestock fairs are still the norm and who witnesses them.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Sibiu, Brașov, Cluj, the Moți, Făgăraș):** trace Romanian village credit beside the Saxon cooperatives.
- **Roma groups:** collect each group's words for trust and credit; Kalderash and Rudari usage may diverge.
- **Armenians and the Jewish community:** compare trade networks kept by kin and faith with the town at large.
- **Csángó:** treat claims about whom they trust, church or state, as contested between Romanian and Hungarian authors.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian savers in Cluj's Caritas scheme go to Hungary's row; Moldova's bank fraud of 2014 is a separate case; parcel-van drivers serving the diaspora carry trust across borders, as context.
