## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** compare *Corbea* and *Novac* variants from Wallachian collectors with those from elsewhere, noting where payback is kept or cut.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** use *Baltagul*'s mountain setting to look for real records of shepherd killings and their aftermath in Neamț and Suceava.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** look in Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan sources for how quarrels end without courts; sources are scarce.
- **Banat and Crișana:** check *iertăciuni* collections from Timiș and Bihor, and Serbian Banat customs of reconciliation.
- **Maramureș:** compare Romanian and Ukrainian ways of answering an insult at dances and fairs.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Sibiu's Mărginime, Alba Iulia, the Apuseni and Moți, Hunedoara):** look at village fights between neighboring lads and how priests and elders stopped them.
- **Roma groups:** set Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari accounts of ending feuds beside press stories of "clan wars", treating the press as a source to examine.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check church and neighborhood records for how quarrels were settled before they grew.
- **Neo-Protestant congregations:** ask how converts describe giving up old grudges.
- **Csángó:** read accounts of village conflict from Romanian and Hungarian authors as separate claims.
- **Comparison only:** feud and reconciliation customs in Transylvania's Hungarian villages go to Hungary's row; Moldova's haiduc songs of payback are a parallel; quarrels carried abroad by diaspora families are context.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ballads and political violence; mixed for village feuds; thin for Turkish, Tatar, Lipovan and Serbian ways of getting even.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian ballad speech for *Corbea* and *Novac* variants; Turkish and Tatar for the Constanța press; Romani for feud-ending accounts; Ukrainian for Maramureș insult customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Legionary movement and the Jilava killings are contested; mob justice and "clan" coverage carry anti-Roma bias. Private vengeance against state punishment belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** militia detective novels of the 1970s–80s probably carry revenge plots, but no title came to mind with certainty, so the genre is noted without a pointer.
