## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** use CNSAS yearly reports to see from which counties people ask to read their files.
- **Oltenia:** follow *călușari* troupes from Olt and Dolj and ask what each keeps from outsiders.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and the Csángó villages:** read census declarations against church registers, presenting each count as a claim.
- **Bukovina and Maramureș:** look at how Ukrainian villages spoke in front of officials, and whether a "kitchen language" kept matters private.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** check Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan ways of keeping community matters inside, such as closed meetings or community-only languages.
- **Banat and Crișana:** compare Swabian and Serbian memories of hiding letters from abroad during the 1980s.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities (Sibiu, Brașov, Cluj, Alba Iulia):** set Saxon lodge records against Romanian membership after 1990.
- **Roma groups:** let Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari speakers describe for themselves what is kept from outsiders, and do not publish what they ask to keep.
- **Greek Catholic families:** look for the hidden practice of a banned church in family memory; name each source.
- **Jewish community:** check how families hid or revealed origins after the war, from community oral histories.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian-language Securitate files on Transylvania's Hungarians belong to Hungary's row; Moldova's KGB archives are a parallel; adoptees raised in Italy or the US are diaspora, met only when they search here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state secrecy and files; mixed for family secrets and privacy attitudes; thin for Tatar, Lipovan and Serbian practices of keeping things private.
- **Languages needed:** 16th-century Cyrillic-script Romanian for Neacșu; German and Latin for the Sibiu lodge; Romani for in-group speech; Ukrainian for Bukovina testimonies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** CNSAS files name living people; clergy and informers are fought over; Roma and *călușari* may keep knowledge private, and that limit is respected. State-secrets law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the idea that Ukrainian villages kept a "kitchen language" for private talk rests on a hunch, not a source, so it stays a question in the bullets rather than a pointer.
