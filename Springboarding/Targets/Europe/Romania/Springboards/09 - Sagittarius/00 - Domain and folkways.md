*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** set the capital's blank and "no religion" census answers against rural communes.
- **Oltenia:** check how Roma churches in Dolj and Gorj show in census figures as against the churches' own rolls.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** compare Iași's diocesan institutions, Suceava's monasteries and the Catholic villages near Roman.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** map mosques, Lipovan churches and Orthodox parishes side by side in Constanța and Tulcea.
- **Banat and Crișana:** list Serbian, Swabian Catholic, Baptist and Orthodox parishes sharing one town in Timiș or Bihor.
- **Maramureș:** Greek Catholic and Orthodox parishes in one village, and Ukrainian parishes in the Vișeu valley.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Blaj, Sibiu and Alba Iulia as church seats; the Moți and the Jiu Valley to test what freedom petitions meant below the elite.
- **Roma groups:** record church membership for Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and other groups separately, never as one Roma figure.
- **Jews and Armenians:** check which synagogues and Armenian churches in Iași, Suceava and Gherla still hold services.
- **Csángó:** read Bacău parish records through both Romanian and Hungarian scholarship, each as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** the Székely Land and Transylvania's Hungarians, whose Reformed and Unitarian churches share these towns (Hungary's row); Moldova, where church jurisdiction is disputed; Aromanian churches; diaspora parishes in Italy and Spain, the far away seen from its other end.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census religion and church history; mixed for freedom ways, read out of petitions and village studies; thin for Slovak, Czech and Banat Bulgarian church life.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script old Romanian for Coresi; Latin for the *Supplex*; German for the Sibiu archive; Church Slavonic for the Brăila metropolis; Turkish for the Muftiate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1948 suppression of the Greek Catholic Church and later property disputes; some interwar thinkers' Legionary ties; outsiders' accounts of Roma conversion. Recognition law: Libra; church-and-state ideals: Capricorn.
- **Lands:** which lands Romania will carry is not yet settled, so this sign is written for the present state.
- **Held for review:** for the map of newer religious movements, South Asian-derived movements and Buddhist groups active in Romania came up; both are held as categories for the author.
