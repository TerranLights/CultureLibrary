# Romania: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani (Vlax varieties); German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia and Bucharest:** weigh weekly attendance against crowds at relic processions, two different measures.
- **Oltenia and Hunedoara:** compare pilgrims at Prislop with the parish life of the towns they come from.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** look at monastery confessors (*duhovnici*) as keepers of lay faith around Neamț and Suceava.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** separate Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan practice by village before reading any county figure.
- **Banat and Crișana:** read neo-Protestant and Orthodox testimonies from Arad and Bihor side by side for how trust in God is put into words.
- **Maramureș:** set Greek Catholic memories of 1948 beside Orthodox parish histories in the same villages; add Ukrainian parishes.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** compare Sibiu's Oastea circles, Blaj's Greek Catholics and Moți villages.
- **Roma groups:** keep a Romani-speaking Pentecostal church, Orthodox Ursari families and Rudari Baptists as separate accounts.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** ask what became of parish devotion after the emigration of the 1990s.
- **Jews:** read faith before 1944 through Sighet and Satu Mare memorial books.
- **Csángó:** Catholic devotion in Bacău villages is studied from rival national frames; read both as claims.
- **Comparison only:** the Székely pilgrimage at Șumuleu Ciuc (Hungary's row), useful only for Romanian Catholics who attend it; Moldova's piety surveys as a parallel; diaspora parishes in Italy, for faith kept abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodox and Greek Catholic history; mixed for neo-Protestant and Roma faith; thin for Armenian, Slovak and Banat Bulgarian devotion.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Neagoe's first text; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Sighet and Satu Mare memorial books; Romani for the scripture translations; Turkish and Crimean Tatar for Dobruja's mosque notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1948 and the Greek Catholic memoirs; Securitate files on the Rugul Aprins (CNSAS access rules apply); claimed miracles at Prislop; the Holocaust behind the Hasidic material. Religious-freedom law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** reports of weeping icons spread on social media were weighed as a lead, but no stable collection of them could be named, so they stay a query for later.
