## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** set national TV news audiences against readers of fact-checks and independent sites.
- **Oltenia and Muntenia's villages:** look at who is believed in a dispute when there is no paper: the priest, the mayor, the oldest neighbor.
- **Moldavia:** test the presumptive mood and *zău* in Moldavian speech against Muntenian usage, with the dialect atlases.
- **Bukovina and Maramureș:** ask how Ukrainian parishes get news, and whether in Ukrainian or Romanian.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** check where Turkish and Tatar readers get news (Turkish broadcasters, *Hakses*, Romanian outlets).
- **Banat:** read Timișoara's 1989 accounts by Romanian, Serbian and Swabian witnesses side by side.
- **Crișana:** check vaccine and measles figures in Bihor against Cluj to see how near counties differ.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** see where the Dacianist claims draw audiences, and where teachers in Alba Iulia and Hunedoara (Sarmizegetusa's county) answer them.
- **Roma groups:** for Kalderash families, find out how an oath (*solax*) works in settling a fact, leaving the *kris* as a court to Libra.
- **Jews:** read the Holocaust-perception surveys beside the Iași community's own record of the 1941 pogrom.
- **Neo-Protestants:** ask how creation and evolution are taught in Baptist and Pentecostal schools.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian-language news in Transylvania (Hungary's row), whose readers form a separate audience; Moldova's Russian-language media space, a different contest over truth; diaspora news groups abroad, as a channel back into Romania.
