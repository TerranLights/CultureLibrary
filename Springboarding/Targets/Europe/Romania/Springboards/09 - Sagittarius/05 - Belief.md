# Romania: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (Bukovina and Moldavian folk terms included); Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia:** compare Bucharest's agnostic and humanist circles with the catechism taught in Ilfov parishes.
- **Oltenia:** look at belief in omens and forbidden days in Dolj villages against the almanacs sold there.
- **Moldavia:** the 1642 synod met in Iași; trace how Orthodox teaching there treats other confessions today.
- **Bukovina:** set Niculiță-Voronca's records against what elderly villagers near Suceava still report.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** line up Islamic, Old Rite and Orthodox catechesis for children in Tulcea and Constanța, one curriculum at a time.
- **Banat:** ask Serbs, Swabians and Romanians which folk beliefs they call their own and which the neighbors'.
- **Crișana:** Oradea's Baptist and Pentecostal churches are where the creed-and-membership question can be asked.
- **Maramureș:** Ukrainian and Romanian folk beliefs in neighboring valleys should be recorded separately.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Brașov for Honterus and the Schei parish, Cluj for Hoia-Baciu, Blaj for the Greek Catholic creed after the union with Rome.
- **Roma groups:** ask each group about omens and luck in its own Romani words; avoid outsiders' lists of "Gypsy superstition".
- **Jews:** look for collections of Yiddish folk belief from Moldavian towns before 1940.
- **Csángó:** studies of folk belief in Catholic Bacău villages come from rival national schools; keep both as claims.
- **Comparison only:** Székely Land folk-belief collections (Hungary's row), to see which beliefs cross a language line; Moldova's answers to the same Pew items; diaspora churches in Spain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for doctrine and folk-belief collections; mixed for surveys; thin for Ukrainian, Slovak and Armenian folk belief.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-c. Romanian for Varlaam's reply; Latin for Honterus; Romani for omen terms; Turkish for Muftiate textbooks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *sectant* is a charged label and is quoted only as others' word; the union with Rome and the 1948 suppression are contested. Freedom-of-belief law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** a television channel of fortune-tellers came up as a media pointer for credulity, but it is closer to divination and so is left for the Pisces sheets.
