## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** the capital's universities against the small-town colleges of Muntenia.
- **Oltenia:** Craiova's university, to see which degrees first-generation students from Dolj and Gorj villages choose.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** the oldest university's own histories, beside how Moldavian village families speak of sending a child there.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** look at the university's role in a region with Ukrainian and Polish minorities.
- **Dobruja (Constanța):** Ovidius University and Medgidia's school, for Romanian, Turkish and Tatar paths.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** the Polytechnic and West University, and Serbian and German-line students.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** a Baptist university and a state one in one city.
- **Maramureș:** Baia Mare's colleges and the Ukrainian lyceum, for first-generation students.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Cluj's Romanian line; Sibiu; Alba Iulia; Petroșani's mining university for the Jiu Valley.
- **Roma groups:** compare Roma students who used reserved places with those who did not; ask each group, not "the Roma".
- **Jews:** read the 1920s university accounts beside community schools after 1945.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** check German-line programs at Sibiu and Cluj and who now attends them.
- **Csángó:** schooling language for Bacău villages is contested; record both the Romanian-language and Hungarian-language programs' claims.
- **Comparison only:** the Hungarian line at Cluj and the Sapientia university (Hungary's row), which share cities with the schools above; Moldovan students in Romanian universities; Romanian academics in the diaspora.
