## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** check address forms toward bosses in ministries against commuter towns.
- **Oltenia:** see whether mayors and priests share or split local standing in Dolj and Gorj.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** trace monastery timekeeping into village routines.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** set Turkish, Tatar, Lipovan and Romanian ways of naming an elder side by side.
- **Banat and Crișana (Timișoara, Oradea):** compare Serb, Swabian, Slovak and Banat Bulgarian parish records of village office.
- **Maramureș:** read Ukrainian and Romanian valleys together for public respect toward the old.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** separate the shepherds' chain of command in Sibiu's Mărginime from the miners' in Hunedoara and the Jiu Valley and the guild towns of Brașov and Cluj; check the Moți, Făgăraș and Alba Iulia apart.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and others):** ask each who speaks for it before outsiders.
- **Each church (Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic, Baptist, Pentecostal, Adventist):** compare who gives orders in a parish and who elects them.
- **Saxons, Swabians, Armenians, the Jewish community:** follow their self-governing bodies before 1945.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** treat rival accounts of their parish authority as claims.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's and the Székely Land's Hungarians, whose councils go to Hungary's row; Moldova, sharing Moldavian court ranks but not the later state; the Aromanians; diaspora workers under foreign managers.
