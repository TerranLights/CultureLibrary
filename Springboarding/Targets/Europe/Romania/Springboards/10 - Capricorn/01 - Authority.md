# Romania: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** check whether trust in police and ministries reported by INSCOP differs from small-town answers.
- **Oltenia and Muntenia:** look at how the 1907 and 1989 memories frame obeying officials in Dolj and Teleorman.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** test whether monastery-goers in Neamț and Suceava rank clergy above state officers.
- **Dobruja:** compare how imams, Lipovan priests and mayors are asked to settle things in mixed villages.
- **Banat:** find Serb and Swabian accounts of Habsburg-era offices still cited as a yardstick.
- **Maramureș:** set Ukrainian and Romanian villages' accounts of the parish priest's say beside each other.
- **Hunedoara and the Jiu Valley:** read miners' union memoirs for orders taken and refused.
- **Sibiu's Mărginime and the Apuseni Moți:** check how shepherd and woodcutter families describe obeying a *baci* or a forest warden.
- **Roma groups:** look for each group's own description of who may tell whom what to do, beside police and NGO records.
- **Greek Catholics, Baptists, Pentecostals, Adventists:** compare how each congregation grants authority to its pastor or bishop.
- **Csángó:** treat who speaks for them before Church and state as disputed.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian Reformed and Catholic bishops in Transylvania, whose authority runs through Hungary's row; Moldova, where the same Orthodox texts meet another state; parents in the diaspora weighing Romanian and host-country rules on discipline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for polls and communist-era sources; mixed for workplaces; thin for Rudari, Lipovan and Banat Bulgarian views. The permission share is scored at Stage 2 by reading the law in force, as in Libra.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the inscriptions on votive paintings; Greek and Ottoman Turkish for investiture accounts; Romani for any Rudari interview kept in Boyash.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** prison data concerns real detainees; use aggregates. The 1968 speech and Ceaușescu's legitimacy are contested ground; give sources, not verdicts.
- **Held for review:** the 2024–25 annulled presidential election came up as a test of who is obeyed; the ruling sits on Libra's Impartiality sheet, and its effect on trust is too recent to source well here.
