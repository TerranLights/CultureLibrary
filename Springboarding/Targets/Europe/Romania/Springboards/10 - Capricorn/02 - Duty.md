*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muntenia and Bucharest:** compare caregiving duty among commuters with families still in villages.
- **Oltenia:** look at conscription memoirs and war memorials for which villages sent the most men in 1916–18.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina:** check monastery and parish records for vows and the duties they bind.
- **Dobruja:** set what Turkish, Tatar, Lipovan and Romanian households say a son owes his parents.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** check Swabian and Serb family histories for who stayed to care when others emigrated.
- **Crișana and Maramureș:** compare Romanian and Ukrainian Greek Catholic teaching on obligations to the parish.
- **The Apuseni Moți and Hunedoara's mining towns:** trace duties to family under mine work and its closure.
- **Mărginimea Sibiului and Făgăraș:** look at shepherd families for who keeps the flock when sons migrate.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari):** ask each group how duties to in-laws and the dead are fixed and enforced.
- **Adventists, Baptists and Pentecostals:** check Sabbath and pacifist teaching where it met army duty.
- **Lipovans and the Jewish community:** compare religious duties of fasting, prayer and burial in small congregations.
- **Csángó:** treat claims about their church duties and language of worship as contested.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarians, whose army and church obligations go to Hungary's row; Moldova, where sons faced Soviet conscription instead; diaspora children caring for parents from Italy or Spain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, statutes and church teaching; mixed for caregiving; thin for Ukrainian, Serbian and Banat Bulgarian sources on family duty.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for the 1387 oath; Romani for Kalderash kin terms in the ethnographies; Hebrew and Yiddish for burial-society records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conscientious objection under the old regime touches persecuted churches; pandemic-era resignations concern named doctors, so use union statements first.
- **Held for review:** the 2025 move toward voluntary military training was weighed, but its law number and terms could not be given with confidence, so it stays a query in the conscription row.
