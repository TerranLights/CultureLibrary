## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** compare conduct-grade disputes in elite city high schools with those in outer districts.
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** look for differences in how grandparents and parents describe smacking in village interviews.
- **Moldavia and Bukovina:** check how monastery towns shape lay fasting and prayer rules.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** set what Turkish and Tatar parents mean by *terbiye* beside Lipovan and Orthodox rules for children in church.
- **Banat:** read Swabian and Serb school memoirs for classroom rules under two languages.
- **Maramureș and Crișana:** see how Ukrainian and Romanian Greek Catholic catechesis treats fasting.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** compare the cadet school at Alba Iulia with civilian schools nearby.
- **The Jiu Valley and Hunedoara:** trace shift discipline in mines and what replaced it after closure.
- **Ursari, Kalderash and Rudari:** ask how each group raises and corrects children, in members' words.
- **Adventists, Pentecostals and Baptists:** compare rules on drink, dress and Sabbath and how members keep them.
- **Csángó:** treat accounts of how their children were schooled and corrected as contested.
- **Comparison only:** the rules of Hungarian Reformed boarding colleges in Transylvania, a case for Hungary's row; the school regime Moldova inherited from Soviet rule; diaspora parents meeting stricter bans on smacking abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school and church sources; mixed for family discipline; thin for Banat Bulgarian, Slovak and Serbian material.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian Cyrillic for Antim's 1714 rules; Turkish and Crimean Tatar to ask Dobruja families about *terbiye*; Romani for Ursari accounts of their trade.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child punishment and youth custody involve minors; use aggregate reports. Animal-welfare debate around the bear trade should not frame Ursari history; quote Ursari voices first.
- **Held for review:** the Tate brothers' case in Bucharest was weighed as a window on online self-discipline talk for young men; it centers on foreign nationals and a trial still under way, so it stays out.
