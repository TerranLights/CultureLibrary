## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** compare what young people in the capital's corporate jobs and in its outer districts name as "making it".
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** check how leaving the village for a degree or a factory is spoken of in local memoirs.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** look at whether university-town ambition and outward migration are told as one story or two.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** trace families that rose through trade, church or school since the 1990s.
- **Dobruja (Constanța):** set Turkish, Tatar and Lipovan routes upward (study abroad, fishing, sport) beside each other.
- **Banat and Crișana:** see what German-language schooling has meant for non-German pupils' plans.
- **Maramureș:** check how the Ukrainian and Romanian villages describe sons who left and came back richer.
- **The Jiu Valley and Hunedoara:** follow miners' children after closure for which ladders opened.
- **Sibiu's Mărginime and the Apuseni Moți:** look for shepherd and timber families that turned to tourism or trade.
- **Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari:** keep each group's own accounts of success apart from outsiders' readings of display.
- **Baptists, Pentecostals and Adventists:** check what each church teaches on wealth and on whether to seek it.
- **Csángó:** treat claims about which language opens doors for their children as contested.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's Hungarian students choosing universities in Hungary, a case for that row; Moldova's young people taking Romanian passports to move on; the diaspora's rising second generation in Italy and Spain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and surveys; mixed for political careers; thin for Lipovan, Serbian and Banat Bulgarian aspirations.
- **Languages needed:** Turkish for the Dobruja scholarship sources; Romani for Roma pupils' own words in the aspiration studies; Cyrillic-script Romanian for the manuscript copies of *Archirie și Anadan*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the envy saying is often used to describe Romanians as a whole; record who says it and why, never adopt it. Data on Roma pupils is easily turned into a deficit story; let the communities' NGOs frame it.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage scholars and the datasets known by their names (category only).
