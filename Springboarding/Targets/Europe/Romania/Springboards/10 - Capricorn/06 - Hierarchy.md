# Romania: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Ilfov:** trace the line between the gated north and the eastern districts.
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** check how old boyar estates and their former laborers are remembered in local histories.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina:** compare Moldavian boyar ranks with the Habsburg-era order in Bukovina.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** set Tatar rank memory, Lipovan church order and Romanian settlers' standing side by side.
- **Banat and Crișana:** look for Swabian, Serb and Slovak village orders of seating and precedence.
- **Maramureș:** check whether the region's small-noble families (*nemeși*) are still named and ranked.
- **Mărginimea Sibiului and Făgăraș:** see how shepherd wealth and old Făgăraș boyar families placed people.
- **Hunedoara and the Jiu Valley:** follow how miners' pay once ranked them high and what closure did to it.
- **Roma groups:** let each group say whether it ranks itself against others, and on what grounds.
- **Each church, Orthodox to Adventist:** set clergy ranks beside the lay boards that elect or check them.
- **The Jewish and Armenian communities:** check their internal orders of office in Iași, Bucharest and Gherla.
- **Csángó:** treat who claims to speak for them as part of a contested order.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvania's old Hungarian nobility and its estates, kept for Hungary's row; Moldova's Soviet-era ranks; the diaspora's place in host countries' job ladders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for boyar history, communist files and inequality data; mixed for domestic service; thin for Tatar, Serbian and Banat Bulgarian rank.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and the old Cyrillic Romanian of the Moldavian chancery for the 1785 act; Crimean Tatar for the *mırza* genealogies; Romani for any group's own account of rank.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class-origin files name real families; use CNSAS rules and published cases. Rank among Roma groups is sensitive and easily misread by outsiders.
- **Held for review:** the ranking of Roma by enslaved status before 1856 was considered; the records are already drawn on for other concepts, and a rank pointer from them would repeat that material, so it waits for the research stage.

## 5. First queries
1. Find a printed text of the 1785 *hrisov* and list every rank it names with its privileges.

4. Read the Orthodox Church statute's chapters on bishops' and priests' election.
5. Collect five "nu știi cine sunt eu" cases from the press, noting who pulled rank and what followed.
