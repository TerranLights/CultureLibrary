# Romania: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Romani; German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Serbian; French (for the Danube Commission's papers). **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest:** set block neighborhoods' quiet-hours quarrels beside the Old Town's night economy.
- **Muntenia and Oltenia:** check how village mayors and local police describe what counts as disorder.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** compare student-town nightlife complaints with rules in smaller Moldavian towns.
- **Bukovina:** look at how monastery feasts and pilgrim crowds are kept orderly.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** compare Sulina's port memory, Lipovan villages and Tatar quarters of Constanța on who keeps order.
- **Banat and Crișana:** see whether Swabian and Serb village rules on streets and fences survived emigration.
- **Maramureș:** check Ukrainian and Romanian villages' customs for policing the Sunday dance.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** compare the shepherd camp's rules in Sibiu's Mărginime with town ordinances of Brașov and Cluj.
- **The Jiu Valley:** trace how order was imposed after the 1977 strike and the 1990s marches, from miners' and officials' sides.
- **Roma groups:** ask how each group settles noise and quarrels in a shared courtyard, before police are called.
- **Pentecostal and Adventist churches:** check rules for worship order and for behavior outside church.
- **Csángó:** treat accounts of how their villages are policed as part of a contested story.
- **Comparison only:** town rules in the Hungarian-majority Székely Land, held for Hungary's row; the hooliganism law Moldova kept from Soviet times; diaspora quarters abroad seen through host-city policing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1980s memory, protest records and city rules; mixed for folk cosmology; thin for Lipovan, Tatar and Banat Bulgarian order ways.
- **Languages needed:** French for the Danube Commission archive at Sulina and Galați; Old Church Slavonic terms in the Lipovan sources; Romani for courtyard accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1970 decree's use against Roma and the August 2018 events are contested; present each claim with its source. Ferentari research must avoid repeating outsiders' labels.
- **Held for review:** popular talk about which regions are "orderly" was weighed as a source for this concept and set aside, because it rests on regional labels the sheet must not repeat; regional comparison stays on measured data.
