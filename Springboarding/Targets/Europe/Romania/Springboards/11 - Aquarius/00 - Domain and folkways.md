# Romania: Domain and folkways (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian and its regional speech; Romani; Saxon and Swabian German; Ukrainian and Hutsul; Lipovan Russian; Dobruja Turkish and Crimean Tatar; Banat Serbian; Yiddish for the Jewish past. **Concept in one line:** the domain of community and ideals: what binds people into groups and what they hope for, with settlement, migration, association and hospitality as the sign's social ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** whether ties on the capital's estates run through owners' associations or through kin in the Teleorman villages people commute from.
- **Oltenia:** whether Rudari hamlets and Romanian villages in Gorj share patron-saint feasts.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** Iași's Jewish institutions before 1941 beside scattered Hutsul homesteads, to see how distance changes gathering.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** whether Medgidia or Babadag hold feasts across faiths; how Lipovan villages near Jurilovca meet.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** which Serb, Swabian, Banat Bulgarian, Slovak and Czech villages kept their own halls.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** neo-Protestant halls in Bihor as meeting places beside the parish church.
- **Maramureș:** the churchyard after liturgy in Romanian and Ukrainian valleys.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Mărginimea Sibiului's shepherd villages against Jiu Valley miners' colonies, Cluj students, Brașov's Șchei, the Moți, Făgăraș, Hunedoara and Alba Iulia.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, Spoitori):** where each one's "we" ends, rather than one Roma community.
- **Churches (Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic, Adventist):** who counts as member and who as guest.
- **Saxons, Swabians, Armenians, Lipovans:** each one's own self-help bodies.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** rival accounts of the community's edges, as claims.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian civic life of Transylvania and the Székely Land, which is Hungary's row; Moldova and the Aromanians, near in speech but outside the target's groups; diaspora hometown associations, which show villages regrouping abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registers, interwar village programs and festivals; mixed for humanitarian practice; thin for Rudari, Slovak and Armenian sociability.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian for the travelers' series (the Arabic original only through it); German for the Kirchenburgen surveys; Yiddish and Hebrew for Iași records; Turkish and Crimean Tatar for Hıdırellez reporting.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1941 Iași pogrom and the Holocaust under Antonescu: survivors' testimony first, claims as claims. December 1989 has rival accounts; name sources, not verdicts.
- **Lands:** Romania's final lands await the author's decision; for community and ideals the pointers stay inside the present state, with Transylvania's and the Székely Land's Hungarians kept for Hungary's row.
- **Held for review:** a Romanian culture file in the Human Relations Area Files was looked for and could not be confirmed from memory; it stays out until found in the catalog.
