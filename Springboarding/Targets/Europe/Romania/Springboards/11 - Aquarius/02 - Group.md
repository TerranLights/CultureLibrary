# Romania: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (Muntenian, Transylvanian and Oltenian usage); Romani; Transylvanian Saxon German; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Banat Serbian. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** the stands at Giulești and Ștefan cel Mare, for how each crowd draws its rivals.
- **Oltenia:** look for whether haiduc lore around Iancu Jianu still names a band people claim as theirs.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina:** set lowland collective-farm villages beside Ukrainian and Hutsul uplands, checking how far collectivization reached each.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** ask who speaks for the Lipovan accords and for the Turkish and Tatar unions.
- **Banat:** Serb *družina* customs beside Swabian youth groups in shared villages.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** how *pocăiți* congregations mark their edge in Bihor.
- **Maramureș:** whether Ukrainian and Romanian youth bands of the winter customs still form by age.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** keep apart the Junii of Șchei, the Moți of the 1784 rising, the miners' brigades of Hunedoara and the Jiu Valley, and Făgăraș's mountain partisans.
- **Roma groups:** ask how Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and Gabori each mark who is "ours", and where the census counts them as one.
- **Saxons and Swabians:** the *Bruderschaft* and guild as the strongest written record of membership rules.
- **Csángó:** treat the census label and the community's own labels as claims to compare.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian student and church groups of Transylvania, which Hungary's row takes; Moldova's own collectivization, which ran under Soviet rule; ultras abroad among the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for guilds, youth companies and collectivization; mixed for crowds; thin for Lipovan accords and Rudari group life.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and German for the 1376 regulation and the brotherhood statutes; Russian for Lipovan church writings; Romani for interviews from the 1990s attacks where kept.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the mountain partisans include men with Legionary ties, and the June 1990 events and the 1990s mob attacks on Roma have rival accounts; name sources, not verdicts. CNSAS files hold third parties' names; access has rules.
- **Held for review:** a cross-national tightness ranking was a natural measure here, but Romania's place in its sample could not be recalled with certainty, so it waits for a check rather than a row.
