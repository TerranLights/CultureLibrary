# Romania: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (older Moldavian and Wallachian chancery usage for the 1700s); Latin and German for the Saxon Reformation and the Habsburg reforms; Lipovan Russian and Church Slavonic; Romani; Hebrew and Yiddish for Jewish religious reform. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare ministry reformers' plans with how Muntenian villages remember receiving them.
- **Oltenia:** test whether Tudor Vladimirescu is recalled as reformer or rebel in Gorj and Mehedinți.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** the Junimea critics and the *Viața Românească* reformers argued from one city; read them as a pair.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** look at the Austrian school and land reforms after 1775 through Romanian, Ukrainian and Hutsul village records.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** see how the Lipovans' refusal of reform and Muslim school reforms in Medgidia sit side by side.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** the Habsburg planned settlement of Swabians as reform by design; check Serb and Banat Bulgarian accounts of it.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** neo-Protestant temperance and conversion in Bihor villages beside the Orthodox reply.
- **Maramureș:** whether reforms reached the high valleys late, and who carried them (priests, teachers, officials).
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Honterus's Brașov, Horea's Apuseni, Trifa's Sibiu and the closing of the Jiu Valley mines show four kinds of reform, each to be checked on its own.
- **Roma groups:** set Roma-led evaluations of inclusion programs beside the official reports, group by group.
- **Jewish community:** the reform and Hasidic sides, with Iași, Bucharest and the Bukovina towns kept apart.
- **Csángó:** the dispute over the language of Mass in their parishes, presented as competing claims.
- **Comparison only:** the reform programs of Hungarian churches in Transylvania, which belong to Hungary's row; Moldova's post-1991 reforms, which run under another state; diaspora returnees who bring reform ideas home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Phanariot, Habsburg and interwar reform; mixed for post-1989 civic reform; thin for Roma-led and Lipovan views.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and French as well as Romanian in the Hurmuzaki volumes; Latin for Honterus; Russian and Church Slavonic for the Old Believer histories; Hebrew for synagogue records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma inclusion programs are judged differently by officials and by Roma groups; give both. Anti-corruption reforms touch prosecutions and courts, which belong to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** lenders' reports on the 1990s structural reforms were looked at as an outside record; they carry the lenders' own framing, so they wait until Romanian accounts of the same reforms are in hand.
