# Romania: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian, including 17th- and 18th-century Moldavian; Church Slavonic for the Neamț monastic writings; Serbian of the Banat; Saxon German; Hebrew and Yiddish for early Zionism; Romani; Crimean Tatar. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality; it includes visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** set Scăieni's colonists beside the 1990 square's students and ask who backed each.
- **Oltenia:** test how Romanați and Caracal mark the Islaz reading, and whether local schools teach it as an ideal.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** the Neamț and Putna monastic ideal beside *Contemporanul*'s secular one, both close to Iași.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** the Tatar ideal in *Emel* against Turkish associations' stated aims today.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** the Serb Enlightenment around Dositej and the Swabian school movement; check whether Banat Bulgarians had a parallel figure.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** look at the Greek Catholic schools of Oradea and Beiuș as homes of an educational ideal.
- **Maramureș:** whether priests and teachers of the Ukrainian villages held an ideal of national awakening, and in which press.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** check how the Transylvanian School in Blaj, the Moți of Avram Iancu's 1848 and Sibiu's ASTRA each tied ideals to schooling, with Roth's Mediaș as the Saxon neighbor.
- **Roma groups:** read the interwar movement's ideals beside today's Roma-led organizations, noting which Roma groups spoke.
- **Jewish community:** the Focșani Zionists, the socialists and the religiously observant held rival ideals; keep each in its own sources.
- **Csángó:** the rival ideals others have set for them (Romanian, Hungarian, Catholic) are claims, not their voice.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian romantic ideals of Transylvania, part of Hungary's row; Moldova's 1989 language movement, a neighbor's story; diaspora idealists who return to found NGOs, since they straddle two places.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century programs and monastic history; mixed for communist ideals; thin for Tatar and Roma writing before 1945.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-century Romanian for Cantemir; Church Slavonic for Paisie's rules; Serbian for Dositej; Hebrew for the Focșani minutes; Crimean Tatar for *Emel*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Piața Universității and the June 1990 events have rival tellings; name sources. Interwar youth idealism includes the Legionary movement; it is contested and any pointer to it needs sources from more than one side.
- **Held for review:** Western volunteers' memoirs of the 1990s children's homes came up; they speak for the givers, not the receivers, so they wait until local voices are found to read beside them.
