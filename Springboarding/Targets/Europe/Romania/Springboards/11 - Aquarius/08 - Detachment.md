# Romania: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (church register and Maramureș and Oaș lament speech); Lipovan Russian and Church Slavonic; Dobruja Turkish; Yiddish; Saxon German; Ukrainian. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare reported loneliness in the capital's one-person flats with Teleorman villages emptied by migration.
- **Oltenia:** check how mourners in Gorj and Dolj divide lament from restraint at a funeral.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** monastic stillness around Putna and Neamț beside lay detachment in Blecher's Roman and the Hasidic towns of Botoșani.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Turkish and Tatar teaching on acceptance against the Lipovan monasteries' silence, and how Delta fishermen speak of keeping calm at sea.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** see how Swabian and Serb families who emigrated or stayed speak of the homes they left.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** how feeling is shown and held back in Bihor's Baptist and Pentecostal services, from their own hymnals and sermons.
- **Maramureș:** the *bocet* tradition in Romanian and Ukrainian villages, and who still performs it.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Cioran's Rășinari, Jiu Valley miners' composure underground, the Moți and the Saxon emptying of villages around Sibiu and Brașov; check each separately.
- **Roma groups:** mourning customs of Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari families, from Roma-led accounts, on showing grief.
- **Lipovans:** silence in worship and the community's view of the lone monk.
- **Csángó:** lament and prayer customs, with rival scholars' readings given as claims.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian Calvinist funeral restraint in Transylvania, which Hungary's row studies; Moldova's emptied villages, a parallel across the Prut; diaspora workers' distance from home, felt abroad rather than here.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for monastic teaching and literature; mixed for surveys of apathy and loneliness; thin for Tatar and Lipovan voices on composure.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Russian for the Lipovan monasteries; Turkish for the *hutbe*; Yiddish and Hebrew for Ștefănești sources; German for Saxon emigration memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Eliade's and Cioran's interwar politics are contested; their essays are pointers, not authorities. Burnout and loneliness data involve health; keep to published aggregates. Impartiality as a legal duty belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for the contemplative facet, Buddhist practice in Romania and South Asian-derived meditation movements were set aside as categories under the standing rules.
