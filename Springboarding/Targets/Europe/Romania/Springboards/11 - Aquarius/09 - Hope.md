*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare what the capital's young workers expect against the hopes voiced in Teleorman's sending villages.
- **Oltenia:** gather the *urări* of Dolj and Olt and note what goods each wishes on a house.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Bukovina (Suceava):** the Paraskeva petitions and Imber's poem in Iași, and Easter egg-painting in Romanian and Ukrainian Bukovina villages.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** *Kandil* prayers among Turks and Tatars beside Lipovan Easter vigils.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** what Swabians who stayed and Serbs and Banat Bulgarians in their villages say they expect for the next generation.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Pentecostal and Baptist hymns of the life to come, from their own songbooks.
- **Maramureș:** houses raised by migrants abroad, in Romanian and Ukrainian villages, as statements of hope.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** the Jiu Valley after the mines, Mărginimea Sibiului's shepherd families, the Moți and Brașov's workers; ask each what they hope for and where they expect it from.
- **Roma groups:** hope after deportation and after 1990, from Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari speakers in their own words.
- **Jewish community:** emigration hopes from the 1880s to the 1950s, kept apart by town and decade.
- **Csángó:** what the community hopes for its language and church, with outside claims kept as claims.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian hopes for autonomy in the Székely Land, which belong to Hungary's row; Moldova's hope of union, argued across the Prut; diaspora hopes of return, which take shape abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk wishing customs, pilgrimage and migration; mixed for survey measures of expectation; thin for Lipovan and Tatar hope in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-century Romanian for Varlaam; Hebrew for Imber's poem and its Iași setting; Romani for the deportation testimonies; Turkish for *Kandil* reports; Ukrainian for Bukovina Easter customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data need care and must stay in aggregates. The Roma and Jewish deportations to Transnistria are told with survivors' voices first; claims as claims. Children in crowdfunding posts are identifiable; cite patterns, not cases.
- **Held for review:** television miracle claims and paid prayer services were considered as a market in hope; telling devotion from fraud there needs church and consumer sources first, so nothing is listed yet.
