# Romania: Domain and folkways (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (regional speech included); Romani in its Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari Boyash forms; the German of Saxons and Swabians; Hutsul and other Ukrainian; the Russian of Lipovan villages; Dobruja's Turkish and Tatar; Serbian of the Banat; Yiddish with Hebrew for Jewish towns. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and transcendence: how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape, with magic ways (charms, healers, divination, protective acts) as its folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** capital psychiatrists against Teleorman *descântătoare*; check which help is sought first.
- **Oltenia:** the *Căluș* healing memory of Dolj villages beside the Poiana Mare ward record.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** how Socola, former Hasidic courts and Orthodox shrines each handled the troubled mind.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** separate the unseen of painted Judgment walls, Hutsul *mol'far* memory (Ukrainian-language ethnography, likely thin) and Armenian pilgrimage.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Tichilești's confinement, Lipovan talk of *нечистая сила*, and the hoca's amulet, side by side.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** did charms against *урок* and the evil eye cross between Swabian, Serbian, Banat Bulgarian and Czech villages?
- **Crișana (Oradea):** whether Baptist and Pentecostal converts name charms as sin and drop them.
- **Maramureș:** which other-world beings Romanian and Ukrainian valleys each name.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** shepherds of the Mărginimea Sibiului, the Moți, Făgăraș villages, and Hunedoara and Jiu Valley miners face different perils; Cluj, Brașov and Alba Iulia for town practice.
- **Roma groups:** record Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari speech about the dead separately.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** read Bandini and later collectors as claims, each with its maker.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian and Székely Hungarians, whose shared shrines tempt but who sit in Hungary's row; Moldovan and Aromanian lore, which share terms; healers in the diaspora, to see what travels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for village belief; mixed for institutions; thin for Hutsul, Tatar and Slovak practice.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Bandini; Romanian in Cyrillic letters for older *Visul* copies; Hebrew for *Pinkas Hakehillot*; German for the Teutsch-Haus files; Ukrainian for Hutsul ethnography; Turkish and Tatar for the hoca material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** closed wards and leprosy involve living people; reach residents through their own spokespeople. Healers may keep charms secret; record only what they share.
- **Lands:** which lands Romania will finally hold is still undecided; this sheet keeps to the present state, minus the Hungarian communities assigned to Hungary's row.
- **Held for review:** a popular psychology bestseller on the "Romanian soul" came to mind but its title and author could not be recalled with confidence, so it was left out.
