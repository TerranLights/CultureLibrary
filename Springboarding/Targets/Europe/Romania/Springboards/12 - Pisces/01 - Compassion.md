# Romania: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian (its church register too); Romani; German of the Saxons and Swabians; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Dobruja Turkish and Tatar; Serbian; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** a beggar at a metro mouth against one at a Teleorman church door: who stops? Gara de Nord's street-children past is the test case.
- **Oltenia:** Craiova and Dolj parishes; check whether alms to Rudari neighbors are tied to feasts or given at need.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** the Sfântul Spiridon charters let a researcher follow a single city's mercy over two and a half centuries.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** monastery guesthouses: who is fed, who turned away.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Kurban sharing against Lipovan feast-day alms; the Lipovan side is thin, so start with their own parish papers.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian deportees' memoirs and Serb neighbors' memories of 1951 relief; who helped across confession.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** whether Adventist (ADRA) and Baptist giving reaches outsiders.
- **Maramureș:** Ukrainian and Romanian villages after floods; whether help is asked of kin, parish or state.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** the Moți of Agârbiceanu's pages against Jiu Valley and Hunedoara towns after the mine closures; Mărginimea Sibiului, Cluj and Brașov charities.
- **Roma (Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari, Spoitori):** pity both given and received; look for Roma-led charities' own words about being pitied.
- **Jewish community and its history:** *rakhmones* in Iași and Bukovina towns, and the rescuers and bystanders of 1941–44, as each source tells it.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** parish charity under Catholic priests; whose language the records use is itself contested.
- **Comparison only:** Caritas branches that serve Hungarian Catholics overlap the target, but that community sits in Hungary's row; Moldovan and Aromanian alms words mirror Romanian ones; remittances from the diaspora show mercy sent home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** church charity and street-children history are well covered; giving figures are patchy; Lipovan and Slovak practice is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Cyrillic Romanian for the hell-journey copies; Greek in some Phanariot-era hospital deeds; German for *Atemschaukel* in the original; Turkish for Kurban reporting.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** street children and the old orphanages involve living adults; use their own testimony. Pity-led appeals can wound the people shown; note who chose the images.
- **Held for review:** a cross-national helping-strangers field experiment was considered, but whether a Romanian city was in its sample could not be recalled, so it waits for a catalog check.
