## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** do documentary theater and long-form journalism reach anyone beyond the capital's own audience?
- **Oltenia:** the *Cântecul zorilor* tradition in Gorj and Mehedinți as a funeral voice for another; set it beside Muntenian laments.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** a medical-school city and the site of the 1941 pogrom; ask how teaching of that history frames the victims' view.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Ukrainian, Romanian and Hutsul villages that sing for one another at weddings; check whether lament crosses language.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Turkish and Tatar schools' Romanian-language civics against home speech; the Lipovan side has few printed sources, so look in parish bulletins.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian, Serb, Banat Bulgarian, Slovak and Czech neighbors after Swabian emigration; read memoirs for who missed whom.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** perspective-taking in neo-Protestant testimony, where a convert retells a former self.
- **Maramureș:** Ukrainian and Romanian laments of the Iza and Vișeu valleys; who is allowed to lament for a stranger.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Saxon writers read by Sibiu Romanians; Moți and Jiu Valley miners' widows; Cluj and Brașov schools as test beds for school feelings curricula.
- **Roma groups:** Giuvlipen's audiences and Ursari or Kalderash family memory of Transnistria; ask what each group expects outsiders to understand.
- **Jewish community and its history:** Sebastian's diary and the Iași survivors' testimony as a case of empathy denied and then claimed.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** the claim that outside sides "speak for" them is itself the debate; collect the community's own voices.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian-language theater in Cluj and Târgu Mureș is Hungary's row, useful only to contrast staging of the other side; Moldova shares the Romanian vocabulary; diaspora workers abroad write of being misread, a mirror case.
