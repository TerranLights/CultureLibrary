# Romania: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian, with its Slavonic-derived martyr words; German of Saxon memorials; Russian of Lipovan parishes; Turkish and Tatar; Ukrainian; Romani; Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** donor-family consent in the capital against Muntenian villages.
- **Oltenia:** WWI memorial culture in Gorj (Târgu Jiu) and village heroes' monuments; which deaths get named.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Neamț:** convent vocations of Agapia and Văratec against the region's emigration; two ways of leaving home for others.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Ukrainian and Romanian parish fasting calendars side by side; who fasts for whom.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Niculițel's martyrs, Lipovan exile for faith and Tatar *şehit* graves: three local stories of giving up life or land.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian and Serb families deported to the Bărăgan in 1951; whose memoirs present the years as sacrifice and whose as theft.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Baptist and Pentecostal pastors jailed under communism, as their churches narrate them.
- **Maramureș:** Greek Catholic priests who refused to join Orthodoxy in 1948, in parish memory.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** how the Moți commemorate Horea's execution at Alba Iulia; Făgăraș partisan memory; Mărginimea shepherds' winters away; Jiu Valley strikers; Cluj and Brașov hospital staff.
- **Roma groups:** Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and Spoitori families each tell Transnistria differently; keep testimony by group and county.
- **Jewish community and its history:** *kidush hashem* in rabbinic writing against survivors' own accounts of 1941–44.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** priests and lay cantors who held services in a disputed language; treat every source's framing as a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian Greek Catholic and Reformed martyr cults of Transylvania and the Székely Land fall in Hungary's row; Moldovan war-memorial language overlaps but is another culture-nation; care workers abroad (Italy, Spain) are a diaspora mirror of sacrifice for the family at home.
