## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** where a fading parent lives: a city flat or a three-generation village house.
- **Oltenia:** what Mehedinți families recall of Ada Kaleh's Turkish island community after the Iron Gates flooding, as a second drowned place beside Bicaz.
- **Moldavia (Iași) and Neamț:** the Bicaz villages and the old Jewish quarters of Moldavian towns, two kinds of emptying.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Ukrainian and Hutsul villages that may be shifting into Romanian; ask the families which language the grandchildren speak.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Lipovan, Turkish and Tatar language keeping, and Sunwaves crowds on the same coast.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian villages after 1990; whether Serb, Slovak, Czech and Banat Bulgarian villages thinned in the same years.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Pentecostal congregations where ecstatic prayer is practiced; compare with Orthodox restraint in the same towns.
- **Maramureș:** does the bride's leave-taking song differ between the Iza's Romanian villages and the Ukrainian ones near Rona?
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** the Apuseni Moți hamlets emptied by out-migration, the Jiu Valley after mines closed, Făgăraș and Mărginimea villages; Cluj and Sibiu as receivers.
- **Roma groups:** whether Rudari, Ursari, Kalderash and Spoitori keep or lose their speech differs by group; ask each about *rromanipen*.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** language shift is the debate itself; never treat either side's count as settled.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian-speaking Transylvania and the Székely Land are Hungary's row, but useful as a contrast for language keeping; Moldova's 1990s union debate shows another merger story; Romanians abroad dissolving into Italian or Spanish life is a diaspora question, not this nation's.
