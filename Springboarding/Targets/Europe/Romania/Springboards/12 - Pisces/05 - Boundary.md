## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** fenced compounds north of the capital against Muntenian village yards; ask how each family explains its fence.
- **Oltenia:** plague-shirt and furrow rites in Gorj and Mehedinți, against the Banat quarantine records next door.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** apartment blocks with shared stairwells; what neighbors may ask or enter.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hutsul homesteads far apart against clustered Romanian villages; how spacing shapes the idea of a visit.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Muslim home spheres in Medgidia, Lipovan table rules, and fishing waters shared across villages.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian gates and Serbian yards; was the cordon remembered differently by each village?
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Salonta and the western border in *Morgen*; Baptist and Pentecostal families on what children may see.
- **Maramureș:** carved gates on both the Romanian and Ukrainian sides of the Tisa, and the bridge between.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Romanian and Saxon houses side by side around Sibiu and Brașov; scattered Moți hamlets; Jiu Valley blocks; Făgăraș villages.
- **Roma groups:** purity and pollution rules differ by group; read accounts by Kalderash and other Roma writers first, and studies of how outsiders describe them second.
- **Jewish community and its history:** the town quarters of Iași and Bukovina and the lines of kashrut and Sabbath, through community records.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** the line between "Hungarian" and "Romanian" Catholic is itself disputed; record who draws it and why.
- **Comparison only:** Székely carved gates invite comparison but belong, with Transylvania's other Hungarians, to Hungary's row; Moldova lies across a line this sheet does not settle; Romanians sharing flats in Italy show boundaries under strain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk architecture and rites; mixed for privacy attitudes; thin for Tatar, Lipovan and Slovak household rules.
- **Languages needed:** German for Habsburg cordon files; Ukrainian for Tisa-valley accounts; Russian for Old Believer table rules; Turkish for the Muftiate's texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma purity rules are easily turned into stereotype; cite group members' own accounts. Sex-education debates are polarized. National borders are not settled here.
