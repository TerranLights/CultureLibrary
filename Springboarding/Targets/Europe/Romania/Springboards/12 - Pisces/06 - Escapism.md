## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** street youth at Gara de Nord against the capital's betting rooms and clubs; one city, several escapes.
- **Oltenia:** Danube villages of Mehedinți and Dolj, where swimmers tried to cross under communism.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** *bejenie* in the chronicles; later, migration as escape from the region's poorer counties.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hutsul and Romanian upland villages; check whether the escape there is the forest and the sheepfold rather than the bar.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** seaside holidays beside Medgidia coffeehouses and Lipovan fishing camps.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** check which border crossings the *frontieriști* tried there, and set Swabian *Kirchweih* and Băile Herculane's spa visitors beside them as escapes of other kinds.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Băile Felix and the neo-Protestant abstinence of many churches in Bihor; compare what each offers as relief.
- **Maramureș:** *horincă* at feasts against total abstinence in some converted households; ask both sides.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Făgăraș huts for hikers, Jiu Valley miners after closures, the Moți leaving the Apuseni, Mărginimea shepherds' long summers away, Cluj and Brașov night life.
- **Roma groups:** ask Rudari, Kalderash and Ursari families about their own leisure, apart from the musician image outsiders attach to them; treat that image as a label to study.
- **Jewish community and its history:** emigration as escape in the 1950s–80s, and the Yiddish stage as earlier release.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** labor migration as a way out, described by both rival literatures; claims only.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian student and festival life in Cluj and the Székely Land belongs to Hungary's row, a parallel scene; Moldova's own emigration is a separate culture-nation's story; Romanian workers abroad tell of escape and of homesickness, a diaspora theme.
