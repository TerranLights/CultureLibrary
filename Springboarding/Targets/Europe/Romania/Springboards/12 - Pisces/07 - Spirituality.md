## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** who waits in the St. Demetrius queue, and who prays instead in storefront Pentecostal halls on the city's edge?
- **Oltenia:** Maglavit's legacy in Dolj; whether vision-seeking still draws villagers or now draws only tourists.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** carol variants of Moldavian villages against the city's relic shrines; sung and visited holiness.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hagigadar draws Armenians, Orthodox and Catholics; record each group's reason for coming.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Lipovan chant, *kandil* nights and the Orthodox majority in one county; three sacred calendars.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian Catholic, Serbian Orthodox and Banat Bulgarian Catholic shrines; which pilgrimages cross lines.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Baptist and Pentecostal testimony of rebirth; what converts call the sacred.
- **Maramureș:** Ukrainian and Romanian parishes; wooden churches as working places of prayer, not only heritage.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Sâmbăta de Sus visitors from Făgăraș and beyond; the Moți's mountain chapels; Greek Catholic devotion around Blaj and Alba Iulia; Cluj and Brașov students.
- **Roma groups:** Romani-language worship among Pentecostal Roma against Orthodox Roma feasts; ask each group.
- **Jewish community and its history:** grave pilgrimages kept largely by visitors from abroad; local keepers' view.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** Catholic devotional language is contested; record prayers in the speakers' chosen language.
- **Comparison only:** the Șumuleu Ciuc (Csíksomlyó) pilgrimage belongs to the Székely Land and Hungary's row, worth contrasting with Romanian pilgrimages; Moldovan monastic life shares saints; diaspora parishes in Italy and Spain show how practice travels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Orthodox practice and pilgrimages; mixed for non-church spirituality; thin for Tatar, Armenian and Jewish living practice.
- **Languages needed:** Armenian for Hagigadar's own texts; Church Slavonic for Lipovan chant books; Romani for the hymnals; Turkish for *kandil* notices; Hebrew for *hilula* notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Maglavit and some elders are entangled with interwar and later nationalism; name sources, not verdicts. Pilgrims' private vows are theirs to share.
- **Held for review:** meditation and seekers' facets led to Buddhist groups and to South Asian-derived movements in Romania; both stay out as categories, at the author's discretion.
