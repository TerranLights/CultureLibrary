# Romania: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian, everyday and liturgical; German for the Saxon church's handovers; Crimean Tatar and Turkish; Ukrainian; Lipovan Russian; Romani; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** capital hospital deaths against Muntenian home deaths; who decides to stop treatment?
- **Oltenia:** test whether the ballad-based "fatalism" claim is pinned to Oltenian shepherds or to the whole country, and by whom.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** legends of refusal from Neculce's region against today's talk in its emptied villages.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Ukrainian and Romanian parishes; how each prays with the dying.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Cadrilater resettlers in Tulcea and Constanța villages beside Turks and Tatars who stayed; two memories of 1940.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** in Swabian and Serb Bărăgan memoirs, does enduring or resisting dominate the telling?
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Baptist and Pentecostal "giving one's life to God" set against Orthodox *Facă-se voia Ta*.
- **Maramureș:** whether elders on both the Romanian and Ukrainian banks of the Iza and Tisa die at home; compare the deathbed rites.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Avram Iancu's refusal to yield in the Apuseni, as the Moți commemorate it; Jiu Valley writers; Făgăraș and Mărginimea villages; Cluj and Sibiu hospices.
- **Roma groups:** how each group speaks of illness and dying, from Roma health mediators and families, not from outside labels.
- **Jewish community and its history:** *bashert* in memoirs of survivors and emigrants; acceptance and refusal after 1944.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** narratives of yielding language or holding it are claims made by rival camps; collect both.
- **Comparison only:** Transylvanian and Székely Hungarian memory of 1940–44 mirrors the cession story but sits in Hungary's row; Moldova's 1940 belongs to another culture-nation; returnees from the diaspora tell of giving up on life abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** war, cession and death customs are well documented; palliative care is patchy; Tatar sayings and Lipovan deathbed practice are thin.
- **Languages needed:** Romanian chronicle language for Neculce; Crimean Tatar and Turkish for proverb collections; German for Saxon church handovers; Ukrainian for parish rites; Yiddish for memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "fatalistic Romanian" idea is a stereotype; treat it only as a claim under study. Memory of the 1940 cessions and of captivity is politically charged; this sheet takes no side on any line.
- **Held for review:** one therapy-related pointer traced to South Asian-derived practice; it is kept back as a category.
