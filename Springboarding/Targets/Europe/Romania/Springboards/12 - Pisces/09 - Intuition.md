## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** dream-book sellers by metro stations against Muntenian village dream-tellers; same signs, different authority.
- **Oltenia:** the *ursitoare* at a newborn's bedside in Dolj and Gorj customs; who claims to hear the Fates.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** Cyrillic-era chapbooks printed and copied here; trace which omen books reached villages.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hutsul weather knowledge on high pastures against valley farmers'; sources likely scarce.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** Lipovan fishers, Turkish and Tatar coffee-cup readers, Romanian farmers on one coast; three expert eyes.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Swabian *Bauernregeln* beside Serbian and Romanian saints'-day sayings in mixed villages.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** do Baptist and Pentecostal converts call omen-reading sin, and what do they turn to for guidance instead?
- **Maramureș:** Christmas Eve divining in Ukrainian homes and New Year customs in Romanian ones.
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Mărginimea shepherds, Moți foresters' accounts of Apuseni weather, Jiu Valley miners' talk of sensing danger underground, Făgăraș villages; Cluj psychology departments for test samples.
- **Roma groups:** read Roma authors and activists on being cast as seers; keep Kalderash, Ursari, Rudari and others apart.
- **Jewish community and its history:** Yiddish dream and omen talk in Moldavian towns, from memoirs.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** their divination customs appear in rival scholarly camps; every description is a claim.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian dream books and weather sayings of Transylvania and the Székely Land would mislead if merged, so they stay in Hungary's row; Moldova shares many chapbooks; diaspora readers buy dream books online from abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for chapbooks and calendar customs; mixed for expert local knowledge; thin for test-based measures and Ukrainian divining in Romania.
- **Languages needed:** Cyrillic-script Romanian for the manuscripts Cartojan cites; Turkish for coffee-reading talk in Dobruja; German for farm sayings; Ukrainian for Maramureș divining.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fortune-telling is often pinned on Roma; never repeat the label as fact. Elders' foresight claims involve contested figures; record claims only.
- **Held for review:** a reading system of South Asian origin that is marketed in Romania surfaced on the commerce side; it remains a category only.
