# Romania: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Hutsul and other Ukrainian (outlaw lore); Romani; German; Turkish; Lipovan Russian; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** compare the Rîmaru panic, told under a muzzled press, with today's evening crime bulletins; how does a city learn whom to fear?
- **Oltenia:** ballad outlaws of Gorj and Dolj, and present-day talk of local bosses; whether the hero image survives.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** blood-libel accusations of the 19th century in Moldavian towns, through Oișteanu and Jewish community records.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hutsul outlaw songs beside Romanian ballads of the same hills.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** accounts of the marshes and the Delta as a hiding place for fugitives; collect Lipovan, Turkish and Romanian village versions.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** fuel smuggling during the 1990s embargo on the Danube; ask villagers on both language sides whether they counted it as wrongdoing.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** converted former offenders' testimonies in neo-Protestant churches.
- **Maramureș:** forest theft and the outlaw Pintea in local memory; does the village call taking timber a crime?
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Moți memory of the 1784 uprising as justice or as crime, how Jiu Valley miners tell 1990 against how others label them, Mărginimea and Făgăraș villages, Cluj and Brașov students' views of petty corruption.
- **Roma groups:** read Roma journalists and organizations on how the "criminal" label is applied, never the label itself; keep groups apart.
- **Jewish community and its history:** accusations, pogroms and the Holocaust as crimes against a people, in survivors' and historians' words.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** accusations each camp makes about the other are claims; record the accuser.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian-language crime reporting from Transylvania and the Székely Land runs its own labels and belongs to Hungary's row; Moldova's organized-crime image is a separate culture-nation's; reports on Romanians abroad accused of crimes show labels exported.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for outlaw legend and media; mixed for victimization; thin for Tatar, Lipovan and Slovak views of wrongdoing.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian for Dovbush lore; Romanian church Slavonicisms for the toll-houses texts; Yiddish and Hebrew for community records cited by Oișteanu; German for Saxon parish discipline if pursued.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** never tie wrongdoing to an ethnic group; the Roma and Jewish rows are about how labels are made. Living victims' families in recent cases need care.
- **Held for review:** for criminal codes and courts, nothing is given here; those pointers belong to Libra and Capricorn, and none was moved.
