# Romania: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Romania, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Romanian; Yiddish; German of the Saxon towns; Hutsul Ukrainian; Old Believer Russian; Dobruja Turkish and Tatar; Romani. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bucharest and Muntenia:** horror films and haunted-place tours in the capital against Muntenian village revenant talk; which fear is entertainment and which is not.
- **Oltenia:** check whether press coverage of *strigoi* cases clusters on Dolj and Olt villages, and how that shapes a regional image.
- **Moldavia (Iași):** fear of the 1941 pogrom's return in survivors' memoirs; Orthodox preaching on hell in the same city.
- **Bukovina (Suceava):** Hutsul forest beings and painted monastery hells; two pictures of danger in one county.
- **Dobruja and the Delta:** jinn beliefs among Muslims, Lipovan end-times talk, Romanian storm charms; three idioms of unseen danger.
- **Banat (Timișoara):** Bărăgan deportees' memories in Vultur's interviews, sorted by Swabian, Serb, Romanian and Aromanian speaker.
- **Crișana (Oradea):** Pentecostal and Baptist talk of the devil against the "superstitions" they reject.
- **Maramureș:** Poienile Izei's hell scenes beside Ukrainian valley churches; did both teach the same torments?
- **Transylvania's Romanian communities:** Saxon trial towns near Sibiu and Brașov, Moți fears of mine accidents, Jiu Valley gas explosions, Făgăraș mountain partisans' families and the Securitate, Cluj students' anxiety.
- **Roma groups:** fears Roma themselves name (deportation, mob violence, eviction), from Roma testimony; Kalderash, Ursari and Rudari separately.
- **Jewish community and its history:** fear in Transnistria survivors' testimony and in the Iași and Bukovina communities after 1944.
- **Csángó of Moldavia:** charm and fear lore collected by rival camps; each collection is a claim with a purpose.
- **Comparison only:** Hungarian witch-trial files and fear lore of Transylvania and the Székely Land are documented separately, in Hungary's row; Moldova's revenant lore shares words; Romanians abroad buy charms online, a diaspora trace.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revenant lore and deportation memory; mixed for anxiety measures; thin for Hutsul, Lipovan and Tatar fear idioms.
- **Languages needed:** German for the trial files; Ukrainian for Hutsul ethnography; Russian for Old Believer texts; Turkish and Tatar for jinn studies; Yiddish for survivors' memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** curses, magic and witchcraft must never be pinned on Roma or any group; use studies of how such labels are applied. Exhumations of suspected *strigoi* involve living families. "Dracula" is a contested product, not a folk figure.
- **Held for review:** fear of newly arrived workers led to one pointer that falls under the South Asian rule; it is kept back as the bare category.
