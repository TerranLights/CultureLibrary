# Sweden: Self and identity, the domain and folkways (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with Scanian, Gutnish, Elfdalian and Norrland speech), the Sámi languages, Meänkieli, Finnish, Romani, Yiddish, Swedish Sign Language, and the languages of newer communities. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the self: what a self is taken to be, when a person begins and ends, the body and appearance, and the ways of naming that mark a person out.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stockholm and the Mälardalen against rural Norrland (Västerbotten, Norrbotten):** check first-name rarity and nickname use, since name rarity is the folkway's measure of setting a person apart.
- **Dalarna, Hälsingland and Halland district-costume wearers:** check who still wears parish dress and at which rites, to see whether dress marks a place-self.
- **Sámi of the North, Lule, Pite, Ume and South Sámi areas, herding and non-herding:** check how *gákti* details and personal names are read across language areas; ask Sámi institutions how they share this.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** check double name forms and the twentieth-century name changes, a record of how names were made to fit.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and the Travellers (*resande*):** check community-held naming customs through the communities' own organizations, not old official files.
- **Jewish community:** check Hebrew and civil names side by side, since one person may carry two names for different settings.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs of Södertälje, Iraqis, Syrians, Kurds, Iranians, Bosniaks, Somalis, Eritreans:** check surname adoption and name changes at registration, which show how a newcomer is entered as a person.
- **Deaf signers:** check the giving of name signs, which happens inside the community.
- **Church of Sweden, Catholic and free-church families:** check baptismal and confirmation names against civil naming.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names (parish registers since the 1600s, name statistics) and dress; thin for how a self is spoken of in the smaller minority languages. Sweden's lands are carried with no recorded change and their final extent is still to be settled; this pass writes for Sweden as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish throughout; North, Lule and South Sámi for dress and name terms; Meänkieli and Finnish for the Torne Valley; Arabic, Syriac, Kurdish and Persian for the naming changes of newer communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi dress and personal yoiks belong to their bearers; the race-biology photographs of the 1920s and 1930s show people without consent.
- **Held for review:** for the domain overview, foreign-authored "national character" handbooks were weighed and left out as outsiders' generalizations; no pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule.
