# Sweden: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish; North, Lule and South Sámi; Meänkieli; Finnish; Syriac (Assyrian), Kurdish, Persian, Bosnian, Somali and Tigrinya for community self-names; Romani. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Skåne, Blekinge and Halland:** check provincial flags, dialect pride and how the region's Danish past is told, since provincial and national identity may be layered differently there.
- **Gotland and Öland:** check island self-labels and Gutnish revival, which mark an identity apart from the mainland.
- **Jämtland and Härjedalen:** check how a former Norwegian past enters local self-description.
- **Dalarna and Bergslagen:** check how mining and the Vasa legend sit in local identity.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding, by language area:** check how identity is stated by members of a herding *sameby* (Sámi village) and by Sámi outside one, as each side puts it.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** check whether people use the minority name, a regional name, or "Swede", and how this shifts across generations.
- **Forest Finns of Värmland:** check how descendants of early modern settlers name themselves now.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash, and others) and the Travellers (*resande*):** check each group's own organizations for self-description, apart from state files.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs:** check both self-names in their own media, never merging them.
- **Bosniaks, Kurds, Iranians, Somalis, Eritreans:** check second-generation memoirs and youth organizations for how layered identities are put.
- **Muslim youth (Sveriges Unga Muslimer) and Jewish youth groups:** check how religion and nation are combined in self-introductions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in surveys, registers and literature; contested and emotionally loaded for Sámi enrollment and for the naming of Assyrians and Syriacs.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish; North and South Sámi for enrollment debates; Meänkieli and Finnish for the commission's hearings; Syriac and Arabic for Södertälje media; Kurdish and Persian for exile presses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race-biology records hold named people's bodies and families; Sámi identity disputes involve living persons; present each contested account as an account.
- **Held for review:** the race-survey volumes published by the institute's own staff were kept out of the table and should be reached only through the archive with its critical histories; no pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule.
