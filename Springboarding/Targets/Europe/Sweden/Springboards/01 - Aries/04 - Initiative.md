## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Småland (Gnosjö, Älmhult) against inland Västerbotten and Norrbotten:** check new-firm rates and the local stories told about them, so that a regional label is tested against data.
- **Stockholm and Gothenburg tech and design scenes:** check founder backgrounds against the national register, since media attention may not match where firms are actually started.
- **Bergslagen mining towns:** check what local groups started after mine and mill closures.
- **Gotland and Öland:** check seasonal businesses and island associations, where the season sets when things can be started.
- **Sámi makers and herding enterprises:** check Sámi organizations' data on new craft and tourism firms, in their own terms.
- **Tornedalians:** check village associations and their projects in Meänkieli and Swedish.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs, Kurds, Iranians, Bosniaks, Somalis:** check SCB register data on self-employment by country of birth and the community associations' own business networks, without reading the figures as group traits.
- **Chilean exiles:** check the cooperatives and cultural associations founded after 1973.
- **Women founders:** check GEM and Tillväxtanalys splits by sex.
- **Free churches and Muslim study associations:** check which voluntary activities each congregation or association started.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in business registers and civil-society surveys; thinner for unrecorded initiative (neighbors helping unasked) and for Sámi and Traveller enterprise.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish; Finnish and Meänkieli for village projects; North Sámi for craft and tourism; Arabic, Persian and Kurdish for community business networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regional and ethnic "enterprise spirit" labels risk becoming stereotypes; test each label against register data and quote its critics.
- **Held for review:** the Gnosjö literature includes religious and ethnic explanations of local enterprise; kept as a pointer, flagged for critical reading rather than set aside. No pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule.
