## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Skåne against Uppland and Dalarna:** check New Year, Walpurgis Night and first-day customs in the regional folk-life archives, since the regional calendars were kept separately.
- **Norrland settlers and the Lapland *nybyggare*:** check settler memoirs against Sámi accounts of the same land, since "new" land had prior users.
- **Sámi of each language area:** check origin stories and the reckoning of the herding year as each community shares them.
- **Tornedalians and Laestadians:** check revival histories for how a personal new start is marked.
- **Pentecostal and Mission Covenant congregations:** check adult baptism and conversion testimony.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs (Akitu), Iranians and Kurds (Nowruz and Newroz):** check which new year each community keeps publicly and which at home.
- **Jewish community:** check Rosh Hashanah observance and how a Jewish new year sits beside the civil one.
- **Estonian Swedes and Estonian refugees of 1944, Bosniaks of the 1990s, Chilean exiles of 1973:** check arrival anniversaries and memorial dates each group keeps.
- **Eritreans and Somalis:** check community associations for new-year and arrival observances; sources may be thin and held mainly in Tigrinya and Somali.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for calendar customs, church statistics and emigration; thin for how recent arrivals mark their own new starts, which sits in association newsletters and oral memory.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and older printed Swedish for almanacs; North and South Sámi for origin and seasonal material; Syriac, Persian and Kurdish for new-year associations; Spanish for Chilean exile writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi origin narratives and drum-related cosmology may be held by communities as their own; consult Sámi institutions before using recorded versions.
