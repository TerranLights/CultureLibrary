# Sweden: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, North and Pite Sámi, Meänkieli, Finnish, Yiddish, Arabic, Persian, Bosnian, Chilean Spanish. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stockholm and Gothenburg flat-dwellers against farm households in Småland and Värmland:** check storage space, barns and attics, since where things can be kept shapes how many are kept.
- **Uppland and Gotland archaeology:** compare grave-goods patterns between the Mälaren graves and Gotland's picture-stone sites, to see whether the dead's possessions were treated alike.
- **Pite and Lule Sámi silver-holding families against South Sámi areas:** check which belongings are named as inheritable, through Sámi museums' own descriptions.
- **Sweden Finns and Tornedalians:** check what war children and 1960s labor migrants kept from home, and what the second generation still holds.
- **Jewish families and Estonian Swedes who came in the 1940s:** check what flight allowed each to bring and keep.
- **Bosniaks, Syrians, Iraqis, Kurds, Eritreans and the Chilean exiles:** check memoirs and oral-history projects for objects carried across borders, each in its own language.
- **Laestadian households in Norrbotten:** read the movement's guidance on ornament and display as teaching, not as a description of homes.
- **Roma and the *resande*:** ask their own organizations what happens to a dead person's things, and leave any private custom unrecorded.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for archaeology, consumer research and secondhand trade; mixed for minority accounts of keeping and letting go, which sit mostly in memoirs and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish; Finnish and Meänkieli for war-children and migrant memoirs; Pite and North Sámi for silver and inheritance terms; Bosnian, Arabic and Persian for refugee object stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi objects in museums carry a disputed history of collection; give the Sámi institutions' account beside the museums'. Some Roma customs around a dead person's belongings may be private to families.
- **Held for review:** Sámi grave finds and human remains in university collections were weighed as "possessions of the dead" and left out pending the reburial and repatriation process; nothing touched the South Asian rule.
