# Sweden: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with Dalecarlian and Elfdalian parish usage), North and South Sámi, Meänkieli, Yiddish, Arabic, Persian, Kurdish. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stockholm and Mälardalen co-op holders against Norrland villages:** check tenure by municipality, since the co-op share may stand in for owning in cities.
- **Skåne estate land against the small farms of Småland and the forests of Värmland:** check survey maps for how evenly land was held before and after enclosure.
- **Dalarna and Härjedalen communal forests:** check who counts as a shareholder and how newcomers are treated, since the commons rests on old parish membership.
- **Reindeer-herding and non-herding Sámi, North to South:** check how each describes *sameby* membership and land use, and how testimony differs by language area.
- **Tornedalians:** check land and forest ownership in the valley alongside the language policies that touched farm families.
- **Million Programme suburbs of Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö (Rinkeby, Angered, Rosengård):** check rental tenure and the 2000s co-op conversions as Somali, Iraqi, Kurdish and other residents describe them.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs in Södertälje:** check family firms and house purchases in community media for whether ownership is pooled across kin.
- **Church of Sweden:** check its reports on church forest and glebe land since the 2000 separation from the state.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land surveys, tenure and forest statistics; thin on how smaller communities pool ownership.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish (with 1700s and 1800s survey language); North and South Sámi for land and grazing terms in testimony; Meänkieli for valley farms; Arabic, Kurdish and Syriac for family-business sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi land and grazing rights are contested among herders, landowners, forest firms and the state; present each claim as an account. Unions and business still tell the wage-earner fund fight differently; use both archives.
- **Held for review:** court rulings on Sámi land and hunting rights and the statutes on tenure were weighed for this concept and routed to Libra; nothing was set aside under the South Asian rule.
