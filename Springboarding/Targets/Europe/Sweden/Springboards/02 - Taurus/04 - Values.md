## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stockholm and Uppsala against the Jönköping area's free-church belt in Småland:** check EVS answers on religion and family by county, since the "Bible belt" label needs testing, not repeating.
- **Västerbotten and Norrbotten labor towns:** check temperance and study-circle records, which recorded a code of conduct as members wrote it down.
- **Skåne and Gothenburg:** check how the "*svenska värderingar*" debate played locally, through regional papers.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding:** check Sámi school curricula and Sámi-language radio for which values are named and in what words.
- **Tornedalians and Laestadians:** check Laestadian sermons and Meänkieli writing separately, since faith and language overlap but are not the same group.
- **Muslim communities (Bosniak, Iraqi, Syrian, Somali and others):** check study-association material by language, since each brings its own teaching.
- **Jewish community, Catholics and the Syriac churches of Södertälje:** check their periodicals for how tradition is ranked against Swedish norms.
- **Chilean exiles and their children:** check exile organizations' papers for political values carried from 1973.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: values surveys go back to the 1980s and the popular movements kept archives in nearly every county. Thinner in Sámi and Meänkieli.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish, including Old Swedish and 1600s print for devotional texts; North Sámi for school material; Arabic and Persian for study circles and exile press; Spanish for Chilean exile papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "Swedish values" debate is contested ground in integration politics; present each side's account and avoid a fixed list. Values surveys report averages; do not let them speak for any group.
- **Held for review:** popular books selling a single national value word abroad were weighed and left out as marketing, not sources; nothing was set aside under the South Asian rule.
