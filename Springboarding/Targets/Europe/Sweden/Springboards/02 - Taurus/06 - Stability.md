## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bergslagen and Gästrikland mill towns against Stockholm's commuter belt:** check years in the same home and same employer, since *bruksanda* is said to rest on one firm's long presence.
- **Skåne and Västerbotten parish data:** compare how long families stayed, using the two regional databases side by side.
- **Kiruna and Malmberget residents and the Gabna and Laevas *samebyar* (*check*):** check how each speaks about the town move and its effect on grazing.
- **North Sámi families moved south and the South Sámi already there:** check both groups' accounts, since a forced move unsettled two communities.
- **Gotland and Öland farm families:** check family-farm continuity and farm names in local history.
- **Tornedalians in Pajala and Övertorneå:** check out-migration from the valley and who stays to keep farms.
- **Chilean exiles, Iranians and Iraqis:** check memoirs and associations for when "settling" was decided and how return was weighed.
- **Recent arrivals with temporary permits (Syrian, Eritrean, Somali):** check lived accounts of waiting and impermanence through community and NGO reports, not statutes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: Sweden's parish data allow long series of who stayed and who moved, and modern registers continue them. Thin on how newer communities experience settling.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish (runic and older parish hands); North Sámi for relocation memories; Finnish for valley out-migration; Arabic, Persian and Spanish for exile and return.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the forced relocation of Sámi families is part of contested ground on Sámi land and herding rights; let the families' and *samebyar*'s own accounts lead. Registry data on small villages can identify families; use aggregates.
- **Held for review:** residence-permit rules shaping who may settle were weighed and routed to Libra; no pointer here came under the South Asian rule.
