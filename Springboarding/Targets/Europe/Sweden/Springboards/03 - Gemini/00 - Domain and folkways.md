## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Scanian, Gotlandic and Dalecarlian speakers against Stockholm and the Mälardalen:** check dialect recordings and attitude surveys to see where regional speech stays at home and where it shifts toward the standard.
- **Norrbotten and the Torne Valley (Tornedalians, Meänkieli speakers):** check school memories and revival projects, since the valley's schools once taught in Swedish only.
- **Sámi of each language area, herding and non-herding:** check the nomad-school history and today's language nests separately for North, Lule, Pite, Ume and South Sámi.
- **Sweden Finns:** check Finnish-language schools and the labor-migrant generation's home language against their grandchildren's.
- **Deaf communities:** check Deaf schools and clubs as a separate world of learning and talk.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs (Södertälje), Kurds, Iranians, Bosniaks, Somalis, Eritreans:** check mother-tongue classes, satellite and web media, and church or mosque teaching per community.
- **Jewish community, Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and the *resande*:** check Yiddish and Romani teaching through their own associations.
- **Church of Sweden, free churches (Pentecostal, Mission Covenant) and Laestadians:** check confirmation classes, Sunday schools and lay preaching as learning settings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literacy history, dialects and media use; thinner for minority-language talk at home. Sweden's tracker records no lands settled for the nation and no change to its pre-war lands, so this sign covers Sweden as it stands today; which lands the post-war nation holds is still open.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish (with runic, Old Swedish and 1600s parish hands); Latin for Olaus Magnus; the five Sámi languages for revival material; Meänkieli and Finnish for valley schools; Syriac, Arabic and Somali for community schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Sámi nomad schools and the Swedish-only teaching of the Torne Valley are before two truth commissions and are contested ground; give survivors' and the state's accounts as accounts. Some yoiks belong to their bearers.
- **Held for review:** the Language Act and the national-minorities act behind the five minority languages were weighed and sent to Libra, keeping only the language bodies' own reports; no item for this domain was set aside under the South Asian rule.
