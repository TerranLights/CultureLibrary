# Sweden: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Inland Norrbotten, Västerbotten and Jämtland against Stockholm and the Mälardalen:** compare broadband maps, local reporting and library reach, because thin coverage decides which sources a household can use at all.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding:** check who counts Sámi and on whose terms, and how herding families get weather, grazing and predator information.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** set their news habits in Meänkieli and Finnish against their use of Swedish outlets.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Arabic-speaking and Kurdish households in Järva, Angered and Rosengård:** check how public-health information in 2020 reached them, as their community organizations and the agencies each describe it.
- **Kale, Kalderash and other Roma, and the *resande*:** read what their federations wrote after the 2013 police-register case about records kept on them.
- **Jewish community:** check its own periodicals as an information channel apart from mainstream media.
- **Older people across all regions:** check exclusion when news, banking and health moved to apps and e-identification.
- **Laestadian and free-church congregations:** check how each body decides which media to use or avoid, from their own publications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; Sweden keeps long registers, media surveys and newspaper archives. Thinner for news use in Sámi, Romani and the newer languages.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish of the 1600s for the first state newspaper; Schefferus's Latin; Somali, Arabic and Kurdish to follow 2020 health messages in the suburbs; Syriac for Södertälje broadcasts; North Sámi and Meänkieli for news bulletins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2013 discovery of a police register of Roma in Skåne and the race-biology records are contested ground on who may hold data on whom; give the communities' accounts and the authorities' side by side. Disinformation claims are often aimed at named groups; record who makes them.
- **Held for review:** the access-to-records and secrecy statutes and the rulings on personal-number data were passed to Libra, leaving only the words people use for them; no pointer here had to be withheld under the South Asian rule.
