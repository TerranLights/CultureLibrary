# Sweden: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with Dalecarlian and Norrland dialect kin words), North and Pite Sámi, Meänkieli, Sweden Finnish, Romani, Somali, Kurdish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

English keywords: Swedish twin registry, sibling relationships, birth order, half-siblings, blended families, sibling caregiving, war children, sibling violence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dalarna and Västergötland farm families:** check how farms passed among brothers and sisters, because heir customs differed across provinces.
- **Västerbotten and Norrbotten (CEDAR's parishes):** check sibling sets in large nineteenth-century families against today's two-child households.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding:** check how siblings share a herd, an earmark or a *sameby* place, from Sámi sources.
- **Laestadian families in Norrbotten:** check family size and sibling roles in their own publications, rather than in outsiders' claims.
- **Finnish war children and their families in Sweden:** check memoirs of sisters and brothers parted between two countries.
- **Somali, Kurdish and Syrian families:** check how older brothers and sisters interpret for parents and help with school, from community and youth-organization reports.
- **Kale and Kalderash Roma and the *resande*:** see when *phral* and *phen* are said to people outside the family, asking Roma speakers and associations directly.
- **Blended families in Stockholm, Göteborg and Malmö:** check how *bonussyskon* are talked about in parenting forums and family-therapy writing.
- **Jewish community:** check family histories of siblings separated by the Holocaust and reunited in Sweden.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registers, twins and household figures; good parish-record depth; thin for sibling duties in minority families, which need community sources.
- **Languages needed:** ballad Swedish and parish hands to trace sibling sets; Pite and North Sámi for age-graded kin words; Finnish for the war children's own memoirs; Yiddish for survivors' family letters; Somali, Kurdish and Arabic where older siblings' interpreting is reported.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sibling violence and children in care involve named minors; use aggregates. "Honor" debates around brothers' control over sisters are contested ground in the integration debates; give the state's category and community organizations' accounts side by side and never attach it to a people.
- **Held for review:** the 1845 change giving sisters equal inheritance with brothers, and today's inheritance code, were considered for sibling shares and passed to Libra; nothing called for the South Asian category.
