# Sweden: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, South and North Sámi, Meänkieli, Sweden Finnish, Bosnian, Spanish, Syriac, Tigrinya. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Villa owners against renters in public housing (*allmännyttan*):** check how each speaks of home, because both tenures are mainstream.
- **Mälardalen families with cottages in Roslagen, on Gotland or on Öland:** check how year-round islanders speak of summer arrivals, since one house is "home" to two groups.
- **Sámi herding families of each language area:** check winter and summer dwellings in Sámi accounts, because one family may have several homes in one year.
- **Torne Valley and Norrbotten:** check Meänkieli memoirs of farmhouses and of moving south for work, because "home" may mean the valley left behind.
- **Million Programme residents (Tensta, Rosengård, Gottsunda, Hammarkullen):** check tenants' own groups on renovations and on staying put.
- **Bosniaks, Chilean exiles, Eritreans, Iranians:** check refugee memoirs and associations for how a Swedish flat became home, and whether "back home" changed.
- **Jewish families and Holocaust survivors; Estonian Swedes; Assyrians and Syriacs in Södertälje:** check how each made a home after flight, through their own archives.
- **Kale and Kalderash Roma and the *resande*:** compare memoirs of camps and caravans with later flats, because their road to a fixed address ran through municipal housing decisions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for design history and second homes; mixed for what home means to people; thin in Meänkieli and Tigrinya sources.
- **Languages needed:** Old Swedish name forms for the *-hem* layer; South and North Sámi for dwelling words; Meänkieli for valley memoirs; Bosnian, Spanish, Syriac and Tigrinya for exiles' stories of a first Swedish flat.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eviction data touch families with children; use aggregates. Sámi dwelling sites and *goahti* places may carry family claims; ask the Sámi Parliament before naming sites.
- **Held for review:** the tenancy and tenant-owner acts behind *hyresrätt* and *bostadsrätt* were considered for the owning-against-renting facet and sent to Libra; the market side of the home goes to Taurus.
