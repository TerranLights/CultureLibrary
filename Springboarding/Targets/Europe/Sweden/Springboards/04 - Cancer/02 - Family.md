# Sweden: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, South and North Sámi, Meänkieli, Sweden Finnish, Persian, Arabic, Kurdish, Somali, Bosnian. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Västerbotten and Skellefteå:** check how the Bure kin and other old farm lineages are still traced, since a printed genealogy shapes who claims descent.
- **Sámi of the South Sámi area and the northern herding areas:** check kin terms and herding-group membership against each other, since the two may define family differently.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** check Finnish-language *sukuseura* gatherings and Meänkieli kin words, because some kin ties cross the Torne River into Finland.
- **Iranians, Iraqis, Syrians and Kurds:** check how the wider kin (*fāmil*, *ʿāʾila*) appear in their own media and associations alongside the Swedish household.
- **Somalis and Eritreans:** check how relatives abroad are counted as family, through remittance and reunification records, without assuming one pattern.
- **Bosniaks:** check families split by the 1990s war, through refugee associations' memory projects.
- **Rainbow families, adoptive families and blended families in Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö:** check each family association's own surveys.
- **Church of Sweden, Laestadian and Pentecostal congregations, Catholics, Muslim and Jewish congregations:** compare what each one's baptism and marriage-preparation material says kin owe one another.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registers, cohabitation research and adoption; mixed for wider kin networks; thin for South Sámi and Somali kin in Swedish settings.
- **Languages needed:** 1600s Swedish for *Bureätten*; German for Hasselbrink's dictionary; South and North Sámi for kin words; Finnish and Meänkieli for cross-border kin; Persian, Arabic, Kurdish, Somali and Bosnian for wider-kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan words (Somali *qabiil*, Kurdish tribal names) can be read as stereotypes in the integration debates; use community researchers and give accounts as accounts. International adoption is contested ground; give adoptees' accounts first.
- **Held for review:** the Parental Code and the family-reunification rules for migrants were weighed and passed to Libra; inheritance among kin goes to Scorpio.
