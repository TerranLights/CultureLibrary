## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Norrbotten, Jämtland and the herding areas:** check herders' predator reports and Sámi accounts of guarding herds, since protecting animals is a daily task there.
- **Sámi of each language area:** check what Sámi scholars accept from the missionary record of household guardians, and what they reject.
- **Skåne and the Öresund coast:** check local memories of the 1943 crossings and of Baltic refugees in 1944, since the coast was the landing place.
- **Rural districts against Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö:** check home-guard units and neighborhood safety groups, because whom people call first differs by distance to help.
- **Women and children in shelters, of every background:** compare shelters' own reports and the multilingual shelters' work, without tying violence to any people.
- **Jewish community:** check community security and survivors' testimony on who protected them.
- **Somali, Syrian, Iraqi, Kurdish and Eritrean parents:** check their associations' material on keeping children safe in a new country.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and *resande* families:** set the white paper's survivor testimony beside agency files, since removals were once done in the name of protecting children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for child-protection bodies and defense volunteers; mixed for folk protection; thin for how minority households guard their own.
- **Languages needed:** older Swedish for the missionary reports and legend collections; North and South Sámi for the *áhkká* tradition; Danish for the 1943 accounts; Arabic, Persian, Kurdish, Somali and Bosnian for parents' groups; Yiddish for survivors' writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi pre-Christian religion is a living and partly restricted heritage; follow Sámi scholars and ask before quoting. Protected-identity data concern people in danger; use counts only. Sweden's wartime neutrality is contested ground; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** the medieval home-peace (*hemfrid*) clauses of Magnus Eriksson's Land Law and today's child-welfare act (LVU) were considered and sent to Libra and Capricorn; the Girjas reindeer-rights case goes the same way.
