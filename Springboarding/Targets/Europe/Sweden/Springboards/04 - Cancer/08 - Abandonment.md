# Sweden: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, North Sámi, Meänkieli, Sweden Finnish, Arabic, Persian, Somali, Bosnian. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Värmland, Småland and Halland:** check emigration-era parishes for wives and children left behind, since emigration rates differed by province.
- **Jämtland and the inland north:** check abandoned farms from the plague years and today's depopulated villages side by side.
- **Tornedalians and Sámi children of the workhouse and nomad-school era:** check the truth commissions' testimony and the communities' own accounts first.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and *resande* families:** trace children placed with strangers through the families' memoirs, then hold the welfare boards' files against them.
- **Adoptees from Korea, Colombia, Ethiopia, Chile and elsewhere:** check each adoptee group's own searches and reports.
- **Bosniaks, Somalis, Eritreans, Syrians:** check how families describe kin left in camps or at home, through their associations.
- **Elders living alone in Stockholm, Malmö and inland municipalities:** check Red Cross and municipal outreach, since being left alone takes different forms in city and countryside.
- **Parishes and congregations of every faith:** find who attends funerals of the kinless, as each one records it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundling archives, care inquiries and maintenance figures; mixed for emigrants' families; thin for children left in other countries by migrants now in Sweden.
- **Languages needed:** older Swedish hands for foundling and parish records; Meänkieli, Finnish and North Sámi for workhouse testimony; Korean and Spanish for adoptees' searches; Arabic, Persian, Somali and Bosnian for kin left behind.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** care-inquiry testimony and workhouse histories name survivors; use published testimony. International adoption and the *resande* removals are contested ground; give adoptees' and survivors' accounts as accounts. Children's names in foundling records need care even after a century.
- **Held for review:** the poor-relief acts behind *bortauktionering* and today's child-welfare act (LVU) were considered and sent to Libra and Capricorn; the 1900s child-removal debates go to Capricorn for their spirit.
