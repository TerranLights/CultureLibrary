# Sweden: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, North and South Sámi, Meänkieli, Arabic, Persian, Somali, Kurdish, Tigrinya, Yiddish, Romani. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Västerbotten and Norrbotten revival villages (Laestadian, Pentecostal):** check memoirs of mothers in large households, since family size and faith set the work.
- **Stockholm, Göteborg and Malmö:** check mothers' age at first birth and work after leave, because the registers show city figures pulling apart from rural ones.
- **Sámi mothers, herding and non-herding:** check Sáráhkká and Sámi writers on bearing and raising children far from maternity wards.
- **Tornedalian and Sweden Finn mothers:** check memories of raising children in a language the school forbade or ignored.
- **Women judged "unfit" under the sterilization laws, including *resande* and Roma women:** check survivors' testimony first and the boards' files second.
- **Somali, Eritrean, Syrian, Kurdish and Iranian mothers:** check midwives' and community reports on birth care, without assuming one experience.
- **Single mothers and mothers in rainbow families:** compare association surveys, since the forms and benefits name them differently.
- **Catholic, Syriac Orthodox and Eritrean Orthodox congregations:** check Marian feasts and mothers' groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the birth register and the midwives' archives run deep, and the policy quarrels are well printed; mothers speaking outside print are harder to find, and Roma and Eritrean mothers' accounts are scarce, so start with their associations.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old Swedish for Birgitta; 1690s Swedish for von Hoorn; North and South Sámi for Sámi women's writing; Meänkieli and Finnish for valley mothers; Arabic, Persian, Somali, Kurdish and Tigrinya for maternity-care reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the sterilization laws of 1934-76 and the State Institute for Racial Biology (Uppsala, 1922) are contested ground; give survivors', the state's and historians' accounts as accounts, and name no person from case files. Birth-register data are restricted to aggregates.
- **Held for review:** the Parental Code's rules on legal motherhood and the abortion law were considered and sent to Libra (abortion as autonomy goes to Aries); the sterilization compensation act goes to Libra as law.
