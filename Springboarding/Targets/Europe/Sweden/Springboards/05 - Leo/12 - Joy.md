# Sweden: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with West Swedish and Scanian speech), North Sámi, Meänkieli, Yiddish, Syriac, Kurdish, Persian, Arabic, Somali, Tigrinya, Bosnian. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Värmland and Dalarna:** compare Lagerlöf's Ekeby and the fiddlers' gatherings with local memory of feasts and dances.
- **Gothenburg and Skåne against Stockholm:** check comedy, slang of praise and festival calendars for what each city counts as a good time.
- **Norrland's long dark against the south:** compare how winter and the return of light are greeted, through local festivals and press.
- **Sámi of each language area, herding and non-herding:** check city festivals against home-district events for what organizers choose to share.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** compare valley festivals with Finnish-language events in the Mälardalen.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs, Kurds, Iranians, Iraqis, Syrians:** check New Year and wedding customs through associations' calendars, by city.
- **Somalis, Eritreans, Bosniaks, Chilean exiles:** check music and wedding celebrations in each community's own media.
- **The Jewish community; Kale, Kalderash and *resande* families:** look at Purim and Hanukkah events and at Romani wedding music, as each group's associations present them.
- **Laestadians, Pentecostal and Mission Covenant congregations, Church of Sweden, Muslim communities:** compare what each one names as rightful joy and as worldly pleasure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, hymns and folk music; mixed for felt joy; thin for joy in newer communities outside festival programs.
- **Languages needed:** 1600s Swedish for Lucidor and Kolmodin; North Sámi for Umeå programs; Meänkieli for Pajala; Syriac, Kurdish and Persian for New Year programs; Somali, Tigrinya and Bosnian for wedding music.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** singing a hymn at school-end ceremonies is contested ground; give each side as an account. Drink at feasts touches health debates; keep the songs here and drinking to Pisces.
- **Held for review:** the rules on hymns and religious elements in school ceremonies and on alcohol sales were weighed and sent to Libra; well-being scores drawn from outside rankings are kept as one pointer only.
