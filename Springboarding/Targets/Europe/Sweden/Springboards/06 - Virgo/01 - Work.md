# Sweden: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, North Sámi, Meänkieli, Sweden Finnish, Romani, Yiddish, Swedish Sign Language, Syriac, Somali, Spanish (Chilean). **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bergslagen ironworks towns against Norrbotten mining towns (Kiruna, Malmberget):** compare memoirs and union papers on shifts, danger and closures.
- **Gothenburg's shipyard districts:** compare yard workers' accounts of the 1970s-80s closures with those of the Malmö Kockums yard.
- **Småland's Gnosjö area against Stockholm's office economy:** compare how small-firm owners and salaried staff describe long hours.
- **Skåne and Östergötland farm country:** compare *statare* memories with crofters' in Värmland and Dalarna.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding, in the North, Lule and South Sámi areas:** check whether Sámi writers treat herding as a job, a livelihood or a way of life, and how wage work enters.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** check Meänkieli and Finnish records of who left for forest, mine or factory work.
- **Kale and Kalderash Roma and the *resande*:** trace which family trades faded after the 1950s, in their own associations' memory work.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs, Somalis, Eritreans, Iranians, Iraqis, Syrians, Chilean exiles:** check labor-force data by country of birth and associations' accounts of credentials and first jobs.
- **Women and men:** compare paid and unpaid hours in SCB's time-use and labor-force data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics and labor history; mixed for informal and unpaid work; thin for Traveller and Roma trades outside police and parish files.
- **Languages needed:** early 1500s Swedish for Peder Månsson; Yiddish for Jewish trades; North Sámi for herding accounts; Meänkieli and Finnish for valley work; Syriac, Somali and Spanish for newer communities' first-job stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the state's role in deciding who counted as a herder is contested and tied to today's Sámi land and herding rights; newcomers' employment is an open political debate. Present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** the employment-protection and eight-hour-day statutes were considered for overwork and job security and went to Libra; the duty-to-work side of the work ethic is left to Capricorn.
