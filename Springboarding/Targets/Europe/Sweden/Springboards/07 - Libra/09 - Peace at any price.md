# Sweden: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish, North Sámi, Meänkieli and Finnish, Estonian, Latvian, Kurdish, Arabic, Persian, Somali. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Norrbotten and the iron-ore towns (Kiruna, Luleå):** check local memory of wartime ore exports and transit, against Stockholm's official histories.
- **Värmland and Jämtland:** look at how people by the Norwegian border recall German transit trains in 1940-43.
- **Gotland:** check island accounts of wartime and Cold War exposure in the Baltic.
- **Estonian Swedes and Baltic refugees:** compare their associations' accounts of 1944-46 with the government's.
- **Sámi:** check how forced relocations and schooling were met with silence or protest, in Sámi testimony.
- **Tornedalians:** look at how families describe keeping quiet about Meänkieli at school.
- **Kurds:** read Kurdish associations' responses to the NATO concessions beside the government's.
- **The Jewish community:** look at community responses to the 1990s debates on wartime Sweden.
- **Residents of the Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö neighborhoods Brå studied:** check how tenants' and local associations themselves describe pressure to keep quiet.
- **Kalderash and Kale Roma, and Traveller families:** look for accounts of when keeping a low profile with authorities was chosen and why, from Roma and *resande* organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime policy and labor law; mixed for workplace and family silence; thin for minority communities' own words on giving way.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and German for wartime files; Estonian and Latvian for Baltic exile papers; North Sámi; Meänkieli and Finnish; Kurdish, Arabic, Persian and Somali.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime neutrality, the extradition and the NATO concessions are contested ground: give each account by name. "Stockholm syndrome" is a disputed label. Brå material touches crime in named areas: never let it describe a people.
- **Held for review:** the state-care abuse inquiry and the 1943 rescue of Danish Jews were weighed; the first was already scouted under Cancer and the second fits courage better, so both were left out; no wartime or exile source here needed holding under the naming rules.
