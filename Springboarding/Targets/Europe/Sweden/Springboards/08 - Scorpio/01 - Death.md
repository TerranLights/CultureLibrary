# Sweden: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with medieval Swedish and Latin), North Sámi, Meänkieli, Yiddish, Arabic, Syriac, Kurdish, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning; funeral practice sits in the domain sheet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Småland (Värend) against Norrbotten:** set folk-belief records of revenants beside the famine dead of 1867-69 in parish books.
- **Gothenburg's Hisingen:** look at how the 1998 dance-hall fire is remembered, through the victims' families and the memorial site.
- **Sámi of the several language areas:** compare herding and non-herding families' accounts of youth suicide, through Sámi-run studies.
- **Tornedalians:** read Laestadian funeral sermons for how the dying are spoken of.
- **Jewish community:** check Holocaust-survivor families' *yortsayt* and memorial customs.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs:** look at Sayfo commemorations and the 2010 Riksdag vote, presenting each account.
- **Bosniaks and Kurds:** compare Srebrenica (July 11) and Halabja (March 16) remembrance events in Swedish towns.
- **Chilean exiles and Eritreans:** look for memorials to the disappeared, and to those lost at sea on the way.
- **Roma and the *resande*:** check what Roma and Traveller organizations publish on remembering wartime persecution.
- **Church of Sweden, Catholics and Pentecostals:** compare teaching on purgatory, prayer for the dead and resurrection.
- **Compared only, outside the target:** Norway, Finland (Åland and Swedish-speaking Finns too), Denmark, Estonia, Iceland; Swedish-American families, under the nation's findings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality and disaster reports; mixed for afterlife belief and grief; thin for minority mourning in its own languages.
- **Languages needed:** medieval Latin and Swedish for Birgitta and church art; nineteenth-century Swedish for folk belief; North Sámi; Meänkieli; Syriac, Kurdish and Arabic for commemorations; Chilean Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, child death and the Sámi youth figures call for care. Assisted dying, Sayfo recognition and the counting of COVID deaths in elder care are disputed: give each side's account by name.
- **Held for review:** contact with the dead through mediums belongs to Pisces; among the grief and afterlife pointers, none was set aside.
