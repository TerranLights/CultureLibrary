# Sweden: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with Old Swedish and Old Norse), Sweden Finnish, Meänkieli, Arabic, Persian, Bosnian, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Danderyd and Djursholm against Stockholm's southern and western suburbs:** compare segregation-barometer scores and school results.
- **Skåne's estate parishes against Norrland smallholdings:** check estate records beside freeholders' records for the length of the ladder.
- **Bergslagen mill towns:** compare house sizes and seating in the mill church by rank.
- **Gothenburg:** set the merchant houses of the 1700s China trade beside dockworkers' union histories.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding:** examine how the 1928 reindeer-grazing law split Sámi into rights-holders and others, from Sámi historians' own accounts.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** look at Finnish-language writing on *herrat* and on foremen.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and the *resande*:** compare their organizations' accounts of state categories with the race-hygiene files.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs (Södertälje):** read church sources on clergy ranks as each church describes them.
- **Iranians, Iraqis, Syrians, Kurds, Bosniaks, Somalis, Eritreans, Chilean exiles:** check UHR decisions and labor statistics on how arrival status and credentials sort newcomers.
- **Jewish community, free churches and the Church of Sweden:** compare how lay and ordained ranks are set, as each describes itself.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nobility, estate and income records; mixed for hierarchy at work (management writing outweighs workers' voices); thin for rank inside smaller communities.
- **Languages needed:** Old Norse and Old Swedish for the poem and the ordinance; Finnish and Meänkieli; Arabic, Persian, Bosnian and Spanish for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race-hygiene records rank real people and some are restricted; the Sámi split under the 1928 law and the maid debate are contested; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; the race-hygiene sources were kept as pointers to read critically, not set aside.
