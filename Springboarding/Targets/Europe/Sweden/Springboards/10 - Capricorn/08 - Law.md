# Sweden: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Sweden, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Swedish (with Old Swedish), North Sámi, Meänkieli, Arabic, Yiddish and Hebrew, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Capricorn takes the spirit of the law; its letter is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Skåne and Blekinge:** check how Danish law gave way to Swedish law after 1683, and what local custom survived.
- **Gotland:** look at how long *Gutalagen* stayed in use and how islanders spoke of their own law.
- **Jämtland and Härjedalen:** set the Taxed Mountains case beside local farmers' and Sámi accounts of the same land.
- **Torne Valley:** collect oral history and customs files on cross-river trade, where law and neighborliness parted ways.
- **Sámi, herding and non-herding:** compare customary-rights claims by *samebyar* with those of Sámi outside them.
- **Roma (Kalderash and Kale) and the *resande*:** ask Roma organizations whether the *kris* is described for outsiders, and read *resande* associations on the courts.
- **Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö suburbs:** read police reports on "parallel social structures" beside residents' and community organizations' replies, as contested accounts.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs, Somalis, Kurds, Iraqis, Syrians, Iranians:** check how family mediation is described by each community's own bodies and by social services.
- **Jewish community and Muslim communities:** see how each congregation's bodies describe religious rules beside Swedish law.
- **Chilean exiles and Bosniaks:** compare exile writing on law under dictatorship and war with life under Swedish law.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal theory, inquiries and trust indexes; mixed for how laypeople relate to law; thin for law as Roma, *resande* and newer communities live it.
- **Languages needed:** Old Swedish for the prefaces; early modern Swedish for Olaus Petri; North Sámi and Meänkieli; Arabic, Hebrew, Spanish and Bosnian for community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sámi land rights, the sex-purchase ban, drug policy and the "parallel structures" debate are contested; present each as an account and name each party. Prison files name private people.
- **Held for review:** none; the letter-of-the-law readings (statute wording, case holdings) that arose were left to Libra, not held.
