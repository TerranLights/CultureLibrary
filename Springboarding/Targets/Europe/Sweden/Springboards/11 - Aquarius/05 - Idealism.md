## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hälsingland and Dalarna's revival parishes:** check how sect departures were recorded by clergy and by the leavers.
- **Torne Valley Laestadians and Västerbotten's free churches:** compare ideals of plain living in sermons against household records.
- **Uppsala and Lund academics against Gothenburg and Malmö activists:** check which ideals each set organized around, from society rolls.
- **Södermanland and Värmland manors' reform schools:** compare idealist ventures run by landowners with those run by movements.
- **Sámi, herding and not:** separate ideals voiced by Sámi writers from outsiders' romantic images, labeling the second as outsiders'.
- **Sweden Finns and Tornedalians:** look in their own journals for ideals of language and community.
- **Assyrians and Syriacs, Kurds, Iranians, Chileans, Eritreans:** compare the ideals each exile press set out, and the disillusion pieces that followed.
- **Jewish community and Muslim communities:** check idealist youth movements in each body's own records.
- **The 1970s green-wave settlers in Jämtland, Härjedalen and Småland:** compare settlers' and villagers' memories of the same years.
- **Comparison only:** Nordic and Baltic neighbors' utopias (Norway, Finland, Denmark, Estonia, Iceland) stay as outside references; the Bishop Hill colony in Illinois counts as Sweden's diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for movements, writers and surveys; mixed for 1970s settlers; thin for newer communities' ideals outside their own periodicals.
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Swedenborg; eighteenth-century Swedish for Wadström's circle; Sámi and Meänkieli for Utsi and Laestadius; Syriac, Arabic, Persian and Spanish for exile ideals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "humanitarian great power" label and the integration debates are contested; quote each side. Romantic images of the Sámi are outsiders' stereotypes, to be named as such.
- **Held for review:** none; the visionary and utopian pointers weighed here were Christian, secular or exile-political, with nothing set aside.
