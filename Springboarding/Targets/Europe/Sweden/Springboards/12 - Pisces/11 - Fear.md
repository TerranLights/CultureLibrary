## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Stockholm and the Mälardalen:** compare plague-year and 2017 records for how the capital frames threat and response.
- **Uppland, Östergötland, Skåne:** check whether bogeyman figures and water dangers differ between plains and coast.
- **Värmland, Dalarna, Gästrikland:** set wolf-fear accounts from farmers, herders and hunters beside conservationists'.
- **Gothenburg and Västra Götaland, Småland, Halland, Blekinge:** compare SOM worry answers by region and town size.
- **Gotland and Öland:** look for fears of the sea and of enemies from the east in island memory.
- **Jämtland, Härjedalen, Västerbotten, Norrbotten, the Torne Valley:** compare lake-monster lore, mining-town fears (Kiruna's ground) and border memories.
- **The Sámi (South, Ume, Pite, Lule, North; herding and non-herding):** find Sámi fears as Sámi media report them (predators, land loss, threats), not as outsiders assume.
- **Tornedalians and Sweden Finns:** look at what Torne Valley and Sweden Finnish parents feared for children in Swedish-only schools and workplaces, from their associations' own oral-history work.
- **Jews and Muslim communities:** compare each one's own security reports and what each says it fears.
- **Roma (Kale, Kalderash) and *resande*:** read their own accounts of dread at a social worker's or police officer's visit, across the twentieth century.
- **Bosniaks, Kurds, Iraqis, Syrians, Iranians, Somalis, Eritreans, Assyrians and Syriacs, Chilean exiles:** compare war-trauma clinics' reports with each community's own words for fear.
- **Comparison only:** Norway (22 July 2011), Denmark, Finland (Åland and the Finland Swedes included), Estonia and Iceland, for how each lives with its own scares; Sweden's diaspora takes its findings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fear of crime, worry polls and legend archives; patchier for anxiety at home; sparse where newer communities would speak of fear in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Early Modern Swedish for plague records; dialect for Isof; Sámi for legends; Hebrew and Yiddish, Arabic, Kurdish, Somali and Tigrinya for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** terror, wolves, nuclear power and community security are contested or painful; present each account as an account, name communities by their own names, and never state what a group fears as a trait.
- **Held for review:** fear of the dead and of ghosts was weighed here but left to the domain sheet's hidden places to avoid doubling; no naming-rule case arose for fear.
