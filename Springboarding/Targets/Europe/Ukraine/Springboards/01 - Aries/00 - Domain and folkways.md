# Ukraine: The domain, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, beginnings, and the naming ways (Fischer's folkway for Aries), with sport ways as runner-up.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia against the Left Bank (Poltava, Chernihiv):** check whether self-placement answers differ under Habsburg and Russian imperial pasts.
- **Hutsuls, Boykos, Lemkos:** compare dress and household roles as markers in each highland group, not one "Carpathian" type.
- **Rusyns of Transcarpathia:** separate those naming themselves Rusyn from those naming themselves Ukrainian, and how each fills a census form.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check self-names and life-cycle rites carried through the *Sürgünlik* and return.
- **Karaim and Krymchaks:** check where each draws its own name and line.
- **Hungarians, Romanians, Bulgarians, Gagauz, Azov Greeks:** compare double naming (state form, home form) in each.
- **Russian speakers of the Donbas, Kharkiv and Odesa:** check how daily language and self-named nationality line up or part.
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions), Greek Catholics, Protestants:** compare baptismal naming by church calendar.
- **Jewish, Mennonite and German colony communities:** check naming records before destruction or departure.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Ukrainian ethnography and surveys; mixed for Crimean Tatars and Rusyns; thin for Krymchaks, Gagauz and several Roma groups (route: community organizations, specialist ethnographies).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with Hutsul and Polissian dialect dictionaries, Russian, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Yiddish, Polish.
- **Lands:** Ukraine's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for Ukraine as the sources treat it, Crimea and the east as lands with their own peoples, settling no border.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** self-identification questions are politicized, more so since 2014 and 2022; occupied areas' data is unreachable or unsafe; deportation and Holocaust material needs care.
- **Held for review:** none; the naming and body pointers drew only on Ukrainian, minority, Jewish and outsider sources, with nothing to set aside.
