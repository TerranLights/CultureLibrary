# Ukraine: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Bazaar traders and shoppers in Odesa, Kharkiv and Lviv:** check how haggling formulas and complaint talk differ by city and language.
- **Villages of Polissia and Podillia against Kyiv:** check who speaks first at a village gathering against a city office meeting.
- **Women and men:** compare how complaints, refusals and testimony are received, using the 2016 campaign and workplace surveys.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check the collective petition tradition of exile against individual claims today.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** compare minority-association speech with Ukrainian-language local media on the same disputes.
- **Greek Catholics and Protestants (Baptists, Pentecostals):** check how churches that lived underground teach saying no to authority.
- **Roma (Servy, Kelderari, Transcarpathian Roma):** check Roma rights groups' advocacy material; sources are thin, so start with the organizations' own reports.
- **Veterans since 2014:** check how they voice claims to the state against civilians' petitions.
- **Russian speakers of the Donbas before 2014:** check local talk-show and press archives where reachable.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest and civic surveys and for dissident texts; mixed for everyday speech etiquette; thin for Roma and Gagauz.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian for talk shows and petitions; Crimean Tatar for the movement's own papers; Hungarian; Yiddish or English translations for Hasidic texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** standing one's ground is now bound up with war and occupation; interviews in occupied areas are unsafe; protest accounts are partisan and need cross-reading.
- **Held for review:** empty. A chronicle, dissident papers, petitions and TV debates raised no standing-rule question for speaking up.
