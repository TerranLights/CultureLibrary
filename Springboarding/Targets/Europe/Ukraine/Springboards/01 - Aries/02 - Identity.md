# Ukraine: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Romanian, Hungarian, Bulgarian, the Greek of the Azov coast (Rumeic) and Urum, Yiddish, Polish. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia, Volhynia and the Left Bank:** check how each answers "who are you" under different imperial and church histories; set the Jewish, Mennonite and German colony records beside them.
- **Rusyns and Ukrainians of Transcarpathia:** compare self-names in census, church and school, and who uses which.
- **Hutsuls, Boykos, Lemkos:** check whether each names itself as a branch of Ukrainians or as its own people, and in which settings.
- **Romanians and self-named Moldovans of Bukovina and the Budjak:** check which name appears where, without settling the dispute.
- **Bulgarians and Gagauz of the Budjak:** compare village associations and school curricula as identity carriers.
- **Crimean Tatars, Karaim, Krymchaks:** check each people's own name and its relation to the Indigenous-peoples term.
- **Azov Greeks (Rumei and Urum):** compare the two language groups' self-names; much of the community has been displaced since 2022.
- **Russian-speaking Ukrainians and Russians of Ukraine:** separate language from self-named nationality, by city.
- **Poles of Zhytomyr and Lviv; Roma groups:** check Polish parish records and Roma groups' own names for themselves; Roma sources are scarce, so start with community organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national-identity polling and history; mixed for regional groups; thin for Krymchaks, Urum speakers and Gagauz.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian throughout; Crimean Tatar (Latin and older Cyrillic script); Rusyn; Romanian; Hungarian; Rumeic and Urum need specialists.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** identity is a front line of the war; labels such as "Little Russian", "Moldovan" or "Rusyn" are each disputed; occupied Crimea and Mariupol are out of reach; present each claim as a named group's account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; genetic-ancestry studies were considered and left out as outside the concept's cultural focus rather than set aside under any rule.
