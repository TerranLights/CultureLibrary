## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician and Volhynian towns with Magdeburg pasts against steppe towns founded later:** check whether town self-government left different civic habits.
- **Zaporizhzhia and the old Sich lands:** check how Cossack council memory is used locally today.
- **Amalgamated hromadas in the west, center and south:** compare how much the reform changed each, using evaluation data.
- **Rusyns and Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** separate the two autonomy claims and each one's own organizations.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check the Qurultay and Mejlis tradition of collective self-rule; current access is blocked.
- **Bulgarians and Gagauz of the Budjak:** check requests for cultural self-rule; Moldova's Gagauz scholarship only where it bears on Ukraine's villages.
- **Old Believers (Lipovans) of Vylkove and the Danube:** check parish self-governance; sources are few, so start with regional museums.
- **Women and men, young and old:** compare who decides on marriage, moving out and health care, using household surveys.
- **Baptists and Pentecostals:** check how self-governing congregations decide against bishop-led churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective self-rule in history; mixed for personal autonomy (survey items exist, ethnography is thinner); thin for Lipovans and Gagauz.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Makhno and imperial records; Latin, Polish and Old Ukrainian for town books; Hebrew and Yiddish for kahal records; German for colony papers; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** autonomy claims in Crimea, Transcarpathia and the east are bound up with war and separatism; reproductive choice and the state's legal frame go to Libra and Capricorn; separate each group's own account from outside readings.
- **Held for review:** none. Comparative federalism studies from abroad were weighed and left for later, as they say little about lived choice; no standing rule applied.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Kohut's introduction for the Hetmanate's autonomy claims, then sample a published Lviv magistrate book.
2. On decentralization.gov.ua, download the hromada lists and pick one per macro-region for a case study.
3. «Закарпатський референдум 1991» in library catalogs and the regional press archive in Uzhhorod.
4. Pull the WVS Ukraine freedom-of-choice item across waves and set it beside the MICS decision items.
5. Look up Urry's chapters on colony administration and follow his archival notes.
