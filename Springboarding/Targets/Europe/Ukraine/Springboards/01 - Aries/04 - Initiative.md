# Ukraine: Initiative (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Romanian, Bulgarian, Hungarian, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** starting things and acting without being told; taking the lead unasked, at work, in business, in civic life and at home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia's co-op villages against Left Bank collective-farm villages:** check how memory of self-organized co-ops affects starting things today.
- **Kyiv and Lviv IT hubs against the Donbas and Kryvyi Rih industrial towns:** compare business-start data and employers' expectations of acting unasked.
- **Odesa and Kharkiv market traders:** check whether 1990s trading is remembered as initiative or as shame, by speaker.
- **Crimean Tatar returnees:** check the record of self-organized settlement against official allotment.
- **Bulgarians, Gagauz and Romanians of the Budjak and Bukovina:** compare *clacă* and similar work parties with Ukrainian *толока*.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check cross-border small business and its Hungarian-language press.
- **Women and men in the volunteer movement:** compare who started the groups against who ran them.
- **Roma (several groups):** check traditional trades and present-day small business through Roma organizations' reports; scholarship is scarce.
- **Mennonite and German colonies:** check colony records for who began new crops, mills and schools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering since 2014, IT and business statistics; mixed for village work parties (old ethnography, little recent); thin for Gagauz and Roma enterprise.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for 1990s trade and much business press; Church Slavonic and Polish for brotherhood papers; Romanian and Bulgarian for southern work parties; Yiddish or translations for shtetl trade.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** volunteer and defense initiatives may involve security-sensitive detail; occupied Crimea's reporting carries risk to sources; shuttle trade overlaps with smuggling, so keep to the traders' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** none. Ratings of startup ecosystems by foreign firms came up and were dropped as thin on how people start things; no standing rule was involved.
