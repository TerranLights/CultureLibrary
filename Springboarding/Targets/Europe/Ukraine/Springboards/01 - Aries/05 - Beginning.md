## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsuls and Boykos against Podillia and the Dnipro villages:** compare first-visitor and New Year sowing customs, and who may do them.
- **Bukovina's Ukrainians and Romanians:** check how each tells the Malanka's origin and its order of masks.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** compare village founding stories and New Year blessings.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check seasonal openings kept in exile and after return.
- **Odesa's Russian speakers, Ukrainians, Jews and Greeks:** separate their versions of the city's beginning.
- **Breslov pilgrims and Uman residents:** check how each side describes the new-year gathering.
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions) and Greek Catholics:** compare which calendar sets the start of the feast year, given the 2023 calendar change in several churches.
- **Displaced people from the east and south:** check how starting over differs by origin, age and language.
- **Mennonite and German colonies:** check how colony chronicles marked their founding years.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the folk calendar and birth rites; mixed for founding debates (partisan); thin for Crimean Tatar seasonal rites in print.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with dialect help for Carpathian rites; Church Slavonic for the chronicle and Patericon; Romanian; Bulgarian; Hebrew and Yiddish; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** origin narratives are weapons in the war (Rus' heritage claims); the calendar shift is a church-jurisdiction issue; displacement data concerns vulnerable people.
- **Held for review:** none. Popular "ancient origins" books claiming direct descent from Trypillia were left out as unreliable sources, not under a standing rule.
