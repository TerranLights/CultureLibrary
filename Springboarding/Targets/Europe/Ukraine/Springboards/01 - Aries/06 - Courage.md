# Ukraine: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost: physical, moral and social courage, endurance, and the courage to leave.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zaporizhzhia steppe and Cossack heritage sites against the Hutsul Carpathians:** compare Cossack and outlaw hero models in local lore.
- **Galicia and Volhynia against the east:** check which wartime figures each region honors, given the contested record of the OUN and UPA and the Volhynia massacres.
- **Jewish communities:** check rescue, self-defense and wartime memoirs in Yiddish and Russian, Babyn Yar testimony included.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare refusal and nonviolent protest models with armed-courage models elsewhere.
- **Women and men:** check how awards, films and surveys count each one's bravery.
- **Russian-speaking cities (Kharkiv, Odesa, Dnipro):** separate language from stance in readiness-to-resist data.
- **Hungarians, Romanians and Bulgarians of the west and south:** check minority press on their own soldiers and rescuers.
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions), Greek Catholics, Protestants:** compare how chaplains and sermons speak of fear and sacrifice.
- **Roma (several groups):** check Roma soldiers' and rescuers' stories through Roma organizations; written sources are few.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war, epic and dissident material; mixed for minorities; thin for Roma and Gagauz courage narratives in print.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Babel and much military writing; Crimean Tatar; Yiddish and Hebrew for rescue testimony; Hungarian and Romanian for the west.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** courage is now a state and wartime theme; hero lists include contested figures (OUN, UPA); Sheptytsky's record is disputed; some military details may be restricted; avoid stating what any group is.
- **Held for review:** none. Unverified social-media hero stories were set aside for reliability; no item met a standing rule.
