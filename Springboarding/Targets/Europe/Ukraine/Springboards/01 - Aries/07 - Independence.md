## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia and Podillia villages against Kyiv, Dnipro and Kharkiv:** compare reliance on the household plot against wages and transfers.
- **Hutsuls and Boykos:** check summer-pasture and forest self-provisioning in each, with their own terms.
- **Galicia and Bukovina against the Left Bank:** compare labor-migration shares and how families describe living on remittances.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check post-return households' self-built homes and farming, and the pressure on them after 2014.
- **Russian-speaking industrial towns of the Donbas and Kryvyi Rih:** check what happened to self-reliance talk when mines and plants closed.
- **Hungarians and Romanians of the west:** compare cross-border work and dual citizenship as household strategies.
- **Elders living alone, especially in the east:** check aid-agency data on isolation and refusal of help.
- **Disabled people:** separate their own organizations' framing from state categories of dependence.
- **Jewish communities:** compare historical mutual-aid societies with today's welfare centers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household plots, remittances and the 1991 vote; mixed for attitudes to help (a few survey waves); thin for Hutsul pasture life today.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for many social-policy reports; Hungarian and Romanian for cross-border work; Crimean Tatar; Polish for migrant-work studies in Poland (comparison only).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national independence is a war theme; aid statistics touch vulnerable people; diaspora and refugee data abroad are comparison only, not units of Ukraine.
- **Held for review:** none. Western homesteading and survivalist literature was weighed as a comparison and dropped as foreign to this nation's sources; no rule-based hold.
