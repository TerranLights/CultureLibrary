## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kyiv and Donetsk football cultures, and Lviv's Karpaty:** check how each club's history frames rivalry with the others.
- **Transcarpathian villages against Dnipro villages:** compare ritual games and youth contests, using Bogatyrev and later fieldwork.
- **Rural and urban school leavers:** check external-test gaps by setting, region and language.
- **Hasidic courts and their followers:** compare court chronicles with outside accounts of their rivalries.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check *küreş* and other festival contests as kept in exile and after return.
- **Bulgarians and Gagauz of the Budjak:** check wrestling and horse-race contests at village feasts; scholarship is thin, so start with regional museums.
- **Hungarians and Romanians of the west:** compare minority-language school results in the testing data.
- **Women and men:** check how sport, talent contests and testing data split by gender.
- **Roma (several groups):** check Roma organizations' own records of sport and school contests, group by group, since published sources are scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for testing, sport and media contests; mixed for folk games (old ethnography); thin for Budjak and Crimean Tatar contests in print.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Soviet-era sport writing; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian and Romanian for minority schools; Hebrew and Yiddish for Hasidic sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** football rivalries carry wartime and political charges (Shakhtar's exile from Donetsk since 2014); the "Death Match" is a contested memory; Eurovision selection disputes tie to the war.
- **Held for review:** none. A betting-industry report on Ukrainian sport was weighed and dropped as promotional; no standing rule applied.
