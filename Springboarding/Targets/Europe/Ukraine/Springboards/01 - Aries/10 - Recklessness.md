## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zaporizhzhia steppe and Cossack heritage against Galician towns:** check how local museums and lore treat daring that ends in disaster.
- **Donbas and Kryvyi Rih mining towns:** separate risk forced by work from risk chosen, using miners' own accounts.
- **Rural and urban drivers:** compare road deaths and drunk driving by oblast and setting.
- **Teenage boys and girls:** check HBSC splits by gender and region.
- **Hutsul and Transcarpathian river trades:** compare raftsmen's songs and records in Ukrainian and Hungarian.
- **Odesa's Russian-speaking and Jewish heritage:** check how outlaw lore is told by the city's museums against popular TV.
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions) and Greek Catholic believers at Epiphany:** compare clergy guidance on ice-hole plunges.
- **Crimean Tatars, Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check whether their own folklore has rash-hero or cautionary figures; sources are scarce, so start with folklore anthologies and community museums.
- **Communities near contaminated or mined land (Polissia, the east and south):** compare how residents weigh warnings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health and road statistics; mixed for lore of daring; thin for minority folklore on rashness.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Odesa lore, 1990s press and much mining reporting; Hungarian for Transcarpathian river trades; Crimean Tatar and Bulgarian for folklore anthologies; Old East Slavic for the Igor tale.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime risk-taking by soldiers and civilians must not be framed as recklessness without their own accounts; mine-accident and mining-disaster data concern victims; drinking statistics invite stereotypes, so present them by subgroup and setting.
- **Held for review:** none; addiction-treatment program files were left aside for privacy, not because of a standing rule.
