# Ukraine: The domain, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Yiddish (historical), Romani. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, values and self-worth, with food ways, dress ways and wealth ways as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Habsburg-ruled west (Galicia, Bukovina) and the Left Bank:** check whether *ґазда* and *хазяїн* vocabularies follow the old imperial line in inventories.
- **Hutsuls and Boykos:** compare livestock, pasture and woven goods as stores of wealth in each group's ethnographies.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check court inventories, then property lost in 1944 and claims since the return, as the community records them.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** compare market trades and dowry records in Podillia and Volhynia with town records.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** check farm size and inheritance practice against nearby villages.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check dowry chests and vineyards in village museums.
- **Roma groups:** check whether household surveys reach Roma settlements before drawing on them.
- **Donbas and Kryvyi Rih:** compare wage, company housing and garden-plot records with farming regions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century ethnography, dress and food; mixed for household money after 1991; thin for Karaim, Krymchaks and Azov Greeks.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian, plus Podillian and Boyko word lists where village terms are unclear; Russian for Soviet sources; Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish for court books; Romanian; Hungarian; Yiddish for dowry and trade records.
- **Lands:** which territory the nation will hold is an open question. This sign's sheets follow the sources' own Ukraine, take Crimea and the eastern oblasts as homelands of their own peoples, and draw no line.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grain seizures of 1932 to 1933, the 1944 deportation, Holocaust-era plunder and war damage all involve taken property; each is a contested account, and occupied areas' archives are out of reach.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the inventories, cookbooks and dress surveys weighed here raised no standing-rule question.
