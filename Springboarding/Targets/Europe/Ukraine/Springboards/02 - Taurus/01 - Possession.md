# Ukraine: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia villages emptied after 1986:** check what evacuees took and what returning elderly residents kept.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare family keepsakes carried in exile with objects recovered on return; check the community's museums.
- **Jewish communities:** check pre-war inventories against postwar records of seized goods.
- **Hutsuls, Boykos and Lemkos:** compare dowry chests, carved household goods and woven bags as each group's own objects; for Lemkos resettled in 1944 to 1946, check what was carried.
- **Russian-speaking city flats of Kharkiv, Dnipro and Odesa:** check Soviet sideboard displays and communal-apartment sharing in memoirs.
- **People displaced since 2014 and 2022:** compare what they packed in each wave, from humanitarian surveys.
- **Bulgarians of the Budjak and Romanians of Bukovina:** check how dowry goods were shown and counted at weddings.
- **Greek Catholic and Orthodox households:** check which blessed objects are kept at home, church by church.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk objects and museum holdings; mixed for everyday belongings since 1991; thin for Karaim and Krymchak household goods (try their community associations and synagogue or *kenesa* records).
- **Languages needed:** Russian and Ukrainian for the Soviet home; Yiddish for shtetl fiction and memoirs; Crimean Tatar for deportation testimony; Polish for estate inventories of the Right Bank.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** looting during the Holocaust and wartime theft are painful and contested; testimony collections may restrict access; what refugees left behind is a live grief.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; the archaeology, museums, Yiddish fiction and witness archives considered here sit outside every standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. In the Ukrainian dialect atlas, find the possessive-construction maps and note where *маю* and *у мене є* dominate.
2. Search the Yahad-In Unum online map for villages in Podillia and Volhynia, then read testimony that mentions belongings.
3. Query «тривожна валіза ДСНС»: save the official list and two years of press articles on what people packed.
4. Ask the Ivan Honchar Museum for its chest collection catalog, sorted by region.
5. Pull the durable-goods tables for the earliest and latest years and compare towns with villages.
