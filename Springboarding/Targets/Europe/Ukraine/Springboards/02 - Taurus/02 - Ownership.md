## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Bank against Right Bank:** check Cossack-era freeholds against former Polish latifundia in inventories and land-share records.
- **Galicia:** check how small peasant plots after the 1848 abolition were split among heirs, against the large estates.
- **Zaporizhzhia steppe and Kherson:** compare Sich common lands with the later grants to colonists and officers.
- **Mennonite and Black Sea German colonies (historical):** check the full-farm system and the landless question in colony records.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check land claims and self-allotted plots after the return, in Mejlis papers and press.
- **Romanians of Bukovina and the Budjak:** check smallholder (*răzeș*) traditions in their own scholarship.
- **Hungarians and Rusyns of Transcarpathia:** compare tenure under Hungarian estates, Czechoslovakia and the Soviet farms.
- **Donbas mining settlements:** check company and state housing against private houses with gardens.
- **Roma households in Transcarpathia:** check how many lack papers for their homes; sources are rights groups' reports, so start there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Hetmanate, serfdom and collectivization; mixed for the land shares of 1999 to 2000; thin for Karaim and Krymchak property history (try community archives).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian; Polish for estates; Old Ukrainian chancery language for the Metrica; German for colony files; Crimean Tatar for return-era claims.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine, deportation and property seized from Jewish neighbors are contested accounts; parish disputes are live; occupied areas' registries are out of reach. The rules of property law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here.
- **Held for review:** empty; the chancery books, estate histories and land registries examined raised nothing under the standing rules.
