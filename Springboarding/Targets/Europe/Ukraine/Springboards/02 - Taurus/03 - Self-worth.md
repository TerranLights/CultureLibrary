# Ukraine: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Donbas and Kryvbas workers:** check how worth tied to mining and steel was spoken of before and after closures and the war.
- **Village against city:** check the slur *селюк* and the counter-pride of village life in humor, film and surveys.
- **Women:** compare the hearth-keeper image with women soldiers' and volunteers' own accounts since 2014.
- **Labor migrants (*заробітчани*) and their families:** check how earning abroad is spoken of at home, in letters and interviews.
- **Russian speakers of Kharkiv, Odesa and Dnipro:** check how language choice and self-respect are discussed in their own press since 2014.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check the honor vocabulary in families and the national movement's language of collective dignity.
- **Rusyns and Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** compare recognition claims in each group's press.
- **Jewish communities:** compare learned standing and lineage with the market-town poor in Yiddish fiction and memoirs.
- **Roma groups:** check dignity campaigns by group; sources are scarce, so start with the organizations' reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for essays and literature on national self-worth; mixed for personal self-esteem data; thin for Krymchaks, Karaim and the Azov Greeks.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian, plus Russian for eastern and Soviet-era sources; Church Slavonic and Polish for Smotrytsky; Yiddish; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "inferiority" essays can slide into stereotypes of a people: read them as arguments, not descriptions; slurs against regions and peoples are data on labels only; veterans' and suicide data need care.
- **Held for review:** none; the psychology and mental-health literature was the one place a set-aside seemed possible, and the pointers chosen are Ukrainian journals and programs.
