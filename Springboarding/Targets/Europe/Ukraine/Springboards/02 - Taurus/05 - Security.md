## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The steppe frontier against the forest north:** compare raid-era defenses in Zaporizhzhia and Kherson lands with Polissian villages.
- **Families of famine survivors:** check whether stores kept and grain hidden appear in the second and third generations' habits.
- **Donbas and front-line towns since 2014:** check shelters, evacuation and staying put in local accounts.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare insecurity in exile, on return and after 2014, in the community's monitors' reports.
- **Jewish communities:** check pogrom self-defense and later emigration as two answers to danger.
- **Mennonite colonies (historical):** check the self-defense dispute in their own records and emigrant memoirs.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check how second citizenship figures in households' plans, from the minority's press.
- **Village pensioners:** compare garden plots and pensions as safety nets with town pensioners.
- **Roma groups:** check eviction and attack reports, mostly from rights groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and famine testimony and for banking data; mixed for household savings; thin for Gagauz, Rumeic and Urum villages (local history societies may help).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Soviet and banking sources; Latin for Michalon; German for the Huzulen study and Mennonite files; Yiddish for pogrom reports; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine, pogroms, deportation and the war are contested accounts and painful for families; Crimean monitors' sources may need protection; front-line data changes fast.
- **Held for review:** none; the raid-era tract and the pogrom archive were weighed for bias, not for the standing rules, and stay in.
