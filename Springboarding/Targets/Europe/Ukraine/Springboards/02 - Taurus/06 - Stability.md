# Ukraine: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Greek (Rumeic and Urum communities), Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Volhynia and Polissia against the industrial east:** compare village continuity across generations with families who moved to the mines and plants.
- **Bukovina:** check continuity under several rulers in its Ukrainian and Romanian villages.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check continuity of place broken in 1944, rebuilt after the return, and broken again after 2014.
- **Jewish communities:** compare Yizkor accounts of long-settled towns with emigrants' and evacuees' accounts.
- **Azov Greeks of the Mariupol area:** check the villages kept since the move from Crimea (1778 to 1780), and their loss since 2022.
- **Lipovans and Bulgarians of the Budjak:** compare how each kept its rite and village plan through changes of state.
- **The displaced of both waves (2014, 2022):** check how routines are rebuilt in host towns.
- **Pensioners who remember the Soviet years:** compare nostalgia for steady prices with younger people's views, by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography and folk-calendar studies; mixed for lived routine; thin for Lipovans, Rumeic villages and Karaim continuity (local museums are the route).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and, for the *Синопсис*, Church Slavonic; Russian; Yiddish and Hebrew for Yizkor books; Crimean Tatar for place-name lists; Greek for Azov sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stability is a loaded campaign word; Soviet nostalgia polls are politicized; Crimean and Azov sources since 2014 and 2022 are partly unreachable; the *Синопсис* line of continuity is itself a contested account.
- **Held for review:** no item. Continuity here rests on registers, memorial books and village novels, and none of those picked came near a set-aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Browse JewishGen's Yizkor list for Podillia and Volhynia towns and pick two with English translations.
2. Kylymnyk's volumes: borrow the first and list the feasts that mark the farm year.
3. «перейменування населених пунктів Криму 1944» for the renaming lists and community commentary.
4. Search the *Населення України* yearbook for internal migration by oblast and note the earliest year given.
5. Look for regional museums that hold *трудові династії* exhibits in Kryvyi Rih and Dnipro.
