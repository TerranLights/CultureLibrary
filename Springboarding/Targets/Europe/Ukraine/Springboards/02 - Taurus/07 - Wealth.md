## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Donbas and Dnipro industrial elites against Kyiv's:** check the origins of post-1991 fortunes by region in rich lists and studies.
- **Slobozhanshchyna:** check the sugar magnates' estates and gifts around Sumy and Kharkiv.
- **Odesa's merchant communities:** compare Greek, Jewish, Italian and Ukrainian fortunes in Herlihy and city records.
- **Galicia:** check Boryslav oil wealth against the poverty of the nearby villages in Franko and the press.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check the Khanate's notables against later Tatar merchant families of the 19th century.
- **Jewish communities:** compare the *gvir* of a shtetl with the magnate families of Kyiv.
- **Bulgarians of the Budjak:** check how village notables' wealth was spoken of in songs and memoirs.
- **Roma groups:** check each group's own organizations for how wealth and want are described, not outsiders' press.
- **Families marked as *куркулі*:** check the descendants' memory of lost farms in oral histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 19th-century commerce and for rich lists and declarations; mixed for informal wealth; thin for Karaim merchants and Krymchak trades (start with community histories).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian; Yiddish for Sholem Aleichem in the original; Polish for estate records; Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish for Khanate material; Bulgarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "oligarch" is a charged label; the *куркуль* label carried violence; sources on Jewish wealth can carry old prejudice and must be read critically; Crimea is unreachable in person.
- **Held for review:** none. Outside economists' studies of fortunes were weighed and left for later; what is kept here are lists, archives and fiction, with nothing to set aside.
