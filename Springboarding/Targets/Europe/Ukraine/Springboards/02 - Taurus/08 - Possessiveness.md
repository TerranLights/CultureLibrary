## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Poltava and the Left Bank:** check the Churai legend's local versions against later literary ones.
- **Galicia:** compare Franko's village drama with Galician court and press reports of feuds between neighbors.
- **Polissia:** check how amber-digging and mushroom grounds are guarded by locals against outsiders.
- **Romanians of Bukovina:** check *Miorița* variants and village studies on herds and envy.
- **Jewish communities:** compare Yiddish fiction on marriage and jealousy with memoirs from Berdychiv and Odesa.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the community's ethnographers describe courtship and the guarding of family honor; sources are scarce, so start with their cultural centers.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check whether Hungarian-language counseling and church programs speak of jealousy differently.
- **Women and men:** compare hotline data and survey items on control by the sex of the person reporting.
- **Generations:** check whether younger respondents still read jealousy as love, in youth surveys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk song, drama and fiction; mixed for control in couples (underreported); thin for Crimean Tatar, Karaim and Roma material on jealousy and turf.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Yefymenko and Hohol's originals; Yiddish; Romanian; Hungarian; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence data needs care with victims' privacy; reports on amber diggers touch on crime and corruption; the Churai attribution is disputed and must stay an account.
- **Held for review:** none. Jealousy research is a field with many outside names; this sheet uses only regional songs, fiction and services, so nothing was set aside.
