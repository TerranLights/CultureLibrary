## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia's returnees to the Chornobyl zone:** check how they talk of staying, against officials' accounts.
- **Galicia's Greek Catholics:** compare the underground church's memory of refusal with Orthodox parishes' memory of the same years.
- **Donbas miners:** check strike records by mine and town, and later accounts by the strikers.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check accounts of single pickets and refusals after 2014, starting with the community's rights monitors.
- **Mennonite and Doukhobor communities (historical):** compare the reasons each gave for leaving rather than yielding.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses and other small faiths:** check deportation and prison records.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check how the minority's press frames its stand over schooling in Hungarian; the language laws themselves go to Libra.
- **Front-line villages since 2022:** compare refusals to leave by age and by language of interview.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious and dissent history and for war reporting; mixed for everyday obstinacy; thin for Krymchaks and Gagauz (no clear route; ask community associations).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian, with Church Slavonic for Kopystensky; Russian and German for Doukhobor and Mennonite sources; Polish for the Volhynia statement; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outsiders' jokes about a "stubborn" people are labels to study, never descriptions; Volhynia is contested between Polish and Ukrainian accounts; Crimea and front-line material is partisan and unsafe to gather; Sentsov's case is political.
- **Held for review:** empty; refusal and dissent sources here come from churches, unions, novelists and community archives, and none needed holding back.
