## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chernihiv and the Left Bank against the Carpathians:** compare Dovzhenko's lowland memories with Lemko and Hutsul writing on the senses.
- **Polissia and Volhynia:** check Kupala customs by village, from field recordings.
- **Odesa:** compare Jewish, Greek and Ukrainian table customs in memoirs and fiction.
- **Hungarians and Rusyns of Transcarpathia:** check bathing and wine customs in each community.
- **Swiss, German and Bulgarian settlers of the Budjak:** compare wine-making and feasting in their village histories.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check coffee, food and hospitality customs as their cookbooks and cultural centers present them.
- **Hasidic communities (historical) and today's Uman pilgrims:** check music and the rebbe's table as sensory worship.
- **Orthodox and Greek Catholic households:** compare fasting rules and the feasts that end them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, song and craft; mixed for touch and bathing; thin for Karaim and Krymchak food and for Rumeic Greek feasts (community cookbooks are the route).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with Lemko and Hutsul usage; Russian for Babel; German for Hnatiuk and Vovk; Yiddish and Hebrew for Beregovsky's material; Hungarian; Crimean Tatar; French and German for Shabo's founders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine memory shapes talk of food and must not be read as taste; erotic folklore needs care in quoting; Crimean Tatar food heritage is tied to the deportation and to occupation since 2014.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; the food, music and spa pointers came from regional, minority and Jewish sources, and no candidate touched the standing rules.
