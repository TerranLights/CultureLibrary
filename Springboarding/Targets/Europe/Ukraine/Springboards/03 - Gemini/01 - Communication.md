# Ukraine: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, *surzhyk*, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish, Yiddish (historical), Romani, Ukrainian Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the local ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and the Kyiv-Dnipro center:** compare address forms (*пане/пані*, first name and patronymic) in etiquette studies and recorded talk.
- **Russian-speaking residents of Kharkiv, Dnipro and Odesa:** check switching between Russian and Ukrainian in public talk since 2014 and 2022, in surveys and recordings, without assuming one direction.
- **Hungarian- and Romanian-speaking families of the west:** check how children broker talk with officials, and which language is used at the counter.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how elders are addressed, and which of three languages is used with outsiders, at home and in exile.
- **Hutsuls and Boykos:** compare calling and signaling across valleys with lowland ways of carrying word.
- **Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** compare testimony and lay preaching with Orthodox and Greek Catholic sermons.
- **Deaf communities:** check regional variants of the sign language, and whether hearing children of Deaf parents interpret.
- **Roma groups (Servy, Kelderara, Transcarpathian Roma):** check which language is used with whom; sources are thin, so start with Roma organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for etiquette studies, sermons and parliamentary records; mixed for recorded everyday conversation; thin for Roma, Karaim and Krymchak talk.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Middle Ukrainian for the oratory, Latin or German for Lassota, Ukrainian and Russian for modern talk, Crimean Tatar for the *mane*, Yiddish for the letter manuals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** which language people speak is contested ground; present each account as an account, and send language law to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Of the outsider guides weighed for directness, the one kept is a practitioner's scale marked as a claim to test; none raised a standing-rule question.
