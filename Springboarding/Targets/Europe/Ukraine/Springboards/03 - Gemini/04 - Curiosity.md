## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kharkiv and Odesa:** compare the university and learned-society traditions of each city, and what each chose to study and collect.
- **Polissian villages:** check tales and sayings that forbid looking or asking (on the forest, the dead, healers), in local folklore archives.
- **Hasidic communities of Podillia and their critics (historical):** compare what Hasidic tales say about inquiry with the writing of the Jewish enlightenment (Haskalah) authors of Odesa.
- **Karaim and Krymchaks:** check each community's interest in its own history, and the outside collecting it drew; sources are few, so begin with Karaim community bodies and museum collections.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check what travelers asked them and what their own scholars chose to record, before and after 1944.
- **Galicia and Bukovina:** compare reading clubs and museum societies under Habsburg rule with the Soviet Znannia lectures in the east.
- **Children and teenagers in war-hit regions:** check how science clubs and online courses carried on, and what children ask about.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for travel writing, science history and tale indexes; mixed for everyday nosiness; thin for Karaim and Krymchak sources inside Ukraine.
- **Languages needed:** Old East Slavic for the pilgrim account, Ottoman Turkish for Evliya, Hebrew and Aramaic for Firkovich's colophons, Russian for the tale index and Znannia booklets, Ukrainian and Yiddish for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Firkovich forgery debate and rival claims about scientists' priority are contested; present each as an account. Some sacred sites and church collections restrict access.
- **Held for review:** none; foreign popular-science claims that Ukraine "invented" many things were weighed and dropped as unreliable, and no standing rule came into it.
