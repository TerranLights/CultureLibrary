# Ukraine: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Greek (Rumeic, Urum), German (colonies), Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and the Donbas:** compare how jobs and help were found through kin, church or workplace ties in migration studies and Soviet-era memoirs.
- **Russian-speaking and Ukrainian-speaking families split by the front line:** check how ties are kept across occupation and exile through displaced people's own accounts, not assumptions.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare ties kept during exile in Central Asia with those since the return and since 2014.
- **Azov Greeks of Mariupol:** check how village associations kept people linked before and after the 2022 destruction of the city.
- **Lemkos and Boykos resettled in the 1940s:** compare old-village ties among those moved to eastern and southern Ukraine.
- **Bulgarians and Gagauz of the Budjak:** check godparent ties and village feasts as connectors in local ethnographies.
- **Hasidic and other Jewish communities (historical):** compare court networks with the town *kahal* and later Soviet Jewish networks.
- **Roma groups:** check family-network ties across regions; data are scarce, so begin with Roma community organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for informal-network scholarship and migration; mixed for loneliness; thin for Krymchak, Karaim and Urum ties, best reached through community bodies.
- **Languages needed:** Greek for Constantine's text, German for the Odesa paper, Hebrew and Yiddish for Hasidic court sources, Ukrainian and Russian for *блат* studies, Hungarian and Romanian for cross-border ties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families split by occupation and the Lemko and Crimean Tatar removals are contested ground; present each account as an account. *Кумівство* as corruption links to Libra and Capricorn for law.
- **Held for review:** none. Informal-network studies of neighboring countries were weighed and kept only where they name Ukraine; no candidate fell under a standing rule.
