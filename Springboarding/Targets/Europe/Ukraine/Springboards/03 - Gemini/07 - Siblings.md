# Ukraine: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, German (colonies), Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Romani. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood here: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia and the Hutsul Carpathians:** compare kin terms for elder and younger siblings and cousins in dialect studies.
- **Left Bank Cossack families and Galician villages:** check how land and the house passed among brothers and sisters, and send the legal side to Libra.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check terms of address for elder siblings and their use for non-kin, and how families scattered in 1944 traced lost siblings.
- **Hungarian and Romanian families of the west:** compare age-marked sibling terms with Ukrainian ones in the same villages.
- **Jewish families (historical):** check memoirs from Podillia and Volhynia for siblings parted by emigration, war and the Holocaust.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** compare sibling sets and naming customs in colony genealogies.
- **Roma families:** check sibling terms and duties in Roma-led sources; outside sources are thin.
- **Families split by the war since 2014:** compare siblings on either side of the front line or abroad, through their own accounts; refugees abroad are comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epic, ballad and literary siblings; mixed for survey data on adult sibling ties; thin for Roma, Karaim and Krymchak families.
- **Languages needed:** Old East Slavic for the Boris and Gleb texts, Ukrainian and its dialects for kin terms, German for colony genealogies, Hebrew and Yiddish for Hasidic tales, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian and Romanian for terms of address.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "brother peoples" rhetoric and the war's separated and deported children are contested ground; present each account as an account. Children's records are protected; inheritance law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing. Birth-order psychology from abroad was weighed for this sheet and dropped for want of Ukrainian data, not under a standing rule.
