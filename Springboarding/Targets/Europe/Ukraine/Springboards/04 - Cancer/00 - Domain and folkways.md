# Ukraine: The domain, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects (Hutsul, Boyko, Lemko, Polissian, Podillian, Slobozhan), the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian and Gagauz, German (colony records), Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** what home is and who counts as family: household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, the home's walls and meals, with building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways as the sign's folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsul, Boyko and Lemko highlands against Polissia and the Left Bank:** compare enclosed homesteads, log houses and clay-plastered *хати*, and which room each kept for guests.
- **Donbas and Kryvbas mining towns:** check company barracks, *гуртожитки* and panel blocks as family homes across generations.
- **Crimean Tatars:** look at the houses self-built on returned land since 1989, as Crimean Tatar sources describe them.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Transcarpathian Hungarians, Bukovinian Romanians:** compare courtyard plans and household words with Ukrainian neighbors.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** compare village plans and house-barns with nearby Ukrainian farms.
- **Shtetl households (historical):** check memorial books for rooms, the shop in the house and who lived there.
- **Families displaced since 2014 and 2022:** compare home-making in temporary flats, through their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk architecture, wedding ethnography and censuses; mixed for household typology between the censuses; thin for Krymchak, Karaim and several Roma groups' homes (route: community bodies and specialist museums).
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Latin for the lustrations, Russian for the 1897 census, German for colony buildings, Czech for the 1930 count, Yiddish for memorial books, Crimean Tatar for return-era housing, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Lands:** the borders of the post-war nation are not yet settled. Every Cancer sheet is written for the lands the sources treat as Ukraine, with Crimea and the east as lands with their own peoples; no line is drawn here.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** destroyed and occupied housing, displacement, and land seized or returned in Crimea are contested; present each account as an account. Housing and family law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Developer brochures were weighed as possibly promotional and kept only through listings; nothing called for a standing-rule hold.
