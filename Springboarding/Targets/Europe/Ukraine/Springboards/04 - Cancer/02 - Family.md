## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician and Bukovinian villages against the Left Bank:** check undivided households and how sons left them, region by region.
- **Hetmanate Cossack lineages and Polish gentry families:** compare how each recorded descent and marriage.
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions), Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic and Protestant families:** compare each church's teaching on family and what members report.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how lineage records and family names were kept through deportation and return.
- **Jewish families (historical):** compare the in-law tie and family rank in Yiddish fiction with memoirs from Berdychiv and Odesa.
- **Mennonite and German colonists (historical):** check the kin chains behind their settlement.
- **Roma families of Transcarpathia and Odesa:** check kin terms and wider family ties through Roma-led sources; outside data is thin.
- **Labor-migrant and refugee families:** compare families split across borders since the 1990s; diaspora is comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gentry and Cossack genealogy, church registers and fiction; mixed for current survey data on kin ties; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Gagauz kin structures.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the commemoration books, Polish for Aftanazy, German for Unruh, Yiddish for Der Nister, Crimean Tatar for *şecere*, Russian for some Soviet-era statistics, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *Свати* dispute touches the war; church teaching on family differs between jurisdictions and is contested; family and inheritance law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld. DNA-ancestry company reports were weighed as a class and left out as marketing, not under any standing rule.
