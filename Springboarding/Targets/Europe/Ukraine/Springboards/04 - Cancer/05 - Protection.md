## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsul and Boyko highlands against Polissia:** compare protective customs against storm, fire and wolves.
- **Podillian and Galician pilgrims:** check which shrines are visited for protection and for whom.
- **Front-line oblasts (Kharkiv, Sumy, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson) and the west:** compare local defense, shelters and evacuation through residents' accounts.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check protection of family and community under occupation, as Crimean Tatar bodies describe it.
- **Jewish communities (historical and today):** compare pogrom-era self-defense with today's community security.
- **Poles and Ukrainians of Volhynia (historical):** compare village defense in each side's archives; contested ground.
- **Roma groups:** check what protection they asked for and received, through Roma-led organizations.
- **Women volunteers and veterans:** compare their own accounts with press framing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime defense, folk belief and church practice; mixed for domestic-violence response; thin for Gagauz, Karaim and Krymchak protective customs.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for the Volhynian bases, Yiddish and Russian for pogrom self-defense, Crimean Tatar for the battalion and community statements, Hutsul dialect for weather guardians, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Volhynia 1943, pogrom history, occupied Crimea and the war since 2014 and 2022 are contested; give each side's account as an account. Military details may be restricted. Gun and domestic-violence law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Neo-pagan sites selling "ancient" amulet meanings were weighed and left out as unreliable; the standing rules were not involved.
