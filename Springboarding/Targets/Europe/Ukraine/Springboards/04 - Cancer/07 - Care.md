# Ukraine: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, German (colonies), Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissian and Hutsul villages:** compare folk healers, rural health posts and who cares for the sick at home.
- **Kyiv, Kharkiv and Dnipro:** check private and state care homes and the families who choose them.
- **Greek Catholic and Orthodox congregations, and Protestant ones:** compare nursing orders and parish visiting.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check care for elders and for the dead, through Crimean Tatar sources and film.
- **Jewish communities (historical and today):** compare the health bodies of the early 20th century with community welfare now.
- **Mennonite colonies (historical):** compare colony institutions for the mentally ill and the old.
- **Families of labor migrants:** check who cares for parents left behind; migrants abroad are comparison only.
- **Roma groups:** check access to care through mediators; little is written from inside.
- **Blind, deaf and disabled people's own bodies:** compare what they ask for with what officials provide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church and Jewish welfare history and wartime volunteer care; mixed for elder-care statistics; thin for unpaid care measures and for Crimean Tatar, Gagauz and Roma care at home.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Middle Ukrainian for healing books, Yiddish and Russian for the Jewish health body, German for Bethania, Crimean Tatar for the film and community, Italian or Polish for care-work studies, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patients' and residents' records are protected; folk healing should not be presented as medical advice; care-home deaths need care for families. Health and guardianship law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Medical-tourism and clinic-ranking sites were weighed as a source type and excluded as advertising, under no standing rule.
