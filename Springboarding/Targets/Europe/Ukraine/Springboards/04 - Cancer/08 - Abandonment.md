# Ukraine: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish, German (colonies), Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia, Bukovina and Transcarpathia:** compare how families with a parent working abroad describe who left and who stayed.
- **Chernihiv, Sumy and Polissian villages:** check depopulation and elders left alone in demographic and photo work.
- **Left Bank and Right Bank villages (historical):** compare songs and customs about the *покритка*.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check accounts of houses and graves left in 1944 and found again after return.
- **Mennonite and German colonists (historical):** compare the 1943 departure with earlier emigrations.
- **Poles and Roman Catholics of Podillia and Volhynia:** check what was left after the border and population shifts of the 1940s.
- **Jewish families (historical):** compare wives left by emigrant husbands with families left in war; rabbinic and press sources.
- **Disabled children and their families:** check institutional care through parents' and survivors' own groups.
- **Roma families:** check claims about Roma children in care through Roma-led organizations; the data is thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migrant families, institutions and literature; mixed for elders left alone; thin for Crimean Tatar property records and Roma family data.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian for the duma, Yiddish for the *Forverts* column, German for the trek, Polish for Catholic church histories, Crimean Tatar for return-era memoirs, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's records are protected; desertion, deportation and the taking of children in the war are contested; present each account as an account. Adoption and child-protection law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Orphanage "voluntourism" sites were considered as a source class and left out as promotional; the standing rules did not decide it.
