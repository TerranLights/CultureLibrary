# Ukraine: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Yiddish (historical), Polish. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and Kyiv:** compare when and how Mother's Day and the Union of Ukrainian Women spread, and the role of the Greek Catholic and Orthodox churches.
- **Poltava and Kharkiv villages:** check mothers' memories of the famine in oral collections.
- **Bukovina Ukrainians and Romanians:** compare confinement and naming customs village by village.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check mothers' accounts of deportation in 1944 and of sons arrested since 2014.
- **Jewish mothers (historical):** compare Holocaust testimony from Podillia, Volhynia and Odesa.
- **Mothers of soldiers and prisoners:** compare families' groups across regions and their demands.
- **Surrogate mothers:** check their own accounts against clinics' and press framing.
- **Hungarian, Bulgarian and Gagauz mothers:** check family-size and birth data by district.
- **Roma mothers:** check birth and maternal-health data through Roma-led groups; little is published.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for icon, song, literature and war testimony; mixed for maternal-health surveys after 2007; thin for Gagauz, Karaim and Roma mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the mosaic's inscription, Romanian for Bukovina, Yiddish and Russian for testimony, Crimean Tatar for community media, Polish for interwar Galician sources, Ukrainian for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Holodomor, the Holocaust, the Mariupol strike and the prisoners of war are contested; present each account as an account. Surrogacy and family benefits law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. Global maternal-instinct literature was considered for this sheet and dropped for lack of Ukrainian data; no standing rule was involved.
