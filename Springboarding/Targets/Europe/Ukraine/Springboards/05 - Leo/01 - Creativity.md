# Ukraine: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Hutsul and Poltava craft vocabularies), the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Polish and German for Lviv's baroque. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Poltava and Kharkiv regions against the Hutsul Carpathians:** compare how potters, weavers and carvers learn and pass on designs, and who may change a family pattern.
- **Kyiv and Kharkiv against Lviv:** check avant-garde and modernist circles in each, and the different states they answered to.
- **Odesa:** look at the nonconformist and literary scenes in Russian and Ukrainian, and how makers there describe their own invention.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare craft lines before 1944, in exile and after return, in their own makers' accounts.
- **Jewish carvers, painters and writers:** check work in Yiddish, Hebrew, Russian and Ukrainian, and how it is credited.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians and Rusyns:** compare painters and craftsmen named in each language's art histories.
- **Women folk painters and embroiderers:** check whose names were recorded and whose work stayed anonymous.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk art, the avant-garde and the 1920s; mixed for everyday making do (mostly in memoirs and the press); thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Gagauz crafts and for Roma metalworkers (look to museum storerooms and community bodies).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian for art histories; Polish, German and Latin for Lviv's baroque; Hungarian for Uzhhorod painters; Crimean Tatar; Yiddish and Hebrew for tombstones.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** museums destroyed or looted since 2022 and artists who died in the war; quarrels over whether Malevych and other figures belong to Ukrainian or Russian art histories; present each account as an account. Copyright law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no entry. The likeliest doubtful class was art-auction price databases, doubtful for access and paywalls, so they are simply not listed.
