# Ukraine: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Polish, with Latin and Middle Ukrainian for baroque panegyrics. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia, the Dnipro center and the south and east:** compare what each region's monuments, place names and festivals boast about.
- **Donbas and Kryvbas mining towns:** check pride in heavy labor and its records, and how plant closures and war altered it, in miners' own accounts.
- **Odesa:** look at civic pride in humor, port and mixed origins, across Ukrainian, Russian, Jewish and Greek sources.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare heroic epic, return-era symbols and exile media as sources of collective pride.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians, Bukovinian Romanians and Budjak Bulgarians:** check which schools, poets and monuments each community marks.
- **Jewish communities:** compare pride in learning, in lineage and in self-defense as their own writers give them.
- **Poles of Lviv and Volhynia:** check commemorations and associations, and the debates they raise with Ukrainian neighbors.
- **Ukrainian Greek Catholics and Orthodox of both jurisdictions:** compare church jubilees and the pride each takes in its history.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, monuments and civic-identity surveys; mixed for pride in work and craft; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Gagauz sources of pride (route: community bodies and museums).
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian and Latin for panegyrics; Polish and Latin for guild records; Crimean Tatar for epic; Hungarian, Romanian and Bulgarian for community pride; Russian for Soviet labor campaigns; Yiddish and Russian for Jabotinsky.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** monuments and symbols of minorities, Soviet labor heroes, and Polish and Ukrainian memory in Lviv and Volhynia are contested; present each account as an account. Symbol and flag law goes to Libra.
