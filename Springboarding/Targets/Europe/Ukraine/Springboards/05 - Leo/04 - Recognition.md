## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Soviet-era recipients and post-1991 recipients:** compare the titles and boards each period used to credit workers and artists.
- **Veterans of 2014 on, Chornobyl liquidators and Afghan war veterans:** check how each group fought for its status and what it calls being forgotten.
- **Crimean Tatars, Karaim and Krymchaks:** compare each people's own reading of the Indigenous recognition, in their own bodies' statements.
- **Rusyns:** check what recognition they ask of the state and of the census, and how others answer.
- **Roma groups:** compare commemorations led by Roma organizations with state-led ones.
- **Greek Catholics and Orthodox of both jurisdictions:** compare how each church names its martyrs and saints.
- **Jewish communities:** check synagogue honors and community books for who was credited and who was left out.
- **Teachers and medical workers in villages and in cities:** compare who gets public thanks, using contest and award lists.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state awards and laureate lists; mixed for workplace praise; thin for recognition inside small communities (Karaim, Krymchaks, Gagauz; try community bodies).
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian for the hetman's charters; Russian for Soviet honors; Crimean Tatar and Romani for community statements; Yiddish and Hebrew for synagogue records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genocide recognition, the status of the Indigenous peoples, the UPA and other contested figures in awards, and combatant status are contested; present each account as an account. Award and status statutes go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** empty, by honest count. The award lists, charters and surveys picked here never brushed against a standing rule.
