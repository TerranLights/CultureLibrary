# Ukraine: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Lemko and Boyko speech, Romani, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western towns with a promenade tradition against eastern industrial towns:** check where people go to be seen and in what dress.
- **Older people in villages of Chernihiv, Sumy, Kherson and Donetsk regions:** compare who visits them and how they are counted, through aid and pension data.
- **Roma groups of Transcarpathia, Odesa and the center:** compare counts, documents and media images, in Roma-led sources.
- **Crimean Tatars, Karaim and Krymchaks:** check how each keeps its people visible since 2014, at home and abroad.
- **Lemko and Boyko descendants:** compare memorial books and festivals as ways to stay on the map.
- **LGBTQ soldiers and civilians:** compare coming out in the army with coming out at home, in their own testimony.
- **Jewish communities:** check memorials, names projects and the eye-related customs in Yiddish sources.
- **Veterans with visible wounds:** compare their accounts of public staring and of care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for memorials, media monitoring and surveillance files; mixed for daily gaze and greeting norms; thin for the unseen elderly and small peoples (look to NGOs and community bodies).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian for files and media; Crimean Tatar, Hungarian and Romanian for customs; Lemko and Boyko speech for memorial books; Yiddish for the eye customs; Romani for Roma-led sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance files name informers and victims; Babyn Yar memorial plans, the deportations of Lemkos and Boykos, LGBTQ visibility in war and occupied Crimea are contested or dangerous; present each account as an account and protect people still at risk. Privacy and data law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none listed. Of the classes in this sheet, only front-line videos raised a doubt, and that doubt is about soldiers' safety, met by citing channels and not posts.
