# Ukraine: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), German (forced-labor records), Church Slavonic for rite books. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former forced laborers, camp survivors and prisoners of war:** compare how each names degradation and what restored their standing.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how exile, return and life under occupation are told as matters of dignity, in their own memoirs.
- **Disabled people and older people in cities and villages:** compare access, queues and care through their own organizations' surveys.
- **Jewish communities:** compare burial and charity customs in pre-war towns and today's communities.
- **Greek Catholic, Orthodox (both jurisdictions), Roman Catholic and Protestant churches:** check each church's teaching on the person and its care for the dying.
- **Roma groups:** check accounts of treatment by police and services in Roma-led sources.
- **Kyiv and western cities against front-line towns:** compare how services and burial work under shelling, through municipal and NGO reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for monitoring reports, oral histories and 2013 to 2014 sources; mixed for dignity in care and old age; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Gagauz voices (route: community bodies).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian; Church Slavonic for the rite book; German for forced-labor records; Crimean Tatar for memoirs; Yiddish and Hebrew for burial-society books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** torture, deaths in custody and the identification of the dead are painful and sometimes classified; the naming of 2013 to 2014 is itself contested by some; present each account as an account. Detention and disability law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** set nothing aside. Religious charities' fundraising pages came closest to doubt, as appeals rather than sources, so the church entry here points to teaching documents instead.
