## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia, the center and the east:** compare who gives to parish, village and army, using foundation surveys by oblast.
- **Pre-1917 patrons of Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa:** check whether each city's gifts went to church, school, museum or hospital.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare endowment records with today's community giving in Crimea and on the mainland.
- **Jewish communities:** compare *tsedoke*, loan societies and burial gifts before 1941 with today's communal welfare.
- **Bukovinian Romanians and Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check funeral and vow feasts as forms of open giving.
- **Greek Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant congregations:** compare tithes, collections and church charity reports.
- **Displaced people and their hosts since 2022:** compare accounts of giving and receiving help, from both sides.
- **Caroling households in Hutsul, Polissian and Slobozhan villages:** check what door gifts each district records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime fundraising and the pre-1917 patrons; mixed for folk gift customs; thin for Karaim and Krymchak charity and for Roma giving customs (try community bodies and Roma-led groups).
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian and Polish for early deeds; Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish for endowments; Yiddish and Hebrew for loan societies; Romanian, Bulgarian and Gagauz for village feasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fundraising for the army, donors' privacy and charity fraud since 2022 are contested; present each account as an account. Charity and tax law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none. Charities' own ranking lists were the one doubtful class, kept off this sheet because they score themselves.
