## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and Bukovina against the Left Bank and Slobozhanshchyna:** compare the names and rules of the same children's games across dialect zones.
- **City courtyards against villages:** check who played where, unsupervised, and from what age, through memoirs.
- **Kyiv, Kharkiv and Lviv gaming and esports scenes:** compare players' ages, sexes and relocations since 2022.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare games kept in exile with those revived in Crimea and on the mainland.
- **Jewish communities:** check games in the *kheyder*, at feasts and in Soviet schools, in Yiddish and Russian memoirs.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians and Rusyns:** compare dance evenings and youth games across the language line.
- **Children in shelters and displaced families since 2022:** check how play continues, in teachers' and psychologists' own reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk games, animation and the game industry; mixed for adult hobbies and playground policy; thin for Karaim, Krymchak, Gagauz and Roma children's games (route: community bodies and regional museums).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with dialect skill for game names; Russian for Soviet memoirs, KVN and gaming forums; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's play in war and occupation and the Russian-language entertainment break after 2014 are contested or painful; present each account as an account and keep children unnamed. Gambling law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no candidate reached this line. Studies of "gaming addiction" written by clinic advertisers were the doubtful kind, left out for the advertising.
