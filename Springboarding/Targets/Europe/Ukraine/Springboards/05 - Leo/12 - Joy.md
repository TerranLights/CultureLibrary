# Ukraine: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Hutsul and Polissian festive usage), the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Yiddish (historical), with Middle Ukrainian for Skovoroda. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsul and Boyko highlands against Polissia and the steppe:** compare which feasts draw the largest gatherings and what music carries them.
- **Bukovinian Romanians, Transcarpathian Hungarians, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check village feast days and harvest dances, in each community's press.
- **Crimean Tatars:** compare seasonal festivals before 1944, in exile and since return, in their own sources.
- **Hasidic and other Jewish communities:** compare joy in prayer and feasts with Soviet-era and present practice.
- **Pentecostal and Baptist congregations against Orthodox and Greek Catholic parishes:** check how each worships with song and gladness.
- **Front-line cities and western cities since 2022:** compare cultural events and festival calendars kept or cancelled.
- **Roma musicians:** check their place at others' weddings against Roma-led accounts of their own feasts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festive ethnography, music and the happiness surveys; mixed for everyday joy; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Azov Greek festivities (try community bodies and regional museums).
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian for Skovoroda; Ukrainian dialects for festive songs; Russian for Soviet-era sources; Crimean Tatar; Hungarian and Romanian; Yiddish for memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** celebrating in wartime, the 2023 calendar change and Russian-language entertainers are contested; present each account as an account. Public-holiday rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** empty for joy. Clinical depression statistics were thought of as a mirror measure, then left to other signs as outside this concept.
