# Ukraine: The domain, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian and Gagauz, Polish, German (colony records), Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** what good work and a well-kept life are taken to be: work, service, the daily round and health, with work ways as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsul and Boyko highlands against the Left Bank and the steppe:** check how herding, timber work and grain farming set the seasonal calendar.
- **Donbas and Kryvbas against Kyiv and Lviv:** check shift work and the trades local papers praise against service and tech jobs.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check orchard and craft work before 1944, in the special settlements and after the return, in their own press.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check artisan work in Podillia and Volhynia against the southern farm colonies.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical), Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check colony work rules against village practice.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians and Rusyns, Bukovinian Romanians:** check how seasonal work abroad fits the home calendar.
- **Roma groups:** check smithing, music and scrap trades in Roma-led sources, not outsiders' labels.
- **Orthodox of both jurisdictions, Greek Catholics, Baptists and Pentecostals:** check which feasts each community keeps free of work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor statistics, Soviet labor propaganda and folk songs; mixed for the daily round; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Azov Greek work ways (start with community bodies and local museums).
- **Lands:** for work, service and health, as elsewhere in the survey, no frontier is fixed yet; the pointers cover what the sources call Ukraine, and Crimea and the east appear through their own peoples' records.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with dialect skill for harvest songs; Russian for imperial and Soviet records; German for the colonies; Yiddish and Hebrew for the Jewish colonies; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced labor, the Holodomor and the special-settlement regime are contested ground; present each account as an account. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** across this domain sheet, the only pointers weighed for setting aside were business-culture guides that sort whole nations into types; dropping them took no standing rule.
