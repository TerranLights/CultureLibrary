# Ukraine: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Rusyn, Yiddish (historical). **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Donbas and Kryvbas against Kharkiv and Dnipro:** check how mining and machine-building towns tell a working life in museums and local papers, and what changed after 2014.
- **Polissia and the Hutsul Carpathians:** check forest, peat and herding work against the steppe's grain fields in the seasonal round.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check craft and orchard work before 1944 and work rebuilt after the return, in their own press.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check the artisan's standing against the scholar's and the merchant's in memoirs and community books.
- **Bukovinian Romanians, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check vineyard and communal work parties against Ukrainian neighbors' parties.
- **Roma groups:** check which trades each group records for itself (smiths, musicians, traders) and where sources are lacking.
- **Women of town and village:** check what the women's press and time data count as their work.
- **People displaced since 2014 and 2022:** check how lost and new work are told in surveys and oral histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet labor propaganda, industrial history and labor statistics; mixed for unpaid work; thin for Crimean Tatar guild history and Karaim trades (try the Gasprinski library in Simferopol, under occupation, and diaspora scholars in Turkey).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian, Russian for Soviet and Donbas sources, Romanian for *clacă*, Yiddish and Russian for ORT records, Crimean Tatar and Turkish for guild studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "parasite" campaigns and forced labor are told differently by former officials and their targets; keep each telling as its own account. Labor law and the Soviet decree go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the doubtful class weighed here was outsourcing-firm marketing that sells "Ukrainian diligence", left out because it is advertising, not evidence.

## 5. First queries
1. Read «Бджола і Шершень» with its moral, then list where Skovoroda's notion of work fitted to nature recurs in school readers after 1991.
2. Pull the State Employment Service's latest vacancies and registered jobless by oblast, and rank the occupations with the most unfilled posts.
3. Search a folktale archive for «Дідова дочка і бабина дочка» and map the villages of each recorded variant.
4. Locate Friedgut's two Iuzovka volumes and note the chapters on hours, pay and the miners' daily round.
