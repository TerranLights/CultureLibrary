## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissian and Chernihiv villages against Kharkiv and Dnipro suburbs:** check how aging villages and city pensioners describe still being needed.
- **Hutsul and Boyko families:** check which tasks pass to the old (herding, carving, minding children) in highland ethnographies.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check elders' place as keepers of language and memory since the return, in community media.
- **Jewish communities:** check how memoirs judged the trade-less man and the scholar supported by others.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Bukovinian Romanians:** check elders' roles against migration that empties villages.
- **Disabled veterans since 2014:** check how they and their families speak of usefulness after injury, apart from official programs.
- **Roma groups:** check how scrap, recycling and trading work is described by Roma speakers rather than by neighbors.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** check colony provisions for widows and the infirm in colony records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet campaigns and wartime volunteering; mixed for elders' and disabled people's own words; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Roma sources on this (start with community bodies and NGOs).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Soviet and imperial material; Yiddish; Bulgarian and Romanian for the southwest; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the steppe's "empty land" label touches Crimean Tatar and Nogai dispossession, a contested history; give each telling as an account. Pension and disability law go to Libra and Capricorn.
