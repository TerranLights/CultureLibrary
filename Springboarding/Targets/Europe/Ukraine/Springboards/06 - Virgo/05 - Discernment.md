*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orthodox (both jurisdictions) and Greek Catholics:** check how each church's monastic and parish writing teaches the testing of thoughts, and where they diverge.
- **Roman Catholic Poles and the Jesuit school heritage of Galicia and Podillia:** check what Ignatian discernment meant in Polish-language schooling there.
- **Hasidic and non-Hasidic Jewish communities:** check each side's test of a true leader in their own polemics.
- **Odesa and the Budjak:** check market and trade savvy in city lore against Bulgarian and Gagauz village market customs.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians and Rusyns:** check wine judging and its terms in each language.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check whose counsel is sought before marriage or a move, in community writing.
- **Elders in villages and towns:** check who is targeted by phone fraud and how families talk about it.
- **People living under occupation since 2014 and 2022:** check how they sort news sources, in surveys and testimony, presented as accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for media literacy and fact-checking; mixed for folk judgment; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Roma sources (look to community bodies and Roma-led NGOs).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian; Church Slavonic; Hebrew and Yiddish; Polish and Latin for the Jesuits; Hungarian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "disinformation" labels are contested across the front line and between churches; record who applies each label. Fraud and media law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** tabloid romance-scam "exposés" that profile the women of one city are held under the no-stereotype rule; read them only as evidence of the stereotype itself.
