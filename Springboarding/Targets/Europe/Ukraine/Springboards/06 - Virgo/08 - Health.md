## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia and the Hutsul Carpathians against Kharkiv and Dnipro:** check how far village healers and herbal lore still sit beside clinics.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check folk healing and the health of the returnees in their own community sources and diaspora scholarship.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check amulet healers against Jewish doctors trained in Vienna and Odesa, in memoirs.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians, Rusyns and Roma:** check vaccination access and clinic distance in reports on the measles years, as questions to test.
- **Bukovinian Romanians:** check charms (*descântece*) against Ukrainian charms of the same villages.
- **Greek Catholics, Orthodox of both jurisdictions and Pentecostals:** check prayers and rites for the sick, and views of doctors, in church sources.
- **Donbas and Kryvbas:** check miners' and steelworkers' health in union and clinic records.
- **People displaced since 2014 and 2022:** check access to care and medicines in survey reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public-health statistics and the health reform; mixed for folk healing; thin for Crimean Tatar, Karaim and Krymchak healing (seek community bodies and Turkish-language scholarship).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with Hutsul and Polissian dialect skill; Russian for Soviet medicine; Hebrew and Yiddish; Romanian; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *мольфари* and other healers may hold knowledge not for outsiders; record only what they share. Patients with HIV or mental illness need privacy. Health law and the reform's legal side go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no pointer was put aside; the near miss was a group of popular "traditional cure" books of uncertain authorship, which fall short on reliability, not on any rule.
