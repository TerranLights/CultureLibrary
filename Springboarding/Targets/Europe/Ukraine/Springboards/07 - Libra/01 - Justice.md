## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and Bukovina against the Dnipro lands:** check how Habsburg codes and Russian imperial law left different court records and legal vocabularies.
- **Transcarpathia's Hungarians and Rusyns:** check what the Hungarian-era courts recorded and which language the parties used.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check *kadı* courts under the Khanate, imperial courts after 1783, and today's cases from occupied Crimea in human-rights files.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check rabbinical courts against state courts, and the trials that drew the empire's attention.
- **Hutsul and Podillian villages:** check which bandit-avenger each region sings of, and how the songs judge him.
- **Liberated and frontline towns (Bucha, Izium, Kherson):** check how local people appear in the war-crimes records, as witnesses and as parties.
- **Roma groups:** check court and police records for how often Roma appear as parties and how their own mediators are described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for old codes, modern statutes and court decisions; mixed for folk justice; thin for Karaim and Krymchak legal practice (start with community histories and Crimean archives).
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Middle Ukrainian for the Statute; Latin and Hungarian for the *Tripartitum*; German for the ABGB; Russian for imperial and Soviet trials; Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish for *kadı* records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the genocide resolution, the war-crimes cases and lustration are contested; give the text and each side's reading, and protect victims' names. What justice is for goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** foreign true-crime books on Soviet serial killers came up as a media angle and were left out as sensational rather than about justice; this was a fit call, not a rule-based hold.
