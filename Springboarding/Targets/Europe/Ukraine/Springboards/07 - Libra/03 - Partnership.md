# Ukraine: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Slobozhan and steppe speech), the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Romanian, Hungarian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), German (colony records). **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Slobozhanshchyna against Galicia:** check matchmaking scenes in each region's literature and the age at which couples registered marriage.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the two families' alliance at a marriage is described in exile and after the return.
- **Bukovinian and Budjak Romanians, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check what wedding sponsors promise the couple and how long the tie is said to last.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check betrothal terms in contracts against the matches in Yiddish fiction.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** check mutual-aid rules beside the rules of nearby state villages.
- **Steppe and Left Bank grain villages against Polissian forest villages:** check whether *супряга* and other work partnerships appear in each, and on what terms.
- **Same-sex couples and their organizations (for example KyivPride):** check how they describe partnership while the law has no category for them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for weddings, the 1654 debate and family statistics; mixed for business partnerships; thin for Azov Greek, Karaim and Roma partnership customs (start with community bodies and regional museums).
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Middle Ukrainian; Russian and Polish for the 1654 historiographies; Yiddish and Hebrew for *tnoim*; Romanian; German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1654 union and the partnership bill are contested ground; give each reading as an account. Wartime separation stories involve living people: use published accounts only.
- **Held for review:** international marriage-agency material marketing Ukrainian women to foreign men was weighed and dropped, since it shows the agencies' framing rather than partnership as couples here describe it; no standing rule was involved.
