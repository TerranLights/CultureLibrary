*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia, the former Hetmanate lands and the Crimean Khanate's heirs:** check how each historiography tells the same treaties (Hadiach, Pereiaslav, Brest-Litovsk) and whom it credits.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians:** check how community bodies spoke during the 2017 dispute, apart from Budapest's and Kyiv's statements.
- **Budjak Romanians, Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check how local press covered the Black Sea case and border talks.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the Mejlis described its dealings with Kyiv, Ankara and international bodies.
- **Volhynian Ukrainians and Poles of the region:** check local commemorations against the national statements.
- **Russian-speaking Odesa and Kharkiv against Lviv:** check how talk-show audiences and local press judged negotiation proposals in 2014 to 2022.
- **Village hosts in Polissia and the Hutsul highlands:** check how hospitality rules differ in ethnographic records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties and state diplomacy; mixed for everyday tact; thin for Karaim and Krymchak relations with authorities in the modern period (start with community histories).
- **Languages needed:** Old East Slavic and Greek for the early treaties; Middle Ukrainian, Polish and Latin for Khmelnytsky's letters; German for 1918; Hungarian; Romanian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2022 talks, Volhynia and the education-law dispute are contested; present each government's and community's account as its own. Treaty law as text belongs here; what treaties are for belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** translated Western negotiation manuals: considered for everyday tact, left out because they teach an imported method; no standing rule was involved.
