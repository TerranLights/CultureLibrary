# Ukraine: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Polish (interwar Galicia), Church Slavonic (early lives), English (diaspora oral-history volumes). **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Frontline oblasts against the far west:** check concession polling by region and how local media frame "any price".
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how open protest and staying silent were weighed in 1944 to 1989 and after 2014, in their own press and rights reports.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check which survivors and families spoke of Babyn Yar before 1991, and where.
- **Galician Greek Catholics and Orthodox:** check clergy accounts of 1946 against the state's record of the same council.
- **Seventh-day Adventists, Jehovah's Witnesses, Baptists and other objectors:** check court files on refusal to serve, case by case.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical), Tavria's Molokans and Doukhobors:** check how nonresistance was kept or broken under pressure.
- **Families in Odesa, Dnipro and Lviv:** check how domestic-violence reporting moved after the 2017 law, using police and NGO figures by oblast.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime negotiation debates, famine testimony and family-violence law; mixed for everyday conflict avoidance; thin for Karaim and Krymchak experiences of accommodation (start with community histories).
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic; Ukrainian and Russian for polling and court files; Polish for 1935; Crimean Tatar; English for the commission's volumes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** concessions, pacifism in wartime, Babyn Yar, the Holodomor and the 1946 council are contested and live; present every position as its holders state it and take no side. Court files name objectors; cite cases, not people. Silence about abuse touches survivors.
- **Held for review:** did anything fall under a standing rule? No. The one exclusion, translated self-help on "people-pleasing", was a matter of fit, since it carries imported advice rather than this nation's own sources.
