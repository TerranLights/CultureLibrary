# Ukraine: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hebrew and Yiddish (historical), German (colony records), Church Slavonic. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lviv and Donetsk, Kharkiv and Ternopil:** check how each city's residents describe the other in surveys before and after 2014.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the "traitor people" charge was answered in their own movement's writings from 1956 to 1989.
- **Jewish communities (historical):** check Hebrew and Yiddish chronicles against Cossack and Polish accounts of the same events.
- **Mennonite and German colonies (historical):** check how deportation files and memoirs each describe the colonists' loyalty.
- **Roma groups (Servy, Transcarpathian Roma and others):** check reports of blame after local thefts or fires, in their organizations' and police records.
- **Russian-speaking Ukrainians of the east and south:** check how they report being labeled from both sides, in oral histories.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check how they appear in national press during the 2017 to 2023 disputes, and how their own press answered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for propaganda, deportation records and wartime media; mixed for everyday blame in families; thin for Krymchak and Karaim experience of being blamed (start with community histories and Holocaust archives).
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic; Hebrew and Yiddish for Hanover; Russian for Soviet and wartime media; German for the 1941 colonists; Crimean Tatar.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the deportation, pogroms, OUN and UPA, memory laws, wartime enemy terms and Roma attacks are all contested; present each account as an account, record slurs as usage only, and never let a label stand as a description of a people. Legal remedies for hate speech belong in the letter here; what they are for belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** Soviet ethnographies that cast village healers as "survivals of superstition" were weighed and kept out of the evil-eye row as framing, to be read only with the regional studies; this was a choice of fit, not a hold under any standing rule.
