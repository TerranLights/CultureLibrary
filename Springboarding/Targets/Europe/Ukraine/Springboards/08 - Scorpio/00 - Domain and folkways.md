# Ukraine: The domain, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Romanian, Hungarian, Bulgarian, Gagauz, Yiddish and Hebrew, Polish. **Concept in one line:** how death, power and what is hidden are faced: death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance and secrets, with death ways and sex ways as the folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissia and Volhynia against the Kherson steppe:** check whether the ancestors' days carry the same dates in old and newer settled lands.
- **Hutsuls, Boykos and Lemkos:** check each group's own collections for wake games and farewell rites.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check burial and memorial readings before 1944, in exile and after the return, through community authors.
- **Jewish communities, Karaim and Krymchaks:** check cemetery inventories, and who tends the graves now.
- **Bukovinian Romanians and Transcarpathian Hungarians:** check the wake in mixed villages against neighbors' records.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Azov Greeks:** check local museums for anything printed on funerals.
- **Parishes of the two Orthodox jurisdictions, Greek and Roman Catholic parishes, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** check each one's burial service and days of memory.
- **Kharkiv and Odesa against Lviv:** check cremation shares and city funeral services.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for funeral ethnography and the war dead; mixed for inheritance and household money; thin for sex ways before the 1990s outside folklore, and for Krymchak and Urum funerals.
- **Lands:** where Ukraine ends after the war is still the author's open question; until it is decided, read each pointer as covering the country its sources describe, with Crimea and the Donbas reached as homes of their own peoples and no line implied.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian, Church Slavonic for monastic texts, Russian for Bulashev and eastern records, Romanian for Marian, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew for grave inscriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine graves, wartime exhumations (Polish and Ukrainian accounts of Volhynia differ), the Lavra's custody and graves in occupied areas; give each as its community's account. Power ways go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** for this overview the pointers weighed and dropped were death-studies titles about other countries; none touched a standing rule, so nothing is held.
