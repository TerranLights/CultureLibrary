# Ukraine: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew, Romani, Romanian, Hungarian, Polish. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are researched here, funeral practice under the domain.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central villages of Poltava and Kyiv regions against Galicia and Volhynia:** check whose family memory holds the famine dead, since the western lands lay outside the Soviet border in 1932 to 1933.
- **Families on both sides of the front in the Donbas and Crimea since 2014:** check how deaths are recorded by Ukrainian bodies, by the occupation's bodies and by rights monitors, keeping each record separate.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check grief formulas and laments in Crimean Tatar against those in Ukrainian and Russian.
- **Jewish communities of Kharkiv, Odesa and the Podillian towns:** check who says *kadish* for the murdered and where memorial days are kept now.
- **Roma of Transcarpathia and of the south:** check community-authored sources on the dead and on mourning; outsiders' accounts dominate, so mark which is which.
- **Poles of Lviv and Zhytomyr:** check All Souls customs beside Ukrainian neighbors'.
- **Orthodox of both jurisdictions, Greek Catholics, Baptists and Pentecostals:** check each church's teaching on praying for the dead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mass death (famine, Holocaust, war) and mortality data; mixed for private grief before 2014; thin for Roma and Karaim views of death in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian with Old East Slavic for the chronicle; Russian for eastern-city sources; Crimean Tatar for laments; Yiddish and Hebrew for memorial texts; Polish for the Lviv cemetery dispute.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** famine counts, the Holocaust in Ukraine and the war dead are disputed or raw; give each count as its source's. Suicide data need care with names. Inheritance after death goes to the domain sheet and law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** a Belarusian oral history of Chornobyl came to mind for dying after the disaster; Belarus is comparison only, so it was dropped, and no rule-based hold applies.
