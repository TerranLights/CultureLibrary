# Ukraine: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Yiddish and Hebrew, Romanian, Hungarian, German, Crimean Tatar. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Baptist, Pentecostal and charismatic congregations against Orthodox and Greek Catholic parishes:** check how each describes a turning point, and whether a dated conversion is asked of new members.
- **Greek Catholics of Galicia:** check the memoirs of the underground years for the language of return and renewal.
- **Jewish communities of Dnipro, Kyiv and Odesa:** check who is called *bal-tshuve* and how families with no religious past describe joining.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the return from exile and the revival of language and faith are told by community authors, before and after 2014.
- **Descendants of the German and Mennonite colonies:** check whether reopened churches in Odesa and the south draw descendants, newcomers or both.
- **Bukovinian Romanians and Transcarpathian Hungarians:** check what *pocăit* and *megtérés* signal in mixed villages where neighbors belong to other churches.
- **Veterans and their families, from 2014 on:** check where veterans' own words about "coming back" differ from charities' and the state's.
- **Residents of rebuilt towns in Kyiv, Chernihiv and Kharkiv regions:** check who returned, who did not, and their stated reasons.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church revival and reconstruction plans; mixed for recovery from addiction; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Rusyn revival in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian; Church Slavonic for the 18th-century drama; Yiddish and Hebrew; German for colony church histories; Romanian and Hungarian for the western churches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rival accounts of the 1946 and 1989 church changes, scrutiny of particular churches, and coerced rehab all need each side's account. Convicts' service is a legal matter for Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** survey items on reincarnation came up for the returning-dead facet; they say little about Ukraine's own forms, so they were left out, and no rule set anything aside.
