## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Displaced people from the east and the families who host them in the west:** check how each side describes the first months of living together.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check whom families relied on in exile and on return, and how trust in the Mejlis and in state bodies is measured in community surveys.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians and Rusyns:** check minority-run surveys against national ones for the same years.
- **Jewish communities, historical and present:** check credit and mutual-aid records against the community's own memoirs.
- **Roma of Transcarpathia and the south:** check whether any survey of trust samples Roma directly; mostly outsiders' data.
- **Russian-speaking cities against Ukrainian-speaking villages:** check trust in neighbors and in institutions in the same survey waves.
- **Greek Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant congregations:** check trust in clergy as each congregation's members rate it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey trust and wartime institutions; mixed for everyday credit and deals; thin for Karaim, Krymchak and Gagauz communities, where community leaders are the likely starting point.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian and Russian; English and Polish for Rosman's sources; Hungarian for the Berehove surveys; Yiddish for cooperative records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survey data from the occupied areas cannot be gathered freely; trust in police and courts is law's spirit and goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** two of the best-known cross-country trust experiments were weighed, but their country lists could not be confirmed to include Ukraine, so they were left out; nothing was set aside under a standing rule.
