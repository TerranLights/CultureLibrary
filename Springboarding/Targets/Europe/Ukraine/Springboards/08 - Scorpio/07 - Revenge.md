## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Poltava and Chernihiv villages against the Hutsul and Boyko highlands:** check how arson, feuds and payback appear in court records and songs of each.
- **Jewish communities and Ukrainian émigré circles:** check how each told the 1926 killing and the 1927 verdict, each in its own press.
- **Descendants of the Mennonite colonies and of Huliaipole's villages:** check how 1919 is remembered by each, with no side's account taken as the whole.
- **Poles and Ukrainians of Volhynia and Chełm land:** check how reprisal and counter-reprisal are told in each community's memoirs, keeping the accounts separate.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check whether community authors speak of the 1944 deportation in terms of justice, remembrance or retribution.
- **Russian-speaking and Ukrainian-speaking respondents in the east and south:** check how each answers post-war punishment questions.
- **Orthodox, Greek Catholic and Protestant congregations:** check what each teaches about forgiving an enemy in wartime.
- **Roma of the south and Transcarpathia:** check community-authored sources on settling quarrels; likely thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political and wartime reprisal; mixed for village payback; thin for revenge in Roma, Gagauz and Azov Greek communities in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian; Russian for Hohol's original; French and Yiddish for the trial; German for Mennonite memoirs; Polish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1919 pogroms, Petliura's record, the Volhynia reprisals, the Makhno movement and wartime retribution are contested; give each community's account and accuse no group. State punishment and the spirit of law go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** an OUN-era political killing of the 1930s came up for political revenge; it was left for a later stage because one case without its context would mislead, not because of any rule.
