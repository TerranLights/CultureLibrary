*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The two Orthodox jurisdictions and the Greek Catholic church:** check each body's parish map, calendar and language of worship.
- **Galicia and Volhynia against Slobozhanshchyna and the Donbas:** check affiliation shares in each survey round, before and after 2014 and 2022.
- **Crimean Tatars, Karaim and Krymchaks of Crimea:** check each community's own religious institutions and texts.
- **Jewish communities of Podillia, Volhynia and Odesa:** check Hasidic, enlightened and secular strands separately in each city.
- **Bukovinian Romanians, Lipovan Old Believers and Transcarpathian Hungarians:** check which parishes worship in which language and under which church center.
- **Rusyns, Hutsuls, Boykos and Lemkos:** check mountain parishes and folk religious specialists in each group's own collections.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Azov Greeks of Mariupol:** check the founding churches each settlement built and the clergy it brought.
- **Baptist, Pentecostal and Adventist congregations; Roma congregations:** check who records evangelical growth, and where.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church history and registers; mixed for unbelief and lived belief before 1991; thin for Krymchak, Urum and Roma religious life.
- **Lands:** nobody has yet fixed the map of the post-war nation. Until the author does, this sign's pointers reach whatever country each source itself describes, Crimea and the east enter through the faiths and records of the peoples who live there, and no frontier is drawn.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for Daniel's account, Russian for the atheist press and Bila Krynytsia, Hungarian for the Reformed yearbooks, Crimean Tatar for the Muftiate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the church jurisdictions, the 1946 liquidation of the Greek Catholic church and the Lavra's custody are contested; give each as its church's account, and name the reporting side for wartime damage to shrines.
- **Held for review:** comparative philosophy-of-religion handbooks were weighed for this overview and left out as off-target; nothing here touched a standing rule.
