# Ukraine: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, Russian (for imperial, Soviet and anarchist sources), Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Hungarian, Rusyn, Polish, Romanian. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a political ideal; autonomy and independence as personal traits stay in Aries.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician villages freed in 1848 against Left Bank villages freed in 1861:** check how each region remembers release from labor dues, and in what monuments and songs.
- **The Zaporizhzhia steppe and Huliaipole:** check how the Makhnovist past is told locally against official and church tellings.
- **Hutsuls of Yasinia and Rusyns of Khust:** check memories of 1919 and of Carpatho-Ukraine (1939) side by side.
- **Crimean Tatar returnees and their congress:** check freedom as return to the homeland, in congress documents and family accounts.
- **Hungarian towns of the Berehove district:** check their 1848 memory and its fit with Ukrainian commemorations.
- **Jewish communities of Odesa:** check emancipation and self-emancipation debates in Yiddish, Hebrew and Russian press.
- **Men of draft age and their families in each region:** check how the exit ban is argued, by whom and in which media.
- **Parishes that were underground before 1989:** check what religious freedom meant to members who remember worship in secret.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political thought and dissidents; mixed for village memory of 1848 and 1861; thin for how Roma, Azov Greek or Gagauz communities speak of freedom, so start with their own associations rather than outside histories.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Arshinov and Lukianenko's camp years, Hungarian for the March 15 press, Crimean Tatar for the 1991 declaration, Yiddish and Hebrew for the Odesa debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Makhno, the Sich Riflemen and the exit ban each draw opposed accounts; give each with its teller. Wartime claims about freedom in occupied areas need the reporting side named.
- **Held for review:** empty by choice: the only candidates cut were general works of liberty theory, which say nothing of Ukraine and which any researcher will meet anyway.
