# Ukraine: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Romanian, Hungarian, Greek (Rumeic and Urum), Hebrew and Karaim, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience) and how disputes over facts are settled.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Viewers of Russian-language channels in the Donbas before 2014 against Lviv's readers of Ukrainian press:** check which sources each called trustworthy in surveys of the time.
- **Karaim writers and community bodies:** check how their own histories answer the forgery dispute.
- **Romanian villages of the Hlyboka and Storozhynets districts:** check how school textbooks on each side of the border treat Fântâna Albă.
- **Azov Greeks of Mariupol and its villages:** check when and how families recovered the 1937 record.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians reading Budapest media:** check trust in outlets from each capital.
- **Congregations of the Kyiv-centered and Moscow-linked Orthodox churches:** check which church media each follows for news.
- **Scientists and teachers in Kharkiv and Odesa:** check institutional memory of the 1930s purges.
- **Crimean Tatar families of the deportation generation:** check how deportation-era truths were kept in families when not in print.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for censorship, historiography and wartime disinformation; mixed for everyday rumor and trust; thin for Karaim and Krymchak voices beyond the forgery dispute.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Kuznetsov, Lysenko and the Kharkiv files; Romanian for Fântâna Albă; Hebrew and Russian for Harkavy and Firkovich; Greek and Russian for the Azov lists.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Babyn Yar, the purges, Fântâna Albă and wartime claims are disputed by states and communities; give each account with its source. The Center for Countering Disinformation is a party to the war's information struggle, and should be read as such.
- **Held for review:** for this concept a cross-border fact-checking network was considered and then dropped because it names no Ukraine-specific work; none of the pointers here fell under a standing rule.
