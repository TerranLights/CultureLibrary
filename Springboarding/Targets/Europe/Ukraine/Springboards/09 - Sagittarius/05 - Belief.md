# Ukraine: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, Church Slavonic, Russian, Polish and German (for the Austrian records), Hungarian, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and the warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholic, Orthodox and Roman Catholic villages of Galicia in 1910:** check affiliation by district against what parish records say people did.
- **Rusyn parishes of the Mukachevo eparchy against Galician Greek Catholics:** check how each tells the story of its union and of 1949.
- **Evangelical villages of the Kherson steppe:** check Stundist family histories against police files.
- **Hungarian Reformed and Hungarian Catholic villages of the Berehove area:** check catechism use and confirmation customs side by side.
- **Crimean Tatar households in Crimea and on the mainland:** check which *ilmihal* editions circulate and who teaches them.
- **Soviet-schooled grandparents against post-1991 schoolchildren:** check which words each generation uses for its own outlook in interviews.
- **Villages of the Polissian marshes and the Hutsul uplands:** check folk-belief collections from each for the same spirits under different names.
- **Jewish communities of Odesa and Chernivtsi:** check rabbinic, Hasidic and secular strands in community publications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for creeds, polemics and folk-belief texts; mixed for lived belief under Soviet rule; thin for Krymchak and Gagauz belief, where general Turkic or Orthodox material must not stand in for them.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic and Latin-inflected bookish Ukrainian for Mohyla, Yavorsky and Gizel; German and Polish for the 1910 census; Russian for police files on the Stundists; Crimean Tatar and Arabic script for older *ilmihal* editions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1946 and 1949 liquidations of Greek Catholic unions, and present disputes between churches, are told differently by each side; name the teller. Reports on belief under occupation come from parties to the war.
- **Held for review:** psychics and healers also touch Pisces's magic ways; here they appear only as popular belief, and nothing had to be set aside under a rule.
