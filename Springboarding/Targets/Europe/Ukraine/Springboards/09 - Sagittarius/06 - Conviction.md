*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kyiv's Sixtiers and Lviv's dissidents of the 1970s:** check which causes each circle took up, and who signed what.
- **Crimean Tatar families and their defenders outside the community:** check how the national movement's allies are remembered in Crimean Tatar sources.
- **Rusyn and Galician Greek Catholics:** check how each church tells its martyrs' stories and who is honored.
- **Jewish families of Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa:** check the line between refuseniks, those who emigrated quietly and those who stayed.
- **Evangelical, Adventist and Jehovah's Witness congregations:** check wartime statements against members' own accounts.
- **Deputies elected in single-seat districts against list deputies:** check faction-switching rates by region.
- **Hungarian and Romanian minority leaders:** check public stands on language and schooling in their own press.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet-era dissent and memoirs; mixed for present-day stands, which are scattered in press; thin for Azov Greek and Bulgarian dissenters, where no single collection is known.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Hryhorenko, Pliushch and refusenik samizdat; Hebrew for refusenik networks abroad (comparison only); Crimean Tatar for Mamut; Church Slavonic and Russian for Matsiievych.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** self-immolation needs care in wording; wartime objectors, faction switching and the OUN past each draw opposed accounts, so give each with its teller and never fold a group into one stance. Criminal convictions belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none. The memoirs above already carry what two foreign biographies of the same dissidents would have added.
