# Ukraine: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, Russian, Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish, Greek (Rumeic and Urum), Yiddish and German (for Brody), Polish, Romanian, Hungarian. **Concept in one line:** going far from home (long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration) and how the road, the foreign and the traveler are regarded; short trips and commuting stay in Gemini.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mariupol Greek villages and Azov Bulgarian settlers:** check each group's founding-journey story in its own museums and press.
- **Crimean Tatars of the steppe and the coast:** check which areas lost most people to 19th c. emigration, and how families remember it.
- **Right Bank villages and Tavria farms:** check who walked south for harvest work and how both ends of the trek recorded it.
- **Galician and Bukovinian villages:** check emigration rates by district from 1890 to 1914 and the letters they received.
- **Jewish communities of Brody, Berdychiv and Odesa:** check emigration routes and the aid networks each used.
- **Hungarian-speaking Berehove and Romanian-speaking Hlyboka:** check work travel west and what their own press says about it.
- **Roma of several groups (Servy, Kelderari, Lovari, Crimean Roma):** check how each describes its own past routes, through community bodies rather than outside lore.
- **Hutsul villages near Bukovel against those far from it:** check who gained from tourists and how hosts talk about guests.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emigration, pilgrimage and border statistics; mixed for leisure travel before 1991; thin for Krymchak and Karaim travel records and for Roma routes told by Roma.
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the pilgrim accounts; Ottoman Turkish and Crimean Tatar for the emigrations; German, Yiddish and Polish for Brody; Greek and Russian for the 1778 resettlement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** displacement since 2014 and refugees since 2022 are wartime matters; the Crimean Tatar emigrations are read differently in Ukrainian, Russian and Turkish histories. Refugees abroad count as diaspora comparison, not units.
- **Held for review:** foreigners' guidebooks to Ukraine belong to a later pass on outsiders' views, so they wait for that; the standing rules touched no travel pointer.
