- **Jewish students of Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa:** check admission memoirs from 1887 and from the 1970s.
- **Crimean Tatar students:** check what higher study in their language was possible before and after 2014.
- **Donbas and Mariupol academic staff now in exile cities:** check accounts from relocated faculties.

- **Village and small-town families:** check first-generation students' accounts, region by region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutional histories and admissions data; mixed for students' own accounts; thin for Gagauz, Azov Greek and Roma higher-education paths, where community bodies are the place to start.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Church Slavonic for the collegiums; Polish for Kremenets; German and Romanian for Chernivtsi; Russian for the Kyiv university files and the admission memoirs; Crimean Tatar for the Simferopol university.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the founding aims of St Volodymyr University, the numerus clausus, the relocations and language-of-instruction rules are disputed, so attribute every account to the institution or community behind it. The language articles of the education laws go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** international rankings were dropped as saying little about standing inside Ukraine. One further pointer was set aside under the standing rule, as a category only: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
