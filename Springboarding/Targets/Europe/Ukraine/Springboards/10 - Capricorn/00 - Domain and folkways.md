# Ukraine: The domain, authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Polish and Latin (Commonwealth records), Hungarian, Romanian, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), German (Mennonite and colonist records). **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys and what is owed: career, status, reputation and responsibility, with age, time, rank, order and power ways as the folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former Hetmanate (Poltava, Chernihiv) against former Commonwealth lands (Volhynia, Podillia):** check which records of rank each side's families kept.
- **Galicia and Bukovina under Vienna, Transcarpathia under Budapest:** check which county, town and church offices each empire left and who filled them.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check the khan's offices, the clan *beys*, and the Mejlis and Qurultay in their own sources.
- **Jewish communities of Podillia and Volhynia:** check kahal, rabbinate and Hasidic court as three separate claims to obedience.
- **Hutsul, Boyko and Lemko villages:** check which local offices (pasture chief, headman, church elder) each group's own collections record.
- **Mennonite and German colonies, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check colonist self-government records and the elected offices in each settlement.
- **Donbas and Kharkiv industrial towns:** check plant directors, mine bosses and union posts in factory histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hetmanate and Soviet records; mixed for village authority; thin for Krymchak and Urum offices (start with community histories).
- **Lands:** how far the post-war nation reaches is still the author's call. These pointers follow each source's own Ukraine, let Crimea and the east speak through their peoples' institutions, and fix no line.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian for chancellery hands, Russian for the party files, Ottoman Turkish for *yarlıq* texts, Latin and Hungarian for the counties.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet personnel files hold private data; the Varangian debate and authority in occupied areas are contested: give each side's account as its own.
- **Held for review:** an outsider's national-character list on obedience was weighed for this overview and dropped as a stereotype source; no standing rule was touched.
