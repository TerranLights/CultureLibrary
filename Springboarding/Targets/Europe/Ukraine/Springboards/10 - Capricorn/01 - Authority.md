# Ukraine: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish, Yiddish (historical), German (Mennonite sources). **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician Greek Catholic parishes and Dnipro Orthodox parishes (both jurisdictions):** check whose word settles parish disputes, the priest's, the bishop's or the lay council's.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check community memory of clan nobles and of khans next to today's elected bodies.
- **Hasidic and non-Hasidic Jewish towns of Podillia and Volhynia:** check rebbe, rabbinate and state as rival sources of command.
- **Transcarpathian Rusyns and Hungarians:** check the *біров* and the Hungarian county as village authority, and what replaced them after 1945.
- **Mennonite colonies, Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz:** check elders, colony mayors and Russian officials in each settlement's records.
- **Industrial Donbas and Kharkiv against farming Podillia and Poltava:** check whom people name as "the boss" in oral histories.
- **Roma groups:** check whether officials treat one person as speaking for many, and how community members describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for chronicles, church disputes and Soviet documents; mixed for parental and village authority; thin for Roma and Krymchak community leadership.
- **Languages needed:** Old Rus' for the chronicle, Middle Ukrainian and Polish for the brotherhood papers, Russian for Ushytsia and the famine documents, German for Friesen, Ottoman Turkish for the khanate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1932 to 1933 orders sit inside the Holodomor debate; the Zaluzhnyi episode is recent and partisan; Roma sources may expose individuals. Obedience to law as such belongs to section 8 of this sign.
- **Held for review:** a foreign obedience experiment run in another post-Soviet country came to mind and was left out, since nothing links it to Ukraine.
