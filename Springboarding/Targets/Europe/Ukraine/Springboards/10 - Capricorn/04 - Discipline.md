## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician Greek Catholic and Studite communities, Orthodox monasteries of both jurisdictions:** check the monastic rules each follows and how obedience is assigned.
- **Evangelical, Pentecostal and Adventist congregations:** check written codes of conduct and how members describe correction.
- **Soviet-schooled grandparents and post-2017 New Ukrainian School pupils:** check how each generation recalls classroom punishment and grading.
- **Shtetl Jewish families of Podillia and Kyiv province:** check kheyder memoirs against Russian-language school memoirs of the same towns.
- **Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, Bukovinian Romanians:** check proverbs and customs of correction in each community's language.
- **Crimean Tatar families:** check upbringing manuals and memoirs from exile and return.
- **Sports and military families in Kharkiv, Dnipro and Odesa:** check how coaches and officers describe training.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school, monastic and Soviet labor history; mixed for household correction; thin for Roma, Karaim and Krymchak child-rearing.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Ukrainian for the 1586 statute, Russian for Myshetsky and Soviet decrees, Yiddish for Sholem Aleichem, Hungarian for Reformed records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence toward children, hazing in cadet schools and church expulsions touch living people; anonymize. The state's reasons for punishing go to section 8 of this sign.
- **Held for review:** Russian-language self-discipline influencers popular with young men were weighed and left off, because their audience spans borders and says little about Ukraine's own forms.
