## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Right-Bank lands of Polish manors against the Left-Bank Cossack country:** check which ranks each region's records name between lord and peasant.
- **Galician Greek Catholic priestly families and Orthodox clergy of the Dnipro lands:** check how far clerical rank passed from father to son in each.
- **Crimean Tatar *mırza* and commoner families:** check how rank is remembered after exile, in the community's genealogies and press.
- **Shtetl Jews of Podillia and Volhynia:** check learning, lineage and wealth as separate measures of standing.
- **Colony offices, church elders and estate owners in the Mennonite and German settlements:** check how each kind of standing ranked against the others.
- **Transcarpathian Hungarians, Rusyns and Roma:** check whether village seating, church places and marriages kept groups apart.
- **Post-Soviet Kyiv and the industrial east:** check where new money and old Soviet rank met or clashed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for noble, Cossack and clerical records; mixed for forms of address; thin for Roma internal ranking and for Karaim and Krymchak status orders.
- **Languages needed:** Greek and Slavonic for seals, Polish and Latin for the armorial, Middle Ukrainian for the 1729 inquiry, Russian for imperial estates and Soviet privileges, Hungarian for Transcarpathia.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class contempt (*хлоп*, *холоп*) and slurs for groups should be described, never used as labels; wealth data on living people needs care.
- **Held for review:** global elite-ranking lists were weighed and left out, because they rank individuals across countries rather than show Ukraine's own order of rank.
