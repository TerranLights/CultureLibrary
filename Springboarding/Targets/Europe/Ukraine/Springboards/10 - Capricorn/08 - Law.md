# Ukraine: Law, its spirit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Church Slavonic (early modern treatises), Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Romani, Hungarian, Romanian. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. What the law says, and courts as written, belong to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galicia and Bukovina, schooled in Austrian courts, against the Dnipro lands under Russian law:** check whether families tell different stories about using courts.
- **Peasant villages of Podillia and Poltava:** check customary judgment in the *волосні суди* records and in memory.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check custom, religious law and the state's law in the community's own legal histories.
- **Jewish communities of Volhynia and Odesa:** check the turn to outside courts across the 19th century.
- **Mennonite, Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** check how each settles quarrels without going to court.
- **Roma groups (Kalderash and others):** check which keep their own tribunals; sources are few, so start with NGO fieldwork.
- **Occupied and front-line areas:** check accounts of whose rules people followed, from each side's sources, marked as accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal theory and trust surveys; mixed for customary practice; thin for Roma, Karaim and Krymchak legal life.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Prokopovych, *Вехи* and imperial legal ethnography; Hebrew and Yiddish for *arkoes*; Romani for *kris*; Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish for the khanate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** vigilante punishment and occupation-era rules are contested; protect identities and present each source's account as its own. Statutes, codes and court rulings are pointed to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the national courts register was set aside here, since reading rulings as written is Libra's work and this sheet keeps to what law is for.
