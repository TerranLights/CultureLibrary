# Ukraine: The domain, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), German (colonist records), Bulgarian of the Budjak, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish, Romani. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, groups, hopes and humanitarian ideals, with settlement, migration, association and affiliation as the social ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boyko and Lemko uplands against Polissian forest hamlets:** check which gathering (parish feast, shared pasture, work party) each region's monographs say every household attends.
- **Left Bank *хутори* against nucleated Right Bank villages:** check how farmstead settlement changed who met whom.
- **Crimean Tatar returnee settlements:** check how mosque congregations and local Qurultay delegates frame membership.
- **Jewish towns of Volhynia and Podillia before 1941:** check the count and kinds of *khevres* per town.
- **Former German and Mennonite colonies, and Bulgarian and Gagauz villages of the Budjak:** check church, colony office and reading club as rival centers of village life.
- **Hungarian villages near Berehove and Romanian villages of Bukovina:** check which societies run each one's public calendar.
- **Russian-speaking mining towns of the Donbas and Kharkiv:** check workplace clubs against parish life.
- **Roma settlements:** check internal leadership as Roma-led organizations describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Jewish community records and civil-society monitoring; mixed for village gatherings; thin for Krymchak, Karaim and Azov Greek associations (start with community newsletters and museum staff).
- **Ground covered:** where the post-war frontier runs is a decision the author has not yet made. For community and ideals, each pointer takes in the territory its source calls Ukraine; Crimea and the eastern oblasts enter through their peoples' own associations and congregations, and no boundary is presumed.
- **Languages needed:** Old East Slavic for the tribes passage, Hebrew for the community encyclopedia, German for colony files, Bulgarian for Budjak clubs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Roma settlement reports name families; Crimean land seizures and 2004 are told differently by each side.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; general post-Soviet civil-society comparisons were weighed and dropped as treating Ukraine as one case among many.
