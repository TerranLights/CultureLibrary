# Ukraine: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine, Church Slavonic (monastic texts), Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Hungarian, Gagauz and Bulgarian (Budjak), Polish, Karaim. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shrinking hromadas of Chernihiv and Sumy against growing suburbs of Kyiv and Lviv:** check how attachment to place is asked about in each.
- **Displaced people from Mariupol, the Donbas and Kherson, with their hosts in Lviv and Uzhhorod:** compare newcomers' and old residents' words for who now belongs.
- **Two Orthodox parishes in one village:** check who changed, who stayed, and whose account the press quotes.
- **Chornobyl evacuees and the workers' town of Slavutych:** check whether resettled people describe a new community or a scattered old one.
- **Odesa's Jewish community and the Hasidic congregations of Dnipro and Uman:** check how each tells its own revival.
- **Karaim of Halych against Karaim of Crimea:** check how a handful of members keep institutions running.
- **Hungarian villages of the Berehove district:** check *falunap* organizers against church elders.
- **Hutsul hamlets spread over the slopes:** check how neighbors far apart define who belongs.
- **Servy, Ruska Roma and Transcarpathian Roma groups:** check how Roma-led organizations describe ties within each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chornobyl and wartime displacement; mixed for parish splits (much is partisan); thin for Gagauz and Halych Karaim (visits will matter).
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the Patericon, Hungarian for village-day reports, Gagauz and Bulgarian for Budjak histories, Russian for much Pripyat and Odesa material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parish transfers are bitterly contested, so quote each church by its own name; siege testimonies hold trauma and names; communities in occupied areas are reachable mostly through displaced members, so record who is speaking.
- **Held for review:** a Western communitarian theory text was weighed and left out, since it says nothing about Ukrainian places; no rule-based holds were needed.
