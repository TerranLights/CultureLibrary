# Ukraine: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (including the guild argot of the *лірники*), the Russian of Ukraine and its slang, Rusyn, Hungarian, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Romani, Polish and German (1920s district records). **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" and "them" divide, and what members are expected to do together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lirnyk and kobzar guilds of Podillia and Volhynia against those of the Left Bank:** check how each admitted, trained and expelled members.
- **The national districts of the 1920s (Polish, German, Jewish, Bulgarian, Greek):** check which groups were given a district and how each was ended.
- **Hungarian voters of the Berehove and Vynohradiv districts:** check how loyalty to the two organizations runs through villages and families.
- **Transcarpathian villages that answer "Rusyn" against those that answer "Ukrainian":** set census returns beside association rolls.
- **Rumei and Urum villages of the Azov coast:** check what name each gives the other and which festivals they share.
- **Crimean Roma, Servy, Kelderari and Ruska Roma:** compare self-names and marriage lines in Roma-led sources.
- **Brigades of the eastern and southern plants (Zaporizhzhia, Kryvyi Rih, Kharkiv):** check how plant papers set work groups against each other.
- **Hasidic and maskilic camps in Galician and Podillian towns:** check memoirs for who crossed from one to the other.
- **Wartime units recruited from one region:** check emblems for local names and symbols.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet workplace and wartime material; mixed for minority politics; thin for Urum and for some Roma groups, where Roma-led and community sources must lead.
- **Languages needed:** Ukrainian guild argot for Hnatiuk, Hungarian for the two organizations, Romani and Russian for Roma group names, Yiddish and Hebrew for the town camps.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some unit names and emblems are disputed at home and abroad; the *колаборант* label and Rusyn recognition are contested, so give each party's account by name; Soviet deportation of district populations needs survivors' accounts first.
- **Held for review:** one pollster's press release ranking nations by "collectivism" was dropped because it publishes no method; the standing rules took nothing out.
