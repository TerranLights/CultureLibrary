*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former Cossack villages of Poltava and Chernihiv against former serf villages of the Right Bank:** check how village histories remember free and unfree status.
- **Right Bank Polish gentry and Ukrainian peasants of the 1860s:** read memoirs from both sides of the *хлопомани* story.
- **Mennonite landholders and the landless:** compare colony settlement lists and petitions.
- **Azov Greek villages near Starobesheve:** compare local and Soviet tellings of Angelina (much is now reachable only through displaced residents).
- **Women and men of different ages:** check how polls on military registration split by age, sex and region.
- **LGBT people in Kyiv, Kharkiv and small towns:** use Nash Mir's regional breakdowns.
- **Roma of Transcarpathia and of the Odesa region:** check what ECRI says of each district's schools.
- **Donor and recipient oblasts:** set transfer flows beside residents' claims about which region supports which.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gender and minority monitoring since 2000; mixed for Soviet pay; thin for equality talk among Gagauz, Krymchak and Rumei communities, where community leaders are the first contact.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for gentry memoirs, German for Mennonite petitions, Russian for kolkhoz records and Angelina's Soviet press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *куркуль* belongs to the Holodomor story, contested in Russian accounts; Femen and LGBT rights draw strong church and political reactions; anti-discrimination law itself goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the Skovoroda phrase's exact source is unverified, so it is marked to be traced rather than cited; no item was held under the standing rules.
