# Ukraine: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Middle Ukrainian and Latin for 16th c. church texts), the Russian of Ukraine, Polish, Hebrew (Galician Haskalah), Hungarian and Czech (Transcarpathia), Crimean Tatar. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholic Galicia against Orthodox Podillia and the Left Bank:** check which churches backed temperance and literacy drives, and how villagers took them up.
- **Steppe provinces against Right Bank and Polissian villages:** compare where Stolypin farmsteads spread and where communes held.
- **Volhynian Ukrainians, Polish settlers and Jews of the interwar years:** read each community's memoirs of land reform.
- **Rusyns, Hungarians and Jews of Transcarpathia:** compare their memory of Czechoslovak schools and land policy.
- **Kharkiv and Kyiv officials of the 1920s:** check who carried Ukrainization and how their careers ended.
- **Hasidic and maskilic circles of Zhovkva, Brody and Ternopil:** check who answered the reformers and how.
- **Crimean Tatar reformers before 1917 and the returnees after 1989:** check which reforms each generation's press called renewal.
- **Doctors and patients in big cities and in villages:** compare how each group's surveys rate the health reform.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for post-2014 reforms; mixed for Stolypin and zemstvo work; thin for reform talk among Bulgarians, Gagauz and Azov Greeks, where village histories are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for the 1595 articles, Hebrew for Krochmal, Czech and Hungarian for interwar Transcarpathia, Russian for 1990s economic debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Union of Brest is told in opposite ways by the two church traditions; interwar settler policy is cited in accounts of the 1943 Volhynia massacres, which Polish and Ukrainian historians tell differently, so name each account's teller; legal reforms belong in Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** donor evaluation reports were weighed as a main source and kept only as context, since they grade reforms by their own funders' aims; the rules on held material did not come into play.
