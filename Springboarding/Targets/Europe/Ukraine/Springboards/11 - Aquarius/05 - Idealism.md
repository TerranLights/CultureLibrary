# Ukraine: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with 18th c. bookish Ukrainian for Skovoroda), the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar (Arabic-script and later), Hebrew and Yiddish, Polish, German (Mennonite sources), Hungarian. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician and Volhynian young Jews of the 1920s and 1930s:** compare Zionist, socialist and religious paths in memoirs.
- **Crimean Tatar reform writers before 1917 and returnee writers after 1989:** check which ideal society each describes.
- **Mennonite colonists and their Ukrainian and Nogai neighbors:** compare how each group's sources describe the colonies' order.
- **Soviet-schooled generations against those schooled after 1991:** check how textbooks frame Korchagin and Shevchenko's "renewed earth".
- **Bukovinian villages and Kyiv intellectuals:** compare who made and who watched films like *Вавилон XX*.
- **Students of 2004 against volunteers of 2014 and 2022:** check movement memoirs for words of hope and of disillusion.
- **Hungarian Transcarpathians and Ukrainian-speaking neighbors:** check whose ideals each community's press honors.
- **Poles of Zhytomyr and Khmelnytskyi parishes:** check whether the romantic "Ukrainian school" is still read in their schools and societies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Soviet and dissident material and for 2004 to 2014; mixed for Crimean Tatar and Mennonite ideals; thin for Roma, Gagauz and Azov Greek writing on ideal societies, which should be sought through their own associations.
- **Languages needed:** Gaspıralı's common Turkic literary language, Polish for the romantic poets, Hebrew for HeHalutz records, Russian for Ostrovsky's and Rudenko's originals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Soviet hero cults are now contested and some monuments removed; Polish romantic images of the Cossacks clash with Ukrainian ones; idealism among fighters in the current war should be quoted from named people.
- **Held for review:** a popular "national idea" self-help genre was skimmed for this sheet and passed over as advocacy rather than source; nothing was set aside under the standing rules.
