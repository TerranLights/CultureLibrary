# Ukraine: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Latin for Skovoroda's letters), the Russian of Ukraine and its slang, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish, Hungarian, Romanian, Polish. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Polissian and Podillian girls' rites against Galician and Bukovinian ones:** check where *кумління* is recorded and where it is missing.
- **Women writers of Volhynia and Bukovina:** compare letters for how friendship crossed regions and churches.
- **Kyiv and Lviv dissident circles:** check how friends were recruited, protected and lost.
- **Men in mining and factory towns of the east:** compare garage and workplace friendships with village ones.
- **Former Artek campers from different republics:** check memoirs for friendships that lasted, and for those that ended in 2014.
- **Jewish and Ukrainian schoolmates of interwar Lviv, Stanyslaviv and Vinnytsia:** look in both communities' memoirs for friendships that outlasted 1941.
- **Crimean Tatars who stayed on the peninsula and those who left for Kyiv or Lviv:** check how old friendships are kept across the occupation line.
- **Hungarian, Romanian and Ukrainian classmates in Transcarpathia and Bukovina:** compare whether school friendships cross language lines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literary friendships; mixed for village rites; thin for adult men's friendship today and for Roma, Gagauz and Karaim friendship customs (start with their own associations).
- **Languages needed:** Latin for Skovoroda, Russian for Gorky-era letters and Artek memoirs, Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish memoirs, Hungarian and Romanian for school friendships in the west.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** friendships with people in Russia, and Soviet "friendship of peoples", are painful and contested topics; dissident letters name informers and victims.
- **Held for review:** a popular-psychology list of "Slavic friendship traits" came to mind and was rejected as a stereotype source; the standing rules required no holds here.

## 5. First queries
1. Open Kovalynsky's biography of Skovoroda and list every passage about their friendship.
2. Search the Rylsky Institute journal for «кумління» OR «кумлення» and note the villages named.
3. Find the Lesia Ukrainka–Kobylianska letters in the complete-works edition and date the first and last.
4. Search Ukrainian news for «гаражний кооператив» «знесення» and collect residents' quotes.
