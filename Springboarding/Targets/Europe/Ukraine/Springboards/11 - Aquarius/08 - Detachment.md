# Ukraine: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Church Slavonic for monastic sources), Ukrainian Sign Language, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar and Ottoman Turkish (Sufi lodges), Yiddish, Hungarian, Polish. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms (calm, objectivity, equanimity) are prized and others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal) condemned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Carpathian monastic communities and lay pilgrims of Galicia:** check how each describes the hermit's turn from the world.
- **Crimean Tatar Sufi heritage and returnee religious life:** check whether mystical withdrawal survives in practice or only in buildings.
- **Kyiv and Kharkiv intellectuals of the 1930s:** compare who fell silent, who left and who conformed, from their letters.
- **Voters who stay home in eastern, southern and western oblasts:** compare stated reasons by region and age.
- **Front-line soldiers, medics and their families:** compare the composure trained in units with what families say comes home.
- **The deaf community of Kyiv and other cities:** check its response to being shown as a closed world on screen.
- **Hutsul and Pokuttian villages:** compare the restrained manner in Stefanyk's prose with village grief customs recorded nearby.
- **Hungarian and Polish communities:** check whether their own press speaks of political indifference in their region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime and media-ethics material; mixed for inner emigration; thin for Sufi practice and for Karaim and Krymchak contemplative traditions (start with community museums).
- **Languages needed:** Church Slavonic for the skete's rule, Ottoman Turkish for tekke inscriptions, Ukrainian Sign Language and its community's own commentary for *Плем'я*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime humor and composure touch on trauma and grief; the Yevpatoria site lies in occupied Crimea, so record the source and date of any report.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist communities and teachings came up as an obvious lead for non-attachment and are held as a category for the author's open question; nothing else was held.
