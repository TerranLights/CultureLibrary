*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (with Old East Slavic for the *Слово*), the Russian of Ukraine, Hebrew and Yiddish, Crimean Tatar, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian of the Budjak, Polish. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families of the missing from Mariupol, Kharkiv and western oblasts:** compare how each group keeps its hope in public.
- **Crimean Tatars in Kyiv and in Crimea since 2014:** check how hopes of return are voiced, and in which language and media.
- **Breslov and other Hasidic pilgrims in Uman and local Jewish communities of Podillia:** compare messianic and everyday hopes in their own publications.
- **Polissian and Podillian villages against cities:** check where *посівання* survives and who still does it.
- **Displaced people from occupied areas:** compare hopes of going home with plans to stay where they are.
- **Young people of Lviv, Dnipro and Odesa:** compare ten-year expectations in polls.
- **Hungarians of Berehove and Romanians of Chernivtsi region:** check which futures (in Ukraine, in the EU, in a kin-state) their press imagines.
- **Bulgarians and Gagauz of the Budjak:** check village and church sources for harvest and New Year hopes; data are scarce, so start with community calendars.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime hope and despair; mixed for folk wish customs; thin for Gagauz, Karaim and Roma hope talk, which needs community-led sources.
- **Languages needed:** Old East Slavic for the *Слово*, Hebrew for the Baal Shem Tov's letter, Russian for many eastern and southern polls and memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families of the missing and of prisoners are in grief, and some units' names are contested; the 2003 anthem change and Tychyna's later Soviet work are argued over, so name each account's teller.
- **Held for review:** prophecy and fortune-telling channels promising the war's end date were looked at and left out as unreliable commercial content, not culture-level sources; the standing rules held nothing back.
