# Ukraine: The domain, the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects (Polissian, Hutsul, Boyko and Lemko above all here), *surzhyk* and the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew (historical), Rusyn, Hungarian, Romanian, Old East Slavic. **Concept in one line:** how suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape are met: the hidden and the unconscious, confinement and institutions, sacrifice, compassion and transcendence, with magic ways as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The two best-recorded folk regions, Polissia and the Hutsul highlands:** check whether the named spirits, the healers' titles and the places held dangerous differ.
- **Left Bank and Galician villages:** check how much of each region's printed witch lore comes from writers rather than field notes.
- **Greek Catholic and Orthodox parishes (both jurisdictions):** check which rites against hidden harm each keeps, and in which service books.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check shrine visits and amulets in their own scholars' accounts, before and after 1944.
- **Jewish Podillia and Volhynia before 1941:** check the *ba'al shem* trade beside the rebbes' courts.
- **The Rusyn and Hungarian villages of Transcarpathia:** check which charm words cross the language line.
- **Southern Bessarabia and Bukovina (Romanian, Bulgarian and Gagauz villages):** check the restless dead and their remedies place by place.
- **The big Russian-speaking cities (Kharkiv, Dnipro, Odesa):** check the late-Soviet market in psychics and healers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk belief and Soviet psychiatry; mixed for present-day mental health (much of it new and wartime); little is known in print on the unseen among Karaim, Krymchaks or the Azov Greeks, so begin with their community museums.
- **Which lands:** the post-war borders have not been drawn. Here every pointer covers the lands its own source treats as Ukraine; Crimea and the east come in through the peoples living there and their own records, and no line is assumed.
- **Languages needed:** Russian for the Polissia volumes and Plyushch, Hebrew beside Rosman's sources, Crimean Tatar for shrine lore, Hungarian for Berehove.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** psychiatric files name patients; Crimean sites are under occupation; some healers keep charms from outsiders, so record only what is shared.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside. Comparative works on magic across all East Slavic lands were weighed and passed over, since they tend to fold Ukrainian material into a Russian frame.
