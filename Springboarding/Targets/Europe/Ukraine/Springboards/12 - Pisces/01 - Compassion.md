## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Greek Catholic parishes of Galicia and the secular volunteer hubs of Kharkiv and Dnipro:** check whether help runs through the church, the state or one's own hand in each.
- **Upland villages (Boyko, Hutsul) and Left Bank villages:** check what was given to the beggar and the blind singer at the door, in the ethnographers' records.
- **Roman Catholics of the Right Bank, many of Polish descent:** check how the Divine Mercy devotion turns into parish charity.
- **Jewish towns before 1941 and the rebuilt communities after 1991:** check the shift from communal charity to aid funded from abroad.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check prisoner-family support and charity at the mosque, in Crimean Tatar sources.
- **Roma of Transcarpathia and of Kharkiv:** check who helps whom, in Roma voices; outsiders' charity reports may cast Roma only as recipients.
- **Displaced families and the towns that took them in (2014, 2022):** check pity alongside friction in host communities.
- **People in *інтернати* and their families:** check how the residents themselves describe being cared for.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime volunteering and church charity; mixed for village almsgiving; for Budjak Bulgarians and Gagauz, little appears in print, so ask local museums and church councils.
- **Languages needed:** Yiddish for Mendele, the Slavonic of the Caves tale, Polish for the Right Bank devotion; a Romani speaker for any Roma field material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families of detained Crimean Tatars face risk if named; mercy toward Russian families and soldiers is disputed in public, so present the backers' and the critics' accounts separately; institution reports name residents.
- **Held for review:** no item withheld. The pointers weighed and dropped here were general aid-sector reports, which count deliveries and say little about how helpers felt toward those they helped.
