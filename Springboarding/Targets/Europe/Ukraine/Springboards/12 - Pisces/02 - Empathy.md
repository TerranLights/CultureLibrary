# Ukraine: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine and *surzhyk*, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew (for the Hasidic tales), Hungarian, Polish, Romani. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Western and eastern oblasts:** check how each side's polls describe the other's experience of the war, and whether that changed after 2022.
- **Displaced people and the families who host them:** check where hosts report understanding and where they report strain, in each group's own surveys.
- **Veterans and civilians:** check who veterans say understands them, and in which settings.
- **Crimean Tatars and their Ukrainian neighbors:** check how far the deportation story has entered schoolbooks and the national media.
- **Kyiv's Jewish community and the city's other residents, around Babyn Yar:** check how memorial projects ask non-Jewish visitors to take the victims' side.
- **Hungarian-speaking towns of Transcarpathia:** check how the language debate is felt from inside the community.
- **Roma families of Transcarpathia and of Kyiv:** check Roma-led accounts of being understood or not.
- **Russian-speaking Kharkiv and Odesa:** check how language choice and empathy are linked in residents' own interviews.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime testimony and documentary art; mixed for measured empathy (few Ukrainian norms); thin for Budjak villages (Bulgarian, Gagauz) and for the Azov Greeks (try displaced Mariupol associations).
- **Languages needed:** Hebrew and Yiddish to trace the Sasiv tale, Crimean Tatar for the memoirs, Hungarian for the Transcarpathian side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slur terms are study objects only; testimony from occupied areas may endanger speakers; the Babyn Yar memorial projects are disputed, so record each party's account.
- **Held for review:** none. Empathy training packages imported by firms and NGOs were looked at and left aside, since they say little that is specific to Ukraine.
