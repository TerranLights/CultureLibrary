# Ukraine: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian (including the Lemko song tradition), Church Slavonic, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Hebrew and Yiddish, German (Mennonite), Hungarian, Romanian. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician parishes and eastern industrial towns:** check whether self-denial is framed through the church year in one and through work or the army in the other.
- **Greek Catholics and Orthodox (both churches):** check whose martyrs each church honors, and whether the lists overlap.
- **The Jewish community of Lviv and its Christian neighbors:** check how each town tradition remembers the Golden Rose story, and in which language.
- **Mennonite and German colonists (historical):** check how nonresistance and martyrdom sat together in the colonies.
- **Crimean Tatar families:** check how service in the Ukrainian army fits into their account of 1944 and return.
- **Lemko and Boyko resettled families:** check whether songs of the fallen carry older laments of exile.
- **Donbas mining towns:** check how Soviet sacrifice stories are now retold or dropped in school and family memory.
- **Medics, rescuers and volunteers (Kyiv, Kharkiv, Dnipro):** check how civilians who took the risks describe the choice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and memorial material since 2014; mixed for sacrifice inside families, where ethnography is patchy; scarce for Budjak, Azov Greek and Karaim communities (ask their displaced associations).
- **Languages needed:** Yiddish and Polish for the Lviv legend's versions, German or Dutch for the *Martyrs Mirror* editions, Crimean Tatar for funeral reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Krasnodon, the Lviv legend's anti-Jewish setting and the Maidan killings are disputed, so keep each party's account apart; names of the fallen and of donors may be protected; occupied Crimea puts Crimean Tatar families at risk.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld. Western "effective altruism" writing was considered as a frame and left out, since it would sort Ukrainian sacrifice by an imported yardstick.
