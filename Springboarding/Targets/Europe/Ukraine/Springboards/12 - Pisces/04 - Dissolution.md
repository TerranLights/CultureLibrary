## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hasidic Volhynia and Podillia, and the Orthodox monastic tradition:** check how each describes the self given up to God, and in which books.
- **Kyiv's club scene and Pentecostal congregations of Volhynia:** check whether participants use the same words for release in such different settings.
- **Donbas mine towns and former collective-farm villages:** check how each describes the end of its central institution.
- **Azov Greeks, Karaim and Krymchaks:** check which language and rites survive and who keeps them; sources are scarce.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check language loss after the deportation and the revival efforts since return.
- **Hungarians of Transcarpathia:** check emigration and mixed marriage as forms of community thinning, in Hungarian-language studies.
- **Mennonite and German colonists (historical):** check what the colonies' end meant in their own memoirs.
- **Russian-speaking and *surzhyk*-speaking families:** check how speakers describe moving between languages, without assuming loss or gain.
