# Ukraine: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian and its dialects, the Russian of Ukraine, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish and Hebrew, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak and Rusyn (the Uzh valley), Romanian, Ruthenian chancery language (16th c.). **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Left Bank and Right Bank villages:** check whether the 1667 line still shows in household plans, speech or church life.
- **Galician and Podillian villages along the Zbruch:** check which kin, trade and parish ties crossed the old frontier.
- **Rusyn, Slovak and Hungarian villages of the Uzh and Tisza valleys:** check how families live with a state border cutting the valley.
- **Crimean Tatars and the people of the Kherson and Zaporizhzhia steppe:** check how Perekop is remembered on each side.
- **Jewish towns of Podillia and Volhynia (historical):** check the *eyruv* and the market square as lines between neighbors.
- **People who crossed the Donbas line (2014 to 2022):** check their own accounts against the monitors' reports.
- **Polissian and Hutsul villages:** check which sacred lines (furrow, fence, threshold) each region's ethnography records.
- **Urban families in Kyiv and Kharkiv:** check where privacy is drawn inside crowded flats and how the young describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for frontiers and borderland history; mixed for household boundaries (older ethnography only); thin for present-day personal space (one cross-national study and popular psychology).
- **Languages needed:** Ruthenian and Polish for the castle inventories, Ottoman Turkish and Crimean Tatar for Perekop, Slovak and Hungarian for the divided village.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Kresy* and the Donbas and Crimean lines are contested names and lines, so each source's term is reported as that source's; crossers' testimony from occupied areas can endanger them.
- **Held for review:** the Pale of Settlement was weighed and left for Libra, since a pointer on it would drift into the rules themselves.
