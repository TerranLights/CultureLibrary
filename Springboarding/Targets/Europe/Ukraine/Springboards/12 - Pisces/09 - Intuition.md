## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Galician almanac readers and Left Bank villages:** check whether printed forecasts or oral weather signs carried more weight.
- **Polish and Ukrainian heirs of the Wernyhora story:** check what each version has him foresee.
- **Frontline soldiers, medics and deminers:** check where each group trusts the hunch and where the drill.
- **Hasidic Volhynia and Podillia (historical):** check how a rebbe's insight is described next to the folk healer's.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check dream-telling and fortune-telling in their own words, apart from outsiders' notes.
- **Hungarian villages near Berehove:** check whether the *táltos* survives in speech or only in books.
- **Kharkiv, Dnipro and Odesa city dwellers:** check the market in tarot and predictions, and who buys.
- **Older rural women:** check, from their own interviews, the knowledge of signs credited to them, without assuming it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk divination and almanacs; mixed for expert intuition (memoirs, few studies); thin for minority seers (Hungarian, Crimean Tatar) beyond a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Polish for most Wernyhora sources, Hebrew for Korets, Crimean Tatar and Hungarian for the minority material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wartime prophecy channels can carry disinformation; soldiers' testimony may reveal units or places.
- **Held for review:** set aside nothing this time; the only thing turned down was a popular "intuitive thinking" test with no Ukrainian norms, which would have read as a finding.
