# Ukraine: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Ukraine, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** Ukrainian, the Russian of Ukraine and its criminal cant, *surzhyk*, Polish and Latin (early town records), Church Slavonic, Crimean Tatar, Yiddish (the Odesa milieu), Hungarian and Romanian (border districts). **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. The law itself is researched in Libra (its letter) and Capricorn (its spirit); this sheet cites that work and does not repeat it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Odesa and the Donbas:** check whether the outlaw is a hero, a villain or a businessman in each city's songs and press.
- **Galician towns of the Magdeburg-law era and Left Bank Cossack towns:** check which wrongs each record treats as grave.
- **Hutsul, Boyko and Polissian villages:** check how theft and shaming are recorded in each region's ethnography.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check how the community names wrongs done to it, in its own media and memory.
- **Residents of liberated towns:** check how neighbors separate collaboration from coping, in interviews from both sides.
- **Transcarpathian border villages (Hungarian, Rusyn, Romanian):** check which border dealings count as crime locally.
- **Odesa's Jewish milieu (historical):** check how Yiddish and Russian songs and stories each treat the thief.
- **Former prisoners in church communities:** check how converts describe their past and whether congregations take them back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war crimes and the 1990s; mixed for victimization (no regular national survey known here); thin for minority communities' own ideas of wrongdoing.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Polish for Lviv, the Russian criminal cant for the songs, Crimean Tatar for testimony, Hungarian and Romanian for border studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collaboration and war-crimes testimony can endanger witnesses and accused alike; 1990s figures may still be powerful or litigious; ethnic labels in crime coverage are recorded as other people's labels only. All law-related questions go to the Libra and Capricorn sheets.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; general criminology textbooks were judged too broad to add anything specific to Ukraine.
