*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hutsul highlands and Polissian forest villages:** check which beings and places each region fears, and the remedies named.
- **Jewish towns of the south and Right Bank (historical):** check pogrom fear in memoirs against what Christian neighbors wrote of the same days.
- **Central and southern villages hit by blacklisting in 1932:** check how fear is told by survivors' children and grandchildren.
- **Crimean Tatars:** check supernatural fears and fear of the state in their own folklore and memoir, kept apart.
- **Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and other frontline cities and Lviv or Uzhhorod in the west:** check what residents say they fear and how often.
- **Galician tourist sites and their visitors:** check how ghost stories are told for money and by whom.
- **Hungarian and Romanian villages of the west:** check the fright illnesses and their cures in each language's folklore.
- **Older people who lived through 1986:** check how radiation fear is remembered across Polissia and Kyiv.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state terror, pogroms and wartime fear; mixed for supernatural fear today; thin for Gagauz, Bulgarian and Azov Greek fear lore (try community museums and displaced associations).
- **Languages needed:** Russian for Gogol and Aronson's sources, Yiddish for pogrom memoirs, Crimean Tatar for the *albastı*, Hungarian and Romanian for fright cures.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Vinnytsia site and the pogroms carry rival accounts that must each be named as such; wartime fear surveys come from people under attack; grave sites are not to be visited without the families' and authorities' consent.
- **Held for review:** this sheet set nothing aside; the one candidate weighed and left out was a cross-national "uncertainty avoidance" score, rejected because it would label a people rather than point to sources.
