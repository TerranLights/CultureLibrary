## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **England by region:** North East, North West, Yorkshire, the Midlands, East Anglia, London and the South East, the South-West; name stocks and dress differ, so the domain should not be read off London.
- **Cornwall:** Cornish identity is recorded as a national minority and has its own naming revival.
- **Scotland:** Gaelic Highlands and Western Isles (patronymics and bynames), Lowland Scots areas, North-East fishing towns (inherited nicknames), Orkney and Shetland (Norse-derived names and a separate island self-image).
- **Wales:** the Welsh-speaking north and west against the English-speaking south-east and the Valleys; surname concentration makes nicknames work as identifiers.
- **Communities:** Romanichal Gypsies, Welsh Kale, Scottish Travellers, Irish Travellers, Showpeople and Roma; British Caribbean and British African (Somali and Yemeni families in Cardiff's Butetown and South Shields); British Chinese (Cantonese-speaking older settlement, newer Hong Kong arrivals); the Irish in Britain; Jewish communities (London, Manchester, Leeds, Gateshead); Polish Britons; Deaf BSL users.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland (a separate culture-nation) and British communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in statistics and archives; thinner for Cornish, Traveller and island sources, which lean on oral recordings.
- **Scope:** the United Kingdom's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; this Aries set is written for the core lands of Great Britain.
- **Languages needed for the domain:** Gaelic and Welsh for naming and rites; Scots for self-reference; Angloromani and Cant for Traveller sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish rites; Cantonese for British Chinese naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census ethnic categories; Traveller sources gathered by outsiders; gender identity debates (legal side: Libra or Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
