# United Kingdom: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and their islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its dialects, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Jamaican Creole as used in Britain, Yiddish, Cantonese for community sources; Old and Older Scots for the boasting record. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Glasgow and Edinburgh:** *gallus* is claimed as a Glasgow trait; worth testing against Edinburgh and the North-East.
- **Northern and Southern England:** "plain speaking" is a self-image some Northern towns claim; compare Yorkshire, the North East, the Midlands, London and the South-West rather than assume it.
- **Highland crofting communities:** standing up to landlords has its own songs and history.
- **North-west Wales and Cardiff:** asserting Welsh in daily use differs where it is the majority language.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** small communities where speaking out carries social cost; check local writing.
- **British Caribbean and British African communities:** *facety* and generational change in answering back to elders.
- **British Chinese families:** face and how far children assert themselves at home against school.
- **Jewish communities:** *chutzpah* in Jewish writing in Britain, compared across Orthodox and secular circles.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** speaking back to settled authorities and to the press.
- **Irish in Britain; women and men; class:** accent and gender shape who may speak.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, a separate culture-nation, for how speaking up in mixed company is handled across a divided public.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on manners and complaint, thin on measured assertiveness by region.
- **Languages needed for assertion:** Scots and dialect speech; Welsh and Gaelic for activist and song sources; Jamaican Creole for youth speech; Older Scots and Old English for boasting verse.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** stereotypes of "the reserved English" or "brash" regions; whistleblower cases; race in complaints data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
