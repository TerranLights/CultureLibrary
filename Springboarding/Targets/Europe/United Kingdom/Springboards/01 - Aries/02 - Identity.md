## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **English regions:** the North East and Yorkshire (strong local labels) against the Home Counties; the Black Country and Birmingham kept apart.
- **Cornwall:** a minority identity with its own language revival and census campaign.
- **Scotland:** Gaelic-speaking islands, the Lowlands, the Doric North-East and the Borders each answer "who are you?" differently.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** Norse heritage claims and a pull away from mainland Scotland.
- **Wales:** Welsh-speaking north and west against the anglicized south; *Cymreictod* without the language.
- **British Caribbean and British African:** generation and the Windrush years mark distinct identities.
- **British Chinese:** older Hong Kong and New Territories families against newer arrivals.
- **Irish in Britain:** Liverpool, Glasgow, London and Birmingham Irish differ by period of arrival.
- **Jewish communities:** Haredi Stamford Hill and Gateshead against secular and Reform life.
- **Gypsies, Roma and Travellers:** Romanichal, Kale, Scottish Travellers, Irish Travellers and recent Roma are separate peoples.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, whose identity choices (British, Irish, Northern Irish) differ sharply; British communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; national-identity surveys run for decades, and community histories exist for most groups.
- **Languages needed for identity:** Welsh and Gaelic for in-group self-description; Old English and early Welsh for origin texts; Shelta and Angloromani for Traveller self-names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "where are you really from?"; contested labels (*teuchter*, Traveller slurs); Brexit and independence politics; Old Firm sectarianism in Glasgow.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
