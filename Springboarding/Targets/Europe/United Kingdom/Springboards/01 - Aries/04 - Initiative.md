## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old coal and steel regions:** South Wales Valleys, County Durham, South Yorkshire, Lanarkshire; start-up rates and attitudes after industry are worth separating from the South East.
- **London and the South East:** high business births; check against the North and the South-West.
- **Cornwall:** a mining past built on bid-for work, then tourism self-employment.
- **Highlands and Islands:** agency-led development set against community trusts.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** small-island initiatives in energy and transport.
- **Welsh-speaking Wales:** language institutions founded by parents and volunteers.
- **Jewish communities:** immigrant trades in the East End, Leeds and Manchester.
- **British Caribbean:** *pardner* savings, churches and carnival as self-started institutions.
- **British Chinese:** catering and family firms; second generation moving into professions.
- **Gypsies, Travellers and Showpeople:** self-employment in trading, scrap and fairs.
- **Polish Britons:** shops and services after 2004.
- **Irish in Britain:** construction subcontracting and the "gangers".
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, a separate culture-nation, whose own founding stories (linen, shipbuilding) can be set beside Britain's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; business statistics, volunteering surveys and company archives are deep and open.
- **Languages needed for initiative:** Welsh and Gaelic for language-movement records; Yiddish for early Jewish trade sources; Cantonese for family-firm accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "enterprise culture" carries 1980s party politics; Traveller trades are often framed by outsiders as illicit.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
