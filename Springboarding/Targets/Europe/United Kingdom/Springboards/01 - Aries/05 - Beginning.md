## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Scotland:** how the weight of Hogmanay against Christmas shifted over time is a question to trace; Lowland and Gaelic year-start customs should be kept apart.
- **Shetland and Orkney:** Foula's old calendar and Norse-derived year customs.
- **Wales:** *Calennig* and *Hen Galan* in Pembrokeshire against the anglicized south-east.
- **England by region:** Plough Monday in the East Midlands and East Anglia; wassailing in the South-West; "lucky bird" first visitors in Yorkshire and the North.
- **Cornwall:** May Day and St Piran's Day as revived starts.
- **Jewish communities:** a separate new year and the readmission story.
- **British Chinese:** Lunar New Year as a second year-start.
- **British Caribbean:** Windrush arrival and watch-night services at New Year.
- **Irish in Britain:** St Brigid's Day (1 February) as the start of spring in Irish custom.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** the spring start of the travelling season and the horse fairs.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, where the marching season sets its own yearly start; British emigrant settlements abroad keep founding myths of their own.
