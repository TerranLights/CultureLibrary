## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coastal communities:** Cornwall, Northumberland, the Hebrides and Shetland, where sea rescue shapes local heroes.
- **Mining regions:** South Wales, North Wales, Durham, Yorkshire and Lanarkshire, where disaster and rescue left their own memorials.
- **Highlands and Islands:** Gaelic soldiers' songs and regimental memory.
- **Lowland Scotland and Northern England:** Border ballads of raiding and defiance, worth reading against regimental lore.
- **Women:** Grace Darling, nurses and munitions workers; endurance may be recorded differently.
- **British Caribbean and British African:** volunteers in both world wars and later moral-courage campaigns against racism.
- **Jewish communities:** refugees and Jewish servicemen; East End resistance in 1936.
- **Polish Britons:** exile servicemen and their descendants.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** wartime service and endurance under persecution, rarely recorded by outsiders.
- **Irish in Britain:** wartime and postwar labor, under suspicion in conflict years.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, where bravery is framed by the conflict years; emigrant British communities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and rescue courage; thinner for moral and everyday courage outside campaigns.
- **Languages needed for courage:** early Welsh and Old English for heroic verse; Gaelic for war songs; Polish and German for exile records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shell shock and soldiers shot for cowardice; empire wars; the white feather; disaster grief in living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
