## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Scotland:** a live independence question; Highland crofting self-provisioning against Central Belt industrial towns.
- **Wales:** the Welsh-speaking west, hill-farming Powys and the post-industrial Valleys may tie self-reliance to different things.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** island trusts and distance from Edinburgh as well as London.
- **Cornwall:** seasonal economies and pride in getting by.
- **North East England and old mill towns:** single-industry dependence and its loss.
- **The South-West and rural England:** smallholders, the self-employed and second-home pressure.
- **Gypsies, Travellers and Showpeople:** self-employment, family trades and dealings with welfare and settled institutions, each to be checked separately by community.
- **British Caribbean:** churches and savings clubs as self-help; generational change.
- **British Chinese:** family businesses as household self-sufficiency.
- **Jewish communities:** communal welfare in London, Manchester and Leeds.
- **Irish in Britain:** single men in construction work and later isolation in old age.
- **Older and disabled people:** independence as a care question.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, where the constitutional question takes a different form.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; independence politics and welfare are heavily studied, and self-provisioning has museum and oral records.
- **Languages needed for independence:** Older Scots and Latin for medieval texts; Welsh for denominational and campaign sources; Gaelic for crofting life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** independence referendums and Brexit rhetoric; "scrounger" discourse; food bank use; Traveller sources written about rather than by Travellers.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
