## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Glasgow and the Central Belt:** labor anger and sectarian football rivalry; compare with Edinburgh and Aberdeen.
- **West Wales and the Valleys:** ritual protest (Rebecca) against industrial strike anger.
- **The North West and Yorkshire:** Peterloo and mill-town memory; the 1984–85 miners' strike in South Yorkshire.
- **Cornwall and the South-West:** food riots and later anger at second-home owners.
- **London:** the East End, Brixton and Tottenham as sites of protest.
- **British Caribbean and British African communities:** anger at policing and its expression through music (dub poetry, grime).
- **British Chinese:** anger directed at the community, from 1911 to pandemic-era abuse; how it was answered.
- **Jewish communities:** Cable Street memory and responses to antisemitism.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** anger over evictions such as Dale Farm (2011).
- **Irish in Britain:** suspicion during the conflict years and later campaigns over wrongful convictions.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** small-community restraint; check whether island writing shows anger differently.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, where collective anger is tied to the conflict and parades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective anger (riots, protest, strikes); thin for private anger at home outside clinical and survey sources.
- **Languages needed for anger:** dialect and youth speech (much anger vocabulary is informal); Gaelic for satire; Welsh for rural protest sources; Old and Middle English for teaching on wrath.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** riots and policing of Black communities; sectarianism; antisemitism and anti-Chinese violence; domestic violence (survivors' records are restricted).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
