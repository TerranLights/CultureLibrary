## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orkney:** the Ba' divides Kirkwall into two sides each winter; set it beside Shetland's own contests (regattas, agricultural shows).
- **Highlands:** shinty, piping and Mòd medals as distinct contest worlds from Lowland football.
- **Glasgow and the West of Scotland:** football rivalry tied to religion; keep apart from Edinburgh and Dundee rivalries.
- **Wales:** north Wales eisteddfod culture against South Wales rugby towns.
- **The North East and Yorkshire:** leek shows, pigeon racing and brass bands in former mining villages.
- **The East Midlands and Derbyshire:** Shrovetide football towns.
- **Cornwall and Devon:** wrestling and hurling against national sports.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** horse trading and fairs; outsiders' coverage needs care.
- **British Caribbean:** cricket support and sound clashes; second-generation shifts into athletics and football.
- **British Chinese:** educational competition should be read from attainment data, not stereotype.
- **Jewish communities:** Jewish sports clubs and their place in British leagues.
- **Irish in Britain:** Gaelic games clubs in British cities.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, where Gaelic games and football rivalries split by community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; sport, festival and show records run back centuries, and clubs keep their own archives.
- **Languages needed for competition:** Welsh and Gaelic for eisteddfod and Mòd adjudications; Old Norse for Orkney; dialect for folk-football and show talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sectarian rivalry; football violence; coverage of Traveller fairs and fights.
