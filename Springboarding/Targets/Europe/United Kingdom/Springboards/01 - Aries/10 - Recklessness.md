## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Borders, Cumbria and Northumberland:** reiving memory and its later romance (Common Ridings) against daily caution.
- **St Kilda and the Hebrides:** cliff-fowling and sea risk where daring fed a community.
- **Shetland and Orkney:** fishing deaths and how risk at sea is remembered.
- **Lowland Scotland:** Darien as a national gamble; drink and danger in Central Belt health data.
- **Wales:** quarrying and mining risk in the north and south; stunt and hellraiser fame.
- **The South-West and Sussex:** fire customs defended against safety rules.
- **The North East and old estates elsewhere:** joyriding and how outsiders framed it.
- **Mountain and coastal England:** the Lake District and Cornwall as sites of call-outs.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** outsider claims about risk deserve scrutiny before use.
- **British Chinese:** the Limehouse gambling panics of the 1920s were outsider-led; separate panic from practice.
- **British Caribbean and British African youth:** "risk" labels from police and press need separating from young people's own accounts.
- **Irish in Britain:** danger on building sites and in tunnels among navvies and later laborers.
- **Jewish communities:** speculation and its antisemitic framing must be kept apart from evidence.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, separate, whose road racing and bonfire customs carry their own risk culture.
