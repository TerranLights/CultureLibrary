## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Outer Hebrides crofting townships:** cattle, cloth and the croft as older measures of worth.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** Norse-derived landholding and knitwear trades.
- **The Welsh valleys:** co-operatives and medical-aid societies as shared goods.
- **The Black Country:** workshop making (chain, nail) as value apart from land.
- **British Caribbean households:** the front room; "pardner" savings; barrels sent home.
- **Somali communities (Cardiff, Bristol, Sheffield):** remittances and rotating savings.
- **Romanichal Gypsies, Irish Travellers, Roma:** portable wealth; fairs such as Appleby.
- **Jewish communities:** charity and family firms in London, Manchester, Leeds.
- **English regions:** the North-South wealth divide; East Anglian farmland against London.
- **Northern Ireland (a separate culture-nation, comparison only):** a near neighbor for testing how land, money and display are talked about across a community divide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, probate and Mass Observation; thinner and often oral for minorities and islands.
- **Languages needed:** English dialects; Welsh and Gaelic for the museum and sound archives; Old and Middle English for the early record; Polish, Somali and Cantonese for community material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wealth gaps by ethnicity; stereotypes of Gypsy and Traveller families; the Highland Clearances; fortunes tied to slavery. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the core lands of England, Scotland and Wales with their islands. Inheritance and property law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
