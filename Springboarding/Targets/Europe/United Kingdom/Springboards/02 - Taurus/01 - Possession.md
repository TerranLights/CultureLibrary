# United Kingdom: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its dialects, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish, Old and Middle English; Angloromani, Polish and Yiddish for community material. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Romanichal Gypsy and Irish Traveller families:** customs for a dead person's belongings and for display china, distinct from house-dwelling neighbors.
- **Jewish refugee families and their descendants:** single objects that stand for a lost home.
- **Polish and other Central and Eastern European arrivals since 2004:** parcels home, rented rooms, what is bought on arrival.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** heirloom knitwear and chests in island houses.
- **East Anglia and the Fens:** detectorists' finds and old gentry archives.
- **Coastal Cornwall:** wreck goods and the line between finding and taking.
- **Irish in Britain:** lodging-house lives of laborers with few belongings.
- **Class across England and Scotland:** council-flat storage against country-house collections.
- **Northern Ireland (scouted on its own; comparison only):** banners, sashes and other objects kept as community markers, a contrast for keepsakes in Britain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on disposal, pawning and crime; thinner on what people carry daily and on minority keepsake customs.
- **Languages needed:** English for most of it; Middle English for the Paston Letters; Gaelic and Shetland dialect for island holdings; Angloromani, Polish and Yiddish for community testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hoarding is a mental-health matter; Gypsy and Traveller death customs should be taken from the families, not older outsider journals; refugee testimony carries trauma. Finds law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Gregson and Crewe's chapters on car boot sales and charity shops; list the categories of goods they found people would not part with.
2. Search the Portable Antiquities Scheme by county for personal items (brooches, buckles, coins) and compare counts for Norfolk, Cumbria and Wiltshire.
3. Search Whitelock's *Anglo-Saxon Wills* for bequests of named objects and tabulate object, giver, receiver.
4. Search *Travellers' Times* for "burning the wagon" and "Crown Derby" and note who is speaking.
5. Request TfL's annual lost property figures and list the top ten categories.
