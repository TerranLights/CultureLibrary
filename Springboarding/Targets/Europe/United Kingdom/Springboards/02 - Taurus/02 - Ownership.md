## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland and Hebridean crofters against Lowland estates:** heritage claims to land against title deeds.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** udal holding and its afterlife in island identity.
- **Welsh-speaking north-west Wales:** local ownership against holiday homes.
- **Cornwall:** the Duchy as landlord; second homes; mining shares.
- **Cumbria and the Lake District:** owner-farmer "statesmen" families, the National Trust and estates.
- **East Anglia and the Fens:** drainage, commons and large arable holdings.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities:** owning a site and the right to stop.
- **Ex-council estates in English and Scottish cities:** tenants turned owners, and their heirs as renters.
- **British African, Caribbean and Chinese households:** ownership gaps; family firms.
- **Northern Ireland (its own culture-nation; for comparison):** land and housing held along community lines, under a separate land registry.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; land and housing are among the best-documented British topics, though full landownership data are patchy outside registered titles.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic for *dùthchas* debates; Welsh for second-home campaigns and tithe apportionments; Old English and Latin for charters and Domesday; Angloromani for Traveller sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Gypsy and Traveller evictions; anti-second-home arson campaigns in Wales (1979 on); the Clearances. Property and tenure law go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
