*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Durham, South Yorkshire and the Welsh valleys:** former coal towns where worth was bound to a vanished trade.
- **Glasgow and west-central Scotland:** "gallus" self-assertion against the "cringe" debate.
- **Welsh-speaking communities:** worth tied to a language once officially belittled.
- **British Caribbean families:** schooling labels and the movements that answered them.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities:** stigma pressed from outside against pride kept inside.
- **Irish in Britain:** prejudice that is often overlooked.
- **Polish and other Central and Eastern European workers:** worth when qualifications go unrecognized and jobs fall below training.
- **Seaside towns of eastern England (the Lincolnshire and Essex coasts):** decline and the worth of those left behind.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** worth after shipyard and mill closures in Belfast, set beside the British coalfields.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for class and unemployment; mixed for minorities, where worth is often studied through discrimination rather than in its own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for the Blue Books' reception; Gaelic for honor and shame terms in song; Old English for the poems; Angloromani and Polish for community testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data; school-labeling history; the word "chav" and similar slurs, to be studied as data, not used as labels.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
