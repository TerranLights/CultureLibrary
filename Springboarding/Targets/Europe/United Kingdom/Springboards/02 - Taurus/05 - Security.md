## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South Wales coalfield towns:** pooled medical aid against later pit-closure insecurity.
- **Fishing towns of the Scottish Borders coast, Shetland and Orkney:** sea risk, widows and crew shares.
- **Cumbrian flood towns:** insurance and recovery after repeated floods.
- **Lincolnshire and the Fens:** Polish, Lithuanian and Romanian farm and packhouse workers on short contracts.
- **Highland and island communities:** famine memory and emigration.
- **Somali families:** remittances as security for relatives abroad.
- **British Caribbean households:** the pardner as an emergency fund.
- **Jewish communities:** a long-standing communal welfare system.
- **British Chinese workers in informal jobs:** risk without protection.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** a check on whether talk of security turns more on physical safety where conflict is recent.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; welfare history and financial surveys are deep. Informal security (pardner, remittance, kin) is thinly recorded and needs oral sources.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic for famine song and testimony; Welsh for valleys mutual-aid records; Somali and Polish for community interviews; Old English for the Chronicle.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** food-bank use and benefit stigma; deaths of migrant workers; remittance firms and banking restrictions. Benefit and employment law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
