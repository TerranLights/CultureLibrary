## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East End families and their descendants in Essex:** kin continuity broken by rehousing.
- **Suffolk and Norfolk villages:** old farm families and newcomers.
- **Upland mid- and north Wales:** chapel, farm and language continuity.
- **Cumbrian hill farms:** hefted flocks and holdings passed on.
- **Shetland and Whalsay fishing families:** island boundaries kept amid change.
- **Highland villages with many incomers:** locals and settlers.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller families:** kin networks and stopping places as separate threads of continuity, each to check in the communities' own accounts.
- **The Windrush generation:** settled lives made unsafe late in life.
- **Polish and Lithuanian families:** staying or going back.
- **Black Country towns:** industrial heritage kept in museums after the trades ended.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** continuity after decades of upheaval and a peace process, as a contrast case.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; Britain has a deep store of community studies and parish records. Many of the classic studies are from the 1950s-1980s and need newer work beside them.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for upland Wales; Gaelic for Highland heritage writing; Shetland dialect for Whalsay; Old English for *The Ruin*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Windrush cases; tensions between locals and incomers; Gypsy and Traveller site evictions. Immigration status law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
