## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **London and the South East against the North and Wales:** the widest regional wealth gaps.
- **Cardiff against the valleys:** coal profits and coal poverty in one region.
- **Glasgow's Merchant City:** tobacco and sugar fortunes and their links to slavery.
- **Scottish Borders and Cumbria:** raiding wealth in history; farm and estate wealth today.
- **Birmingham and the Black Country:** paternal industrial wealth beside workshop poverty.
- **Cornwall:** a poor region with rich second-home owners.
- **British Nigerian and other British African communities:** wealth shown and shared at celebrations.
- **British Chinese families:** wealth built by family businesses.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller families:** what counts as wealth in the communities' own accounts (trailers, horses, gold), checked against how outsiders have recorded it.
- **Polish families:** savings sent home to build houses in Poland.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** Belfast's linen and shipbuilding fortunes as a parallel to Glasgow's and Cardiff's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for top incomes, aristocracy and regional gaps; thin and stereotype-prone for minority wealth practices.
- **Languages needed:** Middle English for Chaucer and Langland; Middle Welsh for the Treasures; Yoruba and Cantonese for celebration customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slavery-derived fortunes; antisemitic tropes about Jewish wealth, which must be named as tropes and never repeated; television that exoticizes Traveller weddings. Tax and inheritance law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
