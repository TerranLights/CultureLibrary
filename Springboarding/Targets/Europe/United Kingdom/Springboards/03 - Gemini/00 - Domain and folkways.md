# United Kingdom: Communication and learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyneside and Wearside against Teesside:** neighboring dialect areas; compare the survey localities before pooling them.
- **Gaelic-speaking Western Isles against the Scots-speaking Lowland towns:** census tables by council area separate the home languages.
- **Gwynedd and Anglesey against the South Wales Valleys:** home Welsh and school medium vary; separate by local authority.
- **Shetland and Orkney:** island speech with a Norse substrate; keep apart from mainland Scots in any dialect count.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities:** Romany Gypsies, Irish Travellers and recently arrived Roma use different home languages (Angloromani, Cant, Romani); never pool them.
- **Deaf BSL users:** check regional BSL variation and Deaf club records.
- **British Caribbean and young Londoners of many backgrounds:** separate generation and neighborhood in the Multicultural London English studies.
- **Hong Kong arrivals since 2021:** period of arrival and home language (Cantonese) are the variables; data are new and thin.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** a separate culture-nation; its Irish and Ulster-Scots policy offers a contrast for the minority-language question, nothing more.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialect, literacy and community studies; thin for recent arrivals and Cornish home use.
- **Lands:** the United Kingdom's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of England, Scotland and Wales with their islands.
- **Languages needed:** English with regional dialect dictionaries; Scots; Scottish Gaelic; Welsh; Cornish; BSL; Old and Middle English, and Latin for medieval schooling; Cantonese and Romani for some communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1847 education reports on Wales and the "Welsh Not" are contested history; research with Traveller and Deaf communities should go through their own organizations.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
