## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ceredigion against south Pembrokeshire:** the Landsker line divides Welsh-speaking and English-speaking areas; compare early-years language routes on either side.
- **The Western Isles:** Gaelic-medium playgroups and schools; check how many children come from Gaelic-speaking homes.
- **Teesside:** steel and chemical apprenticeships; compare time-served routes before and after the plant closures.
- **British Caribbean families:** separate the 1960s to 1980s Saturday-school movement from today's provision.
- **Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot communities:** separate weekend schools, each tied to its own language and church or community body.
- **British Chinese families:** Cantonese and Mandarin weekend schools differ by intake; check the year each opened.
- **Showpeople and Gypsy and Traveller families:** the traveling season and family apprenticeship; check schooling records against families' own accounts.
- **Orthodox Jewish communities in north London and Gateshead:** religious learning institutions alongside state requirements; check community and inspection sources separately.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literacy history, cohort studies and community schools; thin for learning inside Traveller and Showpeople families.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and Old English for Ælfric; Welsh for the circulating schools and Mudiad Meithrin; Gaelic; Greek, Turkish, Cantonese and Hebrew for community schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "educationally subnormal" history and Traveller exclusion are painful records; cohort data need a user license; schooling as an institution belongs under education (Sagittarius).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
