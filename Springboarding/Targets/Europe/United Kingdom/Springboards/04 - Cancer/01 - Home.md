# United Kingdom: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its dialects, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish; Old English for place names; Cantonese, Polish and Yiddish for some communities. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Cornwall and coastal Devon against inland England: second-home shares, and local words for outsiders who buy.
- Gwynedd and Anglesey: housing and the Welsh language at home, in campaign and council records.
- Glasgow and the Clyde towns: tenement and later high-rise homes, a sequence to trace street by street.
- Essex and the Medway towns: plotlands, interwar estates and new towns side by side.
- British Chinese families: homes above family shops; check by generation and by city (Liverpool, Manchester, London).
- Polish and Lithuanian arrivals since 2004: shared rented houses as a first home; check the move to owning.
- Liveaboard boaters and Travelling Showpeople: the home that moves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tenure, condition and furnishings; thinner for home as a feeling, which needs life-writing and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh (*adra*/*adre*) shows north-south variation; Cantonese and Polish for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** second-home disputes in Wales have a history of arson (the 1979-90s cottage fires); handle as contested.
- **Held for review:** housing and home-ownership studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download the English Housing Survey headline report and the matching Scottish and Welsh tables; set tenure side by side.
2. Search the English Place-Name Society survey for *-ham* and *hām-stede* names in one county.
3. Visit or search the Museum of the Home collection for the front room, then read McMillan.
4. Search Cornwall Council and Gwynedd Council for second-homes policy papers and their consultation responses.
5. Query the Leeds Archive of Vernacular Culture for room names ("house-place", "parlour").
