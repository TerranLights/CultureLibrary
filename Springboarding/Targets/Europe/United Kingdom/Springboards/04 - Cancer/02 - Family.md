# United Kingdom: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its regional forms, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old and Middle English; Greek, Turkish, Cantonese and Yiddish for community sources. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Highland and Hebridean clan areas against Lowland Scotland: separate surname-and-kindred reckoning from parish-based family records.
- Welsh-speaking families keeping *ap*/*ab* patronymics: check how many revived them, and when.
- Lancashire and Yorkshire mill towns: kin as a labor network, from Anderson's period onward.
- Greek and Turkish Cypriot families in north London (Haringey, Enfield): check godparent and in-law ties by community.
- Haredi Jewish families (Stamford Hill, Gateshead, Salford): household size and community ties; check with community sources.
- Gypsy, Roma and Traveller extended families: separate Romany Gypsy, Irish Traveller and Scottish Traveller lines.
- British Chinese families: separate Hong Kong, Malaysian and recent mainland origins.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in genealogy and family history; mixed for chosen family and for minority kin systems, which need community sources.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for patronymic and *perthyn* material; Gaelic for kindred terms; Greek and Turkish for Cypriot community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** adoption and donor-conception records are closed or restricted; DNA-test discoveries; clan chiefship disputes.
- **Held for review:** kinship and family-network studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
