# United Kingdom: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English with its regional words for insiders and incomers, Scots and Orkney and Shetland dialect, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish; Old English; Ukrainian for recent arrivals. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Orkney and Shetland: incomer terms and the years it takes to stop being one; check island archive oral histories.
- Cumbria and Westmorland fell farms: generational families against second-home owners and retirees.
- Liverpool's long-settled Black community and Cardiff's Butetown: generations of belonging against outsiders' labels.
- Boston and Spalding (Lincolnshire): Lithuanian, Latvian and Polish arrivals since 2004; separate local press from the arrivals' own media.
- Welsh learners against first-language speakers: whether learning the language changes who is counted in.
- Armenian community (St Sarkis, Kensington): the church as a center of belonging; check by generation.
- Ukrainian arrivals since 2022: guests under a scheme against settled residents.
- Northern Ireland, as comparison only: its surveys offer British, Irish and Northern Irish identities together, a contrast in how belonging is asked.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; belonging is surveyed often and the "incomer" theme is well studied in rural Scotland and Wales.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic for *dùthchas* material; Welsh for *cynefin* and learner sources; Orkney and Shetland dialect for island terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "white settler" and similar labels; Windrush cases; sectarian football rivalries; treat each as contested.
- **Held for review:** surveys of belonging and identity among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
