# United Kingdom: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old English for the word's history; Tagalog, Polish and Cantonese for the care workforce and community elder care. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- South Wales valleys: mutual-aid traditions against later state care; check friendly-society and miners' records.
- Lewis and Harris: the Gaelic *cùram* and church-centered care of the sick and old.
- Shetland and Orkney: care across water and distance; check island health boards' reports.
- Filipino and Polish care workers: separate those employed in care homes from live-in and agency workers.
- British Chinese elders: community elder-care centers in Manchester, Liverpool and London; check language access.
- Quakers and other old dissenting communities: care institutions they founded, and their records.
- Young carers (under 18): separate by nation; Scotland and Wales keep their own counts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; unpaid care is in the census, and regulators publish detailed inspection data.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic for *cùram*; Welsh for carers' services; Tagalog and Polish for migrant care workers' own media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse inquiries; health records are closed; assisted dying belongs to Scorpio and is contested.
- **Held for review:** carer-support and elder-care studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the census unpaid-care tables for England and Wales and for Scotland, by local authority.
2. Read the latest *State of Caring* report and list the questions it asks carers.
3. Search the Borthwick Institute catalog for The Retreat's casebooks and visitor books.
4. Read Rawcliffe on St Giles's, then search the Wellcome Collection for other medieval hospital records.
5. Search Gaelic sources and Tobar an Dualchais for *cùram* in both its senses.
