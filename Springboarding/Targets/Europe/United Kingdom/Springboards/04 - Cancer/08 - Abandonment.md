# United Kingdom: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old English; Caribbean English for separated-family terms. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Sutherland and the north-west Highlands: Clearance memory by township; separate estate papers from Gaelic song.
- Bala and Gwynedd: Tryweryn memory against the Elan Valley reservoirs in Powys.
- Former coalfields (South Yorkshire, Durham, south Wales): "left behind" talk after pit closures; check by village.
- British Caribbean families: separate the Windrush-era parents from the children who followed years later.
- Care leavers and adoptees: separate by decade of adoption and by nation's care system.
- Irish in Britain: older single men living alone; check community welfare groups' reports.
- Former evacuees and child migrants: separate those who settled happily from those who felt sent away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; abandonment leaves records (foundling, adoption, care, eviction) and a strong memoir tradition.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic for Clearance song and *fuadach*; Welsh for Tryweryn sources; Caribbean English for family-separation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** adoption and foundling records are partly closed; forced-adoption, child-migrant and care testimony comes from living people; boarding-school harm is contested.
- **Held for review:** family-separation and migration studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
