# United Kingdom: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its dialects, Scots (including Doric), Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old English; Polari; BSL; Polish and Cantonese for two of the communities below. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tyneside and Aberdeenshire:** two regional speech communities with their own recorded corpora; separate dialect from class.
- **Hebridean Gaelic women's song culture:** waulking and lament as channels for comment; check by island and decade.
- **The South Wales valleys and Welsh-speaking north-west Wales:** choir, chapel and rugby crowd as places of open singing; compare the languages used.
- **Ghanaian Londoners:** cloth, funeral dress and church as expression; separate hometown associations, churches and generations.
- **Polish communities:** post-war exile families against post-2004 arrivals; check the Polish-language press for each.
- **Hong Kong arrivals since 2021:** separate by arrival scheme and age; political expression may carry risk, so anonymize.
- **New Travellers:** the 1980s free-festival scene and its clashes over where and how people may live and gather.
- **Deaf BSL users:** expression in a visual language; Deaf clubs as places of their own.
- **Gay communities:** Manchester's Canal Street, Brighton and Soho, by period (before and after 1967 and 2004).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for subcultures, emotional history and attitudes surveys; mixed for regional speech; thin for recent arrivals, where community media and oral history must stand in.
- **Languages needed:** Polari's lexicon to read coded mid-century sources; Doric and Gaelic for north-east and island song; BSL (video) for Deaf expression; Cantonese and Polish for those communities' own press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** debates on gender expression and on speech are polarized; record each side's sources without taking one. Any legal side of free speech goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as sources on dress, speech and style; held under the standing rule.
