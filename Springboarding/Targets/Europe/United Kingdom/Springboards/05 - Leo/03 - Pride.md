# United Kingdom: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and regional English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish; Old and Middle English; Somali, Shona and Ndebele for two communities below. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yorkshire:** the East Riding, the Dales and the West Riding cities separately; check whether county pride runs the same way in each.
- **County Durham pit villages:** pride tied to a trade that ended; compare banner lodges that still march with those that do not.
- **Hull:** a city's self-image before and after 2017; compare with Sheffield.
- **Cornwall:** Cornish identity on the census and in the revival; separate incomers from long-settled families.
- **Wales:** the rugby south against Welsh-speaking Gwynedd, where language may carry more of the weight.
- **Scotland:** Lowland cities against the Highlands and islands; check how each answers the identity questions.
- **Irish in Britain:** St Patrick's Day parades in London and Birmingham; separate the 1950s generation from later arrivals.
- **Somali communities in Cardiff's Butetown and Bristol:** separate the old seafaring settlement from refugee arrivals of the 1990s.
- **Zimbabwean communities:** separate by arrival period and home region; check their own associations and churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national identity (surveys and census); mixed for civic and trade pride (local archives, gala records); thin for recent communities, where their own media and associations must be found first.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for rugby writing and the Welsh-language press; Cornish for revival material; Somali, Shona and Ndebele for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** flags and national symbols are politically charged; record who flies what and why, without judging. Pride events attract organized opposition; cite both sides' own words.
- **Held for review:** group-pride sources from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, held under the standing rule.
