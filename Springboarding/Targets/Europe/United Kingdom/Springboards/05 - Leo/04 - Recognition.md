# United Kingdom: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Scots, Cornish; Old and Middle English; French and Lingala for Congolese sources, Portuguese for Lusophone communities. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oxford and Cambridge towns against their colleges:** separate the honors of the university from those of the town's own people and trades.
- **Aberdeenshire and the north-east:** local and farming recognition (agricultural-show prizes) beside national lists; check by parish.
- **Swansea and Gower:** a city whose literary fame centers on one poet; compare whose names are marked and whose are not.
- **Welsh- and Cornish-language communities:** bardic orders as their own honors systems.
- **British Caribbean communities:** recognition sought after the Windrush scandal; separate first arrivals from later generations.
- **Congolese communities:** recognition of professional qualifications gained abroad; check by profession and arrival route.
- **Portuguese and Brazilian communities:** separate Madeiran families from mainland Portuguese and from Brazilians; check community media in Portuguese.
- **Sephardi Jews:** synagogue honors (who is called up, who holds office) at Bevis Marks, kept apart from Ashkenazi practice.
- **Deaf BSL users:** campaigns for BSL to be acknowledged as a language (2003 statement; later legislation, whose legal side is Libra's).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal honors (the Gazette, Cabinet Office data, plaque registers); mixed for workplace credit; thin for informal, everyday thanks, which needs ethnography or diaries.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh and Cornish for bardic-order records; French and Lingala for Congolese community sources; Portuguese for Lusophone associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honors carry imperial names, a live dispute; quote stated reasons, not verdicts. Nomination files are closed for decades; only released ones may be used.
- **Held for review:** acknowledgment and honors records for South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; held by the standing rule.
