# United Kingdom: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old and Middle English; BSL; Vietnamese, Cantonese and Turkish for communities below. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Shetland:** a small island community; set Goffman's outsider account beside islanders' own oral history and check by island.
- **Dumfries:** the Guid Nychburris festival and its crowned Queen o' the South; check who is chosen to be the town's face each year.
- **Brighton against rural Sussex:** a city of open gay life beside villages; compare visibility by decade.
- **British Caribbean communities:** self-portraits and photography archives against press images; separate by generation.
- **Vietnamese communities:** refugee families of the late 1970s and 1980s against later arrivals, some undocumented, for whom being seen carries risk.
- **British Chinese communities:** takeaway families, Hong Kong arrivals and students kept apart.
- **Gypsies and Travellers:** census visibility since 2011 against press visibility; Romanichal, Irish Traveller and Roma separately.
- **Turkish, Turkish Cypriot and Kurdish Londoners:** evil-eye charms and practice, checked by community and generation.
- **Deaf BSL users and disabled people:** television presence and public staring as separate variables.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on media representation and census categories; mixed on everyday gaze and greeting, which needs ethnography; thin on undocumented people, by design.
- **Languages needed:** Scottish Gaelic for charm texts; Turkish and Kurdish for evil-eye practice; Vietnamese and Cantonese for those communities' press; BSL for Deaf media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance and immigration status are sensitive: do not identify people whose visibility puts them at risk. Charm texts were collected under unequal terms; read the collector's choices critically.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as cases of representation and visibility; held under the standing rule.
