## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Edinburgh against Glasgow:** endowed charitable schools (George Heriot's, from the 1620s) against merchant and civic giving; compare foundations by century.
- **Somerset, the Mendips and Devon's Dartmoor:** rural visiting customs, harvest suppers and village charities; check parish records.
- **Welsh chapel communities:** collections, chapel debts and *calennig*; compare Welsh- and English-language chapels.
- **Orkney:** neighbor help on farms and in fishing; check local archives for shared-labor customs.
- **York and other Quaker centers:** faith-linked philanthropy; separate firm, family and meeting.
- **Jewish communities:** Ashkenazi and Sephardi giving bodies separately; check boards of guardians and synagogue records.
- **Zimbabwean families:** check remittance and burial-society records; separate Shona- and Ndebele-speaking associations.
- **Kurdish and Turkish community centers:** collections for home villages and for newcomers; check by organization.
- **Pub regulars against non-drinkers:** whether the round reaches those outside drinking culture.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal giving (tax data, surveys, charity registers); mixed for informal help; thin for remittances by community, where estimates are rough.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh and Scots for calendar-gift sources; Hebrew and Yiddish terms for Jewish charity records; Shona, Ndebele and Kurdish for community associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** individual donor and recipient records are private; use aggregates. Remittance data can expose families' circumstances; keep it at group level.
- **Held for review:** giving, alms and remittance sources tied to South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; held back under the standing rule.
