*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland clan country against Lowland burghs:** hereditary chiefs against elected town councils and merchant guilds; check by century.
- **Northumberland's estates against Tyneside's unions:** landed and labor leadership in one county.
- **Glasgow's shipyards and the Lanarkshire and Ayrshire coalfields:** workplace leaders and their later careers.
- **Welsh chapels and choirs:** the *arweinydd* of a choir and the chapel elder; compare Welsh- and English-language chapels.
- **Polish community in London:** the government-in-exile (to 1990) and parish and Saturday-school leadership; separate exile families from post-2004 arrivals.
- **Ghanaian hometown associations:** check whether they install diaspora chiefs or office-holders, and how members choose them.
- **Portuguese associations in south London and Jersey:** check community leadership by island of origin (Madeira, the mainland).
- **Isle of Man and the Channel Islands (comparison only):** Tynwald and the States as nearby self-governing models, not part of the nation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political, military and union leaders; mixed for workplace and community leaders; thin for informal leaders in families and small groups.
- **Languages needed:** Scottish Gaelic for clan-era sources; Welsh for chapel and choir records; Polish for exile-government papers; Twi and Portuguese for community associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** formal authority, rank and obedience go to Capricorn. Union and kirk-session records may name private people; use them at group level.
- **Held for review:** leaders and community associations among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; set aside by the standing rule.
