# United Kingdom: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and its playground dialects, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish; Old and Middle English; BSL; Vietnamese, Portuguese and French or Lingala for communities below. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Yorkshire Dales and the East Riding against Leeds:** rural and urban childhoods; check the Opies' and Roud's place-names for each.
- **Highland shinty and Lowland football:** compare the two games' clubs, villages and language use.
- **Swansea and Gower:** beach and street play; compare Welsh- and English-speaking schools.
- **Shetland:** winter and indoor games; check island oral history.
- **Travelling showpeople:** fairground families as a community with their own guild and records.
- **Deaf children:** playground lore in BSL at Deaf schools against mainstream schools.
- **Vietnamese families:** separate refugee-era and later arrivals; check community associations' youth programs.
- **Brazilian communities:** futsal and capoeira groups; check by city and arrival period.
- **Congolese families:** church youth groups and football; check French- and Lingala-language community media.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's folklore (the Opies, Roud, the Bodleian recordings) and for screen play; mixed for adult play; thin for recent communities.
- **Languages needed:** Scots and regional English for playground terms; Scottish Gaelic for shinty; Welsh for school play; BSL for Deaf playground lore; Vietnamese, Portuguese, French and Lingala for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recordings of named children are restricted; use published or cleared material. Gambling as play belongs to the domain sheet.
- **Held for review:** playground lore and games from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; held aside per the standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. Map the Opies' truce-term and chasing-game names by county, then check Roud for later forms.
2. Read the Owen and Carew passages and list the rules each gives.
3. Ask the Bodleian what Opie recordings are open and search the British Library for the Childhoods and Playground Games project.
4. Pull Ofcom's latest children's media tables for game play by age and nation.
5. Search the National Fairground and Circus Archive catalog for oral histories with showpeople.
