# United Kingdom: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh (including Middle Welsh); Old and Middle English; Cantonese for one community below. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Anglo-Scottish Borders:** Liddesdale and Redesdale feud history; the Common Ridings of Hawick and Selkirk; check how riding towns choose their principals.
- **Highland clan country:** hospitality, loyalty and memory of betrayal; compare Glencoe's place in local and national memory.
- **London's City and East End:** the given word in finance and in street trading, as separate codes.
- **Garrison towns (Aldershot, Catterick, Colchester):** military families and regimental identity; separate officers and other ranks.
- **Welsh-speaking communities:** *enw da* in chapel life; check chapel discipline records for cases of lost good name.
- **Hong Kong arrivals and older British Chinese families:** check how face is spoken of in Cantonese community media, by generation.
- **Masonic and other fraternal lodges:** members against non-members in the same towns.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** a separate culture-nation, useful for contrasting oath-bound fraternal orders and parading.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gentry, military and literary honor; mixed for working-class and street codes; thin for family honor in private life.
- **Languages needed:** Middle Welsh for *Y Gododdin*; Scottish Gaelic and classical Gaelic orthography for the Book of the Dean of Lismore; Scots for Border ballads; Cantonese for community media.
