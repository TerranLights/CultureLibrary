# United Kingdom: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (post-war), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English and dialects; Scots; Scottish Gaelic; Welsh; Cornish; Old and Middle English. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Teesside's chemical towns** (Billingham, Wilton) and **Neath Port Talbot** steel: shift systems in single-employer towns; compare the work week before and after closures.
- **Kent's hop and fruit country:** East End families' "hopping" holidays, then postwar seasonal schemes, then Lithuanian, Romanian and Bulgarian pickers; separate the three periods and their recruitment routes.
- **Milton Keynes and the other new towns:** the warehouse and commuter economy; check against older market towns nearby.
- **Lewis and Harris weavers; Lerwick and Sullom Voe:** croft-house work against oil-terminal rotations.
- **Irish in Britain:** separate construction subcontracting, nursing and the postwar navvy camps by decade.
- **Vietnamese workers in small service trades and Hong Kong arrivals since 2021:** separate the visa route and year of arrival before reading any work data.
- **Romany Gypsy and Scottish Traveller families:** use census economic-activity tables for the "Gypsy or Irish Traveller" group, never outsiders' trade labels.
- **Cornwall:** tin mining's end and seasonal tourism as two work regimes to separate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; national labor statistics run back to the nineteenth century, and deindustrialization has its own oral-history literature. Thinner for informal and Traveller work.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for the slate and coalfield archives; Scots for the North-East farm material; Gaelic for weaving communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1984-85 miners' strike is a sensitive subject in former coalfield towns; benefit "scrounger" talk is charged. Statutes on hours, dismissal and benefits are Libra's material; what labor law is meant to secure is Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
