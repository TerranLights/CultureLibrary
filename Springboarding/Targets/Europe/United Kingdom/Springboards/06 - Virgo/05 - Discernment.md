# United Kingdom: Discernment (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom (post-war), region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, with Cockney, Geordie and Scots slang for shrewdness; Welsh (the eisteddfod's judging vocabulary); Scottish Gaelic; Middle English devotional prose. **Concept in one line:** the ability to tell things apart and judge well: true from false, good from bad, fine from coarse, the trustworthy from the untrustworthy, what matters from what doesn't.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Carmarthenshire and Ceredigion:** local eisteddfodau and chapel competitions as a school of judging; compare with Welsh-learner communities in the south-east.
- **Lewis and the Free Church Highlands:** "the Men" (*na Daoine*), lay elders noted for spiritual discernment at communion seasons (*check*); compare with Lowland Church of Scotland practice.
- **Cumbria's fair and show country:** separate Gypsy and Traveller horse-dealing criteria from settled farmers' show-ring criteria.
- **Lithuanian and Romanian seasonal workers:** separate recruitment routes and reported fraud cases; check Gangmasters and Labour Abuse Authority reports by sector and county.
- **Hong Kong arrivals and mainland Chinese students:** separate the language and platform of their news sources when reading misinformation studies.
- **Orthodox and Haredi Jewish communities:** discernment delegated to kashrut certifiers; separate the standards of the KLBD and Kedassia (*check*).
- **Older people on the Sussex and Lancashire coasts:** separate age, living alone and internet use in fraud data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for taste and class, spiritual discernment and fraud reporting; thin for everyday judging of people, which sits in conversation and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for adjudications (*beirniadaethau*); Gaelic for Mòd and Free Church material; Middle English for devotional texts; Cantonese and Mandarin for community-media studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accent and class prejudice; fraud victims' shame; Gypsy and Traveller representation at fairs, which draws hostile coverage.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
