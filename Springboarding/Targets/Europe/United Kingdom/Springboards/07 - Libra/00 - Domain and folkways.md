## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **England by region:** the far North (Cumbria, Northumberland), with its Border March laws and truce days; the Midlands; London; the South-West. Separate by period, as the records differ.
- **Scotland, Lowland and Highland:** Scots law is a separate legal system, and Highland kinship, clan and crofting rules sit beside it; compare the two before treating "Scotland" as one.
- **Wales, north and south:** Welsh-medium Gwynedd and Ceredigion against the industrial south-east; check where Welsh-language chapel marriage records survive.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** udal tenure and Norse-derived institutions; test whether partnership and inheritance records differ from mainland Scotland.
- **Cornwall:** its Stannary institutions and revival bodies keep separate records.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller; Jewish; British Caribbean; British Chinese; Somali; Polish communities:** separate each one's marriage forms, savings partnerships or dispute bodies (for example Beth Din arbitration), starting from its own organizations.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, a separate culture-nation whose marriage law and 1998 peace agreement offer a contrast; British communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: a long legal record, three jurisdictions, large surveys; thinner for Cornish, Gypsy and Traveller, and recent migrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** Old and Middle English and Latin for early law; Scots; Welsh (medieval and modern); Scottish Gaelic; community languages for the minority pointers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family-court records carry reporting restrictions; feud and sectarian rivalry; domestic-abuse data. The tracker carries the pre-war United Kingdom, and the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets write for Great Britain and its islands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
