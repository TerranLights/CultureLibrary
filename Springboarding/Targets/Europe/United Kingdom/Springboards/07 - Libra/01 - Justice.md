## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Scotland against England and Wales:** separate prosecution (the procurator fiscal), the fifteen-person jury, the third verdict and the children's hearings; compare written rules first.
- **Liverpool and Merseyside; South Yorkshire's mining towns:** Hillsborough and the Orgreave Truth and Justice Campaign; separate the campaigns' own records from press coverage.
- **British Caribbean communities:** New Cross, Lawrence and Windrush; check each campaign's own archive and its period.
- **Irish in Britain:** the 1970s convictions later quashed; separate court record, campaign and memoir.
- **Welsh-speaking west Wales:** Rebecca and *ceffyl pren* as recorded custom; compare Welsh-language court use since the 1942 and 1967 Acts.
- **Shetland and the Highlands:** early court books and estate justice; check survival by parish.
- **Gypsy and Traveller, Polish and Somali communities:** separate self-identified prison and victim data, interpreter provision and elders' mediation, each through its own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, inquiries and court history; thin for community-run justice (Somali elders, Traveller families).
- **Languages needed:** Scots legal vocabulary and its Latin; Welsh for court and chapel records; Polish and Somali for the community pointers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** live cases fall under contempt-of-court rules; inquiry evidence can be restricted; victims' and children's identities are protected by law.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
