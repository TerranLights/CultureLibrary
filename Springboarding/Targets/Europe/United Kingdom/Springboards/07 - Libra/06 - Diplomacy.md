# United Kingdom: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English (spoken hedges and Whitehall idiom), Old English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Latin and French for medieval treaties; Akan (Twi), Yoruba and Cantonese for go-between and interpreting pointers. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Border counties (Cumbria, Northumberland, the Scottish Borders):** March law and truce days; separate English and Scottish wardens' records.
- **Highland and island clans:** the 1609 statutes and the *rèiteach*; compare Hebridean and mainland practice.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** the pledge and later petitions; check islanders' own histories against Edinburgh's.
- **Wales:** the princes' treaties and later choices of Welsh or English in public talks; separate north and south.
- **Coventry and other bombed cities:** twinning; separate church, council and veterans' initiatives.
- **British Chinese families and Hong Kong arrivals:** children who interpret for parents; separate shop-owning families from newer arrivals.
- **Ghanaian and Nigerian associations:** chiefs' spokesmen and go-betweens; check each association's own rules.
- **Greek and Turkish Cypriot Londoners:** contacts across the island's divide; separate generation and borough.
- **Trade unions and employers:** ACAS case types by industry and decade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on state diplomacy and labor conciliation; mixed on everyday tact, where corpus data must stand in for sweeping claims.
- **Languages needed:** Latin, Anglo-Norman French and Scots for medieval treaties; Middle Welsh for Llywelyn's letters; Twi and Yoruba for go-between customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent Foreign Office files are closed for years; stereotypes about British indirectness are common in business guides and must not be taken as data.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

1. Search the spoken British National Corpus for "with respect" and "I'm afraid" by speaker region.
