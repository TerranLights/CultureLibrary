# United Kingdom: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English (pub, welfare and neighborly idiom), Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Scots, Old and Middle English; Somali, Cantonese, Irish and Twi for community exchange forms. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **South and west Wales:** Mari Lwyd and bidding weddings; separate Glamorgan and Carmarthenshire, and date when each lapsed or revived.
- **Scotland:** first-footing and guising; compare Lowland towns and the Gaelic islands.
- **Somerset and Gloucestershire orchard country:** wassailing; separate surviving custom from revival groups.
- **Lancashire mill towns and East London:** rounds and neighbor help; separate period and the effect of slum clearance.
- **British Caribbean communities:** pardner and church collections; separate island of origin and generation.
- **Somali communities (Bristol, Cardiff, Sheffield, London):** *ayuuto* and remittances home; check community-run sources.
- **British Chinese and Hong Kong arrivals:** red packets and association help; separate older and newer arrivals.
- **Irish in Britain:** county associations, wakes and work parties; separate construction-worker networks from settled families.
- **Jewish communities:** communal welfare bodies; separate Sephardi, Ashkenazi and Haredi institutions.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** shared work on crofts and boats; check island oral archives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on mutual-aid institutions, giving surveys and folk custom; thin on everyday favor-keeping, which needs diaries and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Middle English (north-west Midlands) for *Gawain*; Welsh for bidding letters; Gaelic for island custom; Somali, Cantonese and Twi for savings clubs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** welfare rhetoric ("something for nothing") is politically charged; informal savings clubs can be private and unregulated; remittance data on individuals is protected.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask St Fagans for its bidding-letter collection and any lists of *pwython*.
2. Read *The Pub and the People* on rounds and treating; note the rules stated.

4. Search the Black Cultural Archives catalog for "pardner" together with "banker" and "hand", to find accounts of who held the pool and how turns were owed.
5. Read the Beveridge Report on contribution, then later debates on "something for nothing".
