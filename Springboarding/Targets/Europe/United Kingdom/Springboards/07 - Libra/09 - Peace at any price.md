# United Kingdom: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English (proverbs of quiet and wartime idiom), Old English and Old Norse loans, Scots, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Cornish; Polish and Yiddish for two community idioms. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice; with pacifism as its principled, separate neighbor.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wales:** chapel pacifism, objectors and the 1923-1924 petition; separate north and south, and chapel denominations.
- **Quakers and other peace churches:** principle against appeasement; check local meetings' minute books.
- **Northern English minster towns (Beverley, Hexham):** sanctuary custom; compare with Durham's own sanctuary records.
- **Scotland against England and Wales:** separate abuse inquiries and their terms of reference.
- **Jewish communities:** debates over reporting abuse to outside authorities; separate Haredi, Orthodox and Reform bodies and periods.
- **Polish communities:** idioms of quiet and patterns of reporting; separate pre-2004 and post-2004 arrivals.
- **Kurdish, Turkish and Somali communities:** elders' settlements against police and courts; check community organizations' own case notes.
- **Gypsy and Traveller communities:** domestic-abuse reporting; use community-run services' data, not outside assumptions.
- **NHS and other public-service staff:** speaking up; compare trusts and nations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed. Rich for appeasement history, pacifism and recent inquiries; thin on private family silence, which surveys only partly reach.
- **Languages needed:** Old English and Old Norse for frith and tribute; Welsh for chapel and petition sources; Polish and Yiddish for community idiom.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse inquiries hold survivors' testimony; some inquiry evidence is redacted; nondisclosure agreements keep facts hidden by design.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
