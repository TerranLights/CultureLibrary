## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Orkney and Shetland:** udal holding against Scottish feudal tenure; check each source's period.
- **Highland crofting townships against Lowland farms:** separate common grazings and croft succession from the single-tenant farm.
- **Welsh-speaking north-west (Gwynedd, Anglesey) against the south-east valleys:** chapel funeral custom and family land against industrial mutual aid.
- **County Durham and Northumberland pit villages:** miners' welfare and burial clubs; check before and after the pit closures.
- **Cornwall:** separate west from east, and mining districts from farming parishes.
- **British Caribbean communities:** pardner pools and the nine-night; separate first arrivals from later generations.
- **Irish in Britain:** wakes and parish burial societies in Liverpool, Glasgow and London; check each community's county of origin.
- **Northern Ireland (separate culture-nation, comparison only):** its wake custom and public mourning set off what is particular to Great Britain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for death ways, wills and sex surveys; mixed for secrets and the commons (scattered local archives); thin for couples' money outside survey items.
- **Languages needed:** Scots, Gaelic and Welsh for the oral archives; Old English, Old Norse and Middle English for the older strata; Jamaican Creole for pardner and nine-night material.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent death records are closed; Natsal microdata need a user agreement; inheritance law goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for England, Scotland, Wales and the British islands.
