*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English (standard and West Country dialect), Scots, Scottish Gaelic and Welsh; Old English for the feud vocabulary of the heroic poems; Latin for Roman Britain's curse tablets and the Scottish royal motto. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore honor or standing, or deter the next wrong; forgiveness and payment for a wrong are its alternatives.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Anglo-Scottish Border (Liddesdale, Eskdale, north Cumbria, Northumberland):** feud memory in ballad and museum against March records; check period and side.
- **Skye and the Small Isles:** clan-feud traditions (the Eigg cave story among them) against documentary evidence; record each as tradition or record.
- **Carmarthenshire and west Wales:** the *ceffyl pren* in the 1830s-40s press against later memory.
- **Dorset, Wiltshire and the Salisbury Plain villages:** skimmington rides in quarter-sessions papers and newspapers; check dates.
- **Glasgow, Paisley and Renfrewshire:** football rivalry as a source variable for retaliation; check supporters' organizations' own histories and the decade.
- **London's East End gangland memoir genre:** set memoirs against trial reports; never treat the genre as a district's character.
- **Elders' mediation bodies in Somali and Yemeni communities (Cardiff, Sheffield, South Shields):** how disputes are said to be settled; check each organization's own account.

## 4. Flags
