## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lancashire (Pendle) and Essex against Scottish parishes: separate witchcraft belief from the legal machinery that recorded it.
- Cornwall: mine and sea beliefs, by mining and fishing parish.
- Orkney and Shetland: Norse-derived spirits against mainland Scottish fairy lore, by island.
- The Gaelic Hebrides and Welsh-speaking Wales: second sight and fairy belief, comparing collectors' period with present recordings.
- The non-religious: convinced humanists against people who simply tick "no religion", by age.
- British Chinese: Qingming grave visits and household shrines alongside church membership, by generation.
- Latter-day Saints (first British converts at Preston, 1837), Jehovah's Witnesses and Bahá'ís: what each publishes as its beliefs, and how members describe them.
- Gypsy and Traveller families: purity customs and Pentecostal belief, separated by community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in historical folklore and survey items on God; mixed on present-day folk belief, often captured only in commercial polls.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh and Gaelic for recorded belief narratives; Scots and Latin for trial records; Polish and Cantonese for community lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** safeguarding cases tied to beliefs in spirit possession (handle without naming any community); witchcraft trial victims' descendants. Witchcraft statutes are law: Libra (letter).
