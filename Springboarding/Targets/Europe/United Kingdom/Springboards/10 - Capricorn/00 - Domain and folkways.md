## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **England by region:** separate the North-East (Durham's palatine legacy), the North-West, the Midlands, London and the South-East, and the South-West; check regional attitude gaps with class and age held constant.
- **Scotland Highland and Lowland:** separate the clan system and its end after 1746 from Lowland burgh and kirk-session authority; compare by period, not as one Scotland.
- **Wales north and south:** Welsh-speaking Gwynedd against the industrial Valleys; check chapel, union and gentry as rival sources of standing.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** Norse udal tenure and later Scottish lairdship; separate by island group and century.
- **Cornwall:** the Stannary tradition and the Duchy; check what is distinct from the wider South-West.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities (Romany Gypsies, Irish and Scottish Travellers):** separate the groups and check their own published accounts of family and elder authority, against official reports.
- **Jewish communities, British Caribbean, British Chinese, Irish in Britain, Polish parishes:** check each community's own bodies and generation of arrival.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland (scouted separately) where rival authorities are a sharper case; British communities abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for England in English; mixed for Scotland and Wales (good surveys, thinner ethnography); thin for Cornish, for Shetland and Orkney, and for most minorities as they describe themselves. The nation's exact post-war lands are still to be settled.
- **Languages needed:** for the domain as a whole, English and Scots for the record of rank and office; Gaelic and Welsh for chiefship, gentry and chapel; Old and Middle English for the three orders and "degree".
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Clearances memory is contested; coverage of Traveller communities is often hostile, so official sources need balance.
- **Held for review:** domain-level material on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
