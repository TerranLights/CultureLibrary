# United Kingdom: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh; Old and Middle English for early texts. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Church polity:** Church of England bishops, Church of Scotland presbyteries and the Free Church in Lewis and Harris; separate the formal source of authority in each before comparing attitudes.
- **Armed forces:** Highland regiments, the Welsh Guards and garrison towns such as Aldershot; check service records and regimental histories by era, not a single "military" view.
- **Police:** the Metropolitan Police against Police Scotland (merged 2013); compare public-confidence series measured on the same questions.
- **Scotland and Wales on parental authority:** both removed the "reasonable punishment" defense before England; check what changed in surveys either side of the dates.
- **Hong Kong arrivals since 2021:** separate British National (Overseas) holders by year of arrival and check their own associations' statements on state authority.
- **Turkish and Kurdish community centers in London:** check each center's own governance and leadership, kept separate from one another.
- **Somali elders:** check community organizations' own accounts of elder authority beside youth-work reports.
- **Western Isles:** Gaelic-speaking, Sabbatarian parishes; check church and council records for island-specific patterns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on trust and attitude series; rich on canonical political thought; thinner on authority inside families and minority communities as told by themselves.
- **Languages needed:** English for the surveys and treatises; Scots and Latin for Buchanan and the Scottish resistance tradition; Gaelic for the Lordship of the Isles and island church life; Welsh for chapel and community authority.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inquiries into abuse by people in authority involve survivors' testimony; use published reports first. Inquiry material on named individuals may be legally restricted.
- **Held for review:** authority within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Hansard Society: download the *Audit of Political Engagement* volumes; tabulate the "strong leader" and trust items by year and nation.
2. Ipsos: pull the Veracity Index series for doctors, judges, police and government ministers.
3. Library: Filmer and Locke together, then Buchanan and Rutherford, noting where each places the limit of obedience.
4. UK Data Service: search "Millennium Cohort Study" plus "discipline" and "smacking"; compare Scotland and Wales with England.
5. Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry: read the published interim findings on how computer evidence was treated as authoritative.
