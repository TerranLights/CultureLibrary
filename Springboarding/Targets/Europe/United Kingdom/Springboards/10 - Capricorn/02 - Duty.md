# United Kingdom: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Naval and garrison towns (Portsmouth, Rosyth, Faslane, Aldershot):** check service families' own surveys and local histories for how duty is distributed across a household.
- **Quaker and other objector traditions:** separate religious refusal from political refusal using the Pearce Register's recorded grounds.
- **Welsh-speaking chapel communities:** check denominational records for duties of membership and Sunday school.
- **Hebridean crofting townships:** compare common-grazing duties across Lewis, Skye and Uist rather than as one Highland whole.
- **Jewish communities:** the *chevra kadisha* (burial society); check community records for duties to the dead and how they are organized.
- **Ghanaian and Nigerian associations:** check hometown associations' own records of funeral contributions and remittances.
- **British Caribbean:** check Windrush-generation oral histories for wartime service and for obligations to kin abroad.
- **Polish community:** separate wartime veterans' families from post-2004 arrivals; parish and veterans' club records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war, remembrance and care statistics; mixed for everyday family obligation; thin for minority communities' duties as stated by themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Old English and early Welsh for heroic duty; Gaelic for crofting and funeral duty; English dialect sources for lyke-wake and remembrance custom.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war-dead records include named individuals and living descendants; carers' testimony is personal; children's data under safeguarding rules.
- **Held for review:** obligations to kin within South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. ONS and National Records of Scotland: unpaid-care tables by age and local authority; set England, Wales and Scotland side by side.
