## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Selective against comprehensive areas:** Kent and Buckinghamshire against neighboring counties; check aspiration items in cohort data by school type.
- **Lowland and Highland Scotland:** test the "lad o' pairts" separately for parish-school Lowlands and Gaelic-speaking districts.
- **Welsh-speaking north-west and the southern valleys:** check teaching and the ministry as routes up, from chapel and county-school records.
- **Northern industrial towns against London:** separate Yorkshire, Lancashire and Tyneside cohorts; check out-migration of graduates.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** check young people's leaving and returning, from island council and college records.
- **Nigerian, Ghanaian and other West African families:** separate first and second generation; check community-led studies, not press summaries.
- **Hong Kong arrivals and longer-settled British Chinese:** separate by year of arrival and by origin before comparing plans for children.
- **Polish and Lithuanian post-2004 arrivals:** check settled-status and labor-survey data for occupational moves over time.
- **Gypsy and Traveller families:** check community accounts of self-employment and trade as their own measures of getting on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on mobility and attainment statistics; rich in literature; mixed on what people themselves aim for; thin on island and Gaelic-speaking communities.
- **Languages needed:** English and Scots for the "lad o' pairts" and "kent his faither" tradition; Welsh for chapel and county-school ladders; Old English for heroic fame; West African and Cantonese terms only via community sources, marked *check*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ambition is easily turned into a group stereotype; any comparison by ethnicity needs class, region and generation held apart.
- **Held for review:** aspiration research on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
