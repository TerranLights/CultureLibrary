## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Scotland against England and Wales:** separate legal cultures (Scots law, the fifteen-member jury, the Fiscal, the Hearings); never pool trust or attitude data across them.
- **Wales:** the medieval native law and today's emerging Welsh jurisdiction debate; check Senedd and Welsh legal-history sources apart from England's.
- **Cornwall:** the Stannary courts and the revived "Stannary Parliament" claims; check the history and the modern campaigners separately.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** udal tenure and island identity; check court cases where udal law was argued.
- **Showmen and Gypsy and Traveller communities:** separate the Showmen's Guild from Romany and Traveller groups; check each one's own rules and dealings with police.
- **Jewish communities:** check the Beth Din's role in arbitration and status matters, across Orthodox and other bodies.
- **Somali community elders:** check studies of customary dispute settlement (*xeer*) in British cities, as elders and researchers describe it.
- **Comparison only:** Northern Ireland, where non-jury "Diplock" courts and policing reform test what consent and the jury mean.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on legal history and trust surveys; mixed on everyday legal consciousness; thin on community and island legal orders as described from within.
- **Languages needed:** Scots legal vocabulary and Latin for Scottish sources; Middle Welsh for Cyfraith Hywel; Old English for the codes; Norse-derived terms for udal law; Hebrew for Beth Din usage; Somali for *xeer*, via community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious arbitration is politically contested; prisoners' and informants' accounts need care.
- **Held for review:** legal-culture and community-arbitration material on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
