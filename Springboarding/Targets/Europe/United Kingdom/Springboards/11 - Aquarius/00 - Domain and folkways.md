## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **England's regions:** separate the industrial North and Midlands (co-operatives, chapels, working men's clubs) from the South East and the rural South-West using the panel surveys' region codes, not assumption.
- **Scottish Highlands and Islands against the Central Belt:** crofting townships against tenement and housing-scheme life in Glasgow and Dundee.
- **North-west Wales against the south-east valleys and Cardiff:** test whether Welsh-language use changes which associations people join (chapel, eisteddfod, rugby club).
- **Orkney and Shetland:** island institutions (community halls, regattas); check how far sources lean on Norse framing.
- **British Caribbean communities:** pardner savings hands and Black-majority churches as associational forms; separate by decade of arrival.
- **Irish in Britain:** county associations and parish social clubs; split pre-1960s migration from later waves.
- **Northern Ireland (a separate culture-nation, comparison only):** parallel associational worlds, as a contrast for how far one civil society reaches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations, volunteering and community studies, England most of all; mixed for Gaelic (strong sound archives, little survey data); thin for Cornish-language and Angloromani material on groups.
- **Languages needed:** English and Scots for local club and parish records; Welsh for chapel and eisteddfod sources; Gaelic for the sound archives; Old and Middle English for the guild and land-of-plenty material.
- **Scope:** the United Kingdom's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands: England, Scotland and Wales with their islands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugee case files name living people; sectarian associations in western Scotland are contested; keep community-study pseudonyms as published. The legal frame of charities and associations goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (their associational life, for the domain as a whole).
