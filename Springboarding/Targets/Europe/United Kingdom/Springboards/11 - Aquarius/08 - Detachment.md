# United Kingdom: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Cornish; Old English and Middle English for the solitary and anchoritic traditions; Latin for Bede. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Former boarders against state-school pupils:** treat schooling as a variable in survey and memoir data, and note the bias in therapy-based samples.
- **Haredi communities of Stamford Hill and Gateshead:** separation chosen for religious reasons; use the communities' own sources and do not read separation as coldness.
- **Romany Gypsy families in England and Welsh Kale families:** compare death and mourning customs, and check how current each is.
- **Small islands (Foula, Fair Isle, St Kilda's descendants):** distance from the mainland is one variable, emotional style another; keep them apart.
- **Vietnamese refugees of 1979 onward:** dispersal across Britain in small groups as imposed detachment from community; separate first and second generations.
- **NHS and emergency-service staff:** professional distance and burnout by service and grade.
- **Older people living alone in coastal towns against cities:** solitude by place, using the census.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the history of emotions and for solitude in religious tradition; good for living alone and political engagement; thin and stereotype-prone for "national reserve", which must be traced as a claim, not adopted.
- **Languages needed:** Old English for the elegies; Middle English for anchoritic texts; Latin for Bede; Welsh for *pwyll* and the Mabinogi; Gaelic for island speech on calm and solitude.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** therapy-based boarding-school accounts; Traveller death customs belong to families and should be learned from them; impartiality as a legal duty of broadcasters goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (their contemplative and mourning traditions, as a category).
