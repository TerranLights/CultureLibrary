## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gaelic Hebrides and the Lowlands:** separate Gaelic from Scots recordings, and Catholic Barra and South Uist from Presbyterian Lewis and Harris; denomination shaped what was recorded.
- **Welsh north and south:** Welsh-speaking Gwynedd and Anglesey against the industrial valleys and Cardiff; check language of record and chapel history.
- **Cornwall:** date each source; Methodist and mining lore differ from the later Cornish revival's framing.
- **Orkney and Shetland:** use island archives, not mainland summaries.
- **England's North and South-East:** compare measured well-being and suicide rates, not reputation.
- **Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities:** community-run accounts of death customs before outsider folklore.
- **British Caribbean:** separate Pentecostal and Black-majority church sources from colonial records that criminalized obeah.
- **Polish and Lithuanian Catholics (since 2004):** separate devotions brought from home from those formed here.
- **Northern Ireland (comparison only):** a separate culture-nation; NISRA's matching survey items give a neighboring baseline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (folklore, asylum archives, surveys); England, Wales and Scotland keep separate statistics, so match figures before comparing.
- **Languages needed:** English; Scottish Gaelic and Welsh for field recordings; Scots; Cornish for revival writing; Early Modern and Middle English for pre-1700 sources; Polish for newer parishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patient records under long closure periods *check*; suicide reporting guidance (Samaritans *check*); obeah and witchcraft words carry colonial and criminal history; Travellers are mostly written about by outsiders.
- **Scope:** the United Kingdom's exact post-war lands are still to be settled by the author; these Pisces sheets are written for Great Britain's core lands (England, Scotland, Wales and the islands).
