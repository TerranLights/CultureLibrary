## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quakers (York, Birmingham, Bristol):** separate the Quaker business families' charity from Anglican parish charity; compare foundations' founding deeds.
- **Jewish communities (north and east London, Manchester, Leeds):** check the communal welfare archives against the Poor Law, to see who turned to which.
- **Highlands and islands:** famine-relief records and crofting townships; compare relief terms with Lowland parishes.
- **North East former pit villages:** miners' welfare halls and food-bank use; compare the strike years 1984-85 with today.
- **South Wales valleys:** chapel and union mutual aid; separate Welsh-language chapels from English-language ones.
- **Somali communities in Bristol and Cardiff (Butetown):** separate mosque, family and community-organization sources, and check each one's own reports.
- **Ukrainian arrivals since 2022 and their hosts:** separate hosts' accounts from arrivals' accounts of being helped.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; long charity and Poor Law records and current giving surveys. Regional giving figures are often survey estimates with small samples outside England.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh and Gaelic for chapel and church charity; Hebrew and Yiddish for older Jewish welfare papers; Somali and Ukrainian for recent arrivals' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relief and welfare records name poor families; "deserving" and "undeserving" remain charged words in benefit debates. Mercy in sentencing is law: send it to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** almsgiving and community-kitchen practice among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
