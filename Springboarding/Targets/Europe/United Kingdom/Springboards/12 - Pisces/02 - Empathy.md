## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Deaf BSL users:** Deaf-led sources on misreading between Deaf and hearing people; check Deaf clubs and the British Deaf Association *check*.
- **Autistic people:** their own organizations' accounts against clinical measures; separate self-report from test scores.
- **Windrush generation and British Caribbean families:** testimony to the Windrush review and oral histories, read beside officials' records.
- **Vietnamese refugees of 1979-82:** resettlement scattered families across Britain; compare oral histories of how hosts and newcomers read each other.
- **Irish in Britain:** oral histories of the 1970s-90s; check how suspicion and understanding were described by each side.
- **Welsh-speaking audiences:** compare *Pobol y Cwm* storylines and response with English-language serials.
- **Call-center workers in Glasgow, Tyneside and Wearside:** compare emotional labor by sector and employer, not region.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the philosophy and the measurement history; mixed for everyday empathy, which surveys rarely ask about directly.
- **Languages needed:** BSL (with Deaf interpreters or researchers), Welsh, Gaelic; Vietnamese for refugee testimony; eighteenth-century English prose.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** autism and empathy is contested ground; let autistic sources speak first. Windrush testimony is personal and recent.
- **Held for review:** perspective-taking studies centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
