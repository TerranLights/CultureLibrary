## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lewis and Harris Presbyterian congregations against London charismatic churches:** two forms of shared worship; compare by denomination and decade.
- **Manchester and northern club culture against rural free parties (Castlemorton Common, 1992):** separate licensed clubs from free festivals.
- **Welsh-speaking Gwynedd and the anglicized border:** language-shift figures by ward and age.
- **Gaelic Western Isles and Glasgow's Gaelic speakers:** urban and island language loss differ; compare census tables.
- **Cornwall:** revival speakers and the wider population; separate revival writing from census counts.
- **Steel and mill towns (Consett, the Lancashire cotton towns):** the closing of a town's main work; compare oral histories by plant.
- **Huguenot descendants (Spitalfields, Canterbury):** trace surname and church-register change over generations, not family legend.
- **Turkish and Kurdish Alevis (Hackney, Haringey, Enfield):** separate Alevi from Sunni Turkish and Kurdish practice.
- **St Kilda descendants:** family accounts against official and tourist versions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the language, divorce and dementia data are official, and the rave and monastic histories are well documented.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh, Gaelic and Cornish to read language loss in the speakers' own words; Scots; Middle English for the contemplative texts; Turkish, Kurmanji and Zazaki for the cem; French for Huguenot registers that show a community merging.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug use; dementia; the Clearances and Tryweryn remain politically charged; avoid "cult" framing of minority faiths. Drug and festival law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** histories of assimilation and language shift among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
