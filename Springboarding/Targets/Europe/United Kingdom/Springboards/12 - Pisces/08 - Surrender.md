# United Kingdom: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** United Kingdom, region Europe. **Language(s) for searching:** English, Scots, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic; seventeenth- and eighteenth-century English for confessional and Methodist texts; Twi and Yoruba for West African-heritage congregations; Anglo-Romani with community consent. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lewis and Harris:** revival conversions; compare congregations and decades rather than the island as a whole.
- **Cumbria and evangelical England:** Keswick attenders by home town and denomination.
- **Glasgow and west-central Scotland:** recovery communities against clinical services; compare peer-led and treatment-led language.
- **West African-heritage congregations (Peckham, Woolwich, Tottenham, Manchester):** separate Pentecostal and Aladura churches, and first from second generation.
- **Romany Gypsies and Irish Travellers:** death customs differ between groups; use each community's own accounts.
- **East Anglian and Liverpool families of Far East prisoners:** regimental areas lost at Singapore *check*; compare family memory by place.
- **Methodist Cornwall and Yorkshire:** Covenant Service practice by circuit.
- **North and South of England:** home and hospice deaths by deprivation decile, not region alone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dying and recovery (official profiles, hospice history), rich for wartime surrender; thin for private "letting go" outside these settings.
- **Languages needed:** Scots, Welsh and Gaelic for fate sayings and conversion accounts; Twi and Yoruba for prayer-meeting words of yielding; Anglo-Romani, with consent, for death customs; Early Modern English for the confessions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life choices are under live political debate; assisted dying law goes to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit). Prisoner-of-war testimony is traumatic; Traveller customs are community knowledge.
- **Held for review:** surrender-to-God and end-of-life practice among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
