## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gaelic Highlands and islands:** second-sight accounts; compare families and islands named in the record, not "the Highlands" as one.
- **East Anglia and North-East Scotland:** horsemen's societies; compare the Suffolk and Aberdeenshire material by date and informant.
- **Shetland and Cornish fishing communities:** sea omens and taboos; compare oral recordings in the Shetland Archives and Cornish collections.
- **South Wales valleys:** pre-disaster warnings at Aberfan and other tips; compare testimony and records.
- **Nurses in English and Scottish hospitals:** compare guidance and incident reviews by nation's health service.
- **Rural dowsers against water engineers:** compare practitioners' accounts with utilities' records.
- **Spiritualist mediums (comparison with the Spirituality sheet):** separate claimed foresight from everyday hunches.
- **Orkney:** compare Ernest Marwick's Orkney folklore collections (Orkney Library and Archive *check*) with the Shetland material, island by island.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for second sight and craft knowledge in older sources; mixed for present-day gut decisions, which surveys rarely ask about.
- **Languages needed:** Gaelic and Scots for second-sight testimony; Welsh for omen lore; Middle English (Kentish) for *inwit*; seventeenth-century English for Kirk and Martin; Shetland dialect for sea omens.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hunches about people slide into prejudice; police "suspicion" and stop-and-search belong to Libra and Capricorn, and to the Crime sheet. Some horsemen's lore was sworn secret; use published accounts.
- **Held for review:** foresight and omen traditions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
