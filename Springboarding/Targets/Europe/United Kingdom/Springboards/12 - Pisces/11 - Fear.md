# United Kingdom: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lancashire and East Anglia:** both had witch-panics; compare the Pendle and Hopkins records by village and accuser.
- **Lowland Scotland and the Highlands:** test from the Survey's maps whether accusations differ by region and presbytery, not from impressions.
- **Yorkshire, Tees and East Anglian dialect areas:** compare regional bogey and black-dog figures by collector and date.
- **Coastal Lincolnshire, Essex and Lancashire:** flood and tide memory; compare 1953 and Morecambe Bay testimony.
- **Shetland and Orkney:** water-horse, trow and selkie lore; compare island collections with Highland Gaelic material.
- **British Chinese:** Limehouse-era panic and 2004 Morecambe Bay losses; use community histories first.
- **Ukrainian arrivals since 2022:** separate war-related fear and trauma services from the wider population's worries.
- **Sheffield and other Cold War target cities:** compare civil-defense plans and local memory of *Threads*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; deep folklore and witch-trial data, strong film and press archives, good official safety items. Thin for private, unspoken fear.
- **Languages needed:** Old English for the earliest dread vocabulary; Scots, Gaelic and Welsh for supernatural threats; dialect dictionaries for regional bogeys; Ukrainian for arrivals' own accounts of fear.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** witch-trial victims and their descendants; disaster families (Morecambe Bay, 1953); press panics about minorities must not be repeated as fact. Law on witchcraft and civil defense belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** fear and disaster-memory pointers on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside under the standing rule.
