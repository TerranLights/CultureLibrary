# Araucanía-Patagonia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and Patagonia east and west of the Andes). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche of Ngulumapu and Puelmapu**: compare self-labels under two states' censuses and schools.
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche**: check whether territorial names come before "Mapuche" in introductions.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check how identity is reclaimed where the language is in revitalization.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: check how self-naming answers decades of being written off by outsiders.
- **Chilote families on the islands and in Argentine Patagonia**: compare Chilote identity at home and after migration east.
- **Welsh-descended families of the lower valley and of Trevelin and Esquel**: check whether the two settlements tell different origin stories.
- **German-descended, Croatian-descended and Boer-descended families**: check the generation at which the old-country label gives way.
- **Italian families of the Alto Valle, Basque and Lebanese families, Spanish settlers of Magallanes**: check which labels survive in clubs and surnames.
- **NyC and VyQ in the oil towns**: check how a newcomer becomes "from here".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Mapuche and Welsh identity; mixed for German and Croatian; thin for Boer-descended families and the Fuegian peoples, where the record is largely outsiders' and must be read against community voices.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for *Y Drafod*; German for colonist yearbooks; Croatian for club papers; Mapudungun for *ül* texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who counts as Indigenous is disputed in both countries; human remains and photographs from museum and exhibition history; lineage songs may be owned and not for publication.
- **Held for review:** the legal definitions of Indigenous status (Chile's Indigenous Law with its certificates; Argentina's community registry), set aside for Libra.
