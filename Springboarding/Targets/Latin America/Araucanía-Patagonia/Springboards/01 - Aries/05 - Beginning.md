# Araucanía-Patagonia: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and both Patagonias, Chilean and Argentine). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar for origin narratives. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche communities of Ngulumapu and Puelmapu**: compare how *We Tripantu* is kept on each side and whether it marks a personal new start as well.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: check whether a separate origin tradition and ancestor sites shape beginnings.
- **Aónikenk, Selk'nam and Yagán descendants**: check which origin narratives are being retold today and by whom.
- **Chilote families**: compare San Juan night and *minga* house-moves as ways of starting anew.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check whether Landing Day is kept differently in the lower valley and in the Andes.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue and Croatian-descended families of Magallanes**: compare arrival commemorations and what each treats as year one.
- **Chilean migrants and exiles in Argentine Patagonia**: check how they describe their first years.
- **Sheep-station families and oil-camp families**: compare the station year with the shift calendar as frames for beginning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for founding stories and Mapuche new-year observance; mixed for personal fresh starts; thin and mostly secondhand for Kawésqar origin narratives, where Aguilera's texts and Puerto Edén families are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun and Aonekko ʼaʼien for narratives; Welsh for settler diaries; German for Llanquihue arrival letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "first settler" claims clash with Indigenous presence; founding dates of Temuco, Ushuaia or Río Gallegos sit on the "Pacification of the Araucanía" and the "Conquest of the Desert"; tell each founding as an account.
- **Held for review:** birth-registration and newborn-naming rules of both civil registries, set aside for Libra.
