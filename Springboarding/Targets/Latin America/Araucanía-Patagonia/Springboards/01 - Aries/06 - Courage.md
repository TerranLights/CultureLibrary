# Araucanía-Patagonia: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and Patagonia, Pacific and Atlantic sides). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of southern Chile, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán and Selk'nam terms through the dictionaries. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Nagche, Wenteche, Pewenche and Williche**: check which ancestral fighters each territory honors and in what songs.
- **Mapuche women**: check whether women's endurance and leadership (Janequeo, *machi*) are named as courage or something else.
- **Chilote sailors, divers and fishers**: compare courage at sea with that of mainland herders.
- **Volunteer firefighters of German-descended companies in Valdivia and Puerto Montt**: check how company traditions define the brave act.
- **Welsh-descended families of Chubut**: check memoirs for courage framed through chapel faith.
- **Sheep-station hands and strike veterans of Santa Cruz**: compare memories of 1920-22 by side.
- **Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: check how endurance traditions are retold now.
- **Croatian-descended gold-rush families of Tierra del Fuego**: check family stories of hardship and nerve.
- **Mountain guides and rescuers of Bariloche and El Chaltén**: check how they separate courage from recklessness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on colonial warfare, firefighters and sea stories; mixed on moral courage; thin for Kawésqar and Boer-descended families, where oral history and church records are the route.
- **Languages needed:** 16th-17th-century Spanish for chronicles; Mapudungun for *ül* about fighters; Welsh for Eluned Morgan; German for fire-company histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the colonial wars, the "Pacification of the Araucanía" and the 1920-22 strikes are told in rival accounts; detention memoirs carry trauma; present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** current court cases involving Mapuche defendants, cast as courage by some media and as crime by others, set aside: they turn on law (Libra, Capricorn) and on live proceedings.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Vivar and Oña on Lautaro; list words used for courage and cowardice on each side.
2. Ask the Valdivia and Puerto Montt fire services for company histories and award citations.
3. Read Mallon's chapters on risk and endurance; note Mapudungun terms she records.
4. Search Chilean textbook archives for "Lautaro" and "Galvarino" by decade.
5. Request the Club Andino Bariloche rescue commission's annual reports.
