## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pewenche households of the *pewen* country**: compare winter stores of *ngülliw* (pine nuts) with market dependence; *check* the term.
- **Lafkenche and Williche coastal families**: check how shore gathering and fishing sit beside wage work.
- **Chilote island households and Chilote migrants in Argentina**: compare self-provisioning at home with wage dependence abroad.
- **Aónikenk, Kawésqar and Yagán descendants**: check how present-day food and housing projects frame self-reliance.
- **Aysén and Magallanes ranch families**: check isolation, stores and how help is asked for.
- **Welsh-descended farmers of the lower Chubut valley and Trevelin**: compare irrigation-cooperative farming with private holdings.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento and German-descended families of Llanquihue**: check how farm self-sufficiency is told across generations.
- **Oil and state workers of Comodoro Rivadavia, Neuquén and Río Grande**: check the pull between employer or state support and owing nothing to anyone.
- **Young adults in Temuco and Neuquén**: compare ages of moving out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in household surveys on both sides; mixed for food sovereignty; thin for Boer-descended and Croatian-descended families on this concept, where family memoirs and church registers are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for food and exchange words; Welsh for early colony letters; French for Emperaire; Afrikaans for Sarmiento family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** subsidy and welfare debates carry pejorative labels; state programs for Indigenous communities are debated against the history of land loss; record each community's own account.
- **Held for review:** pejorative words for welfare recipients appear above only as search terms and are never applied to any group, under the no-stereotypes rule; the industrial-promotion and wage-supplement laws themselves go to Libra.
