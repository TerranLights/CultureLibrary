## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche *lof* that still play *palin* and those that revived it**: check what a match decides between communities and how losers are treated.
- **Puelmapu and Ngulumapu Mapuche**: compare *palin* rules and tournaments across the two states.
- **Welsh-descended families of the lower valley and of the Andes**: compare eisteddfod entries and prize customs in Trelew, Gaiman and Trevelin.
- **Shearing crews and station owners**: check how contest speed links to pay and pride, and where crews come from (Chiloé, Chubut, Magallanes).
- **Rodeo families of Osorno and *jineteada* riders of Chubut**: compare a Chilean and an Argentine horse contest.
- **Climbers and guides of El Chaltén and Bariloche**: check how first-ascent claims are judged.
- **Croatian-descended, German-descended and Boer-descended families**: check club and school sport as rivalry among immigrant groups.
- **Rival towns** (Neuquén and Cipolletti, Trelew and Rawson, Temuco and Valdivia, Punta Arenas and Puerto Natales): check what each rivalry is about.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the eisteddfod, *palin*, rodeo and football; mixed for school competition by region; thin on Aónikenk, Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar contests, where Gusinde's and Chapman's notes on games and wrestling are the only route.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for adjudications and bardic poems; Mapudungun for *palin* speech and songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *palin* can carry ritual elements a community may not want filmed; climbing disputes involve living people's reputations.
- **Held for review:** derby chants and fan songs that insult a rival town's people, set aside under the no-stereotypes rule; they can be studied later only as sanctions, never as descriptions.
