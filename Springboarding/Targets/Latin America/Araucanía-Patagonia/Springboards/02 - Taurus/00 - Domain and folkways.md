# Araucanía-Patagonia: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; working frame: Wallmapu and Patagonia on both sides of the Andes. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh (Chubut); German (Llanquihue, Valdivia); Croatian (Magallanes); Afrikaans (Chubut); Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Gününa küne in revitalization. **Concept in one line:** possessions, money, resources, values and self-worth: what is worth having, and how is it held? Folkways: food ways, dress ways and wealth ways (building ways runner-up, home sign Cancer).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche households; Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: check what each counts as holdings (sea, *pewen* forest, cropland, herds).
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families of the steppe**: check how scarce sources record goods and food; say where it is thin.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: check which goods (canoes, baskets, shell necklaces) are kept, sold or reclaimed.
- **Chilote families**: compare island food and wool goods with what migrants carried east.
- **Welsh-descended families (lower valley and Trevelin)**: check the place of the tea table and chapel goods.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families (Llanquihue and Valdivia; Magallanes; Sarmiento and Comodoro)**: check which household goods came from the old country and stayed.
- **Lebanese, Spanish, Basque and Italian storekeepers; sheep-station hands and oil workers**: compare the storekeeper's ledger with a wage household's goods.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for spending surveys, farm censuses and Mapuche textiles; thin for the Fuegian and steppe peoples' own reckoning of value. The nation's lands are still to be settled by the author, so this sign's sheets work on the frame above; the Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas are comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and Mapudungun; colonial Spanish for the 1606 grammar; Welsh for household papers on goods and food.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** museum silver and textiles may be claimed by communities; *ngillatun* offerings and food are not for outsiders' cameras.
- **Held for review:** credit, inheritance and land-title statutes that the store ledgers and farm censuses keep bumping into belong to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), so none is listed here.
