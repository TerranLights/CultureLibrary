# Araucanía-Patagonia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and Patagonia, Chilean and Argentine sides). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of southern Chile, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche and Pewenche against Wenteche and Nagche households**: check which goods a fishing or *pewen* family keeps that a farm family does not.
- **Williche families of Osorno and Chiloé**: compare grave goods and keepsakes where Catholic and Williche customs meet.
- **Mapuche families of Neuquén and Río Negro**: check what a herding household carries between summer and winter grounds.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check which objects survive in families rather than in museums; sources are scarce, so look to community archives.
- **Yagán of Villa Ukika, Kawésqar of Puerto Edén, Selk'nam descendants in Tolhuin and Río Grande**: compare goods kept at home with goods held in museums.
- **Chilote families on the islands and migrants east**: check what went into the trunk for the trip to Argentine sheep stations.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and Trevelin**: check which heirlooms (Bibles, harmoniums, china) pass down and to whom.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue and Valdivia; Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas and Porvenir**: check what each kept from the crossing.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check whether wagons and tools survive in families; the record is thin.
- **Itinerant shearing crews and oil workers**: check what a man who moves for work owns and carries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in museum collections and Mapudungun grammar; mixed for family heirlooms (in private hands); thin for steppe peoples' goods outside museums.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and colonial Spanish for Havestadt; Welsh and German for donation letters and inventories; Mapudungun for grave-goods talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Fuegian objects and remains taken under coercion; *chemamüll* and graves; photographing objects of *machi*.
- **Held for review:** pawn and secondhand-trade rules came up beside the *Tía Rica* row and are left for Libra; nothing else was set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Smeets on possession, then check each form in Havestadt; list which nouns take possessives differently.
2. Ask Museo Martín Gusinde and Museo Borgatello for accession lists: who gave or took each object, and when.
3. Search DICREP's site for auction catalogs from southern branches.
4. Search Chilote press and video archives for "tiradura de casa"; note what is carried.
5. Ask the Trelew and Valdivia museums which donated household objects came with letters.
