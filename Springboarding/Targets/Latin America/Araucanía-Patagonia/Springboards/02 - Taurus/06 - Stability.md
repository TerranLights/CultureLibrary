# Araucanía-Patagonia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and Patagonia on both slopes of the Andes). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche families on old reserves**: check how long households have held one site.
- **Pewenche and Neuquén *crianceros***: compare stability kept through a yearly round with stability kept by staying.
- **Lafkenche of Lake Budi and Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison)**: check continuity after the 1960 tsunami.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: check which places and names stayed in families.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check where they settled after the 1880s; records are sparse.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán**: check how a canoe people's fixed places (camps, shell middens) are remembered.
- **Chilote families**: compare house-moving and seasonal migration with deep rootedness.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and Trevelin**: check who stayed after the flood.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue and Valdivia**: check how many generations stayed on one farm.
- **Croatian-descended families of Magallanes; Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check who stayed after the first generation.
- **Company-town families of Natales, Madryn, Río Turbio and Cerro Sombrero**: check stability given and taken by one employer.
- **Recent Chilean migrants in Neuquén and Comodoro**: compare their roots with those of the *NyC*.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: census mobility data are good; family continuity sits in local archives, oral memory and family papers; thin for steppe and canoe peoples outside outsiders' records.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and Mapudungun first; Welsh for flood-era letters; English for the Saskatchewan side.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** displacement after the 1880s campaigns, named as the 'Pacification of the Araucanía' and the 'Conquest of the Desert' in some sources and differently in others; land-conflict memory.
- **Held for review:** the 1902 boundary arbitration itself is not part of this sheet: the Trevelin row is kept only for how families remember staying, and no border is settled here.
