# Araucanía-Patagonia: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and Patagonia on the Pacific and Atlantic sides). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche farm families against Pewenche and Lafkenche**: check whether grain, herds, *pewen* or sea count as wealth in each.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare herd wealth with oil-town wages in the same families.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: check silver and land as wealth where lineages were broken.
- **Aónikenk descendants**: check how horse-and-silver wealth is remembered; most sources are travelers'.
- **Yagán, Kawésqar and Selk'nam descendants**: check what each counts as plenty today, and in their own words.
- **Chilote families**: compare remittances from Argentina and salmon wages as new wealth.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check wheat, shops and the Mercantile Company as wealth, and its fall.
- **German-descended families of Valdivia and Llanquihue**: check family firms (breweries, tanneries, farms) and how they passed down.
- **Croatian-descended families of Magallanes**: compare miners and merchants who became landowners.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento and Comodoro**: check how their holdings are remembered; little is in print.
- **Estancia owners and shearers; oil engineers and day workers in Neuquén and Comodoro**: compare who holds wealth and who sees it pass.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and estancia records and for industry data; mixed for household wealth (national samples); thin for how canoe and steppe peoples reckon plenty in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish; German for Gusinde; Croatian for merchant-family papers; Mapudungun for silver and feast words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Menéndez and Braun fortunes and the Selk'nam killings are contested history; silver pieces sold from families or graves.
- **Held for review:** two kinds of pointer were weighed and left out under the law rule: the royalty and fishing-quota statutes (to Libra) and the case law on estancia land (Libra and Capricorn).
