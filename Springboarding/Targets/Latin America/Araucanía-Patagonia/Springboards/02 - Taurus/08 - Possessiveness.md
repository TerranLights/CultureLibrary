# Araucanía-Patagonia: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (Wallmapu and the two Patagonias). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche families**: check how marriage payment and bride seizure are now told: as custom, theft or story.
- **Lafkenche and Williche fishers**: check how shellfish grounds and boats are guarded from outsiders.
- **Pewenche and Neuquén herders**: check guarding of *pewen* groves, summer grounds and animals.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check what is known of sharing and guarding customs; little is written.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán**: check how sharing a find (a whale, a catch) was told against keeping it; ask communities before using outsiders' accounts.
- **Chilote families**: compare island lore of claimed women with how families speak of courtship today.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check courtship rules and land quarrels in chapel and family papers.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check inheritance quarrels and the guarding of farms and claims.
- **Estancia owners and the public**: compare closed gates and lake access as each side tells it.
- **Mapuche communities and settler farmers in the Araucanía**: check how each describes guarding land and stock.
- **Young couples in Temuco, Neuquén and Punta Arenas**: compare how jealousy is spoken of across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: folklore and partner-violence data are rich; customary marriage practices are recorded mainly by outsiders; thin for canoe and steppe peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for marriage terms; Welsh for courtship letters; colonial Spanish for Quiroga.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence against women; captivity on the colonial frontier; stock theft tied in coverage to land conflict.
- **Held for review:** inheritance rules, the stock-theft offense and the partner-violence statutes were left out as law: Libra takes what they say, Capricorn how they are meant to work.
