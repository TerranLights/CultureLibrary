## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche communities**: check which speech variety elders use and whether greetings differ from coast to *pewen* country.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro against the Araucanía**: compare how two school systems carry Mapudungun.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check where language classes run and who leads them.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam families**: check who teaches, with which materials, where.
- **Chilote families**: check how boat schedules and chapel feasts set the rhythm of visiting.
- **Welsh-descended families (lower valley; Trevelin and Esquel)**: compare home speech with Welsh learned in class.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check the generation in which home speech turned to Spanish, and what school did.
- **Chilean migrants in Argentine Patagonia**: check which address forms they keep after crossing.
- **Oil-camp and sheep-station workers; Catholic, Evangelical and Welsh chapel communities**: compare learning on the job with learning by catechism and Bible class.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Mapudungun linguistics, Chilote speech and Welsh; thin for Gününa küne, Boer-descended families and Selk'nam speech. Which lands this nation takes in has not been settled by the author, so Gemini's sheets read talk and learning across the working frame above; the Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas serve only as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun across its three alphabets; 17th-c. Spanish for the 1621 sermon; Welsh for chapel minute books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** elders' recorded oratory may belong to families or communities; revitalization programs may control who publishes teaching materials.
- **Held for review:** laws on Indigenous-language rights and bilingual schooling surface in the census and school records; that statutory layer is left to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
