# Araucanía-Patagonia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America (frame: Wallmapu and the Patagonian lands either side of the cordillera). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (Chilean south, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun, *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; the Fuegian and steppe languages in revitalization. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche and Pewenche communities**: check which questions about sea, forest and birds are put by elders, and which are asked by visitors and scientists.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: check responses to scientists working near Lanín, Copahue and Vaca Muerta.
- **Aónikenk, Gününa küne, Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar descendants**: check what they now ask of museums and researchers, and which questions they decline.
- **Chilote families**: compare local knowledge of tides and myths with what tourists come to see.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check exploring as a colony tradition (the Rifleros' route) and local history societies.
- **German-descended families of Valdivia and Llanquihue**: check naturalist and school traditions after Philippi.
- **Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check whether memoirs record a curiosity about the land beyond work.
- **Bariloche and Trelew scientists and the towns around them**: compare a science community's ways of asking with neighbors'.
- **Oil-field and sheep-station workers**: check how fossil and artifact finds on the job are reported or kept quiet.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for explorers' and naturalists' narratives; mixed for school science and citizen science; thin for Indigenous peoples' own ways of asking, which reach print mainly through outsiders.
- **Languages needed:** 16th-c. Spanish for Sarmiento; German for Philippi's papers; Mapudungun for the eclipse statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains and grave goods in collections; sacred sites closed to visitors; questions some communities do not answer for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** heritage and excavation permits (who may dig, collect or export a fossil or artifact) appeared behind several museum pointers; the statute text belongs to Libra and its aims to Capricorn.
