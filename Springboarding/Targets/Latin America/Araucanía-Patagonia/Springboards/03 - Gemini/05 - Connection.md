# Araucanía-Patagonia: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Mapuche lands and Patagonia, joined across the Andes and the Strait. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the Chilean and Argentine south and of Chiloé; Mapudungun and Williche speech; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán, Selk'nam, Kawésqar, Aonekko ʼaʼien, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche families of Ngulumapu and Puelmapu**: check how kin ties are kept across the border, and which gatherings renew them.
- **Lafkenche and Williche fishing communities**: compare ties along the coast with ties to inland *lof*.
- **Aónikenk, Gününa küne, Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar descendants**: check how dispersed families keep contact, and through which bodies.
- **Chilote families at home and in Argentine Patagonia**: compare who sends, visits and returns.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and the Andes**: check links among the settlements and with Wales.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue and Valdivia**: check school, church and club ties that join families.
- **Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas and Porvenir**: check *kum* ties and their reach.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento and Comodoro Rivadavia**: check contact with South Africa after some families went back; sources may be scarce.
- **Lebanese, Spanish, Basque and Italian families**: check business ties among towns.
- **Isolated estancia workers and island elders**: check who checks on them, and how.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for transport and border data; moderate for Welsh and Croatian ties; thin for Fuegian and steppe families' networks and for loneliness by region.
- **Languages needed:** Croatian for chain-migration letters; Welsh for society minutes; 18th-c. Spanish for Menéndez.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families split by the frontier wars and by removals; contact data on small Indigenous communities.
- **Held for review:** border and migration rules, and the 2009 Maipú treaty between Chile and Argentina, kept appearing behind the crossing counts; how the treaty's text reads is Libra's, what it is for is Capricorn's.

## 5. First queries
1. Request monthly crossing counts by pass from Chile's PDI and Argentina's Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.
2. Read Menéndez's diaries, noting each host and each introduction.
3. Search catalogs for "compadrazgo Chiloé" and "compadrazgo mapuche".
4. Contact Cymdeithas Cymru-Ariannin for visit and exchange records.
5. Check the *Encuesta de Bienestar Social* tables for southern regions.
