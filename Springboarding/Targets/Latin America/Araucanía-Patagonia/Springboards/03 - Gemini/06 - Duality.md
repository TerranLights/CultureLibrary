# Araucanía-Patagonia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; working frame: a land under two pre-war states, Wallmapu and Patagonia on both sides of the Andes. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (Chilean, Chilote, Argentine Patagonian); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche of Ngulumapu and Puelmapu**: check how being "from" one state is used by others in each country, and how people answer.
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche**: check whether paired ritual terms differ between territories.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check how two state histories describe them differently; record may be thin.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam families**: check lives split by the Chile-Argentina line in Tierra del Fuego and the Beagle Channel.
- **Chilote families in Argentina**: compare second-generation self-descriptions with their parents'.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and the Andes**: compare how Welsh-Argentine belonging is framed in each settlement.
- **German-descended families**: check how wartime suspicion is remembered in Valdivia, Osorno and Llanquihue.
- **Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check which old-country label is used inside the family and which outside.
- **Aysén and Magallanes ranch families**: check Argentine-style customs on the Chilean side, and what locals call them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for German and Welsh two-country history and for Mapuche identity debates; thin for Boer and Fuegian families' own words on being two things at once.
- **Languages needed:** German for wartime community papers; Welsh for 1902 settler testimony; Croatian for family letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *araucanización* debate and wartime German-Chilean history are charged; avoid stating either side's view as fact.
- **Held for review:** dual-nationality provisions in both constitutions and the 1902 award's legal text were met while scouting; Libra reads them as written, Capricorn asks what they were meant to do.
