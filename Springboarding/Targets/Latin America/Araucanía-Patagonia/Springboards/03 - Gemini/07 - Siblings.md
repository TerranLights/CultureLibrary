# Araucanía-Patagonia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu, Chiloé and the Patagonian provinces and regions of both pre-war states. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the south (Chile, Chiloé, Argentina); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán, Kawésqar, Selk'nam, Aonekko ʼaʼien, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche families**: check whether the *peñi*/*lamngen* range and the eldest's duties vary by territory.
- **Mapuche of Puelmapu**: check sibling ties cut by the border or by moves to Neuquén and Bariloche.
- **Yagán, Kawésqar and Selk'nam families**: check how mission years and estancia labor split sibling groups, and how families trace each other now.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check what family histories record about brothers and sisters; sources may be few.
- **Chilote families**: compare sisters who stayed with brothers who went east for work.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check large founding families against present family size.
- **German-descended farm families**: check single-heir succession and what it meant for younger siblings.
- **Croatian-, Lebanese-, Spanish- and Basque-descended families**: check sibling partnerships in shops and trade, from each family's own record.
- **Boer-descended families**: check sibling groups that stayed in Chubut against those who returned to South Africa.
- **Evangelical and Catholic communities**: compare kin words used for fellow believers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for census fertility; moderate for settler family histories; thin for Fuegian, steppe and Boer siblings' own accounts, and for twins beyond the numbers.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun kin terms in local variants; German for farm chronicles; Yagán words in the narrative collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** irregular adoptions and family separations; sibling land quarrels in small communities; stories some families keep private.
- **Held for review:** inheritance shares under each civil code, and the rules on dividing Indigenous land among heirs, sit under every land-and-siblings pointer here; Libra takes the written shares, Capricorn the sense of fairness behind them.
