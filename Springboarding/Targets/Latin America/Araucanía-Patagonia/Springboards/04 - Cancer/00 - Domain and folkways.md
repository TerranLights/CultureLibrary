# Araucanía-Patagonia: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; working frame: Wallmapu and Patagonia on both sides of the Andes, with Chiloé and Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of southern Chile, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun (Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche speech) and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Gününa küne in revitalization. **Concept in one line:** home and family: what is home, and who is family? Folkways: building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways (child-rearing runner-up Leo; gender ways runner-up, home sign Libra).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche households**: check house forms and who shares one plot, coast to *pewen* highlands.
- **Mapuche families of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare how two states' censuses record like households.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check family memory of the *toldo* household.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam families**: check how forced moves reshaped households, in each community's telling.
- **Chilote families**: compare households where men worked away on Argentine stations with those who stayed.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare lower-valley farmhouses with Trevelin and Esquel homesteads.
- **German- and Croatian-descended families**: check which generation left the settler house for town.
- **Oil-camp and sheep-station workers**: compare bunkhouses with family homes.
- **Boer-, Spanish-, Italian-, Basque- and Lebanese-descended families; Chilean migrants in Argentine Patagonia**: check how each kept kin close across distance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for census households, Welsh papers and Mapuche ethnography; thin for Gününa küne, Kawésqar and Boer households. The author has yet to fix this nation's lands, so Cancer's sheets read home and family across the working frame; the Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas serve as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun in three alphabets; 19th-c. Spanish and French for Gay and Domeyko; Croatian (Dalmatian forms) for family letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lineage accounts and *küga* names may be family property; household records of Selk'nam and Kawésqar families touch removals and mission years.
- **Held for review:** the civil codes' definitions of family and the housing-subsidy rules sit behind the census and dwelling rows; what they say is Libra's, what they aim at is Capricorn's.
