# Araucanía-Patagonia: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu, the lakes and Chiloé, Aysén and Magallanes, and the Argentine provinces from Neuquén to Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Patagonian Argentina); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán, Kawésqar, Selk'nam and Aonekko ʼaʼien kin words where communities share them. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche lineages of the Araucanía plains**: check whether descent through the father still names the community, and where it does not.
- **Mapuche *lof* in Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare lineage-named communities with those named after places.
- **Williche families of San Juan de la Costa and Chiloé**: check how land-title families and island families reckon kin.
- **Aónikenk families of Santa Cruz and Chubut**: check which family lines are documented and who keeps that record.
- **Yagán, Kawésqar and Selk'nam descendants**: check how genealogy is used now to rebuild family ties after dispersal.
- **Chilote households**: compare godparent ties with blood ties in deciding who helps whom.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check how marriage outside the chapel community changed who counts as *teulu*.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: compare *Verwandtschaft*, *rodbina* and *familie* as each family uses the word.
- **Lebanese, Spanish, Italian and Basque settler families**: check how far kin networks reached back across the ocean.
- **Evangelical and Catholic congregations**: compare the church as a second family with blood kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for parish and civil registers and for Welsh family papers; mixed for Mapuche lineage; thin and scattered for Aónikenk, Kawésqar and Boer families.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish and Mapudungun for Febrés; Welsh for *Y Drafod*; German *Kurrent* script for early colonist papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies decide who belongs to a community and who may claim land, so families may withhold them; public claims about Selk'nam descent are debated, and each account stays an account.
- **Held for review:** the kind of pointer weighed and dropped here was the registry rule on who may be listed as a relative (civil-status and Indigenous-status registration), which governs the written record of family; that wording is Libra's.
