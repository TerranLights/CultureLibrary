# Araucanía-Patagonia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Mapuche lands of both slopes and the south down to the Beagle Channel, oil towns and sheep country included. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish as spoken from Temuco to Ushuaia; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Aonekko ʼaʼien through their communities. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche and Wenteche *lof***: check how each decides whether a relative raised in a city belongs on return.
- **Mapuche of Puelmapu**: compare belonging measured by community registration with belonging measured by family.
- **Williche and Chilote islanders**: check whether "Chilote" and "Williche" overlap in self-naming, and where they part.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check how a scattered people gathers members again, from whatever sources exist.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam families**: compare membership in a recognized community with belonging by descent.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and the Andes**: check whether speaking Welsh, chapel attendance or descent decides who is "Welsh".
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check what a newcomer married into the group had to do.
- **Chilean migrants in Neuquén, Bariloche and Comodoro**: check how *VyQ* applies to them against migrants from northern Argentina.
- **Oil workers in Añelo and Comodoro; sheep-station hands**: compare short-contract arrivals with old local families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for regional press and school surveys; thin for Fuegian and steppe peoples' own membership rules, which may not be in print at all.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-c. English for Byron; Welsh for the 1865 letters; Mapudungun for assembly records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who belongs to a community is often decided in closed assemblies; the word *winka* and the *NyC* label can wound, so record them only as usage.
- **Held for review:** a source that profiles a group was the kind weighed here: local jokes about newcomers from particular provinces, which would make a group's character the pointer, so they were left out under the no-stereotypes rule.
