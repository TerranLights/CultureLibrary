# Araucanía-Patagonia: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Araucanía and the lakes, Chiloé, Aysén and Magallanes; Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz and Fuegian Argentina. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the south (both countries and Chiloé); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Yagán and Kawésqar as communities use them. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Williche of the Osorno coast and Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison) and Budi**: check how guarding a sacred place and guarding a household are spoken of in each.
- **Pewenche families of the Andean *pewen* country**: check which places are guarded by *ngen* and who keeps the rules near them.
- **Mapuche communities of Neuquén near the oil fields**: compare protection claims made to the province with those made in community gatherings.
- **Yagán and Kawésqar families**: check who speaks for guarding the channels, and in which forum.
- **Aónikenk descendants**: check whether any recorded account speaks of guardian beings or places; sources are few.
- **Chilote fishing families**: compare saints' feasts with practical boat safety.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check how frontier memories of danger are told now, beside Mapuche and Tehuelche accounts of the same years.
- **German-descended families of Valdivia and Llanquihue**: check how fire companies tied families into a duty of guarding the town.
- **Croatian-descended families of Magallanes; sheep-station workers**: compare how isolated households guarded against winter and work accidents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Chilean child-protection reporting and disaster drills; contested and polarized for land defense; thin for Fuegian and steppe protective beliefs.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun and *tsesungun* for *ngen* narratives; Welsh and Spanish for Evans's memoir and its translations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ngillatun* and sacred sites may be closed to outsiders; residential child-care abuse reports name victims and cases; the Araucanía dispute is live.
- **Held for review:** the protective-order statutes and the children's-rights codes of both states lie under the child-protection rows; this sheet names none, leaving their wording to Libra and their purpose to Capricorn.
