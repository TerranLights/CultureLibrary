# Araucanía-Patagonia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: from the Araucanía's forests and the Neuquén cordillera through the wind-swept steppe and fjords to the Fuegian channels. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of Chile's south, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Aonekko ʼaʼien for dwelling words. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pewenche families on *veranada* and *invernada* grounds**: check how summer shelters differ from winter houses and who builds each.
- **Lafkenche coastal households**: compare shelter from sea and storm with inland *ruka* building.
- **Mapuche communities of the Argentine cordillera**: check how *Plan Calor*-type aid shapes winter shelter.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check whether *toldo* memory survives in families; written sources are almost all travelers'.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam families**: check how each describes the move from hut and canoe to state housing.
- **Chilote families**: compare houses built against rain with *mediaguas* after disasters.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and the Andes**: check how first shelters are told in family memory.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue**: compare forest huts of the first years with later houses.
- **Croatian-descended families and sheep-station hands**: check bunkhouse life against the family house in town.
- **People without shelter in Temuco, Osorno, Comodoro and Punta Arenas**: check who they are by the counts, not by assumption.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Chilean homeless counts and disaster coverage; thin for Argentine Patagonian homelessness and for Fuegian and steppe dwellings in their peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for Coña's text; German or Spanish for Claraz; Welsh for the founding writings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homeless counts can identify people in small towns; human remains and grave goods at shelter sites belong to the peoples concerned.
- **Held for review:** private papers came up: logbooks and letters kept by mountain-hut families and Welsh descendants may hold family matters; they stay listed only as types until owners agree.
