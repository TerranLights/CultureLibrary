# Araucanía-Patagonia: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Mapuche country west and east of the Andes, the island and fjord south of Chile, and Argentine Patagonia from the Alto Valle to Ushuaia. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (Chilean south, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Yagán, Kawésqar and Aonekko ʼaʼien where elders' care is recorded. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche families of the Araucanía valleys**: check how often *lawen* care and the clinic are used together, and who decides.
- **Pewenche households in the high cordillera**: check how distance and winter snow change who cares for the sick.
- **Mapuche families of Neuquén**: compare home visits by *agentes sanitarios* with care by kin.
- **Williche and Chilote islanders**: check how *meicas* and *componedores* are chosen, and whether men or women give most care.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: check family memory of healers and nursing; written sources are scarce.
- **Yagán and Kawésqar families**: compare care of elders in town with what elders remember of camp life.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check whether chapel members visited and nursed the sick, and who carries that now.
- **German- and Croatian-descended families**: check when organized charity and care homes replaced family nursing.
- **Oil-rotation and sheep-station households**: check who cares for children and elders when wage earners are away for weeks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chilean disability and care surveys; moderate for intercultural health writing; thin for Fuegian and steppe care and for Argentine care-home data.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for Augusta and for herbalists' terms; Welsh for chapel records; German for *Frauenverein* minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' plant knowledge may be restricted; health records name patients; care-home inspections touch living people.
- **Held for review:** restricted knowledge was the issue weighed here: printed lists of *lawen* plants with their healing uses exist, but naming them could spread what healers keep to themselves, so the herbalist row stays a search type until communities say otherwise.
