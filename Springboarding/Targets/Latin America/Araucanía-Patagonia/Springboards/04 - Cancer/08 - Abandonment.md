# Araucanía-Patagonia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Araucanía and Los Ríos, the Los Lagos archipelago, Aysén and Magallanes, and Argentina's Patagonian provinces with Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Chilean, Chilote and Patagonian Argentine Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Aonekko ʼaʼien narrative; Welsh; Croatian; German. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche and Nagche families**: check whether *epew* of orphans are still told, and to whom.
- **Mapuche families on the Argentine side**: check how the splitting of families after the campaigns is remembered, against the state archives.
- **Williche families of San Juan de la Costa**: check whether accounts of children sent away to work or school speak of being left.
- **Aónikenk descendants**: compare the Elal story with family accounts of separation; sources are few.
- **Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar descendants**: check how removals to missions are named by families now: removal, not abandonment, unless they say otherwise.
- **Chilote families**: check what is said of men who went east and did not return.
- **Welsh- and Croatian-descended families**: compare letters from those who left with those of the families left.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue**: check the colony's records of widows and orphans in the first years.
- **Families of oil-rotation and sheep-station workers**: check whether long absences are spoken of as leaving.
- **Residents of emptied rail, mining and estancia towns**: check whether people describe the state or the company as having left them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for Chilean irregular-adoption cases and press; moderate for parish books; thin for steppe and Fuegian accounts and for elders left in Argentine hospitals.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for *epew*; colonial Spanish for parish books; Croatian for Dalmatian emigration studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** adoption cases involve living people and open inquiries; accounts of children taken after the campaigns are contested; feral-dog quarrels can name neighbors.
- **Held for review:** adoption and filiation statutes, and the court investigation into irregular adoptions, shadow the adoption row; this sheet keeps only the families' accounts and leaves the written law to Libra.
