# Araucanía-Patagonia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Mapuche lands on both slopes, the Chilean south with Chiloé and Magallanes, and Patagonian Argentina from the Alto Valle to the Beagle Channel. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the Chilean south, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán and Kawésqar through elders' and weavers' circles. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche mothers**: check whether a mother's say over children's marriages and names differs by territory.
- **Mapuche mothers in Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare births in provincial hospitals with births attended by midwives.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check what remains of mothers' stories; recorded accounts are few.
- **Yagán, Kawésqar and Selk'nam families**: check how grandmothers' standing is spoken of after the language and craft revival.
- **Chilote mothers**: check how women who headed households while men were away are remembered.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare chapel ideals of the mother with diaries and letters.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended mothers**: check which home language mothers kept longest.
- **Catholic and Evangelical congregations**: compare Marian devotion with Evangelical teaching on mothers.
- **Oil-town and estancia mothers**: check how rotation work and distance from hospitals shape birth and early mothering.
- **Chilean migrant mothers in Argentine Patagonia**: check which Mother's Day they keep.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for vital statistics and Marian feasts; mixed for Mapuche midwifery; thin for Fuegian and steppe mothers' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** 16th-c. Spanish verse for Ercilla; Mapudungun for midwives' terms; Welsh for colony diaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth customs and placenta burial can be private family matters; mothers of people killed or disappeared speak in public on their own terms only.
- **Held for review:** this sheet weighed the health ministry's written rule on returning the placenta and Argentina's law on respectful birth; the text of each goes to Libra, and what each is meant to protect to Capricorn.
