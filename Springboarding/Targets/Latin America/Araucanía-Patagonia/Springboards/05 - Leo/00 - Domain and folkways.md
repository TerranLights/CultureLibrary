## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche and Williche communities**: check which games, dances and feasts each still holds and who may watch.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare *kamarikun* (*camaruco*) gatherings with the Chilean *ngillatun* as occasions of display.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne families**: check what festival or song life is remembered, and through whom.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam communities**: check how ceremonies once recorded by outsiders are now performed or kept back.
- **Chilote families**: compare island festivals with Chilote migrants' feasts in Comodoro Rivadavia and Río Gallegos.
- **Oil and sheep-station workers**: compare camp pastimes with town ones.
- **Welsh-, German- and Croatian-descended families**: check club- and chapel-run leisure against town festivals, valley by valley.
- **Catholic, Evangelical and chapel congregations**: check where each draws the line on dancing, drinking and Sunday play.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for festivals, sport surveys and Welsh cultural life; thin for steppe and Fuegian play and for Boer-descended families. For Leo the author has not yet fixed the nation's lands, so play and display are read across the working frame; the Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas count as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for game and dance names; 18th-c. Spanish for Carvallo; Croatian for club bulletins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Hain* and *kina* knowledge may be held back by communities; *ngillatun* dances are not public shows.
- **Held for review:** licensing rules for casinos, lotteries and race betting sit behind the racing row; their wording is Libra's, their aims Capricorn's.
