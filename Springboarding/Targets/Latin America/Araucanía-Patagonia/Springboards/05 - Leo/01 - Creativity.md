# Araucanía-Patagonia: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu and Patagonia from the Araucanía and Neuquén south to both shores of the Beagle Channel. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; craft words in Kawésqar, Yagán and Aonekko ʼaʼien where makers share them. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche weavers and silversmiths of the Araucanía and of Neuquén**: check what design changes each side allows and who teaches.
- **Lafkenche and Pewenche makers**: compare wood, *pewen* and sea materials in what is crafted.
- **Williche makers of Osorno and Chiloé**: check where Mapuche and Chilote craft overlap.
- **Aónikenk families**: check what is known of painted hide cloaks and who keeps the designs.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán basket makers**: compare rush basketry made for use with work made for sale.
- **Selk'nam descendants**: check what crafts are being revived and who decides on designs.
- **Chilote families**: compare island boatbuilders and weavers with those who moved to Patagonian towns.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare valley and Andes poets competing for the same chair.
- **German-, Croatian- and Lebanese-descended families**: check which crafts or trades their workshops passed down.
- **Oil and sheep-station workers**: check what was invented or fixed on the job.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for literary groups, Welsh poetry and rock art; thin and scattered for everyday improvising and for Fuegian crafts today.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun craft vocabulary; Welsh *cynghanedd* terms for strict-meter poetry; Croatian for workshop histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** weaving designs and carved masks may carry family or ceremonial meaning; whether an outsider may reuse them is the makers' call.
- **Held for review:** restricted knowledge was weighed: printed keys that decode *ñimin* designs; *ñimin* stays only as a search term until weavers say how the meanings may be shared.
