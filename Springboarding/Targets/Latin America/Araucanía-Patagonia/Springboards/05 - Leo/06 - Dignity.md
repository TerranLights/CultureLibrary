# Araucanía-Patagonia: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu, the lakes and Chiloé, the Aysén and Magallanes south, and the Argentine provinces from Neuquén to Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche communities near Temuco**: check how the 1920s organizations are remembered there.
- **Pewenche families of the high valleys**: compare *küme mongen* talk with talk of rights in their statements.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: check how dignity is phrased in Argentine provincial disputes.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: compare burial customs and who may handle the dead.
- **Aónikenk, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: check what they say was taken from their ancestors' dignity, as each tells it.
- **Chilote and Mapuche shearers and meat-plant workers**: compare union records with family memory of the 1920s.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare chapel respectability in the lower valley with Trevelin.
- **German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: check how hardship in early settlement is told as dignity kept.
- **Chilean migrants and seasonal workers in the Alto Valle**: check how housing and pay are described from inside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for labor history and Mapuche political writing; thin for the dignity of the dead outside Mapuche ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for *küme mongen* and funeral terms; colonial Spanish for the just-war debate; Welsh for chapel records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** funeral rites and the handling of remains may be private; testimonies of poverty need consent to quote.
- **Held for review:** under dignity at life's end, Chilean bills on assisted dying and Argentina's dignified-death law (2012) came into view; their text is Libra's, their aim Capricorn's.
