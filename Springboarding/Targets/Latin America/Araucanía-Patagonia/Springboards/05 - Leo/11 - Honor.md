# Araucanía-Patagonia: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu, the Valdivia and Osorno country, Chiloé, Aysén and Magallanes, and the five Patagonian provinces of Argentina. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche communities**: check how *küme che* and good name are taught, and by whom.
- **Lafkenche and Pewenche families**: compare honoring of elders and the dead at *eluwün* and *ngillatun*.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: check how honor is spoken of in Spanish and in Mapudungun.
- **Aónikenk families**: check what is remembered of worth won by hunting and courage, and by whom it is told.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán families**: check how they honor the dead after mission-era removals.
- **Chilote families**: compare a man's word in boat sales with a woman's good name in parish talk.
- **Welsh-descended families**: check how a chapel member's good name was weighed when deacons heard a complaint, in Gaiman and in Trevelin.
- **German- and Croatian-descended families**: check honor in business credit and club membership.
- **Boer-descended families**: check how 1902 is remembered.
- **Sheep-station and oil workers**: compare the fair-fight code of the *boliche* with union discipline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for colonial texts and Freemasonry; thin for honor inside Fuegian and steppe communities and for Boer families.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-c. Spanish for Barrenechea; Welsh for chapel discipline; Croatian and Afrikaans for family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family-honor stories can expose living people; violence justified by honor must be told in survivors' words.
- **Held for review:** where honor turns to violence, police and court files on knife fights and on crimes excused by family honor were weighed; the record is Libra's, the code behind it Capricorn's, and no case is listed.
