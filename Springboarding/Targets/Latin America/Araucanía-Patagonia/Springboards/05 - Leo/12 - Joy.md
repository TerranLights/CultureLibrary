# Araucanía-Patagonia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: Wallmapu from the Araucanía and Neuquén south, the lakes and Chiloé, the fjords and steppe of Aysén and Magallanes, and Argentine Patagonia to Ushuaia. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Arabic terms in Lebanese-descended clubs. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche communities**: compare the *afafan* at *ngillatun* with joy at family gatherings.
- **Pewenche families**: check how the *piñón* harvest is celebrated at home and at the Aluminé festival.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Chubut**: compare *lonkomeo* at *kamarikun* with dances performed for tourists.
- **Aónikenk families**: check what is recorded of the feasting and dancing that travelers saw at a girl's coming of age, and what descendants say of it now.
- **Yagán and Kawésqar families**: check what is celebrated now in Puerto Williams and Puerto Edén.
- **Chilote families**: compare joy at *mingas* and church feasts with the *malón* in mainland towns.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare *noson lawen* and eisteddfod nights.
- **German-, Croatian- and Lebanese-descended families**: check how club celebrations open to the town.
- **Sheep-station and oil workers**: compare the end-of-shearing feast with oil-camp holidays.
- **Evangelical and Catholic congregations**: check where each allows dancing and drink.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for festivals and national surveys; thin for joy as Mapuche and Fuegian communities name it.
- **Languages needed:** 17th-c. Spanish and Mapudungun for Valdivia's grammar; Welsh for *noson lawen* programs; Arabic for club records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ngillatun* dances and shouts are prayer, not shows; ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** joy's edge at festivals is drink: alcohol-licensing rules and closing hours (law) and figures that would portray a people through drinking (a profile) were both weighed, and neither is listed.
