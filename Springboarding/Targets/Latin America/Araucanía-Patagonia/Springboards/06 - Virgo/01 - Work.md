# Araucanía-Patagonia: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Mapuche lands on both slopes of the Andes, the lakes and Chiloé, Aysén, Magallanes and Fuegia, and the Argentine steppe and oil provinces. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of southern Chile, Chiloé and Patagonian Argentina; Mapudungun (Lafkenche, Pewenche, Williche); Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien and Yagán where communities share trade terms. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pewenche families of Lonquimay**: check whether gathering and summer herding show up as work in the census occupation question.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison) and Lake Budi**: look at *cochayuyo* (kelp) gathering as household income and as a family task.
- **Nagche and Wenteche smallholders**: compare forestry crew work with the family plot in the same households.
- **Mapuche of Añelo and Rincón de los Sauces**: check how many appear on oil-union rolls and in rig-crew studies.
- **Aónikenk families of Santa Cruz**: trace hide work into station work; thin, start with Río Gallegos museum files.
- **Yagán of Puerto Williams and Kawésqar of Puerto Edén**: check crab and shellfish permits held, and what work community publications name.
- **Chilote families**: compare diving and salmon jobs with potato plots.
- **Families of Welsh, German and Croatian origin**: set farm diaries, workshop ledgers and shop books beside each other.
- **Boer and Lebanese settler lines**: check the few memoirs for farm, freight and peddling work around Sarmiento and Comodoro Rivadavia.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for employment surveys and oil; mixed for Mapuche and Chilote work; thin for Aónikenk and Kawésqar accounts of their own work.
- **Languages needed:** 18th-c. naval Spanish for Moraleda; Mapudungun for trade names; Welsh for diaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forestry work sits inside the land conflict; the 1859-60 press is a partisan account.
- **Held for review:** the labor rules on special shift systems that set the oil and salmon rosters were set aside: the wording goes to Libra, the intent to Capricorn.
