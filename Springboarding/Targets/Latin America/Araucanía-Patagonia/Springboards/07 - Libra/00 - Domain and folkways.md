# Araucanía-Patagonia: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; Wallmapu and Patagonia under two states' codes (Araucanía to Magallanes; Neuquén to Tierra del Fuego). **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of both states, with each code's legal Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne via their programs. **Concept in one line:** partnership and justice: what is a fair bargain and a good partnership? Folkways: marriage and gender ways; order ways as runner-up (courts here, policing in Capricorn); plus the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche, Nagche and Wenteche communities**: check, in family accounts, how marriages between *lof* are arranged on the coast, plains and Cautín valley.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay and Icalma**: check who rules on a quarrel over *pewen*-gathering grounds, and in which forum.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa and Chiloé**: read the caciques' councils' minute books for rulings on land and marriage.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: set each provincial community register beside Chile's.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: look for any written marriage or dispute rule; scarce, start with Camusu Aike's registration and mission books.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam organizations**: read their statutes as chosen forms of partnership.
- **Chilote families and Chilean migrants across the Andes**: check whether a bargain is sealed at the *minga* table, before a Castro notary, or in Argentine papers.
- **Welsh-, German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: compare marriage registers for unions inside and outside each community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes and courts; mixed for written Mapuche customary rules; thin for Aónikenk, Gününa küne and Boer families. The author has not yet fixed which lands the nation holds, so Libra reads the two national codes and five provincial charters of the frame above; the Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas enter only as contrast.
- **Languages needed:** two codes' legal Spanish, which name one institution differently; colonial Spanish; Mapudungun; nineteenth-century Welsh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land claims, antiterrorism prosecutions, the 'Pacification of the Araucanía' and the 'Conquest of the Desert' are contested: read each source as an account. Family-court files are sealed.
- **Held for review:** readings of the courts' legitimacy came up beside the constitutional pointers; as spirit of the law they go to Capricorn.
