# Araucanía-Patagonia: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; couples, crews, cooperatives and compacts from the Araucanía and Chiloé to Vaca Muerta and the Chubut valley. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of Chile and Argentina; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

|---|---|---|---|

| Spanish (Chile) | *yunta*; *pololo*, *polola*; *conviviente civil* | a pair yoked together, a close partner; boyfriend, girlfriend; civil-union partner | *yunta* keeps its farm sense in the Araucanía: search both senses |

English keywords: civil union southern Chile; Mapuche community agreements oil companies; dairy cooperatives Los Ríos.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche families of rural Cautín and Malleco**: check where a new couple settles after marriage, from family accounts and census household tables.
- **Pewenche of Icalma**: check how the families that gather *pewen* together each autumn form their groups.
- **Mapuche of Campo Maripe and other Vaca Muerta communities**: read their agreements with oil firms beside their complaints.
- **Williche of the Chiloé archipelago**: trace *compadrazgo* in baptism books across islands.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán families**: check how their own publications describe canoe-family partnerships; sources few.
- **Welsh-descended families of Gaiman and Trevelin**: count marriages outside the community by decade in chapel and civil registers.
- **German-descended dairy families of Los Ríos and Llanquihue**: compare cooperative membership with family firms.
- **Croatian- and Lebanese-descended merchants of Punta Arenas and Río Gallegos**: check partnership deeds of trading houses.
- **Boer-descended families of Comodoro Rivadavia's hinterland**: look for church marriage books; likely scarce.
- **Chilean migrants in Neuquén and Río Gallegos**: check how binational couples register their union.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for couple statistics and company law; mixed for cooperative histories; thin for Kawésqar, Yagán and Boer partnership records.
- **Languages needed:** each code's family-law terms; colonial Spanish and Mapudungun for Valdivia's grammar; Welsh for chapel books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** company-community agreements are contested and some clauses are confidential; couple violence surfaces in family files that are sealed.
- **Held for review:** private partnership deeds and marriage contracts kept by families were considered; they need the families' consent, so none is listed by name.

## 5. First queries
1. Ask the Registro Civil for civil-union and marriage counts by region, 2015 to now.
2. Read *Giustiniani c/ YPF* on the Supreme Court's site; list which clauses were at issue.
3. Download CONADI's community register for the Araucanía and Los Lagos; note the dates of founding.
4. Find Colun's founding statutes through the Los Ríos regional archive or the cooperative.
5. Search the Biblioteca Nacional Digital for Valdivia's 1606 *Arte y gramática*.
