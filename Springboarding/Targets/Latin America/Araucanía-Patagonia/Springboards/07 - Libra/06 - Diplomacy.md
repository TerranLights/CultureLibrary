# Araucanía-Patagonia: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; the parliaments of the old frontier, the councils of the steppe, the Andean border talks and everyday tact from Temuco to Ushuaia. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of Chile and Argentina; colonial Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar through its organizations. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *diplomacia*, *parlamento*, *tratado* | diplomacy; a parley (here the Spanish-Mapuche council); treaty | *parlamento* in the south means the frontier council first: "parlamento de Negrete", "parlamento de Las Canoas" |
| Spanish | *mano izquierda*; *muñeca* (Argentina) | skill in handling people; political knack ("wrist") | try in Neuquén and Chubut provincial politics coverage |
| Spanish (colonial) | *capitulaciones*; *agasajo* | the articles agreed at a parley; the gifts and feasting given to envoys | *agasajo* as the costed side of frontier diplomacy |
| Mapudungun | *koyagtun* | here: the formal oratory of a parliament, speech answered by speech | *check* spelling; the speaking order is itself the protocol |
| Mapudungun | *pentukun* | the formal greeting exchange between visitors and hosts | in diplomacy, the opening that comes before any business |
| Mapudungun | *werken* | here: an envoy carrying a message between leaders or to the state | the messenger's standing in a negotiation |
| Welsh | *diplomyddiaeth*; *cwrteisi* | diplomacy; courtesy | colony leaders' letters to Buenos Aires and to steppe chiefs |
| German; Croatian; Afrikaans | *Takt*; *diplomacija*; *diplomasie* | tact; diplomacy | consular and society minutes in Puerto Montt, Punta Arenas, Comodoro Rivadavia |

English keywords: Spanish-Mapuche parliaments; Indigenous treaties Argentina nineteenth century; Chile-Argentina boundary arbitration; Mapuche letters nineteenth century.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Williche of the Osorno coast**: check how their councils cite Las Canoas in meetings with ministries.
- **Lafkenche of Carahue and Puerto Saavedra**: read consultation minutes for coastal claims and who speaks first.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: check meetings with Neuquén Mapuche across the Pino Hachado pass, and who hosts.
- **Confederación Mapuche de Neuquén**: check its written protocol for meeting provincial officials.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: set nineteenth-century treaties with their chiefs beside what descendants' groups say of them now; sources few.
- **Kawésqar groups**: compare the park consultation record with their own statements.
- **Welsh-descended families of the lower valley**: compare the colony's letters to Buenos Aires with its dealings with steppe families.
- **Croatian-descended societies of Punta Arenas**: check minutes for how they handled the 1991 change of homeland state with consuls and members.
- **Chilean migrants in Argentine Patagonia**: check consular complaints and how disputes are settled without courts.
- **Chilote families**: look in folklore and speech studies for formulas that soften a refusal between neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial parliaments, treaties and boundary awards; mixed for nineteenth-century Indigenous letters; thin for Fuegian protocols and Boer families' dealings.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish and Mapudungun of parliament records; Victorian English; Welsh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whether the parliaments are binding treaties is a live claim; present the state's and the claimants' readings as accounts.
- **Held for review:** files of the 1978 crisis between the two states were considered; many remain closed, so they are not listed.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Zavala's edition and list each parliament, its place, and its articles on land and trade.
2. Search Pavez's *Cartas mapuche* for letters to Argentine and Chilean governors.
3. Download the 1881 treaty and the 1994 Laguna del Desierto award from either foreign ministry.
4. Find Martínez's UN treaty study; note what it says of South American parliaments.
5. Ask the Comités de Integración secretariats for meeting minutes since 2009.
