# Araucanía-Patagonia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; returned labor, gifts, vows and favors from Cautín seed fairs and Chiloé house-hauling to steppe stock-markings and town shop credit. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of Chile, Chiloé and Argentina; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán through its community. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chilote families of Castro and Achao**: check who is owed return labor after a house hauling, and how it is remembered.
- **Williche of southern Chiloé (Compu, Quellón)**: compare whether the *minga* debt is kept within kin or across the chapel community.
- **Wenteche of the Cautín valley**: check at *trafkintu* fairs who gives first and whether seed must come back in kind.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: check how the *pewen* harvest is shared with kin living in Temuco and Santiago.
- **Mapuche *crianceros* of Neuquén**: see who helps at a *señalada* and how help is repaid.
- **Yagán of Puerto Williams**: read Calderón's stories for rules on sharing a catch.
- **Croatian- and Spanish-descended families of Punta Arenas and Trelew**: compare mutual-aid society rules on who may draw benefits.
- **Welsh chapel members of the lower valley**: check collections taken for families after floods and deaths, as minute books record them.
- **Chilote migrants in Comodoro Rivadavia and Río Gallegos**: trace money and goods sent home and gifts carried back at holidays.
- **Lebanese- and Spanish-descended storekeepers**: compare credit terms for station families and for townspeople.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich for codes, treaties and *minga* ethnography; one national preferences survey; thin for Kawésqar and Aónikenk exchange accounts, for which no source is named here.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for exchange vocabulary; Chilote Spanish; eighteenth-century Latin and Spanish for Havestadt.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ngillatun* hosting is ritual; frontier raiding is told differently by Mapuche and Chilean historians, so read it as accounts.
- **Held for review:** invitation lists and hosts' names for particular *ngillatun* were considered; they are private to the communities, so only published ethnography is listed.
