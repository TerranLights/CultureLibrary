# Araucanía-Patagonia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; Temuco's hospitals and the Nagche plains to the coves of Chiloé and Aysén and the cemeteries of Punta Arenas, Trelew and Ushuaia. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of both states and Chilote Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Yagán and Kawésqar where the communities publish. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning are researched here (funeral practice is in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche families of Ercilla and Collipulli**: set the Lemún anniversary beside the farm families' memorials for their own dead.
- **Wenteche of Temuco and Padre Las Casas**: check whether city hospital deaths change how the *eluwün* is held back home.
- **Pewenche of Icalma**: listen for grief words in *ül* recorded there against Lafkenche ones.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: see how the Catholic *novena* and Williche practice sit together at an island death.
- **Mapuche near Neuquén's oil fields**: check how deaths blamed on pollution are mourned and told.
- **Yagán of Villa Ukika and Kawésqar of Puerto Edén**: ask what each chooses to share of mourning, and in whose voice.
- **Croatian families of Punta Arenas and Porvenir**: compare mausoleum inscriptions in Croatian and in Spanish over three generations.
- **Lebanese families of Neuquén and Comodoro**: check which church buries them, and the language of the prayers.
- **Evangelical congregations of Temuco and Puerto Montt**: compare wakes without alcohol or *ül* with Catholic ones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality statistics and colonial texts; mixed for Mapuche grief in Mapuche voices (recordings scattered); thin for Kawésqar, Yagán and Aónikenk mourning outside outsiders' books.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for *ül* and orations, with a speaker from the same territory; Welsh *cynghanedd* to read the elegies; Croatian for early notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in the land conflict are grieved on several sides and some are before the courts; suicide data and names need care; recordings of funeral speeches may be restricted by families.
- **Held for review:** the end-of-life statutes (Argentina's dignified-death law and Chile's palliative-care law) came up beside the hospital pointer; their text is Libra's, so only the hospitals stay here.
