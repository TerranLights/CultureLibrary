# Araucanía-Patagonia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; the Pentecostal halls of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison), twice-rebuilt Valdivia, replanted Torres del Paine, revival classes in Tolhuin. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of both states; Mapudungun; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Selk'nam, Yagán and Aonekko ʼaʼien as revived. **Concept in one line:** a new life through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa**: compare converts' testimony with the *ngillatun* families they left or stayed with.
- **Wenteche of Temuco**: check whether *We Tripantu* in city parks reads as revival or continuity to those who attend.
- **Pewenche of Icalma and Lonquimay**: ask which *machi* rites were renewed and which never stopped.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén (Confederación Mapuche de Neuquén)**: compare provincial-side revival talk with the Chilean side's.
- **Selk'nam descendants in Chile and in Tolhuin**: set the Chilean re-emergence beside the Rafaela Ishton community's longer recognition.
- **Yagán of Puerto Williams and Gününa küne descendants**: look for revival classes and who teaches them; Gününa küne material is scarce, start with Río Negro and Chubut provincial culture offices.
- **Welsh chapels of Trelew and Trevelin**: compare chapel revival memory in the valley with the Andes.
- **Boer families of Sarmiento and German families of Llanquihue**: compare migration told as a fresh start against migration told as exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revival and Pentecostal growth; mixed for disaster recovery (press heavy, studies few); thin for Gününa küne and Kawésqar revival in print.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun in several alphabets for revival media; 1900s Welsh for chapel minutes; seventeenth-century Spanish for the catechism.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** revived rites may hold parts reserved to members; forced conversion by missions and the Salesian years on Dawson Island are told from more than one side; prison files are restricted.
- **Held for review:** restricted ceremonial knowledge in revived Selk'nam and Mapuche rites was weighed as a direct pointer; only what the communities have chosen to publish stays, and nothing further was set aside.
