# Araucanía-Patagonia: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; Araucanía healing grounds, Chiloé, the river towns of Los Ríos and Los Lagos, Argentine Patagonia's university towns. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of both states; Mapudungun; Chilote Spanish; German; Welsh. **Concept in one line** (proposed): getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche *machi* and their patients**: compare which harms are removed in a *datun* and which are referred to a *posta*.
- **Williche and Chilote healers of Castro and Ancud**: check how herb and smoke cleansing are combined with Catholic blessing.
- **Pilgrims from Quinchao, Achao and the outer islands**: compare promises paid at Caguach by island and by generation.
- **University towns (Valdivia, Temuco, Neuquén, Comodoro Rivadavia)**: set the dismissed staff's memoirs beside official histories of the same institution.
- **German-descended families of Osorno, Puerto Varas and Bariloche**: check what firm records and family letters say of the 1940s lists.
- **Chilean migrants in Neuquén and Río Gallegos**: compare 1978 memories with those of Argentine neighbors.
- **Tehuelche and Mapuche families of Chubut**: look for family accounts of relatives taken to Martín García.
- **Fishers and farmers on the río Cruces**: compare who spoke of poison, who of jobs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1973 and 1976 purges and the swans case; mixed for wartime blacklists (foreign archives); thin for cleansing rites in practitioners' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Inquisition-era Spanish; English for US blacklist files; German for firm letters; Mapudungun for healing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *machi* rites and healing knowledge are guarded; the 2019 statue events and the Martín García removals are contested; *funa* targets are living people, so name none.
- **Held for review:** two kinds of pointer were weighed and dropped: named persons on the 1940s blacklists, which would point at living families, and individual call-out (*funa*) cases; both are left as categories.
