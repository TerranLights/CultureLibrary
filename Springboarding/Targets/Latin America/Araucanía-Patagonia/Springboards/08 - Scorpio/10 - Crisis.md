# Araucanía-Patagonia: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; Lake Riñihue's outlet, Pucón under its volcano, Los Lagos salmon coves, flooded Comodoro Rivadavia, burned Malleco, a Magallanes cut off by land. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Spanish of both states; Mapudungun (Lafkenche and Williche speech for the coast); Croatian. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche of Puerto Saavedra and Lake Budi**: compare 1960 tellings by elders with what schools now teach about tsunami.
- **Williche of Maullín and Chiloé's west coast**: check who led the first days after the wave and who left for good.
- **Mapuche communities near Pucón and Curarrehue**: set their 2015 evacuation accounts beside the tourist town's.
- **Nagche families of Purén and Lumaco**: compare fire-loss accounts with forestry workers' and farmers' in the same communes.
- **Chilote salmon workers of Quellón and Castro**: compare who stayed and who left for Aysén or Argentina after the virus.
- **Oil families of Comodoro Rivadavia, including Chilean migrants**: check how neighborhood ties held in the 2017 flood.
- **Croatian- and Chilote-descended families of Punta Arenas and Porvenir**: compare memory of the 2020 closures with memory of the 1930s collapse.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Edén and Yagán of Puerto Williams**: look at how remote settlements get help in an emergency; sources are scarce, start with navy and municipal reports.
- **Public employees and teachers of Santa Cruz**: compare union and household accounts of the pay crisis.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters (press, agencies, research centers); mixed for economic crises at household level; thin for remote canal communities.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century Spanish for Mariño de Lobera; Mapudungun for coastal tellings, with a local speaker; Croatian for family memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fire blame in the Araucanía is contested and partly before the courts; suicide data and crisis-line figures need care; record flood stories as tellings, never as anyone's creed.
- **Held for review:** an outsider's book on a death during the 1960 tsunami at Lake Budi was weighed and set aside: it has been used to profile a people, and the community's own account must come first.
