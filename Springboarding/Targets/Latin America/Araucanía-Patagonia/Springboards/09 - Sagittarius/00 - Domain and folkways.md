## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche of Cholchol and Padre Las Casas**: Anglican and Capuchin missions worked side by side here; check which families now host a *ngillatun* and which joined a Pentecostal church.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay and Melipeuco**: read their own statements on the araucaria groves as sacred ground.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison)**: set the coastal *ngillatun* calendar beside inland ones.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa and Chiloé**: map where *fiscal*-led chapels and Williche rites share a feast.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén (Ruca Choroy, Aluminé)**: compare *kamaruko*, the Argentine-side name, *check*, with Chilean *ngillatun* records.
- **Aónikenk, Selk'nam, Yagán and Kawésqar descendants**: outsiders wrote them off; look for what each now makes public of its sacred narratives.
- **Welsh chapel families of Gaiman and Trevelin; German Lutheran and Catholic families of Llanquihue**: compare membership rolls over a century.
- **Croatians of Punta Arenas, Lebanese families of Neuquén, Boer families of Sarmiento**: note which church each built and its prayer language.
- **Oil towns (Comodoro, Cerro Sombrero)**: set no-religion shares beside farm districts'.
