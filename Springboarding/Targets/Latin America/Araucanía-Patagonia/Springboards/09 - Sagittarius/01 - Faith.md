# Araucanía-Patagonia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; the shrines of Lago Budi and Cholchol, the island chapels of Chiloé, Osorno's Lutheran churches, the roadside shrines of Ruta 40 and Ushuaia's Anglican past. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Chilean and Chilote Spanish; Patagonian Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán through the Villa Ukika community. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche of Lago Budi**: compare what is asked for at a lake-shore *ngillatun* with prayer requests at the area's Pentecostal churches.
- **Mapuche in Temuco's urban *poblaciones***: check whether moving to the city changed which church or rite a family trusts.
- **Pewenche of Icalma and Lonquimay**: look at how a petition for the *pewen* harvest is framed, and by whom.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: see whether vows (*mandas*) to a saint and Williche petitions are made at the same feast.
- **Mapuche of Junín de los Andes and Aluminé**: weigh what the Salesian presence and *kamaruko* each ask of a family.
- **Yagán of Villa Ukika**: what remains of the Anglican mission's hymns, and in which language they are sung, if at all.
- **German Lutherans of Osorno and Puerto Montt**: trace which side of the 1975 split each congregation took, and the reasons members give.
- **Welsh chapel families of the lower Chubut**: compare chapel attendance in the valley with the Andes chapels at Trevelin.
- **Lebanese Orthodox and Maronite families of Neuquén and Comodoro**: check which church they used where their own had no priest.
- **Chilean migrants in the Alto Valle**: see whether island saints traveled to their new chapels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mission and church history; mixed for surveys (national samples thin out in the south); thin for faith in Aónikenk, Kawésqar and Selk'nam terms, where outsiders' books still dominate.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun speakers from the same territory to hear a prayer's address; seventeenth-century Spanish for Valdivia; Welsh chapel prose; Afrikaans and Dutch for Boer papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ngillatun* prayers are not for recording everywhere; conversion is a family wound in some communities; mission narratives of martyrdom and the Yagán account of Wulaia are both accounts.
- **Held for review:** a pointer on the *machi*'s calling and healing was weighed and left out, as restricted knowledge that belongs with Pisces's healing ways; private Boer and Welsh family Bibles need the families' leave before any reading.
