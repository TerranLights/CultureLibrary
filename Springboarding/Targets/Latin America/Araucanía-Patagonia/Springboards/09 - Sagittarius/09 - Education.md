# Araucanía-Patagonia: Education (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the Jesuit college at Castro and the Capuchin seminary at San José de la Mariquina to the campuses of Temuco, Valdivia, Neuquén, Comodoro Rivadavia, Coyhaique, Viedma and Ushuaia. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of both states (each with its own school and degree words); Mapudungun; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán and Kawésqar through their teaching programs. **Concept in one line:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person; learning, curiosity and early schooling stay in Gemini.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche students from Lumaco and Ercilla in Temuco**: compare why they chose a degree with what their families hoped it would bring home.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison) and Puerto Saavedra**: check which young people leave for a degree and how many return to teach.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa and Chiloé**: trace the line from Jesuit and Franciscan schooling to today's island campuses.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén**: set intercultural teacher programs in Argentina beside the Temuco degree.
- **Aónikenk, Yagán and Kawésqar descendants**: note who now teaches the language, with what credential, and who grants it.
- **Welsh-descended families of the Chubut**: see whether a Welsh school, a local university or study in Wales counts most.
- **German-descended families of Osorno and Valdivia**: track which children take the German school-leaving exam and study in Germany.
- **Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas**: compare degree choices across three generations of a merchant family.
- **Chilean-born families in the Alto Valle and Comodoro**: test how far Argentina's free universities shaped their move.
- **Oil and salmon towns**: weigh technical schools against university degrees in what parents push for.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for enrollment statistics and university histories; mixed for what education means to families; thin for Aónikenk, Kawésqar and Gününa küne views of schooling.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish and Latin for Jesuit records; German for Capuchin seminary papers; Welsh.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mission schooling is remembered as both gift and loss; intercultural degrees are argued over within Mapuche communities as well as outside; student data must be cited in aggregate.
- **Held for review:** named pupil registers from mission boarding schools were weighed and left out, as they identify children and families; free-tuition statutes go to Libra, and what the state owes a student to Capricorn.
