# Araucanía-Patagonia: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the volunteer fire halls of Valdivia and Osorno to the veterans' vigil in Río Grande and the Boer graves near Sarmiento. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *deber*; *obligación*; *compromiso* | what one owes; a binding requirement; a pledge, or an occasion one must attend | pair with a southern setting: "deber del bombero Valdivia", "compromiso con la comunidad Chiloé", "obligación con los padres Neuquén" |
| Spanish | *manda*; *pagar la promesa* | a vow to a saint; to fulfill it | the pilgrim's duty: try with Chimpay, Junín de los Andes, Andacollo (Neuquén) |
| Spanish (Chile) | *hacer el servicio*; *el sorteo* | to do military service; the call-up lottery | how families speak of the year a son serves, *check* current lottery rules |
| Mapudungun | *mingako* | work given to a household by neighbors, repaid with food and later work | a duty that comes due when one is called; *check* forms |
| Mapudungun | *eluwün* | funeral rite | duties to the dead: who must come, speak, cook and stay |
| Welsh | *rhwymedigaeth*; *addewid* | obligation; promise | chapel and family letters from Gaiman and Trevelin |
| German | *Ehrenamt*; *Verpflichtung* | unpaid honorary office; commitment | fire companies and school boards of Llanquihue and Valdivia |
| Croatian; Afrikaans | *obveza*; *roeping*, *verpligting* | obligation; calling, obligation | Punta Arenas mutual-aid papers; Boer congregation records, *check* |

English keywords: volunteer firefighters Chile; Malvinas veterans Patagonia; Mapuche communal work; Boer settlers Chubut; caregivers southern Chile.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche families near Temuco and Nagche of Traiguén**: check which farm tasks still call a *mingako*, and who keeps count of debts owed.
- **Lafkenche of Puerto Saavedra and Budi**: look at how *eluwün* duties are shared out among kin.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa**: check the duties of hosts at shared feasts of chapel and community.
- **Mapuche of Ruca Choroy and Junín de los Andes**: compare duties of *lof* members with Catholic duties at the Laura Vicuña shrine.
- **Chilote families with men in Comodoro and Río Gallegos**: trace money and letters sent home, and who on the island cared for parents.
- **German-descended families of Puerto Varas and Osorno**: count generations in the same fire company.
- **Welsh-descended families of Gaiman**: check who keeps the chapel's rota of services and cleaning.
- **Croatian-descended families of Porvenir**: check the mutual-aid books for dues paid or forgiven.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: compare the first settlers' reasons for leaving with what their grandchildren say they owe.
- **Veterans in Río Grande and Ushuaia; oil and salmon workers on rotation**: compare duty to comrades with duty to family left behind.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fire corps and veterans' groups; mixed for care data (Chile's surveys split regions, Argentina's less); thin for Aónikenk and Fuegian peoples' duties to kin and dead in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Spanish for Pineda; Afrikaans and Dutch for Boer church papers; German for fire-company minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Malvinas memory is raw for veterans' families; funeral duties in Mapuche communities have parts not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** migrants' private letters home with money were weighed as a pointer; family papers stay unnamed here and are listed only as a source type.
