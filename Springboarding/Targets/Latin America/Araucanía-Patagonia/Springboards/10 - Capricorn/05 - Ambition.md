# Araucanía-Patagonia: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the counting houses of Punta Arenas and the homesteads of Aysén to Temuco's lecture halls and the ships that carried Chilote boys south. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Italian and Basque for settler papers; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *ambición*; *codicia* | drive to rise; greed | the two blur in older southern writing: Ercilla calls the conquerors' drive *codicia*; try both with "Patagonia pioneros" |
| Spanish (Chile) | *surgir*; *salir adelante*; *arribista* | to get on in life; to come through; a social climber | "surgir" is the Chilean everyday word: try with "Chiloé", "Temuco", "Punta Arenas" |
| Spanish (Argentina) | *hacer la América* | to make one's fortune in the Americas | the settler phrase among Spanish, Italian, Basque and Lebanese families of Patagonia |
| Spanish (Aysén) | *colono*; *abrir campo* | settler; to clear land for oneself | the homesteader's ambition against the big concessions |
| Mapudungun | *ülmen* | a wealthy man of standing in the old *lof* | here as a figure one could hope to become, and how wealth had to be shared, *check* |
| Welsh | *uchelgais*; *dod ymlaen* | ambition; to get on | letters and *Y Drafod* columns on sons leaving the valley |
| German | *Ehrgeiz*; *Aufstieg* | ambition; rise | family histories of Llanquihue firms |
| Croatian; Italian; Afrikaans | *napredak*; *fare l'America*; *vooruitgang* | progress, getting ahead, in each language | Croatian Punta Arenas, Italian Alto Valle, Boer Sarmiento papers, *check* |

English keywords: Patagonia pioneers wealth; Magallanes immigrant merchants; Aysén homesteaders; Mapuche university students; Chilote migrant workers Argentina.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche students from rural *lof***: compare what a degree is meant to do for the family and for the community.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison) and Budi**: check how fishing and tourism ventures are judged when one family prospers.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: look at herders who moved into trade or transport.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: compare young men's moves east with those who stayed to fish.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén**: check oil jobs and what communities say of members who took them.
- **Aónikenk families of Santa Cruz**: thin sources; look for what ranch-hand families hoped for their children.
- **Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas**: trace one firm from digging to trade.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley**: compare sons who stayed on the farm with those who went to Buenos Aires or Wales.
- **German-descended families of Puerto Montt**: check family firm histories for who took over.
- **Basque, Italian, Lebanese and Spanish settler families**: compare how each group's climb is told in its own club histories.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check whether land or church was the measure of getting on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for merchant and settler histories; mixed for Mapuche educational hopes (studies exist, scattered); thin for Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam aims in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century verse Spanish; Croatian for emigrant letters; Welsh for colony debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Aónikenk and Selk'nam descendants' accounts, and many histories, tie the great station fortunes to the taking of their lands; set those accounts beside the families' own.
- **Held for review:** popular books that trace the station fortunes to the "character" of an immigrant nationality were weighed and dropped, since they explain a climb by a people's supposed nature.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *La Araucana* canto III and note each line on greed and ambition.
