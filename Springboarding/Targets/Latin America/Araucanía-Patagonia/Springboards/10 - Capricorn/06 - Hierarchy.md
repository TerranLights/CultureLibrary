# Araucanía-Patagonia: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the *lof* of the old Mapuche lineages to the station chain of command on the Santa Cruz steppe, and from the mansions on Punta Arenas's plaza to Temuco's outer neighborhoods. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *jerarquía*; *los de arriba*, *los de abajo* | ranked order; those above, those below | in the south try "jerarquía estancia Santa Cruz", "jerarquía social Punta Arenas", "jerarquía mapuche colonial" |
| Spanish (Patagonian stations) | *administrador*, *mayordomo*, *capataz*, *puestero*, *peón* | manager, overseer, foreman, outpost herder, laborer | the station ladder; each rank had its own house, table and pay |
| Spanish (Argentina) | *cheto*; *negro* (as a class slur) | posh; a slur for the poor, described not used | how class is named in Neuquén and Comodoro speech; record, never adopt |
| Mapudungun | *kona* | young man in a leader's following; retainer, warrior | the lower rung of the old *lof* order as colonial grammars give it, *check* |
| Mapudungun | *ñidol* (*ñizol*) | chief, first, principal | a word for head rank in larger gatherings, *check* spelling and use |
| Welsh | *gwerin*; *bonedd* | the common folk; gentry | the colony's self-image as a people without gentry, to test against land records |
| German | *Stand*; *Herkunft* | social estate; origin | club and school rolls of Osorno and Puerto Varas |
| Croatian; Afrikaans | *gazda*; *rang* | boss, landlord; rank | Punta Arenas shop and farm talk; Boer congregation seating, *check* |

English keywords: Mapuche social organization colonial; estancia hierarchy Patagonia; ethnic inequality southern Chile; discrimination Patagonia; elite clubs Punta Arenas.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche families**: check whether old *ülmen* lineages still carry weight in who becomes *lonko*.
- **Lafkenche of the coast**: compare rank by fishing boats and nets with rank by land.
- **Pewenche of Icalma**: check how herd size and summer-pasture rights order families.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: compare the councils' titled *caciques* with ordinary members.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Chubut**: compare provincial survey gaps with Chilean ones.
- **Aónikenk, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: look at how each community describes rank in its own past, where it chooses to.
- **Station hands of Santa Cruz and Magallanes**: trace who rose to foreman, and from which background.
- **Welsh-descended families**: set the egalitarian self-image against land-holding records.
- **German-descended families of Osorno and Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas**: check club admission rules over a century.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check whether church seating or land size showed standing.
- **Chilean migrants in Comodoro, Neuquén and Río Gallegos**: compare their place in the job ladder with locals'.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for station and merchant hierarchies; mixed for ethnic gaps (Chile measures more often than Argentina); thin for rank among the Fuegian peoples in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century Spanish and Mapudungun for Febrés; German for Claraz's diaries; Croatian for club papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class and ethnic slurs must be described, not repeated as labels; skin-color hierarchies are painful and live.
- **Held for review:** nineteenth-century race-ranking writings on Fuegian and Patagonian peoples were considered as sources for ideas of rank; they were not listed, because they profile peoples rather than record them.
