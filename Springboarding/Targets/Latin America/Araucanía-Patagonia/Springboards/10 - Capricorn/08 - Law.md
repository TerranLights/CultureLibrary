# Araucanía-Patagonia: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the old *parlamento* grounds and today's *trawün* to Temuco's rights offices and the steppe ranches. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: here only the spirit (what law is for, whose it is, obedience, discretion, trust, legitimacy); the letter goes to Libra.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *ley*; *derecho*; *legitimidad* | a statute; law as a system, and a right; rightful standing | pair them with southern claims: "derecho propio mapuche", "legitimidad del Estado Araucanía", "derecho consuetudinario williche" |
| Spanish | *obedézcase pero no se cumpla* | let it be obeyed but not carried out | the colonial formula for honoring a royal order while setting it aside; look for frontier uses |
| Spanish | *hecha la ley, hecha la trampa*; *la ley pareja no es dura* | once a law is made, so is the way round it; a law applied evenly is not harsh | evasion and evenness; compare their use in Temuco and Neuquén press |
| Mapudungun | *az mapu* (*ad mapu*) | the right order of the land and its people | a people's own law: what it protects, who keeps it, how breaches are mended |
| Mapudungun | *trawün*; *koyagtun* | a meeting or assembly; formal parley speech | the setting where disputes and agreements are settled without a state court |
| Mapudungun | *werken* | messenger, spokesperson | here as the one who carries a *lof*'s position to the state's officials and courts |
| Welsh | *cyfraith*; *cydwybod* | law; conscience | colony debates on Argentine law against chapel conscience |
| German; Croatian; Afrikaans | *Recht*; *zakon*; *reg* | law, right; statute; law, right | settler papers on how far the new state's law reached them, *check* |

English keywords: Mapuche customary law; legal pluralism Chile; parlamentos treaties Mapuche; legitimacy of the state Araucanía; rule of law Patagonia surveys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche and Wenteche *lof* of Malleco and Cautín**: compare how often disputes go to a *trawün* and how often to a state court.
- **Lafkenche of the Budi coast and of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison)**: check what the coastal claims are said to be for, by those who filed them.
- **Pewenche of Icalma and Lonquimay**: look at grazing rules kept among families, and where they meet park rules.
- **Williche of Osorno, San Juan de la Costa and Chiloé**: trace how the 1793 agreement is cited today.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare legal strategies with Chilean Mapuche ones on the same land questions.
- **Aónikenk, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: thin sources; check what each says about law that came with the stations and missions.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare the colony's early self-rule with later courts.
- **German-descended families of Llanquihue; Croatian-descended families of Magallanes**: check whether settler papers call the state's law their own.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check what their founders said about leaving British law.
- **Steppe ranchers; Chilean migrants in Argentine towns**: compare talk of stock theft, police and self-help.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and present debates over Mapuche law and the state; mixed for opinion data; thin for Fuegian peoples' own law.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Spanish for frontier debates; Mapudungun for *az mapu* texts; Welsh for colony papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "Pacification of the Araucanía", the "Conquest of the Desert" and present land claims are contested; give every side's account. Some *az mapu* teaching may be closed.
- **Held for review:** Libra's side of this sheet is the constitutions' clauses on Indigenous peoples and Chile's Indigenous law; their wording is left there, and only the arguments over their purpose are pointed to here.
