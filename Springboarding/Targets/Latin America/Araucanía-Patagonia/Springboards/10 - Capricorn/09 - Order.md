# Araucanía-Patagonia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; from the Corral and Niebla forts guarding Valdivia and the grid of Castro to the rural police posts of the old Araucanía frontier, the Gendarmería stations of the steppe and Bariloche's school-leavers' streets. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (southern Chile, Chiloé, Argentine Patagonia); Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

English keywords: public order Araucanía; Patagonia policing history; frontier forts Valdivia; Mapuche cosmology layers; Bariloche student trips.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche of Ercilla and Wenteche of Padre Las Casas**: compare how each describes army patrols during the emergency.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison)**: check how coastal *lof* settle quarrels before police are called.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: look at grazing order kept among families across summer and winter pastures.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa**: check whether feast days and chapel offices set the order of the year.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Chubut**: compare memories of the Gendarmería with Chilean memories of Carabineros.
- **Kawésqar families of Puerto Edén and Yagán families of Ukika**: sources are thin; set Emperaire's account against what the communities say of life in the canoes.
- **Chilote families of Castro and Achao**: compare the plaza-and-church plan with life on the shore.
- **Welsh-descended townspeople of Trelew and Gaiman**: compare chapel minutes on public conduct with police records.
- **German-descended residents of Frutillar and Puerto Varas**: check who argues for and against heritage rules.
- **Croatian-descended families of Punta Arenas; Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check how society and church minutes speak of good conduct in town.
- **Bariloche's hotel and police workers; strikers' descendants in Santa Cruz**: compare who is said to keep order and who to break it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policing history and crime statistics; mixed for southern attitude data; thin for Kawésqar and Yagán ideas of a well-ordered world in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for fort and town records; Welsh for chapel minutes; Mapudungun for cosmology terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1920-22 shootings, the emergency in the Araucanía and frontier policing are contested; present each as one party's account. Some cosmological teaching is not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** online lists that rank southern towns as "dangerous" were weighed and dropped; they label places, and through them peoples, without sources.
