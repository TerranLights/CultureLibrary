# Araucanía-Patagonia: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; working frame: Wallmapu on both slopes and the south to Cape Horn (the Araucanía, Los Ríos, Los Lagos with Chiloé, Aysén, Magallanes; Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish as spoken from Temuco and Neuquén down to Chiloé, Punta Arenas and Ushuaia; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; the revived Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for, with the social ways of settlement, migration, hospitality and gathering.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche coast and Pewenche highlands**: check which gatherings call several *lof* together in each.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: set the councils of *caciques* beside island chapel feasts.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare colony files with family accounts of who was placed with whom.
- **Aónikenk of Santa Cruz and Chubut**: camp hospitality survives mostly in travelers' books; seek descendants' tellings.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Edén, Yagán of Villa Ukika and Ushuaia, Selk'nam of Tolhuin**: outsiders wrote them off; check how each now gathers its members.
- **Chilote families**: compare feasts at home with migrants' clubs in Punta Arenas and Comodoro.
- **Welsh-, German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: list each group's associations and when each opened to outsiders.
- **Chilean migrants, oil workers, station hands**: compare company camps with towns that grew without a company.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler associations and land files; mixed for national surveys; thin for canoe and steppe peoples' group life in their own words. For community and ideals, the frame above is a working one: the author has not yet fixed the nation's lands, and Alto Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas serve only as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun across alphabets for the scale words; colonial Spanish for Rosales; Welsh for the 1865 roll.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land-grant files bear on live claims; *ngillatun* grounds are not open to all.
- **Held for review:** a national-character essay on Chilean or Argentine sociability was weighed as a frame and left out, since it would give the whole south one voice.
