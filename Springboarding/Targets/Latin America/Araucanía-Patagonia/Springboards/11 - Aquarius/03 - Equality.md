# Araucanía-Patagonia: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: from the Araucanía and the Neuquén valleys south through the lakes, Chiloé, Aysén, the Chubut valley and the steppe to Magallanes and Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** southern Chilean and Patagonian Argentine Spanish, Chilote Spanish; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam through community programs. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche of Temuco and Padre Las Casas**: check how urban and rural Mapuche describe being treated at clinics, banks and schools, in testimony collections.
- **Lafkenche of Tirúa (Biobío edge, comparison) and the Budi lake**: compare fishing and land shares inside a community with shares outside it.
- **Williche of Osorno**: check how surnames and land titles were handled, and what families say about reclaiming them.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare oil-royalty towns with communities near the fields.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: sources are thin; look in provincial archives for how they were counted and paid on the stations.
- **Yagán, Kawésqar and Selk'nam communities**: compare equality as each claims it with services each receives.
- **Chilote migrants and their children in Argentina**: check wage and housing records for the gap with Argentine-born workers.
- **Welsh-, German- and Croatian-descended families**: compare rules for women's voice in chapel, Lutheran and club governance.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento; Lebanese, Spanish and Italian storekeepers**: check how merchants and farm laborers stood in small steppe towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income data and the Welsh colony; mixed for discrimination studies; thin for steppe and canoe peoples.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun and colonial Spanish for status words; Welsh for colony election records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs and labels are recorded as usage only; surname experiments and cases touch living families.
- **Held for review:** for the reserved seats and women's suffrage, the electoral statutes themselves were set aside for Libra and the purpose of such rules for Capricorn; outcomes and accounts stay here.
