# Araucanía-Patagonia: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Araucanía and its old frontier towns, Valdivia, the lakes and Chiloé, Aysén, Magallanes, and Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz and Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of southern Chile, Chiloé and Patagonia; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam and Aonekko ʼaʼien in revitalization. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature, and how far the departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche and Wenteche families**: compare how converts, gender-crossing *machi* and returning urban youth are each received, in published testimony.
- **Pewenche of Alto Bío-Bío (comparison) and Lonquimay**: check how members who sided with or against the dams were treated afterward.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: check how island sorcery lore marks the odd neighbor, without taking the lore as fact.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Bariloche**: compare urban youth music scenes with *lof* expectations.
- **Aónikenk, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam descendants**: check whether each community's own histories name members who refused missions or settlement.
- **Chilote migrants in Punta Arenas**: compare what home islands and the city each count as odd.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare chapel members with those who left chapel life, in memoirs.
- **German-descended families of Valdivia; Croatian-descended families of Magallanes; Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check who married out, changed faith or language, and how families tell it.
- **Oil-boom newcomers, drifters and seasonal workers**: compare camp life with town expectations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich in legend and youth studies; thin and partial on gender departure among Mapuche, where outsiders' reports dominate.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for chroniclers; Mapudungun for song texts; Welsh for chapel origins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *machi* gender is a sensitive subject; take Mapuche accounts first. Outlaw legends can turn into stories about a group; keep them about persons.
- **Held for review:** a pointer to police files naming living people in drug or youth cases was considered and dropped; privacy outweighs its use here.
