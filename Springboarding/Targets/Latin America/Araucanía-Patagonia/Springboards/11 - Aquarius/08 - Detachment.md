# Araucanía-Patagonia: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; frame: the Mapuche country and lake district, Chiloé and the channels, Aysén and Magallanes, and the Argentine provinces from Neuquén and Río Negro through Chubut and Santa Cruz to Tierra del Fuego. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the remote south (Aysén, Magallanes, Santa Cruz, the Fuegian towns) and of the Araucanía and Chiloé; Mapudungun and *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam through revitalization work. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; valued as calm or objectivity, condemned as coldness or indifference.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pewenche of Icalma and Lonquimay**: check how families describe the winter withdrawal to lower ground, and who stays behind alone.
- **Lafkenche of the coast**: compare mourning songs and wakes recorded on the coast with those inland.
- **Williche of Osorno**: check whether elders' reserve with outsiders is recorded as respect or suspicion, and by whom.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén's oil country**: compare rotation work's absences in Mapuche and non-Mapuche households.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: mourning accounts come mostly from travelers; search for descendants' own words.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Edén; Yagán of Puerto Williams**: check how remoteness is spoken of from within, against outsiders' "end of the world" writing.
- **Chilote families**: compare men who left for Argentine stations with those who stayed, in letters and songs.
- **Welsh-descended families**: compare *hiraeth* verse with later generations' verse.
- **German-descended families of Puerto Octay; Croatian-descended families of Porvenir; Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check which emotions memoirs allow at deaths and departures.
- **Oil and station workers**: compare camp silence with town sociability.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin on emotion itself; mixed for isolation and work rotation; rich for census living-alone counts.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for Lenz's texts; Welsh for *hiraeth* verse; Victorian English for travelers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "reserved" and "cold" are outsiders' labels; record them as claims. Mourning customs belong to living families.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist practice of non-attachment in the south was weighed as a pointer; it is held here as a category for the author, with no community, center or text named.
