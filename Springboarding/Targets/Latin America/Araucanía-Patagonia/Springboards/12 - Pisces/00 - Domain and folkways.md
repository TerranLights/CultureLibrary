# Araucanía-Patagonia: The domain: the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America; worked over Wallmapu and the south on both slopes (Araucanía to Magallanes; Neuquén to Tierra del Fuego). **Language(s) for searching:** southern Chilean, Chilote and Patagonian Spanish; Mapudungun (Lafkenche, Pewenche, Nagche, Wenteche) and Williche *tsesungun*; Chubut Welsh; Llanquihue German; Magallanes Croatian; Sarmiento Afrikaans; revived Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Gününa küne. **Concept in one line:** how suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape are met, with the magic ways that draw lines between religion, healing, harm and luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche coast and Pewenche of the *pewen* country**: check whether the same words for harm appear on both.
- **Williche of Osorno and Chiloé**: set the 1880 trial's imagery beside Williche healing accounts from the same islands.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén and Río Negro**: compare where the sick are taken across the border, *machi* or hospital.
- **Aónikenk and Gününa küne descendants**: look for spirit and healing words in revival materials, as the communities publish them.
- **Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam**: outsiders wrote them off; seek their present tellings of the unseen.
- **Chilote families on the islands and in Punta Arenas**: check whether island beings travel with migrants.
- **Welsh-, German-, Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: compare chapel, parish and society records of illness and grief.
- **Oil-camp and sheep-station workers**: look for how isolation, drink and accident were handled.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and missionary writing and for Chiloé's lore; mixed for health statistics (region cuts vary); thin for canoe and steppe peoples in their own words. On the hidden and the unseen, the frame used here stays provisional, since the nation's lands are still the author's to settle; Alto Biobío, La Pampa, southern Buenos Aires, southern Mendoza and the Falklands / Malvinas serve only as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Spanish and Mapudungun for the confession manual; Welsh for gravestones; Yagán with living speakers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *machi* trance, *perimontun* visions, *pewma*, the Selk'nam *Hain* and Chilote *brujería* accounts are restricted in part; never tie sorcery accusations to a group or a living person.
- **Held for review:** a foreign psychiatric "culture-bound syndromes" survey was weighed and kept out, since it would rank southern healing as exotic before the healers speak.
