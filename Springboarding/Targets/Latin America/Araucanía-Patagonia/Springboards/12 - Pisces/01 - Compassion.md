# Araucanía-Patagonia: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the south (Temuco to Ushuaia, Chiloé included); Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Aonekko ʼaʼien, Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam where revival materials exist. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

English keywords: charity southern Chile; disaster relief Patagonia; mercy to captives colonial Chile; social assistance coverage by region.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche coastal communities after 1960**: check tsunami testimonies for who sheltered whom across Mapuche and settler lines.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay and Curarrehue**: look at winter-isolation relief by snowbound road and who decides it.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa**: compare Church aid with *kelluwün* among households.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén's colonies**: read provincial relief files beside family accounts of being "assisted".
- **Aónikenk families of Camusu Aike (Santa Cruz)**: check what aid records exist and who wrote them.
- **Yagán of Villa Ukika and Kawésqar of Puerto Edén**: ask which state programs reached them and how they were received.
- **Chilote households**: compare the *minga* to rebuild a burned house with a *bingo* for a hospital bill.
- **Welsh, German, Croatian and Boer congregations**: compare how each chapel or society relieved its own, and whether outsiders were helped.
- **Chilean migrants in Neuquén, Comodoro and Río Gallegos**: check whether Argentine relief reached the undocumented.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in disaster press and Church reports; mixed for giving surveys (national samples, thin southern cells); thin for Kawésqar, Yagán and Aónikenk accounts of being helped in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish of the colonial chronicles; Mapudungun for Coña's original text; Welsh for chapel minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disaster victims and the disabled children in telethon images are living people; appeals for the sick name families, so cite patterns, not persons.
- **Held for review:** a cross-national compassion-scale study was looked at for regional use; it gives only country scores, so it was left for a national comparison rather than counted here.
