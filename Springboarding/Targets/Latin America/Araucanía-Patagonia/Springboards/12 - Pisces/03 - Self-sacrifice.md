# Araucanía-Patagonia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** southern and Chilote Spanish; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán, Kawésqar, Selk'nam, Aonekko ʼaʼien, Gününa küne via revival programs. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche communities of Malleco and Ercilla**: compare who is remembered as *weichafe* in each community's own media.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: look for offerings tied to the *pewen* harvest.
- **Williche of San Juan de la Costa**: check memory of chiefs who gave up land or liberty in councils' defense.
- **Mapuche of Junín de los Andes and Neuquén**: set Laura Vicuña's cult beside local Mapuche views of it.
- **Aónikenk descendants of Santa Cruz**: search for remembered sacrifices in the "Conquest of the Desert" years, as families tell them.
- **Yagán and Kawésqar families**: ask what children were sent away or given up to missions and schools, from the families' side.
- **Chilote households**: compare the wife's years alone with the husband's years at the shearing sheds.
- **Welsh-descended families of the Chubut valley**: read the first-winter diaries against later chapel sermons on sacrifice.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: check how emigration is told across generations.
- **Oil, coal and sheep workers**: compare Río Turbio's memorials with Comodoro's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in war and disaster memorials and Church files; mixed in health data; thin on Native accounts of sacrifice, which sit in oral and community sources.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century Spanish verse; Mapudungun for *weichafe* and offering terms; Welsh for settler diaries; Afrikaans for Boer memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political deaths in the Araucanía are recent and contested; present each commemoration as one side's account and name no living person as responsible. *Ngillatun* offerings are partly restricted.
- **Held for review:** a nationalist textbook series praising soldiers' sacrifice was considered as a frame and set aside, since it would make one state's view of the border wars the voice of the whole south.
