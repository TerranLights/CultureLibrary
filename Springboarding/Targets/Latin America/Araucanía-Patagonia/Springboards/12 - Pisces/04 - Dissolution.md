# Araucanía-Patagonia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the Chilean south, Chiloé and Argentine Patagonia; Mapudungun in its regional forms and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar, Yagán, Selk'nam, Aonekko ʼaʼien and Gününa küne, whose revival is itself part of this concept. **Concept in one line** (proposed; the author decides): the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche communities divided into private plots after 1979**: compare community records before and after the division.
- **Lafkenche and Nagche congregations that are mostly Pentecostal**: check how worshippers describe trance against the *machi*'s.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: collect Caleuche tellings by island, Quinchao against the Big Island.
- **Mapuche of Río Negro's Línea Sur**: test language-shift findings from Chile against the eastern steppe.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Natales and Yagán of Puerto Williams**: compare how each revival program keeps speakers together.
- **Selk'nam descendants in Río Grande and Tolhuin**: check census self-identification against families' own accounts.
- **Welsh-descended families of the valley and of Trevelin**: ask when Welsh stopped being the home language in each.
- **German-descended families of Puerto Varas and Frutillar**: look for generational breaks in club minutes.
- **Croatian- and Boer-descended families**: compare surname and language shifts across two settler streams.
- **Chilote migrants in Río Gallegos and Comodoro**: check how their children name their origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language shift and settler assimilation; mixed for dementia care; thin and partly restricted for trance and ego loss in ritual.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for trance and identity terms; Welsh for colony press; German and Croatian for club and family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *küymi* and other *machi* knowledge; Pentecostal and Mapuche relations are tense in some communities, so describe practices, not one side's view of the other.
- **Held for review:** a pharmacology study of a South American plant brew was weighed as a measure of ego loss, but it says nothing about this nation's people, so it stayed out.
