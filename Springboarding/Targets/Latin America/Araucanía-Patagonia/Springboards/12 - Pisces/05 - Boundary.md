# Araucanía-Patagonia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish on both sides of the cordillera, Chilote speech included; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Yagán, Kawésqar, Selk'nam and Aonekko ʼaʼien for land and sea names. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mapuche of Neuquén's Aluminé valley and of the Chilean Wenteche lands**: check the same lineage names on both slopes for remembered crossings.
- **Lafkenche of the coast and Williche of Los Ríos**: compare which sites each names as *ngen* ground.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: look at *veranada* boundaries between summer and winter grounds.
- **Aónikenk descendants of Santa Cruz and Selk'nam descendants of Tierra del Fuego**: compare family memory of the first fences.
- **Yagán of Puerto Williams and Ushuaia**: set the families' cross-channel ties against state crossing rules.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Natales and Puerto Edén**: check sea and coast names claimed against salmon-farm leases.
- **Chilote families**: compare visiting customs on Quinchao, Lemuy and the Big Island.
- **Welsh-descended chapels of Gaiman and Trevelin**: look at how strictly each kept the Sabbath, decade by decade.
- **German-descended farms of Llanquihue and Croatian-descended families of Porvenir**: compare fence and land papers.
- **Chilean residents of Río Gallegos and Comodoro**: check how often they cross, and on what errands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state borders and land papers; mixed for household and crowding data; thin for personal-space and greeting customs, which need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Mapudungun for site and slope names; Welsh for Sabbath records; Yagán and Kawésqar place names through community programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land and sacred-site disputes are live; the border itself is a matter of state treaties, and nothing here suggests where any line should run. Some *ngen* sites are not to be described in detail.
- **Held for review:** a geopolitics manual on "the southern frontier" from a military school was weighed as a source on boundary thinking; it speaks for one state's strategists only, so it was kept back for a later, labeled use.
