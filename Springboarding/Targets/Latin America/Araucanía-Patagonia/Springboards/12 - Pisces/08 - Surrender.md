# Araucanía-Patagonia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the Araucanía, Los Lagos, Chiloé and the Patagonian provinces; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans (where the Boer war's words for yielding survive); Gününa küne and Aonekko ʼaʼien for the eastern steppe's memory. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher, with release and letting go.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | *rendirse*; *entregarse*; *resignarse* | to give up in a fight; to give oneself over; to accept what cannot change | *entregarse* covers the enemy, God and the lover; pair with "Sayhueque" or "Señor" to split the senses |

English keywords: Sayhueque surrender 1885; strikers' surrender Santa Cruz 1921; dying at home southern Chile; altar call Pentecostal Chile.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche and Nagche families**: compare *nütram* of negotiated submission with those of resistance.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay**: check how the 1934 rising's end is remembered, as surrender or as massacre.
- **Mapuche and Tehuelche families of Neuquén and Río Negro**: set Sayhueque's surrender beside family accounts of the colonies that followed.
- **Gününa küne and Aónikenk descendants of Chubut and Santa Cruz**: look for memory of the last free bands.
- **Chilote fishing families**: compare sea sayings on Quinchao with those of Calbuco.
- **Welsh-descended chapels of Gaiman and Trevelin**: compare hymn choices at funerals.
- **Boer-descended families of Sarmiento**: ask how the war's end is told at home.
- **Croatian-descended families of Porvenir**: check whether letters record giving up Austro-Hungarian ties after 1918.
- **Shearers' and strikers' descendants of Santa Cruz**: set families' accounts of 1921 against the regiments' reports.
- **Residents of Villa Santa Lucía and Chaitén's surroundings**: compare those who left with those who stayed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military and labor history; mixed for health data on dying; thin for Native words of yielding and acceptance, which need speakers.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish; Mapudungun with teachers, not dictionaries alone; Afrikaans and Welsh for family and chapel sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1885 and 1921 are contested; give each side's account and settle no blame. End-of-life choices in families are private: cite patterns, never persons.
- **Held for review:** a medical-sociology paper labeling southern patients "fatalistic" was considered; its label would be taken as a finding, so it stays out until the patients' own accounts are found.
