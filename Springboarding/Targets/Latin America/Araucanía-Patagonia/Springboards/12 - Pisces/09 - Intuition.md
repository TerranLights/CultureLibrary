# Araucanía-Patagonia: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of the south, including the *campo* speech of the steppe and Chiloé's sea talk; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar and Yagán for knowledge of the channels; Aonekko ʼaʼien for the steppe. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lafkenche of the Budi lake and Toltén coast**: check how the flood narrative is told by families who lived through tsunamis.
- **Pewenche of Lonquimay and Mapuche *crianceros* of northern Neuquén**: compare the spring-move judgment on each slope.
- **Williche of the Osorno coast**: look for sea-reading lore beside Chilote sayings.
- **Kawésqar of Puerto Edén and Yagán of Puerto Williams**: compare channel knowledge kept in each language program.
- **Aónikenk descendants of Santa Cruz**: search revival materials for steppe tracking words.
- **Chilote fishermen of Quellón and Dalcahue**: collect weather sayings from working crews, not only folklorists.
- **Welsh-descended farmers of the lower valley**: check flood-reading lore before the 1899 flood.
- **German-descended farmers of Llanquihue and Croatian-descended families of Magallanes**: compare harvest and wind sayings.
- **Sheep-station *ovejeros* and oil-field workers**: set the shepherd's eye beside safety drills on the rigs.
- **El Chaltén and Puerto Natales guides**: compare local and visiting guides' weather judgment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore and travelers' notes; mixed for professional intuition (nursing, guiding); thin for Native knowledge in its own words, which sits with speakers and programs.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish; Mapudungun with fluent speakers for *perimontun* and *pewma*; Kawésqar and Yagán via community programs; Welsh for family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *perimontun*, *pewma* and *machi* knowledge are restricted in part; Chilote omen lore can slide into sorcery talk, so never tie it to a family or living person.
- **Held for review:** left out on purpose: business-coaching books on "intuitive leadership", written for capital-city boardrooms and silent on the south's own ways of judging by feel.
