# Araucanía-Patagonia: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** police-page and street Spanish from Temuco to Río Gallegos; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Selk'nam, Kawésqar and Yagán only as communities publish. **Concept in one line** (named by the author): what the culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law itself (codes, courts, policing) belongs to Libra and Capricorn.

English keywords: banditry southern Andes nineteenth century; Mapuche conflict framing terrorism; femicide statistics Patagonia; free-zone smuggling Punta Arenas.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nagche communities of Ercilla and Collipulli**: compare who is labeled offender in regional press with how community media name the same events.
- **Lafkenche of Carahue and Teodoro Schmidt**: check how wood theft and land recovery are told by communities and by contractors.
- **Mapuche of Bariloche's lakes and Villa Mascardi**: set the 2017 events against urban Bariloche opinion pieces.
- **Williche of Osorno**: look at *El Austral de Osorno*'s police page for Williche names and how they appear.
- **Selk'nam descendants and Aónikenk families**: ask how the killings of their ancestors are named, crime or war.
- **Chilote families**: compare island sayings about thieves with mainland police statistics.
- **Welsh-descended chapels**: trace which sins brought discipline in the lower valley against the Andes.
- **German-descended Valdivia and Croatian-descended Punta Arenas**: compare early settler press on theft and violence.
- **Free-zone towns of Punta Arenas, Porvenir and Río Grande**: check who calls small smuggling a crime.
- **Oil towns of Comodoro and Añelo**: compare boom-time fears with residents' accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in press and official counts; mixed for how communities define wrongs; thin for Native terms for crime outside contested political speech.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish; Mapudungun for moral vocabulary; Welsh for chapel minutes; German and Croatian for early press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Araucanía conflict, Villa Mascardi and the Selk'nam killings are contested ground: present each account as an account, name no living person as guilty, and never let a label describe a people.
- **Held for review:** set aside on principle: any "criminal profiling" study that sorts rural offenders by ethnicity, since it would read one people as a crime group before research begins.
