# Araucanía-Patagonia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Araucanía-Patagonia, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of a land of quakes, volcanoes and wind, from the Araucanía and Chiloé to the Patagonian provinces; Mapudungun and Williche *tsesungun*; Welsh; German; Croatian; Afrikaans; Kawésqar, Yagán and Selk'nam for sea and night lore, as communities share it. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wenteche families near Villarrica and Pucón**: check how volcano fear is told in Mapuche and in settler families.
- **Lafkenche of Toltén and Queule**: compare fear of the sea after 1960 with the 2010 alarms.
- **Mapuche communities of Malleco**: set children's accounts in rights reports beside parents' and teachers' accounts.
- **Williche of Chiloé**: list which fear beings are named on which islands.
- **Mapuche of Neuquén**: look for La Escuelita testimony from Mapuche detainees.
- **Aónikenk descendants**: search for night beings and steppe dangers in revival materials.
- **Kawésqar and Yagán families**: compare sea fears in the two canoe traditions.
- **Welsh-descended families of Epuyén's region and of the lower valley**: check fear of contagion in 2018 against chapel memory of earlier epidemics.
- **Chilean migrants in Río Gallegos**: ask how 1978 felt from inside a family of the "other side".
- **Croatian- and German-descended families**: compare letters on earthquakes, fires and isolation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and state terror; mixed for fear-of-crime and anxiety data in the south; thin for Native fear lore in Native words.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century Spanish; Mapudungun for *epew*; Welsh for sermons; Kawésqar through community programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chilote and Mapuche fear beings touch sorcery belief; Mapuche children and police operations, and state-terror sites, are contested or traumatic: survivors' and families' accounts first.
- **Held for review:** nothing named was set aside; the one candidate, a Santiago gun-ownership survey, fails as a measure of southern fear because its sample stops at the capital.
