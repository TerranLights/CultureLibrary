*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires against Córdoba, Rosario, Tucumán and Mendoza:** check refusal and interruption patterns in recorded speech outside the capital, since most politeness corpora are porteño.
- **Women and men:** check what advice columns, schools and families told girls and boys about speaking up, from Storni's era to the present.
- **Workers, delegates and managers:** compare who may object on the shop floor and in offices.
- **Qom, Wichí and Pilagá of the Chaco and Formosa:** check who speaks for a community in assemblies and before officials, and in what order.
- **Kolla and Diaguita communities:** check how claims are voiced in marches, assemblies and petitions.
- **Afro-Argentines and payadores:** check the nineteenth-century press and the payada for claims of standing.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan traders at markets such as La Salada:** check haggling and complaint practice against fixed-price shops.
- **Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese community bodies:** check how their newspapers answered slurs and defamation.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa and Evangelical congregations:** check sermons and teaching on humility against testimony as speech.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires talk, tango and protest; mixed for provincial speech; thin for Pilagá and Moqoit assembly practice.
- **Languages needed:** Rioplatense Spanish and *lunfardo* glossaries (José Gobello, Academia Porteña del Lunfardo); Guaraní for Litoral talk; Qom for community speech events.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Peronist labor history, the 2001 protests and Formosa land conflicts are contested; give each as an account. *Viveza criolla* is a loaded label and must not frame findings. Consumer-protection law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the self-help pointers considered here are by Argentine authors; no pointer for speaking up or saying no fell under a standing rule.
