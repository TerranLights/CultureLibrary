# Argentina (without Patagonia): Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (Rioplatense and provincial); Guaraní; Quechua of the Kolla and Bolivian communities; Yiddish and Judeo-Spanish; Arabic; Armenian; Korean, Chinese and Japanese for the East Asian communities. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteños and the provinces:** check whether *porteño* and *del interior* answer "who are you" before the national label does.
- **Pampa and Santa Fe farm families of immigrant descent:** check where the *gringo* label is self-applied and where it is imposed.
- **Kolla of Jujuy and Salta; Diaguita of Tucumán and Catamarca; Huarpe of Cuyo:** check community self-identification since the census questions and the disputes over sacred sites such as Quilmes.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá and Mbyá:** check self-names against the names outsiders used in older records.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines of Dock Sud:** check how the two communities name themselves and each other.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian, Volga German, Ukrainian and Polish communities:** check the order of hyphenated labels by generation in each community press.
- **Okinawan, Korean and Chinese communities:** check how *nikkei*, *gyopo* and *huárén* are used against "Argentine".
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check identity at feasts against identity at work and school.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check whether congregation or nation comes first in self-description.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for immigration and Buenos Aires; mixed for provincial identity; thin for Huarpe and Pilagá self-description.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Yiddish for the IWO holdings; Arabic and Armenian for community press; Guaraní and Quechua for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the appropriated children, the AMIA bombing (1994), Peronism and the Malvinas / Falklands (a contested name; comparison only) shape national identity talk and are contested: give accounts. Name, citizenship and Indigenous-registration law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no identity pointer was set aside; the diaspora and religious sources considered are Argentine or regional and fall under no standing rule.
