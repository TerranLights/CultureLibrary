*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Santa Fe and Córdoba farm towns against the Conurbano:** check whether founding stories credit families, cooperatives or the state.
- **Italian, Spanish and Volga German founders of colonies and workshops:** check how each community tells its first businesses.
- **Syrian-Lebanese families of Tucumán, Salta and Santiago del Estero:** check how peddling, shops and credit were organized from the first generation.
- **Jewish colonists of Entre Ríos and merchants of Once:** compare colony and city routes into business.
- **Korean and Chinese families in Flores, Avellaneda and Belgrano:** check how first businesses were financed and staffed.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan workers in horticulture and construction:** check the steps from wage work to own work.
- **Qom, Wichí and Mbyá craft producers:** check who starts a cooperative or a sale point, and on whose terms.
- **Kolla and Andean communities:** check how *minka* and *ayni* work parties are called and by whom.
- **Women in community kitchens and markets:** check who is credited for starting them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for immigrant founders and firm statistics; mixed for informal *rebusque* work; thin for Indigenous enterprise outside NGO reports.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Latin for some Jesuit letters; Italian and Yiddish for community press; Korean for business associations; Quechua and Guaraní for work-party terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crisis-era narratives (2001-02) and social programs are politically contested; give accounts. Business and labor regulation goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; translated hustle-culture and motivation books were weighed for this concept and left out as not Argentine, not held under a rule.
