## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Santiago del Estero, Córdoba and the cities of the old Tucumán:** check which founding each city celebrates and how the older Indigenous presence enters the story.
- **Kolla and Diaguita communities of Jujuy, Salta and Catamarca:** check whether the year opens with Pachamama in August, the June solstice or the civil January.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** compare origin narratives people by people, not as one Chaco story.
- **Mbyá of Misiones:** check what can be shared about first-fruits and naming rites before any use.
- **Italian, Spanish and Volga German descendants of the Pampa and Entre Ríos:** check arrival stories in family memory against arrival records.
- **Jewish, Armenian, Chinese and Korean communities:** check which new year each household keeps and which stays public.
- **Evangelical and Catholic congregations of the Conurbano:** compare conversion and baptism as new starts.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan migrants:** check arrival and first-year accounts in community media.
- **Afro-Argentines:** check how community histories place their beginning against the founding story of the city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for founding records and immigration; mixed for household new-year customs; thin for Pilagá and Moqoit origin narratives outside the old collections.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; colonial Spanish paleography; Italian; Quechua ritual terms; Qom and Wichí to check translated narratives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** founding stories carry conquest and the Chaco campaigns; the dictatorship and its end are contested; some Indigenous origin knowledge is not public. Emigration after 2001 to Spain and Italy is diaspora, comparison only.
- **Held for review:** the Lunar New Year sources may lead to temple rites; any such Buddhist pointer is held as a category, none named.
