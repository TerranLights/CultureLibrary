*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Córdoba, Rosario, Tucumán and Santiago del Estero:** compare how each city's uprising is remembered, celebrated or condemned.
- **Buenos Aires and the Conurbano:** check whether *cacerolazo* and *piquete* draw different classes and different words for anger.
- **Diaguita and Kolla communities:** check how colonial resistance is told in community histories against provincial textbooks.
- **Wichí and Qom:** check each people's moral terms for anger and how disputes are cooled.
- **Football fans of rival clubs:** check what fans themselves count as legitimate anger.
- **Women's and feminist groups:** check how anger is framed as collective claim.
- **Jewish community bodies and AMIA relatives:** compare the voices of different relatives' groups.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian migrants:** check how they describe being targets of anger and how they answer it.
- **Afro-Argentines:** check community accounts of words like *quilombo* and of slurs.
- **Evangelical and Catholic congregations:** compare teaching on anger as sin and as righteous outrage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest and football violence; mixed for household anger and child-rearing; thin for Indigenous emotion terms beyond a few ethnographies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; Quechua for Andean sources; Wichí and Qom for emotion vocabulary; Yiddish for older AMIA-related community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the AMIA investigation, *escraches*, the 2001 crisis, gender violence and xenophobia are contested; give accounts. Criminal law and court cases go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing; anger-management and calming practices were weighed, and only Argentine clinical and church programs came to mind, none under a rule.
