# Argentina (without Patagonia): Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and *lunfardo*; the Spanish of the Northwest; Quechua and Aymara; Guaraní; Qom and Wichí (for possessive grammar); Yiddish; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and what they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteño apartments against Pampa and Córdoba farmhouses:** check where things are stored and how space limits keeping.
- **Kolla and Diaguita families of the Northwest:** check animal marking and the goods of the dead against urban practice in Salta and San Salvador de Jujuy.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each language's possessive grammar against how lending and keeping are described; ethnographies differ by people.
- **Mbyá of Misiones and Guaraní-speaking Correntinos:** check whether *che* + noun covers all belongings or only some.
- **Jewish, Armenian and Volga German families:** check objects carried in migration and survivors' heirlooms, community by community.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan households in the Conurbano:** check what is sent home and what is brought back.
- **Afro-Argentine families:** check estate inventories for enslaved ancestors listed as goods, and the community's own view of those records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires markets, pawn and crime statistics; mixed for colonial probate; thin for possession among the Chaco peoples outside grammar studies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua for the Puna; Qom and Wichí linguistic literature (mostly in Spanish); Yiddish for survivors' papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burials and human remains in museums are under restitution claims by Indigenous communities; handle with the communities' own statements. Enslaved people in colonial inventories are a painful record. Lost-property and theft law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no pointer was set aside; the grave-goods and museum pointers were weighed against restricted-knowledge concerns and kept with the caution above.
