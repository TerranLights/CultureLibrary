## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteño and Conurbano workers against the downwardly mobile middle class:** check whether worth is spoken of through work, through goods, or through schooling.
- **Pampa and Córdoba towns:** check gossip and reputation against city anonymity.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** check old family names and colonial descent against Kolla and Diaguita accounts of being looked down on.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check how Guaraní speakers describe pride in, or shame of, the language.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check bilingual teachers' and elders' own accounts, people by people.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check community press and associations for self-description against outside labels.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian migrants:** check discrimination reports against each community's own media.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian and Korean families:** check what honor words each uses and in which setting.
- **Men out of work and older people out of work:** check unemployment studies for how loss of a role is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires class history, psychology and literature; mixed for provinces; thin for self-worth in Indigenous languages beyond school projects.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní; Qom and Wichí for teachers' materials; Yiddish and Arabic for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class and color slurs and the Peronist "dignity" language are contested; quote neither as a group's trait. Youth suicide data, especially among Indigenous communities of Salta, needs care. Discrimination law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the pointers on psychology and on minority self-description were checked against the standing rules and none required setting aside.
