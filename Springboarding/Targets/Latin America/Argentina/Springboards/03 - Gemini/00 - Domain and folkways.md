## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteños and the Conurbano:** check whether capital-based address-form surveys hold where families came from the provinces, Paraguay or Bolivia.
- **Córdoba:** check its intonation and *cordobés* joke tradition as their own system, not a Buenos Aires variant.
- **The Northwest (Tucumán, Salta, Jujuy, Catamarca, La Rioja) and Santiago del Estero:** check where *usted* and *tú* survive beside *vos*, and where Quichua shapes greetings.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní-Spanish home bilingualism and its place in schools.
- **Cuyo and La Pampa:** check rural speech surveys and Huarpe and Ranquel language recovery as separate cases.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá, Mbyá:** check each people's own teachers and programs; pooled "Indigenous" school data hides them.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian, Ukrainian, Polish, Volga German and Mennonite communities:** check heritage-language schools and when each moved to Spanish.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan, Peruvian, Korean and Chinese families:** check home language against school language for the children.
- **Deaf Argentines:** compare LSA communities by city; Deaf schools may have kept different signs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires speech and schooling history; mixed for provincial Spanish after the 1960s; thin for Moqoit, Pilagá and Huarpe speech ways.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní and Quichua for the two largest home-language areas; LSA through Deaf researchers; Yiddish and Arabic for community school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous-language teaching sits inside recognition disputes; program reports are the state's account, community statements theirs. The 1976-83 dictatorship's school book bans are contested ground; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the pointers weighed for talk, schooling and neighbors (dialect surveys, mission printing, transport data, Deaf bodies) fell under none of the standing rules.
