## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteño speakers by class:** check the Norma Culta recordings against working-class and *villa* speech; one corpus is not one way of talking.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check whether address forms and pause length in elders' talk differ from the Litoral's, using regional recordings.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check how Quichua-Spanish speakers move between languages within one conversation.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check where Guaraní is the language of jokes, quarrels or intimacy and Spanish the public one.
- **Qom and Wichí in cities (Rosario, Derqui, Resistencia):** compare speech to children in town and in home communities.
- **Ranquel and Huarpe descendants:** check community voices on the parley tradition before using outsiders' accounts.
- **Italian- and Spanish-descended families:** check the gesture repertoire against Meo Zilio's sources, generation by generation.
- **Deaf Argentines:** check where LSA interpreters are present and absent (clinics, courts, news).
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families:** check children's role as go-betweens with schools and offices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires speech and media; mixed for recorded provincial conversation; thin for Moqoit, Pilagá and Huarpe speech norms.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quichua for the Tucumán visita and Santiago speech; Guaraní for Litoral code-switching; LSA for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Peronist oratory is contested ground; read every analysis as an account. Free-speech limits and media regulation go to Libra.
