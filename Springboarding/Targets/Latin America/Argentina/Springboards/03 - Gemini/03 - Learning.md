## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rosario and Santa Fe:** check the Cossettini tradition in local schools against the national pattern.
- **Buenos Aires province's rural schools and the Pampa:** check how one-room and *escuela rural* teaching survived against urban schools.
- **The Northwest and the Puna:** compare learning at home (herding, weaving, the loom) with school learning in Kolla and *criollo* households.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check how Quichua-speaking children meet a Spanish-only classroom, and what teachers report.
- **Misiones:** compare Mbyá villages, Ukrainian and Polish Saturday schools, and German-descended colonies as separate learning settings.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check the bilingual teaching aides' training and what Qom, Wichí and Pilagá families say about it.
- **The Entre Ríos Jewish colonies:** check the move from *kheyder* to the state school, and when.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check their own schools and what Plautdietsch-speaking children learn at home.
- **Italian-, Spanish- and Volga German-descended farm families:** check how children learned the farm.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the history of schooling and recent national tests; mixed for home and workshop learning; thin for Moqoit and Huarpe children's learning.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua and Aymara for the 1584 catechism; Guaraní and Mbyá for Misiones; Yiddish for the colony schools; Plautdietsch for Mennonite households.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Mbyá and Wichí teaching is not for outsiders; ask the communities first. Dictatorship-era school purges and Peronist-era readers are contested; present each account as an account. School law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the candidates for learning (primers, campaigns, test data, home-learning ethnographies) fell under no standing rule.
