*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Córdoba:** check the observatory and university tradition against public interest measured in the province.
- **Luján and the Pampa towns:** check local fossil hunting and town museums as amateur curiosity.
- **Jujuy and the Quebrada de Humahuaca:** compare archaeologists' accounts of Tilcara with Kolla communities' views on digging and display.
- **Salta and Catamarca:** check how Diaguita and Calchaquí communities treat questions from researchers about sites and burials.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí and Moqoit communities' own rules on outsiders' questions, and their research agreements.
- **Misiones:** compare Mbyá knowledge of plants with the Jesuit herbal and with modern ethnobotany, asking whose knowledge each records.
- **Small towns with *bibliotecas populares*:** check founding papers to see who started each library and why.
- **Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian families:** check whether the polite limits on questions to strangers and in-laws differ from those in Spanish- and Italian-descended homes.
- **Conurbano schools:** compare science-fair entries from poorer and richer districts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science policy, surveys and the Buenos Aires book world; mixed for provincial museums; thin for Indigenous peoples' own rules on asking.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Latin and Guaraní for the Jesuit herbal; Quechua for Puna community statements; Arabic and Armenian for family etiquette sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains in museums and plant knowledge are claimed by Indigenous communities; some is not open to outsiders. Science funding is a contested political topic; read each side as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; the museums, surveys, magazines and fairs considered for curiosity raised no standing rule.
