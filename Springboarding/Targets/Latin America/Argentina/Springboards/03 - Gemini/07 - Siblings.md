# Argentina (without Patagonia): Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish; Guaraní and Mbyá; Quechua; Yiddish; Arabic; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check the *lobizón* belief and the seventh-child custom where folk accounts place them.
- **Guaraní and Mbyá speakers:** check how kin terms for elder and younger siblings are used today against the 1639 entries.
- **Kolla households of Jujuy and Salta:** check the four Quechua sibling terms in use, and which sibling stays with the herd.
- **Qom, Wichí and Pilagá families:** check whether cousins are called siblings, people by people, in current ethnography.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check older siblings minding younger ones while parents migrate for harvest work.
- **Buenos Aires middle-class families:** check the shift to one or two children and what parents say about it.
- **Italian- and Spanish-descended farm families of the Pampa:** check how land passed among brothers and sisters.
- **Jewish families of Once and the Entre Ríos colonies:** check memoirs for siblings who stayed in the colony against those who left for the city.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check colony records on sibling-set size and inheritance.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families:** check sibling chains in migration and in shared workshops.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for family demography and care surveys; mixed for folk belief and literature; thin for Pilagá, Moqoit and Huarpe kinship.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní for the Montoya entries; Quechua for Puna kin terms; Yiddish for colony memoirs; Plautdietsch for Mennonite records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Guaraní sacred narratives belong to their tellers; use what communities publish. Adoption files are private. Inheritance among siblings as law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the kin-term, foundling, adoption and folklore pointers weighed for siblings met every standing rule.
