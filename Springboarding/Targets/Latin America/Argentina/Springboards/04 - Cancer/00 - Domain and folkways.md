# Argentina (without Patagonia): The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; written for the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish (*voseo*, *lunfardo*, *cocoliche*) and the Spanish of Córdoba, Cuyo and the Northwest; Guaraní (Corrientes, Misiones) and Mbyá; Santiago Quichua; Quechua and Aymara; Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá; Yiddish; Arabic; Armenian; Ukrainian and Polish; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** what is home and who is family: household forms, kin, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** compare apartment, *casa chorizo* and self-built households within one metropolis.
- **The Pampa gringa (Santa Fe, Buenos Aires province, La Pampa):** check whether colony-grid farm households kept their founders' family layout.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní household words against census household categories.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check how households with migrant members are counted when half the year they are away.
- **Salta and Jujuy (Quebrada, Puna):** check whether a household is one house or a set of houses and corrals.
- **Cuyo (Mendoza, San Juan, San Luis):** check how earthquake rebuilding changed house forms.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each people's own account of residence against outsiders' codes.
- **Jewish, Armenian, Syrian-Lebanese, Ukrainian, Polish and Volga German families:** check memoirs for the house left behind against the house built here.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian families:** check shared-lot households, and who is counted in them.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check colony house plans as their own building tradition.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban household statistics and architecture; mixed for rural interior households; thin for the Chaco peoples' own descriptions of the home.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní and Quechua for the two largest home-language areas; Yiddish and Arabic for immigrant family papers; Ukrainian and Polish for Misiones colony records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** census manuscripts name people; follow archive rules. Families broken up under the 1976-83 dictatorship are contested ground; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** household altars of one migrant community, held as a category only (Buddhist practice, an open question for the author); no other pointer was set aside.
