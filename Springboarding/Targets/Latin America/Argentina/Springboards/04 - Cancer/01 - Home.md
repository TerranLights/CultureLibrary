# Argentina (without Patagonia): Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; written for the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish; Mbyá and Corrientes Guaraní; Quechua and Aymara; Yiddish and Judeo-Spanish; Arabic; Armenian; Korean and Japanese. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Renters in Buenos Aires city against owners in the Conurbano:** check whether "home" in surveys and talk means the same for both.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check farmhouses emptied as families move to town, and what "back home" then refers to.
- **Cuyo:** check adobe houses with grape arbors against post-earthquake building codes, house by house.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** check where one family keeps a town house and a herding house, and which one is called home.
- **Mbyá of Misiones and Qom, Wichí and Pilagá of the Chaco:** check each people's own words for the home place against the land titles they hold or claim.
- **Armenian, Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese families:** check memoirs for which house, here or abroad, is called home across generations.
- **Korean, Japanese and Chinese families:** check how the parents' hometown is spoken of by Argentine-born children.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families in self-built barrios:** check the house built room by room as a home in progress.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing policy and tango; mixed for rural houses; thin for the Chaco peoples' own accounts of home, published mostly through outsiders.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Mbyá for the *tekoa*; Quechua and Aymara for house blessings; Yiddish, Arabic, Armenian and Korean for migrants' "old home" writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous home places are tied to land claims; use communities' own statements. Evictions name families; work from aggregates. Tenancy as law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside here; the home-place pointers weighed (state housing, tango, mission ruins, migrant hometowns) raised no standing-rule question.
