*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; written for the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish with its Italian loans (*cocoliche*) and interior Spanish; Quechua and Aymara; Yiddish; Arabic; Plautdietsch; Qom and Wichí through their dictionaries. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Porteño middle-class families:** check how small households stay tied to grandparents through meals and childcare.
- **Italian- and Spanish-descended families of the Pampa:** check whether *paesano* and kin networks still run through towns founded by chain migration.
- **Córdoba and the Northwest (Tucumán, Salta, Catamarca, La Rioja):** check godparent choice and the reach of *compadrazgo* against Buenos Aires.
- **Kolla families of Jujuy and Salta:** check *ayllu* ties across Puna households and town houses.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each people's kin terms and residence rules in their own dictionaries and teachers' writing.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check family memory of lineage hidden or recovered.
- **Jewish families of Once and the Entre Ríos colonies, and Syrian-Lebanese families of the Northwest:** check family firms and marriage within the community over generations.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian families:** check kin spread across two countries and who counts as household.
- **Same-sex couples and their children since 2010:** check how they name their families in surveys and memoir.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check how far *Frintschoft* reaches inside the colony.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and immigrant family history; mixed for current kin networks outside Buenos Aires; thin for Chaco kin systems in the peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua and Aymara for Andean kin groups; Yiddish and Arabic for family papers; Plautdietsch for colony records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genetic records of appropriated children are restricted and their story is contested ground; present each account as an account. Family law (filiation, adoption, divorce) goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; the kin, lineage and ritual-kin pointers weighed (chronicles, parish demography, genetic records, plays) fell under no standing rule.
