**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; written for the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and street slang; interior Spanish; Guaraní (for the mission records); Yiddish and German for refugee aid; Arabic and Spanish for the Syrian visa program. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare state shelters, church shelters and homeless-led centers on rules and who stays away.
- **The Conurbano (La Matanza, Quilmes):** check how land occupations of the 1980s turned into neighborhoods.
- **Rosario and Córdoba:** check street counts and settlement surveys against the capital's.
- **The Litoral (Chaco, Corrientes, Santa Fe, Entre Ríos):** check flood evacuation as recurring family experience.
- **Santiago del Estero and Tucumán:** check rural houses and seasonal workers' lodging at harvest sites.
- **Qom and Wichí in cities:** check urban barrios formed by migrants from the Chaco as shelter on arrival.
- **Jewish, Armenian and Syrian refugees across a century:** check how each community's aid bodies took newcomers in.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian migrants:** check rented rooms in *inquilinatos* and *hoteles* as first shelter.
- **Catholic and Evangelical congregations:** compare church shelters' rules and reach.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settlements and street counts in Buenos Aires; mixed for disaster shelter; thin for rural and Indigenous shelter in people's own words.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** homeless counts and evictions are disputed; set each count beside its method. Women's shelters keep locations secret; use aggregates only. Asylum law goes to Libra.
