## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check foundling records and today's children's homes as one long series.
- **Tucumán's sugar towns:** check how people speak of being left after 1966, generation by generation.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check towns on closed railway branches for who stayed and why.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check children and elders left at home during seasonal migration, and whether families call it abandonment.
- **Corrientes, Misiones and the Northwest:** compare *guacho* and *tyre'ỹ* in use, insult against tenderness.
- **Qom, Wichí and Pilagá communities:** check whether they describe neglect by the state as abandonment, in their own statements.
- **Afro-Argentine families:** check how the account of a people's "disappearance" is answered as being left out of the record; contested ground.
- **Jewish families of Once and Armenian families:** check orphans' and survivors' memoirs for being left and being taken in.
- **Paraguayan and Bolivian families:** check in migration studies whether children stay with kin across the border, and how families name it.
- **Elders across regions:** check living alone and care-home placement against what families say they owe.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundlings and appropriated children; mixed for depopulated towns; thin for abandonment as rural Indigenous families define it.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua for *wakcha* and its history; Guaraní; Yiddish for community orphanage papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** case files of children in care and of appropriated children are closed; their story is contested ground, so present each account as an account. "Abandono de persona" and desertion of the home as law go to Libra.
