# Argentina (without Patagonia): Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America; written for the provinces north of Patagonia. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish; Italian-Spanish immigrant speech; Quechua and Aymara; Guaraní and Mbyá; Yiddish; Armenian. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city middle-class mothers:** check age at first birth and paid work against mothers in the Conurbano.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Kolla families:** check birth customs at home and in hospital, and the grandmother's place beside the mother.
- **Santiago del Estero and the Chaco:** check adolescent motherhood figures and how families speak of them.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Marian and folk devotions tied to mothers (Itatí, local shrines).
- **San Juan and Cuyo:** check the Difunta Correa devotion as told by mothers who keep it.
- **Qom, Wichí, Mbyá and Moqoit:** check each people's own birth and mothering teachings, through community health workers.
- **Italian- and Spanish-descended families:** check the immigrant mother in memoirs and novels against current mothering.
- **Jewish and Armenian families:** check the mother as keeper of language and memory after migration and genocide.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families:** check, where studies exist, mothers' accounts of Buenos Aires maternity wards and of raising children with kin on both sides of the border.
- **Mothers' movements:** compare the Plaza de Mayo, Ituzaingó and anti-drug mothers' groups on how each claims standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for maternalism history and mothers' movements; mixed for regional birth practice; thin for Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá mothering in their own words.
