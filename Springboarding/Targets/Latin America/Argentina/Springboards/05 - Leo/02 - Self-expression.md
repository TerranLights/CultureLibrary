# Argentina (without Patagonia): Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and its slang; the Spanish of the Northwest; Corrientes Guaraní; Qom; Quechua; Yiddish; Arabic; Korean. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check how therapy talk, theater and rock spread across classes and neighborhoods.
- **The Conurbano:** compare *cumbia villera* audiences with church youth groups for what each lets young people say.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check youth scenes and tribes outside the capital.
- **Corrientes and Entre Ríos:** check the *sapukái* and dance-floor display against everyday reserve.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** compare the masked carnival with everyday rules of speech in the same towns.
- **Santiago del Estero and Tucumán:** check folk song in Quichua and Spanish as an outlet for feeling.
- **Cuyo and La Pampa:** check small-town expectations of reserve.
- **Qom and Wichí:** check choirs, radio and school writing as channels each people chose for itself.
- **Mbyá Guaraní:** check which songs a community lets outsiders record.
- **Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese communities:** check theater and verse as their own channels, and how they changed between generations.
- **Korean and Chinese communities:** check young people's style and singing rooms in Flores and Belgrano.
- **LGBTQ Argentines:** check expression in the capital against provincial towns, by decade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theater, music and youth culture in Buenos Aires; mixed for provincial youth; thin for Indigenous peoples' own channels outside choirs and radio.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with youth slang; Guaraní for *chamamé*; Qom for the choir repertoire; Yiddish for the stage press; Arabic for verse; Korean for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** censorship and blacklists of 1976-83 are contested ground; present each account as an account. Some Indigenous songs are not for public recording. Free-speech law belongs to Libra; point there.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; the protest-art, therapy-culture, youth-tribe and community-stage pointers weighed for showing the inner self met every standing rule.
