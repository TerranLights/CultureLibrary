# Argentina (without Patagonia): Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish (with the provincial *tonadas*); Quechua and Aymara of the Puna and Bolivian barrios; Yiddish; Arabic; Armenian; Ukrainian and Polish of Misiones; Italian of the immigrant era. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check pride in the port's defense and culture against how the provinces read it.
- **The Conurbano:** check working-class pride songs and banners against middle-class symbols.
- **Córdoba:** check pride carried by accent, university and humor.
- **Tucumán and the Northwest:** compare independence pride in Tucumán with Andean and Kolla emblems in Jujuy and Salta.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní-speaking pride and its own songs and flags.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check pride in Quichua and in being the oldest founded city.
- **Cuyo and Santa Fe:** check provincial pride in wine, irrigation and farm colonies.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check group pride in the organizations' own words.
- **Qom, Wichí, Kolla, Diaguita and Huarpe peoples:** check how each names pride in its own language and emblems.
- **Italian, Spanish, Jewish, Armenian, Syrian-Lebanese and Ukrainian communities:** check pride in origin against pride in the new country, by generation.
- **LGBTQ Argentines:** check the capital's march against provincial marches, by decade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, football and the press; mixed for provincial pride; thin for Indigenous terms for pride.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua and Aymara for Andean emblems; Italian and Yiddish for immigrant presses; Arabic and Armenian for community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pride around the 1978 World Cup, Peronism and the Malvinas war is contested ground; present each account as an account. Never record a people's pride as one trait; keep it to what each source says.
