# Argentina (without Patagonia): Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and lunfardo; provincial Spanish; Guaraní; Quechua; Qom and Wichí; Yiddish and Judeo-Spanish; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check public cameras, streaming fame and shopping-street promenades as different kinds of being seen.
- **The Conurbano:** check census and news coverage of the *villas*.
- **Provincial towns of Córdoba, Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and La Pampa:** check where the plaza walk still runs, and who takes part.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní greetings and the gaze in rural households.
- **Salta, Jujuy, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check Andean customs of greeting and the evil eye, separately from the Pampa.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check workers away on seasonal migration as households seen only part of the year.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each people's response to being photographed and studied.
- **Kolla, Diaguita and Huarpe:** check visibility after decades of being called extinct; contested ground.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check self-counting in their own organizations against the census.
- **Travesti, trans and gay Argentines:** check visibility as safety or danger, by province.
- **Jewish, Sephardic and Syrian-Lebanese households:** check gaze customs brought from the old country.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for media monitoring, census items and police archives; mixed for town rituals; thin for Indigenous peoples' own accounts of being looked at.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní and Quechua for greeting and gaze terms; Yiddish, Judeo-Spanish and Arabic for evil-eye customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** surveillance files name people and are released under archive rules. Photographs of Indigenous people taken for science may be held back at a community's request. The dictatorship's watch lists and the account of Afro-Argentine "disappearance" are contested ground; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the camera, photo-archive, census-count and evil-eye pointers weighed for being noticed met the standing rules.
