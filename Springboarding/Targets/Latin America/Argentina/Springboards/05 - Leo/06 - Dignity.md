# Argentina (without Patagonia): Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish; Mbyá and Corrientes Guaraní; Quechua; Qom; Yiddish; Arabic; Armenian; Ukrainian and Polish. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check how waiting rooms, hospitals and offices treat people by class, before speaking of one city norm.
- **The Conurbano:** compare the movements' language of dignity with church and union language in the same districts.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check union traditions of dignity at work outside the capital.
- **Tucumán:** check sugar-town workers' accounts after the mill closures of 1966.
- **Jujuy and Salta:** check Kolla housing and work movements against the provincial government's account.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check how seasonal migrant workers describe their treatment.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** compare Mbyá terms with Guaraní-speaking peasants' and colonists' own terms.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check the Chaco peoples' testimony on treatment by the state; Napalpí is contested ground.
- **Afro-Argentines:** check confraternity records and later associations as places of standing.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian communities:** check welfare societies and how they spare the poor from shame.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan workers:** check treatment in textile workshops and brickworks in their own testimony.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for human-rights, prison and movement records; mixed for provincial care; thin for Indigenous terms that might not divide "dignity" from other ideas.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní and Mbyá for *teko*; Quechua for Andean phrases; Yiddish and Arabic for welfare records; Ukrainian and Polish for colony societies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forensic and psychiatric records involve the dead and patients; follow archive rules. The disappeared, Napalpí and the Tupac Amaru quarrel are contested ground; present each account as an account. Rights law is Libra's; point there.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld; the confraternity, movement, forensic, prison and care-home pointers weighed for inherent worth fell under no standing rule.
