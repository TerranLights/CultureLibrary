## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check the commute and the continuous working day, and factory towns such as Berisso against office districts.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check farm work's move from hired hands to contractors, by decade.
- **Córdoba, Santa Fe and Rosario:** check the auto and machinery plants apart from the port.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check the sugar harvest and the Puna herding year side by side.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check what work remains for those who stay home in harvest season.
- **Cuyo:** check the vineyard *contratista* and the harvest as the year's hinge.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check yerba, tea and tobacco work, and where Mbyá households stand in it.
- **Wichí, Qom, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each people's year in its own words, apart from employers' accounts.
- **Jewish, Volga German, Ukrainian, Polish and Mennonite colonists:** check each colony's shared work and rest days.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan workers:** check horticulture, garment and building work through their own associations' papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban labor history; mixed for the daily round and housework before time-use surveys; thin for Indigenous accounts of work and rest.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní and Quichua for rural work talk; Ukrainian and Polish for Misiones colony papers; Yiddish for the farm colonies' and unions' own press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Napalpí (1924), the killings at a Chaco reduction after its Qom and Moqoit cotton workers stopped work, is contested ground: the officials' reports of the time, the 2022 truth trial and the communities' own memory are each an account. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside here; the colonial-labor, factory, hygiene and almanac pointers weighed for the domain raised no standing-rule issue.
