# Argentina (without Patagonia): Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish with *lunfardo*; Litoral and Chaco Spanish; Guaraní; Spanish and Quechua of the Puna; Yiddish; Japanese and Korean for community records. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check office, shop and public-sector work against the factory belt, and what the *ñoqui* charge says about who is judged.
- **The Conurbano:** check popular-economy work (carters, community kitchens, street selling) against registered jobs.
- **Rosario, Córdoba and the Pampa towns:** check port, plant and farm-service work, and the move from hand to machine harvests.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check mill seasons, tobacco and the Quebrada's terrace farming.
- **Chaco and Formosa:** check how the cotton harvest and its decline are told by settlers and by Qom, Wichí and Moqoit workers.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check the yerba camps against small family farms, Mbyá included.
- **Cuyo and Catamarca-La Rioja:** check vineyard, olive and mining jobs.
- **Jewish, Japanese, Korean and Chinese communities:** check how each moved through trades over generations, from the communities' own records.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian workers:** check building, horticulture, garment and domestic work through migrants' associations.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check farm work as church life orders it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, factory history and labor statistics; mixed for informal work; thin for Indigenous wage work in the workers' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní for the yerba camps and Corrientes farms; Quechua for Puna labor exchange; Yiddish for the Jewish labor press; Japanese and Korean for community histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Luis Viale fire and workshop labor touch living families; use public records. Peronism's labor history is contested ground: present each account as an account. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the terrace, labor-history, song and survey pointers weighed for work raised no standing-rule issue.
