## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check household service and the building *portero* (caretaker) against service in cafés and offices.
- **The Conurbano:** check where household workers live and travel from, and community-kitchen service as unpaid work.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe:** check the parish and service-club life of mid-sized cities.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** check old households' service from rural girls (*criadas*), and the promise service of saints' feasts.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní-speaking service roles and the *promesero* who serves the Virgen de Itatí.
- **Mbyá, Kolla, Qom and Wichí:** check service to one's own community (assemblies, prayer houses, churches) apart from service to outsiders.
- **Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian, Jewish and Ukrainian communities:** check each one's charities and church or synagogue service roles.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian women:** check how many work in household service, and how their own associations describe that work.
- **Cape Verdean Argentines and Afro-Argentines:** check service at sea and in households, and how each group's memory tells it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for domestic service history and public employment; mixed for volunteering; thin for service roles in Indigenous communities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Guaraní for Corrientes service words and Mbyá roles; Arabic and Ukrainian for community charities; Portuguese and Kriolu for Cape Verdean memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mbyá prayer-house roles may be restricted; use what Mbyá teachers publish. Household workers' testimony touches living people. Service under the 1976-83 dictatorship (civil servants, conscripts) is contested ground: present each account as an account. Labor rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the household, civic, religious and maritime service pointers considered for this concept raised no standing-rule issue.
