## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check private plans, union *obras sociales* and public hospitals as three separate health worlds.
- **The Conurbano:** check public clinics, community health promoters and the waiting line as everyday care.
- **Córdoba and the sierras:** check the old health-resort towns built for lung patients against today's clinics.
- **Santa Fe and Rosario:** check municipal public-health networks against the provincial pattern.
- **Santiago del Estero and the Chaco region:** check Chagas, rural housing and healers in the same households.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** check Andean healers and hospitals with altitude and distance in the picture.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check border clinics used by Paraguayan and Brazilian patients, and Mbyá health programs.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá, Mbyá and Kolla:** check each people's own health programs and how they describe illness.
- **Volga German, Mennonite, Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese communities:** check healing practices and community clinics, from their own records.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan communities:** check how migrants use public hospitals, and which other healers they turn to, if any, in their own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public-health history, psychiatry and surveys; mixed for folk healing; thin for Indigenous healing in the peoples' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Qom and Wichí for Chaco health; Quechua and Aymara for Andean healing; Guaraní for Litoral cures; German for Volga German colony sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge and ceremonies may be restricted; use what healers publish. Mental-health and HIV data are private; use aggregates. The pandemic response and the "vacunatorio VIP" episode are politically contested: present each account as an account. Health law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the licensing, hygienist, psychiatric and folk-healing pointers weighed for health raised no standing-rule issue.
