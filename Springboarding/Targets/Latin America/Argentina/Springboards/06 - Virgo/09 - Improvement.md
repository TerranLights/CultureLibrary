## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check the university as the ladder, and who in each generation got onto it.
- **The Conurbano:** check self-built housing, new universities and community projects as three routes to betterment.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check how farm "improvement" is described by owners, contractors and laborers.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check industrial-era hopes in car and machinery towns, and what followed plant closures.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check development projects (mining, lithium, roads) as residents of the Quebrada and Puna describe them.
- **Chaco and Formosa:** check settler colonies against Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá accounts of what "progress" took.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check dams, forestry and yerba projects as Mbyá and riverside families tell them.
- **Mennonites of La Pampa:** check debates inside the colonies over new machines and schooling.
- **Italian, Spanish and Jewish families:** check how immigrant ascent is told across three generations, from family memoirs.
- **Afro-Argentine and Cape Verdean communities:** check how their own organizations answer the whitening narrative.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for immigration, education and development history; mixed for self-help and home improvement; thin for Indigenous views of imposed "improvement".
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Moqoit and Qom for frontier memory; Guaraní for the Litoral; Plautdietsch for Mennonite debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the frontier campaigns, whitening ideology and the 1976-83 evictions are contested ground: present each account as an account, and lead with the affected peoples' own words. Land and housing law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the frontier, schooling, development and mobility pointers weighed for betterment raised no standing-rule issue.
