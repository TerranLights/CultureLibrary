## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check *gatillo fácil* cases and lynching reports by district, against what juries decide.
- **Córdoba:** check two decades of mixed juries against judge-only trials in neighboring provinces.
- **Catamarca and La Rioja:** check cases where demands for justice met family power in a small province.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check the repression trials in the sugar districts, and Kolla land cases.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check rural land evictions as the peasant movement records them.
- **The Chaco and Formosa (Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá):** check each people's own testimony on the killings of 1924 and 1947 beside the court records; contested ground.
- **La Pampa and Cuyo:** check the bandit-saint cults and the songs that keep them.
- **Jewish community:** check the separate relatives' groups and their differing readings of the AMIA trials.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check access to interpreters and defense in criminal courts.
- **Afro-Argentines:** check complaints of police profiling filed by their own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the dictatorship trials and court statistics; mixed for lynching and police violence; thin for Indigenous peoples' own ideas of repair.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo* for police and prison sources; Qom and Pilagá for survivors' testimony; Quichua for rural Santiago; Yiddish for the 1990s community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared and the trials, the AMIA case, Napalpí and Rincón Bomba are contested ground; give each account as an account and respect the families' wording. Court files name living people; follow archive rules.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the closest call was the bandit shrines, since grave offerings can be private devotion; keep to what is public.
