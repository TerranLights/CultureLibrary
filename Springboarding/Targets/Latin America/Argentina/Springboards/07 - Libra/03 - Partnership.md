*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and *lunfardo*; the Italian and *cocoliche* of immigrant merchants; Guaraní of Corrientes; Quechua and Aymara of the Puna and of Bolivian communities; Arabic of Syrian-Lebanese families; Wichí. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check the share of cohabiting couples and same-sex unions against the provinces.
- **The Conurbano:** check unions formed young, and how partners divide earning and care.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe:** check Catholic weddings against civil-only unions over time.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check the Kolla paired office and Andean wedding sponsors against city weddings.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check couples apart for long seasons through migrant harvest work.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní partner words and rural unions; check Mbyá unions through Mbyá-authored sources.
- **The Chaco and Formosa (Wichí, Qom, Moqoit, Pilagá):** check each people's own rules of marriage and residence.
- **Italian, Spanish and Syrian-Lebanese families:** check the merchant partnership and the family firm as a marriage of houses.
- **Jewish, Armenian and Ukrainian communities:** check marriage within and across communities, from community registers.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan couples:** check partnerships split across borders and money sent between spouses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, census and business forms; mixed for courtship and household equality; thin for Indigenous marriage told by the peoples themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; colonial Spanish for the *disenso* files; Quechua and Aymara for Kolla and Bolivian couple terms; Guaraní for Corrientes; Arabic and Italian for merchant papers; Wichí for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** marriage rules inside Indigenous communities may be private; use what members publish. Football-club ownership and Peronist "compañero" talk are contested; give each side's account. Same-sex unions involve living activists.
- **Held for review:** nothing; old Chaco marriage ethnographies came closest, and are flagged for careful reading, not set aside.
