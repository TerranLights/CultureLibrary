## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check national-court and civil-service hiring by competition against appointment.
- **The Conurbano:** check how jobs and aid reach households through local brokers, from studies on all sides.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe:** check provincial judicial councils and their competitive exams.
- **Santiago del Estero, La Rioja, San Luis and Formosa:** check the length of single-party or single-family rule and the naming of provincial judges, from both sides' sources.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check sugar-mill towns where one employer weighed on local officials.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check rural police and justices of the peace in small towns where everyone is related.
- **Qom, Wichí, Mbyá and Kolla communities:** check how each people chooses its own leaders, and how that meets the state's registration rules.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Korean communities:** check community networks in hiring and credit, in each community's own records.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan migrants:** check treatment at immigration and police offices, from their associations' complaints.
- **Football clubs and supporters:** check claims of referee favor by club size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, decrees and court controversies; mixed for clientelism and hiring; thin for Indigenous accounts of authority and favor.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and its procedural register; colonial Spanish for the *residencia* files; Qom and Wichí for leadership terms; Yiddish and Mandarin for community networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Court enlargement, "napkin judges", provincial governments and clientelism are contested ground; present each account as an account, and never let a bullet stand for a province's people. The 1992 broadcast names a sitting judge; cite the record, not gossip.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the small-province studies came closest to stereotype risk and are kept only as a check on courts, not people.
