*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and its political slang; diplomatic and colonial frontier Spanish; Guaraní of the Litoral borders; Quechua of the Puna; Korean of Flores; Arabic of Syrian-Lebanese families. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check how *rosca* and *muñeca* are used of national politicians against provincial ones.
- **Córdoba, San Luis and La Pampa (the old southern frontier):** check Ranquel descendants' own reading of the frontier letters and treaties.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check Kolla communities' delegations to provincial and national offices.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check daily dealings across the Paraná and Uruguay rivers, and Guaraní as a bridge language.
- **The Chaco and Formosa (Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá):** check how community delegates are chosen and how they report back.
- **Cuyo:** check Huarpe community dealings with Mendoza and San Juan, in the communities' own statements.
- **Korean, Chinese and Bolivian communities:** check children's role as interpreters, and the community bodies that speak to the state.
- **Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian communities:** check their associations' ties to embassies and homeland causes.
- **Unions and employers:** check how conciliation is described from each side of the table.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for diplomatic history and law; mixed for everyday tact and politeness; thin for Native diplomacy in Native voices after the 19th century.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including 18th- and 19th-century frontier Spanish; Guaraní and Quechua for border talk; Korean and Arabic for community diplomacy; English for the UN and 1982 records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the islands' name and claim, the 1982 war, the Pacto de Olivos and frontier treaties are contested ground; present each side's account. Treaties made under the threat of force should be read with the peoples' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the frontier-treaty pointers raised only the Native-naming rule, applied here.
