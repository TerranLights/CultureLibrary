## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check hosting turns and death notices across classes.
- **The Pampa of Buenos Aires province and La Pampa:** check neighbors' help at harvest and the colony funds of Mennonites and Volga Germans.
- **Córdoba, San Luis and La Pampa:** check Ranquel descendants' reading of the frontier rations and gifts.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check offerings to the earth and the souls, town by town, and who leads them.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check Quichua terms for giving back and the household feasts that bind neighbors.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Mbyá rules of sharing in Mbyá-authored sources, apart from Paraguay's scholarship.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check each people's sharing rules as members state them.
- **Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian and Jewish families:** check credit given on trust and how it was repaid, from family and community papers.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check fiesta sponsorship and the return owed to the next sponsor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for frontier and trade history; mixed for everyday exchange; thin for Indigenous sharing rules told by members.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, with 19th-century frontier and military records; Quechua for offerings; Qom for sharing terms; Arabic for merchant families; German and Plautdietsch for colony funds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some offering rites and words are for participants only; use what communities share. Frontier rations sit on contested ground (the 1870s-80s campaigns); give each side's account. The 1933 pact is a contested political symbol.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the offering rites were the closest call under the restricted-knowledge rule, and only public, community-shared accounts are pointed to.
