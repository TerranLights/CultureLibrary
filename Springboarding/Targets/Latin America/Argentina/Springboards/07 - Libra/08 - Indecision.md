## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check the emigration debates of 2002 and the spoiled-vote rates of 2001 by district.
- **The Pampa and Córdoba:** check how farm families time sales and planting when policy is unsettled, from farm-press sources.
- **Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and the Paraná delta:** check islanders' and ranchers' accounts of the stalled wetlands law.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check community assemblies in the Quebrada and Puna on when a decision may wait.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check family decisions on harvest migration, and who has the last word.
- **Cuyo:** check vintners' sayings about waiting out frost and hail.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Mbyá assemblies on land offers, from Mbyá statements.
- **The Chaco and Formosa (Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá):** check how each people's assemblies treat a matter left open.
- **Jewish, Armenian and Korean families:** check decisions to emigrate or return, from community press.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families:** check the choice of staying or going home, from their own associations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elections, laws and court-ordered projects; mixed for personal and family decisions; thin for Indigenous decision-making told by members.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; early-19th-century Spanish for the founding debates; Quechua and Guaraní for everyday hedges; Mbyá and Qom for assembly practice; Yiddish for community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1957 blank vote, the 2001 crisis and the stalled wetlands law are contested ground; present each side's account. End-of-life choices involve private families; use published cases only.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the card-reader pointer was weighed because the signs serve only as classifiers here, and it points to a practice people use, not to a classifier.
