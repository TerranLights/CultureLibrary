# Argentina (without Patagonia): Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and *lunfardo*; 18th-century mission Guaraní and colonial Spanish; Plautdietsch and German; Yiddish; Arabic of Syrian-Lebanese families. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check silence and speaking out under the dictatorship, neighborhood by neighborhood, from oral-history archives.
- **Córdoba and Tucumán:** check the 1970s silences in university and sugar towns, from survivors' and residents' accounts.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check Mennonite and Volga German colonies' rules against going to court.
- **Cuyo:** check how the Mendoza church and families responded to the Próvolo survivors.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** check how Kolla communities settle quarrels without courts, from their own statements.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check Mbyá accounts of the mission peoples' removals, and present-day land deals.
- **The Chaco and Formosa (Qom, Wichí, Moqoit, Pilagá):** check survivors' descendants on decades of silence about the killings of 1924 and 1947.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian families:** check how each community handles family quarrels and violence at home, from its own advisers and press.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan workers:** check fear of losing papers or jobs as a reason to stay quiet, from their own associations.
- **Football supporters and club members:** check who pays the stands' toughs and who refuses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the dictatorship's aftermath and court records; mixed for workplace and family silence; thin for Indigenous peoples' own ways of keeping the peace.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; mission Guaraní for the 1753 letters; Plautdietsch and German for colony statements; Yiddish and Arabic for family-harmony norms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases involve survivors; use public records and their published words only. The pardons, the 1987 uprising, everyday silence under the dictatorship and the Napalpí and Rincón Bomba killings are contested ground; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the survivor-testimony pointers were weighed against the privacy rule and kept to published statements.
