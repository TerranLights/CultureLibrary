## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check vault, niche and cremation use by district, and home wakes.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check how farm families handle land and burial when a parent dies.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe-Rosario:** check colony cemeteries of Italian and Swiss families.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check roadside folk-saint shrines against parish practice.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check Quichua and Spanish wake songs and prayer leaders.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check Kolla and Quebrada rites for the dead beside the Catholic ones.
- **Cuyo:** check vineyard inheritance and the Difunta Correa shrine as a death story.
- **Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá:** check mourning and the handling of the dead's belongings, as each people publishes it.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian and Ukrainian communities:** check separate cemeteries and mourning periods.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan communities:** check repatriation of bodies and All Souls' tables in migrant barrios.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cemeteries, sex surveys and public deaths; mixed for inheritance customs; thin for Chaco and Puna mourning outside ethnography.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; Quichua and Guaraní for rural death talk; Qom and Wichí for mourning; Yiddish and Arabic for condolence customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared (*Nunca más*), Evita's body and Peronism, and the Napalpí dead are contested ground: give each account as an account. Indigenous remains once held by museums are the communities' matter, in their own words.
- **Held for review:** no item set aside; the San La Muerte search came closest to the stereotype rule, since press ties it to prisons, so the sheet points to devotees' own accounts.
