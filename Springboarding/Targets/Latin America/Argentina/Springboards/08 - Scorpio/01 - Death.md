## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires and the Conurbano:** compare grief talk shaped by psychoanalysis with parish and Pentecostal comfort.
- **The Pampa:** check how small towns announce a death (radio, posters, the club) against city notices.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe:** check the bereaved parents' movement where it began.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check who leads wake prayers, and in which language.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** compare Kolla, Diaguita and town Catholic accounts of the soul's journey.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Mbyá and Guaraní-speaking *criollo* families on shades and roadside souls.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check how Qom, Wichí and Pilagá congregations speak of death, in members' words.
- **Cuyo and La Rioja:** check roadside crosses and *ánimas* shrines along mountain roads.
- **Armenian, Ukrainian, Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese families:** compare memorial calendars that keep a death year after year.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan barrios:** check All Souls tables and bodies sent home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality figures and public mourning; mixed for grief and palliative care; thin for Chaco and Puna beliefs outside a few ethnographers.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quichua for wake prayers; Mbyá Guaraní and Qom for soul terms; Armenian and Ukrainian for liturgies; Yiddish for memorial-book entries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violent deaths at the hands of the state, the disappeared and COVID deaths are contested: give accounts. Youth suicide in the Northwest is painful: handle with care. Mbyá teaching on the soul may be guarded.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the CORREPI register was weighed longest, as a partisan count, and stays in with the label "an account".
