## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Conurbano barrios:** check Pentecostal and Catholic *curas villeros* accounts of leaving drugs, side by side.
- **Buenos Aires city:** check spiritist, Orthodox Jewish and psychotherapeutic routes to a new self.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe-Rosario:** check treatment centers and church recovery homes outside the capital.
- **Cuyo:** check the earthquake towns (Mendoza, San Juan) for rebuilding talk and Huarpe re-emergence.
- **La Pampa:** check Rankülche revival, and separately any revival talk among the province's Mennonites.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Mbyá renewal ideas and Ukrainian Easter rites as separate trails.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Moqoit and Wichí churches' conversion stories, each separately.
- **Jujuy, Salta and Tucumán:** check Holy Week and carnival as cycles of ending and renewal, by village.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check saints' feasts linked to colonial conversion.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan communities:** check whether migration is narrated as a new life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Pentecostal growth and recovered firms; mixed for addiction recovery; thin for Indigenous renewal teaching in its own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, with evangelical and therapeutic jargon; Guaraní and Mbyá; Rankülche for revival materials; Ukrainian for Easter rites; Hebrew for religious outreach.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial conversion was often forced; read missionaries critically. Some recovery homes face abuse complaints: name accounts, not verdicts. Mbyá prophetic teaching may be guarded.
- **Held for review:** the closest call was the *yvy marã e'ỹ* term, as outside scholars shaped its meaning; it stays as a search term, flagged for Mbyá voices.
