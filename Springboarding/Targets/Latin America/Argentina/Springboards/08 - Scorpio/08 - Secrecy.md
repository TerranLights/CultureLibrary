# Argentina (without Patagonia): Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish and *lunfardo* (itself once an argot of concealment); Spanish of the colonial Inquisition files; German for postwar files; Guaraní; Mbyá; Qom; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden on purpose, and how a people judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is a facet.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check privacy in apartment buildings against the *encargado* who knows everyone's business.
- **The Conurbano:** check what neighbors keep from police and from the party organizer.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check small-town silences about paternity and land deals, in memoirs and local histories.
- **Córdoba:** check Catholic and Masonic circles in the city's elite history.
- **Rosario and Tucumán:** check Qom and Diaguita families on concealing and revealing origin.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** check Kolla communities' rules on what outsiders may record.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** check Mbyá rules on what may be told, and whether Guaraní served as a home language for secrets.
- **The Chaco:** check what Qom and Wichí elders keep from researchers.
- **Jewish, German and Ukrainian communities:** check what each kept quiet about the 1930s and 1940s, from community archives.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdeans:** check family memory of hidden ancestry, in their own organizations' words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state secrecy and the postwar fugitives; mixed for family and sexual secrets; thin by design for restricted Indigenous knowledge.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; German for postwar files; Portuguese and Spanish for Inquisition records; Guaraní and Mbyá; Yiddish for community archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mbyá, Qom and Wichí sacred knowledge must not be retold; record only what communities say may be shared. The dictatorship files, the postwar fugitives and the Afro-Argentine 'disappearance' are contested ground: give accounts. Appropriated children belong to the trials and the Abuelas' own records.
