## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check where the "no religion" share and the Pentecostal storefronts sit, district by district.
- **The Northwest (Salta, Jujuy, Tucumán, Catamarca):** separate Catholic fiesta life from Andean earth rites and see how communities join them.
- **Santiago del Estero and La Rioja:** check Quichua prayer and walking *promesantes* at rural shrines.
- **Córdoba:** compare the Jesuit-founded city's institutions with the sierras' newer spiritual seekers.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** set Guaraní mission heritage, Mbyá religion and the Ukrainian and Polish Eastern-rite parishes side by side.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá churches against older ritual specialists, each as its people describes them.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian and Volga German communities:** treat each one's congregations, schools and press as a separate religious world.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check feasts brought from home and how parishes receive them.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check candombe and saints' feasts in communities' own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic history and the belief surveys; mixed for Pentecostal and immigrant congregations; thin for Moqoit, Pilagá and Huarpe religion.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for the bulk; Guaraní and Latin for mission texts; Qom and Mbyá for Native religion; Yiddish, Arabic and Armenian for community congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mbyá sacred words and some Qom healing knowledge are not for outsiders; ask first. The dictatorship and the Church, Peronism and the Church, and the AMIA bombing are contested; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** the registry and atlas rows would bring in two groups held here as categories only: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and Buddhist communities (an open question for the author).
