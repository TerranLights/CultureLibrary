# Argentina (without Patagonia): Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense and interior Spanish; Guaraní; Santiago Quichua; Qom and Wichí; Yiddish; Arabic; Ukrainian. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, saints, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, the nation, science or progress.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires and its suburbs:** compare parish Catholics, megachurch members and the unaffiliated on which institution each trusts.
- **Santa Fe, Rosario and northern Buenos Aires province:** check whether San Nicolás draws mainly from nearby towns or nationwide.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check vow practice among Quichua-speaking walkers and parish records of the same feasts.
- **Salta and Jujuy:** check how Kolla families combine Catholic feasts with offerings to the earth, in their own terms.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom and Wichí church members' accounts of conversion and of keeping or leaving older practice.
- **Misiones:** compare Mbyá villages with Ukrainian and Polish Eastern-rite parishes as faith communities with separate languages.
- **Entre Ríos colonies and Once:** check synagogue life, observance and secular Jewish identity side by side.
- **Syrian-Lebanese families:** separate Maronite, Orthodox and Muslim households before asking about faith.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan communities:** check the Virgins brought from home and whether local parishes host them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic devotion and Evangelical growth; mixed for Jewish and Muslim practice; thin for Moqoit and Pilagá faith in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Latin for Jesuit reports; Qom, Wichí and Moqoit for Bible translations; Yiddish, Arabic and Ukrainian for community faith.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Church under the dictatorship is contested; Afro-Brazilian religions have met hostile press coverage, so prefer practitioners' accounts; some Native sacred speech is restricted.
