## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check how people talk about street scams, tipping and paying taxes, by class and age.
- **The Conurbano:** check what residents call honest work and an honest neighbor where informal work is common.
- **The Pampa and Rosario:** check grain and livestock dealers' word-based deals and who is trusted to keep them.
- **Córdoba:** check university students' views on copying, against those in Buenos Aires.
- **The Northwest:** check whether Quechua maxims on lying are taught in Kolla homes or only in school murals.
- **Santiago del Estero and Corrientes:** check Quichua and Guaraní storytelling of tricksters and what listeners are meant to take from them.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check how Wichí and Qom narrators frame the trickster's lies, in their own words.
- **Italian- and Spanish-descended families:** check the school readers and home sayings each generation recalls.
- **The Mennonites of La Pampa:** check whether they refuse oaths and rely on a plain yes or no.
- **Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese families:** check the honesty teachings each community's schools and congregations give.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corruption reporting and fiction; mixed for surveys; thin for Native views on lying outside tale collections.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; Quechua and Aymara for the 1585 manual; Wichí and Qom for trickster cycles; Yiddish and Arabic for community teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "viveza criolla" label and claims about national dishonesty are stereotypes to examine, never findings; corruption cases tied to parties are contested. Bribery law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing put aside; the riskiest pointer is the 2006 robbery film, which could glamorize theft, so it is kept only to study how audiences judged it.
