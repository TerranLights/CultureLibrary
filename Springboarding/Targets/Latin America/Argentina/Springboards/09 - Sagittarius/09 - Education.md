## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare the elite public colleges with neighborhood schools on who gets in and why.
- **The Conurbano:** check first-generation students at the newer universities against UBA students.
- **Córdoba:** check the old university's prestige against students from the sierras and the north who come to study.
- **Santa Fe, Rosario and Entre Ríos:** check the normal-school tradition and the Jewish farm colonies' first schools.
- **The Northwest:** check boarding schools and university access in Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán for Kolla and *criollo* families.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check Quichua in teacher training and in families' hopes for their children.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá teachers and students, in their own words.
- **Misiones:** check Mbyá village schools beside Ukrainian, Polish and German colony schools.
- **Catholic, Jewish and Armenian schools:** check what each community wants its schools to transmit.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan families:** check how migrant children fare in schooling and what degrees mean to their families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history, university statistics and policy; mixed for family meanings of a degree; thin for Pilagá and Huarpe higher education.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Latin for the colonial library; Guaraní, Quechua, Qom and Wichí for Native schooling; Yiddish and Armenian for community schools.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Peronism's schools, the 1966 and 1976 purges of universities and the "alpargatas" slogan are contested; present each account as an account. Education law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the "alpargatas" slogan came closest to the stereotype rule, since it is used to brand a whole movement as hostile to books, so it appears only as a contested search term.
