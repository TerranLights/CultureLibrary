## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check whether status talk turns on surname, district or job.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** compare the *estanciero*, the *intendente* and the parish priest as authorities in farm towns.
- **Córdoba and Santa Fe-Rosario:** check university title against factory seniority as rival ladders.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** separate landed names, Kolla community officers and sugar-mill management as three chains of command.
- **Catamarca, La Rioja and Santiago del Estero:** check long-ruling provincial families against village elders.
- **Cuyo:** check the wine-estate order beside the Huarpe communities' own officers.
- **Corrientes, Misiones and Entre Ríos:** compare Guaraní-speaking rural ranks with the Ukrainian, Polish and Jewish colony boards.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá leaders as each people names them.
- **Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian, Korean, Chinese and Okinawan communities:** check their associations' presidents and elders as local hierarchies.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check the fiesta committees and market associations that organize migrants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the political and colonial history of authority; mixed for status and reputation; thin for the Huarpe, the Moqoit and the Mennonite colony.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; colonial paleography for the Calchaquí papers; Quechua and Guaraní for community terms; Yiddish and Arabic for community archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** caudillos, Peronism and the dictatorship of 1976-83 are contested ground: give every reading as one account. Credit files concern living people; use aggregates only.
- **Held for review:** the colonial rolls came closest, since their labels include an old slur-like word for Native people; cite it only as the rolls' wording.
