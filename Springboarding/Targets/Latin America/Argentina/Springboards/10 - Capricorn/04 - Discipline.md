## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check how schools in rich and poor districts use the *convivencia* codes.
- **Córdoba:** check convent and Jesuit-school traditions against the province's present school rules.
- **Rosario and Santa Fe:** check club academies that recruit boys young, against those in the capital.
- **The Northwest (Tucumán, Salta, Jujuy):** check how Kolla families and town schools describe correcting children.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check grandparents' and parents' accounts of punishment across three generations.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** compare Mbyá villages, Guaraní-speaking farm families and Ukrainian and Polish settlers on child-rearing.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom and Wichí schooling where Native and criollo teachers share a classroom.
- **La Pampa:** check the Mennonite colony's rules beside neighboring farm towns.
- **Okinawan, Japanese and Korean communities:** check language schools, dojos and churches as places of training.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on schools and law; mixed on the army and sport; thin on Mbyá, Qom and Mennonite practice.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and barracks slang; colonial Spanish for convent books; Guaraní and Mbyá; Japanese and Korean; German and Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hazing, abuse in sport and convent life touch living people; use published material. Conscript discipline under the dictatorship of 1976-83 is contested ground: give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the youth-academy press came closest, since it names minors; keep to adults' and clubs' public accounts.
