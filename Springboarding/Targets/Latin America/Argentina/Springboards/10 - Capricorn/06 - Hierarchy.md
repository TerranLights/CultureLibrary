## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check north-south and center-edge rank, and the old clubs against new money.
- **The Conurbano:** check how *villas* and gated estates sit side by side, and the words each uses for the other.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check estancia ranks from owner to *puestero* across a century.
- **Córdoba:** check the university chair ladder and the old Córdoba families' place in the city.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** compare sugar-mill ranks, old landed names and Kolla community offices.
- **Catamarca, La Rioja and Santiago del Estero:** check how a few families rank above the rest, and how others speak of it.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check criollo, Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá relations, each told by its own side.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní-speaking farmhands and landowners, and the Ukrainian and Polish settler order.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check their own accounts of where they were placed in the city's ranks.
- **Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Syrian-Lebanese communities:** check internal ranks of seniority and notability, community by community.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan workers:** check their place in garment, building and farm hierarchies, in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on elites, the army and domestic work; mixed on class self-placement; thin on Native accounts of rank in the Chaco.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; colonial paleography for militia rolls; Quechua; Wichí; Japanese and Arabic for community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class and color slurs must be described, never used as labels; the Afro-Argentine "disappearance" account and Peronism's labor laws are contested: give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the slur row in the search terms came closest; it stays as a gloss for finding sources, never as a label.
