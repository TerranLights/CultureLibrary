## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** check police "arrangements" and trust in courts district by district.
- **Córdoba:** check youth arrests under the old code and after its replacement, by neighborhood.
- **Santa Fe and Rosario:** check how the drug trade has shaped talk of whose law runs in each barrio.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check Kolla community rules beside provincial courts, and land law as each side reads it.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check courts in provinces long run by one family or party.
- **Cuyo:** check water rights and irrigation inspectors as an older local law.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** compare Mbyá justice with Guaraní-speaking rural justices of the peace.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá relations with provincial police and courts, each in its own terms.
- **Jewish community:** check how rabbinic teaching on state law is taught and used.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check where migrants settle disputes: consulate, association, court.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on legal culture essays, surveys and police studies; mixed on compliance in the street; thin on Mbyá and Qom legal orders.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and police slang; colonial legal Spanish; Mbyá, Guaraní and Qom; Yiddish and Aramaic for Jewish legal teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** police corruption cases name living people; use rulings and published studies. Courts under the dictatorship of 1976-83 and Peronist-era judicial purges are contested: give each account as an account. Native communities may keep internal disputes private.
