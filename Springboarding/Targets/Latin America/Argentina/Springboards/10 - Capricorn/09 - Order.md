*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check noise complaints and street vending disputes by district, and candombe drumming in San Telmo as residents and drummers each describe it.
- **The Conurbano:** check neighborhood alarm groups and *mano dura* campaigns against residents' own accounts of safety.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check homicide maps by neighborhood and how each city's press speaks of disorder.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check carnival in the Quebrada de Humahuaca as a set time of permitted disorder.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check the siesta hours and the town square's routines.
- **Corrientes, Misiones and Entre Ríos:** check carnival towns and how they police their own celebrations.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check how Qom, Wichí and Pilagá communities describe police order in towns where they are a minority.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check drumming and gatherings as order or nuisance, in their own words.
- **Jewish community:** check security at community buildings after 1992 and 1994, and how members speak of it.
- **La Pampa Mennonites:** check their congregation's own order beside provincial rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political order and policing history; mixed on noise and neighborhood order; thin on Wichí ideas of the world's order.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and slang; 16th-century Spanish for the founding plan; Wichí; Yiddish; German and Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1976 coup, Rosas and protest policing are contested: give each as an account. The 1992 and 1994 bombings are grief-laden; follow families' wording. Some Wichí narratives are not for outsiders.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the neighborhood alarm sources came closest, since some posts profile passers-by; cite programs and studies, never the posts.
