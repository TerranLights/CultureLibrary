# Argentina (without Patagonia): The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish with *lunfardo*; the Spanish of Córdoba and the Northwest; Santiago del Estero Quichua; Mbyá Guaraní (for the naming rite); Yiddish; Armenian; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for; with the social ways of settlement, migration, association, hospitality and gathering.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city and the Conurbano:** compare *fomento* society, club and parish as the barrio's meeting places.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check how farm towns, rail towns and the Mennonite colony organize joint life.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check the *centro vecinal* systems against the Buenos Aires model.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** compare Kolla community assemblies, saint's-day committees and city associations.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check *convido* and work-party customs against migrants' hometown clubs.
- **Cuyo:** check Huarpe communities' own accounts beside wine-town associations.
- **Corrientes, Misiones and Entre Ríos:** check how neighbors' shared work in Guaraní-speaking farm areas sits beside the cooperatives and colony associations founded by Ukrainian, Polish, Volga German and Jewish settlers.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá settlements, each in its own terms.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check their feast committees and leagues as bonds across the border.
- **Korean, Chinese and Okinawan communities:** check churches, trade chambers and prefectural clubs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Buenos Aires associations and collectivities; mixed for provincial towns; thin for the Huarpe, the Pilagá and small colonies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for registers; colonial paleography for the 1583 relation; Mbyá Guaraní and Quichua for gatherings told in their own words; Yiddish and Armenian for society minute books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mbyá ceremonies may be closed to outsiders; use what communities publish. School-act costumes touch the contested account of Afro-Argentines' "disappearance": give each reading as one account.
- **Held for review:** the 1583 relation came nearest a rule, since it names Native peoples in conquest-era terms; it is kept for its content, and its words are quoted only as the source's own.
