# Argentina (without Patagonia): Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish; colonial Spanish; Guaraní of Corrientes and Misiones; Quechua of Jujuy and the Bolivian barrios; Arabic; Korean. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare old *fomento* barrios against tower blocks where neighbors may not meet.
- **The Conurbano and the *villas*:** check parish, kitchen and club as the bonds of settlements the state long left unmapped.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check towns that run their own power and phone cooperatives against towns emptied by rail closures.
- **Córdoba, Santa Fe and Rosario:** compare formal *vecinal* systems with how residents rate them.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** check Kolla *comunidades* and the *llaqta* bond against parish and union ties of sugar towns.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check how migrant workers stay members of a home town they leave each harvest.
- **Corrientes, Misiones and Entre Ríos:** check Polish and Ukrainian parishes, the Jewish colonies, and towns moved by dams.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check how Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá settlements name and keep their own membership.
- **Irish, Basque, Paraguayan, Peruvian and Korean communities:** check which institution each gathers around.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Buenos Aires associations and cooperatives; mixed for small towns; thin on how Chaco peoples define a member in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and colonial paleography; Guaraní and Quechua for neighbors' help in their own words; Arabic and Korean for community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *villero* is a slur when used by outsiders; quote it only as each speaker uses it. The 1944 relief drive sits inside Peronism's contested story; give each reading as an account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the 1944 relief pointer came closest, because Peronist and anti-Peronist accounts of it differ, and it stays as a pointer with that warning.
