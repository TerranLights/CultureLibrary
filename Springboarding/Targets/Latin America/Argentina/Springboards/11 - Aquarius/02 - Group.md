## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare club, union and party as rival claims on one person's loyalty.
- **The Conurbano:** check how marching columns, football supporters and church cells recruit in the same streets.
- **Córdoba:** check student *agrupaciones* and the car-plant unions as two group cultures in one city.
- **Rosario and Santa Fe:** check the two-club split of the city as a measure of us-and-them.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** compare sugar-mill unions with Kolla communities' assemblies on how a group decides.
- **Cuyo:** check whether wine-workers' unions and Huarpe communities appear in sources at all; expect gaps.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní-speaking rural groups and the yerba harvesters' crews.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá kin groups and churches, each named as its people names it.
- **Italian, Japanese and Chinese collectivities:** check home-town, prefecture and trade as the basis of each group.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check dance troupes, feast brotherhoods and football leagues.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on unions, Peronism and football; mixed on youth groups and lay Catholic bodies; thin on kin groups of the Chaco peoples in their own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with *lunfardo*; colonial Spanish for Lozano; Guaraní; Italian; Japanese and Chinese for association papers; Quechua for some Bolivian troupe songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Peronism, 1970s armed groups (*la orga*) and the dictatorship are contested: give each as an account. The 2020 case concerns living people and a victim's family; use public court and press records only.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the Jesuit Chaco description came closest, since its group names follow the missionaries' categories rather than the peoples' own.
