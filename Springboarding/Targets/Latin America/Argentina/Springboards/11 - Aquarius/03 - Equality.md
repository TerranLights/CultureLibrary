*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish; late-colonial Spanish; Yiddish; Arabic; Ukrainian; Qom and Wichí (for community statements). **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is the question.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check equality talk in northern and southern barrios, and between domestic workers and employers.
- **The Conurbano:** check how residents of *villas* and *asentamientos* speak of equal treatment by police, schools and clinics.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check whether owners' and hired hands' children describe school and club as levelers.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check student and union traditions of equality against the older family names of each city.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** compare how Kolla and Diaguita communities speak of equality with how city elites do.
- **Santiago del Estero, Catamarca and La Rioja:** check the effect of NBI rankings on provincial self-images.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check how Guaraní speakers and Ukrainian and Polish descendants describe being ranked by language and origin.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá claims to equal services beside their claims as peoples.
- **Afro-Argentines and Cape Verdean Argentines:** check their own accounts of counting and not counting.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check accounts of unequal treatment at work and in public offices.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian communities:** check how each tells its path from newcomers to equals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on income inequality, women's suffrage and Peronism; mixed on racism and color; thin on Chaco peoples' own equality claims.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; late-colonial legal Spanish for status petitions; Yiddish and Arabic for community papers; Qom and Wichí for some declarations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** color and class words are slurs in many mouths; quote only as sources use them. Peronism and the Afro-Argentine "disappearance" are contested: give each as an account. Equality before the law is Libra's: here, no statutes.
- **Held for review:** the colonial petition files were the nearest call, since they rank people by caste labels; the labels stay as the sources' own and never become categories here.
