*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish; late-colonial administrative Spanish; Guaraní; Yiddish and Hebrew terms of Jewish social action; Qom and Wichí (for accounts of reforms done to them). **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** check how socialist, liberal and Catholic reformers each described what needed fixing.
- **The Conurbano:** check how reforms of the 1990s and their reversals are told in former factory towns.
- **Córdoba:** check the University Reform's local memory against the Catholic establishment it challenged.
- **Rosario and Santa Fe:** compare the Liga del Sur and the participatory budget as two local reform traditions.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check Bialet Massé's sugar chapters against what mill workers and Kolla communities later said.
- **Cuyo:** check the Nuevo Cancionero's Mendoza circle and Huarpe accounts of state programs.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check how yerba harvesters and Guaraní speakers met labor and school reforms.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá accounts of reforms done to them, beside the officials' files.
- **The Jewish colonies of Entre Ríos and Santa Fe:** check colony cooperatives and schools as reform projects run by the colonists.
- **Evangelical, Catholic and secular reformers:** check who each tried to change, and with what tools.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the University Reform, socialism and the 1990s; mixed on provincial reform currents; thin on how Chaco peoples themselves judged state programs.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; late-colonial administrative Spanish; Guaraní; Qom and Wichí for testimony; Yiddish for colony and Bundist papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Chaco reductions end in the Napalpí massacre of 1924, contested ground; give survivors' and officials' accounts as accounts. The 1990s reforms and Peronism are disputed. Laws of reform belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the prison-conversion pointer came closest, since inmates' accounts may be published without consent; use only studies that report consent.
