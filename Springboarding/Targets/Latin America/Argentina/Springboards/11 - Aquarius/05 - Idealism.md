# Argentina (without Patagonia): Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish; 18th-century Italian; Guaraní of the mission tradition; the Spanish of the Northwest and Cuyo; Yiddish; Qom, Wichí and Pilagá (for Formosa's memories of a rural doctor). **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality; it includes visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare anarchist, socialist, Radical and Catholic ideals of the good society before 1930.
- **The Conurbano:** check how parish, militant and volunteer workers describe the ideal that brought them.
- **La Plata and Córdoba:** check student generations, the 1918 and 1960s cohorts especially, and how they later judged themselves.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check the ideals carried by Jewish, Volga German and Mennonite colonists, and their later disappointments.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check rural teachers and Kolla communities' own visions of the good life.
- **Cuyo:** check the San Luis and Mendoza settings of cooperative and folk-song ideals.
- **Misiones and Corrientes:** compare the mission-utopia story told by outsiders with Guaraní accounts of the missions.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check Qom, Wichí and Pilagá memories of doctors, missionaries and teachers who came with ideals.
- **Syrian-Lebanese, Armenian and Jewish communities:** check each one's ideal of a homeland and of Argentina.
- **Evangelical, Catholic and secular volunteers:** check what each says moves them to serve.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the Jesuit missions, the Radicals and the 1960s; mixed on volunteers and rural professionals; thin on Native peoples' own ideals of right living in published form.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; 18th-century Italian for Muratori; Guaraní for mission-era and present usage; Yiddish for colony writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1970s, armed militancy and the dictatorship are contested: give each account as an account, never a verdict on a generation. The mission "utopia" is an outsider image; Guaraní views come first.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the 1960s generation pointers came closest, since memoirs and studies often speak for a whole cohort; each stays as one voice.
