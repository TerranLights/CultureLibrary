*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare café, club and corner as the places friendships form, by generation.
- **The Conurbano:** check how *la barra* of the corner is described by its members and by police.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check farm-town friendships around the *boliche*, the *asado* and the bowling club.
- **Córdoba and Rosario:** check student cities, where many friendships start among newcomers from small towns.
- **Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán:** check carnival *compadres* and *comadres*, and Kolla terms for a trusted companion.
- **Santiago del Estero and Catamarca:** check friendships kept by seasonal migrants across harvests.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check Guaraní terms for friends and the Ukrainian and Polish settlers' ties.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check how Qom, Wichí, Moqoit and Pilagá speakers name friendship apart from kinship.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Armenian communities:** check school and youth-movement friendships that last a life.
- **Okinawan, Korean and Chinese communities:** check friendships inside and outside the community, by generation.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check how friendship with Argentines is described at school and work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, tango and television; mixed in surveys; thin for Native languages' friendship terms and for men's friendship studies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with *lunfardo*; 19th-century gauchesque; Guaraní; Yiddish, Arabic and Armenian; Japanese for Okinawan association papers; Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *boludo* and similar words are insults outside friendship; record their use, never adopt them. Baptism books name living families in recent volumes; use older or aggregate data.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the Okinawan saying came closest, since its spelling and gloss come from community publications that need checking rather than any standing rule.
