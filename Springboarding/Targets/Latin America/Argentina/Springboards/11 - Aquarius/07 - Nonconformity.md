## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare the avant-garde, the anarchists and the rock scene as three ways of standing apart.
- **The Conurbano:** check *rolingas*, *cumbieros* and Evangelical youth as rival styles in the same streets.
- **The Pampa and La Pampa:** check how farm towns treat their eccentrics, and the Waldensian and Mennonite colonies as dissenting churches.
- **Córdoba:** check *cuarteto* fans, the hill-town hippies and students as different nonconformities.
- **Rosario:** check the city's anarchist and rock traditions against its image of itself.
- **Tucumán, Salta and Jujuy:** check carnival license and how Kolla and city youth each mark difference.
- **Cuyo:** check how outsiders and artists are received in Mendoza and San Juan towns.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check outlaw memory (Mate Cosido) and how Qom and Wichí young people describe pressure from church and school.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** check *payé* healers and folk-saint devotees as standing outside church norms.
- **Jewish, Syrian-Lebanese and Japanese communities:** check secular and religious dissenters inside each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the avant-garde, rock and anarchism; mixed on provincial youth styles; thin on Native and rural eccentrics in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with *lunfardo*; 19th-century gauchesque; Italian for the anarchist press; Yiddish for secular-dissenter papers; Italian and French for Waldensian records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship's lists of "subversive" books and music, and the 1960s-70s break of artists with institutions, are contested ground: give each as an account. Police files on sexual minorities name people; use published studies.
