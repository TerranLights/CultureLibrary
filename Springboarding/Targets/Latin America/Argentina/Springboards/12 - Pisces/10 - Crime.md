# Argentina (without Patagonia): Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish with *lunfardo*; rural Litoral and Chaco Spanish; Guaraní; Quechua; Yiddish; colonial Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. The law itself is researched in Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet points there and does not repeat it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare the wrongdoer in tabloid and broadsheet pages.
- **The Conurbano:** check how settlement neighbors honor or condemn local young men killed by police.
- **Santa Fe and Rosario:** check barrio residents' own accounts of dealers against the provincial press.
- **Córdoba:** check what the colonial files and today's local press each call the worst crime.
- **Corrientes and the Chaco:** check how rustling is judged by ranchers, peons and Guaraní-speaking villagers.
- **Tucumán and Salta:** check how voters and local papers judge officials' theft, against national press.
- **Bolivian and Paraguayan communities:** check the sweatshop wrongs as the workers tell them.
- **The Jewish community of Once:** check how a community answers crimes against it and by its members.
- **Qom, Wichí and other Chaco peoples:** check how each people speaks of harm and repair, as it shares this.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for urban crime news and political corruption; mixed for rural crime; thin for Native peoples' own ideas of wrongdoing.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; colonial Spanish for the Córdoba files; Guaraní; Quechua; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** victims' families are living; the dictatorship's crimes, the AMIA bombing (1994) and Napalpí (1924) are contested ground, given as accounts; police killings are disputed case by case. Law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** old Litoral and Chaco press linked rustling to Native peoples and the rural poor; the rustling row survives only for gathering how each speaker frames the wrong.
