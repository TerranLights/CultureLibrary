# Argentina (without Patagonia): Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Argentina (without Patagonia), region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Rioplatense Spanish with *lunfardo*; Northwest Spanish; Guaraní; Quechua and Santiago Quichua; Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Buenos Aires city:** compare fear of crime and fear of economic collapse in one household.
- **The Conurbano:** check what parents fear for their sons on the street, and what young men fear from police.
- **La Plata and the Pampa:** check flood memory and how residents now take weather alerts.
- **Salta, Tucumán and Jujuy:** check the Ucumar, *susto* and the earthquake vow as three registers of fear.
- **Santiago del Estero:** check fright illnesses and their cures in Quichua-speaking homes.
- **Corrientes and Misiones:** compare which Guaraní monsters are still told to children, and by whom.
- **The Chaco and Formosa:** check what Qom, Wichí and Pilagá elders warn the young against, as they share it.
- **The Jewish community of Once and Belgrano:** check how members describe fear after the bombings.
- **Bolivian, Paraguayan and Peruvian communities:** check fear of being targeted and of the police.
- **Mendoza and San Juan:** check how quake drills shape everyday fear in Cuyo.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state terror and fear of crime; mixed for folklore of fear; thin for Native peoples' own accounts of dangers.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *lunfardo*; Guaraní; Quechua and Santiago Quichua; Yiddish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship, Rosas's era and the AMIA bombing are contested ground: give each as an account; migrants and Native peoples must not be cast as the feared.
- **Held for review:** the studies of xenophobic talk quote slurs against migrants; they stay on the sheet for analyzing that talk, never for repeating it.
