# Chile: The domain, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Kunza (revitalization); Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, beginnings, initiative, and the naming ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *comunas* across the class line: check whether naming fashions and dress codes separate high-income from *población* districts.
- Norte Grande families of nitrate and copper towns against the Central Valley's huaso families: check regional dress and the working body as a source of self.
- Mapuche families in Santiago (*warriache*): check Mapuche first names, double surnames and lineage terms in an urban registry setting.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay communities of the far north: check what marks a person's full adulthood and whether Kunza names are revived.
- Rapa Nui families: check clan names, body paint and tattoo at the Tapati against daily use.
- Palestinian-, Croatian-, Italian- and Basque-descended families: check how surnames and first names carry origin over generations.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check how names are written into Chilean records and what changes.
- Catholic and Evangelical households: check baptismal and saints' names against conversion as a new self.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names, census and essays; mixed for the body and dress; thin for Diaguita, Colla and Chango personhood. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs, and Rapa Nui's status); this sign's sheets take the north and center as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; Mapudungun and Aymara for personhood terms; Rapa Nui for clan and body marks; Kreyòl and Arabic for the newer and older immigrant naming records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Chinchorro and other human remains; Rapa Nui clan knowledge; class labels (*flaite*, *cuico*) are slurs in many mouths.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; the personal names of ceremonial specialists were considered and kept out, since their own communities decide what is shared.
