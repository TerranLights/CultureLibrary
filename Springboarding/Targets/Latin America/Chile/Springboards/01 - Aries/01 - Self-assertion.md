*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's employers and *trabajadoras de casa particular* (domestic workers) and their union: check who may refuse, ask and complain inside the household.
- Nitrate- and copper-town families of Tarapacá and Antofagasta: check union assemblies as a school of speaking up, generation by generation.
- Central Valley families of the old haciendas (O'Higgins, Maule, Ñuble): check how the memory of *patrón* and *inquilino* shapes requests to authority.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check speaking order at gatherings against workplace speech.
- Aymara and Quechua communities of Arica y Parinacota: check assembly speech in Aymara against Spanish in dealings with the state.
- Rapa Nui families: check clan-based speaking roles against island-wide bodies.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian newcomers: check complaint channels used and avoided.
- Pentecostal congregations: check testimony as a sanctioned form of speaking before others.
- Women and men in the same workplace: check who negotiates pay and who interrupts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest and labor history; mixed for daily talk (conversation studies exist, mostly Santiago); thin for Diaguita, Colla and Chango speech norms.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with colloquial and *coa* registers (literal glosses mislead here); Mapudungun and Aymara for assembly speech; Kreyòl for complaints made by Haitian newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2010 Rapa Nui occupations and the 2019 protests are contested; present each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; complaint-law pointers (consumer rights) belong to Libra, and only SERNAC's statistics are kept here.
