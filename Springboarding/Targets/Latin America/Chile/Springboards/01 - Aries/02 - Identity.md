## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Norte Grande, Norte Chico, Valparaíso, Santiago, Central Valley and Biobío: check which regional label comes first when people introduce themselves.
- Santiago's *comunas* by class: check whether school, surname or *comuna* is asked first.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check *warriache* and *champurria* self-labels across generations.
- Aymara, Quechua, Lickanantay, Diaguita, Colla and Chango peoples: check how recent official recognition changed self-naming.
- Rapa Nui families: check island, clan and Chilean labels used side by side.
- Afro-Chileans of Arica: check self-naming before and after recognition campaigns.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- (Antofagasta), Italian- and Basque-descended families, and the Jewish community: check which ancestry is kept and which is dropped.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check whether children born in Chile answer "Chilean".
- Nitrate-era families: check *pampino* identity after the oficinas closed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national-identity essays and surveys; mixed for regional identity; thin for Chango and Colla self-description, where community organizations are the first route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Kunza word lists for self-names; Rapa Nui for island names; Arabic for community newspapers of the early 1900s.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous recognition, the War of the Pacific and Rapa Nui's relation to the state are contested; racialist texts are sources to study, not views to repeat.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; genealogical societies' elite-lineage catalogs were weighed and left for Capricorn's heritage concepts.
