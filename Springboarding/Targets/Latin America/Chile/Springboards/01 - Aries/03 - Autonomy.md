## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's high-income and *población* households: check the age of leaving home and who decides it.
- Central Valley families of former hacienda lands (Maule, Ñuble, O'Higgins): check how decisions moved after the agrarian reform.
- Antofagasta and Tarapacá: check regionalist claims against Santiago's control of mining income.
- Aymara and Quechua *ayllus* and *comunidades*: check collective decisions over water and herds against individual migration to Arica and Iquique.
- Lickanantay communities of San Pedro de Atacama: check who speaks for the community in dealings with companies.
- Rapa Nui families: check clan bodies against elected island officials.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check personal choice of partner and faith against kin expectations.
- Pentecostal and Catholic congregations: check self-governing churches against a diocesan hierarchy.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Bolivian newcomers: check how far migrants decide their own work and housing, given visa rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for youth and gender surveys; mixed for regional self-rule; thin for Juan Fernández Islands decision-making, where community and fishers' organizations are the first route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Mapudungun for collective-decision vocabulary; Rapa Nui for the island's own bodies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** bodily autonomy (abortion, end of life) is contested; its law belongs to Libra and its legal culture to Capricorn; Rapa Nui self-rule is contested ground.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; medical-ethics sources on patient consent were considered and kept for a later sign's concepts on health and care.
