## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Norte Chico mining families (Atacama, Coquimbo): check whether and how small-scale prospecting and *pirquinería* pass from parent to child.
- Iquique and Arica traders: check the role of free-trade commerce in starting a business.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and high-income *comunas*: check need-driven against opportunity-driven starts.
- Huaso families of the Central Valley: check who calls the work party at harvest and *trilla*.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check collective work against individual business.
- Aymara families moving from the highlands to Arica: check whether *ayni* follows them into town, and in what form.
- Rapa Nui families: check *umanga* in house-building and festival preparation.
- Palestinian-Chilean, Croatian-Chilean (Antofagasta) and Italian-Chilean (Valparaíso) families: check how a first shop or workshop is told in family memory.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check delivery work, street vending and small restaurants as starts.
- Evangelical congregations: check church-planting as initiative by lay members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for entrepreneurship statistics; mixed for civic initiative; thin for Diaguita and Colla communities' work traditions, where regional universities and community councils are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including business and colloquial registers; Kreyòl for newcomers' trades; Arabic for early commercial records of the Patronato families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *campamentos* and informal trade are policed; protect people's identities; the 1980s soup kitchens carry dictatorship-era memories.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; labor-law and tax rules on self-employment were left to Libra.
