## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago founding memory and Valparaíso's port memory: check which founding dates each city celebrates and who is left out.
- Norte Grande towns built for nitrate and copper: check how company towns mark their own founding and closing.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check the June new year against January 1 in the same households.
- Lickanantay and Diaguita communities: check whose origin is told at heritage sites.
- Rapa Nui families: check origin narratives in clan memory against published versions.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check We Tripantu in the city against ties to southern home communities.
- Catholic and Evangelical families: check infant baptism against adult conversion as a person's start.
- Returnees from exile and their children: check starting over in a country left decades before.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian newcomers: check the first months in Chile as told in their own groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for founding history and archaeology; mixed for household rites; thin for Chango and Colla beginnings, where regional museums and community councils are the first stop.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Mapudungun for new-year names and prayers; Rapa Nui for origin chants; Kreyòl for New Year customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some new-year prayers and offerings are kept within communities; Valdivia's account is one side of a conquest.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; birth-registration law for children of foreigners belongs to Libra, so only the parish and civil records stay here.
