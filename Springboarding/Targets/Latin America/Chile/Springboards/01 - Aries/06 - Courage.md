# Chile: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish, Mapudungun, Aymara, Quechua, Haitian Creole, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Navy and army families of Valparaíso and Santiago: check how battlefield courage is taught at home against at school.
- Volunteer firefighters' companies of Italian, German, French and other origins: check how a company's founding people shape its honors.
- Coal-mining families of Lota and Coronel and copper-mining families of the north: check endurance underground in family memory.
- Families of the disappeared and the executed: check courage as these families speak of it, distinct from official accounts.
- Huaso families of the Central Valley: check horsemanship and danger on the land as courage.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how *weichafe* is used in city organizations, and by whom.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay communities: check strength and endurance words in herding and highland travel.
- Rapa Nui families: check sea-going courage in fishing and canoe revival.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check migration stories as tests of nerve.
- Pentecostal congregations: check public preaching on streets and buses as social courage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war, mining and human-rights memory; mixed for everyday courage; thin for Diaguita and Chango traditions of courage, where regional museums are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, with care for *coraje*; Mapudungun and Aymara strength words; Kreyòl and Spanish for migrant testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship period, the War of the Pacific and protest deaths are contested; victims' families decide how names are used.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; military-justice records were weighed for this sheet and left to Capricorn's legal culture.
