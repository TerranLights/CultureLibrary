## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Norte Chico *pirquinero* families: check how independence from employers is weighed against risk and income.
- Santiago's young adults by *comuna*: check *allegamiento* and age of leaving home across classes.
- Central Valley smallholders and seasonal *temporeras* (fruit-packing workers): check self-provisioning against wage dependence.
- Biobío coal-town families after the mines closed (Lota, Coronel): check how families describe the loss of a single employer.
- Aymara and Quechua highland families: check herd and terrace self-provisioning against town wages in Arica.
- Lickanantay communities: check collective independence in water and land against individual jobs in mining.
- Rapa Nui families: check food from land and sea against shipped goods.
- Afro-Chileans of the Azapa Valley: check how the memory of abolition (1823) is told in family history.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian households: check remittances sent home against help received in Chile.
- Elders across classes: check living alone against moving in with children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household and labor surveys; mixed for self-provisioning; thin for Juan Fernández and Chango coastal households, where fishers' organizations and municipal records are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Rapa Nui for land and sovereignty terms; Kunza for the chants of the canal-cleaning rites (*talátur*) *check*; Kreyòl for remittance households.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Rapa Nui sovereignty and northern water disputes are contested; talk of regional secession is left out here except as comparison.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; welfare law and water law were weighed here and left to Libra.
