# Chile: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (with *coa*), Mapudungun, Aymara, Quechua, Haitian Creole, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and high-income *comunas*: check where anger may be shown in public and where it is held in.
- Norte Grande families with nitrate-era memory: check whether and how the 1907 massacre is told in family memory, and with what feeling.
- Football supporters of Colo-Colo, Universidad de Chile, Everton and Santiago Wanderers: check rivalry anger at the stadium against everyday life.
- Women and men in the same households: check how anger is named, and who is allowed to show it.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check anger words and whether anger is linked to illness.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check community means of settling quarrels.
- Rapa Nui families: check how grievance toward the state is voiced, given the contested land disputes.
- Haitian newcomers: check *move san* and anger as experienced in clinics, at work and with neighbors.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish communities: check how anger about distant conflicts is expressed in Chile.
- Pentecostal and Catholic congregations: check teaching on anger and its control.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest and political anger; mixed for family and daily anger; thin for Colla and Diaguita emotion vocabularies, where linguists at northern universities are the first contact.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish slang, which changes fast; Kreyòl for folk-health terms; Mapudungun, Aymara and Quechua for emotion words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 2019 protest violence, the 1907 massacre and family violence are contested or painful; protect victims' identities.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; Mapuche healers' own teaching on anger and illness was considered and left out as restricted knowledge rooted in the southern comparison lands.
