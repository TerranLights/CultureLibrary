*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Young men and young women across Santiago's *comunas*: check drinking and driving data by sex and class, not one youth culture.
- Huaso families and rodeo riders: check how falls and injuries are spoken of, and who is called reckless.
- Copper and nitrate-era mining families of Antofagasta and Tarapacá: check risk as a job requirement against risk as choice.
- Artisanal fishers of the Biobío coast, the Norte Grande and Juan Fernández: check going out in bad weather and who decides.
- Rapa Nui youth: check festival daring against everyday caution at sea.
- Aymara and Quechua highland families: check caution words and risks of high-altitude travel; sources are scarce, so community organizations come first.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian delivery and construction workers: check exposure to road and workplace risk that is imposed rather than chosen.
- Evangelical congregations: check abstinence from drink and betting against the wider holiday calendar.
- Surfers, climbers and off-road drivers: check how they draw the line between skill and rashness.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for road, drug and workplace statistics; mixed for attitudes to daring; thin for Indigenous terms for rashness, which need speakers rather than dictionaries.
- **Languages needed:** Chilean colloquial Spanish, where praise and blame for risk share words; Rapa Nui for festival terms; Kreyòl for betting and lottery speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Antuco deaths and mining disasters are painful and contested; drug-use data on minors needs care.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; drink-driving law (the "zero tolerance" reforms) was weighed and left to Libra.
