# Chile: The domain, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and its regional registers; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, self-worth, and the folkways of food, dress and wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's high- and low-income *comunas* (Vitacura against La Pintana): check how spending shares and the goods households name as most valued differ.
- Copper and port households of Calama, Antofagasta and Iquique against farm households of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how wage booms and harvests shape buying.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check which saddles, spurs and *mantas* are kept, and how they pass down.
- Aymara and Quechua communities of Arica y Parinacota and Tarapacá: check labor and goods exchanged across the agricultural year, and what changes in Arica's markets.
- Lickanantay communities of the Salar de Atacama: check how water and grazing are reckoned as goods.
- Rapa Nui families: check feast duties and family land against goods shipped from the mainland.
- Family firms founded by Palestinian, Croatian, Italian and Basque immigrants: check what each generation is handed, and what it sells off.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian communities: check remittances and savings groups beside bank use.
- Coal households of Lota and Coronel against nitrate-era families: check what company towns let workers own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for spending, food history and the elite; mixed for dress; thin for Diaguita, Colla and Chango material culture (start from archaeology and community organizations). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs, and Rapa Nui's status); these sheets take the north and center, Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández as the core, the south as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for nearly all records; Aymara and Kunza for northern terms of herds, water and exchange; Rapa Nui for land and feasting; Kreyòl and Arabic for household money talk in the two diaspora communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** northern and Rapa Nui water and land claims are contested; grave goods in museums; household finances are private.
- **Held for review:** this sheet set nothing aside; the pointers weighed were national surveys, archives and ethnographies, and none fell under a standing rule.
