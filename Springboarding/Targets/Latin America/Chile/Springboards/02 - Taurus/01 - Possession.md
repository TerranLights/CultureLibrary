# Chile: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (north and center, Rapa Nui, Juan Fernández). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and *coa*; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Población* households of southern and western Santiago against those of the eastern *comunas*: check what is pawned, stolen and replaced, and where belongings are kept in small housing.
- Hill neighborhoods of Valparaíso and Viña del Mar: check what fire survivors say they would carry out next time.
- Central Valley *huaso* and peasant families: check which tools, saddles and furniture pass down, and the *entierro* tales told on old estates.
- Norte Chico pilgrims (Andacollo) and Norte Grande dancers (La Tirana): check which objects are vowed, carried or left at the shrine.
- Lickanantay, Aymara and Diaguita communities: check rules on the goods of the dead and on objects in museums.
- Rapa Nui families: check the two possessive classes in daily speech, and family keeping of carvings.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how a small island community shares tools and boats.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check what was carried in, what is sent back, and what is bought first.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check whether objects or papers from Beit Jala and Bethlehem are kept, and how they are passed on.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for theft, pawning and the colonial inventories; mixed for keepsakes and the dead; thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández material life.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *coa* for theft and pawning; Rapa Nui for possessive classes and repatriation; Aymara and Mapudungun for possessive grammar; Arabic for Palestinian-Chilean family keepsakes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains and grave goods (El Plomo, San Pedro); Rapa Nui and Lickanantay claims on held objects; fire losses are recent grief.
- **Held for review:** set aside nothing; the private collections of named living families were weighed and left out as personal, with the press and survey route used instead.
