## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Former hacienda tenants and reform-sector families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how land received in 1965 to 1973 was kept, sold or lost.
- *Pobladores* of Santiago's occupied-land districts and today's *campamento* residents in Antofagasta and Viña del Mar: check how title is sought and how it changes standing.
- Petorca and La Ligua smallholders against export growers: check how each side frames who should hold water, as accounts.
- Aymara and Quechua communities of the altiplano: check *sayaña* and communal fields against land registered under state titles.
- Lickanantay communities near the lithium and copper operations of the Salar de Atacama: check claims to water and land, and payments from firms.
- Rapa Nui clans, including the Hitorangi claim at the Hanga Roa hotel (2010): check family title against state holding, as each side tells it.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how urban associations hold sites for *ruka* and ceremony.
- Copper-town families of Calama and Rancagua: check how they speak of state ownership of the mines.
- Haitian, Peruvian and Venezuelan renters: check overcrowded rentals and the road to owning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land reform, housing and water; mixed for Indigenous tenure; thin for Colla and Diaguita land claims, where CONADI files and community organizations are the starting points.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for nearly all; Aymara for northern tenure terms; Rapa Nui for family land; Mapudungun for urban ceremonial sites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land reform and its reversal after 1973; water conflicts; Rapa Nui land claims and the island's status; occupations described differently by each side. Present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** none withheld; the letter of the water and land codes was set aside for Libra under the law rule, not for this sheet.
