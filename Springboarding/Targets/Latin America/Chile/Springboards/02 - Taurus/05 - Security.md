## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Pensioners of the old state schemes against those under individual accounts: check how each describes old age and its income.
- Permanent and subcontracted copper workers of Calama, Antofagasta and Rancagua: check how contract status shapes saving and planning.
- *Población* residents and residents of gated estates in Santiago (Chicureo, Peñalolén): check fear of crime, bars, alarms and guards, and who pays.
- Coastal towns of the 2010 tsunami (Constitución, Dichato, Talcahuano) and of the northern quakes (Iquique 2014): check how disaster changed household preparation.
- Aymara herders and farmers of the altiplano: check food stores and the agricultural year's lean seasons.
- Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández households: check how island supply lines shape stockpiling.
- Haitian and Venezuelan residents: check how papers, contracts and housing affect felt security.
- Chilean and migrant domestic workers: check security in live-in work, as their unions report it.
- Catholic and Evangelical congregations: check church funds and *olla común* support in hard years.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pensions, crime fear and labor; mixed for disaster memory; thin for Colla and Chango household security, which needs community contact.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for nearly all; Haitian Creole for the role of papers in security; Aymara for stores and lean seasons; Rapa Nui for the closure years.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pension politics; the 2010 warning failure and blame; fear of crime and migrants in the news, which must not be taken as a finding about any group.
- **Held for review:** this sheet held back no source; private security firms' marketing was considered but left to the commerce stage of research.
