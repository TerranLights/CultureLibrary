## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Former *pampinos* and their children in Iquique and Antofagasta against families of working copper towns: check how continuity is kept with a vanished place versus a living one.
- Chuquicamata families now in Calama: check how a moved community marks anniversaries.
- Central Valley families in old adobe towns hit by the 2010 earthquake: check who rebuilt the old way and who sold.
- Valparaíso hill residents who stayed through fires and those who left: check how each speaks of the neighborhood.
- Old Santiago families and newer arrivals to the eastern *comunas*: check how a "family of always" is reckoned.
- Aymara emigrants to the coast: check yearly returns to the village feast.
- Rapa Nui families: check continuity with land and sites against the island's change.
- Juan Fernández founding-family descendants: check what is kept from the nineteenth century, after the 2010 tsunami.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check what "settling" means to them, and how long it takes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inflation, census and heritage; mixed for family stability (changing definitions); thin for Colla and Diaguita continuity, where community histories are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for nearly all; Aymara for village ties; Rapa Nui for sites and ancestors; Haitian Creole for newcomers' settling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "order" story of Portales and of 1973 is contested, and each account is presented as an account; displaced towns; Rapa Nui sites with restricted meaning.
- **Held for review:** no item was withheld; this sheet kept to Chilean sources and placed comparison data (Hofstede, GLOBE) as a test, not a finding.
