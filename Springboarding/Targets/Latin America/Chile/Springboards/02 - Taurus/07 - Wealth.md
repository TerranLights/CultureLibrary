*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families of the eastern Santiago *comunas* (Las Condes, Vitacura, Lo Barnechea) against families of the southern ones: check how wealth is shown, hidden and talked about in each.
- Old landed families of the Central Valley against new mining and retail fortunes: check which kind is spoken of as respectable.
- Norte Grande copper workers on high wages: check what a mining salary buys and how it is displayed in Calama and Antofagasta.
- Croatian-descended families of Antofagasta and Palestinian-Chilean families: check how a migrant fortune is told across generations.
- Aymara herders of the altiplano: check herd size, land and kin as measures of wealth.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check silver, land elsewhere, and kin as wealth.
- Rapa Nui families: check income from tourism and how it is shared among kin.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian residents: check what wealth is sent home, and what the family there counts as success.
- Pentecostal congregations: check teaching on wealth, tithes and blessing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inequality and the elite; mixed for attitudes to the rich; thin for Indigenous reckonings of wealth outside the Aymara, where community sources are the start.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and class slang for talk of the rich; Aymara for herd wealth; Mapudungun for silver and titles; Arabic for Palestinian-Chilean family histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** named fortunes and families; price-fixing cases; dictatorship-era privatizations and who gained from them, contested and to be read as accounts.
- **Held for review:** the inequality literature in economics was screened under the standing rules, and no pointer from it needed setting aside.
