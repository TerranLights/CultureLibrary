# Chile: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (north and center, Rapa Nui, Juan Fernández). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and slang; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Teenagers and young adults in Santiago, Concepción and Antofagasta: check whether phone checking and location sharing are named as care or control, by age and sex.
- Catholic and Pentecostal couples: check marriage teaching on jealousy and on who may go out alone.
- Mining families on long shift rotations (*turnos*) in the Norte Grande: check jealousy and control when one partner is away for days.
- Artisanal fishers of the *caletas* from Coquimbo to the Biobío: check how seabed areas are guarded and how disputes are settled.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check courtship customs and family control of daughters, researched as questions.
- Rapa Nui families: check how the island's limits on outsiders are talked about at home and in the press.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian couples: check how jealousy and control are spoken of, and which services they reach.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check how *ghīra* is used in family speech, and how it changes across generations.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check whether *cahuín* keeps any sense of its source word in Mapudungun speech.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for partner violence and dating surveys; mixed for jealousy as a feeling; thin for territorial claims among Aymara and Lickanantay communities, which need community sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and youth slang for dating talk; Haitian Creole for service access; Arabic for family terms; Rapa Nui for the island's own view of outsiders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** femicide and partner violence; custody disputes; Rapa Nui limits, read differently on the island and on the mainland.
- **Held for review:** stalking-app marketing and private case files were weighed and left out; nothing here fell under the standing rules.
