# Chile: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (north and center, Rapa Nui, Juan Fernández). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and colloquial speech; Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families of the disappeared and of former political prisoners: check how persistence is told from within, and how others describe it.
- Copper-union members of Antofagasta and El Teniente: check how holding out in a strike is judged at home and in the press.
- Diaguita and farming families of the Huasco Valley: check how two decades of opposition are remembered.
- Coastal residents of Quintero and Puchuncaví: check who stays and who leaves, and why.
- Pentecostal and Methodist congregations of Valparaíso: check how the 1909-1910 split is told on each side.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities in water disputes: check how refusal is decided and voiced.
- Rapa Nui families in land and park disputes: check how persistence is framed on the island.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how the Selkirk story is told locally.
- Older and younger *huaso* families: check the sense of *mañoso* and *porfiado* across generations.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check how the Chilean words for stubbornness are learned and applied to them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for strikes, human-rights persistence and mining conflicts; mixed for personal stubbornness; thin for Colla and Chango, where community contacts are needed.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and its colloquialisms; Mapudungun for urban Mapuche usage; Haitian Creole and Arabic for how newcomers and older diasporas name stubbornness in their own speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship, its victims and the Army's statement; the 1972 stoppage; mining conflicts; each told as an account, never as the trait of a group.
- **Held for review:** the personality-psychology literature on openness was looked at and left for a later stage; no pointer was withheld under the rules.
