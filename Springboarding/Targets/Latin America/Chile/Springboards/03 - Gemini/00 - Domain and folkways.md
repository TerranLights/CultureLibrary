**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (with northern, *huaso* and *coa* registers); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza (revitalization); Rapa Nui; Chilean Sign Language; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: the style of talk, the languages, neighbors, short journeys, and the speech and learning ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *comunas* across income: check whether *voseo*, slang and *coa* mark class and age, and where speakers switch.
- Norte Grande port and mining towns against the Central Valley countryside: check regional words, intonation and the *huaso* register.
- Mapuche families in Santiago (*warriache*): check where Mapudungun is used, taught or lost across generations (Wallmapu, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay communities of the north: check which language is used in the village, the market and school, and how Kunza revival classes run.
- Rapa Nui families: check use of Rapa Nui, Spanish and Tahitian loans at home and at school.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how news moves and the young are taught far from mainland schools.
- Haitian families: check Kreyòl at home, Spanish at work, and the children's role as interpreters.
- Deaf Chileans: check LSCh in schools and at home.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Italian-descended families: check which generation still speaks the ancestral language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialectology, schooling and travel; mixed for neighbors (studies are local); thin for Diaguita, Colla and Chango speech ways (start with community organizations). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs, and Rapa Nui's status); this sign takes the north and center, Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández as the core, the south as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for most records; Mapudungun, Aymara, Quechua and Rapa Nui for teaching materials; LSCh for Deaf sources; Kreyòl for the newest school families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native teaching stays within the community; *coa* and class-marked speech are stigmatized, so read them as data.
- **Held for review:** grammars, surveys and dictionaries were weighed and none set aside; living Native language keepers are left unnamed for their communities to name.
