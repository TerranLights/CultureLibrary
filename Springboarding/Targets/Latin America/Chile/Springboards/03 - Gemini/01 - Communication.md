*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's high-income *comunas* against its *poblaciones*: check how directness, softening and markers like *po* shift by class, and who is corrected.
- Iquique and Antofagasta against Talca and Chillán: check northern and Central Valley intonation and the pace of conversation, as heard and as recorded.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check formal greeting and oratory carried into urban gatherings (Wallmapu's practice as a comparison case).
- Aymara and Quechua speakers in Arica, Putre and Pica: check which language is used at fiestas, markets and the clinic.
- Rapa Nui speakers: check recitation, address and switching with Spanish in Hanga Roa.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian newcomers: check who interprets in clinics and schools, and what newcomers report as hard to follow in local speech.
- Deaf Chileans: check LSCh across generations and the oral-school past.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check Arabic phrases kept in greeting and blessing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corpora, attitudes and telecom figures; mixed for letters (archive-based); thin for Native oratory in central Chile and for Colla and Diaguita speech norms.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and *coa* for recorded talk; Mapudungun for oratory terms; Kreyòl for health-communication materials; LSCh for Deaf media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recordings of 1973 carry opposed readings; Native ceremonial speech may not be for outsiders; clinic interpreting involves patient privacy.
