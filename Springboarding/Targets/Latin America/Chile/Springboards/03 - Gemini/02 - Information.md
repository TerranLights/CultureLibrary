## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Older and younger Santiaguinos: check the move from TV news and radio to phones and group chats, and whom each age trusts.
- Readers across the political divide: check how the same 1973 or 2019 events reach them through different channels, without taking any account as the norm.
- Antofagasta and Iquique against Santiago: check regional dailies, regional TV and the sense of being covered or ignored by the capital press.
- Rural Maule and Ñuble against the metro area: check connection gaps and the role of radio.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check who controls information about sites, water and remains, and how outside researchers are received.
- Rapa Nui: check island-made news against mainland coverage of the island.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check where they learn about papers, rights and work, and how chain messages spread.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check community periodicals of the early twentieth century and today's Arabic-language channels.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the press, surveys and transparency figures; mixed for community and diaspora media; thin for Andean record-keeping in Chile (mostly archaeological) and for Juan Fernández.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; Quechua for khipu terms; Rapa Nui for island radio; Kreyòl for migrant information channels; Arabic for the old community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sealed Valech testimony; human-rights archives; ownership disputes in media history; Native knowledge about sites that communities restrict.
- **Held for review:** press, archive and survey pointers were reviewed for this sheet, and none needed setting aside.
