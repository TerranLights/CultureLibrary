## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families in private, subsidized and municipal schools: check which home learning (reading, tutoring, homework help) separates them.
- Central Valley farm families against Santiago: check children's work at harvest as a learning setting.
- Norte Grande mining towns: check trade learning between fathers, sons and companies, and how it changed after the nitrate era.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check learning by observing in herding and in fiestas.
- Lickanantay families: check Kunza revival classes for children, and who teaches them.
- Rapa Nui: check what children learn at the Tapati beside school.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check grandparents as teachers.
- Haitian and Venezuelan families: check who helps with homework when parents work long hours.
- Pentecostal and Catholic households: check Sunday school and catechism as early teaching.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history and early-childhood surveys; mixed for trade learning (studies are scattered); thin for Colla and Diaguita learning ways and for Juan Fernández schooling.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** chant and ritual knowledge on Rapa Nui and in the north may be withheld; children in surveys and schools need protection.
