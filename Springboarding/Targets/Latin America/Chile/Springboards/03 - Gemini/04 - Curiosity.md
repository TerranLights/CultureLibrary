# Chile: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Elqui and Coquimbo towns living beside observatories: check how residents describe the visitors and the science.
- Santiago's *comunas*: check museum and science-fair participation by income.
- Norte Grande mining families: check curiosity about the desert's past (fossils, ghost towns, nitrate ruins).
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check which questions outsiders may ask about sky, water and sites.
- Rapa Nui: check how the island answers tourists' and scientists' questions, and what it keeps closed.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how visitors' curiosity about Selkirk and the wreck of the *Dresden* is met.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check which personal questions they are asked and how they report them.
- Catholic and Evangelical households: check teaching on prying, idle questions and wonder.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for astronomy outreach, science surveys and naturalists; mixed for children's questions; thin for curiosity norms among the Colla and Chango.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; Aymara and Kunza for sky terms; Rapa Nui for island responses to visitors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native sky and site knowledge; Rapa Nui remains and objects; true crime involves victims' families.
- **Held for review:** individual observatory scientists were left out on purpose, since institutions and their public programs answer this concept better than any one researcher; nothing else was put aside.
