# Chile: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Large rural families of Maule and Ñuble against small urban ones: check eldest-child duties and the split of land.
- *Huaso* families: check which brother takes over the horses, the land and the rodeo team.
- Norte Grande mining families: check brothers working the same shift or the same firm.
- Aymara families: check *jilata* and *kullaka* used for non-kin, and the eldest's role at fiestas.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check *peñi* and *lamngen* as address beyond the household.
- Rapa Nui: check elder and younger sibling roles in family land and feasts.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Italian-descended families: check brothers in family firms and who inherits.
- Pentecostal congregations: check *hermano* and *hermana* as address, and what the term asks of members.
- Haitian, Peruvian and Venezuelan newcomers: check siblings who migrate one after another.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demography and wills; mixed for sibling relations (psychology studies are few and local); thin for twins in folk belief and for Colla and Diaguita kin terms.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; Mapudungun, Aymara and Quechua for kin terms; Rapa Nui for elder-younger terms; Kreyòl for migrant family studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children in state care; irregular adoptions; inheritance disputes and family privacy.
- **Held for review:** for siblings, the candidates were demographic, archival and child-welfare sources; all stayed in, none being caught by a rule.
