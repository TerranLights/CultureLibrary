# Chile: The domain, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (northern, *huaso* and *coa* registers); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza (revitalization); Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what home is and who counts as family: household forms, fathering, roots, family memory, hospitality, the walls of the house, meals, and the building, family and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* against its eastern *comunas*: check generations per lot, and whether *allegados* count as one household or two.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check the country house layout and who inherits the home place.
- Norte Grande port and mining towns: check how shift work and company housing bear on household form.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay families: check the village house kept alongside the city one, and fiestas as the time of return.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how *reñma* is defined away from Wallmapu (Wallmapu itself, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Rapa Nui families: check family land, the *hare* and the feast as the household's frame.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how new households form where houses are few.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Italian-descended families: check whether family firm and family house overlap.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian newcomers: check shared rented rooms and kin abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household statistics, housing policy and architecture; mixed for fathering and family memory; thin for Diaguita, Colla and Chango households (start with their community organizations and CONADI registries). Where the nation's lands end is still to be settled: the southern line, and Rapa Nui's standing. This sheet covers the Norte Grande to the Biobío and the two island groups; the south is comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for household statistics; Aymara, Quechua and Mapudungun for house and kin words; Rapa Nui for family land; Kreyòl and Arabic for newcomer and Palestinian-Chilean households.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** housing poverty and *campamentos*; families split by the dictatorship and exile; Rapa Nui family histories belong to their families.
- **Held for review:** colonial household counts and missionary house descriptions were weighed as outsiders' records; they stay, to be read critically, and nothing was set aside under a rule.
