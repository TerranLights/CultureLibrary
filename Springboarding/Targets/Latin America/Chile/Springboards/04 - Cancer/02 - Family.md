## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Old landed and business families against *pobladores*: check how far kin obligations reach, and who is called on in trouble.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check *compadres* at the *rodeo* and the farm.
- Norte Grande mining families: check kin spread between the mine town and the coast, and who keeps the house.
- Aymara and Quechua families of Arica y Parinacota and Tarapacá: check *ayllu* ties kept in the city.
- Diaguita and Colla families: check how descent is reckoned now that surnames serve as proof.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check *küpal* and *lof* reckoned at a distance (Wallmapu, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Rapa Nui *hua'ai*: check lineage, adoption and land as one weave.
- Chilean Rom families: check the *kumpania* as household and kin; sources are few.
- Palestinian-, Basque-, Italian- and Croatian-descended families: check marriage within the community, generation by generation.
- Peruvian, Bolivian, Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check children and elders left behind and reunions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household data and for elite lineages; mixed for kin support (surveys, few ethnographies); thin for the Rom, Chango and Colla (start with community leaders and CONADI).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Quechua for kin terms in the north; Rapa Nui for *hua'ai*; Romani for Rom families; Kreyòl and Arabic for newcomer and Palestinian-Chilean kin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** paternity and illegitimacy; same-sex families and religious opposition; Rapa Nui genealogies and land are family property; family wealth studies are contested.
- **Held for review:** genealogy websites built by private individuals were weighed for elite lines and left out as unverifiable; no item met a standing rule.
