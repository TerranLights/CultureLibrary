# Chile: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families of the eastern *comunas* and the *poblaciones*: check the scheduled, enriched childhood against the freer one, and who minds the child (nanny, grandmother, kindergarten).
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check children taught to ride, herd and work beside parents.
- Norte Grande mining families: check how rotating shifts (*turnos*) bear on who minds the children.
- Aymara and Quechua families: check *uywaña* for children and animals alike, and children's herding.
- Lickanantay families of San Pedro and the *ayllus*: check teaching through the farming year.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check grandparents' part in passing on language.
- Rapa Nui families: check whose children are raised by kin other than parents.
- Peruvian, Bolivian, Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check children raised by grandmothers back home while mothers work here.
- Pentecostal families: check Sunday school and church discipline in upbringing.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check the role of Arabic, the Orthodox church and the community school.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for early childhood policy and surveys; mixed for everyday child-rearing; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Chango childhood (start with community organizations and local schools).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Quechua for raising terms and rites; Rapa Nui for the language nest; Mapudungun for urban Mapuche teaching; Kreyòl for newcomer families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** physical punishment and child-protection files; Native children's schooling under the missions; minors in any field study.
- **Held for review:** for nurture, the doubtful candidates were popular-psychology influencers whose standing is hard to judge from memory; they stay out until checked, and no rule set anything aside.
