## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Eastern Santiago *comunas* and the *poblaciones*: check private guards and gated streets against neighbor alarms and residents' committees.
- *Huaso* households of the Central Valley: check the dog, the gun and the neighbor as protection on isolated *fundos*.
- Norte Grande mining towns: check how vows to the Virgin and company safety rules sit side by side.
- Coastal *caletas* from Arica to Tomé: check saints, weather lore and rescue crews in guarding fishermen.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay herders: check protecting places, animals and water.
- Diaguita and Colla communities: check land guarded against mining, and how the communities tell it.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check *ngen* and protective practice away from Wallmapu (in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Rapa Nui: check *rahui* and the island's own control of visitors.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check rescue and warning on islands hit by the 2010 tsunami.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check whom they trust for help, and church networks.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check which blessings and devotional objects, if any, came with families from Bethlehem and Beit Jala.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime-fear and child-protection statistics; mixed for folk protection; thin for Chango and Colla practice (start with community and municipal sources).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Quechua for protecting places; Rapa Nui for *rahui*; Arabic for Palestinian-Chilean devotional objects.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** police conduct since 2019 is contested ground, to be read as competing accounts; some offerings to mountains are kept within the community; child-protection files are closed.
- **Held for review:** for protection, the borderline items were security-firm advertising and gun-club pages, kept out as sales material; no rule-based hold applied.
