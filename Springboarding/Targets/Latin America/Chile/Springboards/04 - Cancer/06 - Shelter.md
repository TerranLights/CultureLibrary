# Chile: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Venezuelan and Peruvian Spanish (newcomer sources). **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago center and the *comunas* along the Mapocho: check *rucos* by the river and whether shelters are used.
- Valparaíso's hills: check fire evacuations and kin who take in the burned-out.
- Antofagasta, Calama and Iquique: check newcomers sheltering in *campamentos*, tents and church halls.
- Colchane and the Aymara border villages: check what hosts gave and what strained them.
- *Huaso* families and *inquilinos* of the old *fundos*: check lodging owed to laborers, and the *afuerino* seasonal worker's bed.
- Concepción, Talcahuano and the Biobío coast: check the 2010 *aldeas* and who stayed longest.
- Rapa Nui: check refuge places in oral history and lodging of mainland workers.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check shelter on high ground after the 2010 tsunami.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check shared rooms, church lodging and street sleeping.
- Catholic and Evangelical congregations: check which run shelters, soup kitchens or night patrols.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for street counts, charity records and disaster shelter; mixed for migrant shelter (NGO and press); thin for Inca *tambos* as lived places and for Chango or Diaguita refuge customs (start with regional museums).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Quechua for *tambo* and border-village sources; Rapa Nui for refuge in oral history; Kreyòl for Haitian newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** street people and refuge residents are vulnerable subjects; 1973 asylum and the 2021 border arrivals are contested ground, to be read as competing accounts; women's refuge locations are confidential.
- **Held for review:** night shelters run by single parishes and Evangelical churches came up as candidates; their names and dates could not be recalled with confidence, so only the type appears, with no rule involved.
