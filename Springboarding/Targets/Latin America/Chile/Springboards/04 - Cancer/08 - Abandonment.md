# Chile: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Peruvian and Bolivian Spanish (newcomer sources). **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Mothers and fathers who leave: check whether the press, courts and neighbors judge each alike.
- *Huaso* and *inquilino* families of the old *fundos*: check the *huacho* in rural memory and who raised him.
- Norte Grande nitrate and copper towns: check the families of closed *oficinas* and camps, and who felt left by company and state.
- Lota and Coronel: check what coal families say about being left after the mines.
- Aymara villages of the *precordillera*: check elders left in emptied villages as the young move to Arica.
- Diaguita and Colla families: check emptying valleys and who stays.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check children raised by grandparents in Wallmapu while parents work in the city (Wallmapu, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Rapa Nui: check separations by illness and by schooling on the mainland.
- Bolivian, Peruvian, Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check whether a migrant's departure is spoken of as leaving or as being left, by the parent who went and by the kin who stayed.
- Catholic and Evangelical congregations: check visits to lone elders and orphan homes run by churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foundling history and child-care data; mixed for elders left and child support; thin for Rapa Nui and Diaguita sources on separation (start with local health and parish records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Quechua and Aymara roots for *huacho*; Rapa Nui for island accounts; Kreyòl and Andean Spanish for newcomer families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** foundling and adoption records are closed or restricted; irregular adoptions and child-care scandals; families of the abandoned are private persons.
- **Held for review:** the irregular-adoption groups were weighed again and kept out, since an earlier sign already carries them; nothing on this sheet was set aside by a rule.
