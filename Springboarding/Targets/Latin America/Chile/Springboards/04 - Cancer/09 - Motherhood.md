# Chile: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza (revitalization); Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Mothers in the eastern *comunas* and in the *poblaciones*: check paid help, work and the mother's day.
- Central Valley *huaso* and *inquilino* families: check the mother as the center of the house and the *velorio del angelito* in memory.
- Norte Grande mining families: check who runs the house during long mining shifts, and how mothers describe it.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay mothers: check birth customs, Pachamama and work in herding and fields.
- Diaguita and Colla mothers: check whether any sources record them in their own words; few are known.
- Mapuche mothers in Santiago: check *ñuke* and language handed to children (Wallmapu, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Rapa Nui mothers: check adoption within the *hua'ai* and birth on the island against the mainland.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian mothers: check birth in Chilean hospitals and mothering from afar.
- Catholic, Evangelical and Palestinian Orthodox mothers: check the Virgin, the church and the home altar.
- Single mothers and teen mothers: check how press and policy name them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth statistics, health and literature; mixed for mothers' own voices (memoirs, oral histories); thin for Rapa Nui, Diaguita and Colla mothering (start with community and health-post sources).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Aymara and Quechua for birth and Pachamama; Mapudungun for urban Mapuche mothers; Rapa Nui; Kreyòl for Haitian mothers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship's mothers of the disappeared and the Florvil case are contested ground, to be read as accounts; birth and health records are closed; Pachamama offerings may be private.
- **Held for review:** for motherhood, the weighed items were Marian apparition cults of uncertain dates, left as a type; nothing here was set aside by a rule.
