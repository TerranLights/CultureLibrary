# Chile: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Potters of Pomaire, Quinchamalí and the Diaguita revival in the Elqui Valley: check how each separates a faithful copy from a new piece.
- Saddlers and carvers of wooden stirrups for the rodeo in the Central Valley: check whether designs pass in families and who may vary them.
- Muralists and graffiti crews of Valparaíso and Santiago: check who is called an artist and who a vandal.
- Aymara and Quechua weavers of the far north: check design rights within the community.
- Lickanantay makers in San Pedro de Atacama: check how revival crafts are judged by elders and by buyers.
- Rapa Nui carvers: check the ceremonial-souvenir line and family designs.
- *Warriache* silversmiths and musicians in Santiago: check reworked forms made away from Wallmapu.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check whether the old textile mills and *taṭrīz* embroidery are spoken of as one craft line or two.
- Haitian newcomers: check painting and street trades learned by *degaje*.
- Santiago's design and startup circles: check what "innovation" means in their pitches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, murals and heritage crafts; mixed for invention and the creative economy; thin for Colla and Chango crafts and for Juan Fernández makers (start with the regional culture offices).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including the craft words of each town; Aymara and Quechua weaving terms; Rapa Nui carving vocabulary; Arabic for *taṭrīz*; Kreyòl for newcomers' trades.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Aymara and Rapa Nui designs may belong to families or communities, so publishing them needs leave; murals of the dictatorship years are contested ground and were often painted over. Who owns a work in law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside. Foreign dealers' catalogs of Chilean "folk art" came up beside the Isla Negra and Rari pointers; they were left out of the table and are worth a later look only for how outsiders priced the craft.
