# Chile: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Men in Norte Grande mining camps and men in Santiago offices: check what each is taught about showing feeling at work and at home.
- Secondary students of Santiago, Valparaíso and Concepción: check fights over hair, tattoos and uniforms, school by school.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check *tonada* and *cueca* as the channels for feeling.
- Aymara and Quechua residents of Arica and Iquique: check when dress and the *wiphala* come out in city life.
- *Warriache* in Santiago: check dress, silver and the *wenufoye* at marches and ceremonies.
- Rapa Nui families: check who takes revived tattoos and what the designs say.
- LGBTQ Chileans in Santiago and in smaller regional cities: check where they say they can be open.
- Evangelical congregations: check public testimony set against teaching on modest dress.
- People who came of age before and after 1990: check habits of self-censorship and their loss.
- Haitian newcomers: check what is said in Kreyòl among themselves and in Spanish at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for protest, song and sexual-diversity movements; mixed for everyday emotional expression; thin for Diaguita, Colla and Juan Fernández (start with local radio and school records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including *coa* and youth slang; Mapudungun and Aymara for symbols and songs; Rapa Nui for tattoo vocabulary; Kreyòl for newcomers' speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** song and protest under the dictatorship and in 2019 are contested ground, read as competing accounts; the *wenufoye* at marches touches the Wallmapu conflict (Araucanía-Patagonia), comparison only; LGBTQ informants need privacy.
- **Held for review:** the Pew pointer raised whether a foreign polling house can stand for Chilean attitudes to free speech; it stays, to be paired later with Chilean polls, and no item met a standing rule.
