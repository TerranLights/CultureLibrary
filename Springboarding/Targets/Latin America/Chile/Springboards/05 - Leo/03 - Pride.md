## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Iquique, Antofagasta and Calama: check what each city boasts of, and how it measures itself against Santiago.
- Concepción and the Biobío coast: check pride in the university, the rock bands and the steel and coal past.
- Valparaíso: check pride in the port's history set beside talk of decline.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check pride in horses, land and *chilenidad*.
- Copper and coal miners and their families: check pride in a trade before and after closures.
- Aymara, Lickanantay, Diaguita and Colla communities: check what led people to claim an Indigenous identity in recent censuses.
- Afro-Chileans of Arica: check pride as named by their own organizations.
- Rapa Nui families: check pride in returned heritage and in the language.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check how two prides are held and shown.
- Haitian, Peruvian and Venezuelan newcomers: check pride in origin against pressure to blend in.
- LGBTQ Chileans: check the word *orgullo* in Santiago and in smaller cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national and sporting pride; mixed for regional and craft pride; thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández (start with community groups and island records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including regional speech; Mapudungun and Aymara for slogans of pride; Rapa Nui for heritage statements; Arabic for hometown associations; Kreyòl for newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pride tied to the War of the Pacific touches Peru and Bolivia and is contested ground; the 1990s image of the model economy is disputed after 2019; Mapuche pride slogans relate to Wallmapu (Araucanía-Patagonia), comparison only.
- **Held for review:** chauvinist and supremacist writing on a "Chilean race" was weighed; Palacios's *Raza chilena* already stands on the Aries sheet on identity, so no such item was added here.
