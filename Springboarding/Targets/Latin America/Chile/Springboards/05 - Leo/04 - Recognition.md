## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Teachers of municipal, subsidized and private schools: check how each kind of school recognizes them, and what *tía* conveys.
- Seasonal fruit workers (*temporeros* and *temporeras*) of O'Higgins and Maule: check whose labor is named in the region's stories of export success.
- Venezuelan, Cuban and Haitian professionals: check how long credential recognition takes and what it costs them.
- Diaguita, Chango and Colla communities: check what official recognition changed in daily life.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check how a *mallku* or community president is honored at fiestas, and whether outsiders use the title (*check*).
- Afro-Chileans of Arica: check how their recent acknowledgment is marked locally.
- Rapa Nui families: check which island institutions the state treats as voices.
- Regions and Santiago: check national prizes by region of birth and of work.
- Women in science, letters and sport: check the share of national prizes and orders.
- Families of victims of the dictatorship: check cases officially acknowledged and those left out, as contested accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for prizes, orders and commissions; mixed for workplace and everyday credit; thin for Chango and Juan Fernández (start with CONADI files and island records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including colonial paleography for *probanzas*; Aymara, Quechua and Mapudungun for forms of thanks and for authorities' titles; Rapa Nui for island institutions; Kreyòl for newcomers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** acknowledgment of dictatorship victims and of Indigenous wrongs is contested ground, to be read as accounts; recognition of peoples ties to legal acts, whose text goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** scholars writing on the politics of recognition abroad were left out in favor of Chilean bodies and cases; no one was set aside under a standing rule.
