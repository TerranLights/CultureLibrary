# Chile: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its eastern *comunas*: check who appears in the evening news, and in what role.
- Townspeople of the Norte Chico and the Central Valley: check whether the plaza stroll survives, and who takes part.
- *Warriache* of Santiago: check what *mapurbe* names, and who uses it.
- Aymara and Peruvian-descended families of Arica and Tarapacá: check family memories of the *chilenización* years.
- Afro-Chileans of Arica: check how visibility work tells their history.
- Rapa Nui families: check how they want to be pictured, and by whom.
- Haitian newcomers: check where they feel watched and where overlooked.
- Older people in Valparaíso and in rural Maule: check who visits and who notices an absence.
- Relatives of the disappeared and of the executed: check portraits and names as a form of witness.
- Residents of richer and poorer *comunas*: check how each speaks of cameras, drones and private guards.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for memory sites, media and census debates; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting; thin for Colla and Chango presence in public life and for Juan Fernández (start with census tables and island news).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including youth and online slang; Mapudungun for urban poetry; Aymara for northern family memory; Rapa Nui for image rules; Kreyòl for Haitian voices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** images of the disappeared and of Rapa Nui sacred sites carry rules of use; *chilenización* and the dictatorship's victims are contested ground, to be read as accounts.
- **Held for review:** a foreign photographer's portrait series of Chilean Indigenous peoples was weighed and kept out, as an outsider's gaze better taken up after the peoples' own image-makers.
