*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Descendants of nitrate and copper workers in Tarapacá and Antofagasta: check whether the mutual societies' words survive in today's unions.
- *Pobladores* of Santiago's housing movements: check what *digna* means in demands and in residents' own talk.
- Older people in care homes and at home in Valparaíso and the Central Valley: check what families call a dignified old age.
- Rural women fruit and farm workers of O'Higgins and Maule: check dignity at the packing plant.
- *Warriache* who worked in bakeries and homes in Santiago: check memories of how employers addressed them.
- Afro-Chileans of Arica: check whether the old confraternities appear in today's accounts.
- Protestant, Jewish and other non-Catholic families: check burial history and present practice.
- Migrants facing removal and their Chilean neighbors: check accounts of the expulsion flights.
- Prisoners' families: check what they ask of the prison service.
- Rural families who use *usted* to parents: check whether the habit is fading and how it is felt.
- Rapa Nui elders: check how they describe the sheep-company years in terms of respect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, human-rights reports and housing movements; mixed for everyday dignity; thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández (start with community leaders and municipal social services).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including the address forms; Mapudungun for urban workers' memory; Aymara and Quechua for respect terms; Kreyòl for Haitian accounts of treatment.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the executed buried as unnamed, prison deaths and expulsions are contested ground, to be read as accounts; the end-of-life debate touches faith; bill texts go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** writings that frame dignity through foreign theorists were weighed and dropped in favor of the Chilean bodies that use the word; no item fell under a standing rule.
