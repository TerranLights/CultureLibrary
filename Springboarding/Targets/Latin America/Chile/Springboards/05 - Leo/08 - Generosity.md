## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its eastern *comunas*: check how much giving runs through bingos and *rifas* against foundations and tax-credited donations.
- Aymara and Quechua villages and their city residents: check how fiesta costs are shared and how *ayni* debts are tracked.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check hospitality owed at rodeos, *trillas* and funerals.
- Mining families of the Norte Grande and the coal towns: check union and company funds for the bereaved.
- Rapa Nui families: check who hosts an *umu*, and for whom.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check giving toward Palestine and toward Chilean causes.
- The Jewish community: check how *tzedaká* is organized through its own institutions.
- Pentecostal and other Evangelical congregations: check whether help goes mostly inside the church or outside it.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check help given to fellow arrivals in Chile (loans, a bed, church collections) beside money sent abroad.
- Students and young workers: check tips received and tips given.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster giving and formal charity; mixed for remittances and tipping; thin for Diaguita, Colla and Juan Fernández giving (start with parish and municipal records and island associations).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including fundraising slang; Aymara and Quechua for reciprocity; Rapa Nui for feast terms; Arabic and Hebrew for community foundations; Kreyòl for remittance talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disputes over telethon money and foundations' politics are contested; organ-donation refusals touch faith and trust; Rapa Nui feasts after a death belong to the family.
- **Held for review:** the Teletón (on the Taurus sheet on values) and the *ollas comunes* of 2020 (on the Cancer sheet on care) were weighed and not repeated; *Chile ayuda a Chile* and the neighborhood bingos carry televised and local giving here. No item met a standing rule.
