*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Unions in the copper mines and the ports of the Norte Grande: check how a *dirigente* earns and loses a following.
- Student federations in Santiago, Valparaíso and Concepción: check the *vocero* model against elected presidents.
- Families of the old Central Valley *fundos*: check memories of the *capataz* and the *patrón* as leaders.
- Aymara communities of Arica y Parinacota and Tarapacá: check how the turn of office passes between households, and who may decline it.
- Lickanantay, Diaguita and Colla communities: check whether elders or elected presidents speak for them.
- Rapa Nui families: check which island figures people follow in practice, and whether Miru descent matters to that.
- *Warriache* associations in Santiago: check whether a *lonko* is recognized in the city, and by whom.
- Pentecostal congregations: check how a lay preacher becomes a pastor.
- Family-owned business groups: check founders' succession and heirs' standing.
- Women *dirigentas* of neighborhood and housing groups: check their path, or its absence, into elected office.
- Haitian newcomers: check whether pastors or association heads speak for the community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** political, union and student leaders are well documented; business and church leadership less so; Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández leaders are barely written about, so community registers and island councils come first.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including colonial documents for *cacicazgos*; Aymara and Quechua office titles; Mapudungun for urban associations; Rapa Nui for chiefly lines; Kreyòl for newcomers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** leaders of the Popular Unity and of the military regime are contested figures, to be read as competing accounts; Rapa Nui chiefly claims are disputed among islanders; command and formal authority go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the Wallmapu *lonkos* and their councils were weighed for the Indigenous leadership rows and kept out, since that land belongs to Araucanía-Patagonia; they serve only as comparison.
