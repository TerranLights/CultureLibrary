## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Huaso* men of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check *rayuela* and horse races as play, and whether women join.
- Children of Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check where each may play outdoors, and from what age.
- Families of the Norte Grande: check Carnival water play and memories of childhood in the nitrate towns.
- Aymara children in the altiplano and in Arica: check play while herding against play in the city.
- *Warriache* youth in Santiago: check whether revived games feel like play or like lessons.
- Rapa Nui families: check where the Tapati's contests are play and where they are serious.
- Palestinian-Chilean and other Arab-descended families: check backgammon and cards in Patronato and the clubs.
- Haitian newcomers: check dominoes in plazas and who joins in.
- Older people in municipal clubs: check card games and dances as their play.
- Teenagers across regions: check online gaming and its place beside street play.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** games and festivals are well recorded in folklore collections; children's free play and adult hobbies are less so; Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández play is barely written up, so start with schools and municipal programs there.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including the game names of each region; Mapudungun for revived games; Aymara for Carnival and herding play; Rapa Nui for Tapati contests; Kreyòl and Arabic for newcomers' and Arab-descended families' games.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kite-string injuries are a public-safety topic; some Mapuche games have ceremonial uses that teachers may not share; betting at rural races is noted, not followed, here.
- **Held for review:** casino and lottery records were weighed for adult play and left out; they appear on the Aries sheet on recklessness, and here play is kept to games played for their own sake.
