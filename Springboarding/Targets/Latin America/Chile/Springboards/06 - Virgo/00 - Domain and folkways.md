# Chile: The domain, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, plus Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (popular, *huaso* and northern speech); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what counts as good work and a well-kept life: where work sits in a life, the daily and yearly round, the kept house, chores and paid help, craft, rest, and the ways labor is organized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Norte Grande families with a miner on long rotating shifts: check how weeks away reorder the household day.
- *Inquilino*-descended families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check the farm year and how the old estate duties are remembered.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check who cleans whose house and how each side names the work.
- Lota and Coronel: check what replaced the pit as the center of a working life after the closures.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay villages: check communal water days against wage work in Arica, Iquique and Calama.
- Diaguita and Colla of the Norte Chico; Chango-descended *caleta* families: check herding, small mining and the fishing year (sparse; start with community organizations).
- Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández families: check fishing, tourism and the lobster season as island calendars.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how bakery, building and domestic trades pass on (Wallmapu, in Araucanía-Patagonia, as comparison).
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian workers; Palestinian- and Croatian-descended family firms: check first trades and the shape of the week.
- Catholic and Pentecostal congregations: check Sunday rest and weekday *culto* against the work week.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, mining and time use; mixed for household cleanliness and craft; thin for Chango, Colla and Juan Fernández work rounds. Still to be settled: Chile's own lands, both where the line with the south falls and Rapa Nui's standing; this Virgo set takes the Norte Grande to the Biobío and the two island groups as its core.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for records, unions and the press; Aymara and Quechua for communal labor; Kunza revival lists for canal work; Rapa Nui for island work parties; Kreyòl and Arabic for trades in the two diaspora communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Rapa Nui company years and labor under the 1973 to 1990 regime are contested ground; domestic workers' conditions are private and sometimes litigated; labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** foreign business-culture guides on "how Chileans work" were weighed for the workplace row and left out as outsiders' generalizations, not under a standing rule; nothing on this sheet was set aside under the rules.
