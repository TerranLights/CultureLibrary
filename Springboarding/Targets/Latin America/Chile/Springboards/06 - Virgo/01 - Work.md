# Chile: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Contract and directly hired miners of the Norte Grande: check whether pay gaps and job security shape how each speaks of the job.
- Women packers of the Central Valley fruit sheds: check how seasonal wages are fitted into the farm and house year.
- *Huaso* smallholders of Maule and Ñuble: check the mix of own land, day labor and town jobs.
- Santiago's *poblaciones*: check self-employment, street selling and *pololitos* beside formal jobs.
- Valparaíso's port and Concepción's industrial belt: check dock and factory work after automation and closures.
- Aymara families of Arica y Parinacota: check the pull between village herding and town wage work.
- Lickanantay, Colla and Diaguita communities: check mining wages against community work on water and land.
- Rapa Nui: check tourism, carving and fishing as the island's own range of jobs.
- Mapuche workers in Santiago: check which trades were entered in the migration decades and how that shaped later choices.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check how qualifications earned abroad are or are not used.
- Pentecostal congregations: check sermons and testimonies on steady work after conversion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for employment, wages and labor history; mixed for attitudes to idleness and overwork; thin for Chango, Colla and Juan Fernández working lives (start with fishers' and community organizations).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for statistics, unions and novels; Aymara and Quechua for village work and wages; Kunza lists for Lickanantay job words; Kreyòl and Spanish for Haitian workers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the emergency jobs programs, strikes and repression under the dictatorship are contested; the 1890 and 1907 strikes have competing accounts; working hours and dismissal rules are law (Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** surveys of "work ethic by nationality" were considered as a pointer and dropped because they rank whole peoples; no pointer here fell under a standing rule.
