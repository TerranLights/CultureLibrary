*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (urban and rural registers); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Households in Santiago's eastern *comunas* and the women who work in them: check how each side tells the relation, live-in against daily work.
- Peruvian and Bolivian domestic workers in Santiago, Arica and Iquique: check pay, hours and the words used for them.
- Rural *huaso* households of O'Higgins and Maule: check older deference forms (*mande*, *patrón*) and who still uses them.
- Families with a son or daughter in the armed forces: check service as duty, job or escape, by region and income.
- Aymara and Quechua villages: check the year-long feast sponsorships as public service and their cost.
- Dance societies of La Tirana and Ayquina: check how a *promesero* counts years of service.
- Rapa Nui: check service to the island's councils and to visitors, two different kinds of serving.
- Mapuche organizations in Santiago: check the *werken* and other roles held for the group.
- Palestinian- and Jewish-community welfare bodies: check service given inside the community and beyond it.
- Haitian and Venezuelan workers in delivery and retail: check ratings, tips and customers' treatment.
- Catholic university volunteers and Pentecostal preachers: check whom each serves and who stays to serve.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for domestic work, public service and volunteering programs; mixed for customer service; thin for service roles among the Chango, the Colla and on Juan Fernández (start with island and community councils).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for deference forms and press; Aymara and Quechua for feast sponsorship; Rapa Nui for council service; Kreyòl and Spanish for couriers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** military service under the dictatorship and conscripts' testimonies are contested ground; domestic workers' complaints may be in closed labor files; vows made to the Virgin are private.
- **Held for review:** Catholic missionary orders working abroad were weighed as service pointers and kept out because the work happens outside Chile; no item was caught by a standing rule.
