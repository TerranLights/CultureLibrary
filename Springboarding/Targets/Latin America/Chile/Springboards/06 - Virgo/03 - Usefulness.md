## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Retirees in Santiago and the regional cities: check who keeps working after pension age and how each explains it.
- *Huaso* and smallholder elders of Maule and Ñuble: check the tasks that stay with the old on a farm.
- Families with *allegados* in the *poblaciones*: check how a lodger's help is counted and when a stay grows too long.
- Coal-town men of Lota and Coronel and nitrate-town families: check how job loss changed a man's place in the house.
- Aymara herding families: check children's herding tasks and elders' role once they move to Arica.
- Lickanantay and Colla villages: check which skills are still taught as needed and which are kept as heritage.
- Rapa Nui families: check land and garden work against tourism jobs as measures of a useful life.
- Disabled people and their families: check what supported-work programs offer and what self-advocates ask instead.
- Mapuche elders in Santiago: check language and healing knowledge as roles in urban associations (set against Wallmapu, a separate culture-nation, only for comparison).
- Venezuelan and Haitian professionals working below their training: check how they speak of skills left unused.
- Pentecostal and Catholic congregations: check tasks given to older members and to the unemployed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for older and disabled workers and for child labor; mixed for reuse and repair; thin for the usefulness of elders in Diaguita, Chango and Juan Fernández families (start with community councils and municipal records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for surveys and social policy; Aymara and Quechua for herding tasks; Rapa Nui for land and garden terms; Kreyòl for newcomers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Rapa Nui land registration and returns are contested ground; disability and end-of-life records are confidential; the quota for disabled workers is law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** writings on worth measured by output from the dictatorship's economic advisers were weighed and left out, since they bear on economic policy more than on a person's being needed; nothing here fell under a standing rule.
