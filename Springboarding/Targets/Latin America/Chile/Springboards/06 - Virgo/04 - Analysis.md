## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Engineers, economists and accountants of Santiago's business districts: check how decisions are justified, by numbers or by the boss's word.
- Mining-town technicians of Antofagasta and Calama: check analysis learned on the job against analysis learned at university.
- Farmers of the Central Valley and the Elqui: check weather and harvest reckoning by signs and by forecasts.
- Aymara and Quechua farmers and herders: check forecasting by *señas* beside radio and phone forecasts.
- Lickanantay communities: check how they assess water and mining studies done about their lands.
- Rapa Nui: check how islanders answer outside readings of rongorongo and of island history.
- Teachers and pupils in public, subsidized and private schools: check how critical thinking is taught and tested.
- Catholic, Evangelical and secular families: check reason, faith and experience as grounds for big decisions.
- Mapuche intellectuals in Santiago: check *kimün* as a system of knowing set beside university science.
- Venezuelan and Haitian engineers and teachers: check how foreign training is weighed in Chilean workplaces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, economics and science policy; mixed for everyday reasoning and popular reckoning; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Chango ways of observing (start with community and museum staff).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for policy and science; German and English for rongorongo scholarship; Aymara and Quechua for forecasting signs; Mapudungun for *kimün*; Kreyòl for newcomers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** economic planning under the dictatorship and the Cybersyn story are contested ground; Rapa Nui knowledge of the tablets may be held by families; mining impact studies are disputed in court.
- **Held for review:** the Chilean entries in cross-national "thinking style" experiments were weighed and left out, since they sort whole peoples on a single scale; nothing on this sheet was caught by a standing rule.
