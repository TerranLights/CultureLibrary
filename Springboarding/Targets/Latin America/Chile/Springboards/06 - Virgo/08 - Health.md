# Chile: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (clinical, popular and rural speech); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Fonasa and Isapre members: check waiting times, choice of doctor and how each speaks of being well cared for.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* and its *poblaciones*: check gyms against plazas, private clinics against the *consultorio*.
- Mining families of Antofagasta, Calama and Copiapó: check work injury, lung disease and the company clinic.
- Residents of Arica's polluted neighborhoods and of the Quintero bay: check how illness is tied to place in their accounts.
- *Huaso* and smallholder families of Maule and Ñuble: check rural *postas* and folk illnesses.
- Aymara and Quechua families: check the *yatiri* and herbal medicine beside the hospital in Arica and Iquique.
- Lickanantay, Colla and Diaguita communities: check distance to care and plant knowledge (sparse; start with community health workers).
- Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández: check evacuation to the mainland and island remedies.
- Mapuche people living in La Pintana, Cerro Navia and other Santiago *comunas*: check where *küme felen* is sought, from urban healers, the public clinic or both (the south is Araucanía-Patagonia's and serves as comparison).
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check language barriers and fear of records at clinics.
- Pentecostal, Catholic and Adventist congregations: check prayer, fasting and diet rules in health choices.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish community clinics and charities: check community health networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the health system, surveys and public health; mixed for folk and religious healing; thin for Diaguita, Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández health practice (start with community health workers and municipal records).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for records; Aymara, Quechua and Mapudungun for healing terms; Rapa Nui for island remedies; Kreyòl for Haitian patients' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some healing knowledge is held within families and communities; medical records are closed; the Arica waste case, the pandemic measures and medical experiments under the dictatorship are contested ground.
- **Held for review:** one pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule and is held only as a category (South Asian-origin items in the nation's lands), named nowhere above.
