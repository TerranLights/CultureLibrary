# Chile: The domain, partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (legal and popular); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what a fair bargain and a good partnership are: binding unrelated people, contracts and promises, open enemies, settling disputes, civility, marriage and gender ways, and the letter of the law that frames them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how matches were arranged on the old estates.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* and its *poblaciones*: check church weddings, civil unions and cohabitation.
- Aymara and Quechua villages of Arica y Parinacota and Tarapacá: check the community's role in approving a couple.
- Lickanantay of San Pedro de Atacama; Diaguita and Colla of Atacama and Coquimbo: check how the 1993 law's recognition changed their dealings with the state.
- Mapuche families in Santiago (*warriache*): check whether *mafün* and elders' judgment are kept or changed in the city.
- Rapa Nui: check family land and island-mainland marriage under the island statute.
- Afro-Chileans of the Azapa valley: check marriage records against their legal recognition (2019 *check*).
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check Orthodox and Catholic rites and the Arabic contract.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check how *plase* and other unions meet the registry.
- Catholic and Pentecostal congregations: check marriage preparation and the treatment of divorce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the written law, the marriage reforms and vital statistics; mixed for gender attitudes; thin for Colla, Chango, Diaguita and Juan Fernández marriage ways. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs; Rapa Nui's status); this sign covers the Norte Grande to the Biobío and the two island groups.
- **Languages needed:** legal Spanish for codes and rulings; Aymara and Quechua for highland unions; Mapudungun for urban Mapuche usage; Rapa Nui for how the island statute was received; Kreyòl and Arabic for the two diasporas' marriage forms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the constitutional drafts, the War of the Pacific and the island statute are contested ground, read as texts and accounts; violence and divorce files are confidential.
- **Held for review:** imported etiquette manuals were weighed for the civility facet and left out as not Chile's own; no item here falls under a standing rule.
