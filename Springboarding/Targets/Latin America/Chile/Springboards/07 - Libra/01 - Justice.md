*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (legal, popular, *coa*); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society. ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check reporting rates and recourse to *detenciones ciudadanas*, and who is seized.
- Norte Grande towns (Iquique, Antofagasta, Calama): check prosecutions of drug and border crimes and who sits in the dock.
- Central Valley *huaso* families: check memory of estate-era justice by the *patrón*, and of the 1960s rural cases.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check water and land claims argued in court, and community sanctions beside them.
- Lickanantay of the Salar de Atacama: check court cases over water and mining.
- Rapa Nui: check how land disputes are heard under the island statute, and the 2010 occupations as each side tells them.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how anti-terror prosecutions in Wallmapu (in Araucanía-Patagonia, comparison) are followed from the city.
- Families of the detained-disappeared and of former regime agents: check what each asks the courts for.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check access to interpreters and defenders, and press accounts of the Joane Florvil case (2017) *check*.
- Pentecostal congregations: check prison ministry and its picture of punishment and redemption.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, court statistics and transitional justice; mixed for popular justice and the *animitas*; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Chango experience of the courts.
- **Languages needed:** legal Spanish for codes and rulings; *coa* for prison and street speech; Aymara for water-claim hearings; Rapa Nui for island disputes; Kreyòl for migrants' dealings with defenders.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship's crimes, amnesty and the 2019 *estallido* prosecutions are contested ground; read every report as an account. Minors' cases are sealed.
- **Held for review:** two court-reform studies by Chilean university centers were weighed for the procedure row; the public defender's own figures carry that question instead, and no rule was triggered.
