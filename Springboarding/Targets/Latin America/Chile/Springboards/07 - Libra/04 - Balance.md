## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Workers on long mining shifts in Antofagasta and Calama: check how they describe weeks on and off as balance or strain.
- Santiago office workers in eastern *comunas* and commuters from *poblaciones*: check whether the 40-hour law changed the day, and for whom.
- Residents of Quintero-Puchuncaví and of Huasco: check how "sacrifice zone" is used or refused by those who live there.
- Lickanantay and Aymara communities: check the language of balance with water, mountains and fields, in their own words.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins and Maule: check the farm year's alternation of hard work and feast.
- Rapa Nui: check how the island speaks of balance between visitors and islanders.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check whether *küme mogen* is invoked in urban health and community programs.
- Haitian and Venezuelan workers: check second jobs and rest, and the shift from home-country rhythms.
- Catholic, Pentecostal and Adventist families: check Sunday or Saturday rest against shop and delivery hours.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutional checks, electoral and fiscal rules and labor law; mixed for folk medicine; thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández ideas of balance with land and sea.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for statutes and audits; Aymara and Kunza for land and water talk around the *salares*; Rapa Nui for island planning debates; Mapudungun for urban health programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the binomial system, the Salar de Atacama and "sacrifice zones" are fought over; read every party's account as an account. Healers may keep some knowledge private.
- **Held for review:** a holistic-wellness franchise popular in Santiago was weighed as a balance source and set aside as foreign commercial teaching; no standing rule was in play.
