*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of Arica and Tarapacá: check how the treaties and the Hague rulings are spoken of across the border with Tacna and the Bolivian highlands (both comparison).
- Aymara families with kin in Peru and Bolivia: check border-crossing manners and the greetings that open trade at highland fairs.
- Lickanantay communities: check who negotiates with firms and the state, and how a mandate is given.
- Diaguita and Colla communities of the Norte Chico: check how newly recognized bodies open talks with mining projects (sources sparse; start with community organizations).
- Rapa Nui: check the island's bodies as negotiators with Santiago, and protocol for visiting officials.
- Mapuche organizations in Santiago: check how urban leaders deal with ministries, set beside the colonial *parlamentos* of Wallmapu (Araucanía-Patagonia, comparison).
- Santiago office workers and *poblaciones* neighbors: check refusals by delay and softening, by class.
- Copper-mine unions of Antofagasta and El Teniente: check bargaining styles and who speaks first.
- Palestinian-Chilean organizations: check how a diaspora body addresses the foreign ministry, as their records and critics each tell it.
- Haitian and Venezuelan families: check the role of children and church leaders as everyday envoys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties, court cases and the ministry's archive; mixed for everyday tact (needs corpus work); thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández protocol.
- **Languages needed:** diplomatic Spanish and English for the court files; Aymara and Quechua for border communities; Kunza and Spanish for Lickanantay negotiations; Rapa Nui for the 1888 text; Kreyòl for family interpreting; Arabic for diaspora records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the northern treaties, the 1888 agreement and the lithium deals are contested ground; read every party's account as an account. Bolivia and Peru are comparison only.
- **Held for review:** foreign negotiation guides that profile "the Chilean negotiator" were weighed for the business row and left out as outsiders' generalizations; nothing here met a standing rule.
