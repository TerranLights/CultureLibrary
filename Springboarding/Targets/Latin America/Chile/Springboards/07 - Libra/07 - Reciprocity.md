# Chile: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (popular and rural); Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Huaso* families of Maule and Ñuble: check how help at slaughter, harvest and funerals is returned and who keeps track.
- Santiago *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check how favors from neighbors are weighed against paid services.
- Aymara and Quechua families: check *ayni* between highland villages and town kin, and how money changes it.
- Lickanantay families of San Pedro de Atacama: check exchange between oases and with mining wage earners.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how *trafkintu* and help among kin travel to and from Wallmapu.
- Rapa Nui families: check what an island feast obliges, and between which households.
- Juan Fernández fishers: check how catch and help are shared in the off season.
- Haitian, Peruvian, Bolivian and Venezuelan newcomers: check rotating savings and who sponsors new arrivals.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Croatian-descended families: check gifts and loans within the community and how they are repaid.
- Catholic and Pentecostal congregations: check tithes, vows and help to members in need, and what is expected back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pre-Hispanic exchange, migrant networks and folk tales; mixed for vow practice; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Chango exchange ways, and few studies treat Juan Fernández.
- **Languages needed:** Aymara and Quechua for gift lists; Kunza for oasis exchange terms; Mapudungun for urban *trafkintu*; Rapa Nui for feast obligations; Kreyòl for the *sòl*; Arabic for diaspora help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clientelism names living officials; family debts are private; some exchange ceremonies may be closed to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** the classic Santiago study of middle-class favor exchange was weighed and kept off this sheet, because it already stands on the Gemini connection sheet; here Clara Han opens the *población* side, and no standing rule was involved.
