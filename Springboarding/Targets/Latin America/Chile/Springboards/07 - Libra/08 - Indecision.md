# Chile: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (popular and parliamentary); Aymara; Kunza; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check abstention and blank votes under voluntary and compulsory voting.
- Young adults in Concepción, Valparaíso and Santiago: check study changes and leaving home, and who decides with them.
- Central Valley *huaso* families: check who has the last word on selling land or dividing an estate, and how long it waits.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check consensus and its length against outside deadlines from firms and the state.
- Colla and Diaguita communities: check how new legal bodies reach decisions under consultation rules (sparse; start with community organizations).
- Rapa Nui: check family and clan agreement before land or residence choices.
- Fishing towns of La Higuera and the Coquimbo coast: check how a decade of pending decisions is lived.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check who is consulted before moving or marrying, in the city and in Wallmapu (comparison).
- Haitian and Venezuelan families: check stay-or-move decisions and who weighs in from abroad.
- Catholic, Pentecostal and non-religious families: check end-of-life decisions and who makes them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legislative timing, election returns and higher-education data; mixed for personal and family decisions; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Juan Fernández ways of deciding.
- **Languages needed:** parliamentary Spanish for bill histories; Aymara and Kunza for assembly speech; Rapa Nui for consultation records; Kreyòl and Arabic for migrant families' choices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the constitutional process and the end-of-life bill are contested ground; election results are records, not readings of what any group wants. Assembly minutes may be closed.
- **Held for review:** opinion-poll trackers of the 2020 to 2023 votes were weighed for the wavering-voter row but already stand on the Taurus stubbornness sheet, so the official returns take that role here; no rule was involved.
