# Chile: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (popular, legal, *coa*); Aymara; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; German (for one colony's records); Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families in Lota and Coronel: check how keeping quiet before the coal company is remembered.
- Santiago *poblaciones* under armed groups and the eastern *comunas*: check what each risks by reporting.
- Catholic parishes and Pentecostal congregations: check how each handles complaints against its own ministers.
- Neighbors of Parral and the Maule countryside: check how the colony's decades are now told locally.
- Families of the disappeared and of former regime officers: check what each calls silence and what each calls peace.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check how a quarrel is kept from outsiders, and when it is taken to the state.
- Rapa Nui: check family and clan quiet over land quarrels against public protest.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check accommodation and refusal in dealings with employers and police.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian workers: check who reports abuse and who fears doing so.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check whether disputes are settled inside the community, and by whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the transition, the courts and the church cases; mixed for family and school silence; thin for Colla, Diaguita, Chango and Juan Fernández ways of avoiding conflict.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for the record; German for the colony's papers; *coa* for neighborhood codes; Aymara for community quarrels; Kreyòl for migrant silence; Arabic for in-community settlement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship, the transition, Colonia Dignidad and clerical abuse are contested ground with living victims; read each report as an account and protect survivors; neighborhood violence puts informants at risk.
- **Held for review:** the 1980s anti-torture movement of Concepción was weighed as the nonviolent counter-case, but it already appears on the Aries courage sheet; this sheet uses the women's movement of 1983 instead, and no rule was involved.
