# Chile: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (popular, political); Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Arica, Iquique and Antofagasta: check which newcomers are blamed for which troubles, and how that shifted between Peruvian, Bolivian, Colombian and Venezuelan arrivals.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check the class labels each uses for the other and what faults each label carries.
- Central Valley *huaso* families: check the witch and *salamanca* tales still told, and who is suspected in them.
- Families of the left and of the right who lived through 1973: check how each describes the other as the threat.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check accusations from outside (press labels) and inside (*kalku*), side by side.
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check envy and sorcery against blame placed on mining firms and the state.
- Rapa Nui: check the island's word for mainlanders (*conti*) and the faults linked to it, set against mainland talk of islanders.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian communities: check how each reports being blamed, and whom each blames in turn.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish communities: check how news of wars abroad is turned on them locally, as each community reports.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the legal record, fear-of-crime research and migration coverage; mixed for folk accusations; thin for Colla, Diaguita, Chango and Juan Fernández images of outsiders.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for law, press and folklore; Mapudungun and Aymara for sorcery and envy talk; Rapa Nui for island labels; Kreyòl for Haitian accounts; Arabic for diaspora reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1973 enemy images, the 1948 ban, anti-migrant events and accusations of sorcery are contested and can harm living people; read each as an account and never as a trait of a group.
- **Held for review:** the colonial Inquisition history was considered for the pre-modern row, but rumor turned into denunciation already sits on the Gemini gossip sheet, so the 1647 earthquake sermons take that place; no rule was triggered.
