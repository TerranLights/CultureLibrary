## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how farms pass to heirs and whether the wake is held at home.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check funeral cost against income, and who relies on a mutual or a plan.
- Norte Chico *comuneros*: check how a common-land share is inherited or sold.
- Aymara and Quechua families of Arica y Parinacota and Tarapacá: check care of village cemeteries after the move to the coast.
- Lickanantay of the Loa and the Salar de Atacama: check the return and reburial of ancestral remains.
- Diaguita, Colla and Chango families: check burial places their communities now claim; sources are scarce, so begin with their own associations.
- Rapa Nui families: check how land and family names are inherited.
- Mapuche families in Santiago (*warriache*): check burial in the city against carrying the dead to family land in the south, which lies in Araucanía-Patagonia and serves only as comparison.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish communities: check their own cemeteries and mourning periods.
- Haitian families: check repatriation of the dead against burial in Chile.
- Catholic and Pentecostal congregations: check the funeral rite and the claim of a changed life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality, household finance and Santiago's cemetery history; mixed for sex ways and switching faith; thin for Colla, Chango and Juan Fernández death ways. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs, Rapa Nui's status); this sign covers the Norte Grande to the Biobío and the two island groups.
- **Languages needed:** colonial paleography for wills and burial books; Aymara for the November rite; Rapa Nui for lineage land; Kunza only through its nineteenth-century word lists; Kreyòl for repatriation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains and reburial; the disappeared (taken up in the death and secrecy sheets); minors' sexual data; Rapa Nui land is contested ground.
- **Held for review:** two foreign studies of death attitudes were weighed for the denial-or-familiarity facet and left for later, since neither was written on Chile; no standing rule applied.
