# Chile: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, rural, *coa*); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning (funeral practice is in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aymara and Quechua families of the Arica and Tarapacá highlands: check grief rites and burial in the home village after moving to the coast.
- Lickanantay of San Pedro de Atacama: check views on showing ancestors' bodies in museums.
- Nitrate-era families of the Norte Grande: check who still visits pampa graves, and how.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check home wakes with *canto a lo poeta* against chapel wakes in town.
- Santiago's *poblaciones*: check how young men's violent deaths are mourned, and neighbors' views of the *narcofuneral*.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check whether *eluwün* rites are kept in Santiago's cemeteries, with practice in Wallmapu read only as a point of comparison.
- Rapa Nui: check how the 1860s dying is remembered by lineage.
- Families of the executed and the disappeared: check mourning without a body; use organizations and public records only.
- Palestinian-Chilean Orthodox families: check the *arbaʿīn* and other memorial days.
- Haitian families: check grief far from home and repatriation of the body.
- Pentecostal and Catholic congregations: check teaching on the dead and on suicide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality, suicide counts and the memory of political death; mixed for the *animita* and urban grief; thin for Colla, Diaguita and Chango beliefs about the dead (ask their own associations).
- **Languages needed:** Aymara for the highland dead; Rapa Nui for the 1860s memory; Kreyòl for Haitian mourning; Arabic for Orthodox memorial prayers; *coa* for street talk of death.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains on display; the executed and the disappeared (names of private persons stay out); suicide; the narco funerals as told by police and by neighbors are rival accounts.
- **Held for review:** for the afterlife facet, Buddhist teaching on death among a small Chilean following was considered; it is held as a category for the author, with no community, temple or text named.
