# Chile: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (church, popular, *coa*); Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Pentecostal congregations of Santiago and the coal and port towns: check how a conversion is dated and narrated.
- Catholic parishes: check adult baptism and confirmation against slow renewal through the sacraments.
- Men and women in prison or after it: check what is asked of someone who claims a new life.
- *Pobladores* in recovery: check who reaches a treatment place and who relies on a church.
- Lickanantay of the Salar de Atacama: check who leads the Kunza revival and how elders take part.
- Diaguita of the Huasco and Elqui valleys, and Chango families of the coast: check what a renewed name changes for them.
- Rapa Nui: check language revival against tourism and mainland schooling.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check which rites are revived in city parks and community centers; southern practice is a separate culture-nation's, used for contrast.
- Haitian evangelical congregations: check conversion in Kreyòl as part of settling in.
- Palestinian-Chilean Orthodox and Catholic families: check Orthodox Easter rites and how a grandchild's return to the church is greeted.
- Families of Constitución, Juan Fernández and Valparaíso's hills: check how "starting again" is told after disaster.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Pentecostal history, switching surveys and disaster rebuilding; mixed for prison conversion and recovery; thin for Chango and Colla re-emergence, where few sources exist yet.
- **Languages needed:** Pentecostal Spanish (its hymns and pulpit forms); Kunza for revival classes; Rapa Nui for immersion schooling; Kreyòl for Haitian churches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conversion in prison and addiction are personal; ethnic recognition is contested; disaster rebuilding has winners and losers.
