# Chile: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, folk-medical, political); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of Quintero, Puchuncaví, Tocopilla, Huasco and Coronel: check how neighbors, firms and the state each name the contamination.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* and its outer *poblaciones*: check who was moved in the eradications and how both sides recall it.
- Lickanantay of Socaire, Peine and Toconao: check the *talatur* as communities choose to share it.
- Aymara and Quechua families of Arica and Tarapacá: check smoke offerings and the healer's cleansing.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check access to a *machi* in the capital (southern practice belongs to another culture-nation, for comparison).
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check home cures for *empacho* and the evil eye.
- Former public employees and university staff dismissed after 1973: check how they name the purge; organizations only.
- Catholic laity and survivors of clerical abuse: check views on the bishops' resignations.
- Rapa Nui: check how *tapu* places and waste are handled.
- Haitian families: check the *beny* and other cleansing rites kept in Chile.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish families: check Lenten and Yom Kippur fasting, and which purity customs the young keep.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pollution, the 1973 dismissals and church scandals; mixed for folk medicine; thin for Kunza ritual speech and for Chango and Colla cleansing customs.
- **Languages needed:** the canal songs are sung in Kunza and Spanish; smoke offerings are named in Aymara; the *machi*'s rite in Mapudungun; *tapu* is a Rapa Nui idea; the *beny* is explained in Kreyòl.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the *talatur* and other rites may be closed to outsiders; clerical abuse and the 1973 purges are contested ground with living victims; sacrifice-zone health data concern minors.
- **Held for review:** two recent wellness-cleanse fads were weighed for the bodily facet and left out as imported marketing with no Chilean scholarship behind them yet.
