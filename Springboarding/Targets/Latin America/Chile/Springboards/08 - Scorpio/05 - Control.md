## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nitrate-era families of the Norte Grande: check memory of scrip, the store and the company police.
- Mining families of Rancagua and Calama: check what company towns permitted and forbade, and how the move to town changed it.
- *Huaso* and former *inquilino* families of the Central Valley: check who controlled time and work on the estate.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and eastern *comunas*: check police stops, curfews and pandemic permits by district.
- Aymara and Quechua communities of the precordillera: check rites to steer rain, frost and hail, and who performs them.
- Lickanantay of the Salar de Atacama: check control of water against mining firms.
- Rapa Nui: check views of the limits on mainlanders' stays (the statute goes to Libra), a contested question.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check how often young Mapuche men report street stops in Santiago; police conduct in Wallmapu is outside this nation and only a comparison.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check control by employers and landlords over papers and wages.
- Pentecostal and Catholic households: check teaching on a husband's authority and a wife's place.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the dictatorship's controls, pandemic rules and mining towns; mixed for couples and children; thin for ritual control of weather among Colla and Diaguita families.
- **Languages needed:** decrees, *bandos* and permits are in the state's administrative Spanish; weather rites must be heard in Aymara; the entry debate should be read in Rapa Nui as well; migrant workers' complaints often come in Kreyòl.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Colonia Dignidad and the dictatorship are contested ground with living victims; violence data are confidential; police-stop data can stigmatize districts.
- **Held for review:** for control over one's own safety, figures on registered firearms came to mind; they touch weapons law, which this sign leaves to Libra, so they were not added.
