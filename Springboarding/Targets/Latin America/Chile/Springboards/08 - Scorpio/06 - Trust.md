# Chile: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, commercial, popular); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down, in the expectation of not being harmed (loyalty is scouted here too).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* and its *poblaciones*: check who trusts neighbors, and what walls and gates do to it.
- Norte Grande port and mining towns (Iquique, Antofagasta, Calama): check trust between long-settled families and recent arrivals.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check the handshake deal at livestock fairs and *medialunas*.
- Aymara families of Arica y Parinacota: check trust in godparents chosen at the first haircut, and in coastal kin.
- Lickanantay communities: check trust toward mining firms and the state in consultations.
- Rapa Nui: check whom islanders trust with land and with business, islander or mainlander.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check trust in public clinics and intercultural programs.
- Palestinian-Chilean merchant families of Patronato: check whether firms are run on kin trust.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check trust in banks against informal lenders and remittance agents.
- Juan Fernández fishing families: check how a newcomer is admitted to the grounds.
- Pentecostal congregations: check trust within the congregation in jobs and loans.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** survey series are long and plentiful; credit and neighborhood sources are scattered; for Colla, Diaguita and Chango families almost nothing is in print, so fieldwork would come first.
- **Languages needed:** commercial Spanish for credit records; Aymara for godparent bonds; Kreyòl and Venezuelan Spanish for migrants' lending; Arabic for some family-firm records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** credit records are personal; migrant lending touches crime and extortion; trust in police and courts is the spirit of the law, set aside here for Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** a cross-national lost-wallet experiment was weighed for the facet on strangers; its Chilean coverage is uncertain, so it waits for checking.
