# Chile: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, *coa*, church); Mapudungun (colonial texts and urban speech); Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden, privacy included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and prisons: check *coa* and the rule of silence toward police.
- Santiago's older elite families and its newer professional class: check what is kept within the family (money, illness, illegitimate births).
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check what the old estates kept quiet (children of the *patrón*, debts, land deals).
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how the treasure searches are spoken of on the island.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check which rites are kept from visitors and researchers.
- Lickanantay of the Salar de Atacama: check how the community decides what knowledge may be published.
- Rapa Nui: check lineage knowledge and who may speak for it.
- Mapuche families in Santiago: check what urban healers let researchers record; the *machi* tradition's heartland is a separate culture-nation, read for contrast.
- Families of the disappeared and former members of the forces: check what their associations say remains unknown, through public statements only.
- Women and families affected by irregular adoptions: check how the secret was kept and found.
- Gay and lesbian Chileans in the north and in Santiago: check how discretion and coming out differ by city and generation.
- Haitian and Palestinian-Chilean families: check which matters are kept within the family and in which language.
