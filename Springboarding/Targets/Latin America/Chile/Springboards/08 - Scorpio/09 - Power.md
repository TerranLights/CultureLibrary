# Chile: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (political, popular); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how a culture judges who holds it, how it is held and how it is used (authority and power ways are Capricorn's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Former *inquilino* families and estate-owning families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how each tells the end of the estate's power after the land reform.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* and its peripheral *comunas*: check who reaches office, boards and the press.
- Mining families of Antofagasta, Calama and Rancagua: check where they see power, in the firm, the union or the state.
- Aymara communities: check mountain powers, community authorities and the regional government.
- Lickanantay of the Salar de Atacama: check how mining money shifts weight among households and community bodies.
- Rapa Nui: check the rival bodies and the clans behind them.
- Mapuche associations in Santiago: check how urban leaders are chosen and named; hereditary *lonko* lines in the south fall under Araucanía-Patagonia, compared only.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check representation in business, Congress and the football club.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check who speaks for them, and whether they vote in local elections.
- Pentecostal congregations: check pastors' political weight and members' votes.
- Women's organizations: check how far parity rules changed who holds power (the statutes go to Libra).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** elite history, the regime and turnout are well documented; business networks are partly hidden; how Colla, Diaguita and Chango communities hold and pass on authority is barely recorded.
- **Languages needed:** mostly political and business Spanish, with Aymara where mountain powers are named, Rapa Nui for statements by the island bodies, and Kreyòl for newcomers' associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the regime, the 2019 assemblies and Rapa Nui's status are contested ground; read each body's claims as claims. Spiritual power is recorded only as believers describe it.
- **Held for review:** obedience and the legitimacy of rulers were weighed for this sheet; they are power ways, left for Capricorn, and only the capacity to act is scouted here.
