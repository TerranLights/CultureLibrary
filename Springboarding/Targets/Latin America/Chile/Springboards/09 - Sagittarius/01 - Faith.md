# Chile: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what cannot be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Weekly Mass-goers and those who keep only feasts and vows: check whether surveys catch the second group at all.
- Pentecostal converts in *poblaciones* of Santiago and Concepción: check what they say they left behind.
- Fishing coves of Valparaíso and Coquimbo: check faith set against sea danger and how it changed with industrial fishing.
- Aymara villages of Parinacota and their Pentecostal neighbors: check how one valley holds two faiths.
- Lickanantay families of the Loa: check how Catholic feasts and canal rites share a calendar.
- Rapa Nui parishioners: check how the island tells its conversion story today.
- Mapuche Evangelicals in Santiago: check whether conversion changed ties with kin in the south (Araucanía-Patagonia, compare only).
- Haitian Adventists, Baptists and Catholics: check differences between denominations among the same migrants.
- Jewish and Muslim Chileans: check how faith is kept where it is a small minority.
- People who have stopped believing: check survey items and testimony on leaving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic survey series and Pentecostal sociology; mixed for vows and feasts (mostly ethnography); thin for Muslim and Haitian faith life, so start with community websites and theses.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; French for the Picpus letters; Aymara for highland converts' own terms; Kreyòl for Haitian sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clergy abuse involves victims who are living; avoid naming accusers beyond what they made public; Rapa Nui accounts of the 1860s are a painful history.
- **Held for review:** a foreign study of popular devotion in the Andes was considered and set aside because its fieldwork lay in Peru, not Chile; it can serve later as comparison.
