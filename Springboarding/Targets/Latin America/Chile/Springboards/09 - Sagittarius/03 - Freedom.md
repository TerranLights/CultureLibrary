# Chile: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a belief and a political ideal (deciding for oneself and not depending on others stay in Aries).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Ex-*inquilino* households of the O'Higgins and Maule valleys: check how release from the hacienda is remembered by men and by women.
- Descendants of nitrate-*oficina* workers in Iquique and Antofagasta: check what moving between *oficinas* meant against staying bound to one company.
- Afro-descendant families around Arica (Azapa, Lluta): check which abolition they recall, Peru's or Chile's, since Arica came under Chile only after the 1879-1884 war.
- Rapa Nui families: check generational memory of leaving the island for the first time.
- Former political prisoners and exiles who returned: check how they define freedom regained (exiles abroad as comparison only).
- Supporters and opponents of the 1980 order: check rival meanings of *libertad* in their own publications.
- Evangelical congregations: check the freedom of worship they sought before 1999.
- Newer Haitian and Venezuelan residents: check whether freedom figures in reasons for coming, and how status rules limit it.
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check freedom as a word used about a homeland abroad.
- Sexual-diversity groups in Santiago and Valparaíso: check timelines of freedoms claimed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political and intellectual history and ratings; mixed for rural release; thin for Chango and Colla experience and for Juan Fernández beyond the 1814 prisoners.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for Pineda; Rapa Nui for island memory; Kreyòl and Arabic for community uses of the word.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** detention and exile under the dictatorship are contested ground, as is the 2019 *estallido*: treat each account as an account; any statute (abolition, worship, press) is Libra's or Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** a world freedom-of-thought report compiled by a humanist federation was weighed and kept for later checking, because its Chile entry may rest on outside sources only.
