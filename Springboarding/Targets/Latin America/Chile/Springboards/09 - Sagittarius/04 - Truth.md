# Chile: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and colloquial); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Readers of the main Santiago dailies and listeners of regional radio in Iquique and Concepción: check which outlets each group trusts for facts.
- Families on different sides of 1973: check whether they cite the same sources at all.
- Aymara and Quechua speakers of Parinacota: check whether evidential marking carries into their Spanish.
- Mapuche *warriache*: check oral history as record in Santiago associations; land testimony in Wallmapu is Araucanía-Patagonia's (compare only).
- Rapa Nui elders and younger islanders: check how they weigh tradition against laboratory findings.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check whom they trust when outsiders arrive with claims about their island.
- Evangelical and Catholic believers: check scripture and preacher against school science as sources of truth.
- Venezuelan and Haitian newcomers: check where they get news about status rules, and what they trust.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish community media: check how each reports the same overseas events.
- Hospital physicians in Antofagasta, Valparaíso and the capital: check their trust in official health data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the politics of memory and press history; mixed for science trust (surveys exist, analysis is thin); thin for evidential grammar in Chile's own Aymara and Quechua varieties, where northern universities are the first stop.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish paleography; Aymara and Quechua for evidentials; Rapa Nui for island responses; Kreyòl for newcomers' channels.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1973, the Rettig and Valech findings, the War of the Pacific's rival histories and 2019 are contested ground; Rapa Nui ancestry is a matter for the community, not only for laboratories.
- **Held for review:** the Reuters Institute news survey and the Wellcome monitor of trust in science were weighed for whom people believe; they are left on the Gemini information and Virgo analysis sheets, which already carry them.
