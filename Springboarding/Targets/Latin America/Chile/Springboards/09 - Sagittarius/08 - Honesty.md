## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Bus riders in *comunas* of high and low evasion: check how riders explain paying or not paying.
- Small shopkeepers and street vendors: check receipts given or withheld, and why.
- Students at universities and technical institutes: check where they draw the line between helping a friend and cheating.
- Iquique and Arica traders: check how customs honesty is talked about in a border economy.
- Aymara and Quechua communities: check whether the Andean triad is taught at home or only in schools and politics.
- Shopkeepers of Patronato, from older Palestinian-Chilean firms to newer Korean and Chinese ones: check posted prices, bargaining and guarantees as signs of fair dealing.
- Recent arrivals from Haiti and Venezuela: check what they report about being cheated, and how they judge rule-bending they see around them.
- Rapa Nui families in tourism: check pricing for visitors against islanders.
- Pentecostal converts: check what conduct changes they report.
- Police and military personnel: check their own codes of honor beside the fraud cases.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fraud cases, tax and transit data; mixed for everyday white lies (little direct study); thin for honesty norms in Colla, Chango and Diaguita communities and on Juan Fernández.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for nearly everything; Kreyòl for newcomers' complaints; Quechua and Aymara for the triad's local use; Korean and Arabic for Patronato merchants' own media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fraud cases may still be in court; accusations against named people need care; contraband talk can stigmatize the north; anything on criminal law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the campaign-finance cases of 2014 and 2015 and the 1989 Maracanã incident were weighed as honesty scandals; they already sit on the Scorpio secrecy and Leo humiliation sheets, so they were not repeated.
