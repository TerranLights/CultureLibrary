## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Graduates of private, voucher and public schools: check whom each admires as "educated".
- First-generation students from *poblaciones* in Puente Alto or Hualpén: check how families describe the degree and the debt.
- Students from Arica, Iquique and Calama: check moving to Santiago against studying in the north.
- Technical-institute graduates and university graduates: check pay and respect as each group tells it.
- Families in Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay communities: check what bilingual and intercultural school offers against what they want.
- Rapa Nui households: check what it costs to send a son or daughter to a mainland university, and who comes back.
- Juan Fernández families: check what happens when children must leave the island for secondary school.
- Haitian and Venezuelan professionals: check the long road to having a foreign degree recognized.
- Jewish and Palestinian-Chilean families: check community schools against public ones.
- Catholic and Evangelical schools: check how religious instruction sits inside the state curriculum.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policy, statistics and history; mixed for the standing of the educated person (scattered); thin for schooling among Colla and Chango families and on Juan Fernández.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for policy and history; Rapa Nui for immersion materials; Kreyòl and Arabic for community voices; Hebrew for Instituto Hebreo materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1973's university interventions, the 2006 and 2011 movements and the 2019 school occupations are contested ground; minors' records are protected; education statutes (1920, 1990, 2009) go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** Paulo Freire's Chilean years and the early-childhood surveys belong to formal schooling's edges; Gemini's learning sheet already carries both, so they stay there.
