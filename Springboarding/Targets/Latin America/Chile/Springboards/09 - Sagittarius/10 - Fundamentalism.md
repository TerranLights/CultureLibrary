# Chile: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (church, political and colloquial registers); German for one enclave's records; Aymara; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Catholic traditionalists, mainstream parishes and liberal Catholics in Santiago: check how each uses words like *integrista* and *progresista*.
- Classical Pentecostal congregations and newer neo-Pentecostal churches: check dress, music and attitudes to "the world".
- Pentecostal households of Lota and Coronel: check boundary rules across three generations.
- Aymara converts and Catholic neighbors in Putre and Belén: check what each side says separates them.
- Parral and Maule townspeople near the former enclave: check how neighbors tell its history.
- Rapa Nui churchgoers: check whether any group on the island keeps itself apart from island customs.
- Mapuche Evangelicals in Santiago: check whether their churches ask them to give up *ngillatun* and other rites, and how families who keep both explain it; rites in the south belong to Araucanía-Patagonia and serve as comparison.
- Haitian churches: check doctrinal strictness against Chilean congregations of the same name.
- Jewish and Muslim communities: check stricter and looser branches as each community names them, from inside.
- Secular activists: check where they use "fundamentalist" for religious opponents, and where opponents return the charge.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on Catholic church-state history and Colonia Dignidad, scattered for Pentecostal strictness and small groups; thin for leavers' accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; German for the enclave's records; Aymara for village disputes; Kreyòl for Haitian churches.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Antares case and the Parral enclave involve crimes and living victims; "sect" and "fundamentalist" are charged words, so quote them as each speaker uses them; religious-freedom and homeschooling statutes go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** U.S.-centered comparative studies of fundamentalism were considered as a frame; they say nothing about Chile itself, so none is named here, and they may serve later only to shape questions.
