# Chile: The domain, authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (core: the Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, northern, *huaso*, *coa*); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys and what is owed, with career, status, reputation and the age, time, rank, order and power ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Arica, Iquique and Antofagasta against the mining camps: check whether shift rosters redraw time and rank ways.
- Aymara and Quechua villages above Arica and Pozo Almonte: check how community offices sit beside the municipality.
- Lickanantay of San Pedro de Atacama and the Colla: check who speaks for a community before firms and the state.
- Elqui and Huasco valleys, with Diaguita communities: check rural office-holding and the revival's new bodies.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check which forms of deference outlived the hacienda.
- Santiago from Vitacura to La Pintana, and Mapuche *warriache* families: check whether status markers shift across the city.
- Valparaíso, Concepción, Lota and Coronel: check dockside and union rank ways against the capital's.
- Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández: check island bodies and fishers' groups against appointed mainland officials.
- Palestinian-, Croatian-, Basque- and Italian-descended families, the Jewish community: check their clubs as status systems.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check what each meets from employers and officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state records and elites; mixed for career and time ways; thin for the Colla, the Chango and Juan Fernández. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled (where the southern line runs, Rapa Nui's status); this sign is scouted for the north, the center and both island groups, with the south as Araucanía-Patagonia's, for comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century Spanish for Mariño de Lobera; *coa* for street names of command; Aymara and Rapa Nui for offices; Kreyòl and Arabic for newcomer guides.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** authority runs through 1973 to 1990 and 2019; give the armed forces', Carabineros' and critics' accounts side by side. Indigenous offices are described as each people shares them.
- **Held for review:** cross-national indexes of command and deference were weighed for this overview; they belong on the Hierarchy sheet, where one is carried, so nothing was set aside under a rule.
