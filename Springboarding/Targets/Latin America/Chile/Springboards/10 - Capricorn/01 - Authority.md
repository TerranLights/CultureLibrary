# Chile: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and its popular registers; Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- La Legua, La Victoria and Las Condes: check whom residents call first when trouble comes (Carabineros, the PDI, a neighborhood leader, a pastor) and why.
- Aymara villages of Putre and Colchane: check how traditional offices and elected community presidents share decisions.
- Lickanantay *ayllus* of San Pedro de Atacama: check which body speaks for them before firms and the state.
- *Huaso* families of Maule and Ñuble: check how the hacienda's authority is remembered in family and work.
- Valparaíso, a Navy city: check the Navy's standing there against Santiago's view of the Army.
- Concepción, Lota and Coronel: check how union and church authority changed after the mines closed.
- Rapa Nui: check which island body is accepted for land, heritage and family matters.
- Juan Fernández: check whether fishers' groups or the municipality decides island affairs.
- Catholic and Pentecostal communities: check the reach of each clergy's authority after the 2018 abuse crisis.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian arrivals: check how encounters with police are reported by each group.
- Palestinian-Chilean family firms of Patronato: check who decides when generations disagree.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys, police history and Church records; mixed for Pentecostal authority; thin for Lickanantay and Colla offices and for Juan Fernández.
- **Languages needed:** early-1800s Spanish for the *Catecismo*; Aymara and Mapudungun for office titles; Rapa Nui for clan claims; Kreyòl for reports of police encounters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Carabineros and the armed forces are contested since 1973 and 2019; set their own records beside the INDH's and other critics', as accounts. Mapuche authority in Wallmapu is Araucanía-Patagonia's; here only for comparison.
- **Held for review:** obedience experiments and authoritarianism scales were weighed; no Chilean replication is known to me with enough confidence to name, so the pointer is left for the research stage, not held under a rule.
