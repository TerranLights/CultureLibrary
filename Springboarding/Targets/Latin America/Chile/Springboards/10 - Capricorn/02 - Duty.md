# Chile: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and popular); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families in the Norte Grande with members away on mining shifts: check who takes over household duties during each *turno*.
- Aymara families split between Arica and highland villages: check whether coastal members still send labor or pay a substitute.
- Lickanantay *ayllus*: check how canal-cleaning shares are kept and recorded.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins and Maule: check the duties of godparents and of the eldest son on the land.
- Mapuche *warriache* in Santiago: check which *mingako*-style duties survive in the city; rural Wallmapu is a comparison only.
- Valparaíso and Talcahuano naval families: check how a duty of service passes between generations.
- Coal families of Lota and Coronel: check duties of mutual help in the *pabellones* after the mines closed.
- Rapa Nui families: check what is owed at funerals and to a family's *umu*.
- Palestinian-Chilean and Jewish families: check duties toward parents and toward community funds.
- Peruvian and Bolivian domestic workers: check duties owed to an employer's household against those owed at home.
