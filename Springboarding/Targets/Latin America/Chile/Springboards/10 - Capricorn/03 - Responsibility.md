# Chile: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and colloquial); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Arica neighborhoods near old waste sites and Antofagasta's mining *comunas*: check whom residents hold answerable for harm, firms or the state.
- Aymara and Quechua families: check whether a fault (*jucha*) is set right by ritual, payment or apology.
- Diaguita and Colla communities of the Norte Chico: check who answers to the community for water and pasture.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its eastern *comunas*: check how blame for poverty and crime is told in each.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check who answers for a farmhand's accident or a neighbor's loss.
- Concepción, Talcahuano and Lota: check how blame after the 2010 earthquake was argued.
- Rapa Nui: check how island bodies take on responsibility for heritage the state also claims.
- Juan Fernández: check who is held answerable for transport and rescue at sea.
- Armed forces, Carabineros and victims' groups: check their accounts of the past side by side.
- Catholic and Evangelical congregations: check confession, testimony and public apology as ways of owning a wrong.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check who answers when a newcomer is wronged at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, congressional inquiries and transitional justice; mixed for corporate reports; thin for Indigenous terms of fault.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth-century Spanish for the Valdivia trial; Aymara and Quechua for fault and repair; Kreyòl for complaints by Haitian workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Rettig and Valech material and confessions by former agents name victims and perpetrators; present the armed forces' and the commissions' accounts as accounts. The age of criminal responsibility is a Libra matter; point there.
- **Held for review:** victims' reparation programs (health care and pensions) were weighed; they answer a question of redress more than of answerability, so they were left for later research, not held under a standing rule.
