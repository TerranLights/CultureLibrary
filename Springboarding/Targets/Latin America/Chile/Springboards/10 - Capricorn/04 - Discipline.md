## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Copper and lithium workers on long rosters in Antofagasta and Calama: check how company safety rules shape life off shift.
- Aymara and Quechua youth from the highland villages: check how school rules in Arica and Iquique meet the expectations of home.
- Diaguita and Colla families of the Norte Chico: check how children are corrected and by whom.
- Santiago's *liceos emblemáticos* and private schools of the east: check which offenses each punishes and how.
- *Huaso* households of O'Higgins and Ñuble: check the training of boys for rodeo and horse work.
- Naval Valparaíso and Talcahuano: check how barracks discipline carries into family life.
- Lota and Coronel: check memories of the mine's whistle, fines and shifts.
- Rapa Nui: check how restraint (*tapu*) on fishing and ritual is taught and enforced.
- Juan Fernández lobster fishers: check the self-imposed limits of the season.
- Haitian and Venezuelan parents in Santiago: check how their ways of correcting children meet Chilean schools.
- Pentecostal, Catholic and Muslim households: check fasts and conduct rules as each describes them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military history, schools and fiscal policy; mixed for home discipline; thin for Indigenous child-rearing terms.
- **Languages needed:** early-1600s Spanish for González de Nájera; German for Körner-era sources; Aymara and Mapudungun for correction by counsel; Kreyòl for parents' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** harsh discipline shades into abuse; children's data need care. Quiriquina's 1973 history and military training are contested; give the Navy's and survivors' accounts side by side.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist meditation groups came up as a form of spiritual discipline; they are held as a category only, the author's ruling on Buddhism still being open, and none is named.
