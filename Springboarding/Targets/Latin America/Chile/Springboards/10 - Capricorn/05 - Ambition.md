# Chile: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and popular); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is, in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Families of Antofagasta and Calama tied to mining wages: check whether a mine job is a ceiling or a step for the next generation.
- Aymara families moving from the highlands to Arica: check what parents hope for children who study on the coast.
- Diaguita and Colla youth of the Norte Chico: check whether leaving for La Serena or Santiago is expected.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its eastern *comunas*: check how striving is talked about, praised or mocked, in each.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins and Maule: check whether the land is a career or a thing to leave.
- Valparaíso and Concepción students: check whether rising means moving to the capital.
- Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández: check how island families weigh staying against going.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Basque-descended families: check how family firms pass on ambition, and to whom.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian arrivals: check what each hopes for in Chile and what blocks it.
- Evangelical and Catholic congregations: check teaching on prosperity and on contentment.
- Women and men across class: check whether ambition is described in the same words for each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elites and education; mixed for migrants; thin for Rapa Nui, Colla and Chango aspirations.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for Consulado records; Mapudungun and Aymara for words of standing; Kreyòl and Spanish of Venezuela for migrants' own accounts; Arabic for older family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class words such as *siútico* and *flaite* wound; describe them, do not use them of people. Aspirations of migrants are tied to legal status; protect identities.
- **Held for review:** nothing withheld under a rule. Studies of mining families came up and were kept only where they ask about the next generation's hopes, leaving work itself to the Virgo sheet.
