# Chile: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and class-marked speech); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Arica and the Azapa valley: check how Afro-descendant and Aymara families place themselves in the town's order.
- Mining towns of the Norte Grande: check how company grade (staff, contract, subcontract) sorts neighborhoods and clubs.
- Diaguita and Colla communities: check whether the revival brings back old ranks or builds flat bodies.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* against its southern and western ones: check forms of address used across the line.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check whether the *patrón*-*inquilino* tie still marks address and seating.
- Valparaíso's hills and its *plan*: check how height in the city is read as rank.
- Lota and Coronel: check how the old order of engineers, foremen and miners is remembered.
- Rapa Nui: check how *mata* belonging weighs against island office and wealth.
- Juan Fernández: check whether founding families rank above later arrivals.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Jewish-descended families: check how they moved into or beside the old elite.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check where they are placed in work hierarchies, and by whom.
- Catholic and Pentecostal churches: check each one's ladder of office.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for class, elites and land tenure; mixed for colorism; thin for Rapa Nui and Colla ranks as the peoples tell them.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for parish books; Rapa Nui for *mata* names; Quechua and Aymara for kin rank; Kreyòl for newcomers' own status terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class and color words wound; describe, do not use. Encina's racial ideas are to be read as an account, not adopted. Rapa Nui lineage knowledge belongs to families; ask first.
- **Held for review:** histories of single elite fortunes were weighed and not added, since they speak to wealth more than to rank; no work was held back under the standing rules.
