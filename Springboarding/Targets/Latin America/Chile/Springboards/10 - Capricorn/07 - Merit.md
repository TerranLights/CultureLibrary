# Chile: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard, school and office slang); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Mining towns such as Calama and Tocopilla: check whether company scholarships or contacts open the way more often.
- Aymara and Quechua families of the far north: check how school success is weighed against community roles.
- Diaguita and Colla youth of the Norte Chico: check which yardsticks their schools and families use.
- Santiago's private schools and its municipal *liceos*: check how each explains a student's place at university.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check how skill in rodeo and horse work earns standing.
- Valparaíso and Concepción: check whether regional universities are seen as earned routes or second choices.
- Rapa Nui: check how island contests and family lineage each count toward standing.
- Juan Fernández fishers: check whether skill at sea or family position sets one's share.
- Palestinian- and Croatian-descended family firms: check whether heirs must prove themselves first.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Peruvian professionals: check how foreign degrees and experience are judged.
- Women and men in the same professions: check whether the same record is read the same way.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for schooling, admissions and teacher evaluation; mixed for hiring and promotion; thin for Indigenous and island measures of earned standing.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century Spanish for service records and *oposiciones*; Aymara for students' own accounts; Kreyòl and Arabic for migrants' and community views of connections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** debates over admissions touch class and selection in schools, still raw after 2006 and 2011; give each side's account. Promotion files are restricted.
- **Held for review:** matched-résumé hiring experiments were weighed; the best-known Chilean one already sits on the Virgo Discernment sheet, so it is not repeated, and no pointer here was set aside under a standing rule.
