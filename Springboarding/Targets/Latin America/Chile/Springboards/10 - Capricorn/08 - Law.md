# Chile: Law, its spirit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (legal, popular and *coa* registers); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it. Spirit only; the letter is Libra's.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aymara and Lickanantay communities: check when they follow their own water rules and when they turn to state courts.
- Colla and Diaguita communities: check how they argue legitimacy before mining firms and officials.
- Norte Grande ports near the borders (Arica, Iquique): check attitudes to smuggling and customs rules.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its business districts: check whether law is described as protector or threat.
- *Huaso* families of Curicó, Talca and Linares: check old ideas of rural justice against the police's.
- Valparaíso and Concepción law faculties and student movements: check the legal culture each trains.
- Rapa Nui: check whose rules people say govern land and entry to the island.
- Juan Fernández fishers: check how their own sea rules relate to official fishery law.
- Mapuche *warriache* in Santiago: check whether *az mapu* is invoked in the city; its home is a comparison.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian arrivals: check how each describes dealing with police and permits.
- Palestinian-Chilean, Jewish and Pentecostal communities: check how religious rule, conscience and state law are ranked in each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for judicial history, constitutional debate and survey items; mixed for customary rules; thin for Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández legal culture.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for the *obedézcase* formula; Aymara and Kunza for water rules; Rapa Nui for *ture*; Kreyòl for migrants' accounts of police and permits.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1980 Constitution, the 2019 *estallido* and the pardons are contested ground; present each account as an account. Customary rules may be private to communities.
- **Held for review:** codes, statutes and court rulings were screened out and left for Libra, which covers the letter; nothing was held here under the standing rules.
