# Chile: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (Norte Grande to the Biobío, with Rapa Nui and the Juan Fernández Islands). **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (standard and colloquial); Mapudungun; Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Iquique, Alto Hospicio and Arica: check what border towns call disorder, and whom it is blamed on.
- Aymara villages of the altiplano: check how festival and work calendars keep the community's order.
- Lickanantay *ayllus*: check how tourism in San Pedro de Atacama is felt to disturb local order.
- Diaguita and Colla communities: check order on shared pastures and water.
- Santiago's center and its eastern *comunas*: check how protest damage and clean-up are described in each.
- *Huaso* families of O'Higgins and Maule: check rural order kept by neighbors before police arrive.
- Valparaíso's hills: check how fire, street dogs and festivals are each seen as threats to order or as normal life.
- Lota and Coronel: check how mining-town order was kept by the company, the union and the church.
- Rapa Nui: check whose rules order the coast and the sacred sites.
- Juan Fernández: check how a small island keeps order with few officials.
- Mapuche *warriache*: check gatherings in Santiago parks and how they are policed; Wallmapu is a comparison only.
- Catholic and Evangelical congregations: check processions and open-air worship and how they are permitted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crime statistics, protest records and Santiago's history; mixed for neighbor conflict; thin for island and Colla order.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for the *bandos*; Aymara and Quechua for ideas of balance; Kreyòl and Venezuelan Spanish for tenants' views; Rapa Nui for coastal and site rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2019 protests, the 1973 coup and the Iquique march are contested ground; give police, government, rights groups and residents each as an account. Do not let any bullet describe a group as disorderly.
- **Held for review:** for the measures of order, a cross-national index of tight and loose cultures was weighed; Chile's inclusion is uncertain to me, so it was left out rather than held under a rule.
