# Chile: The domain, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America (from Arica down to the Biobío coast, and the islands of Rapa Nui and Juan Fernández). **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish as spoken in Chile (with *coa*, *huaso* and northern speech); Aymara; Quechua; Kunza; Rapa Nui; Mapudungun; Arabic; Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, associations, hopes and ideals toward humanity at large.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Norte Grande ports against highland villages: check whether joining runs through clubs and unions or through fiesta offices.
- Aymara, Quechua and Lickanantay speakers: check when the inclusive "we" is used about the village, the state or the mining firm.
- Diaguita, Colla and Chango communities: check which new bodies formed after recognition, and who joined.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check rodeo clubs and parish groups as the rural membership map.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* against its gated *condominios*: check which carries social life, council, congregation or private club.
- Mapuche *warriache* associations: check how a gathering is rebuilt in the city, away from any *lof*.
- Concepción, Lota and Coronel: check union halls and fire companies as the coal coast's pattern.
- Rapa Nui and Robinson Crusoe Island: check island-wide gatherings against mainland ones.
- Descent communities (Palestinian, Croatian, Basque, Italian; the Jewish community): check who outside the line enters their schools and clubs.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Bolivian arrivals: check which bodies they found and which older ones admit them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for registers and survey items; mixed for brotherhoods and company towns; thin for Kunza, the Chango and Juan Fernández. Where Chile's lands end is still to be settled, both the southern line and Rapa Nui's status; until then this sign covers the north, the center and the two island groups, and the Araucanía, Los Ríos, Los Lagos with Chiloé, Aysén, Magallanes and Wallmapu are read as Araucanía-Patagonia's, for contrast only.
- **Languages needed:** 1540s Spanish for Valdivia; Aymara and Quechua pronouns for the two kinds of "we"; Rapa Nui for *umanga*; Kreyòl for newcomer groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** groups and hopes run through 1973 and 2019; gather each side's account and its critics'. Brotherhoods and Indigenous gatherings are told as members share them.
- **Held for review:** cross-national individualism scores were weighed for the person-and-group question; they are carried on the Group sheet with a Chilean reason, and nothing was dropped.
