# Chile: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of Chile's towns and *poblaciones*; Aymara and Quechua of the far north; Kunza; Mapudungun as used in Santiago; Rapa Nui; Kreyòl; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Highland villages and their emigrants in Arica: check whether membership is kept by attending, sending money or holding an office.
- Lickanantay communities of the Salar de Atacama: check how membership is recorded and who receives company payments.
- *Comuneros* of the Norte Chico, some of them Diaguita: check how the community treats heirs who left for the city.
- *Huaso* families of the Central Valley: check what parish, rodeo club and harvest work parties each ask of a neighbor.
- *Poblaciones* born of seizures against those the state built: check whether founding stories still set duties.
- Gated *condominios*: check what the residents' committee asks of owners, and how guards and domestic workers stand.
- Mapuche *warriache* families: check whether they name themselves a *comunidad* in Santiago, and on what grounds.
- *Caleta* families, coal families of Lota and Coronel: check how a closed mine or a lost boat redraws who belongs.
- Rapa Nui families and mainland residents on the island, and Robinson Crusoe Island's fishing village: check who is counted in island decisions.
- Haitian arrivals in Quilicura and Estación Central: check whether churches or councils serve as their first community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the *pobladores* movement and state neighborhood programs; mixed for agricultural communities and *caletas*; thin for Kunza, the Colla and Robinson Crusoe Island.
- **Languages needed:** Aymara and Quechua for village duties; Rapa Nui for descent-based membership; Kreyòl for newcomers' congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** councils and base communities under military rule appear differently in official records and in residents' and clergy's memories; gather both. Who belongs on Rapa Nui is disputed between islanders and the state; give each account.
- **Held for review:** no Chilean adaptation of the English-language sense-of-community scale is known to me by name; the research should look for one rather than borrow the original.
