# Chile: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish and *coa*; Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Mapudungun of Santiago families; Kunza; Haitian Creole; Palestinian Arabic. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Descendants of nitrate-port workers in Iquique and Antofagasta: check how the 1907 strike is told as "ours", and by whom.
- Contract and direct-hire miners around Calama: check whether they speak of one crew or two.
- Aymara villages with halves: check whether the halves still run the fiesta when most members live in Arica.
- Lickanantay communities: check when they bargain with lithium firms as one bloc, and when they split.
- Supporters of Colo-Colo, Universidad de Chile, Cobreloa and Santiago Wanderers: check how each *barra* admits, ranks and expels.
- Young people's *piños* in *poblaciones*: check how a small group forms and lets a member go.
- Families of the disappeared and military families: check how each speaks of an institution's shared blame.
- Rapa Nui families: check how the two sides and descent groups appear in today's island bodies.
- Pentecostal congregations: check how a member who leaves is spoken of afterward.
- Haitian arrivals: check which associations join people from different Haitian departments.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history and electoral data; mixed for supporters and mining crews; thin for Kunza, Colla and Chango group forms (no Kunza term offered).
- **Languages needed:** Aymara and Quechua for moieties; Rapa Nui for descent groups; labor-press Spanish of 1900; Palestinian Arabic for family lines.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1907, 1973 and 2019 are contested; give strikers', officials', armed forces' and victims' accounts as accounts. Supporters' violence is described from records, never as a group's nature.
- **Held for review:** police tallies sorting criminal bands by nationality could have served the gangs facet; they stay out because they would label newcomers as groups, which the no-stereotype rule forbids.
