# Chile: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish, with popular and *coa* registers; Mapudungun; Aymara; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Antofagasta, Calama and Iquique against Santiago: check how "equal regions" is framed in each.
- Aymara and Quechua highland families: check whether claims are for equal treatment as citizens or for rights as peoples.
- Lickanantay communities: check what equal standing with mining firms is said to require.
- *Huaso* families and *inquilino* descendants of the Central Valley: check how the hacienda order is remembered beside the land reform.
- Santiago's eastern *comunas* against La Pintana and Bajos de Mena: check which words for class each uses about the other.
- Mapuche *warriache* youth: check experiences of surname and appearance at school and work.
- Afro-descendant families of Arica's valleys: check who pressed for recognition as a people, and who doubted it.
- Rapa Nui families: check how full citizenship is remembered and what it left out.
- Palestinian-, Croatian- and Jewish-descended families: check memories of exclusion from clubs and offices and how they ended.
- Haitian, Peruvian, Bolivian and Venezuelan arrivals: check which inequalities each names first, and to whom.
- Pentecostal congregations: check how "all are brothers" is lived across pastors and members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income data, movements and recent constitutional debates; mixed for manners and address; thin for how Colla and Chango families speak of equal standing, and for any Kunza term.
- **Languages needed:** class slang of Santiago; Mapudungun and Rapa Nui for distinct-standing claims; Kreyòl for newcomers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2019 protests, Unidad Popular's leveling program and the market reforms after 1975 each have backers and critics; present every account as an account. Slurs are recorded, never repeated as description.
- **Held for review:** Chile's anti-discrimination, equal-pay and parity statutes were weighed and sent to Libra, which reads the letter of those laws; this sheet keeps the movements and manners around them.
