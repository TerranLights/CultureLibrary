# Chile: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish (political, colonial and protest registers); Aymara; Rapa Nui (no term offered; island teachers can supply one); Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Land-reform beneficiaries' and former owners' families in O'Higgins, Maule and Ñuble: check how each tells the reform and its partial reversal after 1973.
- Iquique and Arica residents of Peruvian and Aymara descent: check how chilenization is remembered and taught.
- Copper towns (Calama, Rancagua) and coal towns (Lota, Coronel): check how the 1971 nationalization and the 1997 mine closure are judged as reforms.
- Teachers and secondary students in Santiago's public *liceos*: check which reforms they name as theirs and which as imposed.
- Pension savers: check how the 1981 reform is spoken of by those who switched into it and those born into it.
- Catholic social-action groups and Pentecostal churches: check how each weighs changing society against changing the person.
- Mapuche associations in Santiago: check which state programs they call reform and which they call assimilation.
- Rapa Nui families: check which island changes came from the island and which from the mainland.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check how migration-law changes since 2021 are discussed in their own media.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for programs, commissions and the 1970s to 1980s reforms with their critics; mixed for colonial reform seen from below; thin for Kunza and Colla views.
- **Languages needed:** late-colonial Spanish for Salas and the inspection records; Aymara for chilenization memories; Rapa Nui for the schools; economists' Spanish for the 1970s debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Unidad Popular, the market reforms after 1975 and the 2019 movement are fought over; give each side's own sources and its critics' as accounts, and never let one stand as the finding.
- **Held for review:** pension, labor and constitutional texts are reform documents too, but their wording belongs to Libra and their purpose to Capricorn; only the campaigns and quarrels around them stay here.
