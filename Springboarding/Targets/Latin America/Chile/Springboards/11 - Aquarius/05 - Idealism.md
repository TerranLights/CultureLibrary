# Chile: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (literary, political and street registers); Aymara; Quechua; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- University students of Santiago, Valparaíso and Concepción: check which causes draw them, and how former student leaders speak of them later.
- The young right of the 1970s and 1980s and the young left of the same years: check each side's own ideals, in each side's words.
- Teachers, nurses and social workers in public service: check how ideals of a calling meet low pay and burnout.
- Aymara and Quechua households from Putre to Ollagüe: check whether an Andean ideal of good living is claimed, borrowed or refused.
- Lickanantay communities: check what ideal of the land is set against lithium mining, and who in them dissents.
- Mapuche *warriache* youth: check how ideals for Wallmapu are held from Santiago.
- Rapa Nui families: check which images of the island come from outsiders and which from within.
- Palestinian-Chilean youth: check how a distant cause becomes a local ideal.
- Pentecostal and Catholic young people: check how a religious ideal of a renewed life is described.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check the hopes that brought them and how they are spoken of after some years.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, song and political memoirs; mixed for intentional communities; thin for Lickanantay and Colla ideals in their own words and for Robinson Crusoe Island.
- **Languages needed:** colonial convent Spanish for Sor Úrsula; political Spanish of 1970 to 1990; Aymara for the revival writing; Rapa Nui to answer outsiders' images.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** armed militancy, Unidad Popular and the dictatorship's founding ideals are contested; quote each side as an account, give victims' testimony its place, and claim nothing as fact. Colonia Dignidad's "ideal community" belongs with its survivors' accounts, already on other Chile sheets.
- **Held for review:** Chilean writing on philosophical idealism (a theory of mind taught in university philosophy) was weighed and left out as lying beyond this concept's bounds, not under any rule.
