# Chile: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** everyday Chilean Spanish and *coa*; Aymara and Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic; the Venezuelan Spanish of newer arrivals. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Men in mining shifts of the Norte Grande: check how friendships form and lapse across long rotations away from home.
- Riders and families of the Central Valley rodeo: check how a *collera* partnership differs from kin ties.
- Upper-income Santiago schools against *liceos* of the south and west of the city: check how school friendships carry into work and marriage.
- *Pobladores* of La Victoria and La Legua: check how childhood friends of the *pasaje* stay in each other's lives.
- Women in Valparaíso and Concepción: check what female friendship networks do in crisis.
- Aymara families: check whether a *compadre* is chosen for help, honor or closeness.
- Mapuche *warriache* youth: check whether friends come from the association, the school or both.
- Rapa Nui islanders: check how friendships with mainland residents and visitors are spoken of.
- Families of Palestinian, Croatian and Italian descent: check whether lifelong friends come from the community club or from school.
- Venezuelan, Haitian, Peruvian and Bolivian newcomers: check where they meet their first Chilean friend.
- LGBTQ Chileans in Santiago and Valparaíso: check friends as chosen family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and survey items on networks; mixed for ritual friendship; thin for friendship among Colla herders, Chango fishers and the islanders.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for parish books; Aymara and Quechua for *compadrazgo* terms; Kreyòl and Venezuelan Spanish for newcomers' networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parish records name private persons; forums and youth studies may concern minors; use counts, not names.
- **Held for review:** the Trompenaars survey item on shielding a friend who broke a rule was considered for loyalty against duty; whether Chile was in that sample is unclear to me, so it stays a lead to confirm, and the legal side of such a dilemma belongs to Libra.
