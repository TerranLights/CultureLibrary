## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Nitrate-town and port youth of Iquique and Antofagasta: check which styles marked rebellion in each generation.
- Elqui Valley farmers and the newcomers in Cochiguaz: check what each side calls the other.
- Rodeo families of Colchagua and Linares: check how the *huaso* world treats a member who dresses or loves differently.
- Santiago's *poblaciones* and its eastern *comunas*: check which youth styles are policed and which are sold.
- Valparaíso's hills: check whether residents count the port's bohemia as theirs.
- Concepción and the coal towns: check the punk and rock scenes born there and how they were received.
- Mapuche *warriache* artists: check how they answer pressure from the city and from elders at once.
- Aymara Pentecostal and Catholic families: check how a child who leaves the church is spoken of.
- Rapa Nui youth: check how island and mainland youth styles are judged against custom.
- Jewish, Palestinian and Croatian-descended families: check how a member who marries out or drops the faith is treated.
- Haitian and Venezuelan young people in Santiago: check which local styles they adopt and how that is read at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, music and the press; mixed for psychiatric archives; thin for difference within Colla herding families and the island villages.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for Inquisition files; youth slang of three decades; Mapudungun for hip-hop lyrics; Kreyòl.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** psychiatric records are restricted and name patients; sexual and gender difference must be described in people's own terms; the 1980s scene ran under curfew and censorship, so gather the regime's records and the scene's memories as two accounts.
- **Held for review:** the gender-identity statute stands behind the trans pointer, yet it is legal text for Libra to read, and this sheet keeps only how people lived and argued the difference.
