# Chile: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (everyday, clinical, journalistic and devotional registers); Haitian Creole; Arabic; the Aymara and Rapa Nui of mourning customs where families share them. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Aymara families of Arica y Parinacota: check how grief is shown at a burial and at the later mourning rites.
- Port and mining towns of the Norte Grande: check whether composure in danger is taught on the job or at home.
- *Huaso* and farming families of Maule and Ñuble: check what restraint is expected of men at funerals and in disputes.
- Upper-income Santiago against *poblaciones*: check which greetings, distances and silences are polite in each.
- Valparaíso's hills after fire and quake: check who is praised for calm and who blamed for coldness.
- Concepción and Talcahuano: check how officials' composure in 2010 is judged locally.
- Mapuche *warriache* families: check how emotional reserve is read in schools and clinics.
- Rapa Nui families: check mourning customs and how much grief is shown before mainland officials.
- Doctors, nurses, journalists and firefighters: check what detachment their training asks for and what it costs them.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check what they say about distance in Chilean workplaces and buses.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for professional codes, surveys and literature; mixed for mourning customs; thin for composure customs among the Colla, the Diaguita and the islanders.
- **Languages needed:** devotional Spanish for Ignatian sources; clinical and journalistic Spanish; Aymara and Rapa Nui for mourning; Kreyòl.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mourning rites are shared at families' discretion; health data are aggregate only; 1970s press coverage is contested and read as accounts.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist meditation groups in Santiago came up for the equanimity facet; since that tradition is an open question for the author, it stays a category here, with no group or text named.
