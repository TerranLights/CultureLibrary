# Chile: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish; Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Catholic parishes and Pentecostal congregations of one *población*: check who each feeds, and whether help comes with a demand to join.
- Families of the eastern *comunas*: check whether they give through foundations, schools or direct help, and how they speak of those helped.
- Haitian, Venezuelan and Bolivian arrivals: check where they turn first in trouble, and which offers of help they refuse as shaming.
- Iquique and Arica residents: check how local voices on migrants split between pity and refusal, without treating either side as the city's.
- Aymara and Quechua villages: check whether pity runs through kin and godparents rather than outside charity.
- Mapuche families of Santiago: check how help moves among urban associations and kin.
- Rodeo clubs of the Central Valley: check how members answer the animal-welfare charge, and in what terms.
- Descent communities (Palestinian, Croatian, Jewish): check whether their giving stays inside the community or reaches the nation.
- Rapa Nui families: check how help after illness or death is pooled on a small island.
- Lota and Coronel after the mines closed: check which hardship drew national sympathy and which drew none.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Catholic charity and its campaigns; mixed on giving surveys; scant on Andean and island practice, where even the pity words need checking.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish throughout; Aymara and Quechua for the pity verbs; Kreyòl for Haitian help networks; Arabic for alms terms in Palestinian-Chilean homes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** migrants, prisoners and the poor appear in appeals without consent; aid under the dictatorship is contested ground, so take each body's account as an account.
- **Held for review:** international charities with Chilean branches stay off the list, because their appeals address donors abroad more than Chilean pity; no standing rule applied.
