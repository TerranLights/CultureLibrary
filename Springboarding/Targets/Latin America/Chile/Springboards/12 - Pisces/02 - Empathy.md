# Chile: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish, everyday and colloquial; Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of Santiago's *poblaciones* and its wealthy east: check how each side describes the other's life, and with which words.
- Police and young protesters of 2019: check whether either side's testimony grants the other's view, and where it refuses.
- Medical, nursing and social-work students: check how empathy is taught against how it is graded.
- Haitian and Venezuelan newcomers: check what being understood or misread means to them in clinics, schools and work.
- Aymara and Quechua families who moved to Arica: check how they explain the village to children raised in the city.
- Lickanantay guides of San Pedro: check how they decide what tourists are asked to imagine and what stays closed.
- Rapa Nui and mainlanders living on the island: check how each says the other misreads them.
- Fathers and sons in Concepción's coal and steel families: check how feeling for another is spoken of among men.
- Pentecostal congregations: check how testimony in services asks listeners to share a convert's pain.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in psychometric and school data; mixed in media; scarce on Indigenous words for feeling-with, which need native speakers.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including the colloquial forms that carry feeling; Aymara and Mapudungun heart words; Kreyòl for patients' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** truth-commission testimony and 2019 accounts are contested ground; Eltit's subject was a living, unwell man, so treat the text with care.
- **Held for review:** translated "emotional intelligence" self-help on Chilean shelves is not listed; it carries foreign thought rather than Chilean, and no standing rule was involved.
