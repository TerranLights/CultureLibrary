# Chile: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish as used in Chile's stadiums, churches, chronicles and political memoirs; Aymara; Haitian Creole; Arabic; Rapa Nui and Mapudungun terms to come from speakers. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Supporters of the 1973 government and of the regime that followed: check how each side tells giving in and holding out.
- Football fans of the older generation and of the 2015 champions: check whether "moral victory" still sounds like praise.
- AA members in Santiago and in mining towns: check how the language of handing over will fits with ideas of manhood.
- Pentecostal converts and Catholic devotees: check whether giving oneself to God is a single moment or a lifelong practice.
- Coastal families of Talcahuano, Dichato and Iquique: check who left at the alarm and who waited, and why.
- Elders in the Central Valley and in Santiago apartments: check what giving up a house means where land carries the family name.
- Aymara families after drought or the end of herding: check how leaving the village is spoken of.
- Haitian newcomers: check how acceptance phrases are used when plans in Chile fail.
- Rapa Nui families: check how past impositions are told without the word "surrender".
- Palestinian-Chilean families: check how God-willing phrases are used, and by Christians as well as Muslims.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on political events and health research; mixed on recovery and devotion; scarce in Indigenous languages, where defeat words need native speakers.
- **Languages needed:** the conquest-era Spanish of the chronicle; Aymara and Mapudungun words for defeat and leaving; Kreyòl and Arabic for acceptance phrases.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1973 and 1988 are contested ground, and each side's memory is presented as an account; conquest chronicles speak only for the conquerors; Rapa Nui and Mapuche histories of imposed rule belong to those peoples.
- **Held for review:** kept off: the 1888 Rapa Nui agreement. The Aries independence sheet carries its contested readings, and listing it under surrender would frame island history as a defeat.
