*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Chilean Spanish (press, *coa* and church usage); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Here wrongdoing is a moral and social category only; statutes, courts and penal codes are researched under Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), and this sheet points there for them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of Santiago's *poblaciones* and of the wealthy east: check which wrongs each side names first, and which it calls exaggerated.
- Small shopkeepers in Antofagasta and Iquique: check how they talk about theft and extortion, and whom they blame.
- Haitian, Venezuelan, Peruvian and Colombian residents: check how they report being treated as suspects, and what they themselves call crime.
- Aymara and Quechua villages: check whether cattle theft and broken agreements weigh more than offenses the state ranks higher.
- *Huaso* families: check old cattle-rustling stories and how outlaws of the countryside are remembered.
- Evangelical congregations: check whether a converted former prisoner is fully accepted, and on what terms.
- Rapa Nui families: check how wrongs are handled between clans before police are involved, as islanders choose to share.
- Relatives of the disappeared and relatives of officers later convicted: check which acts each calls a crime, and which words each refuses.
- Young people in trap and urban music scenes: check how outlaw imagery is meant and how it is heard.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for surveys and white-collar scandals; mixed for redemption and reintegration; scarce for Indigenous and island sanctions.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish legal-press and *coa* registers; Aymara for village terms; Kreyòl for newcomers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** crimes of the dictatorship, Colonia Dignidad and the 2019 unrest are contested ground, and no single telling stands for all. Migrants and the poor are easily stereotyped; victims of abuse are living people.
- **Held for review:** Colonia Dignidad's crimes stay off this list: four earlier sheets (Scorpio control, Libra peace at any price, Sagittarius fundamentalism, Aquarius idealism) already name the enclave, and survivors' own sources should lead when it is taken up as wrongdoing.
