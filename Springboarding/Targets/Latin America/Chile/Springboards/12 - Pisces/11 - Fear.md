# Chile: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Chile, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Spanish of Chile (nursery, street and newsroom speech); Aymara; Quechua; Mapudungun; Rapa Nui; Haitian Creole; Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Residents of Ñuble, Maule and the Biobío coast: check how quake memory is told in families who lived through 1939 or 2010.
- Northern port dwellers of Iquique and Arica: check fear of the next great quake and wave, and what drills mean to them.
- Families who lived the 1970s and 1980s on different sides: check which fears each names first, and which it never speaks.
- Night-shift workers and students, women and men alike: check who fears the walk home, and who is mocked for admitting it.
- Aymara and Quechua families: check whether fear figures like the *kharisiri* still guide behavior toward outsiders.
- Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals: check fears of the police, of landlords and of being sent away.
- Rapa Nui families: check how the sea and strangers are spoken of as dangers, and how children are warned.
- Former miners' households in Lota and Coronel: check whether pit fear outlived the pits.
- Pentecostal and secular young people: check whether fear of God or fear of the future weighs more.
- Juan Fernández islanders: check how the night of the 2010 wave is retold, and by whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and opinion series; mixed for dictatorship-era fear; scarce for Andean and island fear figures.
- **Languages needed:** period Spanish to read the founder's letters; Aymara and Quechua for fright words; Kreyòl for *sezisman*; Rapa Nui.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the dictatorship and the 2019 unrest are contested, and fear runs on every side of them; the *kharisiri* story can feed fear of outsiders and must not be taken as fact; disaster memories are recent grief.
- **Held for review:** no security-company "fear index" is listed: such figures double as marketing and hide their method, so a public or university survey stands in their place.
