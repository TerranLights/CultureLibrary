# México: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (central, northern, Bajío, Jalisco and coastal varieties); Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi (*tu'un savi*); Zapotec (*diidxazá* and the Valley and Sierra languages); Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol; Hñähñu; Purépecha; Rarámuri; Wixárika; Ayuuk. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming and beginnings.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City against Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check naming and self-introduction in the capital's barrios against the old Nahua towns nearby; the capital sets the media image.
- **Nahua communities of the Huasteca, Sierra Norte de Puebla, Guerrero and Morelos:** check which classical soul terms survive in each variety; the colonial sources are almost all from the Valley.
- **Oaxaca's Ñuu Savi, Zapotec and Ayuuk towns:** check how far a person is introduced through the home town and its offices (the *comunalidad* writers Floriberto Díaz and Jaime Martínez Luna *check*).
- **Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol and Tojolabal towns:** check town-by-town dress and surnames that join Spanish and lineage names.
- **Rarámuri, O'dam (Tepehuan), Wixárika (Huichol) and Náayeri peoples:** check their own accounts against the outsider travel writing that still shapes their image.
- **Purépecha, Hñähñu and Mazahua towns:** check markers beyond the best-studied villages.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check how the new census category is named locally.
- **The north (Nuevo León, Chihuahua, Durango, Sinaloa, Coahuila, Zacatecas, Tamaulipas), the Bajío and Jalisco:** check regional self-labels (*regio*, *norteño*, *alteño*, *tapatío*) and dress as identity statements.
- **Mennonites, the Kikapú of Coahuila, Lebanese-, Chinese-, Japanese- and Jewish Mexicans, descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check how double belonging shows in names and dress.
- **Internal migrants in the cities; Central American migrants in the south:** check how a newcomer's self-presentation shifts; also *quince años* ethnographies by town and class.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán (Peninsula, Petén, Yucatec Maya), Sonora (Yoeme, Comcaac, O'odham), Cascadia (Baja California; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano communities and the diaspora in the United States. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Nahua colonial sources, national-identity debates and census categories; mixed for most Oaxaca and Chiapas peoples; thin for the Kikapú and for Chinese- and Japanese-Mexican self-description. México's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sign is written for the core lands of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the colonial base; living Indigenous languages for any people's own account; Plautdietsch for the Mennonites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** skin-color vocabulary; census categories are political; *muxe'* and other gender identities; Wixárika and Rarámuri ritual knowledge may be restricted to initiates.
- **Held for review:** a South Asian-born figure in Mexican costume lore (category only, under the South Asian rule).

## 5. First queries
1. Open the 2020 census questionnaire on INEGI's site and list every identity question and option.
2. Read López Austin on the *tonalli*, then search the GDN for *tonalli* and *yollotl*.
3. Sample FamilySearch baptisms from one parish each in Oaxaca, Jalisco and Chihuahua (1700, 1850, 1950) for naming patterns.
4. Search CIESAS and UNAM catalogs for "persona" + "etnografía" + each people's name.
5. Read Bartra's introduction to list the national-character essays to treat as sources.
