# México: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (capital, northern, Bajío and Gulf speech); Nahuatl; Isthmus and Valley Zapotec; Tsotsil; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The capital's old barrios (Tepito, La Merced) against its new middle-class suburbs:** check whether the *albur* and market banter still serve as tests of standing, and who joins in.
- **Monterrey and the northern states against the Bajío:** check business-training and school materials for how "speaking up" is taught to employees and pupils; regional sayings about directness need evidence, not repeating.
- **Oaxaca's Zapotec, Ñuu Savi and Ayuuk assembly towns:** check minutes and ethnographies for who may speak at the *asamblea*, in what order, and how women's turns have changed.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check how reputation talk and the cargo ladder limit who can put themselves forward.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika communities:** check the speaking order at gatherings and the elders' councils as each people describes them.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of Guerrero and Oaxaca:** check how claims for recognition are voiced in their own organizations, apart from outside advocates.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check how the church leadership's word and dissenting members' claims are handled in their own records.
- **Lebanese-Mexican and Jewish communities:** check their community bodies' records for who speaks for the community before the state.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán's Maya towns (their own assembly speech), Sonora, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities, plus the diaspora in the United States (speaking up in a second language); where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: rich on language and urban talk (dictionaries, corpora, politeness studies) and on colonial petitions; thin on measured assertiveness inside the country and on the smaller Indigenous peoples' speaking rules.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Nahuatl for the colonial petitions; Tsotsil, Zapotec and Rarámuri for any community's own speech genres.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *albur* material is sexual; *igualado* and its kin carry class and racial loading; recorded confrontation videos involve private people.
- **Held for review:** none; no self-assertion pointer was set aside.
