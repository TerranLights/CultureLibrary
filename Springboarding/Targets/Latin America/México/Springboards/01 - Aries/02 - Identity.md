# México: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish and its regional self-labels; Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi; Zapotec (*diidxazá*); Hñähñu; Purépecha; Tsotsil; Rarámuri; Wixárika; Ayuuk; Plautdietsch for the Mennonites. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City, Monterrey and Guadalajara:** check what each city's people say first when introducing themselves, and the tone of each label (*chilango*, *regio*, *tapatío*) in and out of town.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío:** check parish and family-history sources behind ancestry claims rather than repeating them.
- **Nahua communities (Huasteca, Puebla, Guerrero) and Teenek (Huastec) towns:** check whether *macehual* or a town name answers "who are you" before "Indigenous".
- **Ñuu Savi, Zapotec, Ayuuk, Mazatec, Chinantec and Triqui towns of Oaxaca:** check identity by town against by people; each language has many varieties.
- **Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol and Tojolabal (Chiapas):** check dress and town identity, and how Chiapas's joining to Mexico in 1824 figures in state identity.
- **Purépecha and Hñähñu:** check the place of the language and the home town for migrants to cities.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check which self-names local people prefer.
- **Mennonites, the Kikapú of El Nacimiento, Japanese-Mexicans, descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check how each keeps or blends a double identity across generations.
- **Protestant and Evangelical converts:** check how conversion changes a person's place in a Catholic town's identity.
- **Internal migrants and Central American migrants in the south:** check when they begin to call themselves locals.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán and its Maya, Sonora and its Yoeme, Comcaac and O'odham, Cascadia's Baja California, the CSA's Tejano communities, and the Chicano and Mexican-American diaspora (*pocho* is a word for this boundary). Who falls on which side is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: identity is among the most written-about themes in Mexican scholarship; thinner for Teenek, Chinantec, Triqui, Kikapú and Japanese-Mexican self-accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Nahuatl and Ñuu Savi for colonial records; living Indigenous languages for self-names; Plautdietsch; Arabic and Hebrew for some community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racial vocabulary; outsider words like *chabochi* and *teiwari*; Mennonite and Kikapú privacy; disputes over who counts as Indigenous for state programs.
- **Held for review:** none; no identity pointer needed holding.
