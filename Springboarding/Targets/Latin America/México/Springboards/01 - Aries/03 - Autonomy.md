*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; Ayuuk; Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Purépecha; Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol and Tojolabal; Me'phaa and Ñuu Savi of La Montaña de Guerrero; Nahuatl (colonial and modern). **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled, for a person and for a town or people.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oaxaca's assembly towns (Ayuuk, Zapotec of the Sierra Norte, Ñuu Savi, Chinantec, Mazatec):** check how cargo service and *tequio* bound personal choice, and how migrants and women negotiate them.
- **Zapatista and non-Zapatista towns in the Chiapas highlands:** check differences in decision-making, since both live side by side.
- **Cherán against nearby Purépecha towns:** check whether self-rule spread or stayed local.
- **La Montaña and the Costa Chica of Guerrero (Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi, Ñomndaa, Afro-Mexican towns):** check community policing and marriage choice, with care.
- **The *ejidos* of the Bajío, Jalisco and the north:** check how land reform after 1992 changed who decides for a family.
- **Monterrey, Guadalajara and Mexico City middle classes:** check the age of moving out and say over spouse and career against working-class districts.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua and Durango:** check the colony's control of schooling and how members who leave describe it.
- **The Kikapú of El Nacimiento:** check how the group keeps decisions on land and seasonal travel; sources are scarce, so look to Coahuila state archives and the few ethnographies.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika governance:** check the *siríame* and the *kawiterutsixi* (elders' council) as each people describes them.
- **Evangelical families in Catholic towns:** check disputes over refusing festival cargo and the right to choose a faith.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora's Yoeme towns (a long record of self-rule claims), Yucatán's Maya communities, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities, to set off the core's self-rule cases; the borders are the author's to settle.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on collective autonomy (Zapatista, *comunalidad*, *ejido*) and on surveys of women's decisions; mixed on young people's autonomy by region; thin on the Kikapú and on Mennonite members' own views.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Ayuuk, Purépecha, Tsotsil and Tseltal for community records; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** autonomy movements and community police face conflict; marriage payments in parts of Guerrero are disputed and sensitive; abortion and bodily choice are contested (the legal side to Libra and Capricorn); religious expulsions in Chiapas.
- **Held for review:** none for autonomy.
