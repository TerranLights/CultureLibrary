## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monterrey and Nuevo León against Puebla's textile towns and Jalisco's Guadalajara tech corridor:** check each region's founder stories and business-formation rates side by side.
- **Mexico City's markets and street vending:** check who may start a stall, and through which leaders or vendors' organizations.
- **Oaxaca's Zapotec, Ñuu Savi and Ayuuk towns:** check who calls *tequio*, who volunteers for cargo, and how private ventures are judged.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital Valley:** check the *faena* and the community enterprises built with migrant money.
- **Purépecha craft towns (Paracho, Santa Clara del Cobre):** check family workshops and how a new workshop starts.
- **Rarámuri communities:** check the *tesgüinada* work party as the people describe it.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of the Costa Chica:** check local cooperatives and fishing groups; sources are few, so look in CIESAS Pacífico Sur and state archives.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican families:** check community business histories and their own associations as sources, without assuming a pattern.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check how dairy and machinery businesses start under colony rules.
- **Women in micro-enterprise and *tandas*:** check gendered data in ENAMIN and the inclusion survey.
- **Central American migrants in Chiapas:** check informal work in Tapachula as a forced start.
- **Comparison only:** Cascadia's Baja California (border assembly plants), Sonora, Yucatán and the CSA's Tejano businesses, as contrasts for founding stories; the author settles where México ends.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business statistics and civil-society chronicles; mixed for children's initiative (a few strong studies); thin for Afro-Mexican, Kikapú and Mennonite enterprise.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Zapotec, Hñähñu and Purépecha for community labor terms; Rarámuri for the work party; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** informal vending is political; migrant data involve undocumented people.
