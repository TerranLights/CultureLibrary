# México: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (including rural almanac and farming speech); classical and modern Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi; Purépecha; Ayuuk; Wixárika; Zapotec. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Valley of Mexico against Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check rival founding stories (Tenochtitlan, Tlaxcala, Puebla's own legend) and how each city stages them today.
- **Ñuu Savi, Zapotec and Ayuuk towns of Oaxaca:** check when each town's year begins (*cambio de varas*, patron feast, ritual count) and who opens it.
- **Purépecha towns of Michoacán:** check the Purépecha New Year (*Kurhikuaeri K'uinchekua* *check*): when it was first held, set against older town feasts.
- **Wixárika and Rarámuri:** check the start of the ceremonial and farming year as each people describes it.
- **Nahua and Teenek towns of the Huasteca:** check first-fruit offerings for maize and the start of planting.
- **Northern states and the Bajío:** check *cabañuelas* and almanac use among ranchers and farmers.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check their own origin stories against outsiders' accounts of their origin.
- **Mennonites (arrival in 1922), Spanish Republican exiles (1939), Lebanese-Mexicans (arrival through Veracruz):** check arrival narratives and anniversaries; the Museo Menonita in Cuauhtémoc is one place to start.
- **Internal migrants and Central American migrants in the south:** check how newcomers describe the first months.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán (its own Maya origin and year-start texts), Sonora, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities, to set the core's founding stories beside theirs; the line between them is the author's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for origin texts and colonial founding records; mixed for living year-start customs (scattered in local ethnographies); thin for Afro-Mexican and Kikapú origin accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi for the codices' glosses; Purépecha; Ayuuk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some year-start rites and ritual counts are kept by specialists and not shared; *anexos* involve abuse cases; founding stories carry conquest and dispossession.
- **Held for review:** none; the founding and year-start pointers raised no rule.
