# México: Independence (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (ranchero, northern and urban registers); Nahuatl (colonial wills; Sierra Norte de Puebla today); Totonac; Rarámuri; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** not depending on others: self-reliance and self-sufficiency of persons, households, regions and the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ranchero zones of Jalisco, Michoacán, Zacatecas and the Bajío:** check family histories and microhistories for how debt and owing are spoken of, and where migration money fits.
- **Nuevo León, Coahuila and Tamaulipas:** check claims of standing apart from the capital, from the 1840 bid to modern fiscal disputes, and who makes them.
- **Nahua and Totonac towns of the Sierra Norte de Puebla and Veracruz:** check cooperatives against household provisioning from the milpa.
- **Rarámuri communities:** check how *kórima* and household self-provisioning are described by the people themselves.
- **Ñuu Savi, Zapotec and Triqui households of Oaxaca and Guerrero:** check remittance reliance and seasonal work in other states.
- **Afro-Mexican fishing and farming towns of the Costa Chica:** check provisioning and outside aid; few studies, so start with CIESAS Pacífico Sur.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check colony self-sufficiency in food and machinery, and their water disputes with neighbors in Chihuahua.
- **The Kikapú of El Nacimiento:** check the place of hunting and seasonal travel in their own support; sources are scarce.
- **Elders in Mexico City and in emptied migrant towns:** check living alone against living with kin.
- **Disabled people's organizations in the big cities:** check their definition of independence against family care.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán (its 1840s break with Mexico), Sonora, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA (Texas's 1836 secession as the neighboring case): other culture-nations' independence stories, with the boundary left to the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household surveys, remittances and national independence; mixed for regional self-reliance; thin for the Kikapú, Mennonite households and Afro-Mexican provisioning.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; colonial Nahuatl for wills; Totonac and Nahuatl for cooperative records; Rarámuri; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** welfare and remittance dependence carry stigma; water conflicts in Chihuahua are violent at times; separatist talk is politically charged.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on self-reliance was set aside.
