# México: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (with its folk-medicine and vulgar registers); classical Nahuatl and Guerrero Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Zapotec; Ñuu Savi. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City against Guadalajara and Monterrey:** check emotion surveys and court data for differences, not regional sayings about temper.
- **Nahua towns of Guerrero (Zitlala, Acatlán) and the Huasteca:** check ritual combat and its rules against everyday quarrel-settling.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns:** check how anger is linked to illness and to the cargo ladder's demands.
- **Zapotec and Ñuu Savi towns of Oaxaca:** check how towns settle quarrels through their authorities (the legal side goes to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Rarámuri communities:** check, in their own accounts, whether and how grievances are aired or settled at drinking gatherings.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check local studies of honor and conflict; few exist, so start with Gonzalo Aguirre Beltrán's *Cuijla* (1958) *check* and read it critically.
- **Women and men:** check gendered *coraje* talk in clinics and the help sought.
- **Evangelical and Catholic congregations:** check sermons and teaching on wrath and forgiveness.
- **Students, teachers' unions and families of the disappeared:** check collective anger in movements (1968, 2006, Ayotzinapa 2014).
- **Central American migrants in the south:** check how anger at abuse on the route is voiced in shelter testimonies.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán, Sonora, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities, where folk-illness words for anger may overlap with the core's; record overlaps without merging them, as the boundary is the author's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial court records, folk illness and protest; mixed for measured emotion; thin for small Indigenous peoples' anger vocabularies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for confession manuals; Tsotsil, Zapotec and Ñuu Savi for illness terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic and gender violence; ongoing repression of protest; some healing knowledge is private to healers.
- **Held for review:** none: no anger pointer fell under the standing rules.
