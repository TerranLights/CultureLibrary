# México: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (capital, northern, Bajío, Jalisco and coastal usage); Nahuatl (classical and Huasteca, Puebla and Guerrero varieties); Zapotec (*diidxazá* and the Valley languages); Ñuu Savi (*tu'un savi*); Tsotsil and Tseltal; Purépecha; Hñähñu; Totonac. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, food, dress and wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City's market quarters (Tepito, La Lagunilla, La Merced) against provincial market days:** check secondhand goods, credit and bargaining in the capital against weekly *tianguis* in Puebla, Tlaxcala and Hidalgo.
- **Oaxaca's Central Valleys and the Isthmus:** check *guelaguetza* gift ledgers kept by families, and Isthmus *vela* spending, as records of what is owed and returned.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns:** check how cargo spending has changed since Cancian, including towns where converts left the system.
- **Purépecha craft towns and the Meseta:** check how goods made for sale are spoken of against goods kept.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco, the Bajío, Zacatecas and Michoacán:** check what remittance money buys (houses, trucks, festival gifts) in high-migration towns.
- **Monterrey and the industrial north against ranching Chihuahua and Durango:** check family-firm histories against cattle and land as stores of value.
- **Nahua and Teenek towns of the Huasteca:** check maize, cattle and land in household accounts.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of the Costa Chica:** check land and livestock holding; sources are few, so look to regional universities in Guerrero and Oaxaca.
- **Mennonite colonies, Lebanese-Mexican families, Chinese-Mexican families of Torreón and the capital:** check community histories for how goods and firms are held across generations.
- **Comparison only, for goods, markets and remittances that cross the edges:** Yucatán (Peninsula, Petén, Yucatec Maya), Sonora (Yoeme, Comcaac, O'odham), Cascadia's Baja California (Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano communities and the Mexican-American diaspora; check what is bought, sent home or kept across each line. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: national household surveys, colonial testaments and Mexican economic anthropology are deep; mixed for living Indigenous exchange customs; thin for the Kikapú and for Chinese- and Japanese-Mexican household economies. México's exact lands are still to be settled by the author; this sign is written for the core lands of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for wills and tribute glosses; Zapotec for *guelaguetza* ledgers; Tsotsil for cargo records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** poverty studies (Oscar Lewis's "culture of poverty" is disputed); some ritual objects may not be shown; land disputes are live; property law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none at the domain level.
