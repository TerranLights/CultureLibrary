# México: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (its regional words for things); Nahuatl; Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Tsotsil; Purépecha; Wixárika; Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Isthmus of Tehuantepec (Juchitán, Tehuantepec):** check gold jewelry and embroidered dress kept and handed down for *velas*, as family-held goods.
- **Ñuu Savi towns of the Mixteca and their migrants:** check what is carried to the cities and sent back, against the colonial wills.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns:** check what is buried with the dead and what is given away, in ethnographies of Chamula and Zinacantán.
- **Purépecha towns around Lake Pátzcuaro:** check objects placed for the dead on the night of November 1 (*Animecha Kejtzitakua* *check*).
- **Wixárika family shrines and community temples:** check which ritual objects belong to a family and which to the community, as the people allow it to be known.
- **Monterrey, Ciudad Juárez and Nuevo Laredo:** check *segunda* stores and goods "from the other side" in households.
- **Mexico City by *alcaldía*:** check census goods items against ethnographies of the *vitrina* and home altar.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua and Durango:** check colony rules on vehicles, phones and radios, and how they changed.
- **Japanese-Mexican families moved inland in 1942:** check what they kept or lost, in community histories.
- **Central American migrants in Chiapas and Oaxaca:** check belongings carried and lost on the route.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora (the Sonoran Desert's migrant objects), Cascadia's Baja California (Tijuana's secondhand trade), Yucatán and the CSA's Tejano communities; Mexican-American families sending parcels south are diaspora. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial wills and inventories and for INEGI goods data; mixed for living customs about the dead's things; thin for the Kikapú, Rarámuri and Afro-Mexican household goods.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl and colonial *tu'un savi* for wills; Zapotec and Tsotsil for grammar and burial customs; Japanese for some community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grave goods and Wixárika shrine objects; looted archaeological pieces in private hands; migrants' testimony; theft data by place can stigmatize neighborhoods.
- **Held for review:** none among the possession pointers.
