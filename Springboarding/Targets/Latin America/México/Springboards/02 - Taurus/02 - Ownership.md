# México: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (agrarian and urban-housing vocabularies); classical and modern Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi; Totonac; Rarámuri; Wixárika; Ayuuk. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Morelos and Guerrero's Tierra Caliente:** check what *ejido* members did with land after 1992, against Zapatista-era memory.
- **Oaxaca's communal-land towns (Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Chinantec, Triqui):** check boundary quarrels between neighboring towns, each side's papers and maps.
- **Totonac towns of Papantla and the Gulf coast:** check living memory of the divided vanilla lands against Kourí's archive.
- **The urban rim of the Valley of Mexico (Nezahualcóyotl, Chalco, Ecatepec):** check how settlers speak of owning before and after titles.
- **The Pacific tourist coasts (Nayarit, Guerrero, Oaxaca's coast):** check *ejido* land sold for resorts, and who still holds what.
- **Rarámuri communities of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check the gap between *ejido* membership on paper and Rarámuri use of the forest, as their organizations state it.
- **Wixárika and the Wirikuta pilgrimage lands:** check the dispute over mining concessions in land they hold sacred but do not hold title to.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check colony-held against family-held land, and land and well disputes with neighbors.
- **Women in *ejidos* and in inheritance:** check women's share of rights and how widows and daughters inherit.
- **The Kikapú of El Nacimiento, Coahuila:** check how their land was granted and is held; sources are scarce, so look to state archives and community statements.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora's Yoeme river towns (their own territorial claim), Yucatán's henequen *ejidos*, Cascadia's Baja California (foreign buyers on the coast) and the CSA's Tejano land-grant memory. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: Mexican agrarian history is among the deepest fields in the country's scholarship; mixed for urban ownership feelings; thin for the Kikapú and for Afro-Mexican land on the Costa Chica.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for cadastral codices and titles; Ñuu Savi for codex glosses; Totonac and Rarámuri for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land conflicts between towns can turn violent; Wirikuta and other sacred lands; what the law says about *ejidos* and Article 27 goes to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit), not here.
- **Held for review:** nothing held for ownership.
