# México: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (with the capital's and the north's terms of address); Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Ayuuk; Zapotec; Hñähñu. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Employers in Mexico City, Monterrey and Guadalajara against household workers from Oaxaca, Puebla and Hidalgo:** check each side's account of respect at work, using the workers' own testimony first.
- **San Cristóbal de las Casas and the surrounding Tsotsil and Tseltal towns:** check accounts of being looked down on in the market town, from both sides.
- **Ayuuk, Zapotec and Hñähñu speakers who moved to cities:** check testimony on shame at speaking the language and its later reversal.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of Guerrero, Oaxaca and Veracruz:** check how recognition campaigns since 2015 speak of worth.
- **Industrial men of Monterrey, Saltillo and Ciudad Juárez:** check worth after layoffs and plant closures, in labor and health studies.
- **High-migration towns of the Bajío, Jalisco and Zacatecas:** check how returned and deported migrants are received and how they describe it.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal converts:** check conversion and sobriety *testimonios* as accounts of worth rebuilt.
- **Descendants of Spanish Republican exiles and Jewish refugees of the 1930s and 1940s:** check memoirs for worth rebuilt after losing a country and a profession.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula and the southern shelters:** check testimony on dignity under waiting and detention.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán's Maya towns (their own terms of respect), Sonora, Cascadia's Baja California (Tijuana's deportees), the CSA's Tejano communities and the Chicano "brown pride" movement in the United States. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: discrimination and well-being surveys are good; the "Mexican character" essays are plentiful but are opinion; Indigenous and Afro-Mexican accounts in their own voice are fewer and scattered.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the elders' speeches; Tseltal, Tsotsil and Ayuuk for testimony in the speakers' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data; skin-color, class and ethnic slurs (*naco* among them) appear in sources; domestic workers' testimony needs consent and care.
- **Held for review:** no self-worth pointer was held.
