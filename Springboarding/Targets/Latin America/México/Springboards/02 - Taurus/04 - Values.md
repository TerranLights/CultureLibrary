# México: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (school, church and political registers); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Tojolabal, Tseltal and Tsotsil; Wixárika; Plautdietsch for the Mennonite colonies. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío against Mexico City:** check values-survey answers on religion and family by region, and the Cristero memory behind them.
- **Tabasco:** check how the anticlerical 1920s and 1930s are remembered in families and parishes.
- **Chamula and other highland Chiapas towns:** check the expulsions of converts since the 1970s as value conflicts, told by both the expelled and those who stayed.
- **Tojolabal, Tseltal and Zapatista communities:** check Lenkersdorf's claims against what speakers and community bodies publish.
- **Monterrey's industrial families:** check company histories and codes for the values firms announced to workers.
- **Old Colony and more open Mennonite groups:** check disputes over schooling, vehicles and radios.
- **Wixárika communities:** check their own statements on which ritual crafts may be sold and which may not.
- **Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican and Evangelical schools:** check the values each community's schools add to the national program.
- **Generations in the big cities:** check WVS answers by age cohort before reading any trend into them.
- **Comparison only:** Yucatán (its own church-state and Maya histories), Sonora's Yoeme, Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities; the U.S. Latino value scales (*familismo*, *personalismo*) were built on diaspora samples, so they are tested, not borrowed. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: several waves of national and international values surveys, abundant school and church material; mixed for Indigenous peoples' own statements of values; thin for the Kikapú and Afro-Mexican communities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl and Purépecha for the colonial texts; Tojolabal and Tsotsil for community voices; Plautdietsch for colony debates.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious expulsions; the 2023 textbook quarrel is partisan; narcocorrido debates touch on violence; the law side of church and state goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no values pointer met a standing rule.
