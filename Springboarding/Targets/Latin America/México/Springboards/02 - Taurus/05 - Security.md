# México: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (with its double sense of *seguridad* as welfare and as freedom from crime); classical Nahuatl; Rarámuri; Isthmus Zapotec; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Workers with IMSS coverage in Monterrey and the Bajío against informal workers on the Valley of Mexico's rim:** check how coverage changes savings, housing and plans for old age.
- **Teachers and other holders of a *plaza*, including Oaxaca's teachers' section:** check how a permanent post figures in family strategy and inheritance of posts.
- **Places marked by cartel violence (Michoacán's Tierra Caliente, Guerrero, Tamaulipas, Zacatecas):** check residents' own accounts of changed routes, hours and talk.
- **Tlatelolco, Roma and the Isthmus after the earthquakes of 1985 and 2017:** check neighborhood mutual aid and its memory.
- **Rarámuri communities of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check drought and hunger years as Rarámuri organizations describe them, and how *kórima* works then.
- **Veracruz and the Gulf coast:** check hurricane-season preparation and who rebuilds.
- **The Costa Chica's Afro-Mexican towns:** check hurricane and land-insecurity accounts; sources are few, so ask regional universities.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check colony aid funds set against commercial insurance.
- **Central American migrants in the south:** check how shelters keep people safe and what migrants say about danger.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora (its desert crossings), Cascadia's Baja California (Tijuana's crime reporting), Yucatán (its own hurricane records) and the CSA's Tejano communities; remittances from the diaspora as household insurance are compared, not counted. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: INEGI's crime, employment and financial surveys are frequent and fine-grained; mixed for how security feels to people; thin for Indigenous risk-sharing customs in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the annals; Rarámuri and Zapotec for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence and disappearances; testimony can endanger speakers; Santa Muerte devotion is stigmatized; social-security law belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none for security.
