## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City's *pueblos originarios* against the newer *colonias* around them:** check years in the same home and the continuity of fiestas and family plots.
- **San José de Gracia and other small towns of Michoacán and Los Altos:** check González's portrait against today's migration and return.
- **Tepoztlán today:** check how the town that two ethnographers read differently tells its own continuity.
- **Old Colony Mennonites of Chihuahua:** check the 1922 move as a choice to keep a way of life unchanged, and the later splits and moves to other countries.
- **Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi and Triqui farmworker families from Guerrero's Montaña and Oaxaca:** check how the yearly circuit to Sinaloa's fields shapes schooling, marriage and home.
- **Mining and oil towns (Real de Catorce, Zacatecas, Poza Rica, Minatitlán):** check boom-and-bust memory against Monterrey's long-lived firms.
- **Descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check whether exile institutions (the Colegio Madrid, the Ateneo Español) carried continuity across three generations.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika ceremonial calendars:** check how each people describes yearly renewal, without assuming change or its absence.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check family continuity on the land and the effects of migration north; sources are scarce, so start with community organizations.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora's Yoeme (their own continuity on the river), Yucatán's Maya villages, Cascadia's Baja California (San Quintín's settled farmworkers) and the CSA's Tejano communities. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mobility, divorce and inflation statistics and for community studies; mixed for felt stability; thin for the Kikapú and for seasonal migrants' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the calendar texts; Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi and Triqui for farmworker families; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** one-party stability is still a partisan subject; displacement and evictions; renewal ceremonies may be restricted.
