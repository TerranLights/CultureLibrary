# México: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **San Pedro Garza García against Monterrey's outer *colonias*:** check how wealth is shown or kept quiet, in local press and memoirs.
- **Polanco, Lomas and Santa Fe against Iztapalapa:** check consumption and leisure records for the capital's extremes.
- **Culiacán and Sinaloa's towns:** check how residents describe narco wealth, through local journalists and *corrido* audiences.
- **Guanajuato and Zacatecas:** check colonial silver fortunes in town memory, churches and family names.
- **Zinacantán's flower growers:** check how new greenhouse and trucking wealth sits with cargo duties.
- **Rarámuri communities:** check what counts as wealth (goats, maize stores, kin to call on) in the people's own terms.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check cattle and land as stores of wealth; sources are thin, so start with community organizations and regional universities.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican communities:** check each community's own histories of getting ahead, the families who did not as well as those who did, so outside images are never taken as findings.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check how colony rules treat members who grow rich, and what is shared.
- **Women household heads:** check EMOVI data on women's mobility and widows' holdings.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora (the home of *corridos tumbados*), Yucatán's henequen fortunes (Mérida's Paseo de Montejo), Cascadia's Los Cabos resort economy and the CSA's Tejano communities. Where México ends against them is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: inequality, mobility and elite history are well studied; mixed for the felt meanings of wealth; thin for Indigenous reckonings of wealth in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Mexica economic terms; Tsotsil and Rarámuri for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** class slurs (*naco*); narco wealth and its dangers for researchers; named living billionaires; kidnapping fears.
