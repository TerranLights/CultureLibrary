## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **San Salvador Atenco and the Texcoco lake towns:** check participants' accounts of the 2001-2002 refusal and the later splits.
- **Coahuila's coal region:** check how Pasta de Conchos families describe holding on over the years.
- **Searchers in Sinaloa, Veracruz, Guanajuato and Jalisco:** check each collective's account of persistence and of being told to stop.
- **Oaxaca City and the teachers' towns:** check how 2006 is remembered by teachers, merchants and residents.
- **Purépecha towns like Naranja:** check whether old factional quarrels still shape local politics.
- **Tlaxcala:** check colonial petitions and modern civic memory of the city's privileges.
- **Old Colony Mennonites:** check refusals that led groups to emigrate again (to Bolivia, Belize, Argentina) rather than yield.
- **Rarámuri who kept apart from the missions:** check the people's own accounts of refusal, apart from outsiders' labels.
- **The Kikapú of El Nacimiento:** check how their seasonal travel across the border has been kept; sources are scarce, so look to state archives and community spokespeople.
- **Chinese-Mexican descendants in Torreón and the capital:** check responses to the 2021 apology.
- **Comparison only:** Sonora's Yoeme (a long record of armed and legal resistance), Yucatán (the Caste War and its holdouts), Cascadia's Baja California and the CSA's Tejano communities. Where México ends against them is for the author.
