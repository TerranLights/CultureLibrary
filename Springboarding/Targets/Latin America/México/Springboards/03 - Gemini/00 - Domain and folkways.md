## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The Valley of Mexico against Monterrey, Chihuahua and Torreón:** check atlas points and address-form studies so the capital's speech is not taken for the whole.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos de Jalisco:** check parish catechism classes beside state schools as places where children learned to read.
- **Oaxaca's peoples (Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk, Mazatec, Chinantec, Triqui):** check each language's speech genres and bilingual schools separately.
- **Tsotsil, Tseltal and Ch'ol towns:** check language-socialization studies against towns where Spanish is gaining.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital and Mazahua of the State of Mexico:** check what happens to home languages among their children in the capital.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika of the Sierra Madre Occidental:** check boarding schools (*albergues*) and long footpaths as settings of learning and travel.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check oral verse (*chilenas*, *décimas*) as their own record; written studies are few.
- **Deaf communities:** check LSM's regional variation and Deaf clubs in the three largest cities.
- **Mennonites of Chihuahua, and Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican schools:** check which language each uses at home, in prayer and in business.
- **Comparison only, for dialect and language-shift data cut by the edges:** Yucatán (Peninsular Spanish and Yucatec Maya form their own zone), Sonora (Yoeme, Comcaac and O'odham language programs), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's daily crossers; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa revival), the CSA's Tejano Spanish, and the diaspora (Spanish kept or lost by generation). The line between these and México stays with the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial Nahuatl speech texts, Spanish dialectology, school history and INEGI surveys; mixed for living Indigenous speech ways; thin for Kikapú (Coahuila), Afro-Mexican speech and Chinese- and Japanese-Mexican home languages. Which lands México takes in is not yet decided (that is the author's to settle), so the Gemini sheets are written for the core of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the codices and dialogues; Tsotsil and Tseltal for speech genres; Zapotec and Ñuu Savi for Oaxaca's speech and schooling; LSM for Deaf sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Rarámuri, Wixárika and Tsotsil ritual speech is closed to outsiders; slang collections are bawdy; language-rights law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the domain's speech, schooling and neighborhood pointers met no standing rule.
