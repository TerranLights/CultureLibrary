# México: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (chilango, *norteño*, *tapatío* and coastal usage); classical and modern Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Rarámuri; Isthmus Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Lengua de Señas Mexicana. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and ideas of what good communication is and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahuatl towns of the Malinche (Tlaxcala and Puebla) and Milpa Alta:** check where honorific verb forms are still used, by age, to see how respect registers shift with Spanish.
- **Tenejapa and other Tseltal towns against Tsotsil Zinacantán:** check politeness and irony studies side by side before treating "highland Maya speech" as one thing.
- **Monterrey against Guadalajara and the capital:** check PRESEEA recordings of requests and refusals before any claim about regional directness.
- **Rarámuri communities of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check gathering speeches as the setting where public word is given.
- **Isthmus Zapotec (Juchitán, Tehuantepec):** check written Zapotec media and bilingual radio for the places a home language carries public talk.
- **Triqui, Ñuu Savi and Hñähñu migrants in Mexico City, Guadalajara and Monterrey:** check who interprets for them in clinics, schools and offices.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula:** check accounts of misunderstanding across Spanish varieties.
- **Deaf communities:** check LSM use in families with hearing parents against Deaf-family homes and Deaf clubs.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check who speaks Spanish with neighbors and buyers.
- **Comparison only, each for a talk-across-borders reason:** Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana call centers and bilingual service work for U.S. customers), Yucatán (Maya-Spanish contact speech), Sonora (Yoeme interpreters in old records), the CSA's Tejano code-switching, and diaspora children who interpret for parents. Each boundary with México is the author's call.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for recorded Spanish corpora, colonial Nahuatl speech texts and Cantinflas criticism; mixed for most living Indigenous languages' conversation; thin for Kikapú, Afro-Mexican and Chinese-Mexican communication studies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Molina and the *huehuetlahtolli*; Tseltal for Tenejapa; Rarámuri for gathering speech; LSM.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Malintzin is a charged figure (*malinchismo*); some ritual speech is closed to outsiders; interpreter rights in court belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the talk, interpreting and letter-writing pointers met no standing rule.
