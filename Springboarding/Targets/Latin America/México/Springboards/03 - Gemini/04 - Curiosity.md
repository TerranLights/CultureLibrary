# México: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (colloquial, scientific and church registers); classical Nahuatl; Mazatec; Wixárika; Rarámuri; Purépecha. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore; and how far a culture encourages, channels, limits or punishes it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Huautla de Jiménez and the Mazatec Sierra:** check town accounts of visitors since the 1960s, to see how local people answer outsiders' questions.
- **Wixárika communities and Wirikuta (Real de Catorce):** check which sites, rites and objects the Wixárika ask visitors not to seek out.
- **Rarámuri towns of the Copper Canyon:** check tourism records and community statements on photography and questions.
- **Janitzio and Pátzcuaro (Purépecha):** check how the Night of the Dead became a spectacle for visitors, and community responses.
- **Teotihuacan and other towns beside archaeological zones:** check what residents say about excavation and digging into the past.
- **Puebla's Tonantzintla observatory and Monterrey's Planetario Alfa:** check the public programs of science centers outside the capital.
- **Protestant and Evangelical congregations, Catholic parishes and Jewish schools:** check each one's teaching materials for which questions about faith are opened or closed.
- **Comparison only, for research and tourism that cross the edges:** Yucatán (visitors at Chichén Itzá and the cenotes), Sonora (the Comcaac, long hosts of visiting scientists), Cascadia's Bajas (gray-whale lagoons and marine researchers), the CSA's border archaeology, and diaspora families using DNA tests to trace roots. ## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and Enlightenment science, popular-science institutions and national surveys; mixed for Indigenous peoples' own rules on asking; thin for Kikapú and Afro-Mexican sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Latin and classical Nahuatl for the herbal and Hernández; Mazatec for Huautla; Wixárika for Wirikuta statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** peyote and mushroom rites are restricted and their lands disputed; do not seek sacred knowledge withheld by its owners; Inquisition trials name real people; heritage law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the natural-history, Inquisition and visitor pointers met no standing rule.
