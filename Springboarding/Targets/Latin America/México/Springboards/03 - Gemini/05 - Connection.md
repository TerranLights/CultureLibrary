*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (family, church, business and political usage); classical Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi (codex glosses and migrant speech); Ayuuk; Sierra Norte and Isthmus Zapotec; Arabic and Chinese for some community records; Kikapú, if the community offers sources. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected (or not).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Tlaxcala and Puebla:** check how many *compadrazgo* types are still made, using Nutini's list as the base, and which have lapsed.
- **Ayuuk towns (Tlahuitoltepec) and Sierra Norte Zapotec towns (Guelatao):** check community assemblies and labor turns as ties, in their own writers' terms.
- **Sending towns of Jalisco, Michoacán, Zacatecas and Guanajuato:** check MMP data on which kin and friends opened the way north.
- **Monterrey's business families and Mexico City's political circles:** check firm histories and career studies for how ties are used and talked about.
- **Isthmus Zapotec towns (Juchitán):** check festival sponsorship (*velas*) and kin terms as the scaffolding of ties.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish and Chinese-Mexican communities of the capital and Torreón:** check club, school and marriage records as ties kept within and across communities.
- **The Kikapú of El Nacimiento (Coahuila):** check how ties with kin across the border are kept; sources are scarce, so start with INPI and Coahuila archives and ask the community.
- **Elders in large cities:** check living-alone rates and parish or DIF programs for isolated elders.
- **Ñuu Savi and Triqui migrants in Mexico City and the Bajío:** check hometown groups that keep ties to the Mixteca.
- **Comparison only, for ties stretched across the lines:** diaspora hometown federations and the old Tres por Uno matching program, Tijuana families split by the border (Cascadia's Baja California), Sonora's cross-border kin, Yucatán's Maya migrant links to the coast, and the CSA's Tejano families with cousins in Nuevo León. Drawing those lines is the author's task.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for compadrazgo studies, migration networks and elite-family studies; mixed for loneliness data (recent and national); thin for Kikapú, Afro-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican ties.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Ñuu Savi for codex glosses; Ayuuk and Zapotec for *comunalidad* writings; Arabic and Chinese for some community archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parish registers name living families' ancestors; political networks touch on corruption claims (law goes to Libra and Capricorn); migration status is sensitive.
- **Held for review:** none; the kin-network, *compadrazgo* and migrant-tie pointers met no standing rule.
