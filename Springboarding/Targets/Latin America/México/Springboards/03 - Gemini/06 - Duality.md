# México: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (philosophical, popular and border usage); classical Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Isthmus Zapotec; Totonac and Sierra Norte Nahuatl; Ayuuk; Plautdietsch and High German. **Concept in one line:** how a culture lives with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how it treats people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahua and Totonac towns of the Sierra Norte de Puebla:** check hot-cold healing and *nahual* terms town by town.
- **Tseltal Cancuc and Tsotsil towns:** check soul and companion ideas against Pitarch's account before generalizing across the highlands.
- **Ayuuk towns that keep the 260-day count:** check how ritual specialists hold two calendars beside the civil one.
- **Juchitán and the Isthmus Zapotec:** check muxe' organizations' own statements on gender and two-ness.
- **Villages that stage Santiagos or *moros y cristianos* (Puebla, Veracruz, Zacatecas, Michoacán):** check who plays each side and how parts pass down.
- **Ciudad Juárez, Nuevo Laredo, Reynosa and Matamoros:** check daily crossers and two-country families on the Mexican side of the twin cities.
- **Mennonites (Plautdietsch at home, High German in church, Spanish outside), Lebanese-Mexican and Jewish families:** check how each speaks of holding two heritages.
- **Afro-Mexican communities of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check self-identification since 2015 against how people name themselves locally.
- **Comparison only, for two-ness drawn by the borders themselves:** the Texas halves of the twin cities (the CSA's Tejanos), Cascadia's Tijuana and Mexicali with the Cucapá split by the line, Sonora's two Nogales and its O'odham, Yucatán's Maya-Spanish bilingual towns, and the diaspora's *pocho* and *ni de aquí ni de allá* debates. Placing each is left to the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Nahua dualism, mestizaje, casta painting and lucha libre; mixed for living soul and companion beliefs; thin for Kikapú, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican bicultural accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Durán, Garibay and the codex; Tseltal for Cancuc; Isthmus Zapotec for muxe' sources; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** muxe' identity must be described in muxe' terms; *nahual* beliefs can be tied to witchcraft accusations; casta categories are offensive today; Vasconcelos is contested; ritual calendars may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** none; the paired-thought, mixed-identity and mask pointers met no standing rule.
