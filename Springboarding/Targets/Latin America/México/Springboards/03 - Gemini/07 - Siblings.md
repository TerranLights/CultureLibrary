# México: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (family and slang usage, north to south); classical Nahuatl and Nahuatl of Tlaxcala and Puebla; Tsotsil; Ñuu Savi (colonial *tu'un savi*); Isthmus Zapotec; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahua towns of Tlaxcala, Puebla and Morelos:** check whether the youngest son still keeps the house, using Robichaux's cases, and what elder siblings receive instead.
- **Zinacantán and other Tsotsil towns:** check how senior and junior sibling terms carry into ritual office.
- **Ñuu Savi towns of the Mixteca Alta and their migrants:** check which sibling stays home and which goes north.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío against Mexico City:** check ENADID family size by cohort, so large and small families are compared on data.
- **Monterrey's founding families:** check firm histories for brothers as partners and for splits.
- **Isthmus Zapotec households (Juchitán):** check daughters' and sons' roles in caring for parents.
- **Evangelical congregations and Catholic confraternities:** check how "*hermano*" works as a title for non-kin.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check sibling numbers and land division among brothers.
- **Central American migrant children in Chiapas and Tabasco:** check shelter records of siblings who travel together.
- **Afro-Mexican families of the Costa Chica:** check sibling care across migration; sources are few, so begin with Guerrero and Oaxaca regional universities.
- **Comparison only, for siblings split by moving north or across a line:** diaspora brothers who send for the next one (Los Tigres del Norte, Sinaloan brothers based in San José, as a public case), Cascadia's Tijuana families, Sonora's ranch heirs, Yucatán's Maya households and the CSA's Tejano siblings. Which side each falls on is the author's choice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for demographic and aging surveys and for Nahua colonial kinship; mixed for living Indigenous birth-order customs; thin for twins, sibling violence and Kikapú, Lebanese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican sibling ties.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for kin terms and Ñuu Savi for codex glosses; Tsotsil for Zinacantán; Zapotec for the Isthmus.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children in care and violence between siblings; inheritance quarrels, with land succession law going to Libra and Capricorn; migrant minors' identities.
- **Held for review:** none; the kin-term, birth-order and family-size pointers met no standing rule.
