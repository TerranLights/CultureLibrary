*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (church, labor, bureaucratic and family usage); classical Nahuatl; Sierra Norte Zapotec and Valley Zapotec; Tsotsil; Purépecha; Me'phaa and Ñuu Savi for Guerrero's community bodies. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision; and, more broadly, the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Highland Chiapas (Zinacantán, Chamula, Oxchuc):** check who brokered returns and settlements after religious expulsions, and whether it was church, state or kin.
- **Sierra Norte Zapotec towns:** check whether Nader's harmony model holds in other towns or was local.
- **Oaxaca and Mixteca boundary disputes between villages (including the Chimalapas):** check which third parties are called in, and with what result.
- **Purépecha towns with land and water quarrels:** check priests', teachers' and elders' roles as peacemakers.
- **Guerrero's Montaña and Costa Chica (Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi, Nahua and Afro-Mexican towns):** check community bodies that settle disputes by conciliation, as they describe themselves (the legal side goes to Libra).
- **Mexico City's San Judas devotees against parishes of the Bajío:** check which saints are asked to intercede, by whom and for what.
- **Evangelical and Catholic households:** check how each speaks of praying through saints or directly, and who settles family quarrels.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check the colony's own ways of settling disputes and any outside peace-church ties.
- **Central American migrants in Oaxaca and Chiapas:** check shelter staff as their go-betweens.
- **Comparison only, for brokers working across the lines:** Cascadia's Tijuana fixers and border smugglers, Sonora's Yoeme water talks with the state, Yucatán's old Caste War truces, the CSA's Tejano labor contractors, and diaspora hometown clubs that bargain with Mexican officials. The author settles which side each lies on.
