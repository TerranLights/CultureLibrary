*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (village, city, political and entertainment-press usage); classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Puebla Nahuatl; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) is researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Zinacantán against Chamula and Chenalhó:** check whether Haviland's patterns hold in neighboring Tsotsil towns, using Bricker's festival data as a second source.
- **Nahua and mestizo towns of Puebla and Hidalgo:** check local accounts of rumor-driven violence, and what towns did afterward.
- **Small towns of Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío against Mexico City *vecindades* and *tianguis*:** check studies of reputation and talk by town size, before reading *pueblo chico* as a rule.
- **Mexico City's political class:** check rumor columns and their sources over decades.
- **Evangelical converts in Catholic towns:** check how talk about converts runs both ways.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check how each colony speaks of the *Bann* (shunning) and of talk about members who leave.
- **Jewish community schools and synagogues:** check teaching on *lashon hara* and how it is applied.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check *chilenas* and local verses that name people; written sources are few.
- **Teenagers in cities:** check MOCIBA data and school reports on online rumors.
- **Comparison only, for talk carried in verse, song and screens across the lines:** Yucatán's teasing *bombas* at *vaquerías*, Sonora's ballads about real people, Cascadia's Tijuana press, the CSA's Spanish-language Texas radio, and diaspora gossip shows from Miami watched in México. Where these meet México is for the author to fix.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Zinacantán, colonial denunciations, entertainment media and newspaper archives; mixed for everyday gossip outside Chiapas; thin for Kikapú, Lebanese-Mexican and Chinese-Mexican community talk.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Tsotsil for Haviland's texts; classical Nahuatl for the *Anales*; Puebla Nahuatl for local accounts; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynchings and named victims; Inquisition files naming real families; slander and defamation law go to Libra and Capricorn; online harassment involves minors.
- **Held for review:** none; the rumor, reputation and celebrity-press pointers met no standing rule.
