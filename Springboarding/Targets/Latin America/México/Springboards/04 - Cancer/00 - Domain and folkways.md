# México: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (capital, north, Bajío, Jalisco, coasts); classical and Huasteca Nahuatl; Zapotec (*diidxazá* and Valley varieties); Ñuu Savi (*tu'un savi*); Purépecha; Tsotsil; Wixárika; Rarámuri; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** the domain question: what is home, and who is family? Household forms, roots, house-building, family order and the raising of children.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City *vecindades* against Ciudad Nezahualcóyotl's self-built lots:** check kin sharing in rented rooms against owned, growing houses.
- **Nahua and Teenek households of the Huasteca:** check compounds of related houses.
- **Oaxaca's Central Valleys, Isthmus, Mixteca and Sierra Norte:** check Zapotec, Ñuu Savi and Ayuuk household forms apart, since residence after marriage may differ.
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta:** check the *troje* and the house lot as one family's unit.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika of the Sierra Madre Occidental:** check dispersed ranchería homes and seasonal moves, which a census household undercounts.
- **Monterrey and Saltillo against Los Altos de Jalisco:** check family size and weekly gatherings in industrial cities against ranching towns.
- **Afro-Mexican families of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check Aguirre Beltrán's account against today's self-descriptions.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check colony housing in their own Plautdietsch and German sources.
- **Lebanese-, Jewish- and Japanese-Mexican families:** check family associations as separate records.
- **Comparison only, because household tables and house types stop at these edges:** Yucatán (the Maya house lot and its oval house have their own record), Sonora (Yoeme family compounds), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana households split by the line; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa camps), the CSA's Tejano families, and the diaspora in the United States. Where México ends against them is for the author to say.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census households, colonial family history and capital housing; mixed for Indigenous household forms; thin for fathers' own accounts, Kikapú homes in Coahuila and Afro-Mexican family histories. México's exact lands are still for the author to settle; the Cancer sheets are written for the core of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the codex; Zapotec and Ñuu Savi for Oaxaca kin and house words; Purépecha and Wixárika for house and shrine terms; Plautdietsch for colony sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wixárika family shrines are not for outsiders' cataloging; *Five Families* exposed real households; family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing in this domain pass was set aside.
