*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (housing, real-estate and everyday registers); classical Nahuatl for colonial wills; Purépecha; Hñähñu; Ayuuk; Tseltal; Kikapú sources in Spanish and English. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City's *vecindades* and the housing estates:** check renters' and owners' sense of home separately, since tenure may change the answer.
- **Self-built peripheries (Ecatepec, Chalco, Nezahualcóyotl) against INFONAVIT tracts:** check whether a house built by its family is kept longer than one bought finished.
- **Jalisco, Michoacán, Zacatecas and Guanajuato emigrant towns:** check empty remittance houses against those lived in.
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta:** check the *troje* where it still stands, and what replaced it.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital and Ayuuk of the Sierra Norte:** check house words and house-raising work in their own languages.
- **Rarámuri of the barrancas:** check winter and summer dwellings, which a single address misses.
- **Kikapú of Coahuila:** check the seasonal round; sources are few, so begin with the Latorres and Coahuila archives.
- **Displaced Triqui of San Juan Copala and Tsotsil families pushed out of Chamula:** check what "home" means to those living away from it.
- **Comparison only, because these homes and households lie across the edge:** Yucatán (the Maya house lot), Sonora (Yoeme pueblos; Comcaac camps), Cascadia's Bajas (hillside houses of Tijuana; Kumiai home ranches), the CSA (Tejano homes of the lower Rio Grande; the Kikapú round also reaches Texas) and the diaspora (houses back home paid for from the north). Where México's edge falls is the author's to decide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing surveys, Mexico City architecture and remittance housing; mixed for rural house types now; thin for Kikapú, Ch'ol and Triqui home ways in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the wills; Purépecha, Hñähñu and Ayuuk for house terms; English for the Kikapú ethnography.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families displaced by violence; evictions; deeds, *ejido* house lots and tenancy rules go to Libra and Capricorn; violence at home is for Protection.
- **Held for review:** none; no dwelling, house-building or homecoming pointer here met a standing rule.
