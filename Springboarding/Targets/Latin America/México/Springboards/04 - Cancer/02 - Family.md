# México: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (kin and ritual-kin vocabulary); classical Nahuatl (colonial household censuses); Isthmus Zapotec; Tseltal; Rarámuri; Wixárika; Plautdietsch and German for the Mennonite press. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahua towns of Morelos, Tlaxcala and Puebla:** check today's kin terms and households against the 1530s censuses.
- **Guadalajara and Los Altos de Jalisco:** check kin pooling in the city against ranching families' networks.
- **Isthmus Zapotec towns (Juchitán, Tehuantepec):** check whose side of the family organizes gatherings and trade.
- **Tseltal and Tsotsil towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check lineage and the ancestors' place in family decisions.
- **Rarámuri and Wixárika of the Sierra Madre Occidental:** check how far kin reach across scattered ranchos.
- **Nahua and Teenek of the Huasteca:** check who is received at *Xantolo* and who is left out.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua and Durango:** check kin across colonies and countries, using their own press.
- **Jewish families of Mexico City (Ashkenazi and Sephardic) and Lebanese-Mexican families of Puebla and Mexico City:** check family associations as records of kin.
- **Evangelical congregations:** check where *hermano* and church kin stand beside blood kin.
- **LGBT families in Mexico City and Guadalajara:** check ENDISEG figures against personal accounts.
- **Comparison only, because kin networks cross these edges:** Yucatán (Maya surname lineages), Sonora (Yoeme ritual kin of the eight pueblos), Cascadia's Bajas (families split across the line at Tijuana; Kumiai and Paipai kin across the border), the CSA (Tejano border families) and the diaspora (cousins in Chicago or Los Angeles still counted at home). Where México's edge falls is the author's to decide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial Nahua households and family sociology; mixed for ritual kin today; thin for Kikapú, Chinese- and Japanese-Mexican kin ways, and LGBT families outside the big cities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the Morelos censuses; Isthmus Zapotec and Tseltal for kin words; Plautdietsch and German for the colony press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "la familia" as a criminal group's word for itself; paternity and adoption records; Wixárika ritual knowledge; family law (adoption, custody, marriage rules) goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the kin, ritual-kin and family-survey pointers set nothing aside.
