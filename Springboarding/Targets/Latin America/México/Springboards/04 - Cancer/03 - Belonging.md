## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Morelos towns (Tepoztlán and others) and Mexico City's old *pueblos*:** check *originarios* against *avecindados* in fiestas and assemblies.
- **Mazahua, Hñähñu and Triqui migrants in the capital:** check which hometown ties are kept and which are lost.
- **Tsotsil towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check religious membership as a condition of town membership, and the colonies of the expelled.
- **Rarámuri, Wixárika and Purépecha:** check the words each uses for outsiders and what moves someone across the line.
- **Monterrey and the northern cities:** check how the north labels people from the capital, and how newcomers from the south are received.
- **Jalisco, Zacatecas and Michoacán emigrant towns:** check whether the absent still count, and for how long.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check self-naming (*negro*, *afromexicano*, *moreno*) and its regional differences.
- **Jewish, Lebanese-, Chinese- and Japanese-Mexican communities:** check each one's own membership rules and the country's response.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check church membership and the ban as the line of belonging.
- **Comparison only, because the line between insider and outsider is drawn differently across these edges:** Yucatán (*yucateco* regional identity; Maya membership), Sonora (Yoeme pueblo membership; it also saw the 1931 Chinese expulsions), Cascadia's Bajas (*choyeros* against *fuereños*; Kumiai on both sides of the line), the CSA's Tejanos, and the diaspora (the returnee's "neither here nor there"). Where México's edge falls is for the author.
