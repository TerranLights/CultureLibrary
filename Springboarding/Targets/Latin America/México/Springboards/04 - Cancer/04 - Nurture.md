# México: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (parenting and child-care registers); classical Nahuatl; colonial and modern Ñuu Savi and Zapotec; Rarámuri; Purépecha; Tsotsil and Tseltal for highland childhood. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City professionals against Ecatepec and Iztapalapa families:** check who looks after children while parents work: daycare, grandmother, older sibling.
- **Ñuu Savi and Zapotec towns of the Mixteca and Central Valleys:** check grandparent-raised children in towns with many emigrants.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal highland towns:** check how infant care is described in their languages, not only through outside studies.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check children's work and boarding schools as parts of upbringing.
- **Purépecha towns:** check lullabies and children's songs in Purépecha.
- **Nahua of the Huasteca and Puebla's Sierra Norte:** check midwives' and grandmothers' first-weeks care.
- **Monterrey and Saltillo industrial families:** check company and state daycare against kin care.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check who raises children when parents migrate; sources are few, so begin with regional universities in Guerrero and Oaxaca.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check children's farm and household work in colony sources.
- **Comparison only, because child-rearing studies on these lands form their own bodies of work:** Yucatán (studies of Yucatec Maya children), Sonora (Yoeme and O'odham upbringing), Cascadia's Bajas (children of San Quintín farmworker families; Kumiai and Paipai language work with children), the CSA's Tejano families, and the diaspora (children raised in the United States by Mexican parents). Where México stops is the author's to set.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for child surveys, feeding programs and Mexico City welfare history; mixed for Indigenous parenting in its own words; thin for Rarámuri, Ch'ol and Afro-Mexican childhood.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; colonial Mixtec and Zapotec for the dictionaries; classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil, Tseltal, Purépecha and Rarámuri for living terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's health and family data; disputes over daycare closures; physical punishment (law on it goes to Libra and Capricorn; abuse goes to Protection).
- **Held for review:** none; the upbringing, feeding and children's-song pointers met no standing rule.
