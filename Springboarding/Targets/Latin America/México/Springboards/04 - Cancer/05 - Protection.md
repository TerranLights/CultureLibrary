## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tierra Caliente of Michoacán and the Costa Grande of Guerrero:** check armed self-defense, its spread and its splits.
- **Me'phaa and Ñomndaa towns of the Montaña and Costa Chica:** check community police and land defense in their own words.
- **Wixárika communities and Rarámuri forest defenders of the Sierra Madre (the case of Isidro Baldenegro):** check how each guards land and water and the risk run.
- **Mexico City against Monterrey, Guadalajara and Ciudad Juárez:** check household protective steps and women's transit separately by city.
- **Zapotec and Ayuuk towns of the Sierra Norte:** check *topiles* and night rounds as unpaid service.
- **Tsotsil towns:** check amulets and prayers for infants in Tsotsil terms.
- **Mennonite and Mormon colonies of Chihuahua:** check how each community organizes its own safety.
- **Central American migrants in Chiapas and Tabasco:** check who protects people on the route (shelters, priests, families).
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check community safety arrangements; sources are thin, so begin with local organizations.
- **Comparison only, because security, self-defense and land-defense records change across these edges:** Yucatán (its figures stand apart in national crime tables), Sonora (Yoeme guarding of their river; the 2019 killing of a Mormon family near La Mora), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's violence; Kumiai land defense), the CSA (Tejano border towns), and the diaspora (families shielding relatives at risk of deportation). Where México's edge lies is for the author.
