# México: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (charity, disaster and migration registers); Purépecha for the colonial hospitals; Tsotsil and Triqui for the displaced; the Mayan languages of Guatemalan refugees (Mam, Q'anjob'al, Chuj) for the Chiapas camps. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta and Lake Pátzcuaro:** check the *huatápera* and the hospital tradition where it survives.
- **Mexico City:** check people on the street, the CAIS shelters and the Triqui sit-ins as distinct cases.
- **Monterrey and the northern cities:** check squatter settlements and their later regularization.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns of Chenalhó and the highlands:** check displacement camps and returns.
- **Ñuu Savi, Triqui and Me'phaa seasonal workers in Sinaloa:** check camp housing for families with children.
- **Rarámuri in Chihuahua city:** check when and why the urban settlements formed.
- **Guerrero's Costa Grande and Acapulco:** check disaster shelter after Otis.
- **Central American, Haitian and other migrants in Chiapas, Tabasco and Jalisco:** check which shelters each group uses, and why.
- **Spanish Republican descendants and Guatemalan refugees who stayed:** check their own accounts of being received.
- **Comparison only, because the shelter route runs across these edges:** Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's Casa del Migrante; the camps of 2016 and 2018), Sonora (shelters at Nogales), Yucatán (Petén, and the Peninsula, where many 1980s Guatemalan refugees were resettled), the CSA (shelters on the Texas side) and the diaspora (sanctuary congregations in the United States). Where México's edge falls is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migrant shelters, refugee history and colonial charity; mixed for homeless counts; thin for rural homelessness, Kikapú and Afro-Mexican displacement.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Purépecha; Tsotsil and Triqui for the displaced; Mam, Q'anjob'al and Chuj for the refugee camps; Haitian Creole for recent arrivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** women's shelters keep their locations confidential; migrants' and displaced people's identities; asylum and eviction law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the refuge, asylum and disaster-shelter pointers raised no standing rule.
