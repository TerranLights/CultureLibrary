## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City and Guadalajara:** check children's homes, street children and foundling archives in the two cities apart.
- **Zacatecas, Michoacán, Jalisco and Guanajuato emigrant towns:** check the words used for women and children left behind.
- **Ñuu Savi, Zapotec and Triqui towns of the Mixteca:** check grandparent-raised children and how they speak of absent parents.
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta:** check emptied houses and migrants' returns.
- **Chihuahua and Durango:** check towns left by families fleeing violence.
- **Huasteca Nahua and Teenek families:** check how families speak of daughters who leave for work in northern cities.
- **Elders in Veracruz and Oaxaca:** check living alone where children have left.
- **Central American children crossing Chiapas alone:** check shelter and DIF records of unaccompanied minors.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check migration to the United States and the families left; sources are few, so begin with Guerrero and Oaxaca regional universities.
- **Comparison only, because the record of leaving and being left is split by these edges:** Cascadia's Bajas (deportees set down at Tijuana; Kumiai towns losing their young), Sonora (migrants left in the desert by guides), Yucatán (emigration from Maya towns of the Peninsula), the CSA (Tejano families with members deported) and the diaspora (parents in the United States with children at home). Where México's edge falls is for the author.
