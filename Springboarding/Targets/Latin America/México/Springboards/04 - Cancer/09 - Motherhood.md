# México: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (devotional, film, slang and public-health registers); classical and modern Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Isthmus Zapotec; Ñuu Savi and Me'phaa for maternal-health reports. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City and Guadalajara middle-class mothers against mothers in Ecatepec and Iztapalapa:** check paid work and kin help.
- **Isthmus Zapotec towns:** check mothers' place in trade and in fiestas.
- **Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi and Nahua towns of Guerrero's Montaña:** check maternal deaths and access to birth care.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal highland towns:** check *parteras*, clinics and the *Jme'tik* devotion.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos de Jalisco:** check family size and Guadalupan devotion.
- **Nuevo León, Coahuila and Chihuahua:** check mothers in factory work and single mothers' households.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check birth customs and mothers' migration; sources are few, so begin with regional universities.
- **Mothers searching for disappeared children:** check each collective's own statements state by state.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check family size and birth at home in colony sources.
- **Comparison only, because motherhood's records divide at these edges:** Yucatán (the feminist campaigns of the 1910s that, some historians say, Mother's Day answered; Maya birth studies), Sonora (Madres Buscadoras de Sonora; Yoeme mothers), Cascadia's Bajas (mothers deported to Tijuana apart from children born north), the CSA (Tejana mothers) and the diaspora (mothering from a distance). Where México's edge falls is the author's to decide.

## 4. Flags
