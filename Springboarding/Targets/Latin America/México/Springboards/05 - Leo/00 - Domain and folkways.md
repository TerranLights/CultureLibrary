## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City's *sonidero* street dances against its concert halls:** check where music is shown off in the street and where in seats.
- **Isthmus *velas* (Juchitán, Tehuantepec) against the Central Valleys' Guelaguetza:** check who sponsors, who dresses up, and who watches.
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta and Lake Pátzcuaro:** check the Night of the Dead as held for the town and as shown to visitors.
- **Rarámuri, Wixárika (Huichol), Náayeri (Cora) and O'dam of the Sierra Madre Occidental:** check races, Holy Week and dances, and what each people keeps closed.
- **Totonac of Papantla; Nahua of the Huasteca:** check the Voladores and the *huapango* as ritual and as stage.
- **Sinaloa and Nuevo León:** check *banda* and *norteño* dances and Monterrey's football crowds.
- **Bajío and Los Altos de Jalisco:** check *charreadas*, *jaripeos* and *palenques* as the region's showcases.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check the Danza de los Diablos and the *fandango*.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check what pastimes and music each colony's rules admit, in Plautdietsch sources.
- **Lebanese- and Jewish-Mexican clubs in the capital:** check club yearbooks for their celebrations.
- **Comparison only, because the region's best-known shows sit across these edges:** Yucatán (the *vaquería* and *jarana* have their own record), Sonora (Yoeme *pascola* and deer dancers), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's Agua Caliente era; the Baja 1000; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa gatherings), the CSA's Tejano conjunto halls, and lowrider and Chicano art in the United States diaspora. Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fiestas, folk arts and the capital's popular culture; mixed for regional surveys; thin for children's play in small Indigenous communities and for Kikapú and O'dam pastimes. México's exact lands are still for the author to settle; the Leo sheets are written for the core of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the song manuscript; Purépecha for *pirekua* lyrics; Zapotec for *vela* records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Wixárika pilgrimage to Wirikuta; Náayeri and Rarámuri Holy Week, where cameras may be refused; cockfights and bullfights are contested (law on them: Libra, Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none; the fiesta, sport and folk-art pointers needed nothing set aside.
