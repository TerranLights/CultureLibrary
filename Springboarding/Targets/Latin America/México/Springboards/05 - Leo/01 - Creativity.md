## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Purépecha craft towns of the lake and the Meseta:** check whether a town's assigned craft limits who may take up a new one.
- **Central Valleys of Oaxaca (Arrazola, Tilcajete, Teotitlán del Valle, Coyotepec):** check named innovators against family workshops in sales records.
- **Ñuu Savi palm weavers of the Mixteca Alta and Isthmus Zapotec embroiderers:** check whether a new pattern is counted the maker's or the town's.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal weavers of the Chiapas highlands:** check how revived old designs are told apart from new ones.
- **Hñähñu embroiderers of Tenango de Doria and Ayuuk weavers of Tlahuitoltepec:** check the makers' own word for who authored a design.
- **Wixárika (Huichol) beadworkers in Nayarit, Jalisco and city markets:** check work made for offerings against work made to sell.
- **Mata Ortiz and Monterrey's engineering schools:** check a northern craft revival against industrial invention in the same states.
- **Talavera workshops of Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check what makers say about keeping or breaking old patterns.
- **The capital's *cartoneros*; printers and designers among Spanish Republican exiles:** check who brought new forms.
- **Afro-Mexican mask makers of the Costa Chica:** check for any record; it may be oral only.
- **Comparison only, because crafts and claims to invention cross these edges:** Yucatán (hammock and *huipil* work), Sonora (Comcaac ironwood carving sold since the 1960s), Cascadia's Bajas (Paipai pottery; Tijuana's claim to the Caesar salad), the CSA's Tejano makers, and the diaspora's *rasquachismo* (named by Tomás Ybarra-Frausto, 1989). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crafts, folk-art markets and muralism; mixed for invention and patents; thin for everyday *ingenio* outside online photo genres, and for Afro-Mexican and Kikapú making.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Book 10; Zapotec for Teotitlán and Coyotepec makers; Tsotsil for weavers' own accounts; Wixárika for art made for offerings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wixárika images made as offerings are not market art; design ownership disputes are live, and their law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; every craft, mural and invention source here cleared the standing rules.
