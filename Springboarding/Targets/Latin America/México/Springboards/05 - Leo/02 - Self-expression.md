# México: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Juchitán and the Isthmus:** check how muxe' and *vela* dress are shown before the town and before outsiders.
- **Guadalajara against Los Altos de Jalisco:** check how much *el qué dirán* appears in memoirs and interviews from each.
- **Monterrey and Ciudad Juárez:** check northern style (boots, hats, *corridos*) as chosen self-display, and the 1940s *pachuco* record in Juárez.
- **Mexico City's 1980s *chavos banda* and today's youth scenes:** check what the young say their style announces.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check what writers' groups put on stage and what they keep for the town.
- **Rarámuri in Chihuahua city and Wixárika in Guadalajara:** check what dress is kept or changed when leaving the sierra.
- **Ayuuk and Sierra Norte Zapotec towns:** check whether band music is spoken of as personal expression or the town's voice.
- **Afro-Mexican groups of the Costa Chica:** check their self-naming campaigns (*negro*, *afromexicano*) as public speech.
- **Mennonites of Chihuahua and Evangelical congregations in Chiapas:** check rules on dress and music, and young members' own accounts.
- **Chinese-Mexicans of the capital's Calle Dolores:** check public festival display against family custom.
- **Comparison only, because the youth and music scenes run across these edges:** Yucatán (the *trova yucateca* as personal song), Sonora (*corridos tumbados* began with Natanael Cano in Hermosillo), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's Nortec sound and border art), the CSA's Tejano singers, and the *pachuco* and zoot-suit record of the United States diaspora. Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the capital's youth culture, rock, film and literature; mixed for regional voice; thin for how emotion is shown in daily life outside student samples, and for Kikapú and Náayeri accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with old *caló*; classical Nahuatl for the *Huehuetlahtolli*; Isthmus Zapotec for muxe' and poets; Tsotsil and Tseltal for the writers' group.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** muxe' and LGBT people must not be outed by research; Inquisition files name real people; some *corridos* praise cartels; free-speech law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
