# México: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (with northern and Jalisco usage); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Hñähñu; German and Plautdietsch for the Mennonite colonies. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monterrey and Nuevo León against the capital:** check *regio* pride in the press against the *chilango* label as each side uses it.
- **Guadalajara and the rest of Jalisco:** check how the state's tourism claims mariachi, tequila and *charrería* as its own.
- **Purépecha towns hosting the New Year:** check which towns take part and which stay out, and why.
- **Central Valleys and Isthmus of Oaxaca:** check pride in speaking Zapotec among elders and among young migrants to the cities.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Yanga, Veracruz:** check what Yanga's founding festival and the Encuentros celebrate.
- **Sinaloa and Chihuahua:** check how towns honor their boxers, baseball players and *banda* musicians.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital Valley:** check pride in language and in houses built by migrant labor.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check how humility (*Demut*) is preached in German-language sermons, against pride in the farm.
- **Spanish Republican exiles and their children:** check pride narratives in exile memoirs and the institutions they founded.
- **Internal migrants in Mexico City:** check hometown associations that keep a town's patron fiesta in the capital.
- **Comparison only, because regional pride is sharpest at these edges:** Yucatán (a short-lived separate republic of the 1840s feeds its own record), Sonora (*orgullosamente sonorense* as a slogan), Cascadia's Bajas (*tijuanense* pride campaigns; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa language pride), the CSA's Tejano pride, and "brown pride" in the United States diaspora. Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, schooling, sport and song; mixed for regional pride by state; thin for Indigenous words that split pride from arrogance, and for Kikapú and Chinese-Mexican sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Chimalpahin; Purépecha for the New Year; Me'phaa and Ñuu Savi for radio; German and Plautdietsch for sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pride turned against Central American migrants in the south; narco boasting in songs and on social media; *humilde* carries class meaning; flag and anthem rules are law (Libra, Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none; the creole, labor, boxing and radio sources stand without exception.
