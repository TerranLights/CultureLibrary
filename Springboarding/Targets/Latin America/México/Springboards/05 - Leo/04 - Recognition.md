# México: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical and modern Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Ayuuk; Tsotsil; Purépecha; Cantonese and Spanish for Chinese-Mexican sources. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sierra Norte Zapotec and Ayuuk towns:** check how finishing a ladder of *cargos* is marked in assembly records.
- **Tsotsil towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check standing earned by *cargos* against standing from school titles.
- **Professional circles of the capital and Monterrey:** check *licenciado* and *ingeniero* as address at work and outside it.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check census self-identification against organizations' own counts.
- **Chinese-Mexicans of Torreón and the capital:** check what the community said of the 2021 apology.
- **Domestic workers in the cities, internal migrants among them:** check credit and thanks in their union's (SINACTRAHO) statements.
- **Kikapú of El Nacimiento, Coahuila:** check how the community describes being counted by programs; published sources are few, so start with INPI files.
- **Families of the disappeared from Guerrero's Montaña and beyond:** check what acknowledgment they demand and from whom.
- **Sinaloa's and Nuevo León's baseball towns:** check whose players reach halls of fame.
- **Comparison only, because the largest state apologies and claims lie across these edges:** Yucatán (the 2021 apology to the Maya people), Sonora (the 2021 apology to the Yoeme and its follow-up), Cascadia's Bajas (Kumiai and Cucapá efforts to be counted), the CSA's Tejanos (their place in Texas histories), and the diaspora (California's 2005 apology for the 1930s repatriations). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national honors, census items and the capital's monuments; mixed for workplace praise; thin for everyday thanks in Indigenous languages and for Kikapú and Chinese-Mexican community records.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Nahuatl and colonial Spanish for *probanzas*; Ayuuk and Zapotec for community-service writings; Tsotsil for *cargo* records; Cantonese for Torreón's community memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families of the disappeared and Ayotzinapa need care; census categories for Afro-Mexican and Indigenous people are political; constitutional recognition of peoples is law (Libra, Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none; the honors lists, apologies and census items called for no holding back.
