# México: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; Lengua de Señas Mexicana (LSM); classical Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi and Me'phaa for farmworker sources; Japanese for community records. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women commuters in the capital, Guadalajara and Monterrey:** check accounts of unwanted looks on transit and in the street.
- **Juchitán's market women:** check how they speak of outside photographers against local studio portraits.
- **Rarámuri women in Chihuahua city and Monterrey:** check whether they describe being ignored or stared at in city streets.
- **Ñuu Savi, Me'phaa and Nahua farmworkers in Sinaloa's fields:** check visibility in labor counts against their own organizations' records.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check scholarship of the 1940s against community radio and video now.
- **Deaf communities of the capital and Guadalajara:** check LSM-made media for how members want to be shown.
- **Towns of Los Altos and Zacatecas:** check who still walks the plaza *vuelta*, and where it stopped.
- **Mennonite colonies near Cuauhtémoc:** check how members speak of tourists and cameras.
- **Japanese-Mexican families:** check wartime memory against today's associations.
- **Central American migrants crossing Chiapas and on the freight trains:** check how migrants describe keeping out of sight on the route.
- **Comparison only, because who is shown and who hidden differs across these edges:** Yucatán (Maya faces in resort imagery), Sonora (Iturbide's Comcaac photographs, a parallel to Juchitán), Cascadia's Bajas (deportees left at Tijuana's line; Kumiai and Cucapá absent from tourist maps), the CSA's Tejanos (left out of Texas history books), and the diaspora (Mexican Americans in U.S. media counts). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for photography archives, film and media debates; mixed for workers' visibility; thin for everyday gaze and greeting norms by region, and for LSM and Kikapú sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; LSM, with Deaf researchers; Isthmus Zapotec; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi and Me'phaa; Japanese for wartime family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** photographing Indigenous people without consent; migrants in hiding must not be exposed; the families of the disappeared; wartime relocation of Japanese-Mexicans.
- **Held for review:** none; the photograph archives, surveys and documentaries posed no standing-rule question.
