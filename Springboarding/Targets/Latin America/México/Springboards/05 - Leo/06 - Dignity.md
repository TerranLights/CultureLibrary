# México: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Rarámuri; the Spanish of Central American migrants. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Textile towns of Orizaba and Puebla:** check the wording of strike petitions about treatment, not only pay.
- **Zapatista and other Tseltal towns of Chiapas:** check *ich'el ta muk'* in Tseltal speech against *dignidad* in Spanish communiqués.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check *kórima* during droughts and food aid.
- **Central American migrants in Chiapas, Tabasco and the Isthmus of Oaxaca:** check shelters' records and migrants' own words for treatment.
- **Old people in the capital's homes against those left in Mixteca villages by migrant children:** check where people say dignity is lost.
- **Bracero families of Michoacán, Jalisco, Zacatecas and Guanajuato:** check memories of inspection against official reports.
- **Afro-Mexican people of the Costa Chica:** check accounts of being stopped and asked for papers by police and migration officers.
- **Evangelical families expelled from Chamula and other highland towns:** check what the expelled say was taken from them beyond land.
- **Jewish and Lebanese families of the capital:** check memoirs of the 1930s campaigns.
- **Comparison only, because founding cases of dignity at work and on the road fall across these edges:** Yucatán (henequen hacienda workers' memory), Sonora (the 1906 Cananea strike), Cascadia's Bajas (deportees left in Tijuana; Mexicali's Chinese families), the CSA's Tejanos ("No Mexicans" signs as lived), and the diaspora (the farmworkers' union's language of dignity). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, migration and the Chiapas movement; mixed for old age and care; thin for end-of-life customs by region and for Kikapú and Mennonite views.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the Osuna glosses; Tseltal for Chiapas speech; Rarámuri for *kórima*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** migrants in transit; expelled religious minorities; unidentified dead and common graves; dignity as a legal standard and human-rights law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the labor, migration and church sources gave no cause to hold anything.
