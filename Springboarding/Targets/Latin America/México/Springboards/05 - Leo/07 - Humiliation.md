# México: Humiliation (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (northern and capital slang included); classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Hñähñu; Plautdietsch and German. **Concept in one line:** being lowered in standing or worth against one's will, usually before others; also the act of lowering someone.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monterrey and Saltillo workplaces:** check where *carrilla* among workmates turns to bullying, in workers' own words.
- **The capital's barrios:** check whose effigies were burned as Judas over the decades.
- **Veracruz and Mazatlán carnivals:** check each year's *mal humor* figure and the reaction of the person mocked.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital and Nahua of the Huasteca:** check memories of language punishment at school by generation.
- **Taxco and Guerrero's Tierra Caliente:** check penitents' accounts of chosen humbling against outsiders' readings.
- **Students of Chapingo and the rural normal schools:** check *novatada* accounts and the reforms students made.
- **Women in Puebla and the capital:** check how exposure online is described against older gossip and *balconeo*.
- **Tsotsil towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check *k'exlal* in speech and the scolding of wrongdoers at festivals.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check church discipline and shunning in German and Plautdietsch sources.
- **Afro-Mexican pupils of the Costa Chica:** check school accounts of mockery over color and hair.
- **Comparison only, because collective humiliations are remembered from these edges:** Yucatán (Maya captives of the Caste War sold to Cuba), Sonora (Yoeme deported to Yucatán's plantations under Díaz), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's image as a border vice town), the CSA's Tejanos (the Alamo told against them), and the diaspora (the Zoot Suit Riots). Where México ends is for the author.
