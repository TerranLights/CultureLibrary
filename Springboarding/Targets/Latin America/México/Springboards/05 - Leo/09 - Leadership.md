*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (political and union slang); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Ñuu Savi; Zapotec; Rarámuri; German for colony offices. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta and the lake:** check how *tata* is applied to respected leaders now.
- **Assembly towns of Oaxaca (Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Chinantec, Mazatec):** check who may stand for town president, and women's routes.
- **Rarámuri pueblos:** check how a *siríame* is chosen and what followers do when they stop listening.
- **Chihuahua and Durango:** check Villa's memory beside how present leaders are praised.
- **Monterrey's firms:** check company histories for the founder as leader and his heirs.
- **The capital's unions and the teachers' union sections of Oaxaca and Chiapas:** check long-tenure bosses against dissident leaders.
- **Afro-Mexican groups of the Costa Chica:** check whether leaders come from churches, civic groups or town offices.
- **Evangelical congregations of Chiapas:** check pastors as new leaders against cargo elders.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check the bishop (*Ältester*) and colony head (*Vorsteher*) in German records.
- **Student movements (1968; the 1999 UNAM strike):** check council leadership against the refusal of leaders.
- **Comparison only, because several of the nation's best-known leaders rose at these edges:** Yucatán (Felipe Carrillo Puerto), Sonora (Obregón and Calles; the governors of the eight Yoeme pueblos), Cascadia's Bajas (the 1911 Magonista episode; Kumiai community heads), the CSA's Tejanos (Juan Seguín), and the diaspora (César Chávez and Dolores Huerta). Where México ends is for the author.
