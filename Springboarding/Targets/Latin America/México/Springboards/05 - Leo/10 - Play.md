## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ñuu Savi players in Oaxaca and on the capital's edge:** check where *pelota mixteca* leagues keep their records.
- **Purépecha towns of the Meseta:** check when *uárhukua* is played and whether schools have taken it up.
- **Rarámuri pueblos:** check who organizes races and who holds the wagers.
- **Tsotsil towns of the Chiapas highlands:** check what play happens at *K'in Tajimoltik* and who may join.
- **Monterrey and Nuevo León against the capital:** check youth baseball against street football as children's first game.
- **Veracruz port:** check dominoes and plaza dancing as adult play, by age and sex.
- **The capital's barrios:** check *maquinitas* and street play across three generations.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check children's games and rhymes; few collections may exist.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check which games colony schools and churches allow children.
- **Central American migrant children in southern shelters:** check play programs and what children play while waiting.
- **Comparison only, because several long-studied games sit across these edges:** Yucatán (studies of Maya children's play), Sonora (Yoeme *pascola* clowning as ritual play), Cascadia's Bajas (the Kumiai *peón* hand game), the CSA's Tejano *lotería* nights, and the diaspora (*pelota mixteca* leagues in California). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for toys, *lotería*, folklore of games and youth baseball; mixed for digital play; thin for children's play in small Indigenous and Afro-Mexican communities, and for Kikapú and Mennonite play.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Durán's terms; Ñuu Savi and Purépecha for ballgame names; Rarámuri; Tsotsil.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** betting on races and in arcades involves minors; casinos and their fires and crimes; ritual clowning at Holy Week may not be open to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none; the toy, ballgame and survey sources were all admissible.
