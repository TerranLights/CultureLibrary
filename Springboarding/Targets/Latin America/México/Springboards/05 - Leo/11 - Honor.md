# México: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (ranch, arena and family usage); colonial Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Ñomndaa (Amuzgo); German for the colonies. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and its migrant towns:** check courtship and family-honor talk across three generations.
- **Ranching towns of Coahuila, Durango and Chihuahua:** check handshake deals at sales against written contracts.
- **The capital's arenas:** check what crowds shout at *rudos* and *técnicos*, and why.
- **Afro-Mexican and Ñomndaa (Amuzgo) towns of Guerrero's Costa Chica:** check how insults are answered by custom and who settles them; few sources, so start with local histories.
- **Nahua towns of Tlaxcala and Puebla with old noble lines:** check where *don* still marks lineage.
- **Military families and the Colegio Militar:** check honor in service as families tell it.
- **Isthmus Zapotec women:** check how honor at weddings is described in women's own words.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check refusal of oaths and the plain yes and no in German records.
- **Lebanese-Mexican merchants:** check early trade on credit given by word, in community histories.
- **Purépecha towns:** check *pirekua* lyrics for honor in courtship and parting.
- **Comparison only, because some classic honor narratives come from these edges:** Yucatán (planter families' honor in the henequen era), Sonora (Yoeme accounts of their wars against the state), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's boxing and wrestling halls), the CSA's Tejanos (the ballad of Gregorio Cortez), and the diaspora (*caballerismo* studies with Mexican-American men). Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial honor, cinema and song; mixed for present-day surveys; thin for honor concepts in most Indigenous languages, and for Kikapú, Ñomndaa and Afro-Mexican customs.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, colonial and modern; classical Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Ñomndaa; German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** violence against women defended as honor; narco codes of honor in songs and press; "malinchista" used as an insult; defamation law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the colonial, film and ranching sources required no hold.
