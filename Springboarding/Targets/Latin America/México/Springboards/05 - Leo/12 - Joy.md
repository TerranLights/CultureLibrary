# México: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (with Jalisco, northern and Huasteca usage); classical and Huasteca Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi; Purépecha; Teenek; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sinaloa and Durango:** check how mourners describe *banda* at a burial, kept apart from press framing.
- **Nahua and Teenek towns of the Huasteca:** check *Xantolo* as joy and as mourning, in participants' words.
- **Pentecostal and Catholic families of Chiapas:** check how converts set worship joy against fiesta joy.
- **The capital's eastern barrios and Monterrey's hillside *colonias*:** check dance scenes by age and sex.
- **Purépecha lake towns:** check whether the *Viejitos* is danced for the town or for visitors.
- **Guadalajara and the rest of Jalisco:** check mariachi festivals as local or tourist joy.
- **Afro-Mexican and Ñomndaa towns of the Costa Chica:** check *chilenas* at weddings and who plays them.
- **Mennonite colonies:** check hymn singing as joy without dance, in Plautdietsch accounts.
- **Jewish communities of the capital:** check Purim and wedding celebrations in community papers.
- **Rarámuri pueblos:** check what people call a good fiesta, in their own accounts; sources are few.
- **Comparison only, because some of the region's signature celebrations sit across these edges:** Yucatán (the *vaquería* and *jarana*), Sonora (Yoeme fiestas and the *pascola*'s jokes), Cascadia's Bajas (the La Paz and Ensenada carnivals), the CSA's Tejano dance halls, and the diaspora's Independence Day parades. Where México ends is for the author.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music, dance and fiestas; mixed for happiness surveys below national level; thin for words of joy in most Indigenous languages, and for Kikapú and Náayeri celebrations.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for Book 2 and the *Romances*; Huasteca Nahuatl and Teenek for *Xantolo*; Purépecha; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** alcohol at fiestas; *banda* at funerals tied in the press to drug traffickers; tensions between converts and Catholic towns; some feasts of the dead may be closed to cameras.
- **Held for review:** none; the song, survey and feast sources left nothing for review.
