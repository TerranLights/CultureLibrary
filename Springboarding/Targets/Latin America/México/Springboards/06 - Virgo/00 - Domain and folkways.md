## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The capital's office workers against its *tianguis* vendors:** check where each day places the *comida*.
- **The Bajío's plants (Silao, Celaya, Querétaro) against Los Altos de Jalisco's ranches:** check shift work against the herd's round.
- **Monterrey's industrial firms:** check company housing, welfare and sport as an employer's plan for a worker's whole life.
- **Zapotec and Ayuuk towns of Oaxaca's Sierra Norte:** check how *tequio* and cargo years sit beside wages and migration.
- **Ñuu Savi of the Mixteca:** check a year split between planting at home and field work in Sinaloa and Morelos.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal of the Chiapas highlands:** check how cargo years and the coffee harvest share one calendar.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check work parties and the corn beer (*batari*) shared at them.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica and Veracruz's sugar country:** check fishing and field rounds and the memory of plantation labor.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check colony rules for the working week and Sunday rest, in Plautdietsch and German.
- **Lebanese-Mexican families of Puebla, Torreón and the capital:** check firm histories for how the shop day was kept.
- **Internal migrants in the capital and Monterrey:** check how the day changes after the move.
- **Comparison only, since their work rounds belong to other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (henequen-estate labor), Sonora (Yoeme, Comcaac and O'odham seasonal work; Cananea's mines), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's *maquiladoras*; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa livelihoods), the CSA's Tejano farm and ranch labor, and Mexican-American and Chicano workers in the United States.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, labor-force surveys and colonial labor records; mixed for regional daily rounds; thin for the Kikapú of Coahuila and the O'dam of Durango, where ethnographies are few. México's exact lands are still for the author to settle; the Virgo sheets are written for the core of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including regional workplace slang; classical Nahuatl for Book 10's trade vocabulary; Zapotec and Ayuuk for writing on communal labor.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child labor among farm day workers; domestic workers' privacy; healers' knowledge, shared on their own terms. Labor law: Libra (letter), Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; no domain-level pointer on work or the daily round needed setting aside.
