# México: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (capital, north, Bajío, coasts); classical Nahuatl and Huasteca Nahuatl; Purépecha; Me'phaa; Tu'un Savi; Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **The capital's *godínez* and the *albañiles* building its towers (many from Puebla, Tlaxcala and Hidalgo):** check how desk and site workers each speak of hard work.
- **Monterrey's industrial workforce against Guadalajara's tech firms and small workshops:** check paternalist firm traditions against small-shop and startup ones.
- **Me'phaa (Tlapanec), Ñuu Savi and Nahua families of Guerrero's Montaña:** check how migrant field work is told at home and in the fields.
- **Mining towns of Zacatecas, Hidalgo (Real del Monte and Pachuca, with Cornish heritage) and Chihuahua:** check shaft lore and the danger of the mine.
- **Purépecha craft towns (Paracho's guitars, Santa Clara del Cobre's copper):** check a whole town's trade and how it is learned.
- **Ciudad Cooperativa Cruz Azul, Hidalgo:** check how cement-cooperative members describe owning their work.
- **Fishing towns of Alvarado (Veracruz) and Mazatlán (Sinaloa):** check seasonal closures (*veda*) and idle months.
- **Japanese-Mexican descendants of the Enomoto colony (Soconusco, Chiapas):** check family histories of farm and shop work.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula:** check coffee and street work and how hiring happens.
- **Evangelical congregations in Chiapas and the capital's periphery:** check testimonies that link conversion with changes in work and drink.
- **Comparison only (where México ends is the author's call):** Yucatán (Maya workers in the Cancún tourist strip), Sonora (Cananea's mines and Nacozari's railroad hero, both claimed as national exemplars of work), Cascadia's Bajas (the San Quintín farmworkers' strike of 2015), the CSA's Tejano ranch and Rio Grande Valley farm work, and the diaspora's farmworker movement in California.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, unions and maquila studies; mixed for the work ethic as people state it; thin for the Xi'iuy (Pame) of San Luis Potosí and the Kikapú, whose work rounds are little written up.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including trade slang (*talacha*, *godín*); classical Nahuatl for the *Huehuetlahtolli*; Me'phaa and Tu'un Savi for Montaña workers' testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child labor in the fields; deaths at work and the families' groups after the Pasta de Conchos mine disaster (Coahuila, 2006). Labor law and the 40-hour-week debate: Libra (letter), Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; the work pointers needed no setting aside.
