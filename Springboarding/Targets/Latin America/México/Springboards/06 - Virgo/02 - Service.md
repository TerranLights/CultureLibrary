# México: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Tsotsil; Valley and Sierra Zapotec; Ayuuk; Tu'un Savi. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Household workers from Ñuu Savi, Mazahua, Hñähñu and Zapotec towns in the capital, Puebla and Guadalajara:** check what workers and employers each call the job.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal cargo holders in Zinacantán, Chamula and Oxchuc:** check how cargo years are taken up and handed over.
- **Zapotec and Ayuuk towns of the Sierra Norte:** check the *topil*'s duties and what a town owes a returning migrant who skips *tequio*.
- **Purépecha towns with *huatápera* hospital-chapels (Uruapan and the Meseta):** check service rotas kept since the 16th century.
- **Nahua of the Sierra Norte de Puebla (Cuetzalan and the Tosepan cooperative):** check how cooperative duties sit beside *mayordomías*.
- **Resort staff of Acapulco and Puerto Vallarta:** check service talk and tip income in hotel work.
- **Garrison and naval towns (Veracruz; the capital's Campo Militar 1):** check military families' accounts.
- **Catholic sisters' congregations founded in Mexico:** check their rules for nursing and teaching.
- **Evangelical congregations:** check rotas for ushering, cooking and visiting the sick.
- **The capital's Jewish community welfare bodies:** check how mutual service is organized and named.
- **Comparison only, outside México's core (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán (Maya staff of the Riviera Maya hotels), Sonora (Yoeme Lenten societies, whose members serve under vows), Cascadia's Bajas (Mother Antonia's prison service in Tijuana's La Mesa), the CSA's Tejano *mutualistas*, and Mexican domestic workers in the United States diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the cargo system and domestic work; mixed for military and volunteer service; thin for Triqui and Chinantec community offices (look in Oaxaca's state archive and CIESAS theses).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, especially the courtesy formulas and their class use; Tsotsil for cargo terms; Purépecha for the *Relación*'s office names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse of live-in workers and their privacy; ritual duties some cargo holders keep closed; domestic-work law and the 2019 reform are for Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** the life of a South Asian-born enslaved servant in colonial Puebla and the books about her (South Asian category), set aside under the standing rule.
