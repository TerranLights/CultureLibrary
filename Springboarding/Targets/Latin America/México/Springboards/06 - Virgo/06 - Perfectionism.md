# México: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Valley Zapotec; Totonac; Ñomndaa; Purépecha. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lacquerers of Olinalá (Guerrero) and Purépecha *maque* makers of Uruapan and Pátzcuaro:** check what a flawless finish means to each workshop.
- **Ñomndaa (Amuzgo) backstrap weavers of Xochistlahuaca:** check how weavers judge a *huipil*'s brocade and who teaches the standard.
- **Mata Ortiz potters:** check how families speak of copies and of their own masterpieces.
- **Wixárika (Huichol) bead and yarn artists:** check, as artists share it, what counts as a correct design.
- **Bajío plant workers:** check how "zero defects" at the plant sits with work at home.
- **The capital's students facing the UNAM and IPN entrance exams:** check accounts of exam pressure and family expectations.
- **Mennonite cheesemakers of Cuauhtémoc, Chihuahua:** check the colony's standards for *queso menonita*.
- **Totonac Voladores of Papantla:** check what follows an error in the rite.
- **Catholic contemplative communities:** check rules of exact observance and counsel on scrupulosity.
- **Comparison only, outside México's core (a boundary for the author):** Yucatán (the *hipil* and hammock makers' own quality talk), Sonora (Comcaac ironwood carvers and machine-made copies), Cascadia's Bajas (the cleanrooms of Tijuana's and Mexicali's medical-device plants), the CSA's Tejano *quinceañera* trade, and diaspora studies of school pressure among Mexican-American students.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for craft standards and manufacturing quality; mixed for clinical perfectionism; thin for Ñomndaa and Triqui weaving standards, best reached through community cooperatives and museum catalogs.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, including *ahí se va* and *chambonada* in regional use; classical Nahuatl for Book 9's craft terms; Zapotec for Teotitlán weavers; Totonac for the rite's teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eating disorders and suicide among students; designs and rite details some makers keep closed; copied Indigenous designs and the disputes over them.
- **Held for review:** none; the craft and quality pointers needed no setting aside.
