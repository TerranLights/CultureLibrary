## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahua of the Sierra Norte de Puebla (Cuetzalan):** check how healers and the town hospital share patients.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal of the Chiapas highlands:** check how the healers' association OMIECH and its museum in San Cristóbal present their medicine.
- **Mazatec of the Sierra Mazateca:** check, only through Mazatec practitioners' own accounts, how healing nights are described.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check when *owirúame* are sought and when clinics are.
- **Afro-Mexican Cuajinicuilapa (Costa Chica):** check healing and *susto* in Aguirre Beltrán's *Cuijla* (1958) and in local voices since.
- **Purépecha of the Meseta:** check healers and the herbs sold in Paracho and Uruapan markets.
- **Monterrey's private hospitals:** check how foreign medical patients are courted.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check colony bone-setters and use of Mexican clinics.
- **Pentecostal congregations:** check divine-healing services and testimony.
- **Internal migrants in the capital's periphery:** check *yerbería* use, including the herb stalls of the capital's Mercado de Sonora.
- **Comparison only, outside México's core (the author settles the line):** Yucatán (Maya healers and bone-setters of the peninsula), Sonora (Teresa Urrea, the healer of Cabora, claimed on both sides of the border), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's alternative-cancer clinics and Los Algodones's dentists), the CSA's Tejano shrine to the healer Don Pedrito Jaramillo, and the diaspora's "Hispanic health paradox" studies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk illness, public health and national surveys; mixed for mental health; thin and partly restricted for Kikapú and O'dam healing.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the herbal's plant names; Tsotsil and Tseltal for healers' terms; Zapotec and Chinantec for the *susto* villages.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mazatec mushroom rites and Wixárika peyote, closed or shared only on their keepers' terms; patients' privacy; healers' paid knowledge. Health law and drug policy are for Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-origin health practices offered in Mexican cities and retreats (South Asian category); Buddhist meditation centers (category; an open question for the author).
