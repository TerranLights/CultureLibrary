# México: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (penal and constitutional register; street speech); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Me'phaa and Ñuu Savi for Guerrero's community courts. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered. Libra takes the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Guerrero's Montaña and Costa Chica (Me'phaa, Ñuu Savi, Ñomndaa, Afro-Mexican towns):** check how the CRAC-PC's re-education stands in state law against the federal penal code.
- **Michoacán:** check Cherán's Purépecha council against the Tierra Caliente *autodefensas*: which gained legal form, which were disbanded.
- **Oaxaca's Sierra Norte and Mixteca:** check town *alcaldes* who judge minor wrongs, and where cases pass to state courts.
- **Chiapas highlands:** check the Tsotsil and Tseltal *juzgados de paz y conciliación indígena* against the Zapatista *Juntas de Buen Gobierno*.
- **Chihuahua and Ciudad Juárez:** check an early oral-trial state's files and the cases of murdered women.
- **The capital (Iztapalapa, the *Reclusorios*):** check courtroom galleries and public defenders' caseloads.
- **Wixárika of Jalisco and Nayarit:** check how claims over Wirikuta were argued in amparo suits.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula:** check detention rules and access to defenders.
- **Afro-Mexican peoples:** check how the 2019 constitutional recognition is cited in court.
- **Comparison only, since their courts, community judges and search collectives fall under other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Maya community judges in Quintana Roo), Sonora (Yoeme authorities; the Sonoran mothers' search collectives), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's courts; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano ballads of men pursued by Texas lawmen, and the diaspora before US immigration courts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, rulings and victimization surveys; mixed for Indigenous jurisdictions; thin for community courts' own files, rarely public.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish penal and amparo register; Tsotsil and Tseltal for highland hearings; Me'phaa and Ñuu Savi for CRAC-PC sessions; classical Nahuatl and Purépecha for the colonial accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disappearances and the Ayotzinapa case; lynching videos; minors' files; victims' names. What people make of the law beyond its wording goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no justice pointer needed setting aside.
