# México: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (the capital's slang, the north's and the Bajío's speech, market and sports talk); classical Nahuatl; Mazahua (*jñatjo*); Chinantec and Mazatec; Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mazahua of the State of Mexico and Michoacán:** check water allotments from the Cutzamala works against the towns' own turns.
- **Chinantec and Mazatec of the Papaloapan basin:** check accounts of resettlement and payment in their own languages.
- **Ejidos of the Bajío and Los Altos de Jalisco:** check the one-heir lists against how siblings divided land informally.
- **Ex-braceros of Zacatecas, Michoacán and Jalisco:** check claims to the wartime savings withheld from their wages and the later payment fund.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check footrace (*rarajípari*) wagers and who rules a race fair.
- **Chinese-Mexican families of Torreón:** check the property claims and indemnity talks after the 1911 killings.
- **Ñuu Savi and Triqui day laborers in Sinaloa and Morelos fields:** check piece-rate pay and how crews divide it.
- **Iztapalapa and Ciudad Nezahualcóyotl:** check how water-truck turns are kept among neighbors.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula:** check how asylum appointment turns are given out.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check whether ENADIS samples are large enough to read the region.
- **Comparison only, because each fair-share fight sits on lands of other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (henequen *ejido* shares after 1937), Sonora (the Yoeme's water dispute over the Yaqui River), Cascadia's Bajas (the San Quintín farmworkers' 2015 strike; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano farmworkers' wage strikes, and wage claims of the diaspora in the United States.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for discrimination, mobility and consumer data; mixed for children's choosing games; thin for Mazatec- and Chinantec-language relocation accounts and for *ulama* rulings.
- **Languages needed:** the capital's and the north's slang for cheating and grabbing; Mazahua and Chinantec for water and resettlement testimony; classical Nahuatl for testaments.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** group discrimination data (read as reports of treatment, never as traits); the Torreón killings; migrants' case details.
- **Held for review:** none; the fair-share, wage and playground pointers met no standing rule.
