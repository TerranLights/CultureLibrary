# México: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (couples' and business speech, the north's and the capital's slang); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Ñuu Savi; Tsotsil and Tseltal. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Monterrey's business houses and Puebla's Lebanese-Mexican firms:** check how partnership deeds split shares among brothers, sons-in-law and outside partners.
- **Nahua and Totonac members of Cuetzalan's cooperatives:** check how risks and returns are divided and who may leave.
- **Cooperative workers of Hidalgo and the capital:** check how co-owners settled quarrels inside and outside the courts.
- **Fishing crews of Sinaloa and Nayarit:** check catch shares against debts to buyers.
- **Migrant towns of Zacatecas, Michoacán and Jalisco:** check couples kept across the border and how remittances are managed between them.
- **Isthmus Zapotec of Juchitán and Tehuantepec:** check how spouses split stall, workshop and fiesta duties, from local sources.
- **Purépecha of the Meseta and Lake Pátzcuaro:** check *pirekua* lyrics as a record of courtship speech.
- **Ñuu Savi and Me'phaa of Guerrero's Montaña:** check how families agree to a union and how the 2019 ban on marriage under 18 is applied there.
- **Same-sex couples in the capital, Guadalajara and Monterrey:** check registration counts after each state's reform.
- **Japanese-Mexican families of the capital and Guadalajara:** check marriages arranged with Japan before 1941 and after.
- **Comparison only, because their couples and partnerships are recorded under other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the *trova yucateca*'s love songs), Sonora (Yoeme marriage sponsors), Cascadia's Bajas (Baja California Sur's lobster and abalone cooperatives; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano couples' dance halls, and binational couples in the United States.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, census data, songs and telenovelas; mixed for cooperatives' internal records; thin for Indigenous-language accounts of couples and for Japanese-Mexican marriages.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish business and legal register; Purépecha for *pirekua*; Ñuu Savi for codex couples; classical Nahuatl for Book 6; Tseltal and Tsotsil for highland marriage talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** marriages of minors; same-sex couples' privacy where reforms came late; family-firm quarrels before the courts.
- **Held for review:** none; the couple, cooperative and song pointers met no standing rule.
