# México: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (proverbs, accounting and constitutional register); classical Nahuatl and the Nahuatl of the Huasteca and Puebla's Sierra Norte; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Hñähñu; Rarámuri; Hach Winik (Lacandón Maya). **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nahua of the Huasteca and Puebla's Sierra Norte:** check healers' hot-cold classes in their own words against sixteenth-century sources.
- **Tseltal and Tsotsil of the highlands:** check *lekil kuxlejal* in church and Zapatista writing against everyday speech.
- **Hñähñu of Tenango de Doria and the Mezquital:** check how embroiderers explain symmetry and its breaks.
- **Hach Winik of the Lacandón forest and their Tseltal and Ch'ol neighbors:** check rival accounts of farming against reserve rules.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check healers' accounts of illness as souls out of place.
- **Chinampa families of Xochimilco and Tláhuac:** check how they describe the canals' upset by the city's wells.
- **Chinese-Mexican herbalists of the capital's Calle Dolores:** check what is sold as cooling or warming.
- **Adventist congregations of Chiapas and Tabasco:** check how a Saturday rest is kept against weekly market days.
- **Office workers in the capital and plant workers in Monterrey:** check rest taken after the 2023 vacation reform.
- **Comparison only, as each balance tradition here belongs to another culture-nation (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Chichén Itzá's equinox shadow; Maya hot-cold healing), Sonora (Yoeme deer songs of the wilderness world), Cascadia's Bajas (the Gulf of California in Steinbeck and Ricketts's *Sea of Cortez*; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano *curanderismo*, and folk-medicine surveys of the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional, labor and diet sources; mixed for healers' classes, recorded unevenly by town; thin for Hach Winik and Rarámuri accounts in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl and Huasteca Nahuatl for hot-cold terms; Tseltal for *lekil kuxlejal*; Hñähñu for embroiderers; Rarámuri for healers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healers' knowledge is shared on their terms; land disputes in the Lacandón forest are contested between peoples.
- **Held for review:** none; the healing, craft and constitutional pointers met no standing rule.
