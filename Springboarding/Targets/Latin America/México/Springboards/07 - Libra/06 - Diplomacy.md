# México: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (everyday courtesy, political and foreign-service registers); classical Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Plautdietsch and German for the Mennonite colonies. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tlaxcalan descendants of Saltillo and the northern towns of 1591:** check petitions defending their privileges over two centuries.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua and Durango:** check delegations sent to the capital whenever schooling or exemptions were questioned.
- **Kikapú of El Nacimiento, Coahuila:** check the 1850s settlement terms and their dealings with two governments; sources are few.
- **Yanga, Veracruz:** check the terms the maroon settlement won from the viceroy, as colonial papers record them.
- **Isthmus Zapotec of Juchitán:** check COCEI talks with state and federal governments in the 1980s.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal of the highlands:** check how the CONAI's mediation is remembered locally.
- **Jewish community bodies of the capital:** check their role in easing the 1975 tourism boycott after Mexico's UN vote.
- **Descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check the refusal to recognize Franco until 1977 as seen from the exile community.
- **The Guatemalan border at Tapachula and Ciudad Hidalgo:** check local agreements on crossing cards and day trade.
- **Monterrey's business chambers:** check their own accounts of negotiating with federal governments.
- **Comparison only, because their treaty and border talks belong to other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the Chan Santa Cruz Maya's dealings with British Honduras), Sonora (the Yoeme peace of Ortiz, 1897), Cascadia's Bajas (Friendship Park on the Tijuana line; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai and Kiliwa cross-border visits), the CSA's Tejano go-betweens of the Texas revolution, and the consular network serving the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foreign-ministry archives, treaties and memoirs; mixed for everyday politeness, studied mostly among students in a few cities; thin for Kikapú and Tlaxcalan colonists' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish diplomatic and courtesy registers; classical Nahuatl for the *Coloquios*; Plautdietsch and German for Mennonite delegations; Isthmus Zapotec for COCEI sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** talks with armed groups; the Kikapú's dual status across the border; archives of recent negotiations may be closed.
- **Held for review:** none; the treaty, mediation and politeness pointers met no standing rule.
