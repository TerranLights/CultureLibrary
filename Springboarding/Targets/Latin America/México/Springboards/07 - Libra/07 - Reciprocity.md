# México: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (household, market and church speech); classical Nahuatl; Valley Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Purépecha; Tsotsil and Tseltal. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Purépecha of Tzintzuntzan, Janitzio and Lake Pátzcuaro:** check Foster's paired ties against later studies, and offerings exchanged between houses at the Night of the Dead.
- **Ñuu Savi of Nuyoo and the Mixteca Alta:** check Monaghan's account against what townspeople themselves publish.
- **Valley Zapotec families:** check gift notebooks: how long a debt runs and what happens to it at a death.
- **Puebla Mixteca towns with members in New York:** check who decides how money sent for fiestas and works is spent.
- **Pilgrims of Los Altos de Jalisco and Zacatecas (Plateros):** check vows paid and the votive gifts left.
- **Amatlán and other towns on the Veracruz rail line:** check how giving to migrants is talked about locally.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check the orphans' office (*Waisenamt*) and church aid as mutual insurance.
- **Lebanese-Mexican and Jewish bodies of the capital and Puebla:** check loan funds and aid kept inside each community.
- **Afro-Mexican towns of the Costa Chica:** check contributions at funerals and *fandangos*, from local groups' own accounts.
- **Tsotsil and Tseltal of the highlands:** check how a gift of cane liquor (*pox*) opens a request and binds the one who accepts.
- **Wixárika of Jalisco and Nayarit:** check offerings carried to sacred places, only as the Wixárika publish them.
- **Comparison only, as each exchange system here belongs to another culture-nation (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the *hanal pixan* food for the dead), Sonora (Yoeme *pahko* fiestas sponsored and returned), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's donation-run migrant shelters; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano *mutualistas* of San Antonio, and hometown associations' matching funds in the diaspora.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for household transfers, devotions and hometown ties; mixed for private gift notebooks; thin for Wixárika and Afro-Mexican accounts in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl for Book 2; Valley Zapotec for gift notebooks; Ñuu Savi for Nuyoo; Purépecha for Janitzio; Tsotsil and Tseltal for request rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Wixárika offerings and sacred places; migrants' identities at Las Patronas; family debts recorded in private notebooks.
- **Held for review:** none; the gift, vow and mutual-aid pointers met no standing rule.
