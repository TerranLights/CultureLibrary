# México: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (family sayings, penal-procedure register, crime reporting); classical Nahuatl; Rincón (Sierra Norte) Zapotec; Tsotsil; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rincón Zapotec of Talea and the Sierra Norte:** check Nader's harmony thesis against later studies of the same courts.
- **Tsotsil of San Juan Chamula:** check how the expulsions were justified as keeping the peace, in both sides' documents.
- **Cristero country (Los Altos de Jalisco, Michoacán, Zacatecas, Colima):** check family memories of the 1929 settlement and of the fighters killed after laying down arms.
- **Sinaloa and Tamaulipas:** check how local press and families describe silence under armed groups.
- **Businesses of Guerrero, Michoacán and Guanajuato:** check ENVE extortion reports by state and sector.
- **Railway, oil and teachers' unions:** check rank-and-file accounts of imposed leaders.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check how nonresistance teaching is kept under pressure from armed groups.
- **Women's collectives of the capital and Ciudad Juárez:** check slogans and testimonies that answer *calladita*.
- **Families of the capital and Monterrey tied to the Legion's schools:** check how the long silence was explained after 2006.
- **Japanese-Mexican families moved to the capital and Guadalajara:** check community records of compliance and its costs.
- **Central American migrants in transit:** check CNDH reports on abuse left unreported.
- **Comparison only, as each case of peace bought at a price here belongs to another culture-nation (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the Caste War's 1901 surrender and the Maya who withdrew to the forest), Sonora (the Yoeme deportations after the 1897 peace), Cascadia's Bajas (San Quintín's post-strike pacts; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejano accommodation under segregation, and the diaspora's silence for fear of deportation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for victimization surveys, Cristero history and crime journalism; mixed for union and church archives; thin for expelled Chamula families' accounts and Mennonite records.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish crime-reporting and penal registers; Tsotsil for Chamula; Rincón Zapotec for Talea; classical Nahuatl for Book 12; Plautdietsch.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse survivors; journalists threatened or killed; expelled families; extortion victims. The spirit of the law (why people settle or obey) goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the settlement, silence and extortion pointers met no standing rule.
