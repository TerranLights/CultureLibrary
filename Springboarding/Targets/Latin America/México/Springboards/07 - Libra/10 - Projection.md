# México: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (proverbs, political insults, Inquisition records); classical Nahuatl and Huasteca Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Purépecha. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Huasteca Nahua of Veracruz, Hidalgo and San Luis Potosí:** check how envy and sorcery talk enter quarrels over land and luck.
- **Tsotsil of the highlands:** check *ak' chamel* accusations and the violence that has sometimes followed them.
- **Purépecha and mestizo townspeople of Tzintzuntzan:** check Foster's model against later restudies.
- **Morelos towns (Tepoztlán, Chiconcuac):** check the rival portraits against the towns' own records.
- **The capital's Judas-makers:** check which figures are burned each year and who buys them.
- **Jewish communities of the capital and Monterrey:** check how the Carvajal story is told in community histories.
- **Chinese-Mexican families of Torreón, Mazatlán and the capital:** check anti-Chinese league propaganda in their states.
- **Foreigners expelled under Art. 33:** check whom it was used against, decade by decade.
- **Party supporters in the capital, Jalisco and Nuevo León:** check how *chairo* and *fifí* spread in social-media studies.
- **AA members in *Cuarto y Quinto Paso* groups:** check how the inventory is practiced and spoken about.
- **Central American migrants in Chiapas:** check how local press frames crime and migrants.
- **Comparison only, as each image of an "other" here was made on lands of other culture-nations (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Mérida's press on the "rebel Maya" of the Caste War), Sonora (the 1931 expulsions of the Chinese led from Sonora), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's 2018 protests against the migrant caravan; Kumiai, Cucapá, Paipai, Kiliwa), the CSA's Tejanos cast as "bandits" in 1915, and returnees of the 1930s repatriations from the United States.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Inquisition records, ethnographies of envy and sorcery, and essays; mixed for current political blame; thin for Chinese-Mexican communities' own records.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** sorcery accusations can lead to violence; anti-Chinese killings; living politicians burned in effigy; national-character essays are claims, never findings.
