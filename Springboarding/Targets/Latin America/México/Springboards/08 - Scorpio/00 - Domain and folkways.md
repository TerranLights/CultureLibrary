*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (everyday, devotional and street registers of the capital, the north, the Bajío, Jalisco and the coasts); classical and modern Nahuatl; Purépecha; Isthmus and Valley Zapotec; Ñuu Savi; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** the domain question: how does a culture face death, power and what is hidden? ## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Capital, Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check hospital and funeral-home death against home wakes in the capital's *pueblos originarios*.
- **Lake Pátzcuaro (Janitzio, Tzintzuntzan):** check how the Purépecha souls' night sits beside the tourism built on it.
- **The Huasteca:** check Nahua and Teenek *Xantolo* as its own cycle for the dead.
- **Oaxaca:** check inheritance in communal-land towns against private-plot towns; check muxe' standing in Juchitán.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío:** check parish records of pious bequests.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental and northern cities:** check Rarámuri and O'dam death feasts against Monterrey's funeral trade.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica:** check funeral songs and the *cabo de año*.
- **Mennonite, Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican families:** check their own burial grounds and inheritance customs.
- **Evangelical converts in Chiapas and the cities:** check what a convert drops of the *ofrenda* and wake.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (*Hanal Pixán*, a separate cycle of the dead), Sonora (Yoeme funerary rites), Cascadia's Bajas (Kumiai and Cucapá mourning), CSA Tejanos and the US diaspora (bodies sent home): set beside, never merged.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Day of the Dead, mortality and household surveys; mixed for inheritance outside notaries; thin for Kikapú, O'dam and Chinantec death ways. México's lands are not yet settled by the author; these sheets are written for the core of pre-war Mexico.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for registry and Inquisition files; classical Nahuatl for the Florentine appendix; Purépecha and Isthmus Zapotec for November rites and muxe' self-description.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the disappeared; human remains in museums; names in Inquisition files; living people's sexual identity; rites for the dead closed to outsiders by their keepers.
