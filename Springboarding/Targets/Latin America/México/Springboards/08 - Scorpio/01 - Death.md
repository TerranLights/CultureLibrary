*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tepito and the capital's Santa Muerte shrines:** check how devotees and the Catholic hierarchy each describe the figure.
- **Culiacán, Sinaloa and Durango:** check whether the tomb-and-*banda* funeral for a violent death spreads to other kinds of death.
- **Tlaxcala and Puebla villages:** check Nutini's categories of the dead against today's household *ofrendas*.
- **Tseltal and Tsotsil highlands:** check soul loss as illness that edges toward death.
- **Rarámuri and O'dam of the Sierra Madre Occidental:** check the fiestas held to send the dead's souls away, as their keepers share them.
- **Isthmus Zapotec towns:** check *Xandu'* and the newly dead's first year.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica:** check how a child's wake and an adult's differ.
- **Mennonite colonies near Cuauhtémoc:** check burial and mourning in Low German church records.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Pomuch, Campeche, where bones are cleaned yearly), Sonora (Yoeme deathbed and novena), Cascadia's Bajas (Cucapá mourning songs), CSA Tejanos, and diaspora families who repatriate the body: each frames a death practice to set beside México's.
