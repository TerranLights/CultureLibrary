# México: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (Catholic, evangelical and recovery-house registers); classical Nahuatl; Wixárika; Purépecha; Isthmus Zapotec; Tsotsil. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **San Juan Chamula and the Chiapas highlands:** check how converts and their expelled families each tell a conversion as rupture.
- **Iztapalapa:** check the Passion and storefront Pentecostal churches in one district.
- **Wixárika communities of Jalisco and Nayarit:** check what pilgrims share of being made new, and what they keep back.
- **Rarámuri and Náayeri (Cora):** check Holy Week as renewal, in each people's own terms.
- **Purépecha of Nuevo San Juan:** check rebuilding and the community forest that followed.
- **Juchitán's writers:** check language revival in print and on radio.
- **Monterrey, Guadalajara and Ciudad Juárez:** check recovery houses and AA groups by city.
- **Old Colony Mennonites of Chihuahua:** check how leaving or rejoining the colony is described.
- **Central American migrants in Tapachula's shelters:** check the "fresh start" language of church-run refuges.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Maya Pentecostal congregations), Sonora (the Yoeme Easter cycle), Cascadia's Bajas (Kumiai and Paipai efforts to revive languages with few speakers), CSA Tejano Pentecostals, and deportees starting over: each sets a rebirth path beside México's, not inside it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious switching and Holy Week; mixed for recovery (*anexos* are informal and lightly recorded); thin for Náayeri and Chinantec renewal rites.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse and forced confinement in *anexos*; expelled converts; Wixárika pilgrimage knowledge closed to outsiders; former armed-group members' safety.
