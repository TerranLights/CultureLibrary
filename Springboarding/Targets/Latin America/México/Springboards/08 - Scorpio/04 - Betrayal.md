## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tlaxcala:** check how Tlaxcalans today tell the alliance against the national charge of treachery.
- **Morelos (Anenecuilco, Chinameca):** check local memory of Zapata's killer and his reward.
- **Migrant-sending towns of Jalisco and Michoacán:** check how long absences and rumors of affairs are handled by kin.
- **Rail and oil towns (Aguascalientes, Poza Rica, Ciudad Madero):** check "charro" as an insult among workers.
- **Purépecha of Cherán:** check how the 2011 uprising named officials who sided with loggers.
- **Ayuuk and Zapotec towns under *usos y costumbres*:** check sanctions for members who side with outside parties.
- **Sinaloa and Durango:** check how corridos name an informer.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica:** check the local corrido repertoire for betrayal stories.
- **Descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check how families split by the Civil War's sides tell it.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Caste War alliances), Sonora (Yoeme treaties and deportations), Cascadia's Bajas (the 1911 Magonista rising, later branded filibustering), CSA Tejanos (Juan Seguín, called a traitor by both sides) and the diaspora (*vendido* and *pocho* charges; Chicana rereadings of Malintzin): each shows a contested loyalty to set beside México's.
