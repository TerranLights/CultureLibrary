## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ciudad Juárez and other maquiladora cities:** check how plants govern workers' hours and bodies, from workers' own accounts.
- **Hñähñu of the Mezquital Valley:** check who controls the capital's wastewater used to irrigate their fields.
- **Mazatec and Chinantec of the Papaloapan:** check family memory of resettlement by the dams.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala carnival towns:** check who sets the limits of carnival license.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco:** check how a daughter's courtship is watched, by daughters' own telling.
- **Rarámuri of the Sierra Tarahumara:** check how drinking gatherings set their own limits, as participants describe them.
- **Mennonite colonies of Chihuahua:** check church rules on vehicles, phones and schooling.
- **Evangelical households in Chiapas and Tabasco:** check teaching on a husband's headship.
- **Central American migrants crossing the south:** check control by smugglers and checkpoints.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (henequen-estate debt peonage), Sonora (Yoeme water from the Río Yaqui), Cascadia's Bajas (Mexicali's share of the Colorado), CSA Tejanos (Rio Grande water) and the diaspora (the Bracero Program): each tests control of labor or water beside México's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for partner control (ENDIREH) and water history; mixed for parental control; thin for Kikapú and Triqui households.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish psychology register for Díaz-Guerrero and La Rosa; Mazatec and Chinantec for resettlement memory; Hñähñu for the Mezquital; classical Nahuatl for the Mendoza place glyphs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence and its survivors; people targeted by spyware; maquila workers' health; migrants' safety.
- **Held for review:** none; neither the household-purse leads nor the dam and spyware leads touched a rule that would hold them.
