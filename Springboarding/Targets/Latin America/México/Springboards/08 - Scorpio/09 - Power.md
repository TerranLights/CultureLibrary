# México: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (political, business and street registers); classical Nahuatl; Purépecha; Ñuu Savi; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Ayuuk and Zapotec. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will; and how a culture judges who holds it, how it is held and how it is used.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City's political class against state capitals:** check how a governor's power is spoken of by residents.
- **San Luis Potosí and the Huasteca:** check local memory of the Santos cacicazgo.
- **Ayuuk, Zapotec and Ñuu Savi assembly towns:** check who may speak and hold office.
- **Zapatista communities of Chiapas:** check how "ruling by obeying" works, in members' own reports.
- **Volcano towns of Puebla and Morelos:** check *graniceros*' standing among neighbors.
- **Monterrey's industrial groups:** check business associations' sway over state politics.
- **Sinaloa and Durango:** check how residents describe armed groups beside municipal rule (fieldwork caution).
- **Mennonite colonies:** check dealings with officials over land and water.
- **Afro-Mexican organizations of the Costa Chica:** check how recognition was pressed.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the henequen planters' elite), Sonora (the Sonoran presidents of the 1920s), Cascadia's Bajas (the first opposition governorship, 1989), CSA Tejanos (South Texas political bosses) and diaspora hometown clubs (sway over home politics): each tests where power sits beside México's.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the PRI era, caciques and elites; mixed for assembly power and spiritual power; thin for Kikapú and Chinantec leadership.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl for Book 6; Tsotsil and Tseltal for Zapatista community voices; Zapotec and Ayuuk for assembly minutes; Purépecha and Ñuu Savi for rulers' titles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** armed groups' territorial power; living politicians; *graniceros*' knowledge as they choose to share it.
- **Held for review:** authority as legitimate power and the legal creed of no re-election, set aside and pointed to Capricorn and Libra.
