## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City and the Valley of Mexico:** check "no religion" and Pentecostal shares in Iztapalapa and Nezahualcóyotl against old parish-feast neighborhoods.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío (Guanajuato, Querétaro):** check what Cristero memory and seminary density did to practice.
- **Chiapas (Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol, Tojolabal):** check high non-Catholic shares against the *cargo* system and the expulsions of converts.
- **Oaxaca (Central Valleys, Isthmus, Mixteca, Sierra Norte):** check Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk (Mixe), Mazatec and Chinantec feast calendars town by town.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check how Rarámuri, Wixárika (Huichol) and O'dam ceremonies sit beside Jesuit and Franciscan layers.
- **Northern states (Nuevo León, Chihuahua, Coahuila, Tamaulipas):** check Protestant growth in Monterrey; take Old Colony Mennonite churches and Kikapú ceremonies as their own units.
- **Veracruz, the Huasteca and Puebla's Sierra Norte:** check Totonac and Nahua ritual specialists against parish Catholicism.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica (Guerrero, Oaxaca) and Veracruz:** check church records and brotherhoods for their own histories.
- **Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican families; Spanish Republican exiles' descendants:** check congregations, schools and freethought circles separately.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (its own Maya religious history), Sonora (Yoeme and O'odham ceremonial life), Cascadia's Bajas (Kumiai and Cucapá, mission history), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (hometown saints carried north): check them to see where México's religious map ends, not to fill it.
