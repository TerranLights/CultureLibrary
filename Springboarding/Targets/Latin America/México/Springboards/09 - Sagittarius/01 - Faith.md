# México: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (devotional, Pentecostal and everyday registers); classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Isthmus Zapotec; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tepeyac and the capital:** check how capital residents speak of faith in Guadalupe against pilgrims from Puebla, Tlaxcala and the State of Mexico.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and Michoacán:** check whether Cristero martyrs (Toribio Romo among them) shape vows and family faith differently there.
- **Highland Chiapas (Tsotsil, Tseltal, Ch'ol):** check traditionalist, Catholic catechist and Presbyterian or Pentecostal households in the same municipality.
- **Oaxaca's Isthmus and Sierra Norte:** check Zapotec and Ayuuk (Mixe) *mayordomías* as the setting where faith is shown and paid for.
- **The Sierra Tarahumara and Wixárika lands:** check what Rarámuri and Wixárika (Huichol) speakers trust in, as they choose to share it.
- **Monterrey, Chihuahua and the north:** check evangelical congregations and Old Colony Mennonite churches, where faith is tied to language and schooling.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check saints' feasts and brotherhoods; print sources are thin.
- **Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican communities (Maronite and other Eastern churches):** check how faith is passed on in minority schools and parishes.
- **Central American migrants in the south:** check faith along the route (shrines, shelters run by churches) as a unit of its own.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the Cruzob "talking cross" tradition), Sonora (Yoeme Lenten ceremonies), Cascadia's Bajas (mission-era Catholicism among the Kumiai), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (vows paid by returning migrants): compare how faith is kept away from home, without folding them in.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Guadalupe, ex-votos and Pentecostal conversion; mixed for secular faith in institutions; thin for Afro-Mexican, Kikapú and Triqui faith in print.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl for the 1649 text and the *Psalmodia*; Tsotsil and Tseltal for highland faith in believers' words; Spanish for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** converts and Catholics expelled from their villages; healing claims by named living people; Rarámuri and Wixárika ceremonies not open to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on trust in the unseen was set aside here.
