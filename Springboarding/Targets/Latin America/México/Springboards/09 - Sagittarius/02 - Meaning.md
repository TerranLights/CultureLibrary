# México: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (everyday, self-help and song registers); classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Wixárika; Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City and Monterrey:** check whether work, career and *superación* language dominate meaning-talk in office districts more than in Iztapalapa or San Bernabé.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and the Bajío:** check calling and vocation in seminary-heavy towns against towns emptied by migration.
- **Oaxaca's Mixteca and Central Valleys:** check whether Ñuu Savi and Zapotec speakers place a life's point in community office (*tequio*, *cargo*).
- **Highland Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal sources, Arias among them, against outsiders' ethnography.
- **The Sierra Tarahumara and Wixárika country:** check Rarámuri leaders' speeches and Wixárika ceremonial duty as two distinct sources of purpose.
- **Northern states and the Gulf (Chihuahua, Tamaulipas, Veracruz):** check suicide and addiction figures by sex and age before drawing any regional contrast.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica:** check life histories and *corridos* for how a life is valued; print is scarce.
- **Mennonites of Chihuahua and descendants of Spanish Republican exiles:** check calling in a farming church against meaning in exile memoirs.
- **Internal migrants in the cities:** check how leaving the village is told as purpose or loss.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Maya speakers' own life histories), Sonora (Yoeme ceremonial service), Cascadia's Bajas (factory workers in Tijuana), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (work abroad "for the family"): compare where a life's purpose is located, without merging them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for well-being surveys and literature; mixed in Indigenous languages; thin in print for Kikapú, Triqui and Afro-Mexican sources.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl for the orations and *Cantares*; Tsotsil, Wixárika and Rarámuri for meaning in speakers' own terms; Spanish for surveys and press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data at small-area level; addiction; Wixárika ceremonial knowledge not for outsiders; living families named in death notices.
- **Held for review:** wellness retreats teaching a body discipline of South Asian origin, held as a category; no other pointer on purpose was set aside.
