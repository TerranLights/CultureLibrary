# México: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (political, press and everyday registers); classical Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Plautdietsch for the Mennonite colonies. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check student and press freedom claims from 1968 onward against the national picture, since the capital's sources dominate.
- **Morelos and Puebla:** check *Tierra y Libertad* memory in Zapatista villages of 1910 against later land and water quarrels.
- **Highland Chiapas and the Lacandon:** check Zapatista communities, traditionalist municipalities and expelled converts, whose claims to freedom collide.
- **Oaxaca's Sierra Norte, Isthmus and Mixteca:** check how Zapotec, Ayuuk (Mixe) and Ñuu Savi towns speak of self-rule beside individual freedoms.
- **Guerrero's Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check Afro-Mexican memory of slavery and maroon freedom (Yanga), and Me'phaa and Ñomndaa community police as a freedom claim.
- **Chihuahua and Durango:** check Old Colony and other Mennonite colonies' freedom of school and faith against Rarámuri land claims nearby.
- **Coahuila:** check the Kikapú's cross-border movement as freedom of movement on their own terms.
- **Nuevo León and Jalisco:** check business and Catholic civic groups' meanings of freedom (enterprise, education, worship) against the capital's liberal tradition.
- **Spanish Republican exiles and Jewish refugees:** check freedom as the reason for arrival in memoirs and community archives.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the Caste War as a war of liberation, told by Maya and by Yucatecans), Sonora (Yoeme resistance), Cascadia's Bajas (the 1911 Magonista rising in Mexicali and Tijuana), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (freedom as the stated reason to cross north): compare what freedom was fought for, without merging them.
