*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (slang, press and academic registers); classical Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tojolabal; Ñuu Savi for codex records. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check whose truth the capital's press and the *mañanera* set for the country, against regional papers.
- **Guerrero (Iguala, Ayotzinapa, the Costa Chica):** check the families' and teacher-training students' accounts against official ones.
- **Sinaloa, Tamaulipas and Chihuahua:** check self-censorship and the silence of local papers where reporters were killed.
- **Puebla, Tlaxcala and the Valley of Mexico:** check *títulos primordiales* towns' own histories against parish and viceregal records.
- **Highland Chiapas (Tojolabal, Tseltal, Tsotsil):** check community assemblies as the place where a fact is settled, in their own words.
- **Oaxaca's Mixteca:** check Ñuu Savi codex genealogies and their modern readings as a truth record in their own right.
- **Monterrey and the northern universities:** check trust in science and engineering against religious sources of truth.
- **Evangelical and Catholic communities:** check scripture and the magisterium as authorities beside the press.
- **Mennonites of Chihuahua and Lebanese-Mexican families:** check what sources of news and authority each community reads in its own language.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Maya *Chilam Balam* books as truth records), Sonora (disputed accounts of Yoeme deportations), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's weekly *Zeta* and its murdered editors), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (Spanish-language news consumed abroad): compare who is believed, without merging them.
