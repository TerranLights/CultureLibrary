# México: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (everyday, catechetical and Gulf registers); classical and Huasteca Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Hñähñu (Otomí); Popoluca and Totonac for the Gulf. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City (Tepito, the old center):** check Santa Muerte devotion and street legends as beliefs held by neighborhood, not one city voice.
- **Guadalajara and Los Altos:** check Catholic and evangelical believers in one metropolitan area, with Fortuny's volume as a start.
- **Veracruz, the Huasteca and the Tuxtlas:** check Nahua, Popoluca and Totonac beliefs in *chaneques* and sorcery against tourist versions.
- **Hidalgo and Puebla's Sierra (Hñähñu and Nahua):** check paper-figure rites as belief in their keepers' terms.
- **Highland Chiapas:** check Zinacantec and Chamula belief, then Presbyterian converts in the same towns.
- **Guerrero and Morelos:** check present-day belief where Ruiz de Alarcón recorded spells.
- **Monterrey and Nuevo León:** check the share with no religion and the belief survey items for the north's largest city.
- **Afro-Mexican Costa Chica:** check beliefs about the *tona* and animal companions, where recorded.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses, Mormons and Mennonites:** check how each community's doctrine meets school and civic ritual.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (*aluxes* and other Maya beliefs), Sonora (Yoeme belief in the *huya aniya*, the wilderness world), Cascadia's Bajas (Kumiai beliefs as recorded), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (*La Llorona* tales retold north of the border): compare how a belief travels and changes, without merging them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial records, folklore and survey belief items; mixed for evangelical belief; thin for Kikapú, Triqui and Chinantec belief in print.
- **Languages needed:** classical Nahuatl for Book 5 and Ruiz de Alarcón; Tsotsil for Zinacantán; Hñähñu for Galinier's terms; Spanish for the rest.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** accusations of sorcery can endanger living people; Indigenous ritual not for outsiders; children named in school-conflict records.
