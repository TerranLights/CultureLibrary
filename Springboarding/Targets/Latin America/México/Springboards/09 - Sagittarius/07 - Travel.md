## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Michoacán, Jalisco, Guanajuato and Zacatecas:** check how long migration to the US runs in families, and how the return is celebrated in hometown fiestas.
- **Oaxaca and Guerrero (Ñuu Savi, Triqui, Zapotec, Me'phaa):** check seasonal farmworker routes north against migration abroad.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check Wixárika pilgrimage routes and Rarámuri seasonal labor in Chihuahua's cities and Sinaloa's fields, each on its own terms.
- **Mexico City, Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check walking pilgrimages to Tepeyac and Chalma from parish groups.
- **Chiapas, Tabasco and the southern border:** check the transit of Central American and other migrants, and how towns along the rail lines receive them.
- **Veracruz and the Gulf ports:** check arrivals by sea (Spanish Republican exiles, Lebanese and Jewish immigrants) in port archives.
- **Chihuahua and Coahuila:** check the Mennonite arrival of 1922 and the Kikapú's movement across the border as two different travel histories.
- **Nuevo León and the northern cities:** check internal migrants from the south and the trips home they make.
- **The Guerrero and Oaxaca coasts:** check tourism's effect on Acapulco, Huatulco and Puerto Escondido workers.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (Cancún and Riviera Maya tourism), Sonora (the desert crossing and the Yoeme's movement between Sonora and Arizona), Cascadia's Bajas (Tijuana's crossing and the caravans of 2018), the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (the December drive south): compare the journey's ends, without merging them.
