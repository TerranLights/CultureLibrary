# México: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Wixárika; Rarámuri; Purépecha; Plautdietsch and High German. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check how residents of Iztapalapa and of Coyoacán describe police and officials, since trust in each authority varies by borough.
- **Morelos and the Valley of Mexico's Nahua towns:** check Haskett's colonial councils against today's *ayudantías* and *mayordomos*.
- **Guadalajara and Los Altos:** check church authority in a Catholic heartland beside La Luz del Mundo's own seat.
- **Oaxaca:** check whether Zapotec, Ayuuk and Ñuu Savi assemblies or municipal presidents are obeyed first, town by town.
- **Highland Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal *principales* against party bosses and Zapatista councils.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check how Rarámuri *siríame*, Wixárika *tatuwani* and Náayeri (Cora) governors are chosen and heeded.
- **Coahuila and Chihuahua:** check Kikapú leaders and the Old Colony *Ältester* as authorities beside the Mexican state.
- **Guerrero's Montaña and Costa Chica:** check Me'phaa, Ñomndaa (Amuzgo) and Afro-Mexican towns where community police compete with the state's.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal congregations; Jewish community bodies:** check how pastors and community councils hold authority.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Sonora's Yoeme pueblos (governors outside the municipal ladder), Yucatán's Maya towns, Cascadia's Kumiai authorities, and the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (consulates as Mexican authority abroad): check them to see where obedience runs to other offices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the state, parties and the Church; mixed for household authority; thin for Kikapú, Náayeri and Triqui offices.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Nahuatl for colonial town records; Wixárika and Rarámuri for governors' speech; Tsotsil for *principales*; Plautdietsch for colony leadership.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the La Luz del Mundo abuse case; closed ceremonies that install Native authorities; prisoners' data.
