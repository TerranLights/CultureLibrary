# México: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Valley and Sierra Zapotec; Hñähñu; Purépecha; Wixárika; Ayuuk. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Oaxaca (Sierra Norte, Mixteca, Central Valleys, Isthmus):** check how Zapotec, Ayuuk, Ñuu Savi and Chinantec towns count *tequio* owed by absent migrants.
- **Hidalgo's Mezquital and Querétaro's Hñähñu towns:** check fines and paid substitutes for missed *faenas*.
- **Michoacán:** check Purépecha feast offices around Lake Pátzcuaro and in the Meseta against the duties of the *ronda* in Cherán.
- **Los Altos de Jalisco and Zacatecas:** check the son's duty to send money against the duty to stay and work the land.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check Wixárika temple offices and Rarámuri work parties as owed labor.
- **Veracruz and the Costa Chica:** check militia service and later brotherhood duties in Afro-Mexican towns.
- **Monterrey and the industrial north:** check the company paternalism that framed workers' duties to firm and family.
- **Old Colony Mennonites of Chihuahua; Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican families:** check community duties (schooling, charity, marriage) that run beside the state's.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Sonora's Yoeme Lenten societies (vows kept for years), Yucatán's Maya *fajina* labor and Mérida's guild feasts, Cascadia's Bajas (Oaxacan migrants who still owe *tequio* at home), and the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora (hometown clubs' collections): check them to see where an owed duty is carried across a border.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for civic rites and care surveys; mixed for cargo duties (many village studies, little national data); thin for Chinantec and Náayeri obligations.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Nahuatl glosses on the tribute register; Zapotec and Ayuuk for *tequio* minutes; Wixárika for temple offices; Hñähñu for *faena* rolls.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** fines and expulsions for unpaid communal duty; temple offices whose rites are closed; care data on named households.
- **Held for review:** none; no duty pointer was held under the standing rules.
