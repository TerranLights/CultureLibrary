# México: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (legal, bureaucratic and street registers); colonial Spanish; classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Capricorn takes the spirit of the law; Libra holds its letter.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check how residents of Tepito and of Santa Fe each deal with *trámites*, fixers and police.
- **Oaxaca:** check how Zapotec, Ayuuk, Ñuu Savi and Chinantec assemblies and the state courts each describe a just ruling.
- **Highland Chiapas:** check the Native peace courts against Zapatista justice and against state judges.
- **Michoacán's Meseta and Tierra Caliente:** check Purépecha communal rule in Cherán beside towns that look to the federal state.
- **Guerrero's Montaña and Costa Chica:** check Me'phaa, Na Savi and Afro-Mexican views of community justice and the state's.
- **Chihuahua:** check Old Colony Mennonite, Rarámuri and mestizo farmers on the same water law.
- **Monterrey, Guadalajara and the Bajío:** check business owners on contracts, courts and paying to get things done.
- **Sinaloa and Durango:** check how the law is spoken of where state and criminal rules overlap, with care for safety.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Yucatán (the 1841 constitution where the *amparo* began), Sonora (Yoeme water claims against an aqueduct), Cascadia's Bajas (where the "culture of lawfulness" school program was first tried), and the CSA's Tejanos and the US diaspora ("notario" fraud built on two meanings of one office): check them to see where the spirit of the law is read differently.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional history, surveys and legal scholarship; mixed for law in daily practice; thin for Chinantec, Na Savi and Kikapú legal orders in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; colonial Spanish and Nahuatl for court petitions; Tsotsil and Tseltal for the peace courts; Plautdietsch for colony statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native legal knowledge a community keeps to itself; open cases; water conflicts; witnesses in regions under armed pressure.
- **Held for review:** none; no law pointer was set aside.
