# México: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (neighborhood, parish and civic registers; regional speech apart); classical and modern Nahuatl; Zapotec (*diidxazá* and other varieties); Ayuuk (Mixe); Rarámuri; Tseltal; Purépecha; Hñähñu (Otomí); Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what do they hope for? Friends, groups, hopes and humanitarian ideals, with settlement, migration, association and hospitality as social ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check *vecindades*, *colonias populares* and gated developments as three settings that bring different people together.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check barrio fiesta systems of Nahua towns against unions and parish groups in the mill towns.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check ranchero settlement and Catholic associations against city life in León and Guadalajara.
- **Michoacán:** check Purépecha communal assemblies against mestizo market towns.
- **Oaxaca (Valleys, Isthmus, Mixteca, Sierra Norte):** check *tequio*, *guelaguetza* exchange and the *velas* among Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk, Chinantec, Mazatec and Triqui towns.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil, Tseltal and Ch'ol communities, Zapatista towns and settlements of expelled converts.
- **Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check whether Afro-Mexican towns and Nahua, Me'phaa, Ñomndaa and Totonac neighbors share fiestas or keep them apart.
- **The Sierra Madre and the north:** check Rarámuri and Wixárika ceremonial centers, Monterrey's company neighborhoods, Mennonite *campos* and the Kikapú of El Nacimiento.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican communities; exiles' descendants; Central American migrants; Protestant congregations; internal migrants:** check each one's clubs and its welcome or refusal on arrival.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán's Maya towns, Sonora's Yoeme pueblos, Cascadia's Tijuana and Mexicali, the CSA's Tejano barrios and the diaspora's hometown clubs: check how a community holds together across a border.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for civic surveys, the Revolution and earthquake organizing; mixed for hospitality; thin in print for Kikapú, Triqui and Chinantec community life. Which lands México holds is for the author to settle; the Aquarius sheets cover the central and southern core of the old Mexico and its northern states.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for town-state terms; Isthmus Zapotec for fiesta societies; Rarámuri and Tseltal for sharing and good-life terms; Plautdietsch for colony records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** closed ceremonies at Wixárika and Rarámuri centers; exiles' and migrants' files; expelled converts still at risk.
- **Held for review:** none; every source on settlement, association and the asylum tradition named here passed the standing rules.
