# México: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (barrio, parish and *ejido* speech); classical Nahuatl; Ñuu Savi (*tu'un savi*); Zapotec; Ayuuk (Mixe); Purépecha; Tsotsil; Ñomndaa (Amuzgo). **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check Centro *vecindades*, *colonias populares* of Iztapalapa and Nezahualcóyotl, and closed *privadas*: how membership is earned in each.
- **Morelos and Puebla:** check Nahua fiesta and water committees against weekend-house newcomers.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check the parish-centered town against new industrial suburbs of León and Querétaro.
- **Michoacán:** check Cherán and other Purépecha assemblies against towns where cartels or parties broke communal rule.
- **Oaxaca:** check *tequio* and assembly duties in Ayuuk, Zapotec, Chinantec, Mazatec and Triqui towns, including members who return from abroad for a *cargo*.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal towns, Zapatista communities and expelled converts' colonias for rival definitions of who belongs.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check Ñomndaa, Me'phaa and Afro-Mexican towns, including community police as an expression of membership.
- **Veracruz and the Huasteca:** check Totonac and Nahua *mano vuelta* against oil and port towns.
- **The north:** check Monterrey's company colonias, Mennonite colonies near Cuauhtémoc and Kikapú El Nacimiento, each with its own entry rules.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish and Japanese-Mexican communities; Evangelical congregations; Central American migrants in Tapachula:** check their own clubs, schools and shelters.
- **Comparison only (where the line falls is the author's call):** Cascadia's San Quintín valley and Tijuana, where Oaxacan migrants carry *tequio* into Baja California; Yucatán's Maya towns; Sonora's Yoeme pueblos; Tejano barrios in the CSA; and Oaxacan hometown federations in Los Angeles: check how a community's duties survive moving out of México's lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for community studies, the earthquakes and *comunalidad*; mixed for urban neighboring; thin in print for Kikapú and Triqui life (try local radio and theses).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for wills; Ñuu Savi for colonial records; Purépecha, Ayuuk and Ñomndaa for communal statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** towns under criminal pressure; community police; expelled families. Communal statutes as legal texts belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; no study of towns, assemblies or neighbors had to be set aside.
