# México: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (street, stadium, union and party slang); classical Nahuatl and Sierra Norte de Puebla Nahuatl; Rarámuri; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Wixárika; Isthmus Zapotec; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" ends and "them" begins, and what members are expected to do together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check *barras* of América and Pumas against neighborhood *bandas* and union locals as three ways of being one of the gang.
- **Jalisco and Los Altos:** check Cristero memory, Chivas loyalty and Guadalajara's youth groups for what holding together is said to require.
- **Puebla and the Sierra Norte:** check how Nahua and Totonac speakers draw the line between *masehualmej* and outsiders.
- **Oaxaca:** check *velas* and trade groups of the Isthmus against Ayuuk and Triqui factions in town politics.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal uses of *kaxlan*, and splits between Zapatista, party-aligned and converted families.
- **Guerrero and Michoacán:** check self-defense groups, community police and armed groups as rival claims to speak for "us".
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check who serves together at Wixárika ceremonial centers and Rarámuri pueblos, apart from the outsider words.
- **Chihuahua's Mennonite colonies:** check membership and the church ban as the cost of leaving.
- **Veracruz and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican groups' choice of a collective name and who joined it.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish and Chinese-Mexican clubs; Evangelical churches; Central American migrants:** check how each sets its edges.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Cascadia's Tijuana and Mexicali and the diaspora's *clicas*, where Sureño and Norteño lines run across the border; Sonora's Yoeme and the Caste War groups of Yucatán, as peoples who fought as one; the CSA's Tejanos: check where group lines formed outside México's lands.
