*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (political, press, church and recovery registers); Nahuatl; Isthmus Zapotec; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Plautdietsch. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check the Zócalo *plantones* and reform marches against neighborhood committees that resisted urban renewal.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check Liberal and conservative camps of the Reforma era in these towns.
- **Los Altos and the Bajío:** check how anticlerical reform was received where the Cristero War broke out.
- **Michoacán:** check Cárdenas's home state for land-reform memory in Purépecha and mestizo towns.
- **Oaxaca:** check the teachers' movement of 2006 and the Isthmus COCEI against Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk and Triqui towns' own reform of their customs.
- **Chiapas:** check *indigenismo*'s programs among Tsotsil and Tseltal people against Zapatista self-reform.
- **Guerrero and Veracruz:** check rural normal schools, Afro-Mexican and Me'phaa organizing, and Totonac responses to reform.
- **Monterrey and the north:** check COPARMEX's business reformism, Mennonite colony splits over vehicles and schooling, and Kikapú dealings with land programs.
- **Exiles' descendants, Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican families, Evangelical congregations:** check reforms each sponsored or opposed.
- **Comparison only (the author decides the line):** Yucatán (Carrillo Puerto's socialist reforms of the 1920s), Sonora (the Sonoran presidents behind the anticlerical laws), Cascadia's Bajas (the 1911 Magonista rising) and the CSA's Tejanos (San Antonio, where Madero issued his 1910 plan): check how reforms came into México's core from these lands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Reforma, the 1930s and the political reforms; mixed for reform of persons; thin for Kikapú and Chinantec views of state programs.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; Isthmus Zapotec for COCEI; Tsotsil and Tseltal for responses to *indigenismo*; Plautdietsch for colony splits.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse in *anexos*; teachers killed or jailed; activists under threat. The laws themselves (Reform Laws, electoral laws) go to Libra and their spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; reform sources touching law were redirected to Libra and Capricorn rather than held.
