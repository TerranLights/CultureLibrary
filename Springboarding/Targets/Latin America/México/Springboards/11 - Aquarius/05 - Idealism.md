# México: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (political, church, student and literary registers); classical Nahuatl; Tseltal and Tsotsil; Wixárika; Purépecha (for Quiroga's towns). **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check the 1968 and 1971 student generations against later NGO and civil-society idealists.
- **Morelos:** check Cuernavaca's church base communities and foreign volunteers against village memory of Zapata's ideals.
- **The Bajío:** check how Guanajuato and Querétaro claim Hidalgo's and the Quixote's ideals in civic ritual.
- **Michoacán:** check what Purépecha towns founded by Quiroga keep of his plan.
- **Oaxaca and Chiapas:** check Zapotec, Ayuuk, Tsotsil and Tseltal visions of good living against outsiders' romantic images of them.
- **Guerrero:** check the rural normal school at Ayotzinapa and the Me'phaa and Afro-Mexican communities nearby for what students were taught to live for.
- **Sinaloa and the north:** check Topolobampo's memory, Monterrey's Catholic business idealism and the Mennonites' ideal of a separate life.
- **The Sierra Madre Occidental:** check Wixárika and Rarámuri statements of their ideals against those of outside supporters.
- **Spanish Republican exiles' descendants and Jewish leftists:** check the ideals they brought and how their children judged them.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Cascadia's Valle de Guadalupe (a Molokan religious colony from 1905), Yucatán (the Maya rebels' Speaking Cross), Sonora (Yoeme visions of their homeland), the CSA's Tejanos (the 1915 Plan de San Diego) and the diaspora (*El Plan Espiritual de Aztlán*, 1969): check visions of a better order born at México's edges.
