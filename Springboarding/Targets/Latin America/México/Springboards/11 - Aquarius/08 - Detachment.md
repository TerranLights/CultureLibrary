# México: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (everyday, devotional and philosophical registers); classical Nahuatl (song and moral speech); Rarámuri; Tsotsil and Tseltal; Plautdietsch and High German for Mennonite teaching. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms (calm, objectivity, equanimity) are prized and others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal) condemned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check anonymous metro crowds and *relajo* in offices against neighborhood warmth, as residents describe each.
- **The Bajío:** check retreat houses like Atotonilco and the penitents who use them against secular professionals' detachment.
- **Jalisco:** check Rulfo's *llano* and Los Altos families for how grief and affection are shown or held back.
- **Oaxaca:** check Zapotec, Ñuu Savi and Mazatec mourning customs, and what each permits mourners to show.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal teachings on composure and the heart against mestizo neighbors'.
- **The Sierra Tarahumara:** check whether Rarámuri accounts describe a custom of leaving others to their own choices, and how outside observers have read it.
- **Monterrey and the north:** check business and engineering cultures' ideals of cool competence.
- **Veracruz and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican and Nahua funeral music and wakes for how open grief is.
- **Chihuahua's Mennonites and Coahuila's Kikapú:** check whether distance from neighbors is described as chosen or imposed, and by whom.
- **Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican families; exiles' descendants:** check mourning customs and what children were taught to show.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Cascadia's Baja and Sonora's Yoeme lands, where Jesuit missions also taught Ignatian detachment; Yucatán's Maya towns, the CSA's Tejanos and the diaspora, for how display rules shift across a line (as people there describe them).
