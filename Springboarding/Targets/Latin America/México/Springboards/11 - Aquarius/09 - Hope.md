# México: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (devotional, migrant and everyday registers; rural almanac speech); classical Nahuatl (the Guadalupe text) and Guerrero Nahuatl; Purépecha; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check San Judas pilgrims at San Hipólito against the December 12 crowds at the Basilica of Guadalupe.
- **Jalisco and Los Altos:** check migrant towns' saints and ex-votos against urban Guadalajara's hopes.
- **Michoacán:** check the Purépecha New Fire against Catholic year-turn customs in mestizo towns.
- **Guerrero:** check Nahua rain petitions, Me'phaa towns and searching families in Iguala and Chilpancingo.
- **Oaxaca:** check Ñuu Savi, Zapotec and Triqui migrant towns' hopes sent north and the fiestas remittances pay for.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal hopes voiced in Zapatista and Catholic catechist circles, and Central American migrants in Tapachula.
- **Veracruz and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican and Totonac year-turn customs and petitions.
- **The north:** check Rarámuri *yúmari*, Sinaloa's Jesús Malverde chapel in Culiacán (a folk saint, not recognized by the Church), and Mennonite colonies' hopes for land.
- **Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish and Japanese-Mexican families; exiles' descendants:** check the hopes each migration carried, as told to children.
- **Comparison only (the line is the author's):** Cascadia's Tijuana (the Juan Soldado shrine), Sonora's Yoeme Easter ceremonies, Yucatán's Maya rain rites, the CSA's Tejano shrine at San Juan del Valle, and the diaspora's remittances: check where hope is placed across México's edges, and how it returns.
