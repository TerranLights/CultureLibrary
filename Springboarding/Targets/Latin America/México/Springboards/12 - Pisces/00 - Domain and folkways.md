# México: The domain: the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (clinical, devotional and market registers); classical and modern Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Mazatec; Zapotec (Sierra Norte); Rarámuri. **Concept in one line:** how a culture meets suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape: the hidden, confinement, sacrifice, compassion and transcendence, with magic ways (healing, charms, divination, witchcraft) as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check asylum files against market herb stalls and Spiritualist temples in the same districts.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check Nahua towns of the Sierra Norte on spirits of hills and springs against clinic use in the mill towns.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check how Cristero martyr stories and votive pleas at San Juan de los Lagos frame suffering.
- **Michoacán:** check Purépecha vigils for the dead at Lake Pátzcuaro against how tourism stages them.
- **Oaxaca:** check Mazatec, Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Chinantec and Triqui words for fright and soul loss; they need not match.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal curing against converts who refuse it, as each side tells the dispute.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican, Me'phaa and Ñomndaa accounts of a person's shadow or animal double, as each community gives them.
- **Veracruz and the Huasteca:** check Totonac and Teenek healers against the commercial *brujos* of Los Tuxtlas.
- **The Sierra Madre and the north:** check Rarámuri and Wixárika healing only as shared; Niño Fidencio's healers at Espinazo; whom Mennonite colonies and the Kikapú turn to in distress.
- **Smaller communities:** check exiled Republican psychiatrists' place in Mexican psychiatry; Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican mourning; Evangelical deliverance ministries; migrants in southern shelters.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán's Maya healers, Sonora's Yoeme towns, Juan Soldado's shrine in Cascadia's Tijuana, CSA Tejano healers and the diaspora: check them because much English writing on folk healing comes from South Texas and must not be read as central Mexican.
