# México: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (condolence formulas, school and clinical registers, colloquial speech of the capital and the north); classical Nahuatl (song); Tseltal and Tojolabal; Zapotec; Hñähñu. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check condolence formulas and *pena ajena* in office and barrio speech, and whether younger speakers use them alike.
- **Puebla, Tlaxcala and the bull ranches:** check whether ranch families and urban opponents describe an animal's suffering in the same terms.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check how mourners are accompanied at wakes and novenas, and what is said to them.
- **Jalisco:** check mariachi and *ranchera* performance as a channel for feeling with others in public.
- **Oaxaca:** check heart idioms in each Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk and Mazatec variety, and Hñähñu ones in the Mezquital; assume no match.
- **Chiapas:** check Tseltal, Tsotsil and Tojolabal speakers' own accounts of listening against Lenkersdorf's reading.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check how villagers on different sides of the 1970s war tell one another's losses.
- **Veracruz and the Huasteca:** check Totonac and Teenek mourning songs for who may weep with whom.
- **The north:** check Monterrey's medical schools and call centers; Rarámuri and Wixárika views on guessing another's mind, as they state them; Mennonite and Kikapú norms of showing feeling to outsiders.
- **Smaller communities:** check Afro-Mexican, Lebanese-Mexican, Jewish and Japanese-Mexican families on comforting the bereaved; Protestant testimony services; deaf communities using Lengua de Señas Mexicana.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán, Sonora, Cascadia's Bajas, CSA Tejanos and the diaspora: check them apart because *simpatía* research was built largely on Latino samples in the United States, and its results must not be carried into México.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in clinical and school measures and in media, thin for Indigenous terms of feeling-with outside Chiapas; Lenkersdorf is a single outside voice.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for song; Tojolabal and Tseltal for listening and heart idioms; Lengua de Señas Mexicana for deaf speakers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Chalco girls are identifiable in press coverage; crisis-line callers; war memories in Guerrero.
- **Held for review:** none; the song, clinic, screen and survey pointers on feeling-with raised no standing rule.
