# México: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (mystical, clinical, family and stadium registers); classical Nahuatl; Mazatec; Zoque; Purépecha; Wixárika (with care). **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self losing its edges in something larger (a person, a crowd, nature, the divine), and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check *concheros*, charismatic prayer groups and stadium crowds as three kinds of merging, and who takes part in each.
- **Puebla, Tlaxcala and the Estado de México:** check Nahua and Hñähñu towns on trance in healing against the parish's view of it.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check family breakdown in emigrant towns, and how priests and mothers describe it.
- **Michoacán:** check Purépecha memory of Parangaricutiro against the new town's own story.
- **Oaxaca:** check Mazatec ceremonies as Huautla families describe them against the tourist trade; check language shift in Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Chinantec and Triqui towns.
- **Chiapas:** check Zoque memory of drowned towns; Tsotsil and Tseltal accounts of losing the soul; Evangelical ecstatic worship.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check whether Afro-Mexican and Me'phaa towns describe mixing and identity loss in the national *mestizaje* terms or their own.
- **Veracruz and the Huasteca:** check Totonac and Teenek speakers' shift to Spanish, generation by generation.
- **The Sierra Madre and San Luis Potosí:** check Wixárika statements on visitors at Wirikuta and Real de Catorce; Rarámuri views on drinking and the self.
- **The north:** check Monterrey's dance crowds; Chihuahua Mennonites leaving the colonies; Kikapú language keeping.
- **Smaller communities:** check Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican, Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican intermarriage and name change; descendants of Republican exiles; internal migrants' children who lose a parent's language.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán's coast, Sonora, Cascadia's Bajas, the CSA's Tejanos and the diaspora: check them apart because retreat tourism around vision plants and the pull of English on migrants' children belong to those areas' own stories.
