# México: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (devotional, New Age and everyday registers); classical Nahuatl; Hñähñu (Otomí); Tsotsil; Rarámuri; Ñuu Savi; Huasteca Nahuatl. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; the experience and practice, and the sacred outside the church (faith and churches as institutions are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check Mixquic families against visitors; seekers of "energy" in Coyoacán and Roma against home altars in Iztapalapa.
- **Puebla, Morelos and the Estado de México:** check *tiemperos*' mountain prayer against parish rain Masses in the same towns.
- **The Bajío (Querétaro, Guanajuato):** check Hñähñu chapels at Tolimán against the shrine devotion of the Bajío cities.
- **Jalisco and Los Altos:** check whether residents describe their devotion in parish terms or private ones.
- **Michoacán:** check the Purépecha New Fire as a revived rite against village feasts kept unbroken.
- **Oaxaca:** check Ñuu Savi hill offerings; Zapotec, Ayuuk, Mazatec and Chinantec sacred places, only as each shares them.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal practice in Chamula and Tenejapa against Catholic catechists and Evangelicals.
- **Guerrero, the Costa Chica and Veracruz:** check Afro-Mexican devotions to Black Christs and saints; Totonac and Teenek field rites.
- **The Huasteca and the Sierra Norte de Puebla:** check Nahua and Otomí seed and paper rites against their sale as craft.
- **The Sierra Madre and the north:** check Rarámuri feasts and Wixárika pilgrimages only as shared; Niño Fidencio's devotees; Mennonite and Kikapú practice, from their own published words.
- **Smaller communities:** check Jewish, Lebanese-Mexican (Maronite), Chinese-Mexican and Japanese-Mexican devotions; descendants of Republican exiles (check what practice, if any, they keep); Evangelical, Adventist and Latter-day Saint converts; migrants' shrines in the south.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán's Maya ceremonies, Sonora's Yoeme deer rites, Cascadia's Bajas, the CSA's Tejano shrines and the diaspora's chapels: check them apart because outsiders' books on "Mexican spirituality" often draw on those peoples and would blur México's own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for popular Catholic devotion and Day of the Dead; mixed for seekers outside religion; thin for Kikapú, Triqui and Chinantec practice in print.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish; classical Nahuatl for the *tocotín* and *ixiptla*; Hñähñu for Tolimán; Huasteca Nahuatl for maize rites; Tsotsil for *ch'ul* terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** closed ceremonies (Wixárika, Rarámuri, Tsotsil); photography in Chamula's church; New Age sale of Indigenous rites. Churches as institutions go to Sagittarius.
