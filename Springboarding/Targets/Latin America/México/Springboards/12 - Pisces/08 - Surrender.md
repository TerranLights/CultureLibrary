# México: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (devotional, recovery, deathbed and sports registers); classical Nahuatl; Chichimeca Jonaz (Úza); Plautdietsch and High German. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check *tanatología* clinics and middle-class self-help against parish consolation in working-class boroughs.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check how Tlaxcalan memory of alliance and Nahua towns' memory of conquest treat 1521 differently.
- **The Bajío (Guanajuato, Querétaro):** check Chichimeca Jonaz and Hñähñu accounts of defeat against Querétaro's civic memory of 1867.
- **Jalisco and Zacatecas:** check whether towns of the old Caxcan country mark the Mixtón War at all.
- **Michoacán:** check Purépecha memory of the last Cazonci's fate.
- **Oaxaca:** check Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk and Triqui deathbed customs and what is said to the dying.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal teachings on accepting illness against Evangelical healing prayer that refuses it.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican and Me'phaa wakes for words of acceptance.
- **The Sierra Madre and the north:** check Rarámuri accounts of retreat into the canyons; whether Monterrey's sports and business talk praises refusing to quit; Old Colony yieldedness; Kikapú customs for the dying.
- **Smaller communities:** check Jewish mourning (*shivá*) and Lebanese-Mexican wakes; Japanese-Mexican memories of wartime confinement; exiles' descendants on the Republic's defeat; Central American migrants deciding to stop or turn back.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán's Caste War, Sonora's Yoeme deportations, Cascadia's Bajas, the CSA's Tejanos and the diaspora: check them apart because the best-studied nineteenth-century surrenders of Indigenous peoples belong to Yucatán and Sonora, and the 1848 losses are remembered most in the lost lands.
