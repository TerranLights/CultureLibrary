# México: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (tabloid, street, northern and pulpit registers); classical Nahuatl; Tsotsil; other Indigenous languages only as their own dictionaries publish the terms. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it (as a moral and social category; statutes and courts are in Libra and Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check how Tepito, Iztapalapa and Polanco residents rank street theft against official corruption.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check mob beatings after rumors, and how neighbors justify or regret them.
- **The Bajío:** check Guanajuato towns caught in fuel-theft and cartel violence, and the moral line people draw around *huachicol*.
- **Jalisco and Michoacán:** check Purépecha Cherán's stand against illegal loggers as communities tell it; music, prestige and fear around cartels in Guadalajara.
- **Oaxaca:** check Zapotec, Ñuu Savi, Ayuuk, Chinantec and Triqui towns' own words for grave wrongs, and public shaming customs as each describes them.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal meanings of *mul* against Spanish *delito*; migrants robbed on the southern route.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican and Me'phaa families of the disappeared, and the town patrols' view of wrongdoers.
- **Veracruz and Tamaulipas:** check searching mothers' accounts of graves and silence.
- **Sinaloa, Durango, Chihuahua and Nuevo León:** check the Malverde chapel's visitors; Juárez women's killings as families describe them; Monterrey's fear of crime against its crime rates; Rarámuri communities facing growers; Mennonite colonies under pressure.
- **Smaller communities:** check Lebanese-Mexican and Jewish merchants on extortion; Chinese-Mexican memory of the 1930s campaigns that branded them; Central American migrants as victims and as suspects; Evangelical converts in prisons.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán, Sonora's cartel country, Cascadia's Tijuana (Juan Soldado; the cartel era), the CSA's Texas border cities and the diaspora's gangs: check them apart because much of the "Mexican crime" image in film and news is drawn there and would bury México's own regional patterns.
