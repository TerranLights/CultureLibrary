# México: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** México, region Latin America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mexican Spanish (nursery, pulpit, news and street registers); classical Nahuatl; Tlaxcala Nahuatl; Tsotsil; Ñuu Savi. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexico City:** check street legends, quake dread and fear of crime by borough, and who carries which fear.
- **Puebla and Tlaxcala:** check the blood-sucking witch in Nahua villages against clinic explanations of infant death.
- **The Bajío and Los Altos:** check fear of new violence in towns that once called themselves safe, as residents tell it.
- **Jalisco and Michoacán:** check Guadalajara's urban fears; Purépecha towns that armed themselves against loggers and cartels.
- **Oaxaca:** check Ñuu Savi, Zapotec, Mazatec, Chinantec and Triqui fright cures, only as healers share them.
- **Chiapas:** check Tsotsil and Tseltal fear of witchcraft and soul loss against migrants' fear of detention near Tapachula.
- **Guerrero and the Costa Chica:** check Afro-Mexican and Me'phaa towns under armed groups; fear on the coast after hurricanes.
- **Veracruz, Tamaulipas and the Gulf:** check the Matamoros panic; fear in towns of searching mothers; Totonac fright beliefs.
- **The Sierra Madre and the north:** check Rarámuri and Wixárika communities under growers' pressure, as they speak of it; Monterrey's talk of crime; what Mennonite colonies teach about the outside world; Kikapú customs against danger.
- **Smaller communities:** check Chinese-Mexican memory of the 1930s expulsions; Japanese-Mexican memory of wartime removal; whether Jewish and Lebanese-Mexican families speak of kidnapping as a shared fear; Evangelical deliverance from demons.
- **Comparison only (the boundary is the author's):** Yucatán, Sonora, Cascadia's Bajas, the CSA's Tejanos and the diaspora: check them apart because fear of *la migra* and of the desert crossing belongs to those lines and must not stand in for México's own.
