# Appalachia: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: the Appalachian region of the USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English and its regional vocabulary; Cherokee of the Eastern Band (Kituwah dialect, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster lexical layer of mountain speech; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain, self and identity: what a self is here, and how it asserts itself (the self, the body, appearance, beginnings, initiative; naming ways; sport ways as runner-up).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields** (eastern Kentucky, southern West Virginia, southwest Virginia): check how company towns shaped self-description.
- **Northern Appalachia** (western Pennsylvania, Pittsburgh and the mill towns, Appalachian Ohio, New York's Southern Tier): check whether people there name themselves Appalachian at all.
- **Southern Appalachia** (east Tennessee, western North Carolina, north Georgia, north Alabama; Knoxville, Asheville): check how town and mountain selves split.
- **The Shenandoah Valley and the Blue Ridge:** compare German and Scots-Irish settlement records of the person.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** keep clan and town identity on the Band's own terms; the Seneca of the Allegany Territory on the northern edge, separately.
- **Black Appalachians** (coal-camp communities, Affrilachian writers), **Melungeons**, and **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh** mining and steel communities: check what each names first when asked who they are.
- **Amish and Mennonites** (Holmes County, Ohio; central Pennsylvania): check how church and family records register a new member and a newborn. **Lebanese and Syrian** families and **Latino newcomers** in poultry and orchard towns: check which home-country categories they keep on forms and in names.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati, Dayton, Detroit and Chicago: comparison only, to see what moving north did to the self-label.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore, oral history and the outside image; mixed for measured self-concept, since national surveys rarely isolate the region. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the Appalachian region of the USA.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee (Eastern Band dialect, syllabary) for personhood and naming; Pennsylvania German and older German script for Fraktur and Moravian papers; Spanish, and possibly Maya languages for Guatemalan newcomers (*check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee ceremonial and medicine knowledge is the Band's to share; name outside accounts as outside.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on personhood, naming or the body was set aside under the standing rules.
