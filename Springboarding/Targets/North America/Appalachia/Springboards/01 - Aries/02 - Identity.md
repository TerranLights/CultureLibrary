# Appalachia: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee of the Eastern Band; Seneca on the northern edge; Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central coalfields against Northern Appalachia:** check whether people in western Pennsylvania, Appalachian Ohio and the Southern Tier use "Appalachian" for themselves or only for others.
- **Knoxville and Asheville against their home counties:** check how city newcomers and old families label each other.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee (Qualla Boundary townships, Snowbird) and the Seneca:** check identity in each nation's own terms, enrollment questions left to them.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare coal-camp descendants with Black families of Knoxville, Asheville and Pittsburgh's Hill District.
- **Melungeons:** check who claims the name now against who carried it as a slur.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh communities:** check how parish, fraternal lodge and old-country village each figure in self-labels.
- **Amish and Mennonites; Lebanese and Syrian families; Latino newcomers:** check how much of each group's identity is drawn against the majority.
- **Out-migrant "briers" in Cincinnati, Dayton and Detroit:** comparison only (Jim Wayne Miller's *Brier* poems *check*), for identity after leaving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, heritage groups and oral history; mixed in surveys, as "Appalachian" is not a census category.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee and Seneca for self-names; Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Slovak, Hungarian and Rusyn for parish and lodge records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal enrollment is the nations' own matter; Melungeon and other mixed-ancestry labels carry a history of slurs and DNA disputes; avoid exonyms except as quoted history.
- **Held for review:** none; no heritage, census or self-naming pointer needed to be set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Cooper, Knotts and Elders and note which counties' respondents most and least claim the label.
2. Search the Nationality Rooms committee records for the 1930s statements each community wrote about itself.
3. Ask the Museum of the Cherokee People which identity materials it shares, and read *Unto These Hills*' revision history.
4. Collect fifty "Where I'm From" poems published by Appalachian schools and list what each names first.
5. Search the Khayrallah Center catalog for "West Virginia".
