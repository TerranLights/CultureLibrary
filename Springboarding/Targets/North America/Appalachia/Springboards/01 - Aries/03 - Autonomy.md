# Appalachia: Autonomy (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee of the Eastern Band; Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled, for persons and for collectives such as towns and Native nations.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appalachian English | *say-so* | the right to decide ("it's not your say-so") | search with "who has the say-so" |
| Appalachian English | *suit yourself* | do as you choose | permission or dismissal, by tone |
| Appalachian English | *company town*, *scrip*, *company store* | coal-company settlement and its token money | the setting where choice was most limited |
| Appalachian English | *sang*, *sang digging* | wild ginseng, gathered on open land | a household's own cash, outside wage control |
| Cherokee | ᎦᏚᎩ *gadugi* | communal work, a people helping itself | collective self-help; *check* current Eastern Band use |
| Pennsylvania German | *Ordnung* | the church district's order of living | who decides for the member |
| Pennsylvania German | *Rumspringa* | youth's "running around" before baptism | the time a choice is left to the young; *check* |
| Spanish | *cada quien su vida*; *el patrón* | to each their own life; the boss | for newcomers' talk of choice at home and work |

English keywords: absentee ownership, company towns, local control, War on Poverty community action, self-sufficiency, subsistence commons, worker ownership, tribal self-governance (law aspects to Libra and Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coal camps against independent farm counties:** check whether family decisions differed where the company owned the houses.
- **Steel and glass towns of the Ohio Valley and the Monongahela:** check shop-floor control against the company, before and after unions.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check collective self-rule as the Band describes it; separate from individual choice.
- **Black coal-camp families:** check what segregated camp housing and schools left to their own decision, and what Black churches and lodges ran for themselves.
- **Old Regular and Primitive Baptists against missionary and mainline churches:** compare who governs a congregation.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check who decides for a member before and after baptism.
- **Women and elders:** check who decides on marriage, birth and moving away, by generation.
- **Latino seasonal and plant workers:** check control of time and housing against settled newcomers.
- **Knoxville, Asheville and university towns:** check whether professional newcomers frame autonomy differently.
- **Out-migrants on "Route 23" to Ohio and Michigan:** comparison only, for why leaving was framed as choosing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land, labor and church self-governance; mixed for household decision-making.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for collective self-help terms; Pennsylvania German for *Ordnung*; Spanish for farmworkers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** health and birth records; minors in the youth study; tribal governance is the Band's to describe, and its law belongs to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** none; no land, labor or self-governance source was withheld.
