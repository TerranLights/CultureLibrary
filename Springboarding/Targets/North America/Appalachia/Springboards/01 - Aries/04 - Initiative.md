# Appalachia: Initiative (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee of the Eastern Band (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** starting things and acting without being told; taking the lead unasked.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield counties against farm and tourism counties (Sevier, Watauga, Holmes):** check where starting a business is talked of as normal or as risky.
- **Pittsburgh and Knoxville against small towns:** compare founders' stories in cities with those in county seats.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check cooperative and tribal enterprise on the Band's terms; Sequoyah is claimed by more than one Cherokee nation, so keep the Band's telling.
- **Black Appalachians:** check churches, lodges and businesses founded in coal camps and in Knoxville's and Asheville's Black districts.
- **Jewish, Lebanese and Syrian merchant families; Italian and Slavic lodges:** check who started stores and mutual-benefit societies.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check individual enterprise against church limits.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *tandas*, *tiendas* and hometown clubs as starting tools.
- **Women:** compare settlement-school founders, craft-guild makers and today's owners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions and business statistics; folk material on Jack is large; measured "initiative" as a trait is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee (syllabary-era papers); Pennsylvania German; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reformer-founded schools and guilds often tell their story from outside; read founders' papers against local accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; the founders, lenders and craft pointers raised no standing-rule issue.
