# Appalachia: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee of the Eastern Band; Seneca (northern edge); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee and the Seneca:** check each nation's own beginnings and calendar against settler founding stories.
- **Scots-Irish-, English- and German-descended farm families:** compare New Year and planting-start customs between Shenandoah Germans and Blue Ridge Scots-Irish.
- **Moravians of the Wachovia tract:** check the year's opening in church records against neighbors'.
- **Black Appalachians:** check arrival stories into the coal camps and the Hill District against leaving stories.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak and Rusyn steel and coal families:** check which feast or arrival date opens the family story.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check how baptism as a young adult marks a beginning.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *quinceañera* and first-communion customs as they settle.
- **Out-migrants returning home after a Midwestern factory life:** comparison only, for starting over twice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore and church records; mixed for birth customs; thin and sensitive for recovery.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee; Seneca; Pennsylvania German; old German script for Moravian papers; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Haudenosaunee longhouse ceremonies are restricted to those the Seneca invite; Cherokee ceremony is the Band's to share; recovery accounts involve living people.
- **Held for review:** none; no origin, calendar or birth source was set aside.

1. Read the Brown Collection belief volumes and pull every entry for "New Year", "first" and "moon" from mountain counties.

3. Compare *Blum's* and *Grier's* planting tables for one year.
4. Read Trotter's chapters on migration, then search WPA life histories for Black migrants into the coalfields.
