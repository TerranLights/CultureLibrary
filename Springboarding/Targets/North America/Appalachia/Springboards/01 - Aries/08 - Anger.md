# Appalachia: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee of the Eastern Band; Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Appalachian English | *pitch a fit*, *raise a ruckus* | to blow up, make a scene | anger let out |
| Scots/Ulster layer | *ill* | bad-tempered, cross ("he's ill this morning") | Ulster and Scots sense of the word; *check* |
| Cherokee | (term not given) | anger, and the old red (war) and white (peace) offices | ask Eastern Band speakers; do not guess |
| Pennsylvania German | *bees* | angry (from German *böse*) | *check* form and use |
| Spanish | *coraje*, *muina*, *berrinche* | anger; anger that makes one ill; tantrum | Mexican usage; also *el que se enoja pierde* |

English keywords: feud, grudge, temper, righteous anger, strike violence, anger at outside portrayals, "War on Coal", domestic violence services, sermons on wrath.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield union families against farm families:** check whether anger at a company and anger in a family are spoken of in the same words.
- **Pittsburgh and the anthracite towns:** compare anger at bosses and at police among Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Italian families.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check, on the Band's terms, how anger is handled between clans and within families today.
- **Black Appalachians:** check memories of racial violence in the camps and anger at being written out of the region's story.
- **Old Regular Baptists, Holiness churches and Catholics of the mill towns:** compare teaching on wrath and forgiveness.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check how church counsel and discipline respond when a member's anger becomes a quarrel.
- **Women:** check how women's anger is named in songs, sermons and family settlements.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *coraje* and *muina* against anger at work and in raids.
- **Out-migrants in Ohio and Michigan:** comparison only, for anger at being labeled "brier".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in songs, labor history and the feud literature; outside feud and "hillbilly violence" writing is large and must be named as outside; measured anger is thin.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee; Pennsylvania German; Slovak and Polish for Lattimer sources; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** domestic violence; killings with living descendants; immigration raids; retaliation is a law topic for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the feud, song and labor pointers on anger raised no standing-rule issue.

## 5. First queries
1. Search Chronicling America for "Hatfield" and "McCoy", 1882-1892; note which papers used "feud" first and where they were printed.
2. Read Waller's chapters on outside coverage against the Pike County, KY sources she cites.
3. Watch *Stranger with a Camera* and list the community voices it records.
4. Pull the Pittston strike run of the *UMWA Journal* and note how leaders asked members to hold anger.
