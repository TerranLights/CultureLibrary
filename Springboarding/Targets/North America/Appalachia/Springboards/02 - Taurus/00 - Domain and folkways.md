# Appalachia: Domain and folkways, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA; written here for the mountain region within them). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, Kituwah dialect, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster word layer; Spanish; heritage languages of the coal and steel towns. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having, and how it is held: possessions, money, resources, food ways, dress ways and wealth ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields** against **Northern Appalachia** (western Pennsylvania, Appalachian Ohio, the Southern Tier): check company-owned housing and stores against mill-town wage households.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge**: compare farm-and-craft household inventories with the coalfields, to test whether the domain divides by economy or by state.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: separate the Qualla Boundary, where land is held through the tribe, when asking what a household can call its own.
- **Black Appalachians**: compare coal-camp household records with white neighbors' in the same camps (owned against rented from the company).
- **Melungeon families** of Hancock County and nearby: check whether their property records exist under shifting classifications in the census and courts.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** coal and steel families: compare household budgets by group in the same towns.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families**: check peddling and store-keeping.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities**: check which goods each church district's rules allow.
- **Latino newcomers** in poultry, carpet and Christmas-tree towns: check remittance budgets against local spending.
- **Out-migrants** in Detroit, Cincinnati, Chicago and Ohio's mill towns: comparison only, for what was carried away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for coal, steel and craft history; mixed for present spending, which federal surveys rarely break out for the region.
- **Scope:** no settled lands yet; the Taurus sheets cover the mountain region of the pre-war USA and draw no borders. The Appalachian Regional Commission's county list is one source's definition, not the nation's units.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee with an Eastern Band speaker; Pennsylvania German; Spanish and, for Morganton, Maya languages; Slavic and Italian parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grave goods and burials (Cherokee and earlier Ohio Valley peoples); the outside "poverty" image of the region; Eastern Band cultural property.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on property, the body or material life was set aside under the standing rules.
