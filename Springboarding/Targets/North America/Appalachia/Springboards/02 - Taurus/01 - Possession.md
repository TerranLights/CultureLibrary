# Appalachia: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish; Welsh, Italian and Slavic heritage terms. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield camp families** of Harlan, McDowell and Wise counties: check which furnishings came with company houses and which families owned and moved with them.
- **Families removed for parks and dams** (Smokies, Shenandoah, TVA basins): compare what each removal let people carry.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: check objects held outside the Qualla Boundary against those returned, and who decides where they rest.
- **Black Appalachians**: check which church, school and family objects were carried to the homecomings of former coal-camp residents.
- **Melungeon families**: check where family Bibles, photographs and papers used to prove lineage are kept.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Italian households** in mill and mine towns: separate objects kept at home from those given to the parish.
- **Welsh-descended families** of southeast Ohio and northeastern Pennsylvania: check whether Welsh Bibles and hymnals survive as heirlooms.
- **Pennsylvania German, Amish and Mennonite households**: check the goods given at marriage and handed down in a family.
- **Latino newcomers**: compare what is kept in the household with what is boxed and sent home.
- **Out-migrant families** in Detroit, Dayton and Cincinnati: comparison only, for what left with them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for objects in museums and quilts; thin for what present households hold and why, which needs new interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for possessive forms; Pennsylvania German for household words; Spanish; Rusyn and Slovak for parish and home objects.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burial goods and Native objects in collections; grief over flood losses is recent and raw.
- **Held for review:** none; no source on what households keep and hand down was set aside.
