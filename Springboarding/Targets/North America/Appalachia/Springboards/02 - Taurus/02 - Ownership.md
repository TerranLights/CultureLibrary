# Appalachia: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish; Polish and Hungarian for mill-town parishes. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields**: check how split surface and mineral title shapes who feels they own a farm.
- **Great Smoky Mountains, Shenandoah and TVA lake counties**: compare memories of land taken for public use with the coalfields' memories of land sold to companies.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: separate trust land and possessory holdings from fee-simple land around the Boundary.
- **Black Appalachians**: check land records for Black landowners and tenants in the Valley of Virginia and east Tennessee, and coal-camp renters.
- **Melungeon families** of Newman's Ridge: check how ridge-top holdings were recorded and kept.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns**: compare homeowning paths of Slovak, Rusyn, Polish, Hungarian and Italian families with company housing in the coalfields.
- **Moravian, Amish and Mennonite communities**: check church or community say over who may buy land.
- **Latino newcomers** in Dalton, Gainesville and Morganton: check renting, buying and mobile-home lots.
- **Out-migrants** who kept the homeplace while living in Ohio or Michigan: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land history and absentee holding; mixed for how people speak of owning today.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for land and holding words; Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Polish and Slovak parish papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native land loss and removal; families displaced by parks and dams; mineral-rights quarrels still alive. Deeds and court cases as law belong to Libra; the spirit of property law belongs to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; no land-title, mineral-rights or tenure pointer had to be set aside.
