# Appalachia: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Welsh; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields** against **Southern Appalachian tourist counties** (Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge): check how selling a mountain image to visitors bears on local self-regard.
- **Mill families of Pittsburgh's river valleys**: compare worth after the steel closures with worth after coal layoffs in southern West Virginia.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: weigh boarding-school memories told by Band members against the school's own records, and set both beside language renewal.
- **Black Appalachians**: check how worth is named against both regional and racial images, in writers and in church and school records.
- **Melungeon families**: check who claims the name and who refuses it, and why.
- **Descendants of Welsh, Hungarian, Rusyn, Slovak, Polish and Italian miners**: check how earlier slurs against their forebears appear in family memory.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities**: check teaching on pride and humility in the settlements' own publications.
- **Latino newcomers**: check worth at work in poultry and carpet plants, and in schools.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati, Detroit, Chicago and Dayton: comparison only, for worth under northern city slurs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on outside images; thinner on people's own words about worth, which needs oral history and new interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for words of respect and shame; Pennsylvania German for church teaching; Welsh chapel papers; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide data; boarding-school records; racial classification of Melungeon and Black families (the legal side to Libra).
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on dignity, shame or standing was set aside under the rules.
