# Appalachia: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Welsh and Polish heritage terms; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Spanish | *arraigo*, *echar raíces* | rootedness; to put down roots | newcomer families |

English keywords: out-migration and return Appalachia; mill closing Monongahela Valley; coal employment decline county; lined-out hymn singing; Amish settlement directory; Fontana Dam cemeteries.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields**: compare staying and leaving with each coal bust since the 1950s.
- **Steel towns of the Mon Valley and the upper Ohio**: trace continuity of parish, club and neighborhood after the mills closed.
- **Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains tourist counties**: check how new second-home owners change who stays.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: check continuity of town sites and families on the Qualla Boundary and in Snowbird.
- **Black Appalachians**: check which coal-camp communities kept their churches after the camps emptied.
- **Melungeon families**: check how long families have held the same ridges and hollows, from deeds and censuses.
- **Welsh, Italian, Polish, Slovak, Hungarian and Rusyn parishes**: compare which closed or merged and which lasted.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements**: check founding dates and splits of church districts.
- **Latino newcomers** in Dalton, Gainesville, Morganton and the Christmas-tree counties: check who has settled and who moves with the work.
- **Out-migrants and those who came back** from the northern factory cities: comparison only, for who returned and why.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration and industrial decline; mixed for people's own sense of a steady life, which needs interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for directories; Welsh and Polish parish records; Spanish; Cherokee for place names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grief over drowned and removed communities; Cherokee sacred sites.
- **Held for review:** none; no residence, tenure or job-continuity source was held back.
