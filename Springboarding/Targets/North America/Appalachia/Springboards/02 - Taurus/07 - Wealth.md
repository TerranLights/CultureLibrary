# Appalachia: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish; Arabic and Hungarian heritage terms. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields**: compare coal wealth held by outside owners with what stayed in the county.
- **Pittsburgh's old fortunes** against **the Mon Valley mill towns**: check how the same industry looks from the house on the hill and the house by the mill.
- **Knoxville and Asheville** against **the rural Blue Ridge**: check how tourism and second homes reshape who counts as well-off.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: separate the tribe's gaming revenue and public spending from household wealth, using the Band's published budgets.
- **Black Appalachians**: check Black-owned businesses and professionals in coal towns and in Knoxville and Asheville.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families**: check the peddler-to-merchant route in Charleston, Wheeling and the coal towns.
- **Italian, Slovak, Hungarian, Polish and Rusyn descendants**: compare saving for a house with saving for a family business.
- **Amish shop owners and Mennonite farmers**: check church limits on display alongside business growth.
- **Latino newcomers**: compare plant wages with the shops and restaurants newcomers have opened in Dalton and Gainesville.
- **Melungeon families**: sources on wealth likely scarce; start with tax lists and land records of Hancock County.
- **Out-migrants** who returned with factory savings: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on industrial fortunes and poverty; thin on how ordinary people define being well-off, which needs interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee; Pennsylvania German; Arabic for Lebanese family papers; Spanish; Hungarian and Slovak.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slavery records; tribal finances; the outside "poor region" image (read outside accounts as such).
- **Held for review:** none; no income, wealth or extraction-economy dataset was set aside under the rules.
