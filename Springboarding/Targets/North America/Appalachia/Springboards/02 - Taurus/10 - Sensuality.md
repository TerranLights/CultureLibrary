# Appalachia: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish; Italian and Slavic food words. **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields** against **the Shenandoah Valley**: check how food memories differ between coal-camp kitchens and Valley farm kitchens.
- **Mill-town parishes around Pittsburgh**: compare Slovak, Rusyn, Polish, Hungarian and Italian festival foods with the same groups' foods at home.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: check which foods and gatherings the Band presents to visitors and which it keeps among members.
- **Black Appalachians**: check church homecoming dinners and family cooking, from oral history.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families**: check church food festivals in Charleston and Wheeling.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities**: check where church rules permit or limit music, dress and comfort.
- **Holiness, Old Regular and Primitive Baptist congregations**: compare singing, touch (footwashing, handshaking) and bodily worship.
- **Latino newcomers**: check tiendas, bakeries and taquerías in Dalton, Gainesville and Morganton as places of taste and memory.
- **Out-migrants**: comparison only, for the foods sent or carried north.
