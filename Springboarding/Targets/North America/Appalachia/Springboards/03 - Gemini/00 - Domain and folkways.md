*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether speech and schooling studies drawn from single counties (eastern Kentucky, southern West Virginia) hold across coal-camp and farm hollers.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare Pittsburgh, the Ohio Valley mill towns and the Southern Tier with the south on dialect features and on parochial against public schooling.
- **Southern Appalachia:** check whether Smokies speech studies generalize to north Georgia and north Alabama.
- **Shenandoah Valley and the Moravian settlements:** separate German-speaking schooling traditions from Scots-Irish ones.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** separate syllabary literacy, English schooling and the immersion school, and the Snowbird and Big Cove communities.
- **Black Appalachians:** check segregated coal-camp schools and Affrilachian writers' accounts of speech and learning.
- **Melungeon families:** check how school records classified their children.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** communities: compare heritage-language Saturday schools and parish schools.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** separate Pennsylvania German at home from English in school.
- **Latino newcomers:** check English-learner programs in poultry and nursery towns.
- **Out-migrants** to Cincinnati, Dayton, Detroit and Chicago: comparison only, for speech kept or dropped.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for dialect and school history; mixed for present-day conversation norms, which few studies record outside single communities.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled, and nothing in this sign draws a border. Every sheet here aims at the mountain region as it lay in the pre-war USA; the Appalachian Regional Commission's counties are cited only as that commission's own definition.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee syllabary reading for the *Phoenix* and later letters; Pennsylvania German for Plain households; Spanish; Slovak, Polish and Hungarian for parish school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "hillbilly" speech as an outside joke; accent shaming in schools; Eastern Band language materials the Band controls.
- **Held for review:** none; no speech, school or neighbor pointer for the domain was set aside under the standing rules.
