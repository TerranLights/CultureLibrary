# Appalachia: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster word layer; Spanish; American Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what good communication is held to be and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield counties of eastern Kentucky and southern West Virginia:** compare union-hall speech and church speech as two public registers in the same towns.
- **Pittsburgh and the Monongahela mill towns:** check how multilingual workplaces shaped plain or blunt talk, using lodge and union records.
- **Blue Ridge and the Smokies:** compare the Hall recordings with today's speakers to separate change from continuity.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** separate syllabary writing, English and the immersion program; ask which speech settings the Band keeps for Cherokee.
- **Black Appalachians:** check pulpit and porch talk in coal-camp churches against white congregations in the same camps.
- **Melungeon families of Hancock County:** check what oral sources exist and who has recorded them; sources may be few.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** separate English used with outsiders from Pennsylvania German used inside.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check whether Arabic stayed in the household or dropped within a generation.
- **Latino newcomers:** check interpreting and language brokering by children.
- **Deaf communities:** separate regional sign users from those schooled elsewhere.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan cities: comparison only, for accent shifting at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on dialect and preaching; mixed on everyday conversation, recorded mostly through single-community studies and fiction.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee syllabary for letters and notices; ASL with Deaf researchers; Spanish and possibly K'iche' or Mam for health interpreting (*check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recordings of worship and of tongues need church consent; accent-mocking material in media should be named as outside caricature, not evidence.
- **Held for review:** none; no pointer on talk, preaching, letters or accents had to be set aside.
