# Appalachia: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Polish, Slovak and Italian for the older immigrant press. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how it is gathered, passed on, trusted, withheld and used: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** compare company-town information (company papers, scrip-store notices) with union and independent sources.
- **Northern Appalachia:** check Pittsburgh's daily press and the immigrant weeklies against small Southern Tier papers.
- **Blue Ridge and Shenandoah Valley farm counties:** compare almanac, Extension and radio farm reports as sources of practical facts.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** separate the Band's own outlets and data rules from county and state sources.
- **Black Appalachians:** check which Black papers reached coal camps, and through whom.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how news moves without a home telephone or internet, and what church districts allow.
- **Latino newcomers:** check Spanish- and Maya-language channels, and fear of giving information to officials.
- **Rusyn, Hungarian and Lebanese families:** check church bulletins and lodge newsletters as information channels.
- **Melungeon families:** check how census and court records described them, and how descendants now use those records.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan cities: comparison only, for which hometown weeklies they still take by mail.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on the press, disasters and broadband; mixed on everyday trust in sources, which surveys rarely report below state level.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for newcomer media; Slovak, Polish and Italian for the older weeklies; Cherokee for the Band's own publications.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Band's control of research data; disaster survivors' testimony; avoid reading outside "ignorance" narratives as evidence.
- **Held for review:** none; none of the press, almanac or broadband pointers fell under a standing rule.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Medill news-desert data for every county in eastern Kentucky, southern West Virginia and western North Carolina.
2. Request *Mountain Eagle* microfilm for 1960-1975 and list stories about coal, schools and local government.
3. Search Laws's catalog for mine-disaster and murder ballads collected in Kentucky, Virginia and West Virginia.
4. Look up the Elk River spill coverage in the *Charleston Gazette* for the first two weeks.
5. Ask the University of Kentucky's special collections for the Pack Horse Library photographs and any reading lists.
