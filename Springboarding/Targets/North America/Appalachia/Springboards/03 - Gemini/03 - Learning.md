# Appalachia: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster word layer; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life (schooling as an institution and higher learning go to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how mining skills pass from older miners to "red hats," and how that changed with mechanization.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare mill-town apprenticeships and parochial schools in Pittsburgh's valleys with Southern Tier farm schooling.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** compare craft schools, settlement schools and county schools as three routes of learning.
- **Shenandoah Valley Mennonites:** check how singing schools and church schools taught across generations.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** separate syllabary learning, boarding-school schooling and today's immersion classes.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare Rosenwald and coal-camp schools with the white schools in the same counties.
- **Melungeon families:** check the Vardy mission school's records and who wrote them; sources are few.
- **Italian, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Rusyn families:** check parish schools and heritage-language classes.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities of Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania:** check schooling to eighth grade and learning at home and work afterward.
- **Latino newcomers:** check English-learner and migrant-education programs, and children teaching parents.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati and Dayton: comparison only, for school experience after moving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on schools, settlement schools and crafts; mixed on learning at home, best reached through oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for syllabary primers and today's materials; Pennsylvania German for Plain teaching; Spanish for migrant-education records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the boarding-school history; reformers' records that describe mountain children in deficit terms; Amish teachers' privacy.
- **Held for review:** none; no school, craft or apprenticeship pointer was put aside for this concept.
