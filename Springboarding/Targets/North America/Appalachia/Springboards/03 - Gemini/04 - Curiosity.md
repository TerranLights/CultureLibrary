# Appalachia: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster word layer; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore; and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how coal-camp schools and company towns handled pupils' science interests, using Coalwood as one case and searching for others.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare Pittsburgh's museum and university culture with smaller Allegheny Plateau towns.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Great Smoky Mountains:** check who joins naturalist programs: residents, retirees who moved in, or visitors.
- **East Tennessee around Oak Ridge and Knoxville:** separate a lab town's newcomers from the families displaced to build it.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own rules on who may ask about, record or study sacred places and stories.
- **Black Appalachians:** check segregated-school science teaching and the routes that led some pupils to research careers.
- **Melungeon families:** check how DNA testing and older ancestry theories were received inside families.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check what reading and inquiry each church district allows after eighth grade.
- **Lebanese, Italian and Slovak families:** check parish and lodge records for clubs, reading rooms and hobby groups; sources may be thin.
- **Latino newcomers:** check children's access to museums, libraries and science programs; data likely scarce, start with school and library outreach reports.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan: comparison only, for children's school science after moving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on caving, natural history and space-age memoirs, thin on everyday norms of asking, which need interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the Band's terms on asking and knowing; Pennsylvania German for Plain restrictions on reading; Spanish for library outreach.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee sacred knowledge and sites; human remains in museum collections; outsiders' "strange mountain folk" curiosity, to be named as outside framing.
- **Held for review:** none; no exploration, science or folklore pointer was withheld under the rules.
