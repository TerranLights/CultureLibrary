# Appalachia: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish and Maya languages of newcomers; Slovak, Rusyn and Italian for fraternal records. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how kin networks steered jobs in mines and how layoffs pushed those networks north.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare fraternal lodges in Pittsburgh's mill towns with kin-based ties in Southern Tier farm counties.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Cumberland Plateau:** check how church homecomings and reunions renew scattered families.
- **Knoxville and Asheville:** separate long-rooted residents' networks from those of newcomers and retirees who moved in.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the clan system and the Band's ties with Cherokee in Oklahoma, as the Band describes them.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare reunions of coal-camp families with church-based ties among those who stayed.
- **Melungeon families:** check how name-based reunions differ from kin-based ones.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian, Welsh and Italian** families: compare which fraternal societies each relied on, and how long they lasted.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check ties between parishes in Wheeling, Charleston and Pittsburgh; sources are likely parish-based.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check church-district rules on phones and visiting.
- **Latino newcomers:** check hometown associations and family chats spanning borders.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati, Dayton and Detroit: comparison only, for return trips and reunions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration networks and fraternal records; mixed on loneliness, measured mostly at state level.
- **Languages needed:** Rusyn and Slovak for lodge newspapers; Italian; Spanish and K'iche' or Q'anjob'al for Guatemalan associations (*check*); Cherokee for clan terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** incarcerated people's families on "Calls from Home"; Cherokee clan knowledge; undocumented migrants' networks.
- **Held for review:** none; nothing on kin, lodges, roads or reunions was set aside for this concept.
