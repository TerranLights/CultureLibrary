# Appalachia: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German and the High German of Plain worship; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived with: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated (proposed definition).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether union and company loyalties split families, and how coal's future is argued inside one household.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare immigrant parishes' double loyalties with Ohio Valley towns whose families have no recent homeland.
- **East Tennessee and the Shenandoah Valley:** compare Union and Confederate memory across neighboring counties.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band describes living in two systems (tribal and state), in its own words.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how Affrilachian writers and coal-camp families describe being both Black and mountain-born.
- **Melungeon families of Hancock County and Wise County:** compare older family explanations of ancestry with today's DNA-informed accounts.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check how "Syrian" and "Lebanese" self-names changed over generations.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** separate the Plain-English pair as each church district draws it.
- **Latino newcomers' children:** check first-generation and second-generation views of home and town.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan: comparison only, for "Appalachian" and "Midwestern" held together.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on Civil War divisions and Melungeon history, thin on everyday double roles, which need interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the paired-color terms and town structure; Pennsylvania German and older German print for Plain worship; Spanish for second-generation studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racial classification of Melungeon families; disputed Cherokee ancestry claims; the "two-faced" label in political quarrels; avoid outside "split personality" images of the region.
