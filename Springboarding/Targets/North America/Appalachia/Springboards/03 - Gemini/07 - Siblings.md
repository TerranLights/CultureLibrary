# Appalachia: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Italian and Slavic languages for parish registers. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how mining families' sons followed one another into the mines and how daughters' roles differed.
- **Northern Appalachia:** compare mill-town families in Pittsburgh's valleys with farm families of Appalachian Ohio on who stays with the parents.
- **Southern Appalachia, north Alabama and the Blue Ridge:** check sibling music acts and family farms as two settings where siblings work together.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own kin terms and clan rules on who counts as a brother or sister, as the Band describes them.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare sibling migration chains from coal camps to Northern cities with white families from the same camps.
- **Melungeon families:** check whether siblings appear under different racial labels in the same census or court record.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Italian** families: check sibling sponsorship in migration and godparent choices.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check brothers in peddling and store partnerships.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check sibling order in farm succession and in leaving or staying in the church.
- **Latino newcomers:** check older siblings as interpreters and as earners sending money home.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan: comparison only, for siblings split between home and city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on music families and ballads; mixed on present-day sibling care, which needs local child-welfare and aging data.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for sibling terms; Pennsylvania German; Italian, Slovak and Hungarian for parish registers; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children in foster care; family feuds over land and inheritance; avoid outside jokes about mountain family size or cousin marriage, which are caricature, not evidence.
- **Held for review:** none; no ballad, music or family pointer was dropped for this concept.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Ritchie's chapters and list each sibling's chores and age, as a model for interviews.
2. Search the Perdue collection for every Jack tale with older brothers and note the tellers' names.
3. Look up "The Two Sisters" in Bronson and list the Kentucky, Virginia and North Carolina variants and their singers.
4. Pull AFCARS state tables on sibling placements for Kentucky and West Virginia for 2015-2023.
5. Search legal-aid sites for "heirs property" guides written for Appalachian counties.
