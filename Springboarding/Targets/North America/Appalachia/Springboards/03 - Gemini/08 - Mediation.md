# Appalachia: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Italian and Rusyn for devotional sources. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision; and, more broadly, the go-between (brokers, interpreters, intercessors). Court-annexed mediation and statutes go to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** compare union committeemen, preachers and county officials as go-betweens in strikes and family quarrels.
- **Northern Appalachia and the anthracite towns:** check how federal commissions and parish priests stood between immigrant miners and operators.
- **Shenandoah Valley:** separate peace-church practice (Mennonite and Brethren) from that of Presbyterian and Baptist neighbors.
- **Southern Appalachia and western North Carolina:** check professional mediation centers against church-based settling.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band names its peacemaking roles today, and how it tells Nancy Ward's story.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how Black ministers and union members acted as go-betweens in mixed coal camps.
- **Melungeon families:** sources on go-betweens are likely thin; start with church books and descendants' associations.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Italian and Polish** families: compare reliance on saints and priests as intercessors.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check merchants' role as brokers.
- **Amish communities of Appalachian Ohio:** check how bishops settle disputes within a church district.
- **Latino newcomers:** check crew leaders, consulates and churches as brokers.
- **Out-migrants** in Midwestern cities: comparison only, for agencies standing between newcomers and city offices.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor disputes and treaty councils; mixed for family and neighbor peacemaking, best found in church records and oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for titles of peace roles; Pennsylvania German for church council records; Spanish for farm-labor sources; Rusyn and Slovak for pilgrimage materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** union and company records of strikes; guest workers' fears of reprisal; the Band's own peace traditions.
- **Held for review:** none; no labor, church or peacemaking pointer met a rule that would set it aside.
