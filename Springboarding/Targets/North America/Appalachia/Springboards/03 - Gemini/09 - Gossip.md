# Appalachia: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA). **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; the Scots and Ulster word layer; Cherokee (Eastern Band); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Hungarian and Italian for coal-town folklore. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) is included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** compare company-town housing, where neighbors lived close, with scattered farm hollers in how reputations formed.
- **Northern Appalachia:** check parish and lodge life in Pittsburgh's mill towns against small Allegheny Plateau towns.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check how a single local case, like Frankie Silver's, keeps circulating.
- **Knoxville and Asheville:** separate urban gossip media from rural weekly correspondents' columns.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own words and teachings on talking about others, as it chooses to share them.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how rumor in white newspapers affected Black communities, and how Black papers answered.
- **Melungeon families:** compare outside rumor about origins with family-kept accounts.
- **Italian, Hungarian, Slovak and Polish** coal families: check folklore collections for tales about neighbors.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** sources likely thin; start with parish histories and oral histories.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how talk inside a church district relates to church discipline.
- **Latino newcomers:** check rumor channels about raids and jobs, and fear of being reported.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan: comparison only, for whether hometown talk follows people who move away.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in ballads, legends and church minutes, thin on present-day everyday gossip, which needs interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian and Italian for folklore informants' families; Spanish for newcomer channels; Cherokee through the Band's program; Pennsylvania German.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racial violence records; named people in rumor and scanner pages; immigration fears; never treat rumor about the region's people as evidence.
- **Held for review:** none; no rumor, ballad or church-discipline pointer was set aside here.
