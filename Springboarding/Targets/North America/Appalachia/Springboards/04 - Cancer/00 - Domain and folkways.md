# Appalachia: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with Pittsburgh's vocabulary); Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what home is and who family is: household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, and the building, family and child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield counties of Central Appalachia** (eastern Kentucky, southern West Virginia, southwest Virginia): houses the company owned against homeplaces the family owned.
- **Northern Appalachia** (western Pennsylvania, Appalachian Ohio, the Southern Tier, Pittsburgh and the mill towns): parish and mill records against farm-county records.
- **Knoxville, Asheville and the southern mountain counties** (east Tennessee, western North Carolina, north Georgia, north Alabama): city and resort housing against rural households.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee** (Qualla Boundary, Snowbird): clan and family as the Band describes them, apart from settler sources.
- **Black Appalachians** (Lynch and Benham, Kentucky; McDowell County, West Virginia; Affrilachian writers): family records from segregated camps and migration.
- **Melungeon families** (Hancock County, Tennessee; Wise County, Virginia): what the families' own heritage association has gathered; older writing is largely outside speculation.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn, Welsh, Lebanese and Syrian families** of coal and steel towns: parish and lodge records.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements** (Holmes County, Ohio; Pennsylvania's ridge-and-valley country): farm transfer and the grandparents' house in their own publications.
- **Latino newcomers** (Morganton and the Christmas-tree counties of North Carolina; Dalton and Gainesville, Georgia): how cross-border households are counted.
- **Out-migrant households** in Cincinnati, Detroit and Chicago: diaspora, comparison only; shows which household forms traveled.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for house types and census households; mixed for child-rearing (much by settlement workers); thin for Melungeon and Latino family life in their own voices. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Appalachian region of the United States.
- **Languages needed:** the Cherokee syllabary for clan terms; Pennsylvania German for farm-household words; Spanish, and possibly Q'anjob'al Maya for Morganton (*check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** much has been described from outside (settlement reports, poverty photography, reality TV); label these as outside accounts. Clan matters are the Band's to share; enrollment rolls carry living families' data.
- **Held for review:** nothing in this overview of the domain was set aside; family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
