# Appalachia: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield families in company towns** against those on owned land: whether the word "home" attaches to the house or to the hollow.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns** (Homestead, Braddock, Johnstown): rowhouse streets, parishes and the home as a worker's lifetime purchase; Johnstown's 1889 flood as an early loss.
- **Descendants of families removed** for the Smokies, Shenandoah and Blue Ridge Parkway, and for TVA lakes: what they record of the lost house.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee** (Qualla Boundary, Snowbird): trust land and the mother town, in the Band's own publications.
- **Black neighborhoods cleared by urban renewal** (Asheville's East End and Southside; Knoxville's "the Bottom" *check*): local oral-history projects on homes lost.
- **Families in manufactured housing** across the region: check housing data and residents' own accounts, not outside images.
- **Latino newcomers** in Dalton and Gainesville, Georgia, and western North Carolina: shared and rented homes, and the second home sent money back to.
- **Melungeon families** of Newman's Ridge, Tennessee: the ridge as a home place in their heritage association's materials.
- **Out-migrants** in Ohio and Michigan going "back home" on weekends: diaspora, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the housing stock and for displacement (parks, dams, floods, renewal); mixed for what home means day to day, which sits in oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English oral histories and letters; Cherokee terms for town and home place, checked with the Band; Spanish for newcomer families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pictures of run-down mountain houses were long used as poverty images; treat porch and shack photographs as outside accounts. Recent flood loss is raw for those affected. Property law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none of the housing, song or displacement sources raised a standing-rule question.
