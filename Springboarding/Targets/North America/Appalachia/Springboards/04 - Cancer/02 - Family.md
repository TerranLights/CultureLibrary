# Appalachia: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** set kin-named hollows and family cemeteries against coal-camp families brought in from elsewhere, to see whose kin records survive.
- **Southern Appalachia** (western North Carolina, east Tennessee, north Georgia): compare long-settled farm families' reunions with those of resort and retirement newcomers.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the Ohio Valley, the Southern Tier): ethnic parish families (Italian, Polish, Slovak, Hungarian, Rusyn, Welsh) against older Scots-Irish and English lines.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** clan kin as the Band describes it, set against federal enrollment's blood-quantum lines.
- **Black Appalachians:** church, family and migration records of the coal camps, and Affrilachian writers' family memoirs.
- **Melungeon families:** heritage-association genealogy against court and census records that labeled them.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families** of Charleston, Huntington and Wheeling: church and family-business records.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements** in Holmes and Tuscarawas Counties, Ohio: family news in their own papers.
- **Latino newcomers:** godparent and cross-border kin ties in Dalton, Gainesville and Morganton.
- **Out-migrant kin** in Ohio, Michigan and Illinois: diaspora, comparison only; check how kin chains ran between hollow and city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy and family history; mixed for how kin ties work now; thin and outside-written for Melungeon families.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee kin terms, with Eastern Band speakers; Pennsylvania German for Plain family letters; Spanish for compadrazgo records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "feud" is an outside label as often as an inside one; handle with Waller first. Enrollment rolls and DNA studies of Melungeon families touch identity and belonging; treat them as sensitive. Family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no genealogy or kinship source met a standing-rule exclusion.
