# Appalachia: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us" (Aquarius covers the group side).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Long-settled families and newcomers in resort and retirement counties** (Asheville, Boone, Gatlinburg, Highlands): check local papers for how the "from here" and "from off" line is drawn.
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** compare belonging measured by church and lodge membership with belonging told in oral histories after the mines closed.
- **Pittsburgh and the Ohio Valley mill towns:** dialect and parish as markers, with Polish, Slovak, Hungarian and Rusyn parishes merged or closed.
- **Black Appalachians:** Affrilachian writing and coal-camp reunions (Lynch, Benham) as records of claiming the region.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** belonging to the Band, to a township and to a clan, in the Band's own words.
- **Melungeon families:** gatherings and associations as a new site of shared identity; compare with census labels.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** church membership by adult baptism, and the line to "the English", from their own writings.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families** of Charleston and Wheeling: church and civic clubs as entry points.
- **Latino newcomers:** schools, churches and soccer leagues in Dalton, Gainesville and western North Carolina.
- **Urban Appalachians** in Cincinnati, Dayton and Detroit: diaspora, comparison only; their council's records show belonging rebuilt away from the mountains.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for identity debates and literature; mixed for measured belonging (proxies only at county level); thin for newcomer communities in their own voices.
- **Languages needed:** dialect studies (Appalachian, Pittsburgh) as much as foreign languages; Cherokee self-names with the Band; Spanish for newcomer schooling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "hillbilly" and similar labels are outside slurs that some insiders reclaim; record who uses them. Tribal enrollment and Melungeon ancestry are contested identity matters; let each speak for itself.
- **Held for review:** the identity and dialect pointers needed no exclusion.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the *Journal of Appalachian Studies* index for "identity", "insider" and "Affrilachia".
2. Search the Appalachian Studies Association's conference programs for papers on newcomers, return migrants and "from here".
3. Search county newspapers in western North Carolina for "homecoming" and "dinner on the grounds" notices.
4. Download County Health Rankings' "social associations" for Kentucky, West Virginia and Pennsylvania and map them.
5. Search the Melungeon Heritage Association's site for Union programs and family surnames.
