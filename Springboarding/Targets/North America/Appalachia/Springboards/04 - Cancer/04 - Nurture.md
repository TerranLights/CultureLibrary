# Appalachia: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** compare what grandparents and teachers did in raising children during strikes, layoffs and the opioid years, in oral histories and school records.
- **Southern Appalachia's farm counties** (western North Carolina, east Tennessee): look at children's chores and 4-H records for how work and teaching mixed.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns:** parochial and public schools in Italian, Polish and Slovak parishes; check which language children were taught in and when it changed.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** the Band's immersion schooling and teachings on raising children, in its own publications; boarding-school history kept separate and handled with care.
- **Black Appalachians:** church youth programs, camp schools and family kitchens as records of who raised children.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** their own schools and their own school papers, compared with outsiders' studies.
- **Melungeon families:** where sources on upbringing exist at all; likely only in oral histories and heritage-association materials.
- **Latino newcomers:** bilingual raising in Morganton, Dalton and the tree-farm counties; the children who interpret for parents.
- **Children of out-migrants** sent back to grandparents for summers or school: diaspora, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folktales, songs and school history; mixed for present-day practice (state surveys, few regional breakdowns); thin for Melungeon and Latino upbringing in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for immersion-school materials, with the Band; Pennsylvania German for Plain school writings; Spanish for newcomer parents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** settlement workers and charities long described mountain children as in need of rescue; label those reports as outside accounts. "Nature against nurture" talk about mountain families has a eugenics-era history; handle it as such. Child-welfare cases are confidential. Discipline as authority goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no child-rearing source was excluded; the tales and songs were checked against the rule.
