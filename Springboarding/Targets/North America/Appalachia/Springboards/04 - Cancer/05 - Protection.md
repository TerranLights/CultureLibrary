# Appalachia: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German and Anabaptist High German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** set union, company and family as rival protectors in strike-era records against present-day flood and overdose response.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns:** compare union, parish and lodge as the bodies that guarded steel families, strike by strike.
- **Southern Appalachia's farm and mountain counties:** check folklore collections for protective charms county by county, and who still practices them.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** protection of towns, graves and land in the Band's own statements, kept apart from outside archaeology.
- **Holiness and Pentecostal congregations** (from north Alabama to eastern Kentucky and West Virginia): how members themselves describe faith and safety, against outside coverage.
- **Black Appalachians:** look at lodge, church and union records in the coal camps for how communities organized their own safety under segregation.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** nonresistance teaching in their own publications, and how they handle emergencies with neighbors.
- **Latino newcomers** in east Tennessee, north Georgia and western North Carolina: church, consulate and legal-aid records after enforcement actions.
- **Out-migrant communities** in the Midwest: diaspora, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor conflict and land defense; mixed for folk protection (old collections, little recent work); thin for how newcomer families describe protecting themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for strike and church records; Anabaptist German for nonresistance texts; Cherokee for the Band's own statements; Spanish for families affected by raids.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** serpent handling and mountain gun ownership are favorite subjects of outside sensationalism; prefer members' and scholars' accounts. Domestic-violence and child-welfare records are confidential. Self-defense law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the strike, faith and land-defense pointers called for no exclusion.
