# Appalachia: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** set kin "taking in" against shelters and motels after floods and mine closures, using school and HUD data.
- **Southern Appalachia's resort counties** (Sevier, Buncombe, Watauga): compare shelter needs where short-term rentals and tourism wages meet, and the 2016 Gatlinburg fire's displacement.
- **Pittsburgh and the Ohio Valley:** urban missions and refugee housing against the small-town poor farms of the past.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** refuge in 1838 and housing on the Qualla Boundary, in the Band's own accounts.
- **Black Appalachians:** shelter networks before 1865 and church-run relief in coal and river towns.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** disaster rebuilding and the custom of taking in their own after a fire, from their papers.
- **Latino newcomers:** crowded and shared rentals in poultry and tree-farm towns, and church shelters after raids or storms.
- **Melungeon families:** sources on shelter and displacement are thin; start with county records and heritage-association oral histories.
- **Out-migrants** who found beds in Midwestern city missions and boardinghouses: diaspora, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for homelessness counts, disaster response and old poor-relief records; mixed for kin sheltering (seen mostly as "doubled up" in school data); thin for newcomer housing in residents' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for county records and oral histories; Cherokee for the Band's accounts of 1838; Spanish for Las Posadas and newcomer shelters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** shelter and school homeless records are confidential below the aggregate level; recent flood survivors' stories are personal. Outside "poverty tour" coverage of rural housing should be labeled as such. Law on shelter and eviction goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** a pointer on who was given a roof and taken in was set aside, and is kept only as a category: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
