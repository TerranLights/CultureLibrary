# Appalachia: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appalachian English | *run off*; *took off* | left a spouse or children | oral histories and court records |
| Appalachian English | *bound out* | (of an orphan or poor child) apprenticed by the county | older court usage |
| Appalachian English | *went to Ohio*; *went north* | left the region for work | the leaving that families debate as sacrifice or desertion |
| Pennsylvania German | *Meidung* | shunning of a member who leaves or is put out of the church | Amish usage; *check* |
| Spanish | *los que se quedan*; *padre ausente*; *deportado* | those left behind; absent father; deported | newcomer families split by migration or removal |

English keywords: kinship care Appalachia; grandparents raising grandchildren Kentucky; foster care opioid West Virginia; orphan apprenticeship county court; abandoned mine land; coal bankruptcy miners unpaid; depopulation McDowell County; Cherokee boarding school; leaving songs Appalachian.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** compare how families describe parents lost to overdose or prison with how court and foster records label the same cases.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns:** set the families left by mill closings in the 1980s against coal counties' later losses.
- **Southern Appalachia** (east Tennessee, north Georgia, western North Carolina): check kinship-care data in counties with fast in-migration against those losing people.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** boarding-school separations and child-welfare under the Band's own authority, in the Band's words.
- **Black Appalachians:** coal-camp families who left and the kin and churches left behind; homecoming reunions as answers to leaving.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** those who leave the church and those who stay, from both sides' writings, not outside dramas.
- **Latino newcomers:** children whose parents were deported or are working elsewhere; school and church records in Dalton and Morganton.
- **Melungeon families:** records here are thin; start with orphan and guardianship entries in Hancock and Hawkins County court books.
- **Parents who left for Ohio and Michigan plants**, and the kin who kept their children: diaspora side, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foster care, kinship care and depopulation; mixed for older desertion (court records, scattered); thin for newcomer families split by deportation.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English court and oral records; Pennsylvania German for shunning; Cherokee for the Band's accounts; Spanish for deported families' children.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare records are confidential; opioid-era coverage often turns families into outside spectacles, so prefer kin's and workers' own accounts. Boarding-school history is the Band's to tell first. Custody and support law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no foster-care, ballad or depopulation pointer was excluded.
