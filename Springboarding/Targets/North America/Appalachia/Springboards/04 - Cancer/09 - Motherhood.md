# Appalachia: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Eastern Band Cherokee (syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields:** compare granny-midwife and nurse-midwife memories with present-day maternal-health and NAS data, county by county.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Grafton, Pittsburgh, the Ohio Valley): mothers' clubs, parish mothers' societies and union auxiliaries as public roles.
- **Southern Appalachia** (western North Carolina, east Tennessee, north Georgia): check women's writing and singing about mothers against census and birth data.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** mothers, clan and the Beloved Woman title as the Band explains them.
- **Black Appalachians:** church mothers, kin who mothered children of migrating parents, and Affrilachian writers on their mothers.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** family size, home births and grandmothers' roles as their own publications report them.
- **Italian, Slovak, Rusyn, Polish and Lebanese families:** parish and fraternal women's societies in the mill and mine towns.
- **Latino newcomers:** prenatal care, the forty days and mothers raising children far from their own mothers, in Dalton, Gainesville and Morganton.
- **Melungeon families:** check how mothers' lines are traced in family genealogies; sources are scarce beyond heritage-association materials.
- **Mothers who raised children in Detroit or Dayton** while sending them "home" to grandmothers each summer: diaspora, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth statistics, midwifery history and songs; mixed for mothers' own words outside archives; thin for newcomer and Melungeon mothers.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English birth and kin vocabulary; Cherokee for Selu and the Beloved Woman, with the Band; Pennsylvania German for Plain mothers' letters; Spanish for clinics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal drug use and neonatal withdrawal are heavily stigmatized; avoid outside "crisis" framing and protect identities. Images of the worn mountain mother are a long outside cliché; check who made each image. Reproductive law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no midwifery, song or birth-data pointer was excluded.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Nunn Center catalog for the Frontier Nursing Service oral histories and list the interviews with mothers, not only nurses.
2. Query CDC WONDER natality for mean age of mother and teen births in Leslie, Perry, McDowell and Whitfield Counties, 2016-2023.
3. Search West Virginia archives and the Mother's Day Shrine for Ann Reeves Jarvis's Mothers' Day Work Club records.
4. Search ETSU's Archives of Appalachia for "midwife" and "granny".
5. Search the Library of Congress for field recordings of "The Wife of Usher's Well" from Kentucky and North Carolina.
