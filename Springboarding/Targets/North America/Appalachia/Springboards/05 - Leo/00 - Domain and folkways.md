# Appalachia: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America (pre-war lands carried: USA; the Appalachian region of the United States). **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian English, Pittsburgh speech), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Polish. **Concept in one line:** how people shine, play and take pleasure (romance, children, fame, celebration, gambling, sport ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Central Appalachia's coalfield counties against Northern Appalachia's mill towns: check whether company bands, ballfields and union picnics left different leisure institutions from the parish and lodge halls of the Monongahela Valley.
- Southern Appalachia's tourist corridor (Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Asheville): check which shows are staged for visitors and which for residents.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Band's fall fair, stickball and arts market as the Band presents them, since outsiders long described its dances.
- Black Appalachians in coal camps and in Pittsburgh: check separate dance halls, ball clubs and church events, since segregation split the domain's venues.
- Amish and Mennonite districts of Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania: check which pleasures each church district's rules address, through Anabaptist-studies sources, not tourism material.
- Latino newcomers in north Georgia and western North Carolina: check which celebrations came with them and which formed locally.
- Families who moved to Cincinnati, Dayton, Detroit and Chicago (diaspora, comparison only): check whether fiddle contests, gospel singings and ball leagues were rebuilt there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music, dance and festival history; mixed for gambling and romance; thin for county leisure measures. No scope file exists yet: the nation's exact lands are still to be settled, so this sign is written for the Appalachian region of the United States.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for game and fair terms; Spanish for the newer towns; Polish or Slovak for lodge and parish dance records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Cherokee dances and songs are ceremonial; collector-era festivals were often staged from outside, so record who staged what.
- **Held for review:** no domain-level pointer was set aside; gambling and sport sources still need a county-coverage check.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull the ATUS state leisure tables for West Virginia, Kentucky and Pennsylvania, to learn what can be measured.
2. Read Doddridge's chapters on frontier sports and weddings beside Bartram's Cherokee passages, as the domain's two oldest outside descriptions.
3. Browse the Coal River Folklife Project's online collection for "ramp" and "hunting" recordings.
4. Compare the fair's published programs across several decades for which events (stickball, crafts, rides) stay and which come and go.
5. Ask the Heinz History Center about records of ethnic athletic clubs.
