# Appalachia: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Polish, Slovak. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Settlement-school craft centers (Hindman, Pine Mountain, John C. Campbell Folk School) against home craft no school touched: check whose standards of "good work" each followed, since founders often came from outside.
- Eastern Band of Cherokee makers: check how designs, materials (rivercane, white oak, bloodroot dyes) and teaching lines pass, from the makers' own accounts.
- Black Appalachian musicians and potters: check credit and authorship in instrument styles and stoneware, which outside histories have often assigned elsewhere.
- Pennsylvania German and Mennonite communities of the Shenandoah Valley and southwestern Pennsylvania: check where decorative making sat beside church rules on adornment.
- Polish, Slovak, Rusyn and Hungarian parishes of the mill towns: check Easter-egg writing, embroidery and church carving as kept or changed after migration.
- Latino newcomers in western North Carolina and north Georgia: check which crafts (piñatas, dressmaking, altars) are practiced and sold, and through what outlets.
- Northern Appalachian industrial towns against Central Appalachian coal camps: check where invention was called "creative" (patents, shops) and where it was called "making do".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for craft revival and ballad scholarship; mixed for living makers' incomes; thin for everyday improvising, which leaves few records.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for material and design terms; German script for fraktur; Polish and Slovak for parish craft guilds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** certain Cherokee designs and songs belong to families or ceremony; collectors' and dealers' records are outside accounts and should be read as such.
- **Held for review:** no craft or making source was withheld; the dealers' folk-art catalogs are kept only as outside accounts.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Eaton's chapter list and Becker's introduction together, to set the craft revival's insider and outsider voices side by side.
2. Ask the Southern Highland Craft Guild how its membership jury defines acceptable new work.
3. Search the Qualla Arts and Crafts Mutual's published material for "rivercane" and "double weave".
4. Compare variants of one Child ballad across Sharp's mountain singers.
5. Pull USPTO county patent counts for a Pittsburgh-area county and an eastern Kentucky county.
