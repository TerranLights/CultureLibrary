# Appalachia: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian, Pittsburgh), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Old Regular, Primitive and Holiness congregations against mainline town churches: check where outward display of feeling in worship is expected and where restraint is the norm.
- Amish, Mennonite and Brethren communities: check how each district's dress rules shape what a member may show, and what members say about it.
- Black Appalachians, including Affrilachian writers and the coal-camp communities of Harlan and McDowell counties: check which forms of expression they claim and how they name themselves.
- LGBTQ Appalachians in small towns and in Asheville, Knoxville and Pittsburgh: check rural against urban accounts of being open.
- Pittsburgh's speech community: check how local speech is displayed on shirts and signs, against how it is corrected in schools.
- Eastern Band of Cherokee speakers and learners: check where the language is used in public life as a statement, and where it is kept to the home.
- Latino newcomers: check how expression moves between Spanish and English across generations in the poultry towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in worship studies and dialect research; mixed for LGBTQ and Latino voices (growing, mostly oral history); thin in surveys at county level.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee (syllabary) for the *Phoenix* and letters; Pennsylvania German for Old Order terms; Spanish for songs and radio.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** LGBTQ narrators may have asked for anonymity; Cherokee letters may hold family matters; worship recordings should be used as the congregations allow.
- **Held for review:** no self-expression pointer was withheld; the Folkways recording's consent terms still need reading before quotation.
