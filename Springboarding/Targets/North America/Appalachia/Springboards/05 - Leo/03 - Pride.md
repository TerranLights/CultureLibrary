# Appalachia: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian, Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania German, Cherokee, Spanish, Slovak, Italian. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Central Appalachian coal counties: check pride in mining work against the loss of that work, through union and family sources, not outside commentary.
- Pittsburgh and the Monongahela mill towns: check how Slovak, Polish, Hungarian, Rusyn and Italian parishes marked pride in origin against pride in the new city.
- Melungeon families of Hancock County, Tennessee, and nearby Virginia: check how a once-stigmatized name came to be claimed, and who in the families does not claim it.
- Amish, Mennonite and Brethren districts: check how the humility teaching shapes what members may show about their work.
- Black Appalachians in coal camps and in Affrilachian writing: check which symbols (John Henry, the Black coal miner) they hold up, and why.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check pride in the syllabary, the language revival and the land as the Band states it.
- Mexican and Central American families: check which independence days and home-state symbols are displayed publicly, and by whom, in the poultry and carpet towns.
- Diaspora households in Dayton, Columbus and Detroit (comparison only): check heritage clubs and homecoming trips as pride kept from afar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in songs, memoirs and heritage organizations; mixed for surveys (state-level, rarely regional); thin for small-town LGBTQ events before about 2015.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for Old Order teaching; Slovak and Italian for heritage-society papers; Spanish for newer festivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Melungeon origin claims and DNA results are disputed and personal; supremacist uses of "heritage pride" exist and should be identified as such, not mixed with heritage societies.
- **Held for review:** no pride pointer was withheld; the Melungeon origin literature stays in with a warning attached.
