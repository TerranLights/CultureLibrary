# Appalachia: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Spanish, Welsh, Polish, Hungarian. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Tradition-bearers in western North Carolina and Kentucky against those in Northern Appalachia: check whether state award programs reach the mill-town ethnic arts at all.
- Black Appalachians of Lynch, Benham and McDowell County: check who records their histories now, and what recognition they ask for.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check its own honors (titles, place names, elders' recognition) against outside awards given to Cherokee makers.
- Welsh, Polish and Hungarian coal-town communities: check fraternal-society honors and competition prizes in their own languages.
- Storytelling families of Beech Mountain and Wise County: check how tellers and their descendants speak about credit for the tales.
- Latino newcomers: check certificates, county proclamations and church honors that mark community members.
- Women in mining and union households: check whether union honors and pins reached women miners and auxiliaries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal honors (lists are public); mixed for credit in folklore collections; thin for everyday acknowledgment, which sits in weeklies and family papers.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for eisteddfod reports; Cherokee for titles; Polish and Hungarian for lodge records; German for older Moravian memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Moravian memoirs and obituaries name private families; storytelling families may dispute how their tales were credited.
- **Held for review:** no recognition pointer was set aside; the Beloved Man and Beloved Woman conferrals need dates confirmed before use.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the NEA Heritage Fellowships list by state (Kentucky, West Virginia, Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee) and note art forms.
2. Read Perdue's introduction on tellers and editors beside Chase's preface.
3. Ask the Moravian Archives in Winston-Salem for an English finding aid to the memoirs.
4. Read the Board on Geographic Names' Kuwohi decision and the Band's petition statements.
5. Run "eisteddfod" in Chronicling America, limited to Pennsylvania, 1880–1920.
