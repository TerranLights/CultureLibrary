# Appalachia: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Spanish, Italian. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Central Appalachian coal counties, the most photographed by outsiders: check residents' own image-making against the national press images of the 1960s and 2000s.
- Pittsburgh and the Monongahela mill towns: check how ethnic parishes and Black neighborhoods documented themselves through their own newspapers and photographers.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check where the Band chooses to be visible to tourists (drama, crafts, the fair) and what it keeps for community events only.
- Melungeon families: check how being classified by census-takers and courts compares with families' own self-description.
- LGBTQ Appalachians: check the difference between visibility in Asheville or Pittsburgh and in small coal and farm towns.
- Latino newcomers in poultry and horticulture towns: check undercount and visibility in local institutions (schools, churches, local news).
- Older adults living alone in hollows and small towns: check visiting customs and agency outreach, where being unseen is a risk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich, because outside image-making about the region is so heavily documented; the region's own image-making is a smaller, growing body.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for newer communities; Cherokee for greetings and community events; Italian for evil-eye beliefs in older parishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** families pictured in poverty photographs and their descendants; Melungeon classification records touch racial law (law aspects go to Libra and Capricorn); queer narrators' anonymity.
- **Held for review:** nothing about visibility was set aside; the 1964 Inez follow-ups need sourcing before any family is named.
