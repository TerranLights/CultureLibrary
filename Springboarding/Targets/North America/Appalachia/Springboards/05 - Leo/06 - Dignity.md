# Appalachia: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Spanish, Slovak, Polish, Arabic; Maya languages for some Guatemalan workers. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coal families of southern West Virginia and eastern Kentucky: check how union, church and family sources speak of dignity in work, injury and job loss.
- Steel and mill families of the Monongahela Valley: check Slovak, Hungarian, Polish and Black workers' records separately, since their treatment differed by origin.
- Latino and Guatemalan Maya workers in poultry and horticulture towns: check the terms of respect they use in Spanish and in Maya languages, through worker-center and church sources.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Band's own statements on repatriation and on the dead.
- Lebanese and Syrian families of Charleston and Birmingham: check how peddling and shopkeeping families spoke of honest work and respect, in Arabic and English church records.
- Older adults in remote hollows and small towns: check home-care and hospice records for how dignity in dying is handled.
- People receiving charity from outside groups: check what they say, not only what the groups report.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor and disaster studies; mixed for health care and charity (mostly from providers); thin for the Latino, Arab and Maya communities' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and a Maya language for Morganton workers; Slovak and Polish for union and parish papers; Arabic for older Lebanese and Syrian records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** patients, disaster survivors and aid recipients; ancestral remains (follow the Eastern Band's lead, not museum records); disability benefit data must stay at aggregate level.
- **Held for review:** nothing on dignity was withheld; poverty imagery from free-clinic coverage is kept only as an object of critique.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Library of Congress life histories for Kentucky, West Virginia and Tennessee interviews with miners and their wives.
2. Ask a state archive for a county court order book with an index to "poor" or "paupers" (for example a southwest Virginia county).
3. Read Erikson's chapters on communality and trauma.
4. Search Fink's index for "respect" and "dignidad".
5. Pull SSA county tables for three coal counties and three Northern Appalachian counties.
