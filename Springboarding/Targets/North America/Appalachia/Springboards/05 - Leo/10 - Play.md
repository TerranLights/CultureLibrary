# Appalachia: Play (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Polish, Slovak. **Concept in one line:** activity done for its own sake, set apart from ordinary life by rules or make-believe: games, sport as play, pretending, joking, toying.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Central Appalachia's coal counties: check company-built ballfields and union picnics against home-made games recorded by collectors.
- Northern Appalachia's mill towns and Pittsburgh: check ethnic gymnastic clubs, Black sandlot leagues and parish leagues side by side.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check stickball and other games as the Band plays and explains them today, beside Mooney's 1890 account.
- Church families in strict congregations: check which games (cards, dancing, games of chance) were refused, which were allowed in their place, and how this has changed.
- Old Order Amish youth: check leisure during *Rumspringa* through scholars with community access, not through television portrayals.
- Latino newcomers in poultry and carpet towns: check soccer leagues, *lotería* and children's games brought from Mexico and Central America.
- Mountain-born households in Midwestern cities (diaspora, comparison only): check softball leagues and card games kept up there.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklore collections of games and tales; mixed for adult play (racing, hunting, marbles); thin for Latino and newer play forms.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for game and tale terms; Spanish for leagues; Polish, Slovak and Czech for gymnastic societies' papers; Pennsylvania German for Amish youth terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** parts of Cherokee ball-game preparation involve ceremony and medicine and are not for outside use; Amish youth are minors, and their communities are sensitive to outside study.
- **Held for review:** nothing about play was withheld; televised portrayals of Amish youth are left out as unreliable rather than under a standing rule.
