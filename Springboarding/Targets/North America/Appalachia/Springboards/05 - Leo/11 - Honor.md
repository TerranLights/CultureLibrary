# Appalachia: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Italian, Arabic, Hungarian. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Tug Fork and Big Sandy valleys: check family and local accounts of feuds against national press accounts, since the outside image of "feuding" grew from coverage there.
- The Upper Cumberland and the Cumberland Plateau: check how oral histories explain violence and honor, and how they differ from psychology's state-level claims.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check earned titles, women's standing and the honor of place in the Band's own terms.
- Black Appalachians: check lodges, churches and veterans' groups as keepers of a separate honor record under segregation.
- Italian, Hungarian and Slavic mining and steel families: check how the dead of mine and mill disasters are honored, in the communities' own languages.
- Lebanese and Syrian families and Latino newcomers: check terms of family honor in Arabic and Spanish without assuming a pattern from elsewhere.
- Old Order Amish and Mennonite districts: check how nonresistance shapes ideas of honor and the answer to insult.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich, but much of it on feuds and violence; mixed for everyday honor (keeping one's word, honoring the dead); thin for Latino and Arab communities.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for titles and place terms; Italian, Hungarian and Slovak for disaster memorials and lodge records; Arabic and Spanish for family terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feud descendants are living families; violence and gender-based control must be researched in the words of those affected; legal sides of feuds belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no honor source was withheld; psychology's state rankings remain in, labeled as a disputed measure.
