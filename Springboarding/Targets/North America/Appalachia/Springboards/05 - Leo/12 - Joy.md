# Appalachia: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Appalachian), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Croatian, Polish, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Shape-note singers in north Alabama and north Georgia: check how singing communities describe the day's gladness against how they describe sorrow in the same songs.
- The Primitive Baptist Universalists, Old Regular Baptists and Pentecostal congregations: check how each speaks of joy in worship and how outward it is allowed to be.
- Fiddle and string-band communities of the Blue Ridge and southern West Virginia: check jam sessions as joy, through the players' accounts.
- Pittsburgh's Slavic, Italian and Croatian communities: check park days, polka dances and tamburica events as group joy.
- Black Appalachians in Chattanooga, Knoxville and the coal camps: check celebrations, music and homecomings through the communities' own institutions.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check social dances and festivals as the Band shares them, with ceremonial events left to the Band.
- Latino newcomers: check patron-saint feasts and family celebrations as parishes and families describe them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music and worship joy; mixed for well-being measures (state-level, and weighted toward distress); thin for newer communities' celebrations.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for social-dance terms; Croatian, Polish and Slovak for park-day and dance records; Spanish for parish feasts; Arabic for older church festivals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee ceremonial dances (such as the Green Corn ceremony) are not for outside study; data on despair and overdose must not stand in for the region's own account of joy.
- **Held for review:** no source on gladness or celebration had to be withheld; the despair statistics are kept as context only.
