# Appalachia: Work and service, the domain and its folkways (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with Scots, Ulster and English layers), Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the domain of work, service, the daily round and health: what counts as good work and a well-kept life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** check company-town records against miners' accounts, since the shift may have set the day apart from farm counties.
- **Northern Appalachia** (the mill towns, Appalachian Ohio, the Southern Tier): check mill schedules and parish calendars to separate an industrial week from a farm one.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check how farm, craft and tourism jobs combine within one household.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own publications on the seasonal round first.
- **Black Appalachians** (Lynch, Kentucky; McDowell County, West Virginia): check whether employer records and community memory describe the same working day.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh** families: check parish and lodge records for the week's rhythm of work, feast and rest.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** check church rules on work hours against neighbors' farm calendars.
- **Latino and Maya newcomers** (Gainesville and Dalton, Georgia; Morganton, North Carolina): check shift and H-2A visa-season schedules as a separate yearly round.
- **Melungeon families:** sources thin; start with the Melungeon Heritage Association (*check*).
- **Out-migrants** in Detroit, Cincinnati and Chicago: comparison only, through Harriette Arnow's *The Dollmaker* (1954, *check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for coal and steel history; mixed for the daily round (national time-use surveys are too thin for counties); thin for Melungeon and Rusyn communities.
- **Languages needed:** German script for the Moravian diaries' originals; Cherokee for the Band's own seasonal round; Pennsylvania German for Amish farm calendars; Spanish, and possibly Q'anjob'al (*check*), for poultry-town shift workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** label every reformer's or journalist's account of the region's work habits as outside, and read the region's own archives first. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled: this sheet is written for the Appalachian region of the United States, with ARC's county list named only as one source's definition. Labor law and the old statute road duty go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** every pointer on chores, shifts and the farm year passed the standing rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Library of Congress, "American Life Histories": search "farm" and "mine" by state; list interviews from mountain counties.
2. Read the index of *Records of the Moravians in North Carolina* for "work", "harvest" and "Sabbath".

4. Ask the Cherokee Historical Association which seasonal tasks it demonstrates and on whose advice.
5. Search obituaries for "worked in the mines", "retired from the mill" and "a hard worker" by county; list the kinds of work named.
