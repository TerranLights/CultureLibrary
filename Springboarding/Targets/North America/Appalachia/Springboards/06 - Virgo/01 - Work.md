# Appalachia: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Harlan and the union counties against nonunion operations:** check how union membership changed the language people use for a fair day's work.
- **The Monongahela Valley and other steel towns after the 1980s closures:** check oral histories for how laid-off workers describe idleness they did not choose.
- **Blue Ridge and Smoky Mountain farm counties:** check how ginseng, tobacco, Christmas-tree and tourist jobs are combined across a year.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's records for logging and tourism wage work beside the outside histories.
- **Black miners recruited from the Deep South and Virginia:** check labor agents' records and family accounts of why they came.
- **Women in mines and mills:** check what CEP files and strike histories show about who counted housework as work.
- **Slovak, Polish, Italian and Welsh workers:** check fraternal benefit societies' papers for how members described their jobs.
- **Amish shops and sawmills:** check how church limits on machinery shape which trades a family can take up.
- **Latino workers in Dalton and the poultry towns:** check how carpet work and poultry work are spoken of differently.
- **Out-migrants to Midwestern auto plants:** comparison only; Chad Berry, *Southern Migrants, Northern Exiles* (2000, *check*).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for coal and steel; mixed for farm and informal cash work (ginseng digging leaves few records); thin for Melungeon and Rusyn work histories.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for miners' and millhands' own terms for the job; Slovak, Polish, Italian and Hungarian for benefit-society papers on members' trades; Cherokee for Band records of its work enterprises; Pennsylvania German; Spanish for carpet and poultry workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the outside image of mountain idleness is a stereotype with its own literature (see Henry Shapiro, *Appalachia on Our Mind*, 1978, *check*); treat idleness words as data on speech, never as findings. Labor law and mine-safety statutes go to Libra; black lung goes to Health.
- **Held for review:** no work-history source was set aside here.
