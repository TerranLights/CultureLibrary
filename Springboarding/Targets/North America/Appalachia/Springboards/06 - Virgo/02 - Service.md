# Appalachia: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Regular, Primitive, Free Will and Missionary Baptists, Methodists and Holiness churches:** check whether preachers are paid and how footwashing is held, since those choices may shape what "serving" means in each.
- **Tourist counties** (Sevier County, Tennessee; Asheville; the Blue Ridge Parkway towns): check hospitality workers' own accounts of serving visitors.
- **Union coal counties:** check UMWA locals' strike kitchens and relief funds as service among miners.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band separates service to the tribe from service in the U.S. armed forces.
- **Black Appalachians:** check church auxiliary and lodge records for who served whom, inside and outside the community.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** check whether mutual aid among members and service to outsiders are run separately.
- **Slovak, Rusyn and Polish fraternal societies** (for example the First Catholic Slovak Union, *check*): check their sick and burial benefits as members' service to members.
- **Latino parishes in the poultry and tree-farm towns:** check Catholic dioceses' Hispanic ministry offices for volunteer networks.
- **Melungeon families:** little written; check the Melungeon Heritage Association's gatherings for service roles.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; the Urban Appalachian Council (Cincinnati, *check*) served migrants from the mountains.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for organized and outside-led service (missions, settlement schools, nursing, VISTA); mixed for neighborly and church service; thin for domestic service by Black women in mountain towns.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for church and neighborly service terms; Slovak, Rusyn and Polish to read fraternal societies' benefit rolls; Cherokee for veterans' and community ceremonies; Pennsylvania German for Anabaptist relief writing; Spanish for parish volunteer networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** much service writing comes from outside workers who saw the region as a field of need; keep their framing apart from residents' accounts. The draft and veterans' benefit law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no service source was held back; Catholic, Baptist and Anabaptist sources are all usable.
