# Appalachia: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Pennsylvania German, Spanish, Cherokee. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield counties:** check how disabled and retired miners describe life after work in their own interviews, separate from benefit statistics.
- **Monongahela Valley and Ohio Valley mill towns:** check union-hall and church records for roles men took up after the shutdowns.
- **Southern Appalachian farm families:** check memoirs for the age at which a child's help was expected.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how Snowbird and the Qualla Boundary communities name the tasks given to first-language elders.
- **Black families of Lynch and other coal towns:** check return migrants' accounts of the tasks given to elders and young people.
- **Amish settlements:** check settlement newsletters for how the move into the grandparents' house changes a person's farm and church work.
- **Latino families in the poultry towns:** check school and clinic reports on children interpreting for parents; sources still scarce.
- **Italian, Hungarian and Slovak households in coal and steel towns:** check boardinghouse records for wives' paid roles keeping boarders.
- **Melungeon families:** little written; ask the Melungeon Heritage Association for family papers.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; check how migration studies describe the move from farm hands to factory hands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's farm work and thrift in memoir; mixed for elders and the disabled; thin for newer Latino families.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for words of being "able" or "a help"; Cherokee to hear how elders' tasks are named; Pennsylvania German for the language of old age on the farm; Spanish for children's help in immigrant homes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability, benefits and kinship care in the region are the stuff of outside caricature; treat county benefit figures as context only, and read Gazaway beside the responses to her. Suicide data, if used, need great care. Benefits law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no practical-skills or eldercare pointer had to be withheld for this concept.
