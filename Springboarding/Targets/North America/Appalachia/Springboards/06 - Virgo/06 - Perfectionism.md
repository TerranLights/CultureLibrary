# Appalachia: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Pennsylvania German, Cherokee, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chairmakers, basket makers and potters** in eastern Kentucky and the Blue Ridge: check whether makers' standards differ from those of the guilds and shops that sell their work.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check which weaving rules the Band's weavers share in public before writing about deliberate choices in a piece.
- **Holiness and Pentecostal congregations against Baptist ones:** check how each speaks of a perfected life and outward appearance.
- **Amish settlements:** check members' own words on plainness and on the "humility block" story.
- **West Virginia and Southern Tier glass towns** (Belgian, French and Italian glassworkers): check firms' records for how "seconds" were sorted and sold.
- **Old-time and bluegrass musicians:** check whether Galax-style judging and jam-session norms value exactness differently.
- **Black quilters and craftspeople:** check state quilt surveys for how Black makers were recorded; likely underrepresented.
- **Latino families:** check how *quinceañera* preparations in north Georgia and western North Carolina handle the demand for a flawless day; sources scarce.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; check whether craft standards traveled with them to Midwest cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for craft standards and judged music; mixed for religious perfection; thin for perfectionism as distress (no regional measures known).
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for makers' praise and fault words; Cherokee for weavers' technical terms; Pennsylvania German for plainness and pride; French and Italian for glassworkers' shop papers; Spanish for *quinceañera* planning.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee makers may keep some design knowledge within families; ask first. The idea of mountain craft as "pure" tradition was partly built by outside sellers, so separate makers' standards from the market's. Clinical perfectionism needs care.
- **Held for review:** no craft, music or church source on exacting standards was withheld.
