# Appalachia: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (including Pittsburgh speech), Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, as feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Union and nonunion coal counties:** check blacklist and eviction records to see where openly criticizing an operator carried a cost.
- **The region's writers and scholars against outside journalists:** check how each side's criticism of the other is framed.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the *Cherokee One Feather*'s opinion pages and tribal council records for how members criticize their own leaders.
- **Black Appalachians:** check Affrilachian writers' essays for criticism aimed both outward and within the region.
- **Old Regular and Primitive Baptists:** check church minute books for who was "churched" and for which faults.
- **Amish settlements:** check members' and former members' accounts separately for confession and shunning.
- **Slovak, Polish and Rusyn communities in western Pennsylvania:** check ethnic-press opinion columns on mill owners and parish disputes.
- **Latino residents in north Georgia and western North Carolina:** check Spanish-language radio call-in programs for how complaints are aired.
- **Melungeon families:** check how they answered sensational magazine features of the 1940s (*check* titles).
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; the *Hillbilly Elegy* debate arose partly from an out-migrant family's story.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for criticism of outside images and of coal; mixed for criticism inside families and churches; thin for newer Latino communities.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English and Pittsburgh speech for teasing and reproach; Cherokee to read the 1828 editorials in the syllabary; Pennsylvania German for church-discipline terms; Slovak, Polish and Rusyn for ethnic-press opinion columns; Spanish for call-in radio.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** much writing about the region is itself the object of the region's criticism; cite it only alongside the replies. Church discipline names individuals; keep family names out of public notes. Libel and press law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no critic, satirist or responding essay was held back for this concept.
