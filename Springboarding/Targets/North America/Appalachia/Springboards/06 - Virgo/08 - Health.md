# Appalachia: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee, Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield families:** check whether the dust, the company doctor and the union fund appear together in health memories, since mine work may have shaped who gave care.
- **Pittsburgh's mill towns and the Ohio Valley:** check steelworkers' injury and pollution memories (the 1948 Donora smog) against public-health records.
- **Southern Appalachian farm counties:** check the place of herb healers and granny women against county doctors in oral histories.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check what the Band publishes on wellness before reading any outside record of Cherokee healing.
- **Black Appalachians in coal towns:** check segregated hospital and company-doctor records, which are scattered.
- **Italian, Hungarian and Slovak mining families:** check mutual-benefit society records for sick pay and burial funds.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** check how families combine physicians, chiropractors, herbal remedies and community funds for hospital bills.
- **German-descended families of Appalachian Pennsylvania:** check whether *Braucherei* is still practiced or only remembered.
- **Latino and Maya farm and poultry workers:** check outreach-worker reports for injuries on the line and *susto* or *nervios* as patients name them.
- **Melungeon families:** check popular claims about inherited illness in these families against medical literature and the families' own accounts; sources mixed.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; check urban Appalachian health studies in Cincinnati and Dayton.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for occupational disease, overdose and county mortality; mixed for folk healing; thin for Black, Melungeon and Rusyn communities' health histories.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for illness idioms such as "nerves"; Cherokee for healing texts in the syllabary (with permission); Pennsylvania German for charm books; Italian, Hungarian and Slovak for sick-benefit records; Spanish, and possibly Mayan languages, for patients' own illness names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee medicine knowledge is restricted; ask the Band. Overdose and disease statistics are easily turned into caricature; use them only as context. Health law and black lung benefits law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** no healer, clinic or health dataset was held back here.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Cavender (2003); list the illness terms and healers he names.
2. NIOSH website: download the coal workers' surveillance summary by state.
3. Search the Appalachian Oral History Project index for "midwife", "doctor" and "remedy".
4. ARC website: download "Health Disparities in Appalachia" and note its county definitions.
5. Ask the Eastern Band's health offices which wellness materials are public.
