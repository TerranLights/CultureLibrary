# Appalachia: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (records), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appalachian English (early records) | *improvement* (on a claim) | a clearing and cabin that backed a settler's claim to land | in land entries and deeds; *check* |
| Appalachian English | *better yourself*; *make something of yourself* | to rise by effort | search memoirs and commencement speeches |
| Appalachian English | *fix up*; *build on* | to repair; to add rooms | home-improvement talk |
| Outside reform vocabulary | *uplift*; *backward*; *our contemporary ancestors* | missionaries' and writers' labels | outside terms, to be studied as labels (the last from an 1899 magazine essay, *check*) |
| Pennsylvania German | *Fortschritt* | progress | *check* use among Old Order groups |
| Spanish | *salir adelante*; *superarse* | to get ahead; to better oneself | Mexican and Central American usage |

English keywords: settlement schools Appalachia; Moonlight Schools; TVA removal; Shenandoah National Park displacement; Pittsburgh Renaissance Hill District; Highlander Folk School; Appalachian Development Highway System.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Families displaced for parks and dams** (Shenandoah, the Smokies, Norris and Fontana): check their letters and interviews against the agencies' reports, since the same project is told as progress and as loss.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band tells the "civilization" era and boarding schools, and its own renewal programs.
- **Black Pittsburgh and Black coal towns:** check how urban renewal and school "betterment" are told by residents and their writers.
- **Melungeon families:** check the Vardy school's records and family memories side by side.
- **Settlement-school students** (Hindman, Pine Mountain): check former students' letters for what they themselves took as betterment.
- **Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Rusyn families:** check Americanization classes and parish schools for how immigrants sorted wanted and unwanted change.
- **Amish settlements:** check how church leaders decide which new tools count as improvement and which are refused.
- **Latino newcomers:** check adult English classes and church programs for how *salir adelante* is pursued in mountain towns.
- **Coalfield towns after the companies sold their houses:** check how new owners improved and rebuilt former company housing.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only; check whether moving north was described as bettering oneself or as exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for reform movements, displacement and federal programs; mixed for personal self-improvement; thin for recovery programs and Latino adult education.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for the talk of bettering oneself; German for the Moravian Springplace diaries; Cherokee for that era's own answers to the "civilization" program; Slovak, Polish and Hungarian for Americanization-class materials; Spanish for adult English programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** improvement here was often imposed from outside, on Native, Black, Melungeon and poor white families; tell each project first through those it was done to. Removal and eminent domain law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** a source bearing on getting ahead was set aside under the standing rule and is held only as a category: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
