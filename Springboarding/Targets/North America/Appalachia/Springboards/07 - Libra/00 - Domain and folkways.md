# Appalachia: Partnership and justice, the domain and its folkways (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with its Scots, Ulster and English layers), Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the domain of partnership and justice: what counts as a fair bargain and a good partnership, from marriage and the handshake deal to open enemies, the settling of quarrels and the law as written.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** check how often labor contracts and mineral deeds fill local dockets, against farm counties nearby.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh and the mill towns, the Ohio Valley, the Southern Tier): check union contracts and lodge bylaws as written partnerships.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and the Smokies:** check trade-day and church records for deals and quarrels where farms set the economy.
- **Shenandoah Valley:** check where Presbyterian sessions, German church councils and the county court overlapped as judging bodies.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** the Band's code and court first; pre-removal laws as background.
- **Black Appalachians** (Lynch, Kentucky; McDowell County, West Virginia; Knoxville and Asheville): check marriage and property records for the years state law treated them apart.
- **Melungeon families** (Hancock County, Tennessee; Wise and Lee Counties, Virginia): check how county records wrote their status; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh** communities: check fraternal society bylaws as contracts among members.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements of Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania: check which disputes church order keeps out of court.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *compadrazgo* and hometown associations as bonds between equals.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): urban Appalachian councils in Cincinnati and Dayton may show how migrants kept partnerships and settled quarrels away from home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court records, labor and mineral law; mixed for marriage customs; thin for Melungeon, Rusyn and Latino dispute settlement.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the Band's code and the *Phoenix* syllabary columns; German script for Valley church councils; Spanish for parish registers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feud histories name living families' ancestors; parish registers hold private data. Outside accounts of mountain feuding and marriage are many: label each as outside and read the region's scholars first. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled: this sheet is written for the Appalachian region of the United States; the Appalachian Regional Commission's county list may be named as one source's definition, never as the nation's units.
- **Held for review:** nothing in this overview of partnership and justice was set aside under the standing rules.
