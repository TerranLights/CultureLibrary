# Appalachia: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), German and Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** compare criminal dockets with mine-safety prosecutions to see which harms were charged as crimes.
- **Northern Appalachia:** check steel-strike and anthracite trials (Homestead; Schuylkill County) against coalfield ones farther south.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** set murder ballads beside the trial records they come from, case by case.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** read the Band's code against Reid's account of the older law, as two separate legal orders.
- **Black Appalachians:** check court and lynching records by county (Chattanooga, Forsyth County, McDowell County) for who was tried, by whom.
- **Melungeon families:** search county voting and court records in Hawkins and Hancock Counties, Tennessee, for prosecutions touching their status; sources thin.
- **Italian, Slovak and Hungarian miners:** check consular and immigrant-press coverage of trials involving their members.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** check when a case left church discipline for a state or federal court.
- **Latino newcomers:** compare 287(g) counties with neighbors that did not sign.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): Detroit and Cincinnati court coverage of mountain-born defendants shows how the region's people were judged away from it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for famous trials and federal cases; mixed for ordinary county criminal dockets (many unindexed or burned in courthouse fires); thin for Melungeon and Rusyn cases.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the 1820s law columns; German script for Moravian and Lutheran disciplinary records; Spanish for newcomers' accounts of enforcement.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching and expulsion records name victims and perpetrators whose descendants live nearby; juvenile records are sealed. Outside coverage of mountain murders often fed the region's image: label it as outside.
- **Held for review:** no justice pointer was set aside; restorative and legitimacy questions go to Capricorn.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *State v. Dula* in the North Carolina Reports; list the grounds of appeal.
2. Read *United States v. Shipp* (1906 and 1909 opinions); note the Court's charge against the sheriff.
3. Library of Virginia catalog: search "oyer and terminer" with Augusta, Botetourt or Washington County.
4. Locate the Cherokee Code's criminal chapters; list offense classes and sentences.
5. ICE 287(g) list: filter by Georgia, North Carolina and Tennessee; mark the counties in mountain regions.
