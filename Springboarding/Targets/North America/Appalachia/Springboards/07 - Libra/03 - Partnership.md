# Appalachia: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** compare union contracts with company leases as two kinds of written partnership.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the Monongahela mill towns, the Southern Tier): check parish marriage records of Polish, Slovak, Rusyn and Italian families for marriage within and across groups.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and the Smokies:** check dance-team rules and marriage bonds for courtship partnerships.
- **Cumberland Plateau and the Shenandoah Valley:** check whether German and Scots-Irish families' marriages crossed church lines in early records.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** read the Band's domestic relations law separately from the older clan rules in Payne.
- **Black Appalachians:** check cohabitation registers and later marriage records in Asheville, Knoxville and the coal camps.
- **Melungeon and Monacan families:** check the Virginia Bureau of Vital Statistics' files from the 1920s for the marriages it challenged.
- **Lenape, Shawnee and Seneca histories:** check alliance treaties as their own written partnerships.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** compare marriage within a church district with business partnerships across districts.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *compadrazgo* and hometown-club membership as partnerships beyond kin.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): marriages made in Midwestern cities between migrants and other groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage law and labor contracts; mixed for courtship customs; thin for Rusyn and Hungarian marriage customs in the coalfields (check parish records and fraternal newspapers).
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for clan terms and any syllabary records; Pennsylvania German for Amish family terms; Polish, Slovak and Rusyn for parish registers; Spanish for hometown-club charters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1920s Virginia vital-statistics files are a record of harm to Monacan and Melungeon families; parish and tribal marriage records are private. Same-sex marriage cases name living people.
- **Held for review:** the marriage, treaty and contract pointers all passed; none was held.
