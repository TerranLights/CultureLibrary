# Appalachia: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), Seneca and Shawnee (historical records), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** check strike settlements for who carried terms between miners and operators: governors, clergy or federal mediators.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the Allegheny country, the Southern Tier): compare eighteenth-century Seneca councils with twentieth-century city deal-making.
- **East Tennessee and the Holston Valley:** read Franklin's and the Cherokee's records of the same talks side by side.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** read the Band's own accounts of the joint councils and the compact first.
- **Shawnee, Lenape and Seneca histories:** search each nation's present-day publications for its account of the Ohio Valley treaties.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how Black churches and NAACP branches in McDowell County and Knoxville negotiated with officials and employers.
- **Melungeon families:** check how the Melungeon Heritage Association approached historians and DNA researchers; sources thin.
- **Italian, Hungarian and Slovak** miners: check consular agents' and priests' roles as go-betweens in mine disasters and strikes.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** compare Steering Committee agreements in Ohio and Pennsylvania.
- **Latino newcomers:** check mobile-consulate visits and school liaison programs in Dalton and Gainesville, Georgia.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): how urban Appalachian advocacy groups bargained with city governments.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties and state-level dealings; mixed for everyday tact (dialect studies exist, but few on politeness as such); thin for Seneca and Shawnee diplomacy told in their own present-day voices within the region.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for council terms; Seneca and Shawnee for treaty speakers' names and terms, through each nation's language programs; Pennsylvania German for Amish negotiators' own accounts; Spanish for consular and school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** treaty minutes were kept by one side; read them against each nation's own account. The extradition case touches a feud whose descendants still speak publicly about it.
- **Held for review:** no treaty, council or everyday-tact pointer was held back.
