# Appalachia: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Seneca (historical records), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** compare company benefits with lodge and union aid to see which help carried a return owed.
- **Northern Appalachia** (the mill towns, the Southern Tier, Appalachian Ohio): check philanthropy from mill owners against workers' own mutual aid.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and the Cumberland Plateau:** check store ledgers, labor swaps and oral histories for how farm families kept even.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** read the Band's own descriptions of community labor before Gulick's.
- **Seneca Nation:** read the nation's account of the treaty cloth and Kinzua first.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare lodge benefits with church help in coal camps.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh** communities: compare fraternal societies' terms.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** compare Amish Aid plans and barn raisings across settlements.
- **Latino newcomers:** check *tandas* and remittance obligations; data mostly oral.
- **Melungeon families:** no reciprocity-specific source known; start with Hancock County church records.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): money and help sent home to mountain counties from Ohio and Michigan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties, statutes and fraternal records; mixed for everyday exchange (strong in memoirs, scattered elsewhere); thin for *tandas* and Black burial societies.
- **Languages needed:** Seneca for the treaty rite's own terms, through the Seneca Nation's language program; Rusyn, Slovak and Hungarian for society ledgers; Spanish for *tanda* practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** benefit ledgers and store accounts name families and debts; *tandas* are informal and private. Seneca and Cherokee ceremonies are theirs to describe.
- **Held for review:** the gift, labor-swap and mutual-aid pointers all cleared review.
