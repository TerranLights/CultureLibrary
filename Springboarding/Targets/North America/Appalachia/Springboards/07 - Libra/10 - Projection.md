# Appalachia: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), Pennsylvania German, Slovak and Polish, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties:** compare who officials blamed in strikes (outsiders, foreigners, Reds) with who organized them.
- **Northern Appalachia** (the anthracite fields, Pittsburgh, the steel towns): check press labels for Slovak, Polish, Hungarian, Rusyn and Italian workers against their own newspapers.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** compare outside writers' images with local writers' replies, title by title.
- **Shenandoah Valley:** trace eugenic commitments through Western State Hospital by home county.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** read what the Band chooses to say about *sgili* and harm; do not go past it.
- **Black Appalachians:** compare expulsion and lynching coverage with Black newspapers (for example *The McDowell Times*, *check*).
- **Melungeon and Monacan families:** check how outside studies labeled them, and their own associations' answers.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** compare outside "Amish" images with Amish-written sources.
- **Latino newcomers:** compare coverage of HB 56 in English and Spanish media.
- **Out-migrants:** comparison only, through the "briar" label in Cincinnati and Dayton.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for outside images and legal cases; mixed for images the region holds of others; thin for Cherokee and Pennsylvania German accounts of hidden harm, which are partly private.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for any Band teaching on harm; Pennsylvania German for powwow texts; Slovak and Polish for the immigrant press after Lattimer; Spanish for coverage of HB 56.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the eugenic study and the sterilization records harmed named families: cite as evidence of harm, never as description. Slurs appear only as labels to research. Cherokee knowledge of witches is restricted.
- **Held for review:** the outside-image and blame pointers needed no holding back.
