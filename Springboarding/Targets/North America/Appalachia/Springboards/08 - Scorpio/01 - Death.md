# Appalachia: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Hungarian, Italian, Welsh and Arabic for older parish and family papers. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, including grief and mourning (funeral practice sits in the domain sheet).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Coal-camp counties (McDowell, Logan, Harlan): check how sudden death at work was mourned, in union memorials and widows' accounts.
- Black Appalachians: check whether Hawks Nest and segregated burying grounds are remembered in Black churches and family stories, not only in outside histories.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Band's own telling of removal-era deaths against the drama's staged version.
- Old Regular Baptist and Holiness families in Letcher and Pike counties: check how prayer for the dead and assurance of salvation shape grieving.
- Slovak, Rusyn, Polish and Hungarian parishes of Pittsburgh and the coal patches: check the fortieth-day and yearly memorial services.
- Amish of Holmes County, Ohio: check how a death is reported and grieved inside a plain church.
- Guatemalan and Mexican families in Morganton and Dalton: check grieving at a distance when a body is sent south.
- Families bereaved by overdose in southern West Virginia: check support groups' own words for the loss.
- Out-migrants in Detroit and Cincinnati (comparison only): check whether the dead were brought "back home" for burial.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mining deaths, ghost lore and death in song; thin for grief as felt (diaries, letters), which needs family papers and oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** Hungarian and Slovak for parish memorial books; Cherokee readers for any Band material shared in the syllabary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** overdose and suicide deaths of named people; unmarked graves at Hawks Nest; Cherokee remains and their return under federal repatriation rules, which are the Band's to discuss. Assisted dying as statute belongs to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** every mourning and mortality source named above passed the standing rules; none was withheld.
