# Appalachia: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Welsh and German for older revival and church papers. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Old Regular, Primitive and Holiness-Pentecostal churches of the Cumberland Plateau and the Kentucky-Virginia coalfields: check how each marks a conversion (told experience, altar, creek baptism) and whether a backslider may return.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check how the Band frames language and ceremony revival in its own schools and publications.
- Former steelworkers of the Mon Valley against Pittsburgh's civic leaders: check whose account of the city's renewal is in which archive.
- Coal-county retraining participants: check how they describe starting over, beside how the programs describe them.
- Affrilachian writers and the Black churches of Harlan and Lynch, Kentucky: check how renaming and recovery of a buried history are discussed.
- Amish and Mennonite families of Holmes County, Ohio, and Somerset County, Pennsylvania: check adult baptism as a chosen turn, and what happens to those who leave.
- Latino newcomers in north Georgia: check conversion from Catholic to evangelical churches as a family matter.
- Welsh-descended chapels of the northern coalfields: check memory of the 1904 revival.
- Out-migrants (comparison only): check the return home as a remaking.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for conversion and revival; mixed for urban and land renewal; thin for recovery outcomes by community, since treatment data are anonymized.
- **Languages needed:** Welsh for revival-era chapel papers; Kurrent script for *Taufscheine*; Cherokee only as the Band shares it.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** people in recovery are identifiable in small towns; Cherokee ceremony is the Band's to describe; deaths at serpent-handling services draw outside sensationalism. Reentry after prison and drug courts as law belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the revival, recovery and renewal sources here raised no standing-rule concern, so nothing is held.
