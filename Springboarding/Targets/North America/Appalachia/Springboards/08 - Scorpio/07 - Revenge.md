# Appalachia: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Italian and Polish for coal-town papers. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore honor or standing, or deter the next wrong.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Pike County, Kentucky, and Mingo County, West Virginia: check how descendants and local historians tell the feud and its end, set apart from outside press and television.
- Madison County, North Carolina, and East Tennessee: check whether Civil War reprisals are still spoken of as debts between families.
- Black communities of the coalfields and of Asheville: check how mob killings are remembered and whether markers have changed what is said in public.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Band's own account of clan obligation and how it changed after the nineteenth century.
- Amish of Holmes County, Ohio: check how non-resistance is taught and how members answer harm done to them.
- Union and company families of Harlan and Logan counties: check blacklisting and its answer as remembered on each side.
- Women's accounts in the Frankie Silver tradition and in later cases: check how revenge by women is judged.
- Latino poultry and nursery workers: check how fear of retaliation for complaints is described.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the famous feuds, but heavy with outside writing; mixed for wartime reprisal; thin for everyday grudges outside court records.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Italian for strike-era immigrant press; Cherokee only through the Band.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feud stories are a central outside stereotype of the region and are never to be repeated as a trait; descendants of feuding families and of lynching victims are living; Cherokee clan law and any feud or killing as crime belong to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** feud, reprisal and forgiveness leads were all usable; none waits for review.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Waller's *Feud*, listing each act she reads as revenge and the economic setting she gives it.
2. Search regional newspapers for "Hatfield McCoy truce" (2003) and read descendants' statements.
3. Pull the Equal Justice Initiative's county list for the Appalachian Regional Commission's counties.
4. Search the Library of Congress for Kentucky versions of "The Two Sisters" and "Matty Groves".
5. Read *A Tree Accurst* for the tellers' counties and how each frames the killing.
