# Appalachia: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Slovak and other heritage languages for lodge papers. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden, privacy included.

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Appalachian English | *blockader*; *blockade liquor* | a moonshiner; untaxed whiskey | older mountain terms; *check* |

| Slovak | *tajomstvo* | secret | lodge and parish papers |

English keywords: secret city Oak Ridge, Greenbrier bunker, moonshine secrecy, two sets of books Upper Big Branch, Melungeon ancestry DNA, closet rural Appalachia, fraternal lodge ritual, powwow transmission, union organizing secret meetings.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Oak Ridge's wartime workers and their children: check what each was permitted to tell kin and how each speaks of it since declassification.
- Moonshining families of Franklin County, Virginia, and Cocke County, Tennessee: check how still sites and kin roles are spoken of now that legal distilleries sell the heritage.
- Melungeon families of Newman's Ridge, Hancock County: check how hidden ancestry was discussed before and after the DNA results.
- LGBTQ people in coalfield counties against Asheville and Pittsburgh: check where the closet holds and where it has opened.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Preservation Office's rules on disclosure before using any older ethnography.
- Pennsylvania German healers of the Allegheny ridges: check the rules for passing charms.
- Union families in Harlan and Bell counties: check memories of secret meetings, passwords and spies.
- Mixed-status Latino families in Dalton and Morristown: check what is kept from schools, employers and kin abroad.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state and company secrecy; mixed for family secrets (novels, memoirs, DNA stories); thin for lodge ritual, by design.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for powwow sources; Spanish for newcomer families; Cherokee only within the Band's rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee restricted knowledge and sacred-site locations; outing anyone; immigration status; overdose causes in living families; lodge rites stay as members publish them.
- **Held for review:** no lodge, still, closet or ancestry lead needed holding; restricted Cherokee material is flagged above rather than held.
