# Appalachia: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Polish and Slovak for mill-town parish papers. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Eastern Kentucky's 2022 flood counties and western North Carolina after Helene: check who was expected to act first (neighbors, churches, the county, FEMA) and who did.
- Mon Valley steel towns against the southern coalfields: check how a slow collapse of work is named compared with a sudden flood or explosion.
- Black families of McDowell County: check accounts of the coal collapse and the leaving that followed, from Black sources.
- The Eastern Band of Cherokee: check the Band's own emergency response in the pandemic and after Helene, and what it says of its capacity.
- Mexican and Guatemalan poultry workers: check pandemic-era accounts from the workers and their churches.
- Amish and Mennonite settlements of Appalachian Ohio: check crisis aid given through the church rather than the state.
- Roane County cleanup workers and their families: check how they name a crisis that unfolded over years.
- Residents of East Palestine: check how a village describes an emergency and its trust in officials afterward.
- Out-migrants of the 1950s (comparison only): check how leaving in a crisis is remembered by those who went.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and recent floods (much of it fresh journalism, to be checked as events settle); mixed for slow crises; thin for personal crises beyond hotline counts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for poultry-town accounts; Polish and Slovak for Depression-era parish relief records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recent disaster survivors and the bereaved; overdose and suicide data; outside "crisis region" imagery (poverty tours, the 1964 documentary) is itself a subject, never a finding.
- **Held for review:** disaster and hard-times sources raised no rule problem; nothing is parked here.
