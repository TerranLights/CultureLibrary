# Appalachia: Belief and philosophy (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English with its church vocabulary; Cherokee (syllabary) for the Eastern Band; Pennsylvania German and older church German on the northern edge and in the Valley; Spanish; Slovak, Rusyn, Polish and Arabic for parish records. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain: religion, philosophy, higher learning and long journeys, asking what is true and where meaning is found, with religious ways and freedom ways as its folkways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields against Northern Appalachia's mill towns:** check which bodies lead each, since immigrant labor brought Catholic, Orthodox and Lutheran parishes unevenly.
- **The Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band's programs separate Qualla Boundary congregations from Kituwah traditions.
- **Black Appalachians (coal camps of Harlan and McDowell counties; Knoxville, Asheville, Pittsburgh's Hill District):** check what became of camp churches when camps closed.
- **Jewish, Lebanese and Syrian families (Maronite, Melkite and Orthodox parishes):** check small-town congregation records, including Deborah Weiner's *Coalfield Jews* (2006) *check*, for how minority worship was sustained.
- **Amish, Mennonite and Brethren communities (Appalachian Ohio, western Pennsylvania, the Shenandoah Valley):** check their own periodicals for the domain in their terms.
- **Melungeon families (Hancock County, Tennessee; Wise County, Virginia):** check the Vardy mission records against the families' own accounts.
- **Latino newcomers (Dalton and Gainesville, Georgia; Morganton, North Carolina; Albertville, Alabama):** check parish records for Spanish-language congregations since 2000.
- **Out-migrant communities in Cincinnati, Dayton, Detroit and Chicago (diaspora, comparison only):** check whether migrant-founded churches kept home-region forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Protestant history and folk practice; mixed for counts (independent congregations unreported); thin for small minorities and for unbelief.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English church terms; Cherokee; Pennsylvania German and older German script (Moravian, Lutheran, Reformed records); Slovak, Rusyn, Polish, Hungarian, Italian, Welsh and Arabic parish papers; Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee ceremonial knowledge is the Band's to share or withhold; outside "hillbilly" images color older literature, so outside accounts are marked; law questions go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit). The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the Appalachian region of the United States, and any published county list (the Appalachian Regional Commission's, for one) is that source's definition only.
- **Held for review:** a lead on the region's range of faiths was set aside under the standing rule and stands here only as a category: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; Buddhist communities, held as a category pending the author's ruling.
