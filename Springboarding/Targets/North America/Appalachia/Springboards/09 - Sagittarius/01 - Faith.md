# Appalachia: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (conversion and worship vocabulary); Cherokee scripture and hymn language; Pennsylvania German; Spanish and Maya languages in newer parishes; Polish, Hungarian, Italian, Welsh and Arabic for immigrant church life. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Regular and Primitive Baptists of the Kentucky-Virginia coalfields against Holiness-Pentecostal churches around Cleveland, Tennessee:** check how each describes the way faith arrives, since election and felt experience are taught differently.
- **Eastern Band congregations on the Qualla Boundary:** check what Cherokee-language hymns and sermons record about faith.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Italian parishes (Pittsburgh, Johnstown, Scranton, northern West Virginia):** check jubilee books for which devotions came with the immigrants and which faded in the second generation.
- **Black congregations (Lynch and Benham; Pittsburgh's Hill District; Knoxville):** check church anniversary books for how members speak of keeping faith through camp closures and moves north.
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonites:** check whether their own writings separate faith from obedience to the *Ordnung*, and how the line is drawn.
- **Lebanese and Syrian Maronite and Melkite families (Charleston and Wheeling, West Virginia):** check how liturgy in Arabic and Syriac gave way to English, and what members say changed.
- **Mexican and Guatemalan Maya Catholics and Pentecostals (Dalton, Gainesville, Morganton):** check which home-country feasts are kept in the new parishes and who organizes them.
- **Out-migrant congregations in Detroit and Cincinnati (diaspora, comparison only):** check whether members still travel home for homecomings and memorial meetings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Baptist and Holiness practice and for recorded singing; mixed for Catholic and Orthodox parish life outside Pittsburgh; thin for secular faith (in science, progress, the nation) as a regional subject.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for conversion and worship talk; Cherokee for hymns and the syllabary scripture; Pennsylvania German for Amish faith writing; Spanish and, in Morganton, Awakateko and other Maya languages for feast devotions; Arabic and Syriac for the Maronite and Melkite liturgy; Polish, Hungarian, Slovak and Rusyn for jubilee books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** serpent handling draws sensational outside coverage; prefer believers' and careful scholars' accounts. Cherokee ceremony is the Band's to share. Church-and-state questions go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** every congregation and recording chosen to open faith could be named; no faith source was held back.
