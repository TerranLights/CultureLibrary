# Appalachia: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (oath, tale and testimony vocabulary); Cherokee, in the Band's own usage; Pennsylvania German; Spanish, including Mexican slang; Italian and Rusyn for older community papers. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Restoration Movement churches (Churches of Christ, Christian Churches) against Primitive and Old Regular Baptists:** check how each says scripture is to be read and a doctrinal dispute settled; both grew from the same revival era, so the pair isolates method.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how oral accounts and written sources are weighed against each other in the Band's own histories.
- **Coalfield families and mine operators:** check disaster reports, union papers and family statements for whose account becomes the record after an explosion.
- **Black Appalachians of McDowell County and Huntington:** check newspapers and church histories for how their history was told by others and corrected by their own writers.
- **Italian and Rusyn mining families:** check ethnic newspapers for how mine disasters were reported in their own languages.
- **Latino newcomers:** check Spanish-language radio and church networks for where news and rumor travel, and how they are checked.
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonites:** check their own periodicals for which outside news is trusted and passed on.
- **Out-migrant readers in Ohio and Michigan cities (diaspora, comparison only):** check whether home-county papers mailed north shaped what they believed of home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious debate, press history and oral history; mixed for surveys (rarely cut to the region); thin on Spanish-language and Cherokee-language truth practices.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English, with care for words like *tale* that change sense; Cherokee for how oral record is spoken of; Spanish for rumor-checking in newcomer networks; Italian and Rusyn for disaster reporting in the ethnic press; Pennsylvania German for which outside news Old Order papers trust.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding school findings and Cherokee oral tradition are the Band's to interpret; disaster accounts involve grieving families. Testimony in court is a law question for Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** the press archives, ballads and inquiry reports chosen for truth-telling include none kept back.
