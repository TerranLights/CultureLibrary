# Appalachia: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (creed, sign and haunt vocabulary); Cherokee, as Band storytellers give it; Pennsylvania German with its older printed German; Spanish; Italian and Arabic for household belief in immigrant families. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true: what people take to be so about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and what warrant they want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primitive and Old Regular Baptists against Free Will Baptists in the same coalfield counties:** check their articles of faith side by side, because election and free will are the point on which their creeds part.
- **Bible Student and Jehovah's Witness congregations in western Pennsylvania:** check their own histories for what drew early members in Allegheny and the mill towns.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check what beliefs about beings and places the Band tells in public and what it keeps among itself.
- **Pennsylvania German families on the northern edge and in the Valley:** check how charm books and *Braucherei* were held next to Lutheran, Reformed or Brethren belief.
- **Italian, Lebanese and Syrian families:** check family memoirs and parish histories for household beliefs carried from home, and which survived into the third generation.
- **Black Appalachians in the coal camps:** check oral histories and WPA files for which folk beliefs were held, set apart or rejected, and by whom.
- **Latino newcomers:** check parish and Pentecostal congregation records for how saints' devotions and Pentecostal teaching meet.
- **Out-migrant families in Cincinnati and Columbus (diaspora, comparison only):** check whether second-generation children keep the signs and sayings their parents brought.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for creeds, doctrine and folk-belief collecting; mixed for surveys (state-level only); thin for unbelief in the region and for newcomers' beliefs.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for signs, tokens and haints; older printed German for the 1820 charm book; Pennsylvania German for *Braucherei* talk; Cherokee for names of beings; Spanish for devotional belief; Italian and Arabic for evil-eye lore in family memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee beliefs about sacred beings are the Band's to share; folk-belief collecting has often been used to paint mountain people as backward, so collections are read for informants' own words. Ghost tourism at former asylums touches the patients' memory.
- **Held for review:** creeds, charm books and ghost lore all passed the standing rules; no belief source was withheld.
