# Appalachia: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, revival and union speech above all; Pennsylvania German for peace-church records; Polish for a church split; Spanish; Cherokee where the Band's records speak of refusal. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content (a criminal conviction is not this concept).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primitive Baptists and Missionary Baptists of the same counties:** check minute books from the split years, because a single quarrel left both sides' reasons on paper.
- **Mennonites, Brethren and Amish of the Valley and Appalachian Ohio:** check wartime records from the Civil War to Vietnam for how each body advised members facing the draft.
- **Coal miners and their families in Harlan, Logan and Mingo counties:** check strike records and songs for who was praised as steadfast and who was named a scab, and on what grounds.
- **Polish, Slovak and Lithuanian parishes of the anthracite and Pittsburgh districts:** check schism records for how lay conviction on language and property played out in each group.
- **Black Appalachians in the coal camps and mill towns:** check union locals' and churches' records for stands taken inside segregated settings, and how they were remembered.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own sources on refusal and return, keeping them separate from outdoor-drama versions.
- **Latino workers in poultry plants in north Georgia and western North Carolina:** check organizing campaigns' records for how stands were taken under the risk of firing or deportation.
- **Out-migrants in Ohio's factory cities (diaspora, comparison only):** check union and church records for whether home-region convictions carried into new workplaces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious splits, labor struggles and environmental activism; mixed for peace-church records (scattered among denominational archives); thin for Latino and Cherokee-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for revival and strike speech; Pennsylvania German for peace-church petitions; Polish and Slovak for schism records; Spanish for poultry-plant organizing; Cherokee for the Band's telling of the families who refused to leave.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** union and anti-mining conflicts left living enmities; exclusion records name real families. The criminal sense of conviction and any question of civil disobedience before the courts go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** the revival, peace-church and strike sources raised no exclusion, so no stand-taking source was held.
