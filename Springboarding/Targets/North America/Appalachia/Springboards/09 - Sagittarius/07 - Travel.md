# Appalachia: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (leaving, hosting and homecoming talk); Cherokee for the Removal journey; Pennsylvania German and 18th-century church German for settler routes; Spanish; Welsh, Slovak and Italian for immigrant arrival and return. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler (short trips stay with Gemini).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coal counties against the Blue Ridge tourist counties:** check which mainly send people away and which mainly receive visitors, and how residents of each speak of strangers.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own account of Removal and return, and its stance on tourism to Cherokee, North Carolina.
- **Black Appalachians:** check migration records for when moves north from the coal camps came, and where families went (Pittsburgh, Cleveland, Detroit).
- **German-descended families of the Valley and Scots-Irish families who came down the Wagon Road:** check family papers for why they moved on and what they carried.
- **Welsh, Italian, Slovak and Hungarian coal and steel families:** check passenger lists and ethnic papers for return trips home and for chain migration.
- **Latino newcomers in Dalton, Gainesville and Morganton:** check consulate and church records for travel home and the limits on it.
- **Amish families moving into Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania:** check their newspapers for why settlements were founded where they were.
- **Out-migrants in Ohio and Michigan cities (diaspora, comparison only):** check homecoming and reunion customs, and whether "going home" trips are recorded.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for routes, migration and tourism; mixed for return migration; thin for Latino newcomers' travel and for Cherokee-language accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for leaving and homecoming talk; Cherokee for Removal place names; Pennsylvania German and older church German for settler diaries; Spanish for trips home and their limits; Welsh, Italian, Slovak and Hungarian for passenger lists and return letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Removal is a living grief for the Band; tourism to the region has included poverty tourism, so visitor and media accounts are marked as outside views. Immigration status is not to be probed.
- **Held for review:** routes, migration data and park counts: no journey account or dataset was held back.
