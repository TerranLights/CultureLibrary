# Appalachia: Education (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (schooling and status words); Cherokee for the Band's own schools; Pennsylvania German for Old Order schooling; Spanish for newcomer students; Welsh and Italian for the ethnic societies' scholarship funds. **Concept in one line:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person; early schooling and learning stay with Gemini.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coalfield counties against university towns (Morgantown, Knoxville, Athens, Boone, Blacksburg):** check attainment and where graduates settle, since a campus may draw talent in or send it out.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own reports on its schools, its immersion program and the earlier federal boarding school.
- **Black Appalachians:** check Storer, Bluefield State, West Virginia State and Knoxville College records for who studied, and what became of the schools after desegregation.
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonite communities:** check their own schools' materials for how schooling past the eighth grade is weighed.
- **Holiness, Baptist and mainline congregations:** check whether each asks for a trained ministry, and what the answer reveals about the standing of degrees.
- **Slovak, Hungarian, Welsh, Italian and Lebanese families of the mill and mine towns:** check lodge scholarship funds and parish schools for how the second generation was sent to college.
- **Latino newcomers:** check school and parent-group records on college access for undocumented and mixed-status students.
- **Out-migrant families in Ohio and Michigan (diaspora, comparison only):** check whether moving north changed college rates for the children.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutional histories and county statistics; mixed for how residents view degrees; thin for Cherokee- and Spanish-language sources.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for schooling and status words; Cherokee for immersion and teacher-training materials; Pennsylvania German for Old Order school writing; Spanish for parent groups and college-access work; Welsh and Italian for lodge scholarship records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding school memory is the Band's; "brain drain" talk can slide into blaming those who stay. School funding suits and attendance laws are for Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** one higher-learning source was left out; it stands here only under the category South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
