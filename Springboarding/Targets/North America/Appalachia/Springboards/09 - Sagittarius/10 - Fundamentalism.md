# Appalachia: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (separation, standards and Bible-version vocabulary); Pennsylvania German for Old Order rule talk; Spanish for Latino Pentecostal congregations; Cherokee only where the Band itself discusses missionary teaching. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits at all is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Independent Fundamental Baptist and Southern Baptist congregations of east Tennessee:** check which call themselves fundamentalist and which refuse the word, using their own statements of faith.
- **Holiness churches that keep dress standards and Pentecostal churches that dropped them:** check when and why standards were kept or relaxed.
- **Old Order Amish and Old Order Mennonites (Appalachian Ohio, the Shenandoah Valley):** check why members and scholars reject the English label, and what term they use instead.
- **Black Baptist and Holiness congregations:** check whether studies of the concept include them, and whether their own leaders use or refuse the term.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band's own sources describe missionary teaching that ruled out Cherokee practice, and any churches that set it aside.
- **Kanawha County parents and teachers of 1974:** check both sides' own accounts, not only press coverage.
- **Latino Pentecostal congregations in north Georgia and western North Carolina:** check what *sana doctrina* requires and who decides.
- **Migrant-founded churches in Detroit and Cincinnati (diaspora, comparison only):** check which fellowships they joined, and whether the choice followed home churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1920s controversy, separatist institutions and the textbook fight; mixed for surveys (state level only); thin on how members describe themselves.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English for separation and standards vocabulary; Pennsylvania German for the Old Order terms that replace the label; Spanish for *sana doctrina* teaching; Cherokee for the Band's account of missionary prohibitions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the word is often an insult; the "Bible Belt" image of the mountains is an outside one; leavers' accounts involve abuse claims that must be handled with care. Questions about what school law or church rights allow go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** nothing on fundamentalism was set aside; strict movements in faiths outside Christianity were not scouted here, and any such pointer would go to review first.
