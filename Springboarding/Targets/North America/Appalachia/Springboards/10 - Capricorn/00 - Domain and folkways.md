# Appalachia: Authority and duty, the domain and its folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with its Scots, Ulster and English layers), Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys and what is owed, with career, status and reputation, and the ways a people handles age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how company towns reset who gave orders and kept the clock, against nearby farm hollers.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh and the mill towns, the Ohio Valley, Appalachian Ohio, the Southern Tier): check union, parish and lodge offices as rival sources of standing.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and the Smokies:** check church, county court and family as seats of authority in farm counties.
- **Shenandoah Valley and the Cumberland Plateau:** check Presbyterian, Lutheran and Mennonite church orders against county offices.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee** (Qualla Boundary; Snowbird): the Band's own account of offices and Elders first.
- **Black Appalachians** (coal camps such as Lynch, Kentucky; Knoxville, Asheville): check church, school and lodge offices as standing outside white-run institutions.
- **Melungeon families** of Newman's Ridge and the Clinch valley: check who held church and county offices among them, and who was kept out; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn, Welsh and Lebanese** communities: check parish, fraternal, merchant and union ranks.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check the church district's offices.
- **Latino newcomers:** check crew-leader and church offices.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati and Detroit (comparison only): check what changed in obligations to kin at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for coal-town, labor and church records; mixed for county power; thin for Melungeon, Rusyn and Latino authority.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the Band's syllabary sources; German script for Valley and Moravian church orders; Spanish for newcomer parishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outside writing has long framed the region as backward or lawless: name each outside account as one and read the region's scholars first. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled: this sheet is written for the Appalachian region of the United States; the Appalachian Regional Commission's county list may be named as one source's definition, never as the nation's units.
- **Held for review:** the domain survey turned up no source that the standing rules required holding back.
