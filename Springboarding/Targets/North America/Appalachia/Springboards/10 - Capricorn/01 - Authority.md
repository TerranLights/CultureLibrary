# Appalachia: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check which authorities (operator, union local, sheriff, preacher) people name when asked whom they answered to, decade by decade.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the mill towns, the Ohio Valley, the Southern Tier): check the parish priest and the union steward against the mill foreman.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check the weight of the Baptist moderator and the county sheriff in farm counties.
- **Great Smoky Mountains and the Cumberland Plateau:** check how park and federal authority were received where land was taken for parks and dams.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Principal Chief, Tribal Council and Elders, in the Band's own accounts.
- **Black Appalachians** (McDowell County; Lynch, Kentucky; Knoxville): check church and lodge leaders as authority alongside white-run offices.
- **Melungeon families:** check how they dealt with county officials who classified them; sources thin.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Italian parishes:** check disputes between congregations and bishops.
- **Amish and Mennonite** districts: check the bishop's and ministers' authority, and where the state's ends.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whom families call when things go wrong: priest, pastor, consulate, crew leader or police.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how Detroit and Cincinnati factory authority was described by migrants from the coalfields.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor conflict and church polity; mixed for household authority; thin for Melungeon and Latino experience of authority.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the Band's own terms of leadership; German for Anabaptist confessions in the original; Spanish for newcomer interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse by people in authority (clergy, company police, sheriffs) involves living families; mine-war accounts are told from sides. Images of the region as defiant of all rule circulate widely from outside; test each against the region's own records.
- **Held for review:** no pointer on obedience and command had to be set aside under the standing rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Westminster Confession ch. 23 and the Dordrecht article on government side by side; list where each permits refusing a ruler.
2. Search Berea College's special collections for "Old Regular Baptist minutes"; read three years of one association's rules of decorum.
3. Pull Vera's Incarceration Trends for one Central Appalachian state's counties; chart jail population per adult since 1990.
4. Find Fogelson's "Cherokee Notions of Power"; list the Cherokee terms he uses and check each with the Band's language program.
5. Read Green's chapters on Paint Creek and Blair Mountain; list each authority the miners rejected and each they appealed to.
