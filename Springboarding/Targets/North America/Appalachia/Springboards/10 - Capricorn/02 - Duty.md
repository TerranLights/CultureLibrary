# Appalachia: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), German (church texts) and Pennsylvania German, Italian (older mutual-aid records), Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check who cares for disabled miners and widows, and when union, company or kin took the duty.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh's mill towns, the Ohio Valley): check lodge and parish rolls for what members owed one another.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check workings and graveyard days for which families showed up.
- **East Tennessee and western North Carolina:** check Civil War records for how divided loyalties were explained to kin.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check veterans' honors and communal work in the Band's own programs.
- **Black Appalachians:** check Emancipation Day and church homecomings as duties kept by people who moved away.
- **Melungeon families:** check reunion and cemetery-association records; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** communities: check mutual-aid bylaws for named duties and fines.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check church and family-business obligations in merchant towns.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check care of the aged within the district.
- **Latino newcomers:** check remittances and saints' vows as owed duties.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check visits home and burial in the home county.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, caregiving and church sources; mixed for mutual-aid societies; thin for Melungeon and Latino family duties.
- **Languages needed:** German for catechisms; Italian for early society minutes; Spanish for hometown associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** caregiving and kinship-care records concern living people; casualty files name the dead. Avoid treating any group's sense of obligation as one fixed trait.
- **Held for review:** none of the obligation pointers (catechism, casualty files, lodges, hometown associations) needed setting aside.
