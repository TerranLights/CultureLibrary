# Appalachia: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Italian (consular and press records), Spanish. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check who was blamed after each mine disaster (miner, foreman, operator, inspector) and whether the answer shifted after 1969.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Johnstown, the Monongahela Valley, the Ohio Valley): check how mill towns assigned blame for floods, smog and layoffs.
- **East Tennessee and the Tennessee Valley:** check how TVA's public ownership changed the sense of who answers.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check church and county records for how debts and fatherhood were made answerable.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own accounts of how responsibility for a wrong passed from clan to court.
- **Black Appalachians:** check the Hawk's Nest record and coal-camp sources for whose deaths were investigated.
- **Melungeon families:** check how county records assigned blame and liability to them; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak and Rusyn** miners: check consular, union and church records after disasters.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check church mutual aid as the alternative to insurance and lawsuits.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whether workplace injuries in poultry plants are reported, and who is blamed.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how Appalachian migrants were blamed in Midwestern cities' press.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster investigations and court records; mixed for opioid accountability; thin for Melungeon and Latino experience of blame.
- **Languages needed:** Italian for 1907 consular files and the Italian-language press; Spanish for poultry-plant injury interviews; Cherokee for clan terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction, paternity and disaster deaths touch living families; the word "shiftless" was long applied to the poor from outside: record it, never use it as description.
- **Held for review:** no pointer on blame and accountability was withheld under the standing rules.
