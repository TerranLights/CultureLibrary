# Appalachia: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Polish (fraternal records), Spanish. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check school paddling records and mine safety discipline side by side.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh's mill towns, the Ohio Valley, the Southern Tier): check how mill rules, parochial schools and fraternal drill shaped conduct.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and north Alabama:** check church discipline records and holiness standards.
- **Shenandoah Valley:** check military schools and Mennonite and Brethren church discipline.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the boarding-school record and the Band's own programs for raising children, in its accounts.
- **Black Appalachians:** check segregated schools and churches as places that set their own standards.
- **Melungeon families:** check school and church records for how discipline was applied to their children; sources thin.
- **Polish, Slovak, Hungarian, Rusyn, Italian and Welsh** communities: check Falcon, Sokol and choir training as chosen discipline.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements of Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania: check the *Ordnung* and the ban.
- **Latino newcomers:** check how families and schools meet over discipline, through bilingual staff.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how factory discipline in the Midwest was described by migrants from farm counties.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school, church and industrial records; mixed for home discipline; thin for Melungeon and Latino child-rearing.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for Amish sources; Polish for early Falcon records; Spanish for newcomer parents.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school harms and abuse called discipline concern living survivors; physical punishment of children is a live dispute. "Lazy" and "shiftless" were long pinned on the region from outside: record such labels as outside claims only.
- **Held for review:** paddles, drill halls and minute books alike cleared the standing rules; nothing waits on the author.
