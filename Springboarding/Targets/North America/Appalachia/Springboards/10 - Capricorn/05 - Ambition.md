# Appalachia: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether ambition is spoken of as leaving, and what returnees are called.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the mill towns, the Southern Tier): check mill-to-college paths in immigrant families' second generation.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Smokies and the Blue Ridge:** check tourism and music careers as local routes to rising.
- **Knoxville and Asheville:** check how newcomers and natives describe getting ahead in growing cities.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check collective ambitions (language, land, enterprise) as the Band states them.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how ambition and out-migration intertwined in coal towns whose Black communities moved north and west.
- **Melungeon families:** check accounts of rising past a disputed status; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** families: check union office, priesthood and small business as ladders.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check how merchant success is spoken of across generations.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check the line between enterprise and pride.
- **Latino newcomers:** check parents' hopes for children against their own work.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check Midwestern second-generation memoirs for what "making it" meant.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for memoir, song and education statistics; mixed for mobility; thin for Melungeon and Latino aspirations.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for newcomer families; Pennsylvania German for plain writing on pride; Arabic only for early Lebanese family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the culture-of-poverty argument is charged; record it as a contested claim with its authors named, never as a finding. Success memoirs are single voices, not the region's.
- **Held for review:** a single source on getting ahead was put aside under the standing rule and stands here only as a category: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
