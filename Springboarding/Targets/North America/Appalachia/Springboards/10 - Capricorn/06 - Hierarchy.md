# Appalachia: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check how company housing, scrip and job grade ranked people inside one camp.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh's mill towns, the Ohio Valley, Appalachian Ohio): check the skilled-unskilled line and its overlap with ethnicity.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check land ownership and church office as the old ranks of farm counties.
- **Asheville, the Smokies and resort towns:** check the rank line between estate owners, tourists and the people who served them.
- **Shenandoah Valley:** check the planter, German farmer and tenant ranks.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check clan, office and enrollment as the Band describes them, with any rank only as the Band states it.
- **Black Appalachians:** check segregated housing, schools and job grades in camps and cities.
- **Melungeon families:** check how they were placed in official categories and how they placed themselves.
- **Italian, Polish, Hungarian, Slovak, Rusyn and Welsh** communities: check who became foremen, and when.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check merchant standing in towns where they kept stores.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check the ministry offices and how they are filled.
- **Latino newcomers:** check crew-foreman and supervisor roles in plants and nurseries.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how they were ranked in Midwestern factory towns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plentiful where companies and denominations kept paper (camp maps, assembly minutes); patchy for county elites; scarce for where Melungeon and Latino families stood.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for Amish ministry terms; Cherokee for clan terms; Spanish for plant and crew titles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs and racial classifications must be recorded as used by others, never adopted; enrollment and clan questions belong to the Band. The "hillbilly" image is an outside ranking: study it, never repeat it as a trait.
- **Held for review:** empty for this concept; no rank source met any exclusion.
