# Appalachia: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German and German (Moravian records), Italian, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check the weight of union seniority, kinship and the foreman's favor in who got the better jobs.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh and the mill towns, the Ohio Valley, the Southern Tier): check civil-service exams against ward patronage in city jobs.
- **Southern Appalachia and the Blue Ridge:** check school-board and county hiring for how kin and merit are weighed.
- **Shenandoah Valley and the Moravian settlements:** check the lot and congregational choice against personal merit.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band ties support to school completion, and how it honors earned standing, in its own terms.
- **Black Appalachians:** check seniority, credentials and exclusion from supervisory jobs in mines and mills.
- **Melungeon families:** check school and job records for barriers unrelated to merit; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** families: check recommendation networks against exams.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check ministers chosen by lot and craft standing earned in the shop.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whether foreign credentials count, and how referrals work.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check whether a cousin's word at the Dayton or Akron plant gate counted for more than a diploma.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for scholarships, union contracts and school oversight; mixed for patronage; thin for Melungeon and Latino hiring.
- **Languages needed:** German script for Moravian lot records; Italian for older society correspondence; Spanish for plant workers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** studies of the Band's children concern a living community: read the Band's own statements first and use only published results. Patronage accusations name living officials.
- **Held for review:** every merit source considered here passed the standing rules; none is waiting on a ruling.
