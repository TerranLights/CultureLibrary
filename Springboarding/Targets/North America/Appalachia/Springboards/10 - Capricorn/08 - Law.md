# Appalachia: Law, its spirit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with its Scots and Ulster layer), Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German and German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Capricorn takes the spirit only; statutes and codes belong to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether the sheriff and the courts were seen as the company's or the public's, across the mine-war decades and after.
- **Northern Appalachia** (western Pennsylvania, the Ohio Valley): check the Whiskey Rebellion counties against later mill towns for attitudes to federal law.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Blue Ridge and the Smokies:** check moonshining, dry votes and church discipline as rival laws.
- **Cumberland Plateau and the Kentucky-Tennessee border:** check folk justice in oral histories against court dockets.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own account of what its law is for and how its court relates to clan tradition.
- **Black Appalachians:** check whose crimes were prosecuted and how song and press answered.
- **Melungeon families:** check how they met county law in classification and land cases; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Hungarian and Rusyn** communities: check Prohibition-era enforcement in mining and mill towns.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check how they relate to the state's law (*Obrigkeit*) and whether their disputes ever reach a court.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whom families trust after raids, and how sheriffs' roles are understood.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how migrants and city police in Cincinnati and Chicago described each other.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for moonshining, labor conflict and court records; mixed for trust in police; thin for Melungeon and Latino legal experience.
- **Languages needed:** German for Anabaptist writing on the state; Spanish for raid-affected families; Cherokee for the Band's legal terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** raids, prosecutions and killings involve living people; the "lawless mountaineer" is an outside image: test it, never assume it. Statute texts are Libra's: this sheet points only to how people relate to law.
- **Held for review:** nothing on the purpose of law and obedience to it was held back under the standing rules.
