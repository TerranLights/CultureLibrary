# Appalachia: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with its Scots and Ulster layer and Pittsburgh speech), Cherokee (Eastern Band), German (Moravian records) and Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check company-town rules against the order kept by churches and kin after the companies left.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh, the mill towns, Hazleton and the anthracite edge): check riots, urban renewal and ordinances as contests over whose order.
- **Southern Appalachia, the Smokies and the Blue Ridge:** check whitecapping and resort-town covenants a century apart.
- **Shenandoah Valley and the Moravian lands:** check church-town rules and German household order.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band speaks of balance and of Kituwah, leaving restricted knowledge to the Band.
- **Black Appalachians** (the Hill District, coal camps, Knoxville): check exclusions and removals carried out in the name of order.
- **Melungeon families:** check how outside writers and officials cast them as disorder; record such claims as outside ones; sources thin.
- **Italian, Polish, Slovak, Rusyn, Hungarian and Welsh** communities: check parish processions and festivals as displays of order.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check merchant-town order and church festivals.
- **Amish and Mennonite** settlements: check how church districts state household and community order.
- **Latino newcomers:** check ordinances and neighbors' complaints, and the newcomers' own accounts.
- **Out-migrants** (comparison only): check how Midwestern cities treated "hillbilly" neighborhoods as disorder.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, planning and crime data; mixed for household order; thin for Melungeon and Latino views.
- **Languages needed:** German script for Moravian rules; Spanish for Hazleton and north Georgia newcomers; Cherokee for the Band's terms of balance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kituwah and Cherokee ceremonial order are the Band's to share; removals and exclusions involve living families. "Disorderly" was long used of the region from outside: record the word, not the claim.
- **Held for review:** the order sources gathered here raised no standing-rule question, so the line stays empty.

## 5. First queries
