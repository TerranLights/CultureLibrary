# Appalachia: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (with its Scots and Ulster layer and Pittsburgh speech), Cherokee (Eastern Band; syllabary), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for: associations, social ways (settlement, migration, neighboring, gathering), humanitarian ideals and the forward face of community.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachia's coalfields** against **Southern Appalachia's** farm and resort counties: check whether coal-camp institutions (company store, union local) or church and kin networks carry the gathering role.
- **Northern Appalachia** (Pittsburgh and the mill towns, Appalachian Ohio, the Southern Tier): check the weight of ethnic parishes and fraternal societies against the rural church.
- **The Eastern Band of Cherokee** on the Qualla Boundary: check their own town and clan categories before any regional frame.
- **Black Appalachians** in coal camps and in Knoxville and Asheville: check separate lodges, churches and schools as their own records describe them.
- **Melungeon families** of Hancock County, Tennessee, and nearby: check self-naming and reunions.
- **Amish and Mennonite districts** of Holmes County, Ohio, and western Pennsylvania: check church-district boundaries as the unit.
- **Italian, Slovak, Rusyn, Polish, Hungarian and Welsh** mining and steel towns, and **Lebanese and Syrian** families: check parish and society rolls.
- **Latino newcomers** in Gainesville and Dalton, Georgia, and Morganton, North Carolina: check hometown ties.
- **Out-migrants** in Cincinnati, Dayton and Detroit: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for the coalfields and Cherokee history; thinner for Lebanese, Hungarian and Rusyn towns and Latino associations, where parish and society records matter.
- **Languages needed:** Appalachian English regional dictionaries; Cherokee syllabary reading for the Phoenix and Eastern Band sources; German for Moravian and Harmony Society originals; Spanish and, at Morganton, Maya languages (which ones, *check*).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the region has long been written about from outside; mark each source as insider or outsider. Melungeon ancestry claims are contested and personal. Cherokee ceremonial knowledge stays with the Eastern Band.
- **Held for review:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; no tracker scope file exists, so the ARC list above is a data aid only. No pointer on community and ideals was set aside under the standing rules.
