# Appalachia: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coal camps of southern West Virginia and eastern Kentucky** against **western Pennsylvania patch towns**: check whether the company, the union or the church is named as the center in each.
- **Families removed** from the Smokies, Norris and Fontana: check whether reunions and Decoration Days keep the old community alive in practice.
- **Eastern Band townships** and **Snowbird** (Graham County): check how distance from the Qualla Boundary's center changes township life.
- **Black coal-camp communities** (Lynch and Benham, Kentucky; McDowell County): check their own churches, schools and reunions as sources.
- **Holmes County Amish districts** against **Old Order Mennonite** groups: check the district size and visiting rules each records.
- **Latino newcomers** in Dalton and Gainesville, Georgia: check which churches and soccer leagues they built.
- **Pittsburgh's mill neighborhoods** (Braddock, Homestead): check parish boundaries against municipal ones.
- **Knoxville and Asheville** newcomers: check whether retirees and remote workers join existing community bodies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on coal towns, floods and removals; mixed for Latino and Amish communities; thin for Lebanese and Hungarian enclaves, where parish anniversary booklets may be the main trace.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for township and club records published in the syllabary; Pennsylvania German for Amish community terms; Spanish for parish bulletins and newcomer media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** flood and removal testimony carries grief; Buffalo Creek and the 2022 floods involve living survivors. Erikson writes as an outside scholar; pair him with survivors' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** nothing on community was set aside; Erikson's and Shifflett's outsider vantage is flagged above rather than held.
