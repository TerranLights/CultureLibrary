# Appalachia: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English (incl. Pittsburgh speech), Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where the line between "us" and "them" is drawn, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Union and non-union families** in Harlan, Mingo and Logan counties: check how strike memory sorts families into sides over generations.
- **Tug Valley kin networks** on both sides of the Kentucky-West Virginia line: check whether the state line or the kin line counts more in local records.
- **Black miners in the UMWA** (southern West Virginia; Lynch, Kentucky): check their own accounts of being inside or outside the union's "us".
- **Eastern Band clans** against township membership: check which of the two the Eastern Band's own sources put first in a given setting.
- **Amish who leave** in Holmes County: check ex-members' memoirs against church sources.
- **Rusyn and Slovak parishes** in the Mon Valley: check how Byzantine and Roman rite affiliation split or joined groups.
- **Melungeon families** of Newman's Ridge: check the gap between outside labels and the families' own group names.
- **Latino crews** in Christmas-tree farming (Ashe and Avery counties, North Carolina): check whether crew or hometown is the working group.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for union wars and feuds; mixed for fraternal societies; thin for Latino crews and Hungarian and Lebanese associations.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for clan terms; Pennsylvania German for Amish church rules; Slovak, Rusyn, Polish and Hungarian for lodge minutes; Spanish for crew and church sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** feud and strike violence involves living families; "hillbilly" and "briar" are slurs whose reclaiming is contested; Cherokee clan teaching beyond what the Eastern Band publishes stays with the Eastern Band.
- **Held for review:** no group pointer was set aside; Jack Weller's missionary account of mountain kin groups is left out, reached instead through its critics.
