# Appalachia: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black residents of Knoxville and Asheville** whose neighborhoods were cleared for urban renewal: check oral histories for how equal standing was claimed and lost.
- **Women miners** in southern West Virginia and Virginia: check their accounts against union records of the same years.
- **Eastern Band women** and elders: check what the Eastern Band itself publishes on standing between men and women today.
- **Steel-town ethnic groups** (Slovak, Hungarian, Italian, Polish): check how the mill's job ladder ranked them, from the workers' own papers.
- **Amish districts** in Holmes County against higher-income Mennonite neighbors: check how plainness rules handle rising wealth.
- **Melungeon families**: check how their recorded racial classification shifted in census and school records.
- **Latino newcomers** in north Georgia: check whether church or worker center speaks for them.
- **Old landholding families against coal-camp families** in the Cumberland Plateau: check deeds and tax lists for the gap.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on land and class in the coalfields; mixed for race (strong for southern West Virginia, thin for many rural counties); thin for Latino workers' own voices.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for any Eastern Band material on balance and standing; Pennsylvania German for church sermons and letters; Spanish for poultry-town sources; German script for Moravian originals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** expulsions, lynchings and sundown histories involve living descendants on both sides; colorism and Melungeon ancestry are personal. Civil-rights statutes and court rulings belong to Libra (the letter) and Capricorn (the spirit); here only lived standing.
- **Held for review:** no equality pointer was set aside; the "culture of poverty" literature is pointed to only through its critics, by design.
