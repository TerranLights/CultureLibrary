# Appalachia: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Pennsylvania German, Spanish; Cherokee (Eastern Band) terms to be asked of the Eastern Band's language program. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far that departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East Tennessee and West Virginia Unionist families** against Confederate neighbors: check family papers for how dissent was remembered after the war.
- **Holiness serpent-handling congregations** (Jolo, West Virginia; Sand Mountain, Alabama) against other churches: check how each side speaks of the other in its own sources.
- **Gay, lesbian and trans Appalachians** in Knoxville, Asheville and small towns: check whether staying, leaving or returning is the common story in their own accounts.
- **Amish young people during *Rumspringa*** in Holmes County: check Amish and ex-Amish sources separately.
- **Shenandoah Valley Mennonites and Brethren**: check how pacifist refusal is told in church histories now.
- **Melungeon families**: check how difference was assigned on paper (census, school) against family memory.
- **Rusyn and Slovak families** in Pittsburgh: check parish papers for the reception of artists and other departures.
- **Latino young people** in north Georgia: check school and church sources for how new styles and second-generation choices are met.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on Civil War dissent and religious difference; growing for queer Appalachia; thin for Latino youth and for Cherokee views, which should come from the Eastern Band.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for church writings on leavers; Spanish for youth sources; Cherokee, through the Eastern Band's language program, for any term for one who goes against the town.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** serpent handling involves deaths and legal penalties (the law itself belongs to Libra and Capricorn); queer informants may not be out; Melungeon DNA results are personal. "Hillbilly" caricature appears here only as an outside image the festival plays with.
