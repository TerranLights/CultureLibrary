# Appalachia: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English, Cherokee (Eastern Band), Pennsylvania German, Spanish. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primitive Baptist Universalists** against **Old Regular** and **Missionary Baptist** churches: check funeral sermons for what each holds out to mourners.
- **Coalfield young people** against **Shenandoah Valley** and **Asheville** young people: check yearbooks and school surveys for stay-or-leave hopes.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee**: check how the Eastern Band speaks of hope for its language and land in its own media.
- **Amish families** of Holmes County: check whether *Ausbund* hymns and present sermons voice the same hopes.
- **Black families** in Lynch, Benham and southern West Virginia: check school and church records for hopes tied to education and migration.
- **Pittsburgh's mill towns** after 1980: check renewal plans against residents' own accounts.
- **Latino newcomers and their children** in north Georgia: check parish and school sources for hope tied to legal status.
- **People in recovery** in Huntington and Roanoke: check peer-support groups' own materials.
- **Out-migrants** to Ohio and Michigan: comparison only; check letters for the hopes that sent them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in hymnody, sermon and fiction; mixed in survey data (well-being polls by state, rarely by Appalachian county); thin for Latino and Lebanese families' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for the hymnbook and scripture; early New High German for the *Ausbund*; Spanish for parish and press sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** overdose and recovery stories involve living people; immigration status is sensitive; Cherokee sacred song is used only as the Eastern Band allows. Almanac material is read as farm practice, not as a pointer system of its own.
- **Held for review:** no hope pointer was set aside; Case and Deaton's national despair study is left for the measures stage, since its county data are already reached through the ARC report on the Detachment sheet.
