# Appalachia: The hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (Eastern Band, syllabary); Pennsylvania German; the Scots and Ulster layer; Spanish; the coal and steel towns' heritage languages. **Concept in one line:** the hidden and transcendence: how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape, and the magic ways (healing, charms, omens) that handle hidden forces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether distress maps match mine closures and local church and clinic reports.
- **Southern Appalachia:** check how much of the folk-belief record rests on a few twentieth-century collectors.
- **Northern Appalachia:** check whether powwowing, spiritualism and Catholic devotion draw a different map of the unseen.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's public material on healers and spirit beings, and stop where the Band draws the line.
- **Black Appalachians:** check coal-camp church records and oral histories; the belief collections mostly drew on white informants.
- **Coal and steel immigrant communities:** check parish and lodge records for devotions and protective customs.
- **Latino newcomers:** check clinics and parishes in Dalton and Morganton for folk-illness terms in use.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how their own writings frame suffering.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: county health data are rich; belief collections are rich but old and weighted south. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the Appalachian region of the United States and adopts no unit list.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for the charm books; Cherokee for spirit terms; Spanish for clinic work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee medicine formulas are restricted; read only what the Eastern Band shares. Former asylums sold as ghost attractions hold patients' graves. Visitors' writing on mountain belief feeds the "hillbilly" image; name it as an outside account. Order ways go to this sign's Crime sheet; law to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the healing, belief and confinement sources above are all nameable; the Cherokee limit is respect, not a held pointer.
