# Appalachia: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Pennsylvania German and the German of the Anabaptist books; Cherokee; Italian, Slovak, Polish, Hungarian and Welsh; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check memorials and obituaries for whether a mining death is framed as sacrifice, accident or theft of a life.
- **Immigrant mining parishes:** check feast days and memorial masses tied to disasters and strikes.
- **Amish, Mennonite and Brethren communities:** check how martyr reading figures in present choices about service and refusal.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check whose telling of Removal-era sacrifice the Eastern Band now teaches.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how Hawks Nest and the coal-camp dead are remembered by their own descendants and churches.
- **Cumberland Plateau:** check enlistment and veterans' memorial customs against those of Pittsburgh's mill towns.
- **Latino families in north Georgia and western North Carolina:** check what parents and children say about migration as sacrifice.
- **Grandparent-led households:** check support groups' own words for what they gave up.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mining deaths, martyrs and soldiers; thin for women's household self-denial, which mostly lives in memoir and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century German for the Ephrata *Martyrs Mirror*; Slovak and Italian for parish memorial books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Tsali's story is contested and told differently by the Eastern Band and by outsiders. Disaster families may not want the word "sacrifice" applied to deaths they blame on companies. Wrongful-death suits belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** every martyr, miner and soldier pointer above can be named in full; none waits on the author.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Carnegie Hero Fund case summaries for awardees who died, 1904 to now, and sort them by home county inside the region.
2. Read the Ephrata *Martyrs Mirror* publication history and list which Appalachian Amish settlements hold the book.
3. Search MSHA's fatality reports for "rescue" and "attempting to".
4. Pull ACS grandparent-caregiver counts for the ARC's Central Appalachian counties and compare with the northern counties.
5. Ask the Museum of the Cherokee People's archivist for the Band's published accounts of Tsali and of the families who stayed in 1838.
