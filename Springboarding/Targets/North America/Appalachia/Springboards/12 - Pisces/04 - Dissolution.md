# Appalachia: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee; Seneca; Pennsylvania German; Rusyn, Welsh and Polish for language-shift work; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tennessee Valley reservoir towns (Norris, Fontana, Watauga):** check how former residents' reunions and church homecomings keep a drowned place together.
- **Seneca Nation, Allegany Territory:** check the Nation's own publications on Kinzua before any outside study.
- **Pittsburgh's mill towns:** check Slovak, Rusyn, Polish and Hungarian parish mergers and closures for how each congregation marks its end.
- **Southern West Virginia's coal counties:** check population loss in the census series against school and church closures.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the immersion school's reports for how the Eastern Band frames language loss.
- **Holiness and Pentecostal congregations against Primitive and Old Regular Baptists:** check how each describes being overtaken in worship, or declines to.
- **Melungeon families of Hancock County, Tenn.:** check family histories on an identity that faded and was later revived.
- **Out-migrant families in Ohio cities (comparison only):** check whether second-generation accounts describe a lost tie to the hills.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for removals, disasters and industrial decline; thin for ecstatic experience outside the holiness churches and for dementia talk.
- **Languages needed:** Seneca and Cherokee for language-loss sources; Rusyn and Slovak for parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relocated graves and drowned towns are family griefs; Kinzua is a treaty grievance for the Seneca. The proposed definition may change.
- **Held for review:** drowned towns, closed mills and language-loss sources raised no question for the author.
