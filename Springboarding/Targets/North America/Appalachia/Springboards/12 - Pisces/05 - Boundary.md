# Appalachia: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English and Pittsburghese; Cherokee; Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Polish and Hungarian. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how land in trust, enrollment and community names (Big Cove, Birdtown, Snowbird) set lines inside the Band, as the Band describes them.
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check surface owners' accounts of gates and fences put up by mineral owners.
- **Great Smoky Mountains and Shenandoah park lands:** check descendants' accounts of park lines and cemetery access.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities of Appalachian Ohio and Pennsylvania:** check each affiliation's line with neighbors and with other Anabaptists.
- **Black Appalachians in Knoxville, Asheville and the coal camps:** check maps and oral histories of where the color line ran.
- **Melungeon families:** check how family historians and the Melungeon Heritage Association describe the group's edge.
- **Latino newcomers in Dalton and Gainesville (Georgia):** check school and church records for lines drawn by language.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns:** check parish maps of ethnic territories inside one town.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land, commons, parks and race lines; thin for personal and family boundaries, which need oral history and advice columns.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee place and town names; Pennsylvania German church terms; Spanish for school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** cession lines and the Qualla Boundary are matters of Cherokee sovereignty; give the Eastern Band's account first. Melungeon DNA findings are disputed. Deeds, trespass and title belong to Libra; customs only here.
- **Held for review:** the land, race and dialect sources here are cleared to name; none awaits a ruling.
