# Appalachia: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Welsh, Polish and Italian for the coal and steel towns. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check whether pills, drink and leaving were the escapes named in oral histories from the 1980s onward.
- **The Great Smoky Mountains tourist corridor:** check how residents who work in the escape trade describe their own leisure.
- **Pittsburgh and the mill towns:** check bars, social clubs and lodge halls as places of relief after shifts, through parish and union papers.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check the Band's own reporting on gaming income, recovery programs and youth.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how rumspringa differs between affiliations and settlements.
- **Black Appalachians in the coal camps:** check oral histories for music, baseball and church outings as relief.
- **Latino newcomers in Gainesville (Georgia) and Dalton:** check weekend soccer leagues and dances as relief from plant work.
- **Out-migrants in Ohio and Michigan (comparison only):** check whether the trip home functioned as an escape in reverse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for drink and drugs (and heavily reported by outside media); thinner for ordinary leisure, reading and daydreaming.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German for Amish youth sources; Spanish for league and parish materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the opioid years are a field of outside stereotyping (pejorative nicknames for the drug and the region); favor the region's own reporters and families. Drug law and prosecutions belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none of the escape pointers needed setting aside under the standing rules.
