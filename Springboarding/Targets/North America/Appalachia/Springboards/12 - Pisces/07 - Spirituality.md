# Appalachia: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee; Seneca; Pennsylvania German; Welsh; Slovak and Rusyn; Italian; Spanish. **Concept in one line:** a relation to what is taken as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside the church (religion as institution is Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primitive, Old Regular and Regular Baptists of the Central Appalachian coalfields:** check what counts as a true experience of grace for each, and who may tell it.
- **Holiness and Pentecostal churches of east Tennessee and north Georgia:** check testimony services against the Baptists' restraint.
- **Shenandoah Valley Mennonites and Brethren:** check singing and quiet devotion against revival styles.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check which teachings the Band shares publicly and which stay inside the community.
- **Seneca Nation on the Allegany Territory:** check Longhouse and Christian Seneca practice as each describes itself.
- **Rusyn, Slovak, Polish and Italian Catholic and Orthodox parishes:** check pilgrimages, home blessings and feast days.
- **Black congregations in Knoxville, Asheville and the coal camps:** check anniversary programs and homecoming booklets for how members describe being moved in worship.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Protestant experience in Southern and Central Appalachia; thinner for Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish and Black practice; national surveys rarely break out the region.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee and Seneca for sacred terms (only as shared); Welsh hymn texts; Slovak and Church Slavonic for parish materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee and Seneca ceremonies are closed; respect the limits each Nation sets. Church doctrine and membership belong to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** two spiritual communities set aside: Buddhist practice (an open question for the author) and South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
