# Appalachia: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Pennsylvania German; Cherokee (the syllabary press); Spanish; Polish, Slovak and Italian. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death), with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Primitive and Old Regular Baptists against holiness churches:** check whether yielding is a quiet waiting on grace or a felt moment at the altar.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities of Appalachian Ohio, Big Valley and the Shenandoah:** check how yieldedness shapes baptism, dress and church discipline in each affiliation.
- **Moravians of the Wachovia tract:** check when and why the lot was given up, in the church's own records.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check how the Band's histories distinguish yielding land, refusing removal and staying.
- **Coal and steel strikers:** check union and family memories of lost strikes (Blair Mountain, Homestead) for the words used.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whether *ni modo* and *entregarse a Dios* appear in clinic and recovery settings, as speakers explain them.
- **Black Appalachians:** check funeral programs and sermons from coal-camp churches for the phrasing of a death accepted or resisted.
- **Patients and families in the coalfields and the northern counties:** check hospice workers' accounts of stopping treatment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious yielding and for labor defeats; thin for everyday letting go, which needs oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania German and the *Ausbund*'s older German; Cherokee for the *Phoenix*; Spanish for recovery groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fatalism" has been pinned on the region by outside health writers; record it as a claim. Serpent handling is a practice with deaths and press attention; use believers' accounts first. Surrender to police and courts belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** recovery, hospice and Anabaptist sources on yielding are all nameable; nothing is waiting.
