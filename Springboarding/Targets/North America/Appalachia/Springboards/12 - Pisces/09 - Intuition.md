# Appalachia: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; the Scots and Ulster layer; Pennsylvania German; Welsh; Arabic (Lebanese and Syrian families); Spanish; Italian. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Cumberland Plateau and the Kentucky foothills:** check whether death forerunners are still told, and by which generation.
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check miners' and widows' accounts of feelings before disasters, in disaster oral histories.
- **Old Regular Baptists against holiness and Pentecostal churches:** check whose inner promptings are trusted in each, and how they are tested.
- **Pennsylvania German and Welsh communities of the northern edge:** check almanac use and death-omen lore against the southern counties.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check whether the Band's language lessons include words for dreams, warnings and signs, and use only those.
- **Lebanese and Syrian families:** check how cup-reading is treated at home and by the parish.
- **Latino newcomers:** check whether *corazonada* and dream talk show up in clinic and parish settings.
- **Black Appalachians:** check coal-camp and church oral histories for dream and sign lore, since collectors rarely asked.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for omen and death lore in older collections; thin in surveys, which rarely sample the region on hunches or psychic belief.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic for family sources; Welsh and Pennsylvania German for older almanacs and chapel lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Cherokee divination formulas recorded by James Mooney in the 1890s are restricted knowledge; read only what the Eastern Band shares. Outside writers have used omen lore to paint the region as credulous; treat that as a claim about the writers.
- **Held for review:** omen, almanac and dowsing sources needed no setting aside.
