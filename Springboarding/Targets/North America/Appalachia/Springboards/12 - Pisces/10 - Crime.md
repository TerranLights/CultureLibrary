# Appalachia: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee; Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Italian, Polish and Slovak. **Concept in one line:** what counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how those who commit it are seen. Here only the moral, social and religious side; the law is researched in Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check which wrongs get blamed on "outsiders" (companies, dealers, the state) and which on neighbors.
- **Primitive and Old Regular Baptists against holiness churches:** check how each restores or excludes a wrongdoer, in their minutes.
- **Anthracite and bituminous immigrant communities (Irish, Italian, Slovak, Polish):** check how parishes and lodges remembered men the companies called criminals.
- **Black Appalachians:** check how Black churches and newspapers recorded lynchings and expulsions the white press ignored.
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check what the Band publishes on harm, balance and repair, apart from tribal court law.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how confession before the church works for members who did wrong, as the communities describe it.
- **Latino newcomers:** check how Spanish-language parish and radio talk frames immigration status as a moral question, beside the English-language local press.
- **Prison towns (Big Stone Gap, Wise County, McCreary County):** check how residents talk about the incarcerated people in their midst.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in feud history, ballads and church minutes; thin for everyday views of minor wrongs, which need oral history and local press.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for parish radio; Cherokee for the Band's own harm-and-repair terms; Slovak and Polish for lodge papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "feuding, lawless hillbilly" is a long-running outside image; prefer studies that test it. Lynching records name victims whose families still live in the region. Criminal law, courts and Cherokee legal history (for example, older writing on Cherokee clan law) go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** church-discipline, feud and lynching sources are all named above; none was kept back.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Southern Baptist Historical Library's microfilm list for Primitive and Old Regular Baptist churches in Letcher, Pike and Wise counties.
2. Download EJI's county data and filter to the region's states.

4. Search the Library of Virginia for "Sidna Allen" and "Hillsville" ballad sheets.
5. Search the *Up the Ridge* film's site or distributor for its interview list.
