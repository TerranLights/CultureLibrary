# Appalachia: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Appalachia, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Appalachian English; Cherokee (with older transliterations); Pennsylvania German; Spanish; Italian, Hungarian and Welsh. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Central Appalachian coalfields:** check flood, mine-disaster and black-lung fears in the words of miners' families and local papers.
- **Western North Carolina and the Blue Ridge:** check Helene survivors' accounts against older flood memories (1916, 1940).
- **Eastern Band of Cherokee:** check which feared beings the Band's storytellers tell publicly, and to children.
- **Black Appalachians:** check oral histories of travel through sundown towns and coal-camp life.
- **Latino newcomers in east Tennessee, north Georgia and western North Carolina:** check fear of enforcement in parish, school and clinic records.
- **Pennsylvania German and Amish communities:** check witch and hex lore in older sources against present practice.
- **Oak Ridge and Knoxville:** check how radiation and secrecy fears are told across generations of workers.
- **Ohio Valley towns (Point Pleasant, Huntington):** check how the unexplained and real disasters mix in local memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in tales, ballads and disaster reports; thin in survey data below state level.
- **Languages needed:** Cherokee for being names (only as the Band shares them); Spanish for raid coverage; Pennsylvania German for hex lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Cherokee stories may be kept for certain seasons or listeners; follow the Band's guidance. Raid and flood coverage involves living families. Outside horror (the "menacing hillbilly") is an outside image; keep it as a source on outsiders' fear. Fear of crime goes to this sign's Crime sheet; policing to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** tales, disaster reports and raid coverage raised no case for the author.
