# CSA: Domain and folkways, self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English and its regional varieties, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French and Louisiana Creole (Kouri-Vini), Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Mikasuki, Cherokee, Lumbee English. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland whites of the Tidewater and Deep South against Piedmont and Ozark uplanders:** test Fischer's lines on kin naming.
- **African Americans of the Black Belt and Delta against those of Atlanta, Houston, Memphis:** check whether naming and church dress differ between rural and city congregations.
- **Gullah Geechee of the Sea Islands:** check whether naming survives in Gullah-led sources or only in outsiders' collections.
- **Cajuns and Creoles of color in Acadiana and New Orleans:** separate French nickname systems from Catholic sacramental naming, and see whether the records keep the two peoples apart.
- **Tejanos of the Rio Grande Valley and San Antonio:** compare saint's-day naming and *compadrazgo* with Anglo Texas.
- **Native nations (Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Seminole, Miccosukee, Coushatta, Houma, Lumbee):** check what each shares publicly on names and regalia, and who speaks for it.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast and Cuban Floridian families:** compare heritage and American names across generations.
- **Jewish, Lebanese, Greek, German and Czech Southerners:** check whether heritage naming customs held, blended or lapsed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black and white naming and dress (archives, narratives, name tables); thin for Native naming (much is not public) and for Lebanese and Vietnamese Southern families, where community archives lead.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled (pre-war lands carried: USA); this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the American South.
- **Languages needed:** all listed above, plus Timucua and early Spanish for the Pareja manual.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** names imposed under slavery; racial classification of persons in records; Native names and ceremonies only as the nations share them; colorism; Confederate-era place and school names are contested memory.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose naming customs are set aside here under the standing rule.
