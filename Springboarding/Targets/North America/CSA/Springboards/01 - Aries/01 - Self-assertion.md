# CSA: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English (with Texas and Lowcountry varieties), African American English, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Louisiana French, Vietnamese, Haitian Creole (South Florida), Cherokee, Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Women by race and class:** check what white elite, Black working-class and Tejana daughters were told about speaking up, and who enforced it.
- **Black Belt and Delta towns against Atlanta and Houston:** trace when the sanction words for Black assertion faded or changed, place by place.
- **Piedmont mill villages:** compare collective claims in strikes with individual claims at home and church.
- **Anglo and Tejano Texans:** test whether Johnstone's Texas talk findings reach Spanish-dominant South Texas speakers.
- **Cuban and Haitian Miami:** compare access to public voice (radio, office, press) between two exile and immigrant streams.
- **Vietnamese of New Orleans East and the Mississippi coast:** check the record of the 2006 Chef Menteur landfill fight for who spoke for the community.
- **Cherokee, Muscogee and Choctaw nations:** compare how each recorded its own public claims (print, petitions, council minutes).
- **Jewish Southerners in the civil-rights years:** check which rabbis and congregations spoke publicly and which stayed silent, and why.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for civil-rights and labor speaking-up; mixed for everyday assertion, where politeness studies are scattered; thin for Native council speech and Vietnamese Gulf Coast civic life.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish (two varieties), Haitian Creole for Immokalee and Little Haiti, Vietnamese, Louisiana French, the Cherokee syllabary for the *Phoenix*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racialized sanction words; the armed self-defense record; honor-culture history is contested memory. Stand-your-ground and similar laws belong to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; pointers on their civic and workplace speaking-up are set aside under the standing rule.
