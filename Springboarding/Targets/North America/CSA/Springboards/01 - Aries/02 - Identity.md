# CSA: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Louisiana Creole, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Cherokee, Muscogee, Texas German. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Born Southerners and transplants in Atlanta, Charlotte, Houston and Florida:** check who claims the regional label and who declines it.
- **Great Migration families and their returnees:** check how return migrants define home and where their children place themselves.
- **Cajuns, Creoles of color and Anglo Louisianans:** compare self-labels with the labels in parish records.
- **Tejanos and recent Mexican and Central American arrivals:** check which label each generation answers to, and when.
- **Cuban exiles by wave (1959-62, Mariel 1980, the 1990s rafters):** compare self-descriptions across arrivals.
- **Lumbee, Houma, Melungeons and the Redbones of Louisiana:** separate each people's own name from outsiders' labels; sources for the last two are thin.
- **Removed and remaining nations:** compare Mississippi Choctaw with the Choctaw Nation, Poarch Creek with the Muscogee Nation, Florida Seminole and Miccosukee with the Seminole Nation, as each frames itself.
- **German, Czech, Wendish and Polish Texans:** check where identity moved from language to festival and church.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black and white regional identity (surveys, literature, archives); mixed for Louisiana's French-speaking peoples; thin for Redbones, Isleños and the Mississippi coast's Vietnamese.
- **Languages needed:** Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini, Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw and Cherokee for the nations' own materials, German for Hill Country church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Confederate symbols as identity badges are contested memory, presented with all sides named; passing and racial classification; membership in a Native nation is that nation's decision, not a research finding.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their identity sources are set aside under the standing rule.
