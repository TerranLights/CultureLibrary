# CSA: Autonomy (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English (including Ozark speech), African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee, Choctaw, Mikasuki. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black landowners (Mound Bayou, Mississippi; Black Belt freeholders) against tenants and sharecroppers:** check how land changed who decided a family's work and schooling.
- **Lowland plantation counties against Piedmont and Ozark farm counties:** state indices hide this split; look for county or community data.
- **Baptist, Churches of Christ, Methodist, Pentecostal and Catholic congregations:** compare who decides, from the pew to the bishop.
- **Women across race and class:** check memoirs for who chose husbands, work and moves, and when that shifted.
- **Tejano, Mexican-born and Cuban families:** check how career and marriage choices are negotiated with parents, by generation.
- **Muscogee towns and the Muscogee national government; Seminole and Miccosukee:** check each nation's own account of where self-rule sits.
- **Vietnamese Catholic families on the Gulf Coast:** check the parish's role beside the family's in young people's choices.
- **Gullah families on heirs' property:** check how shared land shapes each member's freedom to move or sell.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; state-level indices exist, household decision data are scattered, slavery-era self-rule is well documented, Native internal self-government is best found in the nations' own publications.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Vietnamese, Louisiana French, Muscogee, Choctaw and Mikasuki for the nations' materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Tuskegee study and medical consent; reproductive choice debates, to be framed with all positions named; "states' rights" rhetoric and its link to slavery and segregation is contested memory. Tribal sovereignty and land titles as law belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their family decision-making are set aside under the standing rule.
