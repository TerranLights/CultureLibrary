*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Durham, Atlanta's Sweet Auburn and Black Belt towns:** check where Black enterprise found room under segregation, and where it did not.
- **Lebanese, Jewish and Chinese storekeepers in the Delta and small towns:** check how each family tells the founding, and who worked in it.
- **Cuban exile founders and Haitian, Nicaraguan and Venezuelan arrivals in South Florida:** compare the credit, networks and reception each had when starting out.
- **Tejano *mutualistas* and Anglo service clubs (Rotary, Lions) in Texas:** check who organized aid, and for whom.
- **Vietnamese families on the Gulf Coast:** check which trades they entered and what made entry possible (boats, licenses, savings clubs).
- **Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw and Seminole nations:** check nation-run enterprises as collective initiative, in each nation's own reports.
- **Women in home demonstration clubs and church missionary unions:** check how women started projects inside institutions men led.
- **Acadiana volunteers and official responders:** compare after-action accounts of who moved first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business statistics and founders' lives; thin for informal enterprise (savings clubs, side trades), which needs community ethnography; founders' memoirs are self-told myths.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Vietnamese, Arabic for Lebanese family papers, Louisiana French, Cherokee.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** colonial and antebellum enterprise rested on enslaved labor; state it plainly. The Washington-Du Bois dispute and the *Black Rice* thesis are contested; present both sides.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their business-founding studies are set aside under the standing rule.
