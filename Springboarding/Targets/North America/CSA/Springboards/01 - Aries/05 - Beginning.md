## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Baptists of the Sea Islands and the mainland:** check whether the seeking period and praise-house entry differ from mainland conversion.
- **White evangelical, Pentecostal and Catholic Acadian believers:** compare how a new religious self begins (revival altar, Spirit baptism, first communion).
- **Muscogee, Seminole and Miccosukee communities:** check what each shares publicly about renewal ceremonies, and leave the rest alone.
- **Tejanos, Mexican immigrants and Cuban Floridians:** compare new-year and coming-of-age customs across arrival times.
- **Vietnamese of New Orleans East and Houston's Bellaire area:** check Tết customs as two communities keep them.
- **Croatian, Isleño, Cajun and Vietnamese fishing families:** compare season openings on the Gulf Coast.
- **Katrina evacuees who stayed in Houston or Atlanta and those who returned:** check how each tells the new start.
- **St. Augustine's Spanish founding and Timucua presence; Charleston's founding and the Kiawah:** compare how each origin is told, and by whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious rebirth, emancipation and disaster recovery; mixed for immigrant new-year customs; thin and partly restricted for Native renewal ceremonies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Vietnamese, Greek, Louisiana French, Muscogee; early Spanish for Cabeza de Vaca.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ceremonial knowledge is shared only by the nations' choice; "discovery" and "first settler" claims are contested; the racial politics of post-Katrina rebuilding are contested.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their new-year and life-cycle customs are set aside under the standing rule.
