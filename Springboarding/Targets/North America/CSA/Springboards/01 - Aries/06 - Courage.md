# CSA: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta and Black Belt local organizers against Birmingham and Atlanta leaders:** check whose courage is commemorated and whose, often women's and rural people's, is not.
- **Confederate veterans' memory against Southern Unionists (Jones County, Mississippi; the Hill Country Germans killed on the Nueces in 1862):** check how each is honored or forgotten.
- **Tejano and Anglo World War II veterans:** compare recognition, from medals to burial.
- **Choctaw, Seminole and Lumbee peoples:** check each nation's own telling of its warriors and defenders.
- **Cuban exiles and Haitian boat arrivals:** compare how dangerous crossings are framed in each community's media.
- **Vietnamese veterans of the southern republic's forces on the Gulf Coast:** check their memorials and the stories they tell.
- **Women's endurance and men's physical courage:** check whether sources treat the first as courage at all.
- **Gulf oil, shrimping and Piney Woods logging families:** check how danger at work is spoken of at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and civil-rights courage; mixed for everyday bravery; Native warrior traditions only as the nations share them.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Louisiana French, Choctaw and Muscogee for the nations' own materials, early Spanish and Portuguese for the De Soto accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching and Klan violence; Confederate monuments and the "Lost Cause" account are contested memory, to be set beside Black, Unionist and Native accounts; veterans' trauma.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their military and civic-bravery records are set aside under the standing rule.
