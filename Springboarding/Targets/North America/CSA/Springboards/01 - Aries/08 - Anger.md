# CSA: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowland and upland white Southerners, and old and new suburbs:** test the herding-ancestry claim in honor studies at county level.
- **Black Southerners under Jim Crow and after:** check how memoir, blues and sermon each carried anger that could not be shown.
- **Women by race and class:** compare display rules in memoir and song, from church to kitchen.
- **Tejanos and Mexican immigrants:** check whether *muina* and *bilis* still name anger as illness, by generation.
- **Cuban Miami during the 2000 Elián González dispute:** check how collective anger was framed in Spanish- and English-language media.
- **Vietnamese families on the Gulf Coast:** check whether forbearance words such as *nhịn* lead family advice.
- **Muscogee people:** check how the Red Stick war is remembered within the nation, and by whom.
- **Descendants on each side of Wilmington 1898 and Rosewood 1923:** compare how anger and grief are handed down.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honor and violence studies and civil-rights anger; thin for Vietnamese and Haitian anger idioms in the South.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Louisiana French, Gullah, Muscogee.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "violent South" and "angry" labels attached to any group are stereotypes to avoid; massacres and lynchings; domestic violence; folk illness treated with respect. Crime and punishment belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their emotion idioms and folk illness are set aside under the standing rule.
