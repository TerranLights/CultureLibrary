# CSA: Domain and folkways, possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English and its regional varieties, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Louisiana Creole (Kouri-Vini), Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Cherokee, Muscogee, Mikasuki. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how it is held (money, resources, thrift and display, food ways, dress ways, wealth ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta and Black Belt households against Piedmont town households:** check which goods marked getting by and which marked getting ahead, decade by decade.
- **Gullah Geechee basket makers and Lowcountry resort buyers:** compare how a craft's value is reckoned by maker and by buyer.
- **Cajun and Creole South Louisiana:** separate the *boucherie* and *lagniappe* customs by parish, and see who still keeps them.
- **Tejano Rio Grande Valley and Anglo Hill Country:** compare market habits (*pulga*, credit at the corner store, the feed store).
- **Cuban, Venezuelan and other Latino Miami:** check how exile shaped what families brought, saved or rebuilt.
- **Vietnamese shrimping families of Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas:** check what the boat means as property, savings and household base.
- **Choctaw of Mississippi, Seminole and Miccosukee of Florida, Poarch Creek of Alabama:** check what each nation publishes on traditional and present goods of value.
- **Greek, Lebanese and Jewish merchant families of small towns:** check what their store ledgers show about customers' buying.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for consumer, food and economic history; mixed for dress below the elite; thin for Native goods of value beyond museum catalogs, and for Vietnamese and Lebanese family economies, where community archives lead.
- **Lands:** which lands the CSA holds is not yet settled (pre-war lands carried: USA); the Taurus sheets address the core Southern states.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for border markets and Miami, Vietnamese for Gulf Coast savings groups, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini for parish customs, Choctaw and Cherokee for money words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved people were held as property, a history any study of Southern possessions must state plainly; food and dress stereotypes of poor whites, Black Southerners and Cajuns; grave goods from mound sites belong to the descendant nations' decisions. Consumer law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their store-owner and motel-owner economies are set aside under the standing rule.
