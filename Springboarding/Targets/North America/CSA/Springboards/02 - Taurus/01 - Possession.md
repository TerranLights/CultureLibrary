# CSA: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Louisiana Creole, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Koasati. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Freedpeople's and planters' households after 1865:** compare the claims files for what each side counted as its own.
- **Gullah Geechee and Black Belt families:** check whether grave decoration with personal objects survives in both, and who keeps the graves.
- **Cajun prairie and New Orleans Creole families:** check what goes into a hope chest (*coffre*) or an armoire, and who inherits it.
- **Tejana households of San Antonio and the Valley:** compare altar keeping between generations and between town and ranch.
- **Cuban exiles by wave:** check what each wave could carry, and what that does to keepsakes.
- **Vietnamese Catholic and Buddhist households on the Gulf Coast:** compare what each altar holds.
- **Coushatta, Choctaw and Seminole basket and patchwork makers:** check which objects are for sale and which are kept from sale, as each nation states.
- **Ozark and Hill Country farm households:** check sheds, barns and farm auctions as where goods are stored and let go.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black and white property history and probate; mixed for present-day keeping (scattered folklore and museum studies); thin for Native and Vietnamese household objects, where the communities' own publications come first.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for altar studies, Vietnamese for shrine customs, Louisiana French for chests and armoires, Koasati and Choctaw for basket terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved people were themselves treated as possessions, which must be stated plainly beside any study of what they owned; grave goods and mound objects removed by collectors are a matter for the descendant nations; conjure objects may be private. Pawn, theft and repatriation law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their household shrines and keepsakes are set aside under the standing rule.
