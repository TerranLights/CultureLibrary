# CSA: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English and Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Muscogee, Cherokee. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black landowning and tenant families of the Black Belt and Delta:** check what each called "ours" as tenancy and land loss came.
- **Gullah Geechee of Sapelo, St. Helena and Hilton Head:** compare family-land claims with resort and tax-sale buyers' titles.
- **Tejano grantee descendants and Anglo ranch owners in South Texas:** check how each side tells the passage of the old grants.
- **Cajun long-lot farmers and Creoles of color of the Cane River:** compare inherited holdings divided by equal shares over generations.
- **Muscogee, Cherokee and Choctaw before removal, and the Mississippi Choctaw, Poarch Creek and Seminole who remained:** check how each nation describes holding land then and now.
- **Upland white smallholders and lowland planters:** compare the open-range fight across the Piedmont, the Wiregrass and the Ozarks.
- **Owners of manufactured homes on rented lots:** check what owning the home but not the ground means; start with ACS tenure by county.
- **Cuban exiles and Vietnamese boat owners:** compare claims kept to property left behind with property built anew.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land history, farm tenure and housing; mixed for present-day talk about ownership; thin for each Native nation's own tenure teaching, best found in the nations' publications.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Texas grants and Cuban claims, French for colonial notarial acts, Vietnamese for boat ownership, Choctaw, Muscogee and Cherokee for land words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved people were legally owned, which must be stated plainly as history; removal and land loss are contested memory, with each nation's account given first; Confederate land and monument disputes. Title law, partition and tax sales belong to Libra; the spirit of property law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their hotel and store ownership records are set aside under the standing rule.
