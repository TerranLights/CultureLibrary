# CSA: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Lumbee English, Chickasaw. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Baptist, Methodist and Holiness congregations:** check how each teaches worth (conduct, grace, the Spirit) and who is held up.
- **Planter heirs and poor whites of the same counties:** compare the words each uses for the other's worth.
- **Cajuns and Creoles of color:** check when French stopped being a mark of shame for each, and whether at the same time.
- **Tejanos of San Antonio and of the Valley:** compare claims of worth through citizenship (LULAC, 1929) and through the Chicano movement *check*.
- **Lumbee, Houma and Coharie peoples:** check how each tells its fight for recognized standing; sources for the last two are scarcer.
- **Cuban exile professionals who lost rank in Miami:** compare what worth rested on before and after exile.
- **Vietnamese shrimpers and their children:** check how worth through work at sea is weighed against worth through schooling.
- **Men in closed mill and plant towns of the Piedmont:** check how layoffs are spoken of, and what replaces work as a ground of worth.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honor, respectability and civil-rights history; mixed for everyday self-esteem, where state-level psychology data are thin; thin for Houma, Coharie and Vietnamese sources, where community organizations lead.
- **Languages needed:** Louisiana French for school-shame memories, Spanish for Tejano and Cuban sources, Vietnamese for shrimpers' accounts, Chickasaw terms for honor titles in the nation's own materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs and sanction words ("uppity", class slurs for poor whites, the Cajun slur reclaimed by some) to be described, not used as headings; colorism; suicide data handled with care. Honor-related violence and its legal treatment belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their sense of standing in Southern towns are set aside under the standing rule.
