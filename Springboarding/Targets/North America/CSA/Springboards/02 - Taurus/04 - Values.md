# CSA: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, and how those things are ranked; and valuation, how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **White evangelical, Black Protestant and Catholic South Louisiana communities:** check how each ranks faith, family and public life, using the same survey items.
- **Gullah praise-house communities and mainland Black Baptist churches:** compare the forms of community discipline that guarded shared values.
- **Lumbee churches of the Burnt Swamp Baptist Association** *check*: check how church life carries the community's priorities.
- **Tejano and recent Mexican immigrant families:** check whether scholars' labels (*familismo*, *respeto*) hold up or are only assumed.
- **Cuban exiles of the 1960s and Cubans arriving after 1995:** compare what each treats as beyond compromise.
- **Vietnamese Catholic and Buddhist families of the Gulf Coast:** compare what parish and temple teach the young to put first.
- **Muscogee, Seminole and Miccosukee communities:** check what each nation publishes of its own teachings, and what it keeps private.
- **Jewish Southerners and German and Czech Texans:** check how minority congregations stated their priorities beside the Baptist majority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious and political values (surveys, church records); mixed for everyday ranking at home; thin for Native teachings outside the nations' own publications and for Vietnamese temple life.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano and Cuban sources, Vietnamese for parish and temple material, Louisiana French for Cajun Catholic life, Muscogee for ceremonial terms the nation chooses to share.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Lost Cause and the defense of slavery as a "value" must be presented as history and contested memory, with all sides named; culture-war topics framed with all positions; ceremonial knowledge (the Green Corn and others) only as the nations share it. Legal codes of morality belong to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their temple and family teachings are set aside under the standing rule.
