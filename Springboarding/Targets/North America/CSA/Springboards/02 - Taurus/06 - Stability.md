# CSA: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sea Island families and resort newcomers on Hilton Head and St. Simons:** check how each side uses the settled-against-newcomer distinction.
- **Black families who stayed in the Delta, who left for Chicago and Detroit, and who returned:** compare what each says kept or broke continuity.
- **Cane River Creoles, Cajun prairie towns and New Orleans neighborhoods:** check how long families stayed in one parish.
- **Tejano families of the old *porciones* and recent border arrivals:** compare depth of residence and what counts as "from here".
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Poarch Creek and Florida Seminole:** check how each nation describes continuity on its remaining lands.
- **Village de L'Est Vietnamese and Biloxi Vietnamese:** compare settled parish communities before and after Katrina.
- **Fast-growing suburbs of Atlanta, Charlotte and Austin:** check how long-time residents and arrivals speak of change.
- **Hill Country German Texans:** check family continuity on land since the 1840s, against Anglo and Tejano neighbors.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for migration, residence and political history; mixed for everyday routine; thin for each Native nation's own account, found in the nations' own publications.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for *Los Pastores* and Cuban hometown groups, Louisiana French for parish life, Vietnamese for community data, Choctaw and Muscogee for place names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Great Migration was driven by terror and want as well as hope; the one-party South rested on disfranchisement; continuity claims around Confederate symbols are contested memory; sacred sites only as the nations present them.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands and studies of their settlement; a mobility dataset led by a South Asian-heritage scholar; both set aside under the standing rule.
