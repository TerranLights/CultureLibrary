# CSA: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Mikasuki and Creek (for Seminole sources). **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta planter heirs and Delta Black landowners:** compare which family assets lasted from 1900 to now, and how each tells it.
- **Old-line New Orleans and Mobile carnival society and Black and Creole carnival organizations:** check how each displays wealth and who is let in.
- **Anglo oil and ranch fortunes and Tejano ranching families:** compare whose wealth survived the twentieth century in South Texas, and why.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Poarch Creek and Mississippi Choctaw:** compare how each nation's own publications describe the use of gaming and enterprise income.
- **Cuban exile business families of Miami and later Latin American wealth in Brickell and Doral:** check how the two speak of making money.
- **Vietnamese business families of Houston and the Gulf Coast:** check the business paths the families themselves describe, and their words for success.
- **Prosperity-gospel congregations and older Baptist and Methodist churches:** compare sermons on whether wealth is a blessing or a danger.
- **Jewish, Lebanese and Greek merchant families:** check how store wealth moved into the professions across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for planter, oil and racial-wealth-gap data; mixed for present-day wealth display and talk; thin for Native gaming wealth as the nations see it outside a few studies, and for Vietnamese and Lebanese family fortunes.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano ranching and Cuban business sources, Vietnamese for Houston business press, Louisiana French for Acadiana's oil-era change, Mikasuki and Creek for Seminole terms the tribe chooses to share.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Old South's wealth rested on enslaved people's bodies and labor, which must be stated plainly; the wealth gap by race is a measured fact, to be shown without blame on any group; tribal per-capita payments are each nation's business. Inheritance and tax rules go to Libra; tax fairness as legal culture goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their business and professional fortunes are set aside under the standing rule.
