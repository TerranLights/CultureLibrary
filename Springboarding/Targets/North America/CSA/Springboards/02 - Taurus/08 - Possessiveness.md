# CSA: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Louisiana Creole, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Planter-class, yeoman and enslaved households before 1865:** compare whose marriages could be claimed by whom, and by what right.
- **Black Southern churches and blues audiences:** check how jealousy is judged in sermon and in song for the same years.
- **Creole New Orleans and Cajun prairie families:** compare courting rules and the guarding of daughters in the two French-speaking worlds.
- **Tejano families across three generations:** check when chaperonage faded and who decided.
- **Cuban exile families and the wider Miami public:** compare how the custody dispute was read inside and outside the exile community.
- **Oystermen of Plaquemines, Apalachicola and Galveston Bay (Croatian, Cajun, Black and Vietnamese):** check how claims to reefs are made and defended in each.
- **Hunters on leased land in Texas and Mississippi:** check how leaseholders and locals speak of access and trespass.
- **Native nations of the region:** check what each nation's publications say about sharing duties, and avoid outsiders' summaries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for songs, marriage history and violence statistics; mixed for everyday jealousy and stinginess; thin for Vietnamese and Native sources on couples, where community services and the nations' own publications are the route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for rancheras and curanderismo, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini for song texts, Vietnamese for family services.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence and its victims; enslaved people's marriages were not protected, which must be stated plainly; conjure practice may be private; the custody dispute remains contested memory in Miami. Custody, family and trespass law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of jealousy and control in their families are set aside under the standing rule.
