# CSA: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Mikasuki. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Seminole Tribe, Miccosukee Tribe and the independent Traditional Seminoles:** check how each describes holding out, and why some refused to join either tribe.
- **White Southern moderates and segregationists in 1954-1965:** compare who held firm, who yielded, and how each was named by its own side.
- **Black churches and civil-rights organizations:** check how steadfastness was taught and kept up in long campaigns.
- **Primitive Baptists, Churches of Christ and Holiness congregations:** compare how each guards its doctrine against change.
- **Tejano farmworkers and Rio Grande Valley landowners:** check how holding out on land and on wages are spoken of.
- **Cuban exiles of the first wave and later arrivals:** compare refusal and dialogue in exile politics across waves.
- **Vietnamese refugees and their American-born children:** check how flag and anti-communist stands are kept or set aside across generations.
- **Cajuns and Creoles who kept French at home against school punishment:** check how the refusal is remembered in each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective refusal (removal, segregation, civil rights); mixed for everyday stubbornness in families and work; thin for the independent Seminole families and for Vietnamese generational change, where community sources lead.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Cuban exile and Tejano strike sources, Vietnamese for flag and community debates, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini for language-keeping memories, Mikasuki for terms the Miccosukee choose to share.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Massive Resistance and civil-rights steadfastness must not be set side by side as equal "stubbornness"; describe each in its own terms and name the contested memory. Border-wall and eminent-domain law belong to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their community stands are set aside under the standing rule.
