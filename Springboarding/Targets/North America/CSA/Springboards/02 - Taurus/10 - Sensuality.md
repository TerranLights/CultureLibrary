# CSA: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee, Czech and German (Texas). **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Baptist and Church of Christ congregations and Catholic South Louisiana:** compare rules on dancing and drink, and when each changed.
- **Delta juke joints and Creole zydeco halls:** check who ran them, who came, and how churches spoke of them.
- **Tejano dance halls and German and Czech Texas halls:** compare the music, food and family presence at each kind of dance.
- **Gullah Geechee cooks and Charleston restaurant kitchens:** check who is named as the source of Lowcountry flavors.
- **Cuban Miami's *ventanitas* and Little Havana cafés:** check coffee and street food as daily shared pleasure, by generation.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast cooks and Cajun cooks:** compare how each tells the story of the crawfish boil they now share.
- **Muscogee, Seminole and Choctaw communities:** check what each nation chooses to share about ceremonial drinks and dances, and what stays private.
- **Mississippi Chinese grocery families and Lebanese families of the Delta:** check how their foods (kibbeh, egg rolls) entered Delta eating.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, music and dance; mixed for touch, scent and comfort, where sources are scattered; thin for Native ceremonial drinks and dances, which belong to the nations' choices.
- **Languages needed:** Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini for dance-hall and song sources, Spanish for conjunto and Cuban café culture, Vietnamese for Viet-Cajun food press, Czech and German for Texas hall records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** juke joints and Black pleasure spaces were often policed and judged; describe them through participants' accounts; ceremonial use of the Black drink only as the nations present it. Sexual pleasure belongs to Scorpio; alcohol and dry-county rules to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; studies of their food and festival pleasures in Southern cities are set aside under the standing rule.
