# CSA: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English, Louisiana French, Kouri-Vini, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Texas German, Choctaw, Muscogee, Gullah Geechee. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and what good communication is taken to be and to be for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white congregations of the same town:** compare the sermon's form and the congregation's spoken part, to separate church tradition from region.
- **Acadiana's Cajun and Creole French speakers:** check whether each speaks French on air, at home or only with elders, and who answers in English.
- **Tejano border towns against Hill Country German descendants:** compare how a home language faded in each, as speakers tell it.
- **Vietnamese shrimpers of the Gulf and their adult children:** check who did the talking with agencies and buyers, and at what age.
- **Miami's Cuban exiles across waves (1959-62, Mariel 1980, the 1990s rafters):** check how each speaks of and to the island.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee and Mississippi Choctaw:** see how each nation's own media chooses its language and its audience.
- **Rural Black Belt and Ozark households:** check how party lines and later cell coverage changed who talked to whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for preaching, letters, dialect and radio; mixed for everyday indirectness and silence (studied in pockets, not region-wide); thin for Native nations' talk norms beyond what each publishes.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for border folklore and exile radio; Vietnamese for spill-era reports in community media; French and Kouri-Vini to hear Lafayette broadcasts; German for Hill Country interviews; Choctaw and Muscogee for terms of speech and speakers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Confederate letters carry contested memory, so read them with Black soldiers' and freedpeople's letters; dialect spelling in old letters and tales has been used to mock; Native diplomatic records were written by colonists. Speech law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their community radio is not scouted here.
