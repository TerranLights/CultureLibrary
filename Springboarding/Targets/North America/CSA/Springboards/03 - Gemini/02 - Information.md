# CSA: Information (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Southern and African American), Spanish (Texas and Miami), Vietnamese, Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini, and the nations' languages for their own records. **Concept in one line:** what is known or reported about the world, and how it is gathered, passed on, trusted, withheld and used: news, facts, data, records.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta and Black Belt counties against Atlanta, Houston and Charlotte:** compare where news comes from when the local paper is gone and broadband is thin.
- **Black and white readers of the same town under segregation:** check which papers each read and which events each learned of.
- **Tejano South Texas against Cuban and Venezuelan Miami:** compare which Spanish-language outlets each trusts, and what role radio plays.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast families:** check which generation reads Vietnamese media and which reads English, and how news crosses between them.
- **Coastal Louisiana parishes, Cajun and Houma:** check how storm warnings were heard, believed and passed on, parish by parish.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Choctaw, Poarch Creek and Lumbee:** check each government's published rules on who may record and keep information about its members.
- **Rural white Ozark and Wiregrass readers:** check what the almanac, the county weekly and the church bulletin carried, and what replaced them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the press, disaster history and state records; mixed for current misinformation (fast-changing, much of it in private chats); thin for Native nations' rules on information, beyond their published policies.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for *La Prensa* and exile radio; Vietnamese for Gulf Coast outlets and fact-checks; French for parish weeklies and storm broadcasts; the nations' languages where their records are kept in them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Sovereignty Commission files name private people; the Tuskegee study must be stated plainly as history, with its harm to the men and families; Katrina rumor coverage carries race and class blame; Native nations may restrict data about members. Records law and access law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their news outlets are left for review.
