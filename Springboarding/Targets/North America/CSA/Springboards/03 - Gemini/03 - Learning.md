## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta and Black Belt Black families, by generation:** compare what grandparents, parents and children say they learned at home against at school, decade by decade.
- **Lumbee of Robeson County against the Seminole and Mississippi Choctaw:** check how each nation's own schools shaped who teaches and in which language.
- **Cajun and Creole families of Acadiana:** separate those who learned French at home from those who learned it later in immersion classes.
- **Tejano families of the Valley and of Houston:** check how *consejos* at home and English at school were weighed across generations.
- **Cuban, Nicaraguan and Venezuelan families of Miami:** check who learned in bilingual classes and who did not.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast families:** check how the weekend school, the church and the boat each teach.
- **White farm and Ozark families:** check what was learned working beside a parent, and when school took over.
- **Charleston and New Orleans craft families, Black and white:** compare how trades passed down.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black education history and for school records; mixed for learning at home and in trades; thin for Native nations' teaching outside what each nation shares.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for LULAC and Miami school records; French for Acadiana memories; Vietnamese for parish school materials; Choctaw for the nation's own teaching terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** laws against teaching the enslaved to read, and the Choctaw Academy's purpose, are history to state plainly; memories of punishment for speaking French or Spanish are painful and contested; some Native teaching is not for outsiders. School law and education policy go to Libra, Capricorn and Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their weekend schools are not covered here.
