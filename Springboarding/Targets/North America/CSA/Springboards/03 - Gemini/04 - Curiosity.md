# CSA: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Spanish (Texas and Miami), Cajun French, Vietnamese, Lebanese Arabic, the Southeastern nations' languages. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gullah Geechee Sea Island communities:** check how they have answered outside researchers and tourists, and what they now choose to show.
- **Lumbee, Seminole and Mississippi Choctaw:** compare each nation's rules for researchers and visitors, and what they keep closed.
- **Black and white families of the Delta and Black Belt:** check how genealogy searches differ when records name ancestors as property.
- **Cajun families and Acadian reunions:** check who takes part in roots tourism, by parish and age.
- **Houston and Space Coast families against rural Texas and Florida:** compare access to museums and science centers, and children's trips to them.
- **Vietnamese and Cuban families:** check what parents ask children to learn about the homeland, and what they leave unasked.
- **Lebanese, Greek and Jewish Southerners:** check family-history projects in their own societies for roots curiosity.
- **Small-town and city newcomers:** compare which personal questions are put to a newcomer, and when.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for natural history, tourism and genealogy; mixed for everyday nosiness (mostly in memoirs and fiction); thin for Native rules on asking, which each nation sets.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and Vietnamese for parents' and grandparents' questions about the homeland; French for Acadian reunion records; Arabic for Lebanese family histories; the nations' languages only with their consent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching photographs show real victims and must be handled as evidence of terror, not spectacle; ancient burials and Native origins belong to descendant nations' decisions; outsider studies of Gullah and Lumbee life carry old biases. Research-ethics law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; any pointer that would depend on them waits for review.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Catesby's prefaces and list the informants he names or describes.

3. Search the Texas Master Naturalist chapter list for county coverage.
4. Pull NEA survey tables for museum visits and reading, South against other regions.
