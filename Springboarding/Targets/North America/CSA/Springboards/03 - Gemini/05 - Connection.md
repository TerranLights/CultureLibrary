# CSA: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Kouri-Vini, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Greek. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families of the Delta and the Carolinas, with their kin in Northern cities:** check how reunions, letters and calls keep a scattered family linked across generations.
- **Lumbee, Poarch Creek and Seminole:** compare how each nation brings members home and receives other nations.
- **Tejanos of San Antonio and the Valley:** check how godparent ties work across the border and across class.
- **New Orleans neighborhoods, Black and Creole:** separate clubs, churches and lodges as ties, and see which survived the post-Katrina scattering.
- **Vietnamese of Versailles (New Orleans East) and Bellaire Boulevard, Houston:** check how home-district ties, parish and boat crews overlap.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Venezuelan Miami:** compare how each community sends money and goods home and what that does to ties.
- **Greek, Lebanese and German Texans:** check parish, festival and club records for how a small community keeps itself linked.
- **Rural white Wiregrass and Ozark towns:** check which ties ("kin to", church, the courthouse crowd) open doors and which close them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kin and fraternal history and for county-level network measures; mixed for present-day ties outside surveys; thin for Native clan ties, which the nations keep largely for members.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for San Fernando registers and Cuban remittance studies; Vietnamese for parish and home-district groups; French for Acadiana kin records; Greek for parish archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "good ol' boy" networks and cronyism touch living officials, so cite only published reporting; slavery broke kin ties, and records of enslaved families must be handled with that history stated; clan knowledge may be closed; remittances to Cuba are politically charged in Miami. Rules on favoritism in office go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and a social-network dataset led by a South Asian-heritage scholar; both sit outside this sheet.
