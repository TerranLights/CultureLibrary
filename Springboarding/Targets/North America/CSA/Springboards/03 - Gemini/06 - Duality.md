# CSA: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English, Gullah Geechee, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee. **Concept in one line** (proposed): how two-ness is lived: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Creoles of color in New Orleans and the Cane River, and Black and white neighbors:** check how each group places them, and how they place themselves.
- **Laredo, Brownsville and El Paso against interior Texas cities:** compare daily two-country lives with Tejano lives far from the line.
- **Cuban, Nicaraguan and Haitian second generations in Miami:** check which pairs each names (Cuban and American, island and exile, home and school).
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast children of shrimpers:** check how they move between boat, home and school talk.
- **Sea Island Gullah Geechee speakers:** check when and with whom they switch, by age and island.
- **Lumbee and other tri-racial communities:** check how outside categories have split them, and the nations' own responses.
- **Black and white church members who also play or follow secular music:** compare how each tradition speaks of the Saturday-Sunday pair.
- **Creek, Choctaw and Seminole:** see how each nation explains its own paired ideas, without outside framing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for passing, double consciousness and border life; mixed for everyday hypocrisy (memoir and fiction carry most of it); thin for Native paired ideas, which need the nations' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for border festivals and Miami youth surveys; Vietnamese for parents' side of the family story; French for Creole records; Muscogee for the town pair.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** passing and hidden families are painful history, and some descendants are living; "tri-racial" labels were imposed by outsiders; racial-category laws were part of segregation and belong to Libra and Capricorn; Native cosmology may be restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their bicultural experience is set apart for later review.
