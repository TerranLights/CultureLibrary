# CSA: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Southern and Black usage), Gullah Geechee, Cajun French and Kouri-Vini, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Lebanese Arabic. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Choctaw, Creek and Seminole families:** check each nation's own account of which relatives are brothers and sisters, before using outside kinship charts.
- **Descendants of the enslaved in Mississippi, Georgia and South Carolina:** check how siblings separated by sale or later migration were traced and reunited.
- **Cajun and Creole families of the prairie parishes:** compare large-family memories and the eldest's duties across generations.
- **Tejano households from Brownsville to San Antonio:** check *el mayor*'s role and the place of the *consentido*, by generation.
- **Vietnamese households of Biloxi and Port Arthur:** compare how *anh*, *chị* and *em* order the household and the boat crew.
- **Store-owning Jewish, Lebanese and Greek kin groups:** check how brothers shared and divided family stores.
- **Cuban families of Miami:** check how siblings split by exile (one in Havana, one in Miami) keep or lose the bond.
- **White Wiregrass and Ozark farm families:** check how land and the old home place pass among siblings.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fiction, slavery-era separation and family firms; mixed for birth order and rivalry in daily life; thin for Native kin terms beyond old studies and what the nations teach.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese for kin terms of address; Spanish for Tejano and Cuban family memoirs; Arabic for Lebanese family papers; French for parish family histories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the sale of enslaved siblings must be stated plainly; sibling violence and estrangement involve living people; Native kinship may be taught only within the nation. Inheritance law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their family-business siblings are left aside here.
