# CSA: Domain and folkways, home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English (Lowcountry, Black Belt, Texas, Ozark), Black English usage, Gullah Geechee, Cajun French, Kouri-Vini, Tejano Spanish, Miami Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Mikasuki. **Concept in one line:** the domain of home and family: what home is and who counts as family (household forms, roots, family memory, hospitality, fathering, building ways, family ways and child-rearing ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Seminole and Miccosukee families:** compare clan, camp and house as each nation describes them.
- **Black Belt and Delta families, and their kin in Atlanta and Houston:** check how city moves and returns changed who lives under one roof.
- **White Piedmont and Wiregrass households:** see how farm and mill village each set the household's shape.
- **Cajun prairie and bayou families; Creoles of color on Cane River:** compare godparent and household ties across parishes.
- **Tejano households of the Valley, Laredo and San Antonio:** check where grandparents and *compadres* stand, by generation.
- **Cuban, Venezuelan and Colombian Miami:** check how exile reshaped the grandparents' place.
- **Vietnamese households of Versailles (New Orleans) and Biloxi:** check which generations share a roof.
- **German, Czech and Greek Texans; Jewish and Lebanese families:** compare house forms and family firms with neighbors'.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census tables, house types and plantation-era records; mixed for fathering and hospitality; thin for Native household ways beyond old ethnographies and what each nation prints.
- **Lands:** the CSA's territory has not been fixed (its tracker carries the USA as pre-war land); the Cancer sheets take the Atlantic South and Florida west through Louisiana, Arkansas and Texas as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Kouri-Vini and parish French for Acadiana family memoirs; Spanish for Valley and Miami family papers; Vietnamese for household terms of address; the Native languages for clan and house words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the family under slavery and the 1965 Black-family debate are contested ground to state plainly; Native clan knowledge may stay within the nation. Family and inheritance law: Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their multigenerational households are not scouted here.
