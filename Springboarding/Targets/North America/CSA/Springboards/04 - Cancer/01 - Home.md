# CSA: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English as spoken across the South (white and Black usage), Cajun French, Tejano Spanish, Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Texas German, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowcountry Geechee households and island newcomers:** check what "home" covers where family yards meet resorts.
- **Owners in Black Belt towns and renters in Memphis or Birmingham:** compare how tenure changes talk about home and leaving.
- **Cajun and Creole families in raised country houses and in Lafayette subdivisions:** check what passed from the old plan to the new.
- **Hill Country Germans and Czechs:** compare the farm home and the town house, and who kept them.
- **Valley *colonia* families:** check how builders and their children speak of a self-built house.
- **Mobile-home owners of the Florida Panhandle and the Piney Woods:** check how "trailer" is heard and what owners call home.
- **Choctaw in Mississippi and Choctaw in Oklahoma:** compare which place each calls the homeland, as each government states it.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Nicaraguan Miami:** check the house left behind against the house built in exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for house forms, housing data and memoirs; mixed for housekeeping and home rituals; thin for Native home places beyond what each nation records.
- **Languages needed:** Cajun French for house-plan terms, Spanish for *colonia* and Miami exile writing, Vietnamese for garden and house talk, Texas German for Hill Country records, Muscogee for town affiliation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** plantation houses were built and run by enslaved people, so a house tour is contested memory; removal took Native homelands; storms and disaster aid fell unevenly by race. Housing and property law are Libra's and Capricorn's.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; held as a category only; nothing on their homes is scouted here.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull ACS B25003 and B25024 for twelve contrasting counties, the Valley and the Panhandle included.
2. Read Glassie's Southern house chapters, then search HABS for each named type.
3. Search Road Home studies for grant gaps by neighborhood.
4. Read Bailey and Morris side by side for how each writer names home.
