# CSA: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and Black English, Cajun French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Greek, Lebanese Arabic, Muscogee, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muscogee, Seminole, Choctaw and Chickasaw:** check how each nation now teaches clan membership and its duties, against the old ethnographies.
- **Delta and coastal Carolina Black kin groups, with their Northern-born branches:** compare whom reunions and funeral programs list as family.
- **White Southern families of planter and of yeoman descent:** check how lineage and family name are spoken of in each.
- **Cajun and Creole-of-color families of Acadiana:** compare godparent choice and cousin networks in the parish records.
- **Tejano families, Laredo to Corpus Christi:** check the reach of *compadrazgo* and the *consuegros* tie, by generation.
- **Miami's Cuban and Venezuelan households:** check the family split between here and *allá*.
- **Vietnamese households, Catholic and Buddhist:** compare kin and congregation as support.
- **Greek, Lebanese and Jewish Southern families:** check how ritual kin, clubs and marriage within the group shaped families.
- **Lumbee families of Pembroke and Prospect:** check how kin lines and settlement overlap.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for slavery-era and Black family history, sacramental registers and survey data; mixed for in-laws and step-families; thin for clan practice today, which the nations keep.
- **Languages needed:** French for parish registers, Spanish for Tejano kin studies and Cuban writing, Vietnamese and Greek for kin terms, Arabic for club records, Muscogee and Choctaw for clan words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved families were broken by sale, and the Moynihan debate still divides scholars; tribal enrollment and clan belonging are each nation's affair. Adoption, custody and inheritance rules are for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; any kin-network pointer for them is held back here.
