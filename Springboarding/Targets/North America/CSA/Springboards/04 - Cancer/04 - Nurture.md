# CSA: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, Black Southern usage, Cajun French, Tejano Spanish, Miami Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families in rural Black Belt counties and in Atlanta's suburbs:** compare who stands in when parents work far away.
- **White tenant-farm and mill families against town families:** check how childhood work and schooling weighed in each memory.
- **Tejano families from Kingsville to the Valley:** check what grandmothers, *madrinas* and *curanderas* each do for children.
- **Cajun families on the prairie and the bayous:** check how much of upbringing was carried in French, and what changed when it stopped.
- **Choctaw of Pearl River and Bogue Chitto, Poarch Creek and Florida Seminole:** read each nation's own guidance on raising children and the grandparents' part.
- **Vietnamese parents who came as refugees, and the children they raised here:** check where the two disagree about praise, food and freedom.
- **Cuban families across three generations in Miami:** compare the *abuela*'s part over time.
- **Baptist and Catholic households from Mobile to Lake Charles:** compare what each church teaches parents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black Southern childhood, infant feeding and parenting surveys; mixed for Tejano and Cajun upbringing in print; thin for Native child-rearing beyond the nations' own materials.
- **Languages needed:** Tejano and Cuban Spanish for grandmothers' memories, Vietnamese for parents' own accounts, French for older Acadiana parenting talk, Choctaw for the tribe's teaching terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved childhood and boarding schools must be stated plainly; physical punishment at home is contested and belongs to Capricorn's discipline; child welfare cases involve living children.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; child-rearing pointers for them are deferred.
