# CSA: Protection (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, Black Southern English, Gullah Geechee, New Orleans and Cajun French usage, Tejano Spanish, Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Hebrew and Yiddish (synagogue records). **Concept in one line:** keeping someone or something from harm: guarding, defending and shielding the vulnerable, one's own and oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families in the Delta and in Houston or Atlanta:** compare what "the talk" covers, and who gives it.
- **White rural households in the Piney Woods and the Hill Country:** check how guns, dogs and neighbors figure in guarding a home.
- **Gullah Geechee households:** check what haint blue and other house charms mean to those who still use them.
- **Tejano families of the brush country:** compare memories of the Rangers and of their own defense of ranch and kin.
- **Cuban Miami and Haitian Miami:** check whom each community says the sea crossings and the Coast Guard protected.
- **Muscogee Nation and Poarch Creek:** keep each government's statement on sacred-site protection separate.
- **Jewish congregations of Atlanta, Birmingham and Jackson:** check how security and civic voice were weighed after the bombings of the 1950s and 1960s.
- **Vietnamese shrimpers and their neighbors on the Gulf:** check who guarded boats and docks, and against whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for armed self-defense in the movement years, lynching history and gun surveys; mixed for house charms and amulets; thin for Native protective ways, which may be restricted.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley memory projects and Miami exile radio, Vietnamese for Chef Menteur organizing, French for Acadiana prayers of protection, Hebrew for synagogue records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching and the 1910s killings of Tejanos are history to set down without softening, naming whose memory is contested; Native protective medicine and burial sites may be closed to outsiders; gun policy belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; no protection pointer for them is given here.
