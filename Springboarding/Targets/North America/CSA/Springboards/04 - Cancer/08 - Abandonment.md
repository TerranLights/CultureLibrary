# CSA: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, Cajun French, colonial French (Ursuline records), Tejano Spanish, Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

English keywords: orphan, foundling, orphanage, orphan train, desertion, absent father, child support, children left with grandparents, elder abandonment, ghost town, rural depopulation, dog dumping.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Arkansas, Mississippi and Alabama families with kin in the North:** compare how being sent to grandparents is told by those who went and those who sent them.
- **White rural families of the Carolinas and the Wiregrass:** check how orphanhood and desertion are told in memoirs and court records.
- **Acadiana parishes that took orphan-train riders:** check how the riders and their adoptive families spoke of it later.
- **Cuban families split in the 1960s:** compare the views of parents who stayed and children who went.
- **Amerasian arrivals of the late 1980s in Vietnamese neighborhoods:** check how they were received.
- **Mississippi Choctaw:** check how the tribe tells the years after removal in its own histories.
- **Elders on the Gulf Coast and in South Florida:** check how storm evacuations of nursing homes are discussed by families and staff.
- **Tejano families with migrant fathers in the oil fields or the North:** check what *los que se quedan* means at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for orphanages, memoirs and federal data; mixed for desertion and elder abandonment; thin for Native accounts of broken federal promises outside the nations' own histories.
- **Languages needed:** colonial French for Ursuline registers, Spanish for exile memoirs and Valley family histories, Vietnamese for Amerasian arrivals' stories, Choctaw for the tribe's own history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** orphans, foster children and elders in court records are often living people; fathers' absence is a charged public topic, so read child-support data with its limits; *bụi đời* is a slur; safe-haven and child-support law: Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; nothing about family separation among them is scouted here.
