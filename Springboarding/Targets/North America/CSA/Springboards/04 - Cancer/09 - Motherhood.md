# CSA: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, Black church usage, Cajun French, Tejano Spanish, Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture (the mother of the nation, the Virgin Mother, Mother Earth).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Baptist and Pentecostal congregations:** compare how a woman becomes a church mother, and what the title lets her do.
- **White Protestant households of the Deep South:** check how memoirs and sermons speak of a mother's sacrifice, and who disagrees.
- **Choctaw, Chickasaw and Muscogee women:** read each nation's own accounts of clan mothers and women's standing.
- **Tejana mothers in the Valley and San Antonio:** check how the *abnegada* ideal is described by daughters of different generations.
- **Acadiana's Cajun and Creole households:** compare grandmothers' and mothers' parts as home births gave way to hospitals.
- **Cuban and Haitian mothers in Miami:** check how mothering at a distance (children left in Cuba or Haiti) is spoken of.
- **Vietnamese mothers in Houston and Biloxi:** compare postpartum customs kept and dropped over two generations.
- **Rural and urban mothers in Mississippi and Alabama:** check how distance to a maternity ward shapes birth.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the antebellum record of mothers enslaved and free, midwifery history and maternal-health data; mixed for Valley and Acadiana birth practice; thin for Native motherhood beyond the nations' own teaching and a few histories.
- **Languages needed:** Choctaw for kin and mother terms, Spanish for Valley *partera* studies, French for Acadiana birth stories, Vietnamese for postpartum care, Haitian Creole for Miami mothers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** enslaved women's childbearing, Black maternal deaths and coerced sterilization (including the case of Fannie Lou Hamer, Mississippi, 1961) must be stated as history with the women's own words first; mothers who lost children are living people; abortion belongs to Aries, and reproductive law to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; mothering among them is set aside for review.
