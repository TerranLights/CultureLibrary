# CSA: Domain and folkways, creativity and self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English (Tidewater to Texas), Black Southern speech, Gullah Geechee, Cajun French, Valley and Miami Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Muscogee, Mikasuki. **Concept in one line:** the domain of creativity and self-expression: how people shine, play and take pleasure (romance, children as joy, fame, celebration and display, gambling, and the sport ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Gulf Carnival cities and rural Acadiana's *courir*:** separate the city parade from the horseback begging run; check who may join each.
- **Baptist, Methodist and Pentecostal congregations, Black and white:** check where each draws lines between worship and performance, and on dancing and cards.
- **Texas regions (Panhandle and West Texas, Hill Country, the Valley, East Texas):** compare which sports, fairs and dances carry a town's name.
- **Choctaw, Poarch Creek, Seminole, Miccosukee and Alabama-Coushatta:** check which games and dances each nation opens to the public.
- **Gullah Geechee communities of Sapelo, St. Helena and McIntosh County:** trace how shout and basketry moved from home use to stage and market.
- **Cuban, Puerto Rican and Venezuelan Miami:** compare the *quince* and the dance club by origin and arrival wave.
- **Vietnamese Catholics and Buddhists of the Gulf:** compare festival calendars and who organizes them.
- **Lumbee, German and Czech Texans, Lebanese and Jewish Southerners:** find each group's own record (dance halls, club banquets) before folding it into a larger one.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Carnival, music, sport and gambling; mixed for courtship and children's play; thin for Native games beyond each nation's own publications, and for small groups (start with local papers and congregational archives).
- **Lands:** the CSA's lands are not yet fixed (pre-war lands carried: USA); every Leo sheet aims at the core Southern states from Virginia's Tidewater to Texas.
- **Languages needed:** French for *courir* songs; Spanish for Tejano and Miami festival media; Vietnamese for parish programs; Choctaw, Muscogee and Mikasuki for game and dance words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Green Corn ceremony and some dances are closed to outsiders; plantation festivities took place under slavery, and minstrelsy and blackface (Carnival's included) are to be stated plainly as history; Confederate-themed pageants are contested memory. Gambling law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their festivals are not scouted here.
