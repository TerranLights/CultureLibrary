# CSA: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Cajun French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw, Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Edgefield, Catawba and Piedmont potters, Black, white and Native:** check whose names the records kept on the wares and whose they dropped.
- **Black Belt and Piedmont quilters:** compare how each group describes improvising against following a set pattern.
- **New Orleans Black masking groups and second-line clubs:** check how a sewer's own design is weighed against the group's style.
- **Atlanta, Houston and Memphis hip-hop:** compare how each scene tells its own origin and polices copying.
- **Acadiana's Cajun and Creole musicians:** check where innovation is welcomed and where purists push back.
- **Tejano San Antonio and the Valley:** compare *rasquache* making-do with art-school training.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Venezuelan artists in Miami:** compare exile studios and the reach of the fair market.
- **Choctaw, Seminole, Miccosukee and Lumbee makers:** check what each nation says about who may use a design.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast:** look for creative work in the community's own press; sources are likely scarce.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folk art, music and literary craft; mixed for invention and everyday making-do; thin for Vietnamese and Native craft beyond each nation's and museum's own catalogs.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano art criticism; French for accordion builders' interviews; Vietnamese for community press; Choctaw and Muscogee dictionaries for whether "art" has its own word.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** work made under slavery, and the collectors' market for Black self-taught art (who profited), are to be stated plainly; some Native designs and regalia are restricted; the label "outsider art" is itself disputed. Who owns a work: Taurus.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their crafts are set aside here.
