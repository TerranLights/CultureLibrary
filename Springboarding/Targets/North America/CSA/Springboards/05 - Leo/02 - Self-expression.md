# CSA: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, Black Southern English, Cajun French, the Spanish of South Texas and of Miami, Vietnamese, Lumbee English, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pentecostal and Holiness churches against Episcopal and Presbyterian ones:** check how each sets the bounds of emotion in worship.
- **Shape-note singers of the Alabama and Georgia hills and Black gospel choirs:** compare how full-voiced singing is taught and judged.
- **LGBTQ Southerners in Atlanta, Houston and New Orleans, and in small towns:** check where the record shows open lives and where silence.
- **South Texas Tejanos, Valley towns and San Antonio barrios:** see how dance hall, *grito* and joking frame what may be said.
- **Cuban exile waves in Miami:** compare political display among the early exiles, the Mariel arrivals and recent arrivals.
- **Lumbee of Robeson County:** check how speech marks the speaker's people.
- **Mississippi Choctaw:** check where dress and dance are shown to visitors and where kept within the community.
- **Vietnamese Houston and New Orleans East:** compare parents' and children's own accounts of voicing feeling.
- **Acadiana's French speakers:** check how song carried grievance and joking once French was punished at school.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for worship, song and memoir; mixed for everyday display (yard signs, car flags), best found in local papers and photographs; thin for Native and Vietnamese accounts in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** French for *complaintes*; Spanish for Tejano joking and Miami exile press; Vietnamese for family accounts; Choctaw for the nation's own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spirituals were sung under slavery and must not be read only through the collectors' eyes; the Confederate flag as a personal display is contested memory to state plainly; queer lives in small towns involve living people. Rules on dress and speech as law: Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their forms of display are left out here.
