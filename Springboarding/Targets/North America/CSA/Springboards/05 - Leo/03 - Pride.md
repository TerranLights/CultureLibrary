# CSA: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, and the English the Seminole Tribe of Florida uses of itself. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **White Southerners of small towns and of the growing metros:** check how surveys separate regional pride from Confederate symbols, and where they merge.
- **Black Southerners of the Black Belt, Atlanta and Houston:** compare pride carried by HBCUs, churches and civil-rights memory.
- **Texans across Dallas, the Panhandle, the Valley and East Texas:** check whether Anglo, Tejano and Black Texans claim state pride alike.
- **Cajuns and Creoles of color:** compare how each revival named its pride since the 1960s.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Poarch Creek and Lumbee:** check how each nation speaks of survival, and of others using its name.
- **Cuban, Venezuelan and Nicaraguan Miami:** check how success stories are told and challenged between waves.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast families:** check how a child's achievement is announced; sources lie mostly in community press.
- **LGBTQ Southerners:** compare Pride in Atlanta, Houston and New Orleans with smaller cities.
- **Greek and Lebanese Texans, Jewish Southerners:** read heritage clubs' and congregations' anniversary books.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state and regional pride, music and movement histories; mixed for pride in work; thin for Native and Vietnamese voices in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Raza Unida papers and Miami exile press; French for the revival's festival programs; Vietnamese for award notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Confederate pride and its monuments are contested memory; state both the heritage claim and the record of slavery and segregation plainly, without taking either as the region's voice; mascots drawn from Native nations are disputed within and outside them; white-supremacist groups belong in the record as history, not as a voice of the region.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their heritage events are omitted here.
