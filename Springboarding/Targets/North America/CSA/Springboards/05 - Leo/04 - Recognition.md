# CSA: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, with Muscogee and Choctaw for the nations' own honors. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners in church, lodge and funeral home:** check how a person's worth was recorded where public honors were withheld.
- **Houma, MOWA Choctaw, Lumbee and other state-recognized peoples:** compare how each tells its pursuit of federal standing.
- **Federally recognized nations (Mississippi Choctaw, Poarch Creek, Seminole, Miccosukee, Chitimacha, Coushatta):** check how each honors elders and veterans in its own ceremonies.
- **Tejano veterans and their families:** compare how delayed medals and refused funerals are remembered across South Texas.
- **Older and younger Cajuns:** check how the 2003 acknowledgment was received, and whether it mattered.
- **Cuban professionals of the 1960s and later arrivals:** compare how credentials were recognized in each decade.
- **South Vietnamese veterans and their children:** check what honors each generation seeks, and from whom.
- **White mill and farm families of the Piedmont and Wiregrass:** check where credit for work lands in company histories and local papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for medals, memorials and state honors; mixed for everyday credit and thanks; thin for Native honors, which are partly restricted, and for Vietnamese veterans, where community press is the start.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese for veterans' groups; Spanish for Valley and Miami press; French for Acadiana's reaction to the 2003 proclamation.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching and massacre memorials touch living descendants on all sides and contested local memory; tribal recognition is disputed between peoples, so present each side's own account; name-taking may be closed knowledge. The legal criteria for recognition go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their professional credentialing is not scouted here.
