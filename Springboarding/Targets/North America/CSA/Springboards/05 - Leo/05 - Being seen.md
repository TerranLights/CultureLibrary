# CSA: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Gullah Geechee, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Greek (of the Texas and Florida parishes). **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners across Jim Crow and after:** check how looking and being looked at are remembered, in oral histories and court records.
- **Latino newcomers in poultry and carpet towns, and long-settled Tejanos:** compare who is counted, and who works unseen.
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Houma, Lumbee and Chitimacha:** check how each describes being misnamed or dropped from state histories.
- **Gullah Geechee:** compare on-screen portrayals with the community's own judgments of them.
- **Cuban and Haitian Miami:** check how much space each gets in Miami's papers and stations.
- **Vietnamese of New Orleans East:** check how a congressional seat changed coverage of the community.
- **Greek Orthodox families:** check how the *máti* belief is kept or dropped by generation.
- **LGBTQ New Orleanians and small-town residents:** compare who chooses visibility and who stays private.
- **Elders living alone in rural counties:** look for isolation in public-health and church visiting records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for representation, the census and the Black literary and photographic record; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting; thin for Native accounts of erasure in their own words, best found in tribal publications.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for South Texas and Miami press; Vietnamese for coverage of the 2008 race; Greek for parish records; French for Acadiana if the Houma record is pursued.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "reckless eyeballing" charges belong to a history of racial violence; pilgrimage pageants are contested memory; the identities of people in undercounted or unhoused groups need care. Surveillance falls under Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; how visible they are is not scouted here.
