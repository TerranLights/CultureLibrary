# CSA: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Cajun French, Tejano Spanish, Cuban Spanish, Haitian Creole (Miami), Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners, Delta and Piedmont, rural and urban:** check how courtesy titles, church dress and funerals answered daily indignity.
- **White sharecroppers and mill hands:** compare union papers and memoirs on being treated as "trash" and the answer to it.
- **Tejano families of the Coastal Bend and the Valley:** check how burial, address (*usted*, *don*) and work were sites of dignity.
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Muscogee, Seminole and Miccosukee:** check what each nation asks for its returned ancestors and how it speaks of it.
- **Cuban arrivals by wave, and Haitian Miami:** set the welcome each group met beside the ways each claimed respect.
- **Vietnamese former prisoners and their children:** check how standing lost in the camps was rebuilt in the family and parish.
- **Cajun and Creole Acadiana:** check memories of being shamed for French at school against later pride.
- **Migrant workers and border crossers in South Texas:** look for their own testimony, not only officials' accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the freedom struggle, Southern labor history and funeral customs; mixed for elders and care; thin for detention and migrant deaths beyond NGO reports and court files.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for border and Miami sources; Haitian Creole for Krome testimony; Vietnamese for reeducation-camp memoirs; French for Acadiana school memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** human remains and repatriation are each nation's own business to tell; migrant deaths and detention involve living families; Jim Crow address forms are quoted only as history. Assisted dying and detention law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their accounts of respect at work are not scouted here.
