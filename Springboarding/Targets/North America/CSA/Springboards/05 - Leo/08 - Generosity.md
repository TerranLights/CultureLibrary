# CSA: Generosity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, Black church usage, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Caddo and Muscogee for the older record. **Concept in one line:** giving freely, beyond what is owed: money, goods, time, food, attention or praise.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Baptists, Methodists and Pentecostals, Black and white:** check how tithing is taught and whether giving goes first to the church or to neighbors.
- **Black congregations, rural Mississippi and Alabama against Atlanta and Houston:** compare how givers are honored aloud in services.
- **Tejano families in the lower Valley and in Bexar County:** see how the cost of a celebration is split among sponsors and recorded.
- **Cuban Miami by arrival wave:** compare giving to the shrine, to exile causes and to family on the island.
- **Vietnamese Catholics and Buddhists:** compare how donations are announced (posted lists, read aloud, kept private).
- **Lebanese, Jewish and Greek Southerners:** check how small communities funded hospitals, schools and churches.
- **Poarch Creek, Lumbee, Seminole and Mississippi Choctaw:** check what each nation says about giving at gatherings.
- **Acadiana's farming parishes:** look for neighbors' work help in oral histories rather than in charity records.
- **Wealthy donors of Atlanta, Houston and Dallas:** compare named and anonymous giving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious giving and big philanthropy; mixed for disaster giving; thin for informal sharing, remittances and Native giveaways, which leave few records.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial journals and Acadiana oral histories; Spanish for Valley and Miami sources; Vietnamese for donor lists.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some giveaways and ceremonial gifts are the nations' own to describe; colonial accounts of Native hospitality need critical reading; donors who chose anonymity may wish to stay so. Tax rules on giving go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their temple and community giving is not scouted here.
