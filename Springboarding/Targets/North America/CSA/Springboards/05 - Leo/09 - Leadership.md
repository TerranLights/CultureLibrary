# CSA: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Muscogee, Choctaw, colonial French for the oldest record. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Baptist and Methodist congregations:** compare preacher-led and member-led traditions, and who is followed outside the pulpit.
- **Organizers of the Mississippi and Georgia movements:** check how local leaders, not famous ones, gathered followers.
- **Tejano South Texas, from ranch towns to San Antonio:** compare boss rule and later organizing (Raza Unida, COPS in San Antonio *check*).
- **Muscogee, Mississippi Choctaw, Seminole and Miccosukee:** check how each nation describes chiefs, councils and elected chairs.
- **White Protestant small towns of the Deep South:** check where deacons, coaches and sheriffs lead outside formal office.
- **Cuban Miami by generation:** compare loyalty to exile leaders among older and younger Cuban Americans.
- **Vietnamese Catholics and Buddhists along the Gulf:** see how priests, monks and elected councils divide standing among themselves.
- **Acadiana's prairie towns:** look at the courir *capitaine* beside local political leaders.
- **Gullah Geechee and Lumbee communities:** check who speaks for each people, and how that standing is earned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious and political leaders and for the civil-rights movement; mixed for women's informal leadership; thin for Native governance beyond each nation's own publications.
- **Languages needed:** colonial French for Natchez and Louisiana accounts; Spanish for South Texas machines and exile politics; Vietnamese for community elections; Muscogee and Choctaw for leadership titles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Confederate leaders as icons are contested memory; tribal leadership disputes belong to each nation to tell; living community leaders may be in open dispute. Formal authority and office go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their civic leaders are not scouted here.
