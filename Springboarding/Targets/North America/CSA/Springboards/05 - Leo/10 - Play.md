## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black children on St. Simons and Sapelo and in inland Georgia towns:** compare ring and hand games across generations.
- **Cajun, Creole and Anglo Louisianans:** check where card games, jokes and dances sit in a week's life.
- **German and Czech Texans of the Hill Country and the coastal prairie:** compare club games (Kegel, cards) and who keeps them.
- **Tejano families:** check how *lotería* and backyard parties shift between Valley towns and big cities.
- **Miami's Caribbean and South American households:** set games of chance and table games side by side by country of origin.
- **Vietnamese of the Gulf:** check how Tết games are kept by children born here.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee and Mississippi Choctaw:** check which games each nation opens to outsiders and which stay within families.
- **Baptist and Church of Christ families against Catholic Acadiana:** compare limits on cards, dice and dancing.
- **Florida's resort towns and rural North Florida:** compare play as an industry with play at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for children's folklore and theme parks; mixed for adult table games, best found in local papers and club records; thin for Native and Vietnamese play beyond festival coverage.
- **Languages needed:** French and Kouri-Vini for card-table talk; Spanish for *lotería* and *charada*; Vietnamese for Tết coverage; German for Hill Country club records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** children's play under slavery belongs to the narratives' tellers and is to be read with that history stated; some Native games are tied to ceremony; ethnic joke cycles need the teller's own community's view. Gambling's legal side goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their games and play are not covered.
