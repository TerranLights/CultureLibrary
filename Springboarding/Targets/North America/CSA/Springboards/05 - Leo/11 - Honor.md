# CSA: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **White Southerners of the Carolina low and up country, the Delta and Texas:** compare how honor is described across planter, yeoman and working-class families.
- **Black Southerners:** check how honor, respect and the good name were claimed under slavery, under Jim Crow and in the street, in their own sources.
- **Tejanos of the ranch country and the cities:** compare *charro* codes, family *honra* and the memory of the Rangers.
- **Cajuns and Creoles:** check how insults were answered in prairie dance halls and on bayou docks.
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Seminole, Miccosukee and Poarch Creek:** check how each honors veterans, elders and the dead, as each nation shares it.
- **Cuban exiles by generation:** compare oaths, lodges and the weight of a given word.
- **Vietnamese families of the Gulf:** check how family honor and *danh dự* are spoken of by parents and children.
- **Military families near Fort Bragg, Fort Hood and Pensacola:** compare military honor with civilian codes in the same towns (bases renamed 2023 and later *check*).
- **Women across groups:** check how much a woman's honor is tied to her conduct in each source, without assuming it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** very rich for white Southern honor and dueling; mixed for Black, Tejano and Cajun honor; thin for Native and Vietnamese codes in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for *charro* and exile sources; French for Acadiana oral histories; Vietnamese for community press; Choctaw for honor words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor was used to justify slavery, lynching and violence against women; state this as history in the victims' and survivors' words first; the honor-culture thesis is contested and must be read with its critics; eagle feathers and honor songs belong to the nations. Feud and revenge go to Scorpio; dueling and honor-code law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; family honor among them is not scouted here.
