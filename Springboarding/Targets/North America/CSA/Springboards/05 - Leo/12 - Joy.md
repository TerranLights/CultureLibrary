# CSA: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English, African American English, Louisiana French, Tejano and Cuban Spanish, Vietnamese, Texas German, Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Pentecostal, Baptist and Methodist congregations:** compare where and how joy may be shown in worship.
- **New Orleans second-line clubs and rural Black funerals elsewhere:** check whether joy at a funeral is shared or a New Orleans way.
- **Cajun prairie and Creole zydeco communities:** compare dance-hall celebration and who goes.
- **Tejano South Texas and Hill Country German Texans:** compare festival joy, drink and family presence.
- **Cuban, Colombian and Puerto Rican Miami:** check how music and dance carry gladness for each.
- **Vietnamese of the Gulf:** check how festivals for children are planned and how elders take part.
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Seminole and Poarch Creek:** check which celebrations are open to guests.
- **Primitive Baptists and Churches of Christ beside Pentecostals:** compare restraint and exuberance in worship.
- **College football towns of the Deep South:** check how game-day joy is shared across race and class.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black sacred music, festivals and survey happiness items; mixed for everyday delight; thin for Native and Vietnamese accounts of joy in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** French for dance-hall talk; Spanish for Tejano and Miami music writing; Vietnamese for Trung Thu notices; German for Wurstfest and New Braunfels sources; Choctaw for dance names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Jonkonnu and holiday license under slavery must be stated as what they were, not as contentment; some ceremonies (the Green Corn) are not public; game-day traditions carry contested symbols. Faith as such goes to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their festivals of joy are not scouted here.
