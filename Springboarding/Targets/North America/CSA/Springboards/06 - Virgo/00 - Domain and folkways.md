# CSA: Domain and folkways, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Southern, Black Southern, Gullah Geechee), French (Cajun) and Kouri-Vini, Spanish (Texas, Miami), Haitian Creole, Vietnamese; Choctaw and Muscogee where the nations publish terms. **Concept in one line:** work and service as a domain: what good work and a well-kept life are, across the daily round, the kept house, craft, rest and health.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sea Island Gullah Geechee communities against Delta and Black Belt cotton counties:** check whether task and gang labor left different notions of a day's work.
- **Tidewater planter, Piedmont yeoman and mill-village families:** compare which jobs each counted as respectable, and the shift after 1865.
- **Cajun, Creole and Isleño households:** check the round of trapping, fishing, cane and rice, and who in a household took which part.
- **Tejano ranch families of South Texas against German and Czech Hill Country farmers:** separate their work calendars and their words for a good hand.
- **The Seminole, Miccosukee, Poarch Creek, Coushatta and Mississippi Choctaw nations:** ask each what of its seasonal tasks and healing it shares publicly before using outsiders' accounts.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Bahamian South Florida:** compare the day and week each group brought, and what shift work changed.
- **Vietnamese shrimping households:** check how the season's openings order family time and school.
- **Greek, Lebanese and Jewish storekeeping families:** check how shop hours set the household's clock.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for plantation-era labor and twentieth-century work and health statistics; mixed for the daily round and housekeeping, held in diaries, cookbooks and oral histories; thin for Native seasonal rounds beyond each nation's own teaching, and for Haitian and Vietnamese home routine (begin with community press).
- **Lands:** the author has not yet drawn the CSA's borders (pre-war lands carried: USA); these Virgo sheets aim at the core South, Virginia's Tidewater to Texas and Florida.
- **Languages needed:** Acadiana's French and Kouri-Vini for words for a job and a cure; Spanish for Texas and Miami; Haitian Creole for South Florida's field and hotel hands; Vietnamese for shrimping families; Choctaw and Muscogee for each nation's round of tasks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** much of the early record of work is slavery's bookkeeping, to be named as such; verdicts on who was "industrious" are claims, not data; the Green Corn and other rites of the seasonal round are closed. Labor codes belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their family businesses and routines are not scouted here.
