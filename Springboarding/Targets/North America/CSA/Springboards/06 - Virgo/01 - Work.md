# CSA: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English and Black Southern speech, Cajun French, Texas and Cuban Spanish, Greek of the Tarpon Springs sponge families, Vietnamese, Haitian Creole, Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white cotton-belt tenants:** compare, in their own memoirs, what each called a fair day's work and a fair settlement at year's end.
- **Piedmont mill-village families against kin who stayed on farms:** check whether the move changed which work counted as a man's or a woman's.
- **Valley Tejano families who followed crops north:** check how the yearly trek set schooling and marriage.
- **Ybor City's Cuban, Spanish and Italian rollers:** separate the three groups' strike memories and craft pride.
- **Tarpon Springs Greek families:** check how a dangerous trade was passed from father to son, and when sons left it.
- **Marshallese and Latino poultry workers of the Ozarks:** compare two recent arrivals on the same line; Marshallese sources are scarce (start with Springdale community groups and local press).
- **Oil-patch families of East Texas and South Louisiana:** check how boom-and-bust cycles shaped talk of steady work.
- **Seminole ranchers and Mississippi Choctaw wage workers:** ask each nation how wage and tribal-enterprise work are spoken of.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history and work statistics; mixed for everyday talk about effort and idleness; thin for Marshallese workers and for Native wage labor outside tribal newspapers.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and Italian for Ybor's union and mutual-aid papers; Greek for the sponge trade's family records; Vietnamese for fleet associations; Marshallese for Springdale; Haitian Creole for Belle Glade's field crews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slavery, convict labor and peonage are forced work and must be named so; "lazy" was hurled at Black, Mexican and poor white workers alike, so treat every such verdict as a claim; Baptist's figures are disputed; immigration status shadows the poultry and field data. Rules on wages and unions belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their trades and family firms are not scouted here.
