# CSA: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English as spoken by Southern households, churches and kitchens, Black and white; Cajun French; the Spanish of Texas parishes and Miami shops; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black domestic workers and the families who hired them:** read both sides' testimony on the same kitchens, and note who got into print.
- **Black and white Catholic orders and sodalities of the Gulf:** check how race set which service each could give, and to whom.
- **Baptist and Pentecostal churches of both races:** compare ushers, deaconesses and relief crews as forms of serving.
- **Military-college towns (Lexington, Virginia; Charleston; College Station):** check how service is taught and remembered in each.
- **Tejano parish women and Cuban exile volunteers:** compare where each puts unpaid service: parish, club, or family.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami in tourism jobs:** separate two arrivals working the same hotels.
- **Vietnamese salon owners and workers:** check how service to customers and duty to kin are balanced in a family shop.
- **The Pamunkey, Mattaponi and Apalachee descendants:** ask how each tells the history of service forced or owed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for domestic service and church relief; mixed for restaurant and hotel work as workers see it; thin for Vietnamese salon life and for Apalachee descendants (scattered after 1704; look to the Talimali Band's own materials *check*).
- **Languages needed:** French for Holy Family and parish records; Spanish for Guadalupana minutes and Miami press; Haitian Creole for hotel workers; Vietnamese for salon owners' associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "service" in the big house was enslavement, and later domestic work ran on segregation, both to be said in plain words; Vodou service is religious practice, not folklore; the tribute ceremony rests on a treaty, whose terms belong to Libra. Pay and tip rules go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their roadside hospitality businesses are not scouted here.
