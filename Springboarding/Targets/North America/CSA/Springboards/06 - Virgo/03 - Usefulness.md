# CSA: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** rural and church English of the South, Cajun and Creole French, Spanish of the Valley and of Miami, Vietnamese, and the Creek-derived plant words of Florida's Seminole. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or a piece of knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white farm families, tenant and landowning:** check at what age each counted a child as a hand, and who decided.
- **Lumbee and other eastern North Carolina tobacco families:** compare which barn tasks went to children, women and elders.
- **Acadiana storytellers and their audiences:** check whether the fool tales are told as warnings, as comedy, or both, by setting.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee families:** ask which plant knowledge may be shared at all; much is not for outsiders.
- **Vietnamese refugee parents and their grown children:** compare how each generation describes being a burden or a help.
- **Cuban exile elders and their grandchildren in Miami:** check what roles elders keep in the household as it changes.
- **Rio Grande Valley Tejano families:** check how the praise *acomedido* is taught to boys and girls.
- **Disabled Southerners and their families in the Delta and Black Belt:** set local accounts of worth beside the federal counts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for child labor and farm-extension work; mixed for elders' roles; thin for Native plant knowledge (partly restricted) and for craft skills like net knitting, best found through folklife programs and in person.
- **Languages needed:** French for Jean Sot tales; Spanish for Valley family life; Vietnamese for resettlement memories; the Seminole nation's own terms, by its leave.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hariot and the ethnobotanies are outsiders' inventories; the folktale title carries a period word, kept only as the published title; disability and "idle youth" labels can wound, so report them as labels; eugenic sterilization in Southern states belongs here only as history told by those it struck. Benefit rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their elders' household roles are not scouted here.
