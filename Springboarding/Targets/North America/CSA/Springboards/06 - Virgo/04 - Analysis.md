## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Planters' ledgers and the testimony of the enslaved:** compare what each side said about the scales and the weights; the second survives mainly in narratives and interviews.
- **Black college scholars (Atlanta, Fisk, Wiley) and white regional sociologists (Chapel Hill):** check which questions each school asked about the same counties.
- **Huntsville engineers and nearby farm and mill families:** compare what each calls an expert, and whom each trusts on a technical matter.
- **English-, Spanish- and Haitian Creole-speaking households of South Florida:** compare how each reads and acts on a forecast.
- **The Miccosukee and Seminole nations and state agencies:** check whose measurements carry weight in disputes over water.
- **Tejano scholars and Anglo folklorists of the border:** compare their methods on the same towns.
- **Vietnamese and Cuban small-business families:** check who keeps the books, by generation and gender.
- **French-descended landholders along the bayous:** trace how arpent boundaries are still argued from old surveys.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, plantation records and science institutions; mixed for everyday reasoning styles, which surface in oral history and family business; thin for Native and Vietnamese pointers outside their own reports.
- **Languages needed:** colonial French and Spanish for survey plats; Spanish for border scholarship; Haitian Creole and Spanish for forecast studies; Vietnamese for small-business services; Chickasaw and Choctaw for counting terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** plantation accounting measured human beings as property, to be stated so; *De Bow's Review* argued for slavery; Huntsville's rocket team includes men tied to forced labor in Germany, contested in local memory; disputes over land and water rights go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their roles in Southern engineering and medicine are not scouted here.
