# CSA: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of Southern homes, churches, drill fields and workshops (Black and white), Holiness church vocabulary, Acadiana French, the Spanish of San Antonio and Miami, and Gulf Coast Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Holiness, Pentecostal and Baptist believers:** compare how each speaks of perfection as a gift, a goal or a danger to the soul.
- **Texas drill-team girls and their mothers, across Anglo, Tejana and Black schools:** check who sets the standard and who pays for it.
- **Piedmont potters and Pamunkey and Catawba potters:** compare what each tradition calls a flaw, by the makers' own words.
- **Black quilters of the Black Belt and white guild quilters:** check how each judges a finished quilt and why.
- **Golf-club and garden-club towns (Augusta, Athens) and mill or farm neighborhoods nearby:** compare who keeps a "perfect" yard and who is judged by it.
- **Cuban and Venezuelan Miami families in dance and music schools:** check how strictness is described by teachers and by students.
- **Vietnamese students and parents of Alief and Versailles:** check how grades are spoken of at home; treat pressure as a question, never a premise.
- **Ozark and Hill Country craftsmen (smiths, saddlers, bootmakers):** compare how a master's mark is earned.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church doctrine, drill and pageant culture, and craft; mixed for private perfectionism, which shows in memoirs and clinical studies; thin for Native making standards beyond what each nation shares.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for dance-school and bootmaking records in Miami and San Antonio; Vietnamese for family talk of grades; French for Acadiana craft words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Mississippian objects belong to the descendant nations' heritage, and their meaning is not for outsiders to settle; Augusta National's long exclusion of Black and women members is to be stated plainly; body standards for cheerleaders and pageant girls touch eating disorders, to be handled with care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their spelling-bee and academic contest records are not scouted here.
