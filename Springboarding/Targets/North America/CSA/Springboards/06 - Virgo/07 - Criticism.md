# CSA: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** newspaper, pulpit and porch English, Black vernacular speech, Cajun French, the Spanish of San Antonio's stages and of Cuban Miami, Vietnamese of the Houston press, Lumbee English. **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, and the forms this takes: feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small-town Mississippi and Alabama editors and their readers:** check who could print criticism of local power, and what it cost each side.
- **Black churches, barbershops and the Black press:** compare where criticism of white and of Black leaders could be voiced, and in what form.
- **Agrarian and New Critic circles (Nashville, Baton Rouge) and Black writers answering Mencken:** check which critics judged Southern letters, and whom they left out.
- **Tejano audiences of carpa and radio comedy:** compare satire aimed at Anglo officials with satire aimed at their own.
- **Cuban exile media and younger Cuban Americans:** check who may mock whom after each arrival wave.
- **Vietnamese community leaders and younger critics:** trace how the anticommunist line has framed dissent.
- **Lumbee readers of their own paper and the county power structure:** check how tribal criticism moved into print.
- **Cajun audiences and outside entertainers:** compare insider teasing (the Boudreaux tales) with mockery from outside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plentiful for newspapers, literary criticism and outsiders' critiques; mixed for criticism inside families and churches; thin for Vietnamese and Lumbee press archives, which may be partly undigitized (start with the papers' own offices and university special collections).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for carpa scripts and Miami radio; Vietnamese for Houston weeklies; Acadiana French for Cajun reactions to stage dialect.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Southern critics were threatened, boycotted and run out of town, while outside critiques often carried contempt for the region; Helper's book attacks slavery from a racist position; *chụp mũ* has been tied to violence, so name no individual as a suspect; Mencken's essay remains contested memory. Press and speech law: Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; no pointer for their newspapers or radio appears here.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Mencken's essay, then search the 1920s *Virginia Quarterly Review* and Southern papers for replies.
