# CSA: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** clinic and kitchen English of the South, Black Southern health talk, Ozark speech, the French and Creole of Louisiana's healers, South Texas and Cuban Spanish, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, and Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black patients in Delta, Black Belt and Lowcountry clinics:** check how folk categories of illness sit beside clinic diagnoses.
- **Ozark and Wiregrass white families:** compare home cures and distrust of doctors, by generation.
- **Cajun and Black Creole households:** separate *traiteur* practice from Creole healers' work, and who visits each.
- **Tejano families of South Texas:** check when a family turns to a *curandera* and when to the clinic.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Puerto Rican Miami:** compare *botánica* customers and what each group seeks there.
- **Vietnamese households:** check how elders' home cures and children's biomedicine meet in one family.
- **Each Native nation of the region (Choctaw of Mississippi, Poarch Creek, Seminole, Miccosukee):** ask which healing knowledge it keeps closed.
- **Residents of the Mississippi River chemical corridor:** compare how Black and white neighbors speak of illness and the plants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemics, public health and statistics; mixed for folk healing, which is well collected but often by outsiders; thin for Haitian and Vietnamese home medicine and for Native healing, much of it restricted.
- **Languages needed:** Louisiana's French and Creole for remedy names; Spanish for *curanderismo* and *botánicas*; Haitian Creole for South Florida herbalists; Vietnamese for home cures; Choctaw and Muscogee for words for sickness and healing, by each nation's leave.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native medicine and Afro-Caribbean religious healing are closed or partly closed practice; medicine under slavery treated people as property; outsiders have misread coining as abuse; environmental-health claims are disputed in court. Insurance and health-care law are for Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their physicians in rural Southern hospitals are not scouted here.
