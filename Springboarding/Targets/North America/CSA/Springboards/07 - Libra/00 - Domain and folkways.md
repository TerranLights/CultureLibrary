## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Louisiana's civil-law parishes against the common-law states around them:** check how marital property, contract and inheritance differ on paper.
- **Spanish-era Texas and East Florida against Anglo settlement:** compare who brought quarrels to the alcalde and, later, to the county court.
- **Black families across emancipation:** check how unions without legal standing were recorded once the law recognized them.
- **Planter families against Piedmont and Wiregrass yeomen:** separate the duel, the lawsuit and the church trial as routes for righting a wrong.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Poarch Creek, Mississippi Choctaw, Coushatta, Chitimacha and Lumbee:** read each nation's own code and court before outsiders' accounts.
- **Gullah Geechee praise-house communities:** check what they settled before any courthouse did.
- **Tejano, Cuban, Haitian and Vietnamese families:** compare godparenthood, betrothal steps and who speaks for the family.
- **Jewish, Greek and Lebanese Southerners:** check each congregation's marriage contracts and religious arbitration.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for codes, appellate cases and marriage statistics; mixed for customary dispute settling (church minutes, family papers); thin for Native internal practice beyond what the nations publish, and for Haitian and Vietnamese betrothal (start with parish media).
- **Lands:** the author has still to draw the CSA (pre-war lands carried: USA), so the Libra sheets aim at the core South, Tidewater to Texas and Florida.
- **Languages needed:** French and Spanish for the early codes and alcalde papers; Choctaw and Muscogee for the nations' words for law; Vietnamese for wedding media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** early law here regulated slavery and barred marriage across color lines, to be stated plainly; Confederate and Reconstruction law are contested memory; Native law and peacemaking only as the nations share them.
- **Held for review:** this sheet leaves out the betrothal customs of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
