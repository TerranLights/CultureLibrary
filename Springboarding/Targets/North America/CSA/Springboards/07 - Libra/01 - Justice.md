# CSA: Justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of trial transcripts and prison writing; Black Southern sermon and song; Louisiana French; Valley and border Spanish; Cuban exile Spanish; Vietnamese of the Gulf shrimp ports; Choctaw for the Lighthorse era. **Concept in one line:** giving each person and group their due: how wrongs are judged and answered, and how rights, goods and burdens are rightly ordered in a society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plantation counties of the Delta and the Black Belt, set beside Atlanta, Houston and Charlotte:** check how convict leasing, county farms and death sentences fell by place and race.
- **Black Southerners pressing for redress (Rosewood, Greensboro, reopened cold cases):** compare what each campaign asked for: trials, payment, apology or truth.
- **Tejano families of the border counties:** look at how the Ranger killings of 1915-1919 enter court records, family memory and new markers.
- **The Poarch Creek, Mississippi Choctaw, Miccosukee and Seminole nations:** see which wrongs each nation's own court hears and which pass to federal or state courts.
- **Black, Cuban and Haitian Miami:** compare readings of the 1980 McDuffie acquittal and uprising, of detention, and of exile courts of memory.
- **Italian, Jewish and Vietnamese Southerners:** check how each group's cases were tried and later revisited.
- **Prisoners and their families at Angola, Parchman and Huntsville:** read what they write about sentence length, clemency and release.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court rulings, prison records, lynching data and historical justice; mixed for justice inside families and churches; thin for Native justice before 1830 beyond printed codes, and for Vietnamese and Haitian views (start with community papers and court files).
- **Languages needed:** Choctaw for the nation's law terms; Spanish for border ballads and family testimony; Vietnamese for the Galveston Bay case's community side; French for Louisiana's justice-of-the-peace records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching photographs and the names of victims' families; convict leasing as forced labor; the death penalty and named prisoners; Confederate and civil-rights memory are contested, and no one group's account stands for the region.
- **Held for review:** not scouted here: the dealings of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands with Southern courts.

## 5. First queries
1. Read *Powell*, *Norris* and *Brown v. Mississippi* together; list the rights each set out.
2. Search the TDCJ site for "last statement" and sample entries by decade.
3. Search Florida's statutes and legislative journals for the 1994 Rosewood bill and its hearings.
4. Search printed Choctaw law compilations for "Lighthorse" and the punishments listed.
