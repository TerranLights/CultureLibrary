# CSA: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** cotton-market and farm English; Black Southern tale-telling; Cajun French of the *boucherie*; San Antonio and Valley Spanish; Cuban and Haitian Miami; Vietnamese of Houston's credit circles. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Belt tenants and landlords in Raper's counties, and the Delta's larger plantations:** check who recorded the year's settlement and how disputes over it ended.
- **Tejano districts of San Antonio and the Valley against suburban Anglo districts:** compare what each argued fairness in school money meant.
- **Rural and suburban Texans after the Top Ten Percent law:** check whose students gained and lost places and how each side named the rule.
- **Louisiana heirs against heirs in neighboring common-law states:** compare inheritance quarrels with and without fixed shares.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami:** compare how arrivals from each island were processed and what each community called unfair.
- **Cajun families who keep the *boucherie*, and Vietnamese families in *hụi*:** check how each rotation decides turns and what happens when someone breaks it.
- **Enrolled Seminole members, descendants and Black Seminole descendants:** check where membership rules decide a share.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court rulings, admissions data and school finance; mixed for sharecropping settlements (account books survive unevenly); thin for credit circles and family rotations, which live in memory and must be gathered by interview.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for mission water records and Valley school fights; French for *boucherie* talk; Haitian Creole for Miami's reception of HRIFA; Vietnamese for *hụi*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sharecropping debts and race; affirmative action is a live quarrel; Seminole enrollment and payments are the tribe's own affair, so use only what it publishes.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands: their business partnerships and credit circles fall outside this sheet.
