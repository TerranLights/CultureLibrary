*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Low Country and Piedmont yeoman households against planter households:** check how headship differed when the farm had few or many hands.
- **Croppers, share tenants and landlords in the Delta and the Black Belt:** compare how each side named the bond and what courts held it to be.
- **Free people of color and Creole families in New Orleans:** separate legal marriages, informal unions and the later legend.
- **Southern Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal and Black church couples:** check how each body's own texts describe a husband's and wife's parts.
- **Tejano families:** compare *compadrazgo* chosen at baptism, *quinceañera* and wedding, and how it ties two households.
- **Caddo, Muscogee and Choctaw peoples:** read alliance and kinship as each nation publishes it, not as missionaries framed it.
- **Cuban and Vietnamese family firms:** check who counts as a partner (spouse, sibling, cousin, fellow parishioner).
- **Same-sex couples in Alabama, Texas and Mississippi:** compare what each state's texts did between 2004 and 2019.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage law, denominational statements and survey data; mixed for sharecrop and franchise contracts (court cases survive better than the agreements); thin for Caddo alliance practice outside Spanish sources, and for Vietnamese firms (start with the community press).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for the Hasinai reports and Tejano interviews; French for *accotés* and Creole family papers; Caddo and Muscogee for alliance words; Vietnamese for business directories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *plaçage* is surrounded by legend and racial fantasy; the marriage bans across color lines and against same-sex couples are recent law and live hurt; Native kinship rules only as the nations share them.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, including their family firms, are set aside under the standing rule.
