# CSA: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** constitutional, tort and folk-healing English; Valley Spanish; coastal Louisiana French; Vietnamese of the herb shops; Mikasuki where the Miccosukee publish on water. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural and urban counties in Georgia, Alabama and Tennessee before and after 1964:** check who lost and gained seats, and how each side described the old weighting.
- **Contributory-negligence states against comparative-fault states:** compare jury results and the arguments of farm, business and plaintiffs' lobbies.
- **Black Southern families who use folk remedies:** check how "high blood" and "low blood" sit beside clinic diagnoses in family accounts.
- **Tejano curanderas' patients in the Valley and San Antonio:** separate the hot-cold scheme from clinic care, as each family practices them.
- **The Seminole Tribe, the Miccosukee Tribe and South Florida growers:** compare each party's stated aims in the water compacts and restoration plans.
- **Coastal Cajun, Houma and Isleño fishing families against parish and state planners:** check what each says is lost and saved by a diversion.
- **Wet and dry counties in Arkansas and Texas:** compare who campaigns for each side and which churches are involved.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for constitutional, tort and apportionment law and for coastal and Everglades planning; mixed for county liquor law; thin for folk ideas of bodily balance beyond published studies, and for Vietnamese herbal practice (start with shop owners).
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley healing and Hinojosa's Spanish volumes; Vietnamese for remedies; French for coastal families on land loss; Mikasuki for the tribe's water statements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Confederate Constitution is contested memory and its slavery clauses are stated plainly; Native ceremonies of renewal are closed, so use only the tribes' published statements; folk healing is not to be presented as quackery or as a trait of a people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their healing traditions are not scouted on this sheet.
