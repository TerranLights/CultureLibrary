# CSA: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of jury rolls, ethics codes and county politics; South Texas Spanish of the old political bosses; Cuban Spanish of Miami; Louisiana French; Vietnamese; the English of tribal codes. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners called for juries, and struck from them, in Mississippi, Alabama and Louisiana:** compare strike rates by county and decade.
- **Tejano jurors and voters in Jackson, Jim Wells and Duval counties:** check how exclusion and boss rule show up in court and election records.
- **Small-county residents against big-city ones:** see whether recusal motions and changes of venue track how well judges know the parties.
- **Black, Cuban, Haitian and Anglo Miami:** compare how each community read the judges' bribery cases and the 2000 recount.
- **Louisiana parishes:** check nepotism complaints to the ethics board by parish and office.
- **The four Native nations above:** compare how each separates its judges from its elected council.
- **Spanish, Vietnamese and Haitian Creole speakers in Houston, New Orleans and Miami courts:** check interpreter access and complaints.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for jury and judicial-election cases, ethics statutes and court stings; mixed for county patronage (much in newspapers, little in surveys); thin for tribal courts' practice beyond published codes.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Duval and Jim Wells County ballots and Tejano jurors' testimony; Vietnamese and Haitian Creole for interpreter complaints; French for older parish records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** jury exclusion by race is recent and named prosecutors are involved, so cite rulings, not rumor; Reconstruction officeholding and its overthrow are contested memory; named officials in corruption cases only as convicted or as courts found.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their dealings with Southern courts and offices are outside this sheet.
