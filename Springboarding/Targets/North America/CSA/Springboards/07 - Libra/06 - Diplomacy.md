# CSA: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** treaty English and the French and Spanish of colonial envoys; Choctaw and Muscogee titles of office; Black Southern speech of the mask; Louisiana French of the Francophonie ties; Miami Spanish of the migration talks; Vietnamese of Houston's community politics; Gullah Geechee. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Creek, Choctaw and Seminole treaty councils:** compare who spoke, who interpreted, and who the nations later said had no right to sign.
- **Mississippi Choctaw and Seminole leaders today:** check how compacts and talks with the states are described in each nation's own press.
- **Black civil-rights negotiators in Atlanta, Birmingham and Albany:** compare what talks with white business and city leaders won or lost, by city.
- **Texas under the Republic, the Confederacy and the Union:** check what each government's envoys promised Native nations and Mexico.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Vietnamese communities:** compare how each meets the consulates or governments of the countries they left.
- **Cajun and Creole Louisiana:** check how ties with France, Quebec and Acadia are pursued and who speaks for them.
- **Gullah Geechee:** compare local, federal and international routes for the people's claims.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for treaties, compacts and court records; mixed for everyday tact (manners books, oral sources); thin for Native diplomatic practice beyond treaty minutes written by U.S. agents, so read the nations' own histories first.
- **Languages needed:** French for Iberville and the Francophonie files; Spanish for Texas and Cuba records; Choctaw and Muscogee for titles and speeches; Vietnamese for consulate coverage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** removal treaties were signed under pressure and some by men the nations disowned, so present the nations' accounts first; Confederate diplomacy is contested memory; protests at the Vietnamese consulate involve living people.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; their consulates and community bodies are not scouted here.
