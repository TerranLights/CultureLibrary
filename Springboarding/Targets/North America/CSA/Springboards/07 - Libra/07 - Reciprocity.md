# CSA: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** country-neighbor English; Gullah Geechee of the Sea Island societies; Louisiana French and New Orleans English; Haitian Creole of Little Haiti; Tejano Spanish; the Spanish of Tampa's mutual-aid centers; Vietnamese of the temples. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Plain white and Black rural neighbors in the Piedmont and the Wiregrass:** check how workings and loans of tools and teams were repaid, and whether across color lines.
- **Sea Island Gullah Geechee and Lowcountry Black societies:** compare dues, benefits and the duties of members at a death.
- **Cuban, Spanish and Italian Tampa:** check how each mutual-aid club wrote the exchange of dues for care into its rules.
- **Haitian, Tejano and Vietnamese savings circles:** see how each sets turns, trusts members and handles a defaulter.
- **Vietnamese Buddhist and Catholic families:** see how the debt to parents is marked at temple and at parish.
- **The Chickasaw, Choctaw and Seminole:** read gift and hospitality obligations as each nation states them, before the colonial accounts.
- **Louisiana and the common-law states:** compare when the courts enforce a promise made without anything given back.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mutual-aid societies' records and interstate agreements; mixed for neighbor exchange in rural memory; thin for savings circles (unwritten; gather by interview) and for Native obligations beyond published accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Haitian Creole for *sòl*; Spanish for Ybor City club minutes; Vietnamese for temple programs; Louisiana French for neighbor help.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** McWhiney's thesis is disputed and must not be read as fact about a people; savings circles can involve undocumented members, so protect identities; Native hospitality rites only as the nations publish them.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, whose gift customs and savings groups are left aside under the standing rule.
