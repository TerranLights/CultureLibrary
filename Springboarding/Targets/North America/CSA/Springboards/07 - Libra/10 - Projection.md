# CSA: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of trials, propaganda, legislative committees and folk belief; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French and Kouri-Vini; border and Cuban Spanish; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowcountry planters and Black Charlestonians in 1822:** read what each record says about who feared whom.
- **Cherokee, Creek and Choctaw nations against removal-era Georgia and Mississippi:** compare the nations' memorials with legislators' speeches.
- **White and Black Delta towns:** check whose fears and accusations reach court and newspaper.
- **Mexican and Tejano families of South Texas in 1954:** compare U.S. citizens caught up in the sweeps with migrant workers, in family memory.
- **Cuban, Haitian and Anglo Miami around 1980:** check which group was named as the problem by whom.
- **Haitian Floridians in the 1980s:** see how churches, radio and doctors in the community answered the "risk group" label.
- **Vietnamese families of Alief, Bellaire and Versailles in 2020-21:** check reports of anti-Asian harassment and how community groups recorded them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trials, propaganda, committees and scholarship; mixed for folk ideas of harm sent by others (much in collections made by outsiders); thin for Vietnamese and Haitian experiences of blame before recent years (community press and oral history).
- **Languages needed:** Haitian Creole for community answers to the AIDS label; Spanish for 1954 and 1980 accounts; Vietnamese for pandemic-era reports; Gullah Geechee and French for conjure and *gris-gris* terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** racist images and slurs are history to cite, not repeat; the Vesey record is disputed; the Johns Committee files name private people; conjure is a living practice and is not to be used to mock; the West Memphis case involves living people cleared of murder.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; any blame directed at them is not scouted on this sheet.
