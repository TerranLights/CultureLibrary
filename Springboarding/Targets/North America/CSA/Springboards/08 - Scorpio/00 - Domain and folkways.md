# CSA: Domain and folkways, death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of Southern obituaries and pulpits; African American English; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Choctaw and Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** how death, the dead, sex, inheritance, held-in-common goods and what is kept hidden are handled, and how people are thought to change.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white congregations in one county:** check where wake, burial and decoration customs part, and what segregated funeral homes did to them.
- **Gullah Geechee Sea Islands against the inland Black Belt:** test which grave practices belong to the coast.
- **Catholic south Louisiana (Cajun, Creole, New Orleans):** set All Saints' customs and family tombs against Protestant neighbors.
- **Tejano South Texas and the German Hill Country:** compare graveyard layout, mourning dress and who inherits ranch land.
- **Muscogee, Choctaw, Seminole, Miccosukee, Chitimacha and Coushatta:** each nation's own published account of mourning first; mortuary archaeology second.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast, Cuban Miami and Haitian Florida:** check how home altars meet the American funeral home.
- **Jewish Southerners, Greek Orthodox Tarpon Springs and Lebanese Texans:** compare burial societies and congregational cemeteries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Civil War death, cemeteries and vital statistics; mixed for sex ways; thin and partly closed for Native mortuary practice and Lucumí rites for the dead.
- **Lands:** the CSA's borders are not yet set by the author (its row carries the USA's pre-war lands); every Scorpio sheet therefore targets the core South, Tidewater and Carolinas through Texas and Florida.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial burial registers; Spanish for the Soto chronicles and Tejano wakes; Vietnamese for death anniversaries; Muscogee and Choctaw terms as the nations publish them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths under slavery, lynching and Native removal are history to state plainly; Confederate burial and memorial are contested memory; Native remains and burial sites follow the nations' rules; private religious rites stay private.
- **Held for review:** funeral and cremation customs of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
