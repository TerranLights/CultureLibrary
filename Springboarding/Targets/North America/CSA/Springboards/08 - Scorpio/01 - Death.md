# CSA: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English of the deathbed, the hospice and the ghost story; African American English; Gullah Geechee; Cajun French; Tejano Spanish; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese; Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Wiregrass and Piedmont whites against the Black Belt:** check whether deathbed customs and ghost lore differ within the same counties.
- **Gullah Geechee coast:** compare grave customs for children and the newly dead with inland Black churches.
- **Cajun prairie and New Orleans:** check how Catholic last rites, *revenant* tales and Carnival's mock death meet.
- **Muscogee, Choctaw and Seminole:** removal-era deaths, as each nation tells them, against the federal record.
- **Tejano South Texas:** test where *angelito* and the *velorio* survive and where the funeral home replaced them.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami:** compare spirit-of-the-dead rites with Catholic funerals in the same parishes.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast:** compare Buddhist and Catholic families' rites for the soul's passage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality statistics, Civil War death and memorial history; mixed for ghost lore; thin for Native grief practice and for Haitian rites of the dead.
- **Languages needed:** Haitian Creole for Gede coverage; Cajun French for tales of returning souls; Vietnamese for temple rites; Spanish for Tejano wakes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths in slavery, removal and lynching are history to state plainly; Confederate memorial work and its Black counterpart are contested memory; suicide data need care; Native burial and grief practice only as the nations share it.
- **Held for review:** death rites of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Pull WISQARS suicide and homicide rates for each Southern state, 1999 to now.

5. Ask the Muscogee Nation's cultural office for its account of the *Monmouth* deaths.
