# CSA: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** revival-tent and testimony English; African American English; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; the Tunica language as its revivers publish it. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Baptist and Methodist white congregations against Holiness and Pentecostal ones:** check whether conversion is dated to one moment or told as slow growth.
- **Black churches of the Black Belt and the Delta:** compare older "travel" and vision accounts with today's altar calls.
- **Sea Island congregations (St. Helena, Johns Island, Sapelo):** check what of the seeking journey remains and who still guides seekers.
- **Catholic Acadiana and Tejano South Texas:** set sacramental renewal and Cursillo weekends against evangelical conversion in the same towns.
- **Tunica-Biloxi, Lumbee, Mississippi Choctaw and Alabama-Coushatta:** each people's language or ceremonial revival as it narrates it.
- **Galveston, New Orleans and Mississippi Gulf towns:** compare who returned after storms and how rebuilding was told.
- **Vietnamese Buddhists and Catholics:** check how rebirth belief and baptism coexist in one family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revivals, camp meetings and disaster rebuilding; mixed for prison redemption (official accounts outweigh prisoners' own); thin for Native ceremonial renewal, by design.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Cursillo materials; Louisiana French for revival-era writing; Vietnamese for temple teaching on rebirth; Tunica only as the nation publishes it.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Redemption" as a name for violent overthrow is contested memory; prison conversion claims are disputed; mission conversion of Native peoples was often coerced; renewal ceremonies stay with their keepers.
- **Held for review:** conversion and renewal accounts from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
