# CSA: Control (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of plantation ledgers, levee boards and pulpits; African American English; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Mikasuki and Creek as the Seminole Tribe publishes them. **Concept in one line:** directing or restraining people, events and one's own life, and the felt need to do so.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tidewater and Lowcountry planter families against Piedmont yeomen:** check how household command over wives, children and labor is described in each.
- **Black families of the Yazoo Delta and Alabama's cotton counties:** check how peonage, patrols and ways of slipping them are told in family memory and testimony.
- **Mississippi and Atchafalaya river parishes:** compare how levee boards, farmers and Cajun fishers each speak of controlling or living with the river.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee:** compare how each tribe frames control over land, water and government.
- **Tejano families of the Valley and San Antonio:** check the chaperone and the father's say over courtship across generations.
- **Vietnamese and Cuban families:** check who decides for teenagers and how that shifts after a generation.
- **Holiness and Pentecostal congregations against Catholic Acadiana:** test how much of life each assigns to God's will and how much to human effort.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for slavery-era control, Jim Crow etiquette, river and fire management and school discipline; mixed for control between partners and within households; thin for control inside immigrant families, where oral history is the main route.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano oral histories; French for levee and parish records in Acadiana; Vietnamese for family-control studies; Mikasuki and Creek terms as the tribe publishes them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slavery and peonage are history to state plainly; school paddling and partner control involve children and victims; rootwork as control is private practice, recorded only as practitioners share it.
- **Held for review:** parental and household control in South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
