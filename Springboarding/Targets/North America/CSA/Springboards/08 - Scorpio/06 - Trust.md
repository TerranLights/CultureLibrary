# CSA: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of the country store, the clinic and the pulpit; African American English; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down, in the expectation of not being harmed; loyalty is studied here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners in cities and in rural counties:** check how trust in banks, clinics and churches differs, and what memories are given as reasons.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami:** compare trust in federal agencies after each people's different welcome.
- **Tejano South Texas:** check which messengers (promotoras, priests, Spanish radio) carry trust that agencies lack.
- **Rural white Protestants of the Piedmont and the Wiregrass:** compare trust in preachers, local merchants and distant institutions.
- **Small-town storekeepers of Jewish, Lebanese and Greek descent:** check whom they carried on credit, and how customers remember being trusted or refused.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast:** check whom families trust for news, money and health, by generation.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Choctaw and Lumbee:** read each nation's own account of trust and distrust with federal and state agencies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for survey trust, medical mistrust and banking history; mixed for everyday credit and handshake trust; thin inside small communities, which leave few written traces of whom they trust.
- **Languages needed:** Haitian Creole for the 1980s-1990s protests; Spanish for Cuban exile banking and promotora materials; Vietnamese for whom families trust for news.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** medical experiments and their victims are history to state plainly; trust gaps between groups must not be turned into traits; trust between nations and governments is told by the nations.
- **Held for review:** trust and credit networks of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
