# CSA: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of ballads, frontier reports and riot coverage; African American English; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong; forgiveness and amends as its alternatives.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upcountry and Piney Woods whites against Tidewater gentry:** check where grudges were settled by duel, by fist, by fire or by lawsuit.
- **Black communities of Atlanta, the Delta and the Carolina Piedmont:** compare how armed defense, forbearance and conjure are each remembered as answers to wrongs.
- **Creole New Orleans:** check how the duel and its etiquette differed from Anglo Louisiana.
- **Muscogee, Seminole and Miccosukee, and peoples descended from the Yamasee:** set the nations' tellings of raids and reprisals against colonial dispatches.
- **Tejanos of the lower Rio Grande Valley:** check how the 1915 reprisals are told by families and in corridos.
- **Cuban and Vietnamese exile communities:** compare how political punishment of "traitors" is spoken of now, and by whom.
- **Gulf Coast port towns:** check how rivalries over fishing grounds turned into reprisals and how they were damped down.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for lynching, riots and frontier wars; mixed for private feuds and grudges; thin for revenge inside exile communities, where fear limits what is said.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley corridos and Miami exile press; Vietnamese for exile-community violence; French for Creole dueling records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching, massacres and reprisals are history to state plainly, with victims named as their communities name them; the Charleston massacre involves living families; exile bombings involve living people.
- **Held for review:** feud and reconciliation customs in South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
