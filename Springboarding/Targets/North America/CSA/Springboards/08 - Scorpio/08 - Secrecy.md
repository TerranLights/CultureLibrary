# CSA: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of lodge proceedings, hearings and small-town fiction; African American English; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how it is judged what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is one of its facets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small towns of the Black Belt and the Wiregrass against Atlanta, Houston and New Orleans:** check whether open secrets work differently where anonymity is possible.
- **Black fraternal and benevolent orders and white lodges:** compare what each keeps closed and why members joined.
- **Gay and lesbian Southerners across generations:** check how far the closet reached in Protestant towns and in Catholic New Orleans.
- **Families across the color line, white and Black:** check how kin hidden for generations are now acknowledged, or not.
- **Cuban exiles of the 1960s:** check what veterans of secret operations will now say.
- **Vietnamese and Tejano families:** compare what is kept from children, schools and agencies, and why.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee and Muscogee:** separate the public from the restricted as each nation itself sets the line; nothing restricted is researched.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Klan hearings, Cold War sites and fraternal records; mixed for the closet (oral history strong, written records few); thin, by design, for restricted Native knowledge and for families' immigration secrets.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Miami exile memoirs and Valley studies; Vietnamese for escape accounts; French for Creole family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Klan terror and slavery are history to state plainly; outing the living is never done; restricted Native knowledge stays closed; immigration status endangers people and is studied only in aggregate.
- **Held for review:** privacy and family-secret customs in South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
