# CSA: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of emancipation songs, revolution flags and rally speeches; African American English; Louisiana French; Spanish (Texas, Cuban Miami); Vietnamese; Texas German; Seminole and Miccosukee usage in English. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a belief and political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowcountry and Sea Island descendants of the enslaved and of planters:** check how each tells the same emancipation story.
- **Black Belt, Delta and Texas towns with different "freedom day" dates:** compare when and why each kept its own.
- **New Orleans Creoles of color and Anglo-American neighbors:** check how freedom before 1865 shaped later claims.
- **Tejanos, Anglo Texans and Black Texans:** compare how the Texas Revolution and Mexican free soil are remembered.
- **Cuban, Venezuelan and Nicaraguan exiles in Miami-Dade:** test whether exile meanings of freedom match or differ by homeland.
- **Vietnamese refugee parents and the generation born in Texas and Louisiana:** check whether freedom means flight, rights or choice to each.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee and Lumbee:** freedom as sovereignty, in each nation's own words.
- **Hill Country Germans and Ozark hill people:** compare Unionist dissent and suspicion of government as their own traditions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emancipation and civil rights; mixed for exile communities; thin for Native freedom concepts in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Spanish Florida and Texas records; French for self-purchase papers; Vietnamese for April 30 media; German for Hill Country Unionism.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "liberty" claimed for slaveholding and secession, and freedom won by the enslaved, are contested memory and must be stated plainly as history; exile politics can be bitter; no group's narrative stands for the region.
- **Held for review:** freedom stories of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
