# CSA: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** courthouse, newsroom and testimony-service English, with Black and Gullah Geechee usage; Cajun French; border and Miami Spanish; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it, and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white Southern Baptists in the same towns:** check how each speaks of the Bible as the measure of truth, and whether they read it the same way.
- **Gullah Geechee coast against inland Georgia:** compare whose oral accounts become accepted history.
- **South Texas Tejano and Anglo families:** check how each tells the Rangers' years and the 1915-1919 killings.
- **Acadiana storytellers and New Orleans media:** compare how each signals fact against tale.
- **Houston's Vietnamese and Miami's Cubans and Venezuelans:** test how exile media and home-country news shape belief.
- **Coushatta, Alabama-Coushatta, Chitimacha and Houma:** each nation's own record keepers first.
- **Research Triangle, Atlanta and Houston science workers against rural neighbors:** check trust in scientific claims by place, not by assumption.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for polling on science and news and for Lost Cause history; mixed for rumor studies; thin for Native and Vietnamese ways of settling disputes over facts.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for the Laredo press; Vietnamese for radio archives; Cajun French for tale openings; Gullah for oral sources on the Landing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lost Cause and civil-rights history are contested memory; lynching reports name families; Jefferson's writing on race must be read as history, not as fact.
- **Held for review:** what counts as true among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
