# CSA: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** everyday Southern English of hedges, omens and church membership; Black usage and Sea Island speech; Cajun and Houma French; Valley and Miami Spanish; Vietnamese; Choctaw story terms as the nation publishes them. **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and the warrant wanted for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Coastal Plain, Piedmont and Ozark counties in the same folklore archives:** check whether omen beliefs cluster by subregion or by family origin.
- **Sea Island and Black Belt Black families:** compare whether "hag" and rootwork beliefs are told as fact, warning or memory.
- **Houma and Cajun communities of the lower bayous:** check how shared creature lore is claimed by each.
- **Valley Tejanos and San Antonio Mexican Americans:** compare *lechuza* and *ojo* beliefs by generation and church.
- **Hialeah's Cubans, Little Haiti and Kissimmee's Puerto Ricans:** check how saints and spirits overlap in each group's practice.
- **Vietnamese households, temple-going and parish-going:** compare which taboos each keeps and drops.
- **Highly churched rural counties against Atlanta, Austin and New Orleans:** test the standing of nonbelievers in each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for folklore archives and paranormal surveys; mixed for Tejano and Cajun creature lore; thin for Choctaw and Houma belief as the nations frame it.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for mission letters and Valley lore; Cajun French and Houma French for *rougarou* tellings; Vietnamese for taboo talk; Haitian Creole and Lucumí terms for Miami devotion.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** older collections label Black and Native belief as "superstition"; Native stories may be seasonal or closed; Lucumí rites are initiatory.
- **Held for review:** folk belief among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Sort Hand's entries by county and code them Coastal Plain, Piedmont or mountain.
2. Read *Paranormal America*'s regional tables and note the Southern sample size.
3. Search Valley newspapers and the Texas Folklore Society indexes for "lechuza".
4. Ask the Houma tribal office and Nicholls State's folklore holdings about *rougarou* recordings.
