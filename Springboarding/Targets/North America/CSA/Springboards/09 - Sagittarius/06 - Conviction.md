# CSA: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** revival and movement English (the altar call, the mass meeting, the floor speech); Black church usage; Louisiana French; Spanish of San Antonio's labor halls and Cuban exile prisons; Vietnamese; Miccosukee leaders' English. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content (a finding of guilt is not this concept).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lowcountry gentry families and their Quaker or abolitionist dissenters:** check how families remember those who broke with them.
- **Black Belt movement veterans and their white neighbors:** compare how each county honors or forgets those who took stands.
- **Plaquemines and Orleans Catholics:** check how bishops' and parishioners' convictions met over schools.
- **Westside San Antonio and the Valley:** compare labor and church conviction across Tejano generations.
- **Cuban exile prisoners' families and younger Cuban Americans:** check whether the *plantado* ideal still binds.
- **Miccosukee and Seminole:** compare how each nation's leaders chose their course in the 1950s, as each tells it.
- **Alabama and Texas church members on public religion:** test conviction against compromise county by county.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for civil-rights and segregationist conviction; mixed for labor and exile conviction; thin for Vietnamese and Native conviction told in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tenayuca's and Valladares's own words; Vietnamese for community stances; Louisiana French for Plaquemines parish memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** segregationist conviction and the Ten Commandments case are contested history to state plainly; martyrs' families keep their own memory; exile politics divide families.
- **Held for review:** principled stands taken by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
