# CSA: Education (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of commencements, college catalogs and the school-board meeting, in white and Black usage; the French of Louisiana parish schools; Spanish (South Texas, exile Miami); Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person; early schooling stays in Gemini.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families tied to HBCUs and those who chose flagships:** check how each weighs the two kinds of degree.
- **White rural families of the Wiregrass and Piedmont:** compare the first in the family to finish college with neighbors in the trades.
- **First-generation Tejano students from the border and from San Antonio:** check how *preparación* and family obligations shape where students enroll.
- **Cuban exile alumni and newer Venezuelan arrivals:** compare Catholic and exile schooling with public universities.
- **Vietnamese parents in Alief, Versailles and Biloxi:** check how the ideal of exam honor is framed for daughters and sons.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee, Mississippi Choctaw and Lumbee students:** compare tribal scholarship and regional campuses in each nation's own terms.
- **Hill Country Germans, Jewish Southerners and Greek Texans:** test how small groups' schools and colleges carried their languages or faiths.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for HBCU history, desegregation and federal statistics; mixed for Hispanic-Serving and Catholic schooling; thin for Native higher education in the South and for Vietnamese and Cuban educational ideals.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano family interviews and Cuban school histories; Vietnamese for honor and diploma talk; French for Louisiana parish schooling records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** desegregation violence and universities' slaveholding are history to state plainly; segregation academies and their memory are contested; Native students' records need the nations' consent.
- **Held for review:** schooling and college paths of South Asian-heritage families in the nation's lands.
