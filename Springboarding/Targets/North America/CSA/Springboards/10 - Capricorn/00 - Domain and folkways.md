# CSA: Domain and folkways, authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English as spoken from the Tidewater to the Rio Grande, white and Black; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Texas German; Muscogee and Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys, and what is owed: career, status, reputation, the ages of life, the keeping of time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tidewater and Piedmont against the cotton Deep South:** check whether gentry-led and planter-led command leave different records.
- **Lowcountry and Sea Islands:** praise-house offices and task-labor memory beside inland church and work authority.
- **Acadiana and New Orleans:** the rural *capitaine* and the urban Carnival court as two kinds of granted authority.
- **South Texas Tejanos and Hill Country Germans:** compare patron-led county politics with association-run towns.
- **Poarch Creek, Seminole, Miccosukee, Mississippi Choctaw, Coushatta, Chitimacha, Catawba, Lumbee:** what every nation publishes about its offices comes before colonial sources.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast and Houston; Cuban and Haitian South Florida:** check how parish, association and exile-group leaders are chosen.
- **Jewish Southerners; Greek, Lebanese and German Texans:** test whether small communities keep separate lines of authority.
- **Ozarks:** compare county politics with Deep South courthouse rings; Appalachian material stays comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elites, plantation records and elections; mixed for Tejano and Gulf Coast immigrant communities; thin for Native offices beyond what the nations publish.
- **Lands:** no territory is fixed yet (the row carries pre-war USA lands, and the author decides the borders); this sign aims at the Chesapeake, the Carolinas, the Gulf states, Florida and Texas.
- **Languages needed:** Portuguese and Spanish for entrada narratives; German for Texas association minutes; Vietnamese and Haitian Creole for living community leadership.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slavery's chain of command and Jim Crow deference are to be described plainly, as history; memory of Confederate post names is contested; Native installations appear only as each nation chooses.
- **Held for review:** elder and family-head roles among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
