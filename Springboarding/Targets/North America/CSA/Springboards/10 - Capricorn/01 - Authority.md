# CSA: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English; Gullah Geechee; Cajun French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Texas German; Mikasuki and Muscogee through the nations' own programs. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Belt and Delta, Black and white households:** check whether church and parental authority are described differently, and by whom.
- **Acadiana and New Orleans Catholics against Baptist and Methodist congregations:** compare bishop-led and congregation-led authority, and what each does when trust breaks.
- **Rio Grande Valley and Nueces Strip Tejanos beside Comal and Gillespie County Germans:** compare memories of Ranger and sheriff authority, and of association rule.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee:** set two Florida nations' forms of council authority beside each other, each in its own words.
- **Vietnamese parishes in Versailles, east Biloxi and Alief:** check how far priests' and elders' authority extends into secular life.
- **Cuban and Haitian Miami:** compare how exile organizations and churches claim to speak for a community.
- **Military towns (Fayetteville, Killeen, Columbus, the Florida Panhandle):** test whether a chain of command shapes civilian life around a post.
- **Lumbee and Robeson County; Melungeon families at the region's edges:** check who has had authority to classify them; Appalachian comparisons stay outside the core.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for policing, incarceration and evangelical family studies; mixed for Catholic Louisiana and Tejano communities; thin for Native council practice beyond public documents.
- **Languages needed:** German for Adelsverein correspondence; Spanish for mission records and Tejano accounts of the Rangers; Vietnamese for parish newsletters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clergy abuse and police violence touch living survivors; segregationist sermons are history and contested memory; tribal council deliberations may be closed. The permission share is scored from the law in force, under Libra.
- **Held for review:** studies of parental authority among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
