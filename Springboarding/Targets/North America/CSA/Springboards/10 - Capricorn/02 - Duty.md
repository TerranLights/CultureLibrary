# CSA: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of the Southern states and of Black Southerners; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese; Gullah Geechee for Sea Island family records; Choctaw and Muscogee through the nations' language offices. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Piedmont and lower Chattahoochee yeoman counties against planter counties:** check how duty to the Confederacy was argued over by class.
- **Black Belt and Delta families:** compare church, lodge and burial-society obligations, and whether these have shifted to kin or the state.
- **Military towns and the Florida Panhandle:** see whether duty language from the base reaches the wider county.
- **South Texas Tejanos:** set wartime service against what families say was owed to them in return.
- **Acadiana:** check whether Cajun French duty words separate family from parish obligations.
- **Haitian and Cuban South Florida; Vietnamese Houston and the Gulf Coast:** compare duties owed to a homeland, to parents and to the new country.
- **Tunica-Biloxi, Chitimacha, Coushatta, Seminole, Poarch Creek:** each nation's own statements of duty to its dead and its land.
- **Sea Islands:** grave-tending against land loss; inland Black cemeteries as a contrast.
- **Lebanese and Greek families of the Delta and Texas:** check whether family-business duty appears in their oral histories, and say so if not.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military service and jury exclusion; mixed for mutual aid; thin for filial duty among Vietnamese and Haitian Southerners outside community media.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese for veterans' and parish publications; Haitian Creole for family accounts of remittances; Spanish for Tejano veterans' letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** catechisms written to teach obedience to the enslaved are history, to quote sparingly; Uvalde families are living survivors; tribal burial matters follow the nation's lead.
- **Held for review:** filial-duty studies among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
