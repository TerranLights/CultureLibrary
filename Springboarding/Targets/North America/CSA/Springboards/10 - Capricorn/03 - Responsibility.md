## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Louisiana's river parishes and the coast:** compare how Black residents, Cajun fishing towns and industry speak of who answers for harm.
- **Delta and Black Belt counties:** set personal-responsibility rhetoric against what residents say about public services that failed them.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese fishing families:** check how responsibility for losses was argued after storms and the spill.
- **South Texas:** examine lawsuits and juries as the place where blame is settled, and how Tejano jurors are described.
- **Chickasaw and Choctaw homelands in Mississippi; the Mississippi Band of Choctaw:** seek the nations' own accounts of kin answerability; Adair is only a start.
- **Florida's coast and South Florida's Haitian and Cuban families:** compare hurricane preparedness duties assigned by the state with what households say they can carry.
- **Presbyterian, Baptist and Free Will Baptist congregations:** check whether doctrine on free will shapes talk about blame.
- **Piedmont and Carolinas youth courts:** see how the age of answerability changed and who argued for it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, lawsuits and church resolutions; mixed for youth caregiving; thin for Native kin answerability outside colonial accounts.
- **Languages needed:** German for the Salzburger originals; Vietnamese for fishers' claims; French for coastal parish meetings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** blame after Katrina and Jackson's water failure falls along race lines and is disputed; the Thornwell doctrine and church defenses of slavery are history to read with care.
- **Held for review:** accountability and blame studies among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
