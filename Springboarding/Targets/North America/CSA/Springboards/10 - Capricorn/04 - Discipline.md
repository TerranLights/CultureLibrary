# CSA: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, Black and white; Cajun French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Cuban Spanish; Choctaw and Muscogee through tribal education offices. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mississippi, Alabama, Arkansas and Texas school districts:** compare districts that still paddle with neighbors that stopped, and who decided.
- **Black and white Protestant households in the Black Belt and the Piedmont:** check how parents and pastors explain correction, without assuming one answer per group.
- **Catholic Acadiana and New Orleans:** compare Lenten and convent-school discipline with Baptist and Methodist church discipline.
- **Military counties around Parris Island, Fort Moore and Fort Cavazos:** test whether training culture shapes local schools and families.
- **South Texas Tejanos:** set school language punishment beside family discipline words such as *la chancla*.
- **Vietnamese families in Alief and on the Mississippi coast; Haitian and Cuban Miami:** check how immigrant parents and their children each describe correction.
- **Choctaw, Seminole and Creek families:** boarding-school memory alongside each nation's own teachings on raising children.
- **Ozarks:** troubled-teen boarding schools such as Agape (Stockton, Missouri) as a regional cluster to check.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school paddling, reform-school cases and military training; mixed for home discipline; thin for Vietnamese and Haitian parenting outside community media.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano school memories; Vietnamese for martial-arts and parish youth programs; Haitian Creole for parenting talk in Miami.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Dozier, boot-camp and hazing deaths involve living families; boarding-school testimony belongs to survivors first; home spanking is contested and easily stereotyped.
- **Held for review:** child-discipline research on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
