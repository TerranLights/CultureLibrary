# CSA: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English and African American English, including hip-hop usage; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Cajun French; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Muscogee for Poarch Creek enterprise materials. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Black households of the Delta and Alabama's Black Belt, and Atlanta's Black middle class:** compare what rising meant in a rural county and in the city that advertised itself for it.
- **Old planter families and new-money climbers:** check how each side's writings describe the other.
- **Texas oil and real-estate fortunes against Tejano civic striving:** set two kinds of ambition in one state beside each other.
- **Miami's Cuban, Haitian and Venezuelan arrivals:** set exile enterprise side by side across arrival waves without assuming one story.
- **Vietnamese families of Alief, Versailles and Biloxi:** check how fishing, retail and professional ambitions differ by place and generation.
- **Poarch Creek, Seminole and Mississippi Choctaw:** see how every one of these nations frames enterprise as serving members.
- **Women in home-party selling and pageant circuits:** examine what routes were open and who describes them.
- **Greek, Lebanese and Jewish small-business families:** look for each community's own accounts of rising; sources are scattered, so note where they are thin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business biographies and Atlanta history; mixed for Tejano and Vietnamese enterprise; thin for women's ambition outside sales and pageant records.
- **Languages needed:** Vietnamese for Bellaire business history; Spanish for exile and Tejano civic papers; Haitian Creole for migrants' own words on seeking a living.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "uppity" and lynching-era punishment of Black success are history and contested memory; casino enterprise is debated inside nations.
- **Held for review:** a mobility dataset led by a South Asian-heritage scholar, and studies of striving among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, both set aside under the source rule.
