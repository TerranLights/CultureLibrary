# CSA: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English of planter, mill, farm and city; African American English; Louisiana French and Louisiana Creole; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; French for the Natchez accounts. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Charleston and the Lowcountry against Delta planter towns:** compare old-family lists with cotton-fortune rank.
- **Black communities of Atlanta, Houston, New Orleans and the rural Black Belt:** check how class and color rank inside each, in members' own words.
- **New Orleans Creoles of color, white Creoles and Cajuns:** test how the same word sorted people in different ways.
- **South Texas Tejanos and Anglo ranchers; Mexican, Black and poor white cotton workers in Central Texas:** examine a three-way order in one state.
- **Lumbee, Robeson County's Black and white residents:** check how three school systems and church networks fixed rank.
- **Cuban arrival waves in Miami, and Afro-Cubans within them:** seek accounts from each wave.
- **Vietnamese Catholics and Buddhists; Northern and Southern origins:** check whether old Vietnamese distinctions carry over in Gulf Coast towns.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee clans; Natchez descendants among the Muscogee:** let each describe clan or rank in its own terms.
- **Piedmont mill villages and Ozark farm counties:** compare white class lines where race is less the main sort.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Jim Crow and planter society; mixed for color and class inside Black, Creole and Cuban communities; thin for Vietnamese and Native internal rank outside community voices.
- **Languages needed:** French for du Pratz and New Orleans Creole papers; Spanish for Miami exile papers and Texas Spanish-language newspapers; Vietnamese for parish and association records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slurs in titles and sources are quoted only as evidence; colorism is painful inside communities and needs their framing; clan matters are each nation's to share.
- **Held for review:** caste and class studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read du Pratz's Natchez chapters in French and in English and list his rank terms.

3. Search the *Crimson White* archive for "Machine" and "sorority" (2013).
4. Search Black newspapers (*Atlanta Daily World*, *Louisiana Weekly*) for "cotillion" by decade.
