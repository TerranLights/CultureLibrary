*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural and suburban high schools in Texas:** check how class rank treats schools of very different strength.
- **Black families and HBCU graduates in Atlanta, Houston and the Delta:** compare the "twice as good" teaching with institutional measures of merit.
- **Courthouse towns of Mississippi and Louisiana:** set patronage and kin networks against civil-service rules, using local reporting.
- **Tejano school districts from Laredo to Brownsville and in Bexar County:** check gifted-program and scholarship access.
- **Vietnamese and Cuban families:** test how "model minority" framing is used and contested, in their own voices.
- **Haitian Miami:** look for proverbs and sermons on unrewarded labor.
- **Muscogee (Poarch Creek), Mississippi Choctaw, Seminole:** compare earned titles with elected and inherited office, in each nation's words.
- **Calvinist and Arminian Baptists; Catholic Acadiana:** compare grace and works in sermons and catechesis.
- **Lumbee in Robeson County:** check how teacher training opened a path to merit-based standing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for scholarships, admissions and school data; mixed for patronage; thin for Native earned titles beyond each nation's publications.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Tejano parent groups; Vietnamese for community honor-roll notices; Haitian Creole for proverb collections.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "model minority" language can harm the people it praises; patronage cases involve named living officials; earned names may be private to families and nations.
- **Held for review:** credential and merit studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
