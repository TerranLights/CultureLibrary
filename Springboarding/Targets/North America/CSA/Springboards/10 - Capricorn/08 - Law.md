# CSA: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** courthouse and street English of the South, white and Black; Border Spanish of the Rio Grande counties; Louisiana French; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Choctaw and Muscogee through tribal justice offices. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. This sheet takes the spirit of the law; the letter belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Dry and wet counties of Mississippi, Arkansas, Texas and Alabama:** check where moral law and practice part, county by county.
- **Black and white residents of Tallahatchie, Sunflower and Dallas (Alabama) counties:** compare trust in juries, sheriffs and federal courts, and how memory of trials shapes it.
- **Rio Grande Valley Tejanos:** examine how checkpoints, Rangers' history and local courts shape views of law.
- **Acadiana and New Orleans:** compare smuggler and outlaw legends with Catholic teaching on law and mercy.
- **Vietnamese Gulf Coast communities:** test how the village-custom proverb is used, if at all, in disputes.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami:** check whether homeland experience of law is cited in how each group speaks of U.S. courts.
- **Choctaw, Poarch Creek, Seminole and Miccosukee:** set tribal courts beside federal and state law in each nation's own words.
- **West Texas ranch counties and the Ozarks:** compare frontier self-rule legends with present-day sheriffs' discretion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for civil-rights-era law and capital punishment; mixed for trust in courts by state; thin for tribal court practice beyond published codes.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley press and Border Patrol accounts; French for Barataria papers; Vietnamese and Haitian Creole for community views of courts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching, the Till case and executions involve living families; conjure practice is private for some practitioners; jail conditions touch people still held. The letter of the law (statutes, cases as written) stays in Libra.
- **Held for review:** legal-culture studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
