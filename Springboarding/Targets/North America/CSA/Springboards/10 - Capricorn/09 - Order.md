## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Savannah and Charleston against cotton-boom towns:** check whether planned colonial order left a different civic culture.
- **Atlanta and Miami Black communities:** compare how riots and crackdowns are remembered, and by whom.
- **South Florida after storms:** set Cuban, Haitian, Black and white neighborhoods side by side for who got order restored first.
- **Houston's unzoned neighborhoods against zoned Dallas and San Antonio:** examine what residents mean by an orderly street.
- **Rio Grande Valley colonias:** set residents' own accounts against state measures of order.
- **Vietnamese neighborhoods of Alief, Port Arthur and Biloxi:** test whether order words differ between generations.
- **Seminole, Miccosukee and Poarch Creek:** compare public-order practice on tribal lands in each nation's own terms.
- **Florida's planned and retirement towns:** compare HOA order with older small towns nearby.
- **Black Belt yards and churchyards:** check yard and grave decoration as order by its makers' rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, town plans and disaster studies; mixed for neighborhood disorder data; thin for tribal public order beyond public statements.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Bexar colonial records and colonia residents; Haitian Creole for post-storm Miami accounts; Vietnamese for Gulf Coast community records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** race riots and uprisings involve contested memory and living families; colonias and yard practices invite stereotype and need residents' framing.
- **Held for review:** public-order and neighborhood studies on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
