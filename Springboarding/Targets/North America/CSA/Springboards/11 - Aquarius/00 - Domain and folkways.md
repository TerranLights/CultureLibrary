# CSA: Domain and folkways, community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English; Gullah Geechee; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Texas Czech and German; Muscogee and Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups and what they hope for: friends, associations, humanitarian ideals, the future, and the customs of settling, migrating, gathering and greeting.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **East Texas freedom colonies and Black Belt county seats:** check whether church, school and lodge bind alike in dispersed and compact settlements.
- **Central Texas Germans and Czechs:** compare lodge hall and parish as the main meeting place.
- **Acadiana, prairie against bayou:** test whether evening visiting and dance halls work the same way.
- **Cuban Miami; Vietnamese Houston and New Orleans East:** compare hometown, parish and veterans' groups as ways in.
- **Mississippi Choctaw, Poarch Creek, Seminole, Miccosukee:** start from what each nation publishes about its communities.
- **Lumbee of Robeson County:** test how church, settlement and surname mark membership.
- **Small-town Jewish Southerners:** check how one congregation served a county's few families.
- **Ozarks hill settlements and Piedmont mill villages:** compare built and grown settlements; Appalachia is comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for congregations, lodges and newspapers; mixed for immigrant groups; thin for smaller Native communities beyond what each publishes.
- **Lands:** the CSA's territory is not settled yet (pre-war USA lands are carried; the author will fix them); these sheets look mainly at the old Confederate states, Florida and Texas.
- **Languages needed:** Czech and German for Texas lodge minutes; Vietnamese for directories; Spanish for exile and Tejano groups; French for the tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Klan and the Citizens' Councils belong to associational history; describe them plainly, from records and their targets' accounts; memory of 1836 is contested; Native gatherings only as each nation opens them.
- **Held for review:** associations of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; social-capital atlases led by U.S. scholars of South Asian heritage.
