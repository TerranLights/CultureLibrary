# CSA: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, white and Black; Lumbee English; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Africatown and Princeville:** compare a town founded by Africans and one founded by freed people for how each names its founders.
- **Charleston and Lowcountry church kitchens:** check who signs the recipes in white and Black cookbooks of one county.
- **San Antonio's West Side and the Valley colonias:** compare parish-based and colonia-based organizing.
- **Little Haiti, Little Havana and Hialeah:** check whether parish, hometown or block is the first circle named.
- **Port Arthur, Biloxi and New Orleans East Vietnamese:** compare small fishing towns with a large urban parish.
- **Lumbee settlements and Seminole reservations:** read each people's own sources on which unit (settlement, reservation, church, clan) is called the community.
- **Mountain View and Ozark hill towns against Delta towns:** test whether gatherings run through music, church or courthouse.
- **Jewish congregations of Natchez, Port Gibson and Vicksburg:** check how shrinking congregations close or hand over their cemeteries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for community studies of the 1930s-50s and for church records; mixed for colonias and immigrant parishes; thin for small Native and Lumbee settlements outside their own media.
- **Languages needed:** Creole for the Miami parish's own bulletins and radio; Vietnamese for Gulf Coast parish bulletins; Spanish for COPS and LUPE records; Choctaw for the Mississippi Band's own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** community studies of the Jim Crow era used caste language; read them as period documents; Africatown descendants' accounts come first on the *Clotilda*; flood buyouts in Black towns are contested.
- **Held for review:** neighborhood and temple communities of South Asian-heritage residents in the nation's lands.
