# CSA: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of the white and Black South; Louisiana French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Texas German; Choctaw. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Piedmont mill villages and Black Belt farm counties:** check whether union drives and church groups drew on the same networks.
- **Black Masking groups, Baby Dolls and Carnival krewes:** compare how each admits members and ranks them.
- **Laredo Tejanos and San Antonio Germans:** compare a political congress and a singing society as ways a people acts as one.
- **Vietnamese elders' associations and campus VSAs:** check where the two generations' groups overlap in members and causes.
- **Cuban Miami's generations:** test whether exile-era and post-1990s arrivals draw the "us" line in the same place.
- **Tunica-Biloxi, Mississippi Choctaw and Alabama-Coushatta:** each nation's own account of clan, town or tribe as the unit.
- **Sorority rush at state flagships and HBCU band life:** compare campus groups across the color line.
- **Ozark and Lowcountry counties:** check one-party sorting in places with different settlement histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, campuses and elections; mixed for masking groups (much held in oral tradition and photography); thin for small Native nations outside their own pages.
- **Languages needed:** German for Männerchor minutes; Spanish for the 1911 congress papers; Vietnamese for student-club programs; French for Cajun usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching crowds and their photographs need care and context; Reconstruction insults are period evidence, not labels; masking groups decide what of their practice is shown.
- **Held for review:** campus and regional groups of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, set aside by rule.
