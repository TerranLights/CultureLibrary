# CSA: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern English, white and Black; Louisiana French; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Texas Alliance farmers and Georgia Populists:** check whether cooperative buying or politics came first in each.
- **White Progressives and Black clubwomen of the same towns:** compare which reforms each pursued and how each wrote about the other.
- **Southern Baptist moderates and conservatives:** compare how each side's papers describe the same convention votes.
- **Tremé residents and city planners:** test how each describes the highway's purpose and cost.
- **New Orleans parents, teachers and charter founders:** compare accounts of the school overhaul by role and by race.
- **Valley colonias and San Antonio's West Side:** check whether reform came through parishes in both.
- **Chitimacha, Mississippi Choctaw and Seminole:** read each nation's own account of change it chose against change imposed.
- **Cuban, Vietnamese and Haitian arrivals:** check how each group's press uses the word "reform", given the regimes each left.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Populism, Progressivism and civil-rights-era reform; mixed for church reform (partisan sources on both sides); thin for Native-led renewal beyond each nation's own pages.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for colonia and exile sources; Vietnamese for refugee views of *đổi mới*; French for the Cajun revival; Haitian Creole for Miami Haitian organizing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eugenics survivors and their families come first; the Southern Baptist split and the New Orleans school overhaul are contested; segregation-era reformers must be read with their limits stated plainly.
- **Held for review:** reform and civic groups of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
