# CSA: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and African American English; Louisiana French and colonial French; Tejano and Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 1. Search terms
| Language | Term | Gloss | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Louisiana French | *c'est pas mes affaires* | that's not my business | *check* Cajun form |

| Vietnamese | *buông bỏ*; *thờ ơ* | letting go (in the Buddhist sense); indifferent | praise and blame |

English keywords: composure, reserve, solitude, hermit, non-attachment, contemplative life, objectivity, apathy, living alone, bystander. (Impartiality in judging belongs to Libra.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Okefenokee and Atchafalaya swamp families:** check how each describes living apart, in their own oral histories.
- **Trappist and Buddhist monastics:** compare how each teaches withdrawal, and who comes to them.
- **Texas ranch men and Gulf Coast fishermen:** check what reserve each group's own stories praise or fault.
- **Older and younger Cuban Americans:** test distance from homeland politics by birth decade.
- **Vietnamese elders and their grown children:** check whether "letting go" means the same thing in temple and in family talk.
- **Rural elders living alone and urban professionals living alone:** compare reasons given and supports used.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee:** begin with how the two tribes' own media speak of composure and restraint, since outside observers have long misread respect as coldness.
- **Black Southerners under Jim Crow:** check how memoirs describe guarded composure in public as a survival practice, in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature and census counts; mixed for contemplative communities (their own writing); thin for swamp and backwoods families beyond a few oral-history projects.
- **Languages needed:** colonial French for Hachard; Vietnamese for monastery teachings; Spanish for Cuban and Tejano idioms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the guarded calm segregation forced on Black Southerners belongs in the record as plain history; elders living alone are a privacy concern in local records; monastic communities set what visitors may record.
- **Held for review:** contemplative and retreat traditions of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Hachard's letters in English translation; note each passage on leaving the world.
