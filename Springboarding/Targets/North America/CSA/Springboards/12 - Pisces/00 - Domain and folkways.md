# CSA: Domain and folkways, the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern and Black Southern English; Gullah Geechee; Cajun French; Kouri-Vini; Tejano Spanish; Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Haitian Creole; Mikasuki, Choctaw and Muscogee. **Concept in one line:** how people meet suffering, the unseen and the wish to escape: the hidden and the unconscious, confinement and institutions, sacrifice, compassion and transcendence, with magic ways as the folkway.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Sea Islands and Lowcountry Gullah Geechee:** separate spirit-and-sleep lore here from inland Black Belt conjure, to see if one tradition or two.
- **South Louisiana:** set Catholic treaters, Creole Vodou in New Orleans and Protestant north Louisiana side by side for where healing, magic and prayer divide.
- **Rio Grande Valley and South Texas Tejanos:** check healer networks against clinic use, county by county.
- **Miami-Dade Cubans and Little Haiti:** compare spirit work in two Caribbean traditions sharing one city's botánicas.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** check Buddhist and Catholic households separately for rites to the dead.
- **Native nations (Choctaw, Seminole, Miccosukee, Poarch Creek):** start only from what each publishes.
- **Lumbee:** ask Lumbee writers and churches first where revival ends and older healing knowledge begins.
- **The Delta, the Ozarks and the Piedmont mill towns:** compare white rural healing, haunt lore and asylum use across three economies; Appalachia stays comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for asylums, health statistics and ghost lore; mixed for living healing practice; thin and properly guarded for Native ceremonial knowledge.
- **Lands:** the author has yet to fix how much of the pre-war USA falls to the CSA; this sign's sheets aim at the former Confederate states, with Florida and Texas.
- **Languages needed:** Kouri-Vini and Cajun French, since treaters pray in them; Spanish and Haitian Creole for Miami and Valley spirit work; Vietnamese for temple notices; Native languages only via each nation's own materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** asylum and leprosarium records hold patients' names; plantation ghost tours touch slavery's memory, which is contested; Native medicine and ceremony are restricted unless a nation shares them; hoodoo and Vodou have been sensationalized, so start from practitioners' accounts.
- **Held for review:** spirituality and healing practices of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and U.S. teachers and movements of that heritage active in the region.
