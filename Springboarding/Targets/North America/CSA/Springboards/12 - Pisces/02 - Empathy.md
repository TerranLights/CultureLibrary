# CSA: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English; African American English; Tejano Spanish; Vietnamese; Cuban Spanish; Louisiana French; Gullah Geechee. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **White and Black Southerners in the same counties:** check perspective-taking across the color line with paired interviews, never one side's account alone.
- **Southwest Louisiana industrial towns against New Orleans:** test whether Hochschild's political divide shows the same "deep story" elsewhere in the state.
- **Rio Grande Valley:** separate listening by clinicians, crisis volunteers and kin.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese, by generation:** compare how parents and American-raised children name and show understanding.
- **Miami Cubans and Haitians:** check how far each community reads the other's arrival stories.
- **Gullah Geechee and Sea Islands:** start from Gullah speakers on being heard by outsiders and researchers.
- **Native nations:** sources on listening customs are thin and partly restricted; begin with each nation's public health and language offices.
- **Lumbee and Robeson County's three-way history:** compare how Lumbee, Black and white residents describe being understood by the others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for race and empathy research and for fiction; mixed for clinical data by state; thin for smaller communities, where interviews will be needed.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Texas crisis-line scripts; Vietnamese for family studies; Gullah for oral sources on outsiders; French only where older Acadiana speakers are interviewed.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "walk in their shoes" projects can wound the people imitated; read *Black Like Me* with Black readers' responses; pain-bias research touches live harm in care; avoid any one group's account of another.
- **Held for review:** clinical-empathy studies by scholars from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands working in Southern medical schools.
