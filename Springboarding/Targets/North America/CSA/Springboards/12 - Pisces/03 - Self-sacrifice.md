# CSA: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English; African American English; Tejano Spanish; Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Texas Czech; colonial Spanish (for mission records); Apalachee and Guale only through mission-era sources. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families in the Black Belt and Delta:** check which sacrifices (movement deaths, military service, caregiving) are honored in church and which go unspoken.
- **White Southern families with Confederate and later military ancestors:** separate memorial customs for ancestors from present service, without assuming continuity.
- **Tejano families of South Texas:** compare vows to saints, military service and parents' migrant labor as named sacrifices.
- **Cuban Miami:** check how the exile story frames who gave what for whom, across arrival waves.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** set the parent generation's escape beside children's accounts of what was owed.
- **Czech and German Texans:** compare lodge and volunteer-fire-company traditions after deaths on duty.
- **Native nations (Seminole, Choctaw, Poarch Creek):** only what each nation publishes about service and loss; thin otherwise.
- **Lumbee:** thin; start with Lumbee veterans' and church histories for what is said of service.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for memorials, military and health data; mixed for caregiving by subregion; thin for Native and small-community accounts.
- **Languages needed:** colonial Spanish for mission martyr records; Vietnamese for escape memoirs; Czech for older West and Ellis County sources; Spanish for vows.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martyr lists are contested (Spanish missions; the Alamo; Confederate and civil-rights dead): give each side's records; caregiving and donation data touch health privacy; plasma selling ties to poverty.
- **Held for review:** devotional fasting and vow customs of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
