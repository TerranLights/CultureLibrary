# CSA: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Pentecostal, Holiness and Baptist congregations, Black and white:** check which allow or discourage being overcome in worship, and how members describe it.
- **Cajun and Creole Louisiana:** compare French loss and revival on the prairies, the bayous and among Kouri-Vini speakers.
- **South Texas Tejanos:** check generational Spanish loss against border-town retention.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** compare parish and temple families on language and on fear of *mất gốc*.
- **Miami Cubans:** test assimilation talk across exile waves and among the U.S.-born.
- **Choctaw, Seminole, Miccosukee and Poarch Creek:** start from each nation's own language and history programs.
- **Melungeon and Lumbee families:** check how identities once pushed to dissolve are now reclaimed, in their own writers.
- **Piedmont mill towns and Delta farm towns:** compare how places ended and what residents say was lost.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language and dementia data and church records; mixed for ecstatic experience; thin for dissolution of small Native and immigrant groups.
- **Languages needed:** Kouri-Vini and French for revival materials; Vietnamese for school notices; Spanish for border oral histories; Choctaw only through the nation's own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** assimilation history (Removal, schooling against Spanish and French) needs the affected people's accounts first; church splits are live disputes; dementia accounts touch privacy.
- **Held for review:** meditation and absorption practices brought by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
