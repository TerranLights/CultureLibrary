*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Border Tejanos in Laredo, Brownsville and El Paso against inland San Antonio:** check whether a river line is lived as a seam or a wall.
- **Black and white Southerners in former Jim Crow towns:** compare how each describes the lines once enforced and those still felt, never one account for both.
- **Creoles of color and Cajuns in Louisiana:** check how category lines were drawn and crossed inside Catholic parishes.
- **Lumbee, Black and white Robeson County:** set the three-way lines of school, church and marriage side by side.
- **Jewish Southerners in small towns:** compare observance and mixing in towns with one synagogue and with none.
- **Vietnamese and Haitian households:** check family privacy across generations against school and therapy norms.
- **Seminole and Miccosukee:** territory and access only as each nation states it; reservation-line disputes are theirs to describe.
- **Texas ranch country and Piedmont suburbs:** compare fences, posted land and gated subdivisions as ways of marking land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for border data, segregation and land history; mixed for personal space and privacy, where surveys rarely sort by region; thin for Native territory beyond each nation's statements.
- **Languages needed:** border Spanish for Martínez-style interviews; Vietnamese for family forums; Haitian Creole for Little Haiti residents; Hebrew and Yiddish food terms for kosher practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the color line and passing are painful histories, so let the people who lived them speak first; border debates are partisan; Native land claims are live; household privacy studies need consent.
- **Held for review:** neighborhood and household studies of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, such as Houston suburbs.
