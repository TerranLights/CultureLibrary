*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Delta and Black Belt Black communities:** check juke joints, church and travel North as three different ways out, in residents' words.
- **White working-class Gulf Coast and Texas towns:** compare honky-tonks, beach weekends and hunting camps as named retreats.
- **South Texas Tejanos:** set *cantina*, dance hall and family fiesta apart, and check who is welcome at each.
- **Miami Cubans:** check whether nostalgia works as escape or as politics, across arrival waves.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** compare karaoke, video and temple festivals as relief for first and second generations.
- **Native nations with gaming (Mississippi Choctaw, Seminole, Poarch Creek):** read what each says of its casinos and its own recovery programs.
- **Florida retirees and snowbirds:** test whether moving South is described as escape from the North, and how locals see it.
- **Cajun Acadiana:** separate the tourist slogan of good times from house dances and the camp on the bayou.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for alcohol, gambling and tourism data; mixed for addiction by small area; thin for how smaller groups describe their own escapes.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for South Texas bar culture; Vietnamese for karaoke and media; Cuban Spanish for nostalgia media; Cajun French for house-dance memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** addiction research needs people in recovery speaking for themselves; tribal gaming should be framed in each nation's terms; avoid tying any ethnic group to a vice.
- **Held for review:** entertainment, film and media consumed by South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
