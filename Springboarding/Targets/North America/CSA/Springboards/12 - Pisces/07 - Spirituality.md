# CSA: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English; African American English; Tejano Spanish; Cuban Spanish and Lucumí ritual terms; Louisiana French; Vietnamese; Natchez, Choctaw and Muscogee only through the nations' own materials. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion; here the experience, the practice and the sacred outside the church (institutions belong to Sagittarius).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **New Orleans Black Spiritual churches, Baptist congregations and Vodou temples:** check how practitioners draw the line between church and practice.
- **Tejano Catholics in San Antonio and the Valley:** compare parish-led and home-led devotion, by generation.
- **Miami Cubans:** separate Catholic, Lucumí and Espiritismo practice, noting people who move among them.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** set Buddhist meditation and Catholic devotion side by side, with ancestor rites in both.
- **Natchez, Choctaw, Muscogee and Seminole people:** begin with each nation's own statements; closed ceremonies are not researched.
- **White evangelicals and the unchurched rural South:** check whether nature and family land carry sacred talk outside church.
- **Greek, Lebanese and other Orthodox and Eastern Catholic Southerners:** compare icon corners and saint days with neighbors' practice.
- **Gullah Geechee:** test older seeking and spirit practice against present church life, in Gullah speakers' own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic devotion, New Orleans and Miami practice; mixed for the unchurched; thin and partly closed for Native spiritual life.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and Lucumí terms for Miami; Spanish for Guadalupan devotion; Vietnamese for temple retreats; French for older Acadiana devotions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native ceremony is closed unless a nation opens it; Vodou and Lucumí have been sensationalized and legally targeted, so start from practitioners; Spiritual-church borrowings of Native figures need careful framing.
- **Held for review:** meditation centers of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, and U.S. spiritual teachers of that heritage active in the South.
