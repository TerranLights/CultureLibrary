# CSA: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English; African American English; Tejano and border Spanish; Cuban Spanish; Vietnamese; Cajun French. **Concept in one line:** what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Statutes, courts and legal culture belong to Libra and Capricorn; this sheet takes wrongdoing as a moral and social category.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Southerners and white Southerners in the same counties:** check which acts each names as the worst wrongs, and how lynching and policing shape that, with each side speaking for itself.
- **Tejanos of border towns:** compare views of smuggling, theft and violence in Laredo, El Paso and the Valley.
- **Miami Cubans across arrival waves:** check how the Mariel stigma is remembered by those who carried it.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese:** start from community press on crimes against the community and on silences inside it; sources are mostly Vietnamese-language.
- **Haitian Miami:** compare how the community describes being labeled and being victimized.
- **Native nations:** wrongs done to them (Removal, land fraud) and their own ways of repair, only as each nation states them; thin.
- **Lumbee of Robeson County:** look for Lumbee writers on wrongdoing and reputation, rather than outside crime coverage.
- **Rural white Texas and Louisiana:** compare outlaw legends, feud memory and church talk of sin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for outlaw history, lynching data and true crime; mixed for how people judge wrongs (few regional surveys); thin for smaller communities' own accounts.
- **Languages needed:** border Spanish for Campbell-style interviews; Vietnamese for community press; Cuban Spanish for exile reactions; French for Acadiana outlaw tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lynching, policing and race are the core of the subject and must be stated plainly, with victims' communities first; Afro-Cuban religion was smeared after 1989; avoid any link between a people and crime; law questions go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** victimization and hate-crime studies centered on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
