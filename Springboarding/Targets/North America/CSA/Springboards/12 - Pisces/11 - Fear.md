# CSA: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** CSA, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Southern American English; African American English; Gullah Geechee; Tejano Spanish; Haitian Creole; Vietnamese; Cajun French. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black and white Southerners in the Black Belt and Delta:** compare what each was taught to fear as children (patrols, police, the Klan, crime, the dark), with each group's own accounts.
- **Dixie Alley tornado towns and Gulf hurricane towns:** check how fear and drills differ for storms with minutes of warning and storms with days.
- **Tejano families on the border and inland:** compare fear of *la migra*, cartels and street crime, by citizenship status.
- **Haitian and Cuban Miami:** check how legal status shapes fear of the state.
- **Gulf Coast Vietnamese fishers:** set storm, spill and debt worries side by side, in Vietnamese-language sources.
- **Evangelical and Pentecostal congregations:** compare fear of hell in preaching across denominations and generations.
- **Gullah Geechee and Cajun communities:** check night-spirit and swamp-creature dread as living or remembered lore.
- **Native nations:** what each fears or warns of in the land is theirs to share; begin from their published environmental and cultural pages.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster, crime-fear and mental-health data; mixed for folk fears; thin for Native accounts and for small immigrant communities.
- **Languages needed:** Haitian Creole for deportation fear; Vietnamese for fishers' worries; Spanish for raid coverage and bogeyman lore; Gullah for night-spirit accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slave patrols and racial terror are the history of fear for many families, so state them plainly and let descendants speak; raid coverage involves children; fear of a group must be recorded, never repeated.
- **Held for review:** anxiety and fear studies drawn from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
