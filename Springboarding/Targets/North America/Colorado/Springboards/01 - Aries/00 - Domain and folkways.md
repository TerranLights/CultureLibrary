*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range metro:** check how "native" and "transplant" work as self-labels by decade of arrival.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; the Northern Ute's Colorado history):** check each nation's own published accounts of naming, kept separate because removal made the White River and Uncompahgre record a history.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsétsėhéstȧhese:** check Plains histories and Denver's urban Native community for naming practices shared in public.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley; Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo; newer Mexican and Central American arrivals:** check which self-labels each uses on forms and in speech.
- **Japanese Americans of the Amache generation and after:** check generation terms as self-location.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield):** check studio portraits and church records.
- **Volga German families of Weld and Logan counties:** check surname changes across generations.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check how home naming forms meet US records.
- **Latter-day Saint towns of Conejos County; evangelical and military Colorado Springs; ranching families:** check genealogy, congregation, unit and brand as places a self is located.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for names, census categories and health surveys; mixed for self-concept studies at state level; thin for smaller communities in their own languages. Colorado's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the state as it stands, with the neighboring Mountain West as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** *manito* Spanish for the valley's naming record; the three Native languages only through tribal channels; Japanese for camp-era family papers; the five Aurora community languages for naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nuche, Arapaho and Cheyenne naming belongs to those nations; use only what they publish. Amache papers and agency rolls were made under confinement and removal: read them as accounts.
- **Held for review:** naming studies pooling all Asian American communities were considered; none was held, but research should split communities rather than take a pooled figure.
