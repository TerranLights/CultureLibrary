## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boulder and Denver tech founders against Eastern Plains and San Luis Valley small businesses:** check whether necessity or opportunity is the stated reason for starting.
- **Western Slope (Grand Junction, the North Fork) orchard and winery founders:** check how agricultural ventures are begun and financed.
- **Ski and mining towns (Aspen, Telluride, Silverton):** check founding stories that cite outsiders against local starters.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley; Mexican and Central American families of Denver and the Arkansas Valley:** check *tandas* and family money behind first businesses.
- **Nuche:** check who starts programs in tribal life, from the Tribes' own reports, not outside accounts.
- **Black Coloradans (Dearfield, Five Points):** check self-started towns, churches and businesses as a line of initiative.
- **Japanese Americans after Amache:** check postwar farm and business restarts in the Arkansas Valley and Denver.
- **Volga German families:** check how beet laborers moved to owning farms.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check rotating savings clubs as start-up money.
- **Evangelical Colorado Springs; Latter-day Saint towns:** check church-founded charities and ministries.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business formation, ballot measures and the gold rush; mixed for volunteering by community; thin for rotating savings clubs, which are informal and rarely recorded.
- **Languages needed:** Mexican Spanish, Korean, Vietnamese, Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for savings clubs and founders' stories; Japanese for camp co-op papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Bent's Fort trade and the Union Colony sit in the history of land taking; read founders' accounts against Native ones. Amache co-ops ran under confinement. Ballot rules are law: to Libra.
- **Held for review:** national entrepreneurship texts with no Colorado tie were set aside as off-target; nothing held under the standing rules.
