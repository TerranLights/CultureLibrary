## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range newcomers against long-settled families:** check how long a person stays "new" in Denver against Pueblo or the Eastern Plains.
- **Mountain and ski towns (Leadville, Aspen, Telluride, Silverton):** check founding, bust and refounding stories told by old miners' families and new residents.
- **Nuche:** check which calendar and which origin account each Tribe offers publicly, and the Northern Ute's telling of leaving Colorado.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsétsėhéstȧhese:** check how their histories of the Plains before 1858 enter Colorado's founding story.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check acequia and wedding rites as the community's own beginnings, separate from the gold-rush story.
- **Mexican and Central American Denver; Chicanos of Pueblo:** check *año nuevo, vida nueva* practices and arrival stories.
- **Volga German and Japanese American families:** check arrival in the beet fields and the restart after Amache's closure in 1945.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Vietnamese and Korean communities:** check which new year is kept at home, which in public.
- **Latter-day Saint towns; Colorado Springs evangelicals:** check pioneer founding against born-again starts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the gold rush, mining towns and migration; mixed for Hispano rites (scattered recordings); thin for Ute calendars, which the Tribes may keep private.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for ditch and wedding rites; Amharic and Tigrinya for Enkutatash; Vietnamese and Korean for Lunar New Year programs; Volga German dialect for New Year wishes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bear Dance and Ute origin accounts are the Tribes' own; cite only what they share. The gold rush and Sand Creek sit behind any founding story: present both sides' accounts. Mesa Verde stays the Pueblo peoples' heritage; it is not Colorado's origin to claim.
- **Held for review:** a New Year observance pointer tied to a religious site was considered; held as a category only (Buddhist communities in the nation's lands, a religion the author has not yet ruled on).
