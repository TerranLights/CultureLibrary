## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Eastern Plains and North Park ranching counties against the Front Range metro:** check federal-transfer and farm-program dependence alongside self-reliance talk.
- **Western Slope (Grand Junction, the North Fork):** check orchard and ranch families' use of federal water and grazing against local self-images.
- **Mountain towns:** check seasonal workers' housing dependence in Aspen and Telluride against older miners' self-provisioning.
- **Nuche:** check Ute Mountain Ute farm enterprise against Southern Ute energy wealth as two routes to tribal self-sufficiency, told by each Tribe.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check *matanza*, shared ditches and commons as provisioning that is collective rather than individual.
- **Mexican and Central American Denver:** check multi-earner households and remittances.
- **Black Coloradans:** check Beckwourth and Dearfield homesteaders as early self-made lives in records.
- **Japanese Americans:** check Nisei and Sansei oral histories for when leaving the family farm or household for college, service or a city job came up, and how it was decided.
- **Volga German families:** check when grown children left the family beet contract for wages or farms of their own, and how church and parents answered it.
- **Latter-day Saint towns; Colorado Springs evangelicals and military families:** check Church welfare against base and church support networks.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check how household help, kin abroad and public programs are weighed.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for homesteads, transfers, water projects and disability history; mixed for household self-provisioning; thin for remittance practices, which are mostly informal.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for *matanza* and village provisioning; Somali and Amharic for remittance and kin support; Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese for household arrangements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal enterprise is each Tribe's to describe. Homestead records sit on ceded and taken Nuche, Tsétsėhéstȧhese and Hinóno'éí lands; read them with that history. Water and secession law go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** global studies of remittances were considered and left out as not Colorado-specific; nothing set aside under the standing rules.
