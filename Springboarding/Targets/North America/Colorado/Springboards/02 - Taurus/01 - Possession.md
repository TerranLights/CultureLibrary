## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche of Ignacio and Towaoc:** check what each tribe chooses to share about belongings and the dead before any outside source; customs may differ between the two.
- **Hispano families of Costilla, Conejos and the Arkansas Valley:** compare household *santos* and estate divisions across valley and river towns.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse histories:** check the Sand Creek testimony for property taken or destroyed, as told by survivors and by officers.
- **Japanese Americans:** separate Amache camp goods from the Denver Issei community that was never sent to camp.
- **Volga German families of Weld and Larimer counties:** heirlooms from Russia against goods bought in America.
- **Black Coloradans:** Dearfield homestead inventories and Five Points household records.
- **Front Range suburbs (Louisville, Superior, Colorado Springs):** fire-loss accounts from commuter households.
- **Mountain towns (Leadville, Silverton, Aspen):** compare goods left in abandoned camps with what second-home owners keep.
- **Somali and East African households in Aurora and Greeley:** what was carried through migration; sources likely in community groups only.
- **Vietnamese families along Federal Boulevard and Korean families on Havana Street:** heirlooms from before migration.
- **Latter-day Saint households of the San Luis Valley:** check family accounts and ward histories for how household stores are spoken of.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fire and flood losses and for museum collections; mixed for Hispano household inventories; thin for East African families.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for *hijuelas* and inventories; Japanese for the wartime press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nuche customs for a dead person's things and any ceremonial items are for the tribes to share, not to infer; repatriation files involve ancestors' remains; Amache and Sand Creek losses are contested accounts. Inheritance law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist institutions and teachings in the state's lands (category only; an open question for the author).
