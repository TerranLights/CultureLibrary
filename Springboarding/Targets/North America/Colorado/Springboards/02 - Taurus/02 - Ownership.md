## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** compare each tribe's own account of holding land and enterprise in common.
- **Northern Ute history in Colorado:** check the White River and Uncompahgre bands' removal as their descendants tell it, apart from the two Colorado reservations.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check the Fort Wise Treaty era and Sand Creek for competing accounts of title to the Plains.
- **Hispano grant heirs (Costilla, Conejos):** compare commons use rights with private deeds.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check housing records and movement-era land rhetoric separately.
- **Black homesteaders and homeowners:** Dearfield patents against covenant-bound Denver blocks.
- **Japanese American farmers (Arkansas Valley, Brighton, Fort Lupton):** check land held before and after 1944.
- **Volga German beet families:** track tenancy to ownership in Weld and Larimer farm records.
- **Ranching families of the Eastern Plains, Moffat County and the Parks; mining-claim holders of the San Juans:** compare deeded land with federal grazing permits and unpatented claims.
- **Ski-town workers and second-home owners:** deed-restricted against free-market housing.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Korean and Vietnamese business owners:** check strip-mall ownership and leasing along Havana Street and Federal Boulevard.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land patents, water and ranch land; mixed for grant commons (records in two states and two languages); thin for East African ownership.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for grant papers; Japanese for Issei land records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Ute cessions, Sand Creek, the grant disputes, water under prior appropriation and the 1944 measure are contested; quote each side as an account. Property, water and treaty law belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no pointer set aside; tribal land-tenure detail was left for the tribes' own publications rather than held.
