## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispano villages of the San Luis Valley and the upper Arkansas:** compare Hermandad devotional language with family sayings on *vergüenza*.
- **Chicanos of Denver's Northside and Pueblo's Bessemer:** separate movement-era pride from steel-town work dignity.
- **Nuche, Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check each nation's own account of boarding-school harm and renewal; do not merge them.
- **Coal and hard-rock mining families (Trinidad, Walsenburg, Leadville):** union and company records give rival accounts of worth at work.
- **Japanese Americans:** compare Nisei restraint in published writing with later redress-era voices.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points:** church, lodge and press records of honor and achievement.
- **Evangelical and military households of Colorado Springs:** check ministry teaching and service culture as separate sources.
- **Ranching and farm families of the Eastern Plains and North Park:** check stress-line and extension records for worth tied to land.
- **Ski-town service workers (Aspen, Vail, Telluride):** wage and housing coverage on being made to feel lesser.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora; Korean and Vietnamese Denver:** look for *kibir*, *sharaf*, *chemyeon* and *thể diện* in community media; sources may be thin.
- **Volga German families:** check memoirs of beet-field labor for how child workers' worth was spoken of.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor, movement and health-survey material; mixed for Native nations (much is theirs to share); thin for East African communities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for *alabados* and movement press; Japanese for Issei writing; each nation's language through its own program.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hermandad rites are private; boarding-school accounts and Sand Creek belong to survivors' descendants; suicide data needs careful handling; Ludlow and the boarding schools are contested ground.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist teaching and institutions in Colorado, as a category (the author's open question).
