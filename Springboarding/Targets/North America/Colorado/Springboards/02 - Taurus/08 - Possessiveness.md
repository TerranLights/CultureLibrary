## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range newcomers and long-settled residents:** compare letters pages and campaign rhetoric for who claims the state, and how each side frames it.
- **West Slope and Front Range water users:** set Glenwood Springs and Grand Junction press beside Denver Water and Aurora statements.
- **Ranchers and hunters or anglers:** check access fights from both sides.
- **Ski-town locals and visitors (Crested Butte, Aspen, Telluride):** check local-pass and parking debates.
- **Hispano families of the San Luis Valley:** compare wedding-song texts across decades for changes in how claims are voiced.
- **Mexican and Central American families in Denver:** check service-agency materials in Spanish; avoid outside generalization.
- **Nuche (Ignacio, Towaoc):** check the tribes' own rules on outsiders and recording.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse descendants:** check consultation records on who speaks for the Sand Creek site.
- **Military households of Colorado Springs:** deployment-related counseling records.
- **Japanese American, Korean and Vietnamese families:** check community counseling resources for how jealousy and control are named.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check community-based family services; sources likely thin.
- **Volga German and Latter-day Saint families:** check memoirs and ward or church histories for courtship rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for water and land-access quarrels and for violence statistics; mixed for courtship customs; thin for East African communities.
