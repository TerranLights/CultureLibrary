## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche:** check hot-spring, plant and food knowledge only as the Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute choose to share it.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check what the nations publish on foods and on the Plains landscape; ceremonial practice stays theirs.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley and southern Colorado:** Lenten foods, *bailes* and church sound against weekday plainness.
- **Pueblo's Chicano and Italian families:** compare the slopper, chile-roasting and steel-town tavern life.
- **Mexican and Central American Denver:** check *antojitos*, bakeries and dance halls along Federal Boulevard and in Westwood.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points:** jazz clubs, churches and cooking.
- **Japanese Americans:** festival foods and gardens at Sakura Square.
- **Volga German families of Weld and Larimer:** check wedding dances and *bierocks*-type foods in family records.
- **Latter-day Saint, temperance and Adventist communities:** what is abstained from, as each community explains it.
- **Mountain towns (Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat Springs):** snow, sun and après-ski as marketed and as lived by workers.
- **Western Slope (Palisade, Paonia, the North Fork):** orchard and farm-to-table pleasure.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora and Denver:** coffee, tea, barbecue and soup houses as each community describes them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for beer, food media and tourism; mixed for Hispano foodways (in cookbooks and memory); thin for Somali and Eritrean food life outside restaurants.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for parish cookbooks; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for community food talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Nuche, Arapaho and Cheyenne sweat lodges, plant medicine and ceremonial springs are not for outside description; Ute-derived place names need checking with the tribes; sex itself belongs to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** Boulder's Buddhist-inspired contemplative institutions, as a category only, until the author rules.
