## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range cities:** check speech and neighbor survey results city by city, since in-migration varies between them.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check atlas forms against younger speakers to see which village speech ways continue.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; Northern Ute history):** check each tribe's own language reports, since the reservations run separate programs.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check how the descendant nations outside the state tell their Colorado past.
- **Eastern Plains and Arkansas Valley towns:** check visiting columns against the farm calendar.
- **Western Slope:** check where news and schooling came from at a distance from Denver.
- **Mining and ski towns:** check how mixed crews, then seasonal workers, handled talk across languages.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese residents:** check heritage-school records for which language each generation uses where.
- **Five Points and Dearfield; Volga German and Latter-day Saint towns; Colorado Springs military families:** check church, colony and base newsletters as settings of talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English, Hispano Spanish and Arapaho linguistics; mixed for Ute (much held by the tribes); thin for Somali, Eritrean and Central American communities, where community groups are the start.
- **Lands:** Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the state as it stands, with the Mountain West around it as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** for the domain overview, southern Colorado Spanish reading and contact with Ute and Arapaho speakers through their nations; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for Aurora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Ute and Arapaho language knowledge is the nations' to share or withhold; Amache records touch living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in Colorado's lands.
