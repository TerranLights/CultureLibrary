## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ute Mountain Ute and Southern Ute:** check which sites and stories each tribe opens to visitors and which it keeps closed.
- **Pueblo nations with heritage at Mesa Verde (comparison only):** check their statements on excavation and return, as the frame for any Colorado collecting history.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos and the *Hermandad*:** check how outsiders' photographs and reports were received in the villages.
- **Front Range science workers (Boulder, Golden, Colorado Springs):** check outreach programs for who is invited to ask.
- **Mountain towns (Leadville, Silverton, Telluride):** check mine tours and museums for how mining curiosity is presented.
- **Eastern Plains and Western Slope ranch families:** check weather-observer and fossil-find records, since many discoveries came from ranches.
- **Volga German and Latter-day Saint families:** check genealogy-society records for what each searches for.
- **Black Coloradans:** check the Black American West Museum's programs for who brings questions about family history.
- **Aurora's newer communities:** check library and museum programs offered in Amharic, Somali, Korean or Vietnamese.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse descendants:** check their responses to museum holdings taken from the Plains.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science, exploration and museum history; mixed for everyday curiosity about people; thin for curiosity norms among East African and Central American families.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish for Nordenskiöld's report; Spanish for Hermandad sources; German for Volga German genealogy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** collecting at Mesa Verde and in Ute country, and the Holy Week rites, are contested; the nations and the brothers speak first; repatriation law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; consumer DNA-test data on ancestry searches was weighed and left out, since it gives no Colorado breakdown and belongs to the later research.
