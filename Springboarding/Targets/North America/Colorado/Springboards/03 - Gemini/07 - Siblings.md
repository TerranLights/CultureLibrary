*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check family-history projects for whether cousins are reckoned as brothers and sisters, in each tribe's own words.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse descendants:** check the nations' kinship teaching against Eggan's chart.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check *Hermandad* rolls for brothers by birth who joined together.
- **Denver and Pueblo Chicanos:** check movement records for *carnal* as a word of solidarity.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American families:** check school and clinic records for older siblings acting as interpreters and caregivers.
- **Volga German beet families:** check church registers for family size and sibling chain migration from one village.
- **Latter-day Saint towns of the San Luis Valley:** check family histories for very large sibling sets and how land was shared.
- **Eastern Plains and South Park ranch families:** check succession records for which sibling stays on the land.
- **Amache generation:** check family histories for brothers raised in Japan and brothers raised in Colorado (Kibei and Nisei).
- **Aurora's Korean, Vietnamese, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali families:** check heritage-school materials for how address terms for older and younger siblings are taught.
- **Mining towns:** check boarding-house and lodge rolls for brothers and cousins from the same home district.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for twin and adoption studies and birth data; mixed for kinship, where older ethnographies need the nations' correction; thin for East African sibling customs in Colorado.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Hermandad documents; German for Volga German family papers; Korean, Vietnamese, Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for address-term teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** older ethnographies of the Ute, Arapaho and Cheyenne are outsiders' records; foster-care records involve minors; inheritance belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; birth-order personality books were considered and left out as popular claims, not sources for this state.
