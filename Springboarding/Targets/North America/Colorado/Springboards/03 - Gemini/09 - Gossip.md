## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check tribal newspapers' letters pages for how disputes about named people are handled in print.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse histories:** check how rumors of attacks in 1864 are recorded in the press against the nations' accounts.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos and the *Hermandad*:** check which village matters reached the Spanish-language press and which stayed in talk.
- **Denver and Pueblo Chicanos:** check movement newspapers for rumor answered in print.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American residents:** check how rumors of immigration raids spread and were checked by community groups.
- **Chinese Denver of the 1870s-80s:** check what little the community left in its own hand, and where.
- **Amache generation:** check camp and town papers for rumor in both directions.
- **Eastern Plains and Western Slope towns:** check oral histories for gossip's role in a small place.
- **Ski towns:** check how seasonal and year-round residents appear in local gossip columns.
- **Colorado Springs military and evangelical communities:** check spouse forums and congregational teaching on gossip.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese residents:** check community-radio call-ins and group chats for how reputation news travels.
- **Latter-day Saint towns:** check ward histories for teaching on speaking of others.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rumor in crises (1880, the Klan years, Columbine); mixed for everyday gossip, which needs oral history; thin for the Chinese community's own record and for East African gossip norms.
- **Languages needed:** Chinese for any 1880 community records; Spanish for village sources; Amharic and Somali for diaspora channels; Japanese for camp records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1996 case and Columbine involve living families; the 1880 riot and the Klan years are contested; defamation law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; celebrity-gossip television ratings were considered and set aside as national, not Colorado's own talk.
