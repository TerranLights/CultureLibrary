# Colorado: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; the Spanish of the San Luis Valley and southern Colorado; Mexican and Central American Spanish; Núuchi-u (Ute); Hinóno'éitíít (Arapaho); Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse (Cheyenne); Volga German; Japanese; Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali, Korean, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** what home is, and who is family: household forms, roots, building ways, family ways, child-rearing ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley and southern Colorado:** check 1860-1880 census lists for the Conejos and Costilla plazas against family histories: who was counted under one roof.
- **Mexican Americans and Chicanos (Denver, Pueblo); newer Mexican and Central American families:** check parish registers by arrival period so old and new households are not merged.
- **Nuche [Ute] (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; Northern Ute history):** check which band and family terms the tribes' own publications use.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check trading-post camp records against the nations' own histories, kept largely outside Colorado.
- **Volga German beet families (Greeley, Windsor, Sterling):** check church books for household makeup by colony of origin.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield) and Japanese Americans (Amache):** check homestead files and relocation-agency family numbers against oral histories.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese families:** check whether any household table separates them before planning interviews.
- **Ranching, mountain-town, military and Latter-day Saint households (North Park, Telluride, Colorado Springs, Manassa):** check seasonal worker housing, base turnover and ward histories apart from civilian counts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census tables and the Front Range; mixed for Hispano and Nuche family life, much of it held by families and tribes; thin in print for Eritrean and Somali households.
- **Lands:** Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the state as it stands, with Wyoming, Utah, northern New Mexico and the Four Corners as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Valley Spanish for plaza-era records; German in old script for Volga German church books; Japanese for Issei letters; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for newcomer family media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek, the Ute removal and Amache are contested; descendants' family memory comes first. Ute ceremonial knowledge stays with the tribes.
- **Held for review:** Buddhism (category).

## 5. First queries
1. data.census.gov: "household type" and "multigenerational" for every Colorado county; flag Costilla, Conejos, Denver, Adams, Weld, Pitkin.
2. History Colorado catalog: "pioneer interviews" plus Costilla, Weld, Mesa or Las Animas.
3. Densho: "Amache" plus "family" and "barrack apartment"; then the *Granada Pioneer* run.
4. Library: Deutsch and López Tushar, read for household lists and the home place.
5. Ask the Southern Ute Cultural Center and Museum which tribal publications on family and band life are open to outside readers.
