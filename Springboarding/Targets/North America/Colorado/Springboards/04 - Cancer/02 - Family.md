## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check Conejos and San Luis baptism entries for whether *padrinos* came from inside or outside the kin group, 1860-1920.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check how Crusade for Justice writing stretched *familia* beyond the household, against parish family records of the same years.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American families:** check consulate and church records for families split between Colorado and a home country.
- **Nuche [Ute], Hinóno'éí [Arapaho], Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check each nation's own kin terms, and how families divided by removal trace one another.
- **Volga German families:** check the colony of origin recorded in marriage books, to see whether marriages stayed within it.
- **Black Coloradans:** check post-emancipation family-search notices and Dearfield's homestead clusters for kin settling together.
- **Japanese Americans:** check newspaper family notices before, during and after Amache.
- **Latter-day Saint families of Manassa and Sanford:** check ward histories for the Southern-born convert families who settled together.
- **Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox families:** check how parishes record godparents at baptism.
- **Korean and Vietnamese families:** check whether lineage books (*jokbo*, *gia phả*) were carried to Aurora and Denver and who keeps them.
- **Ranching families (Western Slope, North Park):** check how many generations work one ranch together today.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hispano lines (sacramental records and active genealogists) and Volga German lines; mixed for Black and Japanese family papers; thin in print for Somali and Eritrean kin networks.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish and church Latin for Conejos registers; Arapaho and Cheyenne for kin terms; Japanese for the Denver papers; Korean and Vietnamese for lineage books.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal membership and descent rules belong to the tribes; Somali lineage talk can be sensitive; ritual kinship within Hispano religious brotherhoods may be private. Family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; outsider ethnographers' kinship charts of the Plains and Great Basin peoples were considered and left for a later, critical pass behind the nations' own dictionaries.

## 5. First queries
1. FamilySearch catalog: "Conejos, Colorado" church records; sample baptisms each decade for *padrino* names.
2. Library: Cowell and Moss, *The Arapaho Language*, kin-term section; the Chief Dull Knife College dictionary for "mother", "father", "cousin".
3. *Southern Ute Drum* archive: obituaries, ten years' worth, listing survivors.
4. AHSGR: Colorado chapter holdings and Volga village surname files.
5. Denver Public Library: *Rocky Shimpo* and *Colorado Times* family notices, 1940-1950.
