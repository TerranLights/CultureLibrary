*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the Valley and southern Colorado:** check which self-label (*Hispano*, Spanish American, Mexican, Chicano) appears in Valley and Trinidad papers by decade.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check *El Gallo* against Pueblo's movement press for whether Aztlán was claimed the same way.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American arrivals:** check for hometown associations (*clubes de oriundos*) in Denver, Greeley and Grand Junction.
- **Nuche [Ute]:** check which gatherings the two Colorado tribes open to outsiders, and how invitations are worded.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check descendants' gatherings in Colorado (the Healing Run; Denver powwows) for how return is spoken of.
- **Volga German families:** check when Lutheran and Catholic congregations ended German-language services.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield):** check the "Harlem of the West" label in the Black press against mainstream papers.
- **Japanese and Chinese Coloradans:** check 1942 town resolutions barring Japanese Americans, and Denver's Chinese numbers after 1880.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check resettlement and church records for arrival cohorts.
- **Military families and evangelical congregations of Colorado Springs:** check spouse clubs and church membership rolls for how newcomers are drawn in.
- **Latter-day Saints of Manassa and Sanford:** check local histories for relations with Catholic Hispano neighbors.
- **Ranching families and mountain-town locals:** check county records and papers for disputes with "ranchette" and second-home arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in newspapers and on exclusion's history; mixed for felt belonging, which surveys touch only for youth; thin for Eritrean and Somali self-description in English print.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley papers and movement press; Japanese for 1942 community papers; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for diaspora media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal membership is the tribes' business; the Klan years, the 1880 riot and the Sand Creek history are contested; present-day migration debates are heated. Citizenship and status law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** on the facet of belonging among newcomer communities: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).
