# Colorado: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the Valley and southern Colorado:** check whether Las Posadas still runs house to house in San Luis and Antonito, and who opens the door.
- **Mexican and Central American families:** check doubling-up rates by tract against church-run shelter use.
- **Recent Venezuelan and other arrivals:** check city shelter records for length of stay and where families went next.
- **Nuche [Ute]:** check which brush-shelter sites the Ute Mountain Ute and Southern Ute tribes have commented on, and on what terms.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check intake records of Denver's urban Native service centers for descendants of Colorado's Plains peoples seeking shelter.
- **Jewish Coloradans (West Colfax):** check sanatorium admission registers for where patients came from.
- **Japanese Americans:** check how Amache families describe the first night in an unfinished barrack.
- **Vietnamese Coloradans:** check church sponsorship records of 1975-80 refugee families.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali families:** check how often new arrivals stayed with kin before their own lease.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points):** check boarding houses and the "colored" lodging of the segregation years in the Black press.
- **Mountain-town workers (Aspen, Vail, Telluride):** check reports of workers sleeping in cars and campgrounds.
- **Eastern Plains and Dust Bowl families (Baca, Prowers):** check dugout and soddy records and storm-shelter memories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Denver homelessness and sanatorium history; mixed for Ute shelter sites (archaeology outweighs tribal voice in print); thin for doubling-up among newcomer families.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Posadas verses and newcomer shelter records; Japanese for camp writing; Somali and Amharic for kin-hosting accounts; Vietnamese for refugee memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ute sites and their locations; Amache; camp sweeps and sanctuary cases involve living people. Camping bans and asylum go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; national homelessness scholarship was set aside in favor of Colorado's own counts and the words of people without housing.
