*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check oral histories for which kin and neighbors sat up with the dying, and which healers are named.
- **Mexican and Central American workers:** check ACS occupation by birthplace for home-care and domestic work in Colorado.
- **Nuche [Ute]:** check the Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute elder programs for how care of elders is described.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check how the nations' health programs reach members living in Denver.
- **Japanese Americans:** check Sakura Square's housing records for the age and origin of residents.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points):** check lodge and church sick-committee records.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali families:** check what *iddir* dues cover, and whether Somali-run home-care agencies exist.
- **Korean and Vietnamese elders:** check adult day programs offered in their languages.
- **Volga German parishes:** check women's societies' sick-visiting rosters.
- **Mining and steel towns (Telluride, Leadville, Pueblo):** check union and company hospital records.
- **Ranching families:** check county nursing-home use against care at home on the place.
- **Disabled Coloradans and veterans:** check ADAPT oral histories and veterans' caregiver programs for who chooses the carer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions and Front Range surveys; mixed for Hispano healing (oral, in Spanish); thin for East African and Asian mutual aid in print.
- **Languages needed:** Valley Spanish for healers' vocabulary; Amharic and Tigrinya for *iddir* records; Japanese for Issei care; Korean and Vietnamese for senior programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native healing knowledge stays with the nations; medical and care-home records are closed; *curanderismo* may be private family knowledge.
- **Held for review:** none; the Pueblo state hospital's patient files were considered and set aside as closed records, so only its published histories are listed.
