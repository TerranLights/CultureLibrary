# Colorado: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; Valley Spanish; Mexican and Central American Spanish; German; Japanese; Amharic, Somali, Korean, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the Valley and Huerfano County:** check local stories behind the name El Huérfano, and Valley tellings of La Llorona, for who is left and why.
- **Mexican and Central American families:** check how families split by deportation arrange care of children who stay in Colorado.
- **Nuche [Ute]:** check which word the tribes use for 1881 (removal, not abandonment) and what they say of the promises broken.
- **Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne] and Hinóno'éí [Arapaho]:** check descendants' family accounts of children orphaned at Sand Creek and who raised them.
- **Volga German families:** check Colorado papers for appeals to help kin left on the Volga, 1921-22.
- **Black Coloradans:** check Dearfield families' accounts of the town's emptying in the 1930s.
- **Japanese Americans:** check what families who came to Amache say of farms and homes left on the coast.
- **Korean, Vietnamese and Ethiopian adoptees:** check adoptees' own writing and camp materials for how relinquishment is described.
- **Mining-town families (Leadville, Silverton, Cripple Creek):** check papers after the 1893 silver crash for notices of deserted wives and children.
- **Eastern Plains homesteaders:** check land-office relinquishments in the 1890s and 1930s.
- **Military families of Colorado Springs:** check how spouses describe long deployments.
- **Elders living alone (mountain towns, the Plains):** check county data on elders without kin nearby.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ghost towns and child welfare; mixed for adoptees, whose accounts are scattered; thin for deportation's effects, which families keep private.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Valley tales and migrant families; German for Volga letters; Korean and Vietnamese for adoptees' searches; Amharic for Ethiopian adoptee materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare and adoption files are closed; the Ute removal and Sand Creek are contested and are removal and massacre, not abandonment, in the nations' words; living adoptees and deported parents.
- **Held for review:** none; adoptee reunion-registry data were considered but are confidential by design, so only published and camp materials are listed.
