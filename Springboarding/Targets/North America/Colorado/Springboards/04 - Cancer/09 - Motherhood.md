*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check birth registers and oral histories for the *parteras* who attended births, 1900-1950.
- **Mexican Americans and Chicanos (Denver, Pueblo):** check how Guadalupe processions and May 10 serenades are organized, and by whom.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American mothers:** check births by mother's birthplace and Medicaid payment, by county.
- **Nuche [Ute]:** check how the two tribes present Chipeta and other Ute mothers in their own publications.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsétsėhéstȧhese [Cheyenne]:** check the nations' accounts of the women of Bent's Fort, such as Owl Woman, against traders' versions.
- **Volga German families:** check births per mother in parish books, 1900-1930.
- **Black Coloradans:** check surviving records of Justina Ford's practice for the mothers she served.
- **Japanese Americans:** check births at the Amache hospital and mothers' accounts of them.
- **Latter-day Saint mothers (Manassa, Sanford):** check Relief Society minutes for mothers' work.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali mothers:** check hospital and doula programs for postpartum-rest customs they accommodate.
- **Korean and Vietnamese mothers:** check church women's associations' records in Aurora and Denver.
- **Ranching and mountain-town mothers:** check CattleWomen histories, and where rural hospitals stopped delivering babies.
- **Evangelical and military mothers of Colorado Springs:** check MOPS group listings and Blue Star chapter rolls.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for birth data and mothers' organizations; mixed for Hispano *parteras* (oral, Spanish); thin for East African and Asian mothers' own accounts in print.
- **Languages needed:** Valley Spanish for midwifery and devotion; Arapaho and Ute terms through the nations' sources; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for newcomer mothers; Korean and Vietnamese for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maternal deaths and birth records are confidential; sterilization history touches Native and Mexican American mothers; women's ceremonies belong to the nations.
- **Held for review:** none; national motherhood surveys were passed over for Colorado's own maternal reviews and mothers' groups.

1. Colorado Women's Hall of Fame: Justina Ford entry; then the Blair-Caldwell library's holdings on her practice.

5. CDC WONDER: Colorado births by county and mother's birthplace, five years.
