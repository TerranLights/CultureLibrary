## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mountain resort towns (Aspen, Telluride):** check how dress and humor were used in local-control campaigns.
- **Colorado Springs evangelical households:** check church modesty guides and youth-group testimony nights.
- **Military families (Fort Carson, the Air Force Academy):** check grooming and uniform rules as limits on off-duty display, read as texts, not as law.
- **Chicano Denver and Pueblo:** check movement papers for which self-names each group used and when.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check *trovos* and *décimas* for criticism said through song.
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check tribe-curated exhibits against state-curated ones.
- **Amache survivors:** check camp literary pages for what was said and what was left out.
- **Five Points:** check dance programs and church choir anniversaries.
- **LGBTQ Coloradans in Denver and Colorado Springs:** compare Pride programs in the two cities.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check singing rooms and church talent nights as places for public song.
- **Eastern Plains youth:** check 4-H and FFA speech contests as sanctioned outlets.
- **Somali Aurora:** check wedding poetry, where sources exist; likely oral only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the counterculture, Chicano and LGBTQ press; thin for Somali and Eritrean expression, where community interviews are the route.
- **Languages needed:** *manito* Spanish to read *trovos* and their wordplay; Japanese for the camp press; Somali for wedding verse.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Hermandad* rites and Ute songs are not open for recording; camp writing was censored, so silence in it is evidence too.
- **Held for review:** none; Boulder's countercultural scene was reached through general press archives, so no single institution came up for screening.
