*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse nations (Oklahoma, Wyoming, Montana):** check their testimony in the Blue Sky renaming and the Sand Creek site record.
- **Nuche bands:** check which leaders each treaty named, against how band histories name them.
- **Hispano land-grant families:** check heirs' associations and Valley obituaries for how standing is acknowledged.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points and Dearfield's descendants:** check who prepared historic-site nominations and whose names appear.
- **Chinese Coloradans:** check the drafting of the 2022 resolution and plaque wording.
- **Japanese Americans:** check redress claims and the Carr memorial's dedication program.
- **Military families (Colorado Springs, Aurora):** check citations and memorial name lists.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali professionals:** check programs that recognize foreign credentials.
- **Ranching and Volga German beet families:** check Centennial Farm lists for which families appear.
- **Ski towns:** check how 10th Mountain Division veterans are honored at the Colorado Ski and Snowboard Hall of Fame.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for renaming, memorials and halls of fame; mixed for treaty-era recognition, which needs tribal accounts beside federal ones; thin for East African communities' own honors.
- **Languages needed:** Hinóno'éitíít and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse to read tribal testimony on names; Chinese for nineteenth-century community sources; Amharic and Somali for community award nights.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek, Ute removal, Amache and the 1880 riot are contested; present each acknowledgment as one account. Treaty law goes to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; land acknowledgments were pointed to as a practice through the naming boards, so no campus statement or its authors came up for screening.
