*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche bands (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute):** check how band leaders were chosen before and after tribal councils were set up (governance law goes to Capricorn).
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check tribal histories for the peace chiefs' and military societies' separate paths to leading.
- **Hinóno'éí:** check age-graded lodges as the route to leadership.
- **Valley Hispanos:** check *mayordomo* election records and *Hermano Mayor* terms.
- **Southern Colorado politicians of Barela's era:** check how patronage and kin networks carried leaders.
- **Coalfield immigrants:** check strike committees for leaders by language group.
- **Denver's Black and Latino civic leaders:** compare how Webb and Peña built followings.
- **Colorado Springs military and evangelical communities:** compare leadership curricula with pastor-selection practice.
- **Ranching families:** check stockmen's officer lists across generations.
- **Amache:** check block managers and camp councils, and who was trusted.
- **East African and Korean associations:** check how elders and boards are chosen, and contested elections.
- **Women in state politics:** check the first legislators' backgrounds and networks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political, military and Ute and Cheyenne leadership histories (mostly outsiders' writing); mixed for acequia and church leaders; thin for East African and Vietnamese community leadership.
- **Languages needed:** territorial-era Spanish for Barela's biography; Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éitíít for leadership titles; Somali and Amharic for elders' roles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Hermandad* rules are private; Ute and Cheyenne leadership histories are contested between bands and outsiders; church scandals involve living people.
- **Held for review:** none; the outdoor and military schools were named as institutions only, so no individual instructors or writers needed checking under the standing rules.
