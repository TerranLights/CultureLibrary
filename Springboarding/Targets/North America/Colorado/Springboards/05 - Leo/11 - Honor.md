# Colorado: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** American English (military, ranch and boxing registers); the Valley's *manito* Spanish and Mexican Spanish; Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse; Hinóno'éitíít; Núuchi-u; Volga German; Japanese; Korean; Vietnamese; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse descendants:** check how the Summit Springs ledger's drawings are read in tribal accounts.
- **Hinóno'éí:** check honor songs for veterans at gatherings tied to Colorado land.
- **Nuche:** check which honors are given at fairs and Bear Dance seasons, as the tribes share them.
- **Valley Hispanos:** check *entriega* verses for duties set on each family.
- **Newer Mexican communities:** check *charro* associations around Denver and their codes of conduct.
- **Ranching families:** check sale-barn customs and how broken deals are handled.
- **Military families of Colorado Springs:** check honor-code proceedings and funeral customs.
- **Nisei and Amache families:** check how service is told by those who served and by those who resisted the draft.
- **Black Coloradans:** check lodge records and memory of the Buffalo Soldiers at Fort Garland.
- **Somali and Ethiopian families:** check how elders settle disputes over a family's name.
- **Korean Aurora:** check church and business accounts of keeping face.
- **Boxing gyms in Pueblo and Denver:** check what fighters say a fair fight requires.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, Cheyenne-ledger and veterans' sources; mixed for Hispano family honor (private, oral); thin for East African and Korean honor practices in Colorado.
- **Languages needed:** Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse to read coup terms behind the English; Valley Spanish for wedding verses; Somali and Amharic for elders' dispute settling.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honor violence must be researched in the words of those who lived it, never assumed for any group; ledger art and eagle feathers carry tribal protocols; Amache service and draft resistance are contested.
- **Held for review:** none; honor-violence research is pointed to through the published state classification, with no group singled out.
