## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range cities:** check commute and office-day patterns city by city; Pueblo's steel record against Boulder's lab work.
- **Eastern Plains, South Park and North Park ranch and farm families:** check how calving, haying and harvest set the day, from diaries and 4-H records.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check acequia minutes and parish calendars for where the work year and feast year meet.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; Northern Ute history):** check tribal seasonal-round accounts against agency reports, read as outsiders' claims.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse histories:** check Bent's Fort ledgers against tribal oral histories of the trade season.
- **Mexican, Chicano, Central American and Volga German beet families:** check when the family field crew ended in each, by generation.
- **Black Coloradans and Japanese Americans:** check city directories and farm records for which jobs were open, decade by decade, and the break of 1942.
- **Mining and ski towns; Western Slope growers:** check how seasonal and year-round residents divide work and rest.
- **Colorado Springs military and evangelical communities; Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese communities; Latter-day Saint towns (Manassa, Sanford):** check each separately for how duty, ministry, shop hours or ward work days shape the week.
- **Neighboring Mountain West:** comparison only; check where Colorado's work, service and health routines stop matching those of Wyoming, Utah and northern New Mexico.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Front Range, mining and beets; mixed for Hispano villages; thin and partly restricted for Native daily life as the nations tell it. Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the state as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for acequia minutes; Japanese for Issei camp writing; the Native languages through tribal programs only.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ludlow, Amache and Sand Creek are contested ground; Native seasonal and ceremonial knowledge is shared as each nation chooses.
- **Held for review:** one pointer held as a category only: Buddhist institutions.
