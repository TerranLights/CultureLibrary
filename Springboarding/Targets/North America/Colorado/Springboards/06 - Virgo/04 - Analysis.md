## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Boulder and Fort Collins research staff against mountain, mining and ranch towns:** check how local papers report expert advice on wildfire and water, and how readers answer.
- **San Luis Valley acequia irrigators against center-pivot growers:** check how each measures water (turns on the ditch, meters on wells).
- **Eastern Plains ranchers:** check CSU Extension range and soil analysis against inherited judgment in ranch accounts.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute):** check tribal environmental reports against federal ones on the same land.
- **Pueblo peoples' heritage at Mesa Verde and Chimney Rock:** comparison only; check how descendants' accounts and archaeologists' readings differ.
- **Japanese Americans:** check WRA community analysts' reports against Amache writers.
- **Volga German, Japanese and Mexican beet growers:** check grower-association minutes for disputes over the sugar test.
- **Chicano Denver and Pueblo:** check who authored studies of their neighborhoods, insiders or outsiders.
- **Korean, Vietnamese, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check whether state and city data break these groups out at all.
- **Hardrock mining towns:** check assay records against miners' accounts of "salting" and tested claims.
- **Four Corners neighbors:** comparison only, for snow-survey and stream-measurement practice on shared rivers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science, water and mining; mixed for how ordinary people use or doubt analysis; thin for Native and immigrant communities' own analytic traditions.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese for residents' camp-era writing; *manito* Spanish for acequia accounts; Amharic and Somali for any community surveys.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** WRA analysis of incarcerated people and data on Native missing persons need care; Pueblo knowledge at Mesa Verde and Chimney Rock is theirs to share.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage scholars in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Search the WRA records (National Archives; Densho's copies) for "Community Analysis" and "Granada".
2. Download the USGS Hayden atlas and note its Colorado sheets and survey methods.
3. Search CSU and History Colorado catalogs for "Great Western Sugar" with "tare" or "sugar content".
4. Pull the latest Colorado Blue Book and the State Auditor's recent reports list.
5. Read the Southern Ute environmental programs' public pages for monitoring reports.
