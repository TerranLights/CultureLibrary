## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; the Northern Ute's Colorado past):** check each tribe's own telling of the 1868 treaty and the 1881 removal against the text.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse (now in Wyoming, Montana, Oklahoma):** read their own accounts of Fort Wise before settler histories.
- **Hispanos of the Valley and southern Colorado:** compare acequia bylaws with brotherhood rules as two written neighbor bargains.
- **Mexican Americans, Chicanos and newer Mexican and Central American arrivals:** look in parish and county books for civil-plus-church weddings and named *compadres*.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield):** find Dearfield's land-sale terms and lodge minutes.
- **Japanese Americans of Amache:** check how camp records registered marriages.
- **Volga German beet families:** read grower contracts with the sugar company.
- **Latter-day Saint towns (Manassa, Sanford):** check ward records on marriages and quarrels.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** ask which associations hear family quarrels before a court does.
- **Evangelical and military Colorado Springs; ranch families; ski and mining towns:** compare marriage-preparation programs, base family services and ranch-partnership papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state law and the Front Range; mixed for the Valley; thin and partly restricted for Ute, Arapaho and Cheyenne legal orders. Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the state as it stands, with the neighboring Mountain West as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Valley Spanish for grant and brotherhood papers; the three Native languages only through each nation's programs; Japanese for Issei papers; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for association bylaws.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek, the Ute removal, the Taylor Ranch dispute, Amache and Ludlow are contested; present accounts. Brotherhood and tribal ceremonies may be closed.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the brotherhood rule books were weighed for restriction and kept, since Steele and Rivera's edition is published.
