## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí descendants:** check the tribes' own statements on the 2014 state apology and on the yearly Spiritual Healing Run, as justice in their words.
- **Nuche:** read the 1879-80 congressional hearings on the White River conflict for whose punishment was demanded and whose was not.
- **Hispanos of Costilla and Conejos counties:** check court files of trespass charges against grant heirs, as the law's view of a contested right.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** look at Crusade for Justice files for the cases they took up as injustices.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points:** read Denver's Office of the Independent Monitor reports for complaints filed and outcomes.
- **Ludlow families (Greek, Italian, Mexican, Slavic miners):** check whose testimony the 1915 hearings recorded and whose was left out.
- **Japanese Americans of Amache:** read redress-claim files for how harm was proved.
- **Somali, Ethiopian and Eritrean Aurora:** ask how elders' councils describe their role beside the courts.
- **German-speaking families:** check World War I loyalty prosecutions in Colorado.
- **Military Colorado Springs:** treat courts-martial at Fort Carson and the Academy as a second system of justice.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sand Creek, Ludlow and state statistics; thin for Somali and Ethiopian community justice; tribal court records mostly closed.
- **Languages needed:** Cheyenne and Arapaho only through tribal historians; Japanese for Issei redress letters; Somali and Amharic for elders' practice.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek, Ludlow and Amache are contested; present each as accounts. Victims' families and tribal oral history may restrict use.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; lynching records of the 1880s-1920s were weighed and kept as a source type for the newspapers collection.
