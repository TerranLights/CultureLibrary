# Colorado: Death and transformation, domain and folkways (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; southern Colorado (*manito*) and Mexican Spanish; Núuchi-u (Ute); Volga German; Amharic; Korean; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** death and transformation: how death, power and the hidden are faced, with the folkways of death, sex, shared resources and inheritance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley and southern Colorado:** compare the *velorio* and the *camposanto* with Front Range funeral homes, to see which death customs held after urban migration.
- **Nuche (Ute): Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check which death and renewal practices each tribe publishes and which it keeps to itself.
- **Mexican Americans and Chicanos (Denver, Pueblo) and newer Mexican and Central American arrivals:** separate the Chicano-era *Día de Muertos* revival from the observance brought by recent arrivals.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check whether the funeral associations brought from home now also carry savings and dispute settlement.
- **Japanese Americans of the Amache generation and after:** compare camp-era burials with later memorial pilgrimages.
- **Ranching families of the Plains, North Park and the Western Slope:** check how land is willed, split or kept whole.
- **Evangelical and military Colorado Springs:** compare military funerals at the national cemeteries with church memorials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Front Range in English; mixed for Hispano, Ute and Volga German sources in archives and oral memory; thin for Somali, Eritrean and Vietnamese Colorado.
- **Languages needed:** regional Spanish above all for the domain's death customs; Amharic and Tigrinya for the funeral associations; Ute only with the tribes' consent.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ute ceremonies and burial places are the tribes' to share or withhold; Sand Creek and Amache remains and graves are grief, not curiosity.
- **Lands:** this nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheets of this sign write for Colorado as the state stands, with neighbors as comparison only.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside here: the domain's pointers come from state data, Colorado archives and tribal media, none of which fell under the standing rules; questions on wills, water decrees and the land grants as law go to Libra and Capricorn.

4. *The Southern Ute Drum* archive: "Bear Dance" and "funeral services".
5. Search "Riverside Cemetery Denver burial records" for the register and any ethnic sections.
