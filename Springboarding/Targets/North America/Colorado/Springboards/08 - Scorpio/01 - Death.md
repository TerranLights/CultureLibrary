*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check whether *alabados* are still sung through the night at wakes, and who leads them now.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute):** check how each tribe speaks in public of burial, reburial and avoiding the dead, and what it declines to say.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí:** their nations now sit in Oklahoma, Montana and Wyoming; check how descendants' remembrance at Sand Creek is organized from outside Colorado.
- **Japanese Americans:** compare graves at Amache with later family burials in Denver and the beet country.
- **Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox families of Aurora:** check the timing of memorials after a death (days, forty days, a year) as each family describes it.
- **Somali Muslims:** check access to burial grounds that allow burial within a day.
- **Volga German families:** check whether German-language gravestones in Weld and Logan county cemeteries mark when the language faded.
- **Military Colorado Springs:** compare funerals at the national cemeteries with those in town.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in state data and newspapers; mixed for Hispano and mining-town sources; thin for Somali, Eritrean and Vietnamese mourning, which will need community interviews.
- **Languages needed:** regional Spanish for wake hymns and prayers; Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for mourning gatherings; Cheyenne and Arapaho terms only through the nations' published dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek remains, Ute burials and Amache graves are grief held by living families; suicide data needs care in how it is quoted.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist funeral and memorial practice in the nation's lands, as a category only (the author has not yet ruled on Buddhism).
