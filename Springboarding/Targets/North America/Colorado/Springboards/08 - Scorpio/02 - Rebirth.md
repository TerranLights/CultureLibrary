*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche (Ute):** compare what each tribe says in public about the Bear Dance with how it presents its language classes.
- **Hinóno'éí (Arapaho):** check who leads the revival work tied to Colorado institutions, and whether Colorado itself plays any part in it.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check how the Holy Week cycle, from penance to Easter, is kept today and by whom.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check how movement veterans tell their own turning points in oral histories.
- **Jewish Coloradans:** check how descendants of sanatorium patients describe the family's start in Denver.
- **Latter-day Saints of Conejos County:** check whether family histories present migration as part of conversion.
- **Evangelical Colorado Springs:** check how testimonies treat relapse and backsliding.
- **Korean congregations in Aurora:** check how adult baptism and conversion stories pass between generations.
- **Vietnamese, Ethiopian and Somali refugees:** check whether resettlement is told as survival, as a new life, or as neither.
- **Western Slope coal families:** check how miners themselves name the shift from coal.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health seekers, ski towns and evangelical media; mixed for recovery; thin for refugee and Korean accounts, which will need oral histories.
- **Languages needed:** Arapaho and Ute only through the tribes' own revival materials; Korean and Vietnamese for church and resettlement narratives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bear Dance and other ceremonies are the tribes' to describe; recovery stories involve health privacy.
- **Held for review:** for the reincarnation facet, one category: Buddhist communities and centers in the nation's lands.
