# Colorado: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispano Hermandad villages:** compare what brothers publish themselves with what outsiders wrote about them.
- **Japanese Americans:** check whether families who left Amache spoke of it to their children, and when that changed.
- **Mexican and Central American workers of Weld and Morgan counties:** check how hidden status shaped what workers told employers and neighbors after 2006.
- **Arvada and Westminster families near Rocky Flats:** check what workers' families say they knew at the time.
- **Garfield and Rio Blanco residents:** check local memory of the blasts against official notices.
- **Military Colorado Springs:** check how spouses describe living beside classified work.
- **LGBTQ Coloradans:** compare the closet in rural counties with Denver, and across generations.
- **Catholic, Jewish and Black Denver of the 1920s:** check how the Klan's targets learned who its members were.
- **Nuche (Ute):** check which ceremonies each tribe closes to recording and how it posts the rule.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check what newcomers say about privacy from fellow exiles, using their own media only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Cold War and the Klan; mixed for mining and lodges; thin for private family secrets, which surface only in memoirs and interviews.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for Hermandad and workplace sources; Japanese for camp-era letters; Amharic and Somali for diaspora media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ute ceremony content and Hermandad rites are closed and are not to be sought; Klan ledgers name real families; immigration status puts people at risk.
- **Held for review:** nothing held; the one candidate that bordered on restricted knowledge, a published outsider description of brotherhood rites, was left out in favor of the heritage files.
