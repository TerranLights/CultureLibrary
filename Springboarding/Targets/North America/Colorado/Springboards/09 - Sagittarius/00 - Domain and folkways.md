## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos beside Manassa and Sanford's Latter-day Saints:** compare parish and ward records for how each organized worship and mutual aid in one valley.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; the Northern Ute's Colorado past):** separate the Bear Dance, Sun Dance and Native American Church as each tribe frames them.
- **Hinóno'éí [Arapaho] and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse [Cheyenne]:** check how much of their Colorado record is removal history and what Denver's urban Native centers hold on present practice.
- **Colorado Springs evangelical and military families:** check ministry headquarters and base chaplaincies against the county congregation census.
- **Boulder and Denver unaffiliated:** test where "none" rises in county data, and whether mountain recreation is described in sacred terms by its own practitioners.
- **Five Points congregations and Dearfield:** church founding dates and the pastor's role in a homestead colony.
- **Volga German beet towns:** Lutheran, Reformed and Catholic parishes carried from the Volga colonies.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali and Korean congregations; Vietnamese Catholics:** check feast calendars and how each community's newspapers or radio report them.
- **Comparison only:** northern New Mexico's brotherhood (for what crossed the border), Utah's ward system, Diné practice in the Four Corners.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Front Range Christianity and Jewish Denver; mixed for the Hermandad, much of it private; thin for Somali, Eritrean and Arapaho life in the state, so start from parish bulletins and community radio.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish with *manito* forms, which differ from Mexican usage; Ute and Cheyenne for ceremony names; Amharic and Tigrinya for parish life.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ute and Cheyenne ceremonial knowledge is not public; Sand Creek and Ute removal are contested. Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the state as it stands.
- **Held for review:** two categories set aside from the religious landscape: Buddhist communities and institutions in Colorado's lands (an open question for the author); South Asian-heritage religious communities in Colorado's lands.
