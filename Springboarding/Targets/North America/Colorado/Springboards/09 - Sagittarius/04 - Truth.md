## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí descendants (in Oklahoma, Montana, Wyoming) on Colorado sites:** check which oral accounts they have chosen to publish on Sand Creek, and through which tribal offices.
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check how tribal newspapers name 1879 ("Meeker Massacre" against "White River War" and other names).
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check whether *dichos* on truth differ between older *manito* speakers and newer Mexican arrivals.
- **Coalfield families of Las Animas and Huerfano counties:** check memorial speeches at Ludlow for which account of 1914 they treat as settled.
- **Amache generation:** check how survivors' accounts of the questionnaire differ between "yes-yes" and "no-no" families.
- **Front Range scientists (Boulder, Golden):** check public lectures for how labs explain their own measures of certainty.
- **Western Slope and Eastern Plains readers in news deserts:** check where county residents now get local facts (radio, Facebook groups, the co-op).
- **Somali, Ethiopian and Eritrean Aurora:** check which diaspora outlets and messaging groups carry news, and how corrections spread.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check home-language papers and radio for trust in American media against home-country outlets.
- **Comparison only:** New Mexico's Hispano proverb collections, to separate Colorado-heard sayings from those recorded only south of the line.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Sand Creek, Ludlow and Columbine, each heavily documented and disputed; thin for diaspora news habits, which need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Cheyenne and Arapaho for any oral-history holdings, with tribal permission; Japanese for Issei testimony; Spanish for proverb work.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek, the boarding schools, Amache and Ludlow are contested ground: present every account as an account; descendants' oral histories are theirs to share; Columbine involves living families.
- **Held for review:** nothing held; the science-trust row was the one that could have led to named individual researchers at the Boulder and Fort Collins labs, and it stays at the level of the institutions.
