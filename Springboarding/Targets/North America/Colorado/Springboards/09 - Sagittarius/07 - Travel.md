# Colorado: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; *manito* and Mexican Spanish; Núuchi-u; Volga German; Japanese; Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali, Korean, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler (short journeys stay in Gemini).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Nuche:** check how the Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute speak of the old seasonal range now cut by state lines, and which trails they mark or keep quiet.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check family records for seasonal work trips north and the return each winter.
- **Latter-day Saint families of Manassa and Sanford:** check how many young members leave on missions and what returnees bring back.
- **Mountain resort towns (Aspen, Telluride, Silverton, Summit County):** check how locals and seasonal workers describe tourists and second-home owners.
- **Eastern Plains farm towns:** check school reunions and Facebook groups for out-migration and homecoming customs.
- **Fort Carson and Air Force families:** check how deployment and frequent moves are handled by base family programs.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers of Greeley, Fort Morgan and Denver:** check remittance shops and home-town associations for the return trip.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check community events for the journey stories told to children.
- **Vietnamese Denver:** check April commemorations for how the escape by boat is narrated.
- **Volga German beet families:** check how the Volga-to-Kansas-to-Colorado route is retold at heritage events.
- **Comparison only:** northern New Mexico's walking pilgrimages, to check which Colorado parishes send walkers south and how the trip is prepared.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trail diaries, tourism and mountaineering; mixed for Hispano labor migration; thin for Somali and Eritrean journey accounts, which are mostly oral.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for labor-migration oral history; Vietnamese, Amharic, Tigrinya and Somali for refugee testimony; Japanese for Issei letters about removal.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refugee and undocumented journeys carry risk to the people who tell them; Ute routes may include places the tribes keep private; forced journeys (removal, Amache) are contested ground. Border and immigration law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing held; travel's pilgrimage facet was the one likely to reach a held faith's destinations, and the pilgrimages kept here (the Amache return; Catholic walks south) are civic or Christian.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Deutsch, "No Separate Refuge"; map the villages and work sites named in its chapters.
2. Open web: Magoffin's diary, the Bent's Fort entries of summer 1846.
3. Colorado Refugee Services Program: annual arrival reports, by country of origin.
4. Census ACS: county-to-county flows into Denver, El Paso and Larimer counties, top ten origins.
5. Colorado Historic Newspapers: "health seekers" OR "consumptives" Colorado Springs, 1875-1910.
