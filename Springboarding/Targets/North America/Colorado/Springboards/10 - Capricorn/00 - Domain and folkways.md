# Colorado: Domain and folkways (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West American English; southern Colorado Spanish; Mexican and Central American Spanish; Núuchi-u, Hinóno'éitíít, Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse; Volga German; Japanese; Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali, Korean, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the domain of authority and duty: who commands, who obeys, and what is owed (with the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Front Range metro (Denver, Aurora, Boulder, Fort Collins, Colorado Springs, Pueblo):** check whether accepted sources of authority (office, expertise, congregation, union) differ city by city.
- **Eastern Plains, South Park and North Park ranching families:** check the owner-foreman-hand chain and brand inspection as a separate order of command.
- **San Luis Valley Hispanos:** check the village offices (*mayordomo*, *hermano mayor*) beside county government.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute, Northern Ute history):** check how councils, elders and reservation agents divided authority, in the tribes' histories.
- **Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse histories:** check which records of chiefs' councils before 1864 survive, mostly outside the state.
- **Mining and ski towns (Leadville, Aspen, Telluride, Silverton):** check how mine owners, then resort companies, shaped town authority.
- **Arkansas Valley and Western Slope (Grand Junction, the North Fork):** check attitudes to federal land agencies against water districts.
- **Colorado Springs evangelical and military communities:** check where chain of command and church leadership overlap in sermons and base papers.
- **Aurora's Ethiopian, Eritrean, Somali, Korean and Vietnamese communities:** check which associations carry obligations across the ocean.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield) and Japanese Americans (Amache):** check whose authority was recognized inside communities shut out of office.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Anglo Front Range and territorial history; mixed for Hispano villages (much oral or private); thin for Colorado Somali and Eritrean records.
- **Languages needed:** archaic southern Colorado Spanish to read the village offices of the domain; Ute, Arapaho and Cheyenne terms only through tribal language programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *morada* rites are private; the Ute Sun Dance is restricted; Sand Creek descendants' accounts belong to the tribes. Capricorn takes the spirit of law only; statute and treaty texts go to Libra. Colorado's exact post-war lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the state as it stands, with the neighboring Mountain West as comparison only.
- **Held for review:** for the domain overview, national-character essays and business-culture guides were weighed and left out as too general for one state; nothing was set aside under the standing rules.
