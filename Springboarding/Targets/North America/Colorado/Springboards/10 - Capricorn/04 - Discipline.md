## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check how Lenten penance is described by brothers who have spoken publicly, against outsiders' accounts.
- **Colorado Springs evangelical households:** check congregation parenting classes for how often firm-discipline teaching is followed or questioned.
- **Military families of Fort Carson and the Air Force Academy:** check base school and youth-program rules for whether drill carries into child-rearing.
- **Nuche, Hinóno'éí and Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse families:** check tribal accounts of boarding-school discipline at Hesperus and Grand Junction, survivors first.
- **Mexican American and Chicano families (Denver, Pueblo):** check school-discipline campaigns and oral histories of punishment for speaking Spanish.
- **Endurance communities (Boulder, Leadville):** check runners' and climbers' memoirs for self-imposed routine.
- **Volga German congregations (Greeley, Windsor):** check church minutes for members disciplined for drink or Sabbath work.
- **Korean Aurora:** check taekwondo schools' rank tests and church youth programs for what self-mastery is taught.
- **Vietnamese Aurora:** check how weekend language schools keep order among children.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali families:** check community-led parenting workshops on correcting children under U.S. law; Colorado-specific sources likely sparse.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions (army, academy, prisons, schools); thin for home discipline in smaller communities.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for penance and school records; Korean and Vietnamese for youth programs; Amharic and Somali for parenting workshops.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Holy Week penance is private, and the brothers decide what is shared; boarding-school testimony comes first from survivors; abuse in sport involves living people.
- **Held for review:** on the meditative facet of self-discipline: Buddhism (category only).
