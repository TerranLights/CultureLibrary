*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Coloradans of Dearfield and Five Points:** check how colony promoters and later Five Points business owners described their goals, in their own papers.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check whether leaving for Denver or Pueblo was framed as getting ahead or as loss, in family histories.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check movement school records for what Escuela Tlatelolco hoped its students would become.
- **Newer Mexican and Central American workers (meatpacking towns, Front Range construction):** check worker-center reports for plans to buy land or houses back home.
- **Southern Ute and Ute Mountain Ute:** check tribal papers on how enterprise income is tied to the people's goals.
- **Volga German beet families:** check how the move from field labor to owning farms is told in family books.
- **Japanese Issei and Nisei farmers (Brighton, Weld County):** check produce-association records for farm expansion before and after the war.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check community papers for small-business and education goals between generations.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check community business associations for founders' accounts along East Colfax.
- **Ski towns and Boulder:** check founders' memoirs against workers' accounts for whose ambitions the towns serve.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mining-era and tech ambition; mixed for immigrant communities; thin for East African Colorado specifically.
- **Languages needed:** English for the rush and boom records; Spanish for immigrant aims; Korean, Vietnamese and Amharic for business papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the gold rush's ambition and the Plains peoples' dispossession must be read together; *ambicioso* as insult is a usage to test, not assume.
- **Held for review:** on the facet of whether striving pays off across generations: South Asian-heritage scholarship (category only).
