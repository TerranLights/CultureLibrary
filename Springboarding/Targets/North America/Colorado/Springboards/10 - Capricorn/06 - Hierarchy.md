## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check land-grant and church records for the line between landed families and laborers, and how it eased or held.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute):** check tribal histories for how leaders, families and elders are ranked, as the tribes describe them.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí histories:** check whether surviving accounts describe the chiefs' council as rank or as service.
- **Ski towns (Aspen, Telluride, Vail):** check housing lotteries and council minutes for the sorting of owners, locals and seasonal workers.
- **Pueblo steelworkers and southern coal families:** check union records for seniority lists and the ethnic ladder of jobs.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield):** check club rosters and church records for ranks inside the community.
- **Chinese Denver:** check court and press records of the 1880 riot for the place given to Chinese residents.
- **Military Colorado Springs:** check whether officer and enlisted lines show in housing and church membership.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora:** check how honorific speech and seating are kept or relaxed in second-generation church and student groups.
- **Volga German beet towns:** check whether the colony ranks of farmer and laborer carried into Colorado parishes.
- **Ranch families of North Park and South Park:** check memoirs for the ranks of owner, cowboss, hand and seasonal crew.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for class and race in Denver and mining towns; mixed for tribal rank, where outsiders' ethnographies dominate the old record.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for brotherhood and land-grant records; Korean and Vietnamese for honorific forms; Amharic for formal address.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** captivity and the *genízaro* past are painful histories for several peoples; Klan history names families still in the state; brotherhood ranks are partly private.
- **Held for review:** for ranked forms of address in Aurora's communities, the scan checked each language row against the standing rules; nothing had to be set aside.
