## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ranch and farm families of the Eastern Plains and Arkansas Valley:** check fair records for which families' youth win, and how judging disputes are settled.
- **Hispanos of Pueblo and southern Colorado:** check memoirs of public employment for whether godparent ties are described as help or as favoritism.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers:** check adult-education and credential-recognition programs for how foreign diplomas are judged.
- **Nuche and Native students at Fort Lewis:** check student and tribal statements on the waiver as treaty-like obligation.
- **Military families of Colorado Springs:** check spouses' accounts of promotion boards and their effect on family moves.
- **Denver teachers and parents:** check union and district papers on seniority against measured results.
- **Engineering and tech workers (Golden, Boulder, the Denver Tech Center):** check employer-review sites for how promotion is described.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora families:** check school-board testimony for views on gifted programs and admissions tests.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali professionals:** check licensing-board cases for how training from abroad is weighed.
- **Black Denver (Five Points):** check civil-service lawsuits and union histories for barriers to promotion.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public institutions (courts, schools, civil service); thin for merit inside families and small communities.
- **Languages needed:** English for most records; Korean and Vietnamese for test-culture ads; Spanish for patronage talk; Amharic for licensing advocacy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** affirmative-action debates are contested; the tuition waiver is a live political argument; youth fair disputes may involve minors.
- **Held for review:** for the hiring-and-promotion facet, the pointers to employer-review sites and licensing cases were checked; none had to be set aside under the standing rules.
