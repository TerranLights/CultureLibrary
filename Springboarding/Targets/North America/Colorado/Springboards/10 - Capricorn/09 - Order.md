## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check how the plaza towns and the common *vega* are kept, and who decides, against the county grid.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute):** check how each tribe describes seasonal ceremonies of renewal, in what it chooses to share.
- **Front Range suburbs (Highlands Ranch, Castle Rock, Aurora):** check HOA rules and complaints for which kinds of untidiness draw action.
- **Denver's core neighborhoods (Five Points, Capitol Hill, Globeville):** check 311 and encampment records for whose complaints lead to sweeps.
- **Ski towns (Aspen, Telluride):** check local ordinances on noise, short-term rentals and night life, and who argues for them.
- **Eastern Plains towns:** check town newspapers for what counts as disorder in a small place (loose cattle, weeds, speeding).
- **Volga German beet towns:** check church and family records for household standards of cleanliness and Sabbath quiet.
- **Japanese Americans of Amache:** check the camp paper for self-governing block councils as residents' own order.
- **Vietnamese and Korean Aurora:** check festival and church calendars for New Year rites of putting the house in order.
- **Ethiopian and Eritrean Aurora:** check procession organizers' accounts of permits, routes and crowd marshals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public order (crime, 311, encampments); mixed for household order; thin for diaspora festivals.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for ditch and *vega* rules; Japanese for Amache-era papers; Vietnamese and Amharic for festival materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ute ceremonies are the tribes' to share; encampment sweeps and policing after 2020 are contested; Amache records name living families.
- **Held for review:** for the seasonal-order pointers, the sources on renewal ceremonies and New Year rites were checked; none had to be set aside under the standing rules.
