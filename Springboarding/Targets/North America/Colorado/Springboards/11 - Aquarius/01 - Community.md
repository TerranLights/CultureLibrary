# Colorado: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; southern Colorado (*manito*) Spanish; Mexican and Central American Spanish; Núuchi-u; Japanese; Amharic and Tigrinya; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), who recognize one another as members and owe one another something, and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of Costilla, Conejos and Las Animas counties:** compare *limpia* turnout and *morada* charity to see what membership costs in labor.
- **Ute Mountain Ute and Southern Ute:** check how each counts its community across reservation and state lines, since Towaoc and Ignacio keep separate records.
- **Mountain resort and mining towns (Aspen, Telluride, Leadville, Silverton):** check public-comment records on workforce housing and second homes for who is treated as a resident member.
- **Master-planned Front Range suburbs (Highlands Ranch, Castle Rock):** check covenants and developer marketing, where community is bought with the house.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers (Westwood, Commerce City, Greeley):** check hometown associations and parish fiesta committees as the community's own organs.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points, Park Hill and Montbello:** check oral histories of displacement for how the loss of a neighborhood is told.
- **Volga German families of the beet-worker neighborhoods (Fort Collins, Windsor):** check congregation histories for how a church held a whole neighborhood.
- **Eastern Plains towns:** check school-consolidation fights and booster clubs, where a town's survival is argued.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, Five Points and the Front Range suburbs; mixed for acequia communities (ditch records are local and uneven); thin for Vietnamese and Eritrean community bodies outside their own newsletters.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for ditch and *morada* records; Vietnamese for Federal Boulevard community newsletters; Amharic for association flyers in Aurora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Marshall Fire survivors and Amache descendants: oral history with consent only; *morada* rites are private to the brothers; Ute gatherings only as the tribes publish them.
- **Held for review:** Plains church and auxiliary cookbooks (with their member lists) were weighed for a row and left out until a library holding is found; nothing set aside under the standing rules.
