# Colorado: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; Chicano and Mexican Spanish; southern Colorado Spanish; Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse (Cheyenne); Hinóno'éitíít (Arapaho); Somali; Korean. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit: how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hard-rock mining towns (Cripple Creek, Leadville, Telluride):** check strike-era deportation lists for who was expelled from the town as a group.
- **Southern coal-camp families (Las Animas and Huerfano counties):** check the languages of union meetings in 1913-14 camps, where many origins formed one strike.
- **Chicanos of Denver and Pueblo:** check the Crusade for Justice paper *El Gallo* for how the movement named its own and its opponents.
- **Nuche bands:** check whether pre-reservation band names still shape enrollment or council seats today, as the tribes describe it.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí:** check descendant-nation historians on the military and age societies of the Colorado plains.
- **Colorado Springs's cadets and soldiers:** check base newspapers for unit-identity rites (patches, mottoes, reunions).
- **Evangelical congregations:** check membership covenants and small-group curricula for what joining commits a member to.
- **Somali and Ethiopian packing-plant workers (Greeley, Fort Morgan):** check union local grievance files and mosque statements as two group voices.
- **Mountain-town locals and newcomers:** check local slang ("flatlander", "transplant") in letters to the editor for where the line is drawn.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor conflict and the 1920s Klan; mixed for Chicano movement groups (archives exist, government files are contested); thin for Somali and Korean associations in print.
- **Languages needed:** Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éitíít for society names; Somali for mosque and community statements after the Greeley dispute.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ludlow, Cripple Creek, Hop Alley and the plant dispute are contested; gang research needs consent and care; some Plains society knowledge is not for outsiders.
