# Colorado: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Mountain West English; Mexican and Chicano Spanish; southern Colorado Spanish; Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse; Japanese; Amharic and Tigrinya; Somali; Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mining towns (Leadville, Creede, Cripple Creek):** check boosters' pamphlets against miners' letters home for promised and lived hope.
- **Plains farm families of Baca and Prowers counties:** check Dust Bowl oral histories for how waiting for rain was spoken of.
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check petitions left at the San Luis shrine and the *mandas* recorded in parish life.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers:** check hometown-association projects funded from Colorado as hopes acted on back home.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse:** check what descendant keepers make public of Sweet Medicine's teachings, and what they hold back.
- **Nuche:** check what each tribe publishes on language revival and youth programs as statements about the future.
- **Japanese Americans:** check Amache families' accounts of schooling and work after the camp for hopes placed in children.
- **Black Coloradans of Five Points:** check Juneteenth festival programs and church anniversaries for how hope is preached and sung.
- **Refugee households from the Horn of Africa:** check community graduation celebrations and first-home stories.
- **Aerospace and military families of Colorado Springs:** check Space Symposium speeches for the future the industry promises.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for gold-rush promises, memorials and measurable despair; mixed for religious hope (parish practice is local and oral); thin for Somali and Vietnamese hope in Colorado print.
- **Languages needed:** Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse for Sweet Medicine's name and teachings; Amharic and Tigrinya for church and community celebrations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sweet Medicine's sacred teachings are restricted; Columbine and overdose data touch bereaved families; DACA statements can expose people.
- **Held for review:** a Front Range renewable-energy laboratory's public outreach on the future was weighed for a row and left for a later pass; nothing set aside under the standing rules.
