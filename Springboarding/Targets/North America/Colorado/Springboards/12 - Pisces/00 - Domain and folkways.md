## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley and southern Colorado:** check *velorio* and *alabado* texts against Rael's tales: song versus story.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers:** check which folk illnesses Denver clinic guides list, and whether the terms match Hispano ones.
- **Nuche (Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute; Northern Ute history):** check what each tribe publishes about ceremony and what it keeps closed.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí descendants:** check how Sand Creek's dead are spoken of in descendants' statements.
- **Black Coloradans (Five Points, Dearfield):** check church funeral programs at Blair-Caldwell Library for the language of suffering and deliverance.
- **Japanese Americans of Amache:** check camp newspapers against later testimony.
- **Volga German beet families (Windsor, Loveland, Sterling):** check whether *Brauche* survives in family memory or only in archives.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check where holy water and possession arise in clinic and congregation records.
- **Colorado Springs evangelical and military households:** check deliverance-ministry materials for what is named as spiritual attack.
- **Mining, ski and ranching towns:** check ghost-town lore for who haunts what.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for asylums, sanatoria, Amache and Sand Creek; mixed for folk healing; thin for Volga German and East African practice in Colorado. Colorado's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the state as it stands.
- **Languages needed:** southern Colorado Spanish for *alabados* and tales; Amharic and Somali for possession and evil-eye words; Ute and Cheyenne through tribal programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sun Dance and Bear Dance details are the tribes' to share; Penitente rites are private to the brotherhood; asylum patient files are restricted by health privacy; Sand Creek and Amache are contested ground, so give every account as an account.
- **Held for review:** two categories set aside from the retreat-and-transcendence facet: Buddhist institutions in the state's lands, and South Asian religious institutions in the nation's lands.
