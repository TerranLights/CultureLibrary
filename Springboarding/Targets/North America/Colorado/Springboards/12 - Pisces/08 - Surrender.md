# Colorado: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (hospice, military, mountaineering and recovery speech); Spanish of *alabados* and of Mexican Denver; Japanese of the camp generation; Amharic; Somali; Korean; Vietnamese; German hymn language; Cheyenne, Arapaho and Ute memory through the nations. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher: God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death (release and letting go included).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check *alabados* sung at *velorios* for words of submission against words of hope.
- **Mexican and Central American newcomers:** check clinic and hospice chaplains' notes on *voluntad de Dios* in end-of-life talks.
- **Nuche:** check how the 1880 agreement and its signers are remembered by each tribe.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí descendants:** check the September 1864 Camp Weld council record against descendants' accounts of coming in to Fort Lyon, for how giving up arms and seeking protection is told on each side.
- **Japanese Americans:** check Sansei writing that contests *shikata ga nai* as the camp's lesson.
- **Volga German beet families:** check letters from Soviet-era kin and the replies sent from Colorado.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check palliative-care interpreter guidance on speaking of fate.
- **Korean and Vietnamese congregations:** check sermons on obedience and acceptance in illness.
- **Colorado Springs military families:** check POW and captivity memoirs by Colorado veterans.
- **Ranching families:** check sale and easement stories in stock-grower magazines.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for end-of-life data and Sand Creek testimony; mixed for Hispano hymnody; thin for East African and Korean surrender talk in Colorado.
- **Languages needed:** northern New Mexican and southern Colorado Spanish for *alabados*; Japanese for Issei sources; Amharic and Somali for palliative-care sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Sand Creek and Ute removal are contested; aid-in-dying data are confidential at the person level; Penitente hymns are partly private.
- **Held for review:** no pointer held: the letting-go practices kept here are Catholic, evangelical, Orthodox and Muslim, and the hospice and mountaineering trails raised nothing under the standing rules.
