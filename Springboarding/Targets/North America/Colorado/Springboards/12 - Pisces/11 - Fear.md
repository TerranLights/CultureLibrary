# Colorado: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Colorado, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (newsroom, school-drill, ranch and canyon-warning registers); Spanish, from the bogeymen of southern Colorado to the deportation fears of new arrivals; Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali; Korean; Vietnamese; Japanese; German; Ute and Cheyenne only through the nations. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and a culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it (worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Hispanos of the San Luis Valley:** check which bogeyman names grandparents used and when they stopped.
- **Mexican and Central American families:** check school-attendance dips after immigration raids, as districts report them.
- **Nuche:** check what Ute storytelling restrictions (seasons, places) say about naming dangerous beings.
- **Tsėhésenėstsestȯtse and Hinóno'éí descendants:** check how 1864 panic and its newspapers appear in descendants' teaching.
- **Black Coloradans:** check Denver's Black press in the 1920s Klan years for warnings to readers.
- **Japanese Americans:** check Denver's Japanese-language press in December 1941 for advice to families.
- **Volga German beet families:** check World War I loyalty-campaign records for fear of German speech.
- **Ethiopian, Eritrean and Somali Aurora:** check refugee-resettlement mental-health reports for named fears.
- **Korean and Vietnamese Aurora and Denver:** check community responses to anti-Asian incidents after 2020.
- **Ranching families of North Park and the Western Slope:** check stock-grower meeting minutes on wolves and lions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, nuclear sites and school safety; mixed for folklore; thin for East African and Korean fear vocabularies in Colorado.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for bogeymen and deportation sources; Japanese for 1941-42 press; German for wartime loyalty records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Hungate killings and Sand Creek are contested; school-shooting materials involve living families; immigrants in sanctuary are at legal risk, and their legal side is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** fear-of-God and deliverance pointers here came from Christian congregations, and the disaster and policing trails raised nothing under the standing rules, so none was held.
