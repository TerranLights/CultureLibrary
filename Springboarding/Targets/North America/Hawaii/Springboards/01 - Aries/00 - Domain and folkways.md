# Hawaii: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii, region North America; the Hawaiian Islands, the eight main islands with Papahānaumokuākea. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (with Niʻihau speech), Pidgin (Hawaiʻi Creole English), island English; Ilocano, Tagalog, Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean, Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming ways, sport as a statement of the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli across districts (Kaʻū, Waiʻanae, Windward Koʻolau, Hāna, the Molokaʻi homesteads): check whether naming and birth practices recorded in one district hold in others, since the classic accounts rest on few informants.
- Niʻihau's community and Niʻihau families in Kekaha: check what the community itself shares on naming before letting the main-island record stand for it.
- Plantation-era descendants (Japanese, Okinawan, Hakka and Zhongshan Chinese, Korean, Ilocano and Visayan, Portuguese, Puerto Rican): compare where English and heritage given names sit, generation by generation.
- Samoan and Tongan families in Lāʻie and Kalihi: separate church naming from title naming.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents under the Compacts: check how families present themselves to schools and clinics; written sources are few.
- Haole residents by arrival period, and military families at Pearl Harbor–Hickam and Schofield: check which self-labels each uses.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Upcountry Maui; Congregational, Catholic and Latter-day Saint congregations; māhū and other gender-diverse people: check dress, baptismal names and self-naming in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kanaka Maoli naming and personhood and for Japanese American plantation memory; mixed for Filipino, Korean, Portuguese and Puerto Rican self-accounts; thin for Marshallese, Chuukese and Niʻihau.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries USA as pre-war lands and records no settled lands; no scope file exists yet, so this sign's sheets take the Hawaiian Islands (with Papahānaumokuākea) as the core and draw no borders.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for name chants; Pidgin for oral histories; Japanese and Ilocano for first-generation papers; Marshallese and Chuukese (Marshallese-English and Trukese-English dictionaries) for the newest communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies, dream-given names and *iwi kūpuna* matters are family-held; go through families or the community's own institutions. Niʻihau is private land with restricted access.
- **Held for review:** memorial and naming practices of the islands' Japanese Buddhist temples, held as a category under the open Buddhism question.
