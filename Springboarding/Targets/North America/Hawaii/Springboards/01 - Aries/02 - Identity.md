# Hawaii: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi and Pidgin for self-labels; English of Hawaii; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean, Ilocano, Tagalog, Portuguese for community papers; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese for newer arrivals. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli by island and setting (Molokaʻi homesteads, Waiʻanae, Hilo, urban Honolulu): check whether genealogy, place or fraction is named first.
- Niʻihau families: check what is said by the community itself, not by outsiders, about how they name who they are.
- Japanese against Okinawan descendants: compare when each set the two labels apart.
- Chinese (Zhongshan, Hakka), Korean and Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan, newer Tagalog-speaking) descendants: compare which origin label each generation keeps.
- Portuguese (Azorean against Madeiran) and Puerto Rican descendants: check where each sits in island race categories over time, a shifting record.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: compare self-labels in church, school and census forms; Micronesian sources are thin.
- Haole residents (missionary-descended, ranch, postwar, recent) and military families: check how "local" and "kamaʻāina" are granted or withheld in their own accounts.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Upcountry: check whether ranch heritage comes before ethnicity in introductions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kanaka Maoli and Japanese American identity; mixed for Chinese, Korean, Filipino and Portuguese; thin for Puerto Rican and Micronesian residents and for Niʻihau.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa genealogies; Japanese and Korean for prewar community papers; Ilocano for the Filipino press; Marshallese and Chuukese where a community has its own media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies and *iwi kūpuna* are family-held; identity rules (blood quantum, admissions, the 1893 overthrow and annexation) are contested ground, so give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** the community histories kept by the islands' Japanese Buddhist missions, set aside as a category only while Buddhism remains an open question.

## 5. First queries
1. Papakilo Database: search "moʻokūʻauhau" in *Ka Nupepa Kuokoa*, 1861–1900, and log which families are printed.
2. DBEDT: download the latest detailed race and ancestry tables for Hawaii and note every category offered.
3. Library catalog: "Hawaiian blood" Kauanui and "On being Hawaiian" Holt.
4. UH ScholarSpace: "local identity" Hawaii, sorted by date, to trace the term since the 1970s.
5. HUOA and FilCom Center: list their published histories and oral-history projects.
