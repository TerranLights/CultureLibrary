*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for collective self-rule; island English and Pidgin for household choices; Japanese and Korean for picture-bride and plantation records; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan for family authority; Portuguese, Cantonese. **Concept in one line:** deciding for oneself: freedom from being controlled, for persons and for groups.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare individual choice inside the ʻohana with collective self-rule claims, and check homestead, Waiʻanae, Hāna and Kalaupapa accounts separately.
- Niʻihau's community: check what decisions residents describe as their own under private ownership, going only through the community's channels.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: check spouse choice and career choice across picture-bride, Nisei and Sansei generations.
- Filipino families (Ilocano, Visayan): compare sakada-era control on the plantations with post-1965 family migration, where relatives sponsor relatives.
- Korean, Chinese, Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check how each describes leaving plantation contracts for town life.
- Samoan and Tongan households: check how titled heads and churches figure in personal decisions.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how Compact status shapes their own account of choice in housing, health and work; sources are scarce.
- Haole newcomers and military families: compare their household decision norms with island-raised neighbors' as each side describes them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective self-rule (sovereignty, land, education) and plantation control; mixed for household choice and end-of-life decisions; thin for Pacific Islander family decision-making in the islands.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kingdom-era sources; Japanese for picture-bride letters; Ilocano for sakada records; Samoan and Tongan for family-authority terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalaupapa patients and descendants are living people (visits need permits); sovereignty and Kahoʻolawe are contested ground; legal questions of self-determination go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing; bedside-ethics and schooling literature for this concept raised no pointer needing to be set aside.
