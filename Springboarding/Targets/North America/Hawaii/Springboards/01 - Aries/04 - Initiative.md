## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in rural districts (Waiʻanae, Waipiʻo, Molokaʻi, Hāna) against Honolulu: check whether starting a project is described as personal or as a family's *kuleana*.
- Japanese descendants in Kona and Hilo against Honolulu: compare small-farm and storefront starts.
- Chinese, Korean and Okinawan families: check which credit circles each used and when they faded.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare plantation-era community clubs with today's Waipahu and Kalihi businesses.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check craft and trade starts on Maui and Hawaiʻi Island.
- Samoan, Tongan and Micronesian residents: check how church and community roles shape who may start something; sources are thin.
- Haole newcomers (including founders who move in) against kamaʻāina families: check how each is described when starting ventures.
- Military families: check spouse-run businesses near bases as a separate case.
- Lahaina and Upcountry Maui residents: compare accounts of 2023 relief.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business history and voyaging; mixed for everyday unasked help; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau initiative.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kamakau's original serials; Japanese for credit-circle and shop records; Korean for association papers; Ilocano for club newsletters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Lahaina fire and its rebuilding are contested and recent; residents' grief calls for care; chiefly genealogies tied to the 1819 events may be family-held.
- **Held for review:** no item; the business statistics and founders' accounts scouted for this concept carry nothing under the rules.
