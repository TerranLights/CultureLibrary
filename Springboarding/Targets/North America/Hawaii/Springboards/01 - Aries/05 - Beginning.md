# Hawaii: Beginning (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for origin chants and the moon calendar; Japanese, Cantonese, Korean, Tagalog and Ilocano for birth and new-year customs and arrival histories; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Pidgin and island English for newcomers and fresh starts. **Concept in one line:** how a culture treats starting: origins, firsts, new starts, and starting over after failure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli lineages and *hālau*: compare the origin accounts each keeps (Kumulipo line, Papa and Wākea, Kahiki arrivals) rather than merging them.
- Hawaiʻi Island districts (Kona, Kaʻū, Puna, Hilo, Waipiʻo): check how starts of seasons and recovery after lava and tsunami are told locally.
- Japanese, Okinawan, Chinese, Korean and Filipino descendants: compare how each marks a birth (first month, hundredth day, first birthday) and a new year.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check their arrival anniversaries and whether these are marked publicly.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check what counts as arrival and how the newest arrivals are named by others; few sources yet.
- Haole residents and military families: check fresh-start narratives of moving to the islands, and how local media answer them.
- Congregational, Catholic and Latter-day Saint congregations: compare baptism and conversion as new beginnings.
- Kauaʻi and Niʻihau: check which beginnings (Cook's 1778 landing at Waimea, older traditions) each community tells first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for origin chants, the Makahiki and arrival histories; mixed for birth customs across groups; thin for Micronesian arrival stories.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Kumulipo and Malo in the original; Japanese and Cantonese for birth and new-year customs; Korean for centennial papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some chants and *hālau* protocols are restricted; *piko* sites and Papahānaumokuākea need care and permits; statehood and the 1893 overthrow are contested ground for any "founding" story; "FOB" is derogatory.
- **Held for review:** the Japanese Buddhist missions' New Year and infant ceremonies, a category set aside under the Buddhism question.
