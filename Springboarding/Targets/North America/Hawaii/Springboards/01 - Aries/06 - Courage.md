# Hawaii: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for warrior and epic sources; Pidgin and island English for veterans, watermen and lifeguards; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for wartime and endurance terms; Korean, Cantonese, Tagalog, Ilocano, Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli men and women: compare warrior revival groups with women's accounts of steadfastness (the 1897 petitions, the Kahoʻolawe landings).
- Japanese Americans: separate soldiers' accounts from Issei parents' and from families interned at Honouliuli.
- Okinawan families: check whether wartime courage is told differently, given the battle in their homeland.
- Filipino and Korean residents: check plantation-strike and homeland-cause courage in their own presses.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check how sport and service are spoken of in their own churches and media, rather than through outsiders' labels.
- Niʻihau's community and Kauaʻi neighbors: compare their own tellings of 1941 with outside books.
- North Shore and Mākaha watermen, lifeguards and fishers: check what counts as brave and what as foolish in the water.
- Military families: compare service courage with that of island-raised enlistees.
- Kalaupapa patients and caregivers: check how endurance is told by residents themselves.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime Nisei, voyaging and Kahoʻolawe; mixed for women's courage and everyday ocean rescue; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau voices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Hiʻiaka epic in its nūpepa form; Japanese for Issei letters; Ilocano and Korean for community presses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lua and *hālau* teachings are partly restricted; Kahoʻolawe, the overthrow and the Niʻihau incident are contested ground; war trauma and lost-at-sea families need care.
- **Held for review:** none; the war, sea and warrior sources weighed for courage contained no item to set aside.
