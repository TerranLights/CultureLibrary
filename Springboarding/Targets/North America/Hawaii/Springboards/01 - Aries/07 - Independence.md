# Hawaii: Independence (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for land, subsistence and the Kingdom; island English and Pidgin for households; Korean and Cantonese for homeland-independence causes run from Honolulu; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Tagalog, Portuguese for mutual-aid societies; Samoan and Tongan for family obligation. **Concept in one line:** not depending on others: self-reliance and self-sufficiency, from the household to the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kuaʻāina families (Molokaʻi, Hāna, Kahikinui, Puna, Waipiʻo) against urban Kānaka Maoli: check how each speaks of living from the land and of wage work.
- Niʻihau's community: check what residents and their kin on Kauaʻi say of self-provisioning, through their own channels.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: compare prefectural and village mutual aid with family self-support.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: compare homeland-independence causes with household self-reliance.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) and Portuguese families: check remittance and benefit-society records.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese households: check how family obligation and public help are weighed in their own accounts; Micronesian sources are few.
- Puna's off-grid settlers and Upcountry homesteaders: compare chosen self-sufficiency with Kanaka Maoli subsistence.
- Lānaʻi residents: check the effect of single ownership on how people describe independence.
- Military families: check how base support systems figure against civilian neighbors' self-reliance.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Kingdom, sovereignty and rural subsistence; mixed for household independence; thin for Pacific Islander and Micronesian help-seeking.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kingdom-era sources; Korean and Cantonese for homeland-independence papers; Japanese for *kenjinkai* records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims to national independence and the events of 1893 and 1898 are disputed, so cite each as one side's account (legal questions to Libra and Capricorn); fishing grounds and family subsistence spots may be held privately.
- **Held for review:** none; the self-sufficiency and independence-movement sources here raised no item for the held line.
