# Hawaii: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi first for everyday anger words and family repair; island English; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Korean, Cantonese, Tagalog and Ilocano for emotion terms; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan for apology customs. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families using *hoʻoponopono* against those who do not: check how widely it is practiced today and by whom, on Oʻahu and the neighbor islands.
- Japanese, Okinawan and Korean descendants: compare restraint vocabulary and the folk illnesses linked to held anger in each.
- Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog-speaking Filipinos: check sulking, quarrel and reconciliation terms in each language.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check how apology ceremonies and church leaders handle grievance in the islands.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check accounts of discrimination and conflict in schools and housing, told by community members; scarce in print.
- Haole residents and military families: compare accounts of school conflict and of the Red Hill crisis.
- Niʻihau families on Kauaʻi: check for any public record before assuming one exists.
- Mauna Kea kiaʻi, telescope supporters and Hilo residents: separate each side's account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for *hoʻoponopono*, protest and plantation conflict; mixed for everyday household anger; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau sources.
- **Languages needed:** Pidgin for street speech; Hawaiian for *mele* and *kanikau*; Ilocano and Tagalog for family emotion terms; Korean for *hwabyeong* literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Massie, Mauna Kea, Red Hill and accounts of "Kill Haole Day" are contested and painful; handle each as an account. Family *hoʻoponopono* sessions are private. Domestic-violence data concerns survivors.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the emotion-term and mediation sources weighed here fall outside the held categories.
