## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in *hālau*, canoe clubs and Makahiki groups: check how each sets rules for winning, wagering and grace in defeat.
- Hilo, Molokaʻi and Kona against Honolulu: compare where contests are hosted and how neighbor islands speak of Honolulu teams.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare community baseball, sumo and karate as separate contest traditions.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan), Korean and Chinese descendants: check plantation-era boxing, basketball and pageants in their own presses.
- Samoan and Tongan families in Lāʻie, Kahuku and Kalihi: check how church, family and coaches speak of sport, in their own words.
- Marshallese and Chuukese youth: check school-sports records and community tournaments; little is published.
- Ranching (paniolo) families of Waimea on Hawaiʻi Island and of Kula and Makawao: compare rodeo with other contests.
- North Shore and Mākaha surfers, haole surfers and visiting pros: separate local and outsider accounts of surf competition.
- Military families: check base leagues against island-school teams.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hula, surfing, canoe racing and sumo; mixed for school and pageant competition; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau contests.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Makahiki sources and hula chants; Japanese for sumo and AJA league records; Samoan and Tongan for church and community sports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *hālau* judging protocols and chants may be restricted; North Shore localism is contested and can be violent; school admissions rivalry touches contested law (to Libra and Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** none; the sport, pageant and game sources on this sheet involved nothing under the standing rules.
