# Hawaii: Recklessness (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and island English for risk talk, ocean safety and traffic; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for old daring sports, wagering and warning signs; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean, Tagalog for rashness and fatalism; Portuguese; Samoan, Marshallese and Chuukese for community safety outreach. **Concept in one line:** courage or initiative pushed past its useful range; acting without regard for danger or consequence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli watermen and fishing families: check where they draw the line between skill and rashness at sea, in their own accounts.
- Kauaʻi's north shore, Oʻahu's North Shore and Windward side, Maui's Peʻahi, Hawaiʻi Island's Puna: compare each place's danger spots and how locals and visitors are warned.
- Japanese, Okinawan, Chinese and Korean families: check New Year fireworks customs against complaints and injuries.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: check *bahala na* and risk terms as used in island families, from their own writing.
- Pacific Islander communities (Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese): check public-health outreach in their languages and how communities answer it; few sources yet.
- Haole residents, visitors and military families: compare drowning, hiking and traffic records across these groups, since visitors and newcomers appear separately in injury data.
- Young men and women across groups: compare risk surveys by sex and age rather than by assumption.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ocean injuries, traffic and youth risk surveys; mixed for old daring sports; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau risk practices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *hōlua* and *lele kawa* sources; Pidgin for youth talk; Marshallese and Chuukese for health-outreach materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug use and drowning deaths involve grieving families; Haʻikū Stairs and fireworks enforcement are disputed; Pele protocols at the volcano are a living practice, not a curiosity.
- **Held for review:** none; the injury, ocean-safety and old-sports sources for this concept raised nothing to set aside.
