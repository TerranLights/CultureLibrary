# Hawaii: The domain: possessions and value (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii, region North America: the Hawaiian Islands, main islands and Papahānaumokuākea. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin and island English first; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean, Ilocano, Tagalog and Portuguese for plantation-era households; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese for exchange goods. **Concept in one line:** what is worth having and how it is held: money, goods, land and water, food, dress, wealth and its giving away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli by district (Waiʻanae, Hāna, Kaʻū, the Molokaʻi homesteads): check whether land, kin and food are reckoned as wealth the same way where homestead leases replace fee title.
- Niʻihau's community: check, through the community and the landholding family's published statements only, whether a cash economy and gift goods sit apart there.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare funeral-money and gift ledgers between the two, since both keep such records.
- Chinese (Zhongshan, Hakka), Korean and Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: check how shops, land and money sent abroad were handled after the plantation years, older *sakada* families apart from newer arrivals.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check feast foods and church-society gifts as household goods.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese households: separate mat and cloth wealth from cash; Micronesian sources are scarce.
- Haole residents (missionary-descended, recent), military families and paniolo families of Waimea and Upcountry Maui: compare owning, renting, base housing, and cattle brands kept as heirlooms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land tenure and prices; mixed for household budgets by group; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau households.
- **Lands:** the tracker names USA as pre-war land and settles no territory, and there is no scope file; these Taurus sheets write for the Hawaiian Islands (main islands and Papahānaumokuākea) and leave all borders to the author.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Māhele testimony and nūpepa; Japanese for the island dailies; Samoan and Tongan for exchange vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Māhele, the overthrow and annexation, water rights and the Lahaina fire are contested; give every reading as an account. Grave goods and *iwi kūpuna* are family-held; go through families and their institutions only.
- **Held for review:** funeral offerings and memorial-gift customs at the islands' Japanese Buddhist temples, held as a category while Buddhism stays an open question.

## 5. First queries
1. Kīpuka database: open three Land Commission Awards from one ahupuaʻa on each of Oʻahu, Maui and Hawaiʻi Island; list every resource named.
2. wehewehe.org: search *waiwai*, *hoʻokupu* and *lako*; record each sense with its source dictionary.
3. Hoji Shinbun: search 香典 in *Hawaii Hochi*, 1920–1941.
4. UHERO site: list cost-of-living and housing reports, newest first.
5. Library catalog: "Anahulu" Kirch Sahlins and "Aloha attire" Arthur.
