# Hawaii: Ownership (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for tenure words and boundary testimony; island English for leasehold talk; Japanese and Cantonese for plantation-era land and shop holding; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese where family land elsewhere is part of a household's holding. **Concept in one line:** the recognized claim to have, use, keep others from, and give away or sell a thing, and what that claim rests on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli holding kuleana land, Hawaiian Home Lands leases and fee land (Hāna, Waipiʻo, Keaukaha, Molokaʻi): check how each kind of holding is spoken of by its holders.
- Niʻihau's community: check what residents of a privately owned island say about their own holding, through their own channels.
- Japanese and Okinawan farm families in Kona coffee, Waiākea and Kula: compare leased and owned farms.
- Chinese families in rice and taro lands of Waipiʻo and Waikīkī's old wetlands: check how early land purchases were recorded and passed down.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families from plantation camps: check when company housing became owned housing.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare small homesteads in Kula and Hilo with camp housing.
- Lānaʻi residents of every background: compare an island-wide owner's role with ordinary home ownership.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese households: check how land held in the home islands figures in a family's sense of what it owns.
- Haole landed families, ranch estates (Parker, Ulupalakua, Molokaʻi Ranch) and military families in base housing: compare old estate holding with renting and housing allowances.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land records and tenure history; mixed for current household views of owning; thin for Micronesian households and Niʻihau.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for boundary testimony; Japanese for prewar farm leases in the community press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Māhele, the Crown and ceded lands, quiet-title suits, water and military land are contested; give each reading as an account. Claims rest on law: take the letter to Libra and the spirit to Capricorn, and keep this sheet on the lived claim.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the tenure sources weighed (boundary testimony, estate papers, agency and fishery records) raised no standing-rule question.
