# Hawaii: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi and Pidgin for pride, shame and standing; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for face; Tagalog and Ilocano for shame and honor; Samoan and Tongan for dignity and respect; Portuguese for family pride. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is taken to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Waiʻanae, Nānākuli and Papakōlea against Hilo and Hāna: check whether hula, language and canoe programs are named as sources of worth in each place.
- Niʻihau speakers on Kauaʻi: check how those raised speaking Niʻihau Hawaiian say schools treated their speech, from their own accounts.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare *enryo* and *haji* across the 442nd generation and their grandchildren.
- Korean and Chinese families: check how face and schooling are tied in their own community papers.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan, Tagalog-speaking) residents: check accounts of ethnic jokes and slurs, and how worth was reclaimed, from community writers.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare how each group answers the jokes aimed at it.
- Samoan and Tongan youth in Kalihi and Lāʻie: check whether sport, church and family service are named as what worth rests on.
- Marshallese and Chuukese newcomers: read their own statements on the stereotypes they meet before anything others write about them.
- Haole residents and military families: check how being an outsider shapes standing in schools and workplaces.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for plantation hierarchy and Native Hawaiian well-being; mixed for language stigma; thin for Micronesian, Puerto Rican and Niʻihau voices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for rank words; Pidgin for oral testimony; Japanese for postwar papers; Ilocano and Chuukese where community media exist.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic humor and slurs (*kauwā*, slurs aimed at Filipinos and Micronesians) should be described, not quoted; suicide data needs care; genealogical rank is family-held.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the face, shame and rank pointers come from veterans' writing, health reports and the community press, and none needed a religious community's own records.
