# Hawaii: Values (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for mottos, school outcomes and the patriotic press; Pidgin for family teaching; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Korean, Cantonese, Ilocano and Tagalog for the sayings households pass on; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese for church and family duty. **Concept in one line:** what a person or a culture holds worth having, doing or being, how those things are ranked, and how worth is assigned to things.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in sovereignty groups, civic clubs and churches: check how *pono* and *aloha ʻāina* are ranked when they pull apart, in each group's own statements.
- Niʻihau families: check what is shared through their own church and school about the values they teach, rather than outsiders' summaries.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare *oyakōkō* with *ichariba chōdē* as named ideals, generation by generation.
- Korean and Chinese families: check how filial terms appear in church bulletins and society newsletters.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) households: check *utang na loob* in remittance and town-association writing.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare Holy Ghost and saints' feast committees as places values are taught.
- Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese church communities in Lāʻie, Kalihi and Hilo: check how service to family and church is weighed against school and work.
- Congregational (Kawaiahaʻo), Catholic and Latter-day Saint members: compare their teaching on alcohol, work and family.
- Haole newcomers and military families: check which island value words they adopt and how long-time residents respond.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in official and educational statements; mixed in surveys (the islands are often a small subsample); thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau families.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the patriotic press and mottos; Japanese for issei writing; Samoan and Tongan for church teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *aloha* and *pono* are often used by outsiders and in marketing; record who uses them and for what. Burial council items concern *iwi kūpuna*; work from published minutes only. Sovereignty and statehood readings are contested; give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** the values taught in the islands' Japanese Buddhist temple schools and youth groups, held as a category while Buddhism remains open.
