# Hawaii: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for safety, preparation and famine; island English for jobs and disaster drills; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese and Korean for saving; Tagalog for savings and remittances; Portuguese, Samoan and Tongan for family cushions. **Concept in one line:** protection against loss and want: keeping one's livelihood, holdings and person safe now and in the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Waiʻanae, Waimānalo and on Molokaʻi: check how fishponds, gardens and kin are spoken of as a cushion against want.
- Niʻihau families: check, through their own channels, how a small island without stores keeps supplies.
- Former sugar and pineapple towns (Hāmākua, Kaʻū, Waialua, Lānaʻi City): compare what plantation housing and pensions meant before and after closure.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare saving habits recorded in Japanese-language papers with later oral histories.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: check how money sent abroad and savings at home are balanced.
- Chinese, Korean and Portuguese families: check family firms and church societies as a source of security.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check church and extended-family pooling in hard times.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check health coverage and housing as they describe them; sources are thin, look to community health centers.
- Military families and civil-service households: compare job security against high costs.
- Puna, Lahaina and Kaʻū residents: compare disaster exposure with insurance and rebuilding.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, housing and disaster programs; mixed for private saving habits; thin for Niʻihau and Micronesian households.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for fishing *kapu* and famine accounts; Japanese for prewar savings notices; Marshallese and Chuukese for health testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homelessness sweeps, COFA coverage and military fuel and land are contested; give each account. Fishpond knowledge may be held by the families who keep the ponds.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; savings and mutual-aid pointers here come from banks, credit unions and food banks, not from any religious body's own records.
