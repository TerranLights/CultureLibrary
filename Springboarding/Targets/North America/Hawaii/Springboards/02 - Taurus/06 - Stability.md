# Hawaii: Stability (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for steadfastness and birthplace; Pidgin for "born and raised" talk; Cantonese and Japanese for settling; Tagalog, Portuguese, Samoan and Tongan for rootedness and steadiness. **Concept in one line:** continuity: things, places, relationships and routines staying as they are over time.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Hāna, Kaʻū, Waipiʻo and the Molokaʻi homesteads: check how long families have held the same land and what breaks that hold.
- Niʻihau families on Niʻihau and in Kekaha: compare continuity on a closed island with continuity among those who moved to Kauaʻi.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check farms, shops and neighborhoods held for generations against those cleared by disaster or development.
- Chinese families of Chinatown and Mānoa: check how many stores and society halls stay in the same family.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: compare plantation-camp continuity with later moves to Waipahu and Kalihi.
- Portuguese families of Kula, Hilo and Kalihi: check feasts and church societies kept by the same households.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check moves between Hawaii, home islands and the continent; Micronesian data are scarce.
- Military families: compare rotation every few years with the long tenure of civilian neighbors.
- Haole residents: separate missionary-descended families from newcomers and seasonal residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church records, company histories and vital statistics; mixed for household continuity; thin for Niʻihau and Micronesian families.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for church records and birthplace chants; Japanese for Hilo community papers; Portuguese for feast records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birthing stones and burial places are wahi pana with stewards; follow their protocols. Evictions and redevelopment are contested; give each account.
- **Held for review:** the continuity of the islands' Japanese Buddhist congregations and their yearly calendars, held as a category under the open Buddhism question.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: "Kalama Valley" 1971 and "Waiahole Waikane"; read press coverage of both.
