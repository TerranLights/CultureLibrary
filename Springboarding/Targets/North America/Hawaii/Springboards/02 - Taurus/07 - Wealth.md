# Hawaii: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for prosperity, stinginess and chiefly riches; Pidgin and island English for neighborhood bywords; Japanese, Cantonese and Korean for New Year money and fortune; Ilocano and Tagalog for wedding money; Samoan and Tongan for family contributions. **Concept in one line:** an abundance of what a culture counts as valuable, and the standing that comes with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: separate aliʻi-descended families and trust beneficiaries from homesteaders and from families in Waiʻanae and Puna; check what each names as wealth (land, kin, knowledge, money).
- Niʻihau's community: check, through its own channels, how cash, shell-lei income and goods from Kauaʻi are counted there.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare plantation savings, postwar business and professional wealth, generation by generation.
- Chinese families: compare merchant, land-developer and professional fortunes.
- Korean families: check church-based fundraising and business founding after 1903 and after 1965.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: check how wedding and milestone gifts and remittances are counted as wealth.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare ranch, trade and bakery fortunes with plantation incomes.
- Samoan, Tongan and Micronesian households: separate wealth gathered for giving from personal saving; sources for Micronesians are scarce.
- Haole old families, recent wealthy arrivals and military households: compare land, trust income and salaries.
- Paniolo and ranch families (Parker, Ulupalakua): check cattle and land as wealth against cash.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kingdom and sugar-era fortunes and philanthropy; mixed for household wealth by group; thin for Micronesian, Niʻihau and Puerto Rican families.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa on chiefly households and royal display; Japanese and Cantonese for business histories in community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** outside wealth and land purchases, the trusts and the overthrow are contested; give each reading as an account. Family giving at Samoan and Tongan events is private; ask through churches.
- **Held for review:** donations and pledge lists kept by the islands' Japanese Buddhist temples, held as a category only under the open Buddhism question.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Kuykendall "Hawaiian kingdom" vols 1–3; Adler "Claus Spreckels".
2. Chronicling America: "jubilee" Kalākaua 1886 in Honolulu papers; compare with nūpepa coverage.
3. Hawaii Community Foundation: list its oldest funds and their founders.
4. ACS: Gini index for Honolulu, Hawaiʻi, Maui and Kauaʻi counties.
5. Search "first birthday luau" Hawaii caterers; note package prices and menus.
