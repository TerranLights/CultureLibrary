# Hawaii: Stubbornness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for hardheadedness and holding fast; Pidgin for "hardhead" talk; Japanese and Cantonese for obstinacy idioms and wartime holdouts; Tagalog and Ilocano for strike histories; Portuguese, Samoan and Tongan for family and church quarrels. **Concept in one line:** holding to a position, habit or course against persuasion, evidence or pressure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Waimānalo, Waiʻanae, Molokaʻi and Lahaina: check how holding out on land is described by those who did it, and by relatives who chose otherwise.
- Niʻihau's community: check how its own members speak of keeping their speech and way of life, rather than outsiders' labels.
- Japanese descendants: separate *kachigumi* families from those who accepted defeat; the memory may still be painful.
- Okinawan, Chinese and Korean families: check how disputes between societies and churches were carried on or settled.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: compare the memory of 1920 and 1924 among descendants on Oʻahu and Kauaʻi.
- Portuguese families: check family feuds over land and their endings, in oral histories.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: check church splits and how leaders explain them.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how they speak of holding to custom under pressure to change; written sources are few.
- Haole residents and military families: check how outsiders and locals name each other's refusals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for strikes, sovereignty actions and land fights; mixed for family feuds; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau voices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for 1819 and 1895 accounts; Japanese for postwar holdout records; Ilocano for strike memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kuamoʻo is a burial ground; evictions, telescopes and Lahaina land are contested; give each account as an account and avoid labeling any group stubborn.
- **Held for review:** histories kept by the islands' Japanese Buddhist temples, which a search of the postwar years may turn up, held as a category only.
