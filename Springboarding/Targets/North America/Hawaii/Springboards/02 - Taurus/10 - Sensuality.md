# Hawaii: Sensuality (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for taste, scent, touch and ease; Pidgin for food praise and the after-work hour; Japanese for bathing; Cantonese, Korean, Ilocano and Tagalog for flavor words; Portuguese for festival sweets; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese for kava and feast terms. **Concept in one line:** enjoyment of the senses (taste, touch, smell, sound and sight) and of the body's comfort and pleasure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Hāna, Kaʻū, Waiʻanae and Molokaʻi: compare foods named as *ʻono* and the place of poi, fish and limu, island by island.
- Niʻihau families: check how scent and adornment (shell lei, flowers) are spoken of by makers themselves.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare camp bathing, New Year foods and *andagi* across generations.
- Chinese and Korean families: check dim sum, Lunar New Year sweets and *kimchi* making as household pleasures.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: compare festival foods by home region (Ilocos, Visayas); ask which tastes are kept from home and which were dropped.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check bread, sweets and music (*cavaquinho*, *pasteles*) at feasts.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check feasts, *umu* cooking and kava, and who may enjoy what at each event.
- Latter-day Saint households in Lāʻie and other congregations with food and drink rules: compare restraint with feast customs.
- Haole residents, retirees and military families: check which island foods and music they adopt and which they avoid.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, music and festivals; mixed for touch and bathing customs; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau sensory life.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for chant and hula vocabulary; Japanese for camp histories; Tongan and Samoan for kava etiquette.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some hula, chant and lomilomi knowledge is held within hālau and lines; go through them. Kava and cannabis touch on rules elsewhere (law goes to Libra and Capricorn). Do not exoticize the body or present residents as a show.
- **Held for review:** the senses in worship at the islands' Japanese Buddhist temples, held as a category only while Buddhism stays open.
