# Hawaii: The domain: communication and learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands, with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (with the Niʻihau speech), Pidgin and island English; Ilocano, Tagalog, Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean and Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese; Hawaiʻi Sign Language. **Concept in one line:** how people talk, learn and connect close to home: speech, schooling, siblings and neighbors, and short journeys.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: check how talk and learning differ where Hawaiian is a home language, a school language or neither.
- Niʻihau's community and its families in Kekaha (Kauaʻi): check the Niʻihau speech as a living home language only through the community's own schools and spokespeople.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants, Chinese (Zhongshan and Hakka) and Korean families: compare which heritage language survived in each and through what kind of school.
- Filipino households (Ilocano, Visayan, Tagalog): separate older *sakada* families from recent arrivals for language at home.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check what traces of their languages remain in island speech and music.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities (Kalihi, Lāʻie, Hilo): check language at home, church and school; sources for the last two are scarce.
- Haole residents and military families: check how newcomers pick up local speech and routes, and what orientation they get.
- Island by island (Waiʻanae and Windward Oʻahu, Hilo against Kona, Upcountry Maui against Hāna, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi): compare travel distances and neighbor ties by town size.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for language history and schooling; mixed for neighbor ties and daily speech; thin for Micronesian, Puerto Rican and Niʻihau speech.
- **Lands:** the tracker names USA as the pre-war land and leaves territory to Stage 0, and there is no scope file yet, so the exact lands are still to be settled; the Gemini sheets write for the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea and draw no borders.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kingdom schooling; Pidgin for oral sources; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for heritage-school records; HSL through Deaf community partners.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hālau teaching, family chants and Niʻihau speech are held by their keepers; go through their institutions. The Hawaiian-language ban in schools after 1896 and its effects are told in competing accounts; give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** the Japanese-language schools run by the islands' Japanese Buddhist missions, held as a category while Buddhism is an open question.
