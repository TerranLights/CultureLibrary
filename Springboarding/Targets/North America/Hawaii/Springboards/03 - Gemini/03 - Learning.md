# Hawaii: Learning (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for Kingdom schools, crafts and hālau; English and Pidgin for public schools; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for heritage-language schools; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese for newer arrivals' schooling. **Concept in one line:** how people come to know and to do: in the family, by watching and pitching in, through play, in early school, at work and across a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli children in immersion, Kamehameha, charter and regular public schools: check how teaching by showing and by telling differ across the four.
- Niʻihau families in Kekaha: check how their own schools describe teaching in the Niʻihau speech, without going around them.
- Japanese, Okinawan, Korean and Chinese descendants: compare which after-school language schools lasted and what they taught besides language.
- Filipino families (Ilocano, Visayan, Tagalog): separate children of recent arrivals in English-learner programs from older families.
- Samoan and Tongan families in Lāʻie and Kalihi: check church schools and BYU–Hawaii as learning routes.
- Marshallese and Chuukese children: check attendance and English-learner records; studies are few.
- Paniolo and farm families (Waimea, Kona, Upcountry Maui, Molokaʻi homesteads): check work learned at home against school.
- Military and haole newcomer families: check how often children change schools and what that does to learning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kingdom schooling and Hawaiian immersion; mixed for heritage-language schools; thin for Micronesian, Puerto Rican and Niʻihau children.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Kingdom reports and Kamakau's originals; Japanese and Korean for language-school records; Marshallese and Chuukese through family liaisons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ʻūniki* and hālau teaching are held by the kumu; voyaging knowledge belongs partly to Satawal's navigators. The 1896 shift away from Hawaiian-medium schools and the English Standard Schools are told in competing accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; the language-school sources listed are secular studies, and no single religious school's records were taken up for this concept.
