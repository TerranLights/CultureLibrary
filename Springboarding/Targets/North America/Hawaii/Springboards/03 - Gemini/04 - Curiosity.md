# Hawaii: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for seeking and observing words and for nineteenth-century travel writing; Pidgin and island English; Japanese for visitors' and residents' guidebooks; Tagalog, Cantonese, Korean, Portuguese, Samoan and Tongan for nosiness words. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore, and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli practitioners and scholars: check which questions they describe as welcome and which as *nīele*, in their own writing and teaching.
- Niʻihau's community: check what outsiders have tried to learn about it and how the community has answered; leave unshared matters unshared.
- Japanese and Okinawan, Chinese and Korean descendants: compare science-fair and museum participation with heritage-school schedules.
- Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) and Portuguese families: check ghost-story and folk-healing curiosity alongside school science.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese youth: check who reaches science outreach; sources are scarce.
- Haole residents, astronomers and military families: check which newcomers join citizen science, and how they meet sacred-site limits.
- Hilo and Waimea (observatory towns), Puna (lava watchers), Hāna and Molokaʻi: compare local interest in science institutions near and far.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for astronomy, volcano science and exploration history; mixed for everyday curiosity norms; thin for Micronesian youth and Niʻihau.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa travel letters and star lore; Japanese for visitor guides; Ilocano and Visayan for folk tales.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Maunakea and Papahānaumokuākea access are contested; give each side's account. Some star lore, burial caves and chants are restricted; ask keepers first. Ghost stories may touch *iwi kūpuna* and sacred places.
- **Held for review:** none; the observatory, museum and travel sources weighed here raised nothing under the rules, and no religious community's teaching on asking was needed.
