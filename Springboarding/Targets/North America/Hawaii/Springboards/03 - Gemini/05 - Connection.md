# Hawaii: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for kin and relation words; Pidgin for fictive kin; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for network and society words; Tagalog and Portuguese for godparenthood; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese for relational space and clan. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected or not.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families across islands (Waiʻanae, Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua, Hāna): check how *hānai*, reunions and homestead ties hold kin together across distance.
- Niʻihau families on Niʻihau and in Kekaha: check how the two halves keep in touch, only from what the families publish.
- Japanese and Okinawan, Chinese (Zhongshan and Hakka) and Korean descendants: compare society and church networks as a fourth and fifth generation take them over.
- Filipino households (Ilocano, Visayan): check sponsorship of godparents and the use of contacts in jobs.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare godparent ties and feast committees.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities: check how *vā* and clan ties are kept in island churches; Micronesian sources are few.
- Haole residents (missionary-descended and newcomers) and military families: compare old networks against short postings.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Upcountry Maui, and the residents of Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi: check how small, isolated communities keep links to the rest of the islands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kin terms, voyaging and community societies; mixed for measured loneliness; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau networks.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kalaupapa letters; Cantonese for society records; Marshallese and Chuukese with community partners.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies and Kalaupapa patients' names belong to families and the community there; use the community's own consent rules. The Superferry dispute and the overthrow's effect on kin networks are told in conflicting accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; the kin, transport and survey sources set out here are civic or scholarly, and nothing for this concept needed setting aside.
