# Hawaii: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for paired terms, chant and hidden meaning; Pidgin and island English for local and mixed identity; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for inner and outer faces; Korean, Cantonese, Tagalog and Portuguese for two-facedness; Samoan and Tongan for "our way" and "the foreign way". **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived with: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in sovereignty groups and in state service: check how each speaks of holding Hawaiian and American identity, in its own statements.
- Niʻihau families moving between Niʻihau and Kauaʻi: check how the move between two settings is described by the families themselves.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the wartime loyalty question and the Naichi and Uchinaanchu line.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: check homeland politics against island civic life before 1945.
- Filipino households: compare Ilocano and Visayan identities, and island-born against Philippine-born members.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check how both were counted apart from "haole" in old censuses and what that meant to them.
- Samoan and Tongan families (Lāʻie, Kalihi): check *faʻasamoa* and island ways in church and school; Marshallese and Chuukese sources are scarce.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Upcountry Maui: check how they name the Hawaiian and the Spanish-Mexican sides of ranch life.
- Haole residents and military families: check *kamaʻāina* status and its limits for long-time and short-time residents.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mixed identity and the Pidgin divide; mixed for paired worldviews in daily life; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau experience.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for chant and *kaona*; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for oral histories; Samoan for *faʻasamoa*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some *kaona* is meant for a few; do not decode family or hālau chants without leave. Kū and Lono readings, the loyalty question and statehood are contested; give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; the identity, school and chant sources gathered here raised no standing-rule question.
