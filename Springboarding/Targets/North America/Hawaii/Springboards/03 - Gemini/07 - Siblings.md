# Hawaii: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for sibling terms by age and sex and for chiefly genealogies; Pidgin for sibling address; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Korean and Cantonese for seniority and inheritance; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan for brother-sister obligations. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families (Keʻanae and Hāna, Waiʻanae, Hoʻolehua): check how the elder and younger roles and *hānai* siblings shape land, work and care.
- Niʻihau families: check sibling and cousin terms in their own speech through their school, not from outside summaries.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the eldest son's place and the tablet's inheritance between the two.
- Chinese and Korean families: check brothers in chain migration and inheritance of shops.
- Filipino households (Ilocano, Visayan): separate *manong*-era brothers from later families with sisters and children.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check siblings' roles as godparents to each other's children.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: check brother-sister obligations as practiced in the islands; Micronesian sources are scarce.
- Haole, military and paniolo families: compare siblings scattered by postings or ranch work with those who stay on the land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hawaiian kin terms and sibling caretaking; mixed for inheritance among plantation-era groups; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau families.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kepelino and genealogies; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for family records; Samoan and Tongan for obligation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chiefly genealogies and brother-sister unions among aliʻi are sacred and family-held matters; use published sources and consult families. The *Blu's Hanging* dispute is contested.
- **Held for review:** the place of siblings in the memorial rites of island Japanese families, where they are tied to temple practice, set aside as a category under the Buddhism question.
