# Hawaii: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for go-between and peacemaking words; Pidgin for settling a quarrel; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for matchmakers and arbiters; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan for chiefs' councils and reconciliation; Chuukese and Marshallese through community liaisons. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision, and more broadly the go-between who stands between parties.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families, agencies and churches: check who leads *hoʻoponopono* in each setting and whether the leader must be kin, elder or trained.
- Niʻihau's community: check, through its own spokespeople only, how it handles disputes with outside bodies.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare marriage go-betweens and association elders before and after 1945.
- Chinese and Korean residents: check society elders and church leaders as arbiters.
- Filipino households (Ilocano, Visayan): check sponsors and town-association officers as peacemakers.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check godparents and parish priests as go-betweens.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities: compare chiefs, pastors and school liaisons; Micronesian sources are thin.
- Military families and haole newcomers: check whether they use base programs, courts or neighbors to settle disputes.
- Kona, Hilo, Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi: compare island mediation services against reliance on kin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for *hoʻoponopono* and labor history; mixed for community mediation by group; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for practice terms; Japanese for go-between records; Samoan for *matai* procedure; Chuukese and Marshallese through liaisons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family *hoʻoponopono* is private, and practitioners dispute adapted versions. Maunakea talks are contested; take each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; the agency, labor and church sources listed for go-betweens did not need any item set aside.
