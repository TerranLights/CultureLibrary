# Hawaii: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and island English for everyday talk about others; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi for rumor, slander and teasing song; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Korean and Cantonese for backbiting words; Tagalog and Portuguese; Samoan for church and family talk. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct, and rumor: unconfirmed reports passed from person to person.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli homestead and rural communities (Nānākuli, Waimānalo, Hāna, Hoʻolehua): check how talk about kin is handled and when it is brought to *hoʻoponopono* instead.
- Niʻihau's community: check what rumors outsiders have spread about it, and what the community has said in reply, through its own statements only.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare plantation-camp talk and the wartime rumors that targeted them.
- Chinese and Korean residents: check talk inside churches and societies, and how disputes behind it were settled.
- Filipino households (Ilocano, Visayan): check the reach of homeland rumor through radio and messaging apps.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check feast and parish talk against the island press.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities: check church talk and stigma in news coverage; Micronesian voices are seldom recorded.
- Haole newcomers and military families: check how island grapevines and base talk overlap or stay apart.
- Lānaʻi City and Molokaʻi against Honolulu: compare how far news travels in a town where nearly everyone knows everyone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for wartime rumor and press columns; mixed for everyday talk by group; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau sources.
- **Languages needed:** Pidgin for oral histories and comedy; Hawaiian for the nūpepa columns and *mele*; Japanese for wartime community papers; Ilocano for homeland radio.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalaupapa patients' names and family stigma are private; work through Ka ʻOhana O Kalaupapa and the park. Lahaina rumors touch the dead and grieving families. Micronesian residents have been the subject of hostile talk; check coverage, do not repeat it.
- **Held for review:** none; the press, radio, survey and oral sources weighed for rumor raised no standing-rule question.
