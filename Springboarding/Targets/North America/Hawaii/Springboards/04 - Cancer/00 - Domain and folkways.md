# Hawaii: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands, with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (and the Niʻihau speech), Pidgin, island English; Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Cantonese, Korean; Ilocano, Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** what home is and who counts as family: households, parents and fathering, roots, family memory, hospitality, building ways, family ways and child-rearing.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Households on Hawaiian Home Lands (Nānākuli, Waimānalo, Keaukaha) against Kānaka Maoli households off them: check whether a land program changes who lives together.
- The people of Niʻihau and their relatives in Kekaha: ask about household forms only through the community's own school and spokespeople.
- Descendants of Japanese and Okinawan migrants: trace how the *ie* line and the Okinawan house moved into camps and then suburbs.
- Chinese and Korean families of Chinatown, Mānoa and Kalihi: look at shop-houses and living above the store.
- Filipino households of Waipahu, Kauaʻi and Maui: split recruit-era families (1906–46) from post-1965 arrivals.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican lines (Kula, Hilo): weigh Sunday gatherings and godparenthood as the measure of family reach.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese households (Lāʻie, Kalihi, Kona): look at kin hosting and crowding; Micronesian material is scarce, so begin with community groups.
- Haole, military and paniolo households (Waimea, Upcountry Maui): compare base housing and short stays with ranch land held for generations.
- Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi against urban Honolulu: test how company towns and small islands shape home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** household counts and house history run deep (Kingdom censuses, ACS, architectural surveys); fathering, hospitality and family memory are scattered across memoirs and community papers; Micronesian and Niʻihau households have little in print.
- **Lands:** no scope file exists and the tracker names only the USA as pre-war land, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these Cancer sheets take the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea as the core and fix no borders.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Kingdom's census forms; Japanese and Korean for community histories; Ilocano and Samoan for family associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies and family burial sites (*iwi kūpuna*) are family-held; reach them through families and their institutions. Accounts of the mission household and of Home Lands eligibility are contested; give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** New Year and memorial-day rites at the household altar in Japanese and Okinawan homes, where Buddhist, held as a category; Shinto *kamidana* shelves and Catholic home altars were not held and stay open.
