# Hawaii: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin and island English; Japanese; Korean and Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families with Home Lands leases against those on the waitlist: compare home as a lease, a promise or a wait.
- Families with Niʻihau roots in Kekaha and Waimea (Kauaʻi): check, with the community's own school, what "home" means when the home island is closed to visitors.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants in Hilo and Mōʻiliʻili: trace the *genkan* threshold and the camp house into later homes.
- Chinese and Korean households in Honolulu: check whether "home" names Honolulu or an ancestral village or province.
- Filipino families in Waipahu and Kahului: set homes here beside houses built in Ilocos with remittances.
- Portuguese families of Upcountry Maui (Kula, Makawao) and Puerto Rican families: check how long-held house lots are spoken of.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents in public housing and Hilo rentals: compare home here with the village or atoll; sources are thin, so start with community groups.
- Haole second-home owners, military families in base housing (Schofield, Kāneʻohe) and paniolo families of Waimea: set short tenure against generations on one ranch; Lahaina and Lānaʻi City add a burned town and a company town.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** housing costs and tenure are well measured; the meaning of home lives in songs, poems and testimony; Micronesian and Niʻihau homes are barely documented.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Native Testimony and song texts; Japanese for threshold and blessing customs; Ilocano for the remittance houses of Ilocos.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lahaina rebuilding, Home Lands waitlists and short-term rentals are contested; give each account as one. House blessings by kahu belong to their churches and families; ask them.
- **Held for review:** house-blessing rites led by Japanese Buddhist clergy were considered beside the Shinto *jichinsai* and set aside as a category under the Buddhism question.

## 5. First queries
1. OHA Kipuka Database: open one ahupuaʻa (Waipiʻo, Hawaiʻi Island) and list every *pā hale* claim with its testimony number.
2. Pull ACS tables B25003 (owners and renters) and B07001 (moves in the past year) for Maui and Kauaʻi, then Honolulu, and set them side by side.
3. UHERO site: search "Housing Factbook" and note each county table.
4. Library catalog: "Hilo rains" Kono; "Hawaiian modern" Ossipoff.
5. News archives: "Fishing for Housing" Kāʻanapali; read coverage from each side.
