*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: set belonging by genealogy beside belonging by blood quantum and by enrollment, and note who refuses all three as a frame.
- People of Niʻihau: check, only through their community, how being of Niʻihau is reckoned once a family lives on Kauaʻi.
- Japanese, Okinawan, Chinese and Korean descendants: trace when each came to call itself "local" and in which towns (Hilo, Mōʻiliʻili, Wahiawā).
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: compare *kababayan* ties with acceptance as local, by generation.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: see how census categories and nicknames once set them apart.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: record their own accounts of welcome and exclusion, school by school; sources are thin.
- Haole residents and newcomers, military families, paniolo families of Waimea: compare how many years or generations each is told it takes.
- Island against island: Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Hāna against Honolulu for how fast a newcomer is taken in.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the idea of "local" is well theorized; measured belonging is patchy; Micronesian residents' accounts sit mostly with advocacy groups.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian to read the 1890s association rolls; Pidgin to hear the plays as staged; Chuukese and Marshallese for any account of being received, with community interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** who is Native Hawaiian, enrollment, sovereignty and settler status are contested; present each account as an account. Hostility toward newcomers and Micronesian residents must be described as reported, never as a trait. The Moʻokini line's history is its own to tell.
- **Held for review:** empty; the membership pointers looked at (enrollment drives, heritage and advocacy groups, school and military surveys) needed nothing set aside.
