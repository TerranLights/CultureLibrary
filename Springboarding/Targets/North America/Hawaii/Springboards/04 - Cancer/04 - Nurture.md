# Hawaii: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese with community help. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare nurture by grandparents (*hānai* to *tūtū*) in Hāna, Molokaʻi and Waiʻanae with nurture through Kamehameha and charter preschools.
- Niʻihau families: learn how children are raised only from the community's school in Kekaha and its own statements.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: test "for the sake of the children" against later generations' own memories.
- Chinese and Korean families of Honolulu: note which manners and language were kept through after-school classes.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: separate families raising children with grandparents in Ilocos or Cebu from those raising them here.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants on Maui and Hawaiʻi Island: ask how much of a child's upbringing a *padrinho* or *madrinha* took on.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese parents in Kalihi, Lāʻie and Hilo: hear how they describe raising children far from village elders, through church youth groups; Micronesian parenting has almost no literature.
- Haole and military families: compare nurture without nearby grandparents with local multigenerational care; paniolo families add ranch work as upbringing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** programs and surveys document nurture of the very young well; older Hawaiian practice rests on a few recorders; immigrant parenting is mostly in oral sources.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Pukui's terms and children's songs; Japanese for issei letters on child-rearing; Ilocano and Visayan for grandparents in the Philippines.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child-welfare findings, removal of Native Hawaiian and Micronesian children, and judgments of "at-risk" families are contested; give each side. Hālau teaching of children belongs to the hālau.
- **Held for review:** Japanese-language Sunday schools for children run by Buddhist missions were considered for moral formation and held as a category under the Buddhism question.
