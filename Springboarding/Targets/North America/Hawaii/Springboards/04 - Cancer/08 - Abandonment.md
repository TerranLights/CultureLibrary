*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare Kalaupapa families' accounts of separation with later accounts of the Kingdom's overthrow told by some as abandonment; keep each account separate.
- Niʻihau's people: departures from the island to Kauaʻi are the community's story; ask its school and leaders.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: ask whether issei parents who returned to Japan, leaving children in Hawaii, are remembered and how.
- Chinese families and societies: check whether laborers who never sent for wives appear in Chinatown rooming-house and society records, and how they are remembered.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare children left in the Philippines with children brought over later, by decade.
- Korean picture-bride families and Portuguese plantation families: check whether desertion appears in their community histories, and how households coped when it did.
- Samoan, Tongan and Micronesian families (Kalihi, Hilo): hear their own accounts of kin left in Samoa, Tonga, Chuuk or Majuro; sources are thin.
- Military families and haole transplants: compare partners and children left by deployments and moves; paniolo families add ranch closures.
- Hāmākua, Kaʻū, Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi: compare towns left by plantations and ranches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** Kalaupapa and plantation closures are richly documented; family desertion and elders alone are mostly in statistics and oral history; Micronesian accounts are rare.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kalaupapa petitions and chiefly traditions; Ilocano and Visayan for children left in the Philippines; Japanese for return-migration letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalaupapa remains home to a few surviving patients and holds their graves; follow Ka ʻOhana O Kalaupapa and the park's protocols. Calling the overthrow or Kalaupapa "abandonment" is one reading among several. Child removal and desertion law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the Kalaupapa, plantation and foster-care pointers raised nothing that had to be held.
