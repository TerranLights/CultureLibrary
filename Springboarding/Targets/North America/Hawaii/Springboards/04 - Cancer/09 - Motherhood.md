## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare grandmothers raising *hānai* children in Keaukaha and Waiʻanae with mothers in Hawaiian-medium schools; ask how each names a mother's role.
- Niʻihau mothers: birth and child-rearing there are the community's own; learn of them only through its school and leaders.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare picture-bride mothers with their nisei daughters as working mothers.
- Chinese and Korean mothers: test the late-arriving wives of laborers against those who never came.
- Filipino mothers (Ilocano and Visayan): separate mothers raising children here from those mothering from afar.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican mothers: look at devotion to Our Lady of Fátima and other Marian feasts as mothers' gatherings.
- Pacific Islander mothers in Kalihi, Lāʻie, Kona and Hilo: set Samoan and Tongan women's fellowships beside how Marshallese and Chuukese mothers reach prenatal care, asking the mothers themselves; little of the latter is written down.
- Haole and military mothers: compare mothers far from their own mothers with island multigenerational support; paniolo ranch mothers in Waimea add rural distance.
- Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Hāna mothers: who must fly to give birth, and who stays.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** maternal health and history are rich; Hawaiian and Okinawan birth customs survive in a few recorders and living practitioners; Micronesian mothers' accounts are scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for birth terms and Hina traditions; Japanese for *sanba* records; Samoan for church-fellowship materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *piko* and *ʻiewe* burial places are family-held; do not seek locations. Kapiʻolani's and the missionaries' programs are read differently by different accounts; give each. Mothers' legal rights belong to Libra; abortion to Aries.
- **Held for review:** empty; the questions here turned on birth customs, mothers' fellowships and maternity records, and the standing rules touched none of them.
