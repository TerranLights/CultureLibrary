## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in hālau, canoe clubs and Makahiki groups: check which displays are for the community and which for visitors.
- The people of Niʻihau (shell lei makers): ask only through the community's school and spokespeople.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants (Hilo, Mōʻiliʻili, Wahiawā): compare stage arts (taiko, classical dance, *eisaa*) by generation.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan (Waipahu, Kauaʻi, Maui): separate town-association display from church fiestas.
- Chinese, Korean, Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: compare festival courts, feast days and dance music.
- Samoan and Tongan communities (Lāʻie, Kalihi, Waiʻanae): check how *fiafia* nights and church fundraising shows are judged.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents (Kona, Kalihi): search church Christmas dance teams; print sources are few.
- Haole residents, military families and paniolo of Waimea and Upcountry Maui: compare clubs, rodeo and surf culture.
- Waikīkī against Hilo, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Hāna: test how tourism shapes local fun.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for hula, surf and festival history; mixed for measured leisure; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau expression.
- **Lands:** the tracker names only the USA as pre-war land and no scope has been written, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these Leo sheets take the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea as the core and fix no borders.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian to read chant texts and nūpepa festival notices; Samoan and Tongan for performance judging terms; Ilocano for town-association programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some chants and hālau practice are restricted to their lineages; go through the hālau and kumu, never around them. Tourist hula and the sale of Hawaiian culture are contested; give each view as an account.
- **Held for review:** one festival-season pointer was Buddhist and is held as a category (Buddhism); Shinto and Christian festival pointers were not held.
