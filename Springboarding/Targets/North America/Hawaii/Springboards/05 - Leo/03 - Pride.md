# Hawaii: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through community partners. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: separate pride in lineage, in *ʻāina* and in the Hawaiian Renaissance; check whether students of Kamehameha Schools and of Hawaiian immersion speak of pride in the same terms.
- People of Niʻihau: what is said of pride on Niʻihau should come only from the community's own spokespeople.
- Japanese descendants: weigh veterans' pride against the pride of later generations who never served.
- Okinawan descendants: check whether community histories tell pride as an answer to past slights, and from whom.
- Chinese and Korean families: look at pride in children's schooling and professions, as families write it in their histories.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: check pride in province of origin against pride in being local.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check how heritage-club newsletters respond to ethnic jokes.
- Samoan and Tongan residents of Lāʻie, Kalihi and Waiʻanae: look at sport and church as places of pride.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: search youth and church groups; sources are thin.
- Haole and military families; paniolo of Waimea: compare pride in a ranch name with pride in a unit.
- Neighbor Islands against Oʻahu: check whether Hilo and Kauaʻi sources set local pride against Honolulu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for music and sport pride; mixed for ethnic pride; thin for Micronesian residents.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the 1886 chants and the dictionary's examples; Japanese for veterans' Japanese-language papers; Samoan for Flag Day programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chiefly chants carry genealogies that families still hold; quote no more than titles. National pride (Hawaiian against American) is contested ground; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** empty; the pride-of-group pointers considered here (veterans, schools, festivals, brands) gave nothing to set aside.
