# Hawaii: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese through community organizations. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare chosen visibility (marches, hula on stage, language on signs) with chosen privacy (burials, family chants).
- The people of Niʻihau: an island long closed to visitors; check, through community spokespeople only, how its people regard outside cameras and attention.
- Former Kalaupapa patients and their families: check the line between being remembered and being shown.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: look for oral histories on *seken no me* in camp and town life.
- Chinese and Korean families: check how each appears in school textbooks and museum displays.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: compare visibility in politics and media with work in hotels and care homes.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check whether census and textbook categories once hid them among other groups.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check representation in data and news; Micronesian voices are scarce in print.
- Military families and haole newcomers: check how base life is seen from outside the gate, and how locals are seen from inside.
- Hāna, Lānaʻi and Kaʻū against Honolulu: test whether small towns know everyone by sight.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census and media representation; mixed for everyday gaze and greeting norms; thin for Micronesian residents.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the old *kapu* accounts in the original; Ilocano and Tagalog for baby-gaze beliefs; Chuukese and Marshallese with community interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalaupapa residents' privacy, Niʻihau's closed community and burial sites (*iwi kūpuna*) are not to be exposed; go through their own institutions. Census categories and blood-quantum debates are contested; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** empty; no item in the census, screen or gaze-belief pointers needed holding back.
