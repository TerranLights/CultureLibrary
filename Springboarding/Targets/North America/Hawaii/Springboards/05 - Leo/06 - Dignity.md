# Hawaii: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare dignity tied to genealogy and *ʻāina* with dignity claimed through sovereignty and through care of *iwi kūpuna*.
- People of Niʻihau: their terms for proper bearing should come from the community itself.
- Kalaupapa and Hale Mōhalu former patients and families: check their own accounts first, not outsiders' pity.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare internees' memoirs with veterans' and plantation memoirs on keeping one's bearing.
- Chinese and Korean families: look at mutual-aid societies' care for the old and the dead.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: compare plantation-era claims with today's care-home and hotel work.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check accounts of the *luna* (overseer) system and how each group recalls its treatment.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: look at church-led respect teaching and at advocacy accounts; Micronesian sources are few.
- Haole residents, military families and the paniolo of Waimea: compare dignity at work on ranch and base.
- Islands: the old of Molokaʻi and Lānaʻi who must fly to Honolulu for care, against those cared for at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in labor and internment memory and in care reports; thin in everyday address norms and Micronesian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Liliʻuokalani's Hawaiian-language entries; Japanese for internee writing; Ilocano for plantation-era pamphlets and letters; Marshallese with community interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burials and *iwi kūpuna* are protected by families and burial councils; reach them only through their protocols. The overthrow, Kalaupapa and the nuclear legacy are contested; give each account as an account. Assisted dying is debated; present each side.
- **Held for review:** empty; the care, labor and memoir pointers were checked and none was set aside.
