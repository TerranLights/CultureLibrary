*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare personal shame with collective humiliation tied to 1893 and 1898; check how *hoʻoponopono* and *mihi* are said to restore standing.
- People of Niʻihau: questions of shame inside the community are for its own spokespeople.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare wartime memoirs with Okinawan community histories, checking what each says about teasing and from whom.
- Chinese and Korean families: check press and memoirs for slurs met in school and work.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: compare plantation-era insults with today's "FOB" teasing; separate generations.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check whether family memoirs mention ethnic jokes and how the families answered them.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: look at schoolyard reports and church discipline; Micronesian sources are few.
- Haole residents and military families: check "haole" as a word of mockery against "local" as a word of belonging, from both sides.
- Pidgin speakers across all groups: test how classroom correction is remembered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in historical memory and comedy, thin in measured humiliation outside schools.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for 1898 accounts in the nūpepa; Japanese for wartime memoirs; Tagalog and Ilocano for students' essays; Chuukese for school-family testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow and annexation are contested; give each account as an account. Ethnic jokes and slurs are sensitive: quote no slur beyond what is needed to name it. Hazing and bullying cases involve minors and victims; use published reports only.
- **Held for review:** empty; the joke, school and wartime pointers were weighed and none was set aside.
