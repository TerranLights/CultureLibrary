## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare giving tied to *ʻohana* and *ahupuaʻa* sharing with the aliʻi trusts' giving; check how *hoʻokupu* is offered today and to whom.
- People of Niʻihau and their relatives in Kekaha: any account of sharing should come through the community.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare return-gift customs with gifts to churches and community halls.
- Chinese and Korean families: check society scholarships and church building funds.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: weigh gifts sent home against giving in Hawaii.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check feast-day meals and who pays for them.
- Samoan and Tongan residents: check how givers, pastors and critics within each community describe church giving.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: look at funeral and church contributions; few sources exist.
- Haole residents, old island families and newcomers: compare named foundations with anonymous giving.
- Military families: check unit drives and base volunteering.
- Neighbor Islands against Oʻahu: compare giving after Hurricane ʻIniki (Kauaʻi) and the Lahaina fire (Maui).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal giving (tax data, foundations, blood and organ donation); informal sharing and remittances are undercounted; church giving is mostly unpublished.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Kingdom's hospital subscription notices; Tongan and Samoan for church giving lists; Ilocano and Visayan for hometown-association reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bishop trust's governance is disputed; give each account as an account (law itself: Libra and Capricorn). Lahaina fire funds are contested; quote reports, not rumor. Church giving within Pacific Islander families is a private matter; ask, do not assume.
- **Held for review:** empty; the trust, donor and church-giving pointers were reviewed and nothing was held.
