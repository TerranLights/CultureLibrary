*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare chiefly models, church *kahu*, kumu hula and elected trustees; check how each is chosen and set aside.
- People of Niʻihau: who leads there is a community matter; use only what its spokespeople publish.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the *sewayaku* of clubs with elected office-holders.
- Chinese and Korean families: compare society presidents and church elders; check for faction fights.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: compare town-association presidents with union and political leaders.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check who led clubs, bands and festa committees.
- Samoan and Tongan residents: separate *matai* and church leadership, and check how youth leaders are chosen.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check whether island-of-origin titles carry over; sources are thin.
- Haole residents, military families and paniolo ranch managers: compare leadership by rank and by ownership with island habits of leading.
- Islands: Molokaʻi and Lānaʻi community leaders against Honolulu's political leaders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political leaders and memoirs; mixed for community leadership; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau groups.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Desha's original serial; Korean for the Rhee factions' papers; Samoan and Tongan for church and *matai* records; Chuukese and Marshallese with interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** OHA voting rules and sovereignty leadership are contested; give each account as an account. Hālau and family leadership is governed by its own protocols; respect them.
- **Held for review:** empty; the political, church and lineage pointers looked at here raised nothing to hold.
