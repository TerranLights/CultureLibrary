# Hawaii: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese with community help. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare honor owed to aliʻi and *iwi kūpuna*, honor in sovereignty struggles, and honor in sport and fighting.
- People of Niʻihau: their codes of conduct are internal; use only what the community publishes.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare honor in obituaries and family names with honor in veterans' memory; separate the two groups.
- Chinese and Korean families: check honor to forebears in society records and memorials.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: weigh eskrima codes and *puri* in family talk against plantation-era accounts.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check feast-day vows (*promessa*) kept as a matter of honor.
- Samoan and Tongan residents: compare *tatau*, title and church office as sources of honor.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how elders are honored at funerals and feasts; little is printed.
- Military families and veterans of every group: check unit honor against island ties.
- Paniolo of Waimea and Upcountry Maui: the given word in cattle dealing.
- Hilo and Waiʻanae fight gyms against Honolulu's: test how respect is earned in each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for military and royal honor and for epic tradition; thin for everyday honor codes and Micronesian groups.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Kamapuaʻa text and the Wilcox-era nūpepa; Japanese for the *Hawaii Hochi*; Samoan for *tatau* terms; Tagalog and Ilocano for eskrima teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Royal Mausoleum and burial matters are under their *kahu* and families; seek leave before any visit or question. Family honor tied to women's conduct is sensitive; research it in the words of those who lived it. The 1889 and 1895 risings are contested; give each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** empty; the burial, sport and family pointers considered here gave nothing to set aside.
