## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare joy in hula, song and canoe with joy in worship; check how *hauʻoli* and *leʻa* are used in each setting.
- People of Niʻihau: hymn-singing and gatherings should be studied only through the community's own spokespeople.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: New Year customs and stage music; separate the two groups.
- Chinese and Korean families: Lunar New Year and church celebrations; check where *sinmyeong* appears in Korean drumming groups.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: Christmas novenas, fiestas and family parties, by town of origin.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: dance music and feast days.
- Samoan and Tongan residents: dance, *fakapale* and church choir festivals.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: birthdays, church Christmas and youth nights; print sources are few.
- Haole residents, military families and North Shore surfers: compare surf stoke, base celebrations and holiday parades.
- Paniolo of Waimea and Upcountry Maui: rodeo days and ranch parties.
- Hilo, Molokaʻi and Hāna against Waikīkī: test whether joy is sought in local gatherings or in visitor entertainment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals and music; mixed for measured happiness; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau celebrations.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for hymn texts and chant notes; Tongan for *tauʻolunga* and *māfana* talk; Tagalog and Ilocano for parish programs; Marshallese for *kemem*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** celebration after the Lahaina fire is contested; give each account as an account. Funerals are private; use published obituaries and families' own posts. The "happy islands" image sold to visitors is disputed; set residents' accounts beside it.
