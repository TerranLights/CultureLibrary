# Hawaii: The domain: work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands, with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (with the Niʻihau speech), Hawaiʻi Creole English (Pidgin), the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** what good work and a well-kept life are: work, service, the daily round, the kept house, craft, rest and health.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in loʻi kalo, fishpond and shore fishing (Waipiʻo, Keʻanae, Hāna, east Molokaʻi): check how subsistence work and wage jobs are combined and named.
- Niʻihau families, on the island and in Kekaha: ranch and shell work; ask only through the community's own spokespeople.
- Plantation-era descendants (Japanese, Okinawan, Chinese, Ilocano and Visayan Filipino, Korean, Portuguese, Puerto Rican): compare by camp and generation what the plantation day left behind.
- Paniolo families of Waimea, Upcountry Maui and Molokaʻi ranches: set the ranch calendar beside the plantation one.
- Samoan and Tongan communities (Lāʻie, Kalihi, Waiʻanae): check whether church and family duties are counted as work.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents (Kona coffee farms, Kalihi, Hilo): check which jobs open to them and what their own groups report; print is thin.
- Haole residents (old kamaʻāina families, recent arrivals, remote workers) and military households at Pearl Harbor, Schofield and Kāneʻohe Bay: check how each group's hours are set.
- Visitor-industry workers of Waikīkī, Kāʻanapali and the Kohala coast, and Lānaʻi under one landowner: compare shifts, commutes and a single employer's hold on the day.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for plantation labor and wages; mixed for the household round; thin in print for Micronesian, Niʻihau and paniolo daily life.
- **Lands:** no `00 - Scope.md` has been written and the tracker carries only the USA as pre-war land, so Hawaii's exact lands are still to be settled; the Virgo sheets are written for the Hawaiian Islands, the main islands with Papahānaumokuākea, and draw no borders.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa fishing and planting columns; Japanese for camp-era newspapers and work songs; Ilocano for *sakada* letters and association papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kapu-era purity and the *kauwā* (outcast) class are known from recorded accounts and touch living families. Planters' and workers' descendants tell plantation history differently; give each as an account.
- **Held for review:** one pointer on the yearly round of observances was set aside as a category (Buddhism); the Christian and Shinto observances weighed beside it were kept.
