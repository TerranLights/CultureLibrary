# Hawaii: Service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; the English of Hawaii and Pidgin; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese for church and community service. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: check how *kahu* as attendant and *kahu* as pastor sit together, and how service to aliʻi trusts, civic clubs and churches is described.
- Samoan and Tongan families (Lāʻie, Kalihi, Waiʻanae): compare the words elders and island-born youth use for service to chiefs and church.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the wartime generation's accounts of service with their grandchildren's.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Visayan: check service in hotel and hospital jobs against church and hometown-association service.
- Latter-day Saint members in Lāʻie and Lāʻie's student workers: check how mission service and paid work blur.
- Chinese and Korean societies and churches: check elder homes and church kitchens as service.
- Portuguese descendants: check how a *promessa* is repaid by serving a feast.
- Hotel workers of Waikīkī, Kāʻanapali and the Kohala coast: compare what trainers teach with what housekeepers and cooks report.
- Military families: check how "service" frames the household and how civilian neighbors receive it.
- Marshallese and Chuukese congregations (Kalihi, Kona, Hilo): public data are sparse; ask church leaders.
- Kalaupapa residents and Niʻihau families: small communities; go only through their own spokespeople.
- Haole residents: compare old-firm boards and club service with newcomers' volunteering.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, hospitality and volunteering data; mixed for church service; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau service.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for church and court records; Samoan and Tongan for congregation minutes; Japanese for wartime community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *kauwā* touches a recorded outcast class whose descendants are living; avoid speculation. Hospitality's use of Hawaiian values is contested; give each view as an account. Kalaupapa's records include patients' private lives.
- **Held for review:** one pointer on organized volunteering by a religious women's group was set aside as a category (Buddhism).
