# Hawaii: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Chuukese and Marshallese with community interpreters. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or a piece of knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: check the roles given to kūpuna in hālau, canoe clubs and schools, and when someone is called *kaumaha* to others.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare elders' talk of not wanting to be *meiwaku* with how their children describe caring for them.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check what household roles are given to grandparents brought over under family reunification.
- Chinese and Korean families: check elders' places in family shops and restaurants.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check backyard gardens, ovens and repair skills as remembered from camp life.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check how *aogā* and *ʻaonga* are used of young men and women in church and family.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how language and job barriers shape who gets called useful; sources are thin.
- Disabled residents and self-advocates: compare workshop records with their own accounts.
- Houseless residents who redeem cans: check how they name the work.
- Haole retirees and second-home owners: check volunteering as a claim to belong.
- Retired military families: check second careers and how they are framed.
- Rural Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, Hāna and Kaʻū: check whether practical skill carries more standing than in Honolulu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: disability and employment data exist, but feeling useful is mostly in oral history, memoir and press.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the moon-night names and kūpuna program materials; Japanese for issei and nisei elders' writings on burden; Tagalog and Ilocano for elders' accounts in Filipino families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** disability, old age and houselessness need consent and care; never describe a group as useful or useless. What kūpuna teach in hālau is their own to share.
- **Held for review:** empty; moon-calendar records, school programs, disability bodies and reuse groups were weighed, and none needed setting aside.
