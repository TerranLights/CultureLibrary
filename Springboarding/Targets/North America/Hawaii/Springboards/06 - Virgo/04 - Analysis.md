## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli navigators, healers, fishpond keepers and Native Hawaiian scientists: check how each describes observing, testing and naming.
- Heirs of the Satawal navigation lineage and Chuukese residents: check who may teach what, and how credit is given.
- Japanese and Chinese descendants in family firms: check the abacus and ledger as a household skill across generations.
- Filipino, Samoan and Tongan families: check how science and accounting careers are discussed at home and in church.
- Korean residents: check church and small-business record keeping.
- Haole residents in the scientific workforce (observatories, labs, UH): check how their analysis is received locally.
- Military planners and shipyard engineers: check the military as a large employer of analysts.
- Hilo and Waimea on Hawaiʻi Island: check how residents of observatory towns talk about the science above them.
- Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Niʻihau: data are thin; check whether local knowledge of weather and sea is recorded at all.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science, statistics and audit records; mixed for Hawaiian reckoning (recorded but scattered); thin for household accounting among most groups.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the wind lists and recorded counting; Japanese and Cantonese for ledgers and abacus-school materials; Chuukese for navigation terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** voyaging knowledge belongs in part to Satawal's lineages, and healing knowledge to its practitioners; ask, do not extract. Science on Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa is contested ground; give each account. The Charlie Chan figure is disputed.
- **Held for review:** empty; the wayfinding, statistics and observatory pointers here are institutions and published works, and none needed holding.
