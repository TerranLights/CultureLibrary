# Hawaii: Discernment (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through community groups. **Concept in one line:** the ability to tell things apart and judge well: true from false, good from bad, fine from coarse, the trustworthy from the untrustworthy, what matters from what doesn't.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli fishers, healers and chant practitioners: check how naming, signs and practice train the eye; much is held by families and teachers.
- Kona coffee households (Japanese-descended farm families, Micronesian pickers, newcomer estate owners): compare who judges quality and in what words.
- Fish buyers and longline owners of Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and haole origin: check how grading skill is learned and trusted.
- Chinese and Korean shopkeepers and shoppers of Chinatown and Keʻeaumoku: check judging goods at market and in the store.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check how elders advise on partners and money, and which scams target newcomers.
- Samoan and Tongan elders and pastors: check how counsel on choices is given and weighed.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check exposure to housing and job fraud through community groups; sources are thin.
- Catholic, Congregational and Latter-day Saint members: compare teaching on knowing a calling or an answer to prayer.
- North Shore, Mākaha and Hilo surfers and lifeguards: check how reading the ocean is taught to newcomers.
- Haole residents and visitors: check judgments of "real local" food and places, and of tourist traps.
- Molokaʻi and Niʻihau communities: check, only through their own spokespeople, how newcomers and researchers are vetted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food, coffee and fish trades; mixed for scams; thin for spiritual discernment outside the churches and for Micronesian residents.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for rain names and signs; Japanese for coffee-farm and fish-market terms; Tagalog and Ilocano for elders' advice and scam warnings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family signs and *ʻike pāpālua* are often private; ask, never collect them. Speech prejudice touches living people's jobs; give each study as a study. The coffee fraud involves named people; keep to court and press records.
- **Held for review:** empty; taste, trade, fraud and church sources were weighed for this concept, and none required holding.
