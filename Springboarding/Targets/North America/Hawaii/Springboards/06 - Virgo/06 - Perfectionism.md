# Hawaii: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, with the Niʻihau speech; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese for weaving terms. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli makers (featherwork, kapa, lauhala, chant): check how each speaks of exactness and of the maker's mark; teaching belongs to the teachers.
- Niʻihau speakers and university-trained speakers: check how "correct" Hawaiian is argued; hear Niʻihau's side only through its spokespeople.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare graded arts with workplace standards; check pressure by generation without assuming it.
- Chinese and Korean families: check school pressure as students themselves describe it.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check pressure around nursing and licensing exams.
- Samoan and Tongan women's groups: check how fine work is judged and who may judge it.
- Marshallese weavers (*jaki-ed*): few sources; check community craft fairs.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check feast preparations and baking as standards kept.
- Waimea and Upcountry Maui saddle makers and lei makers: check paniolo craft standards.
- North Shore and Kauaʻi surfers and shapers: check talk of the perfect board and wave.
- Haole newcomers and visitors: check the paradise ideal brought in and how locals respond.
- Military households: check base inspection standards at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for crafts and arts; mixed for school pressure; thin for measured perfectionism by group, and for Micronesian and Niʻihau crafts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for featherwork and chant terms; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for graded-arts ranks; Tongan and Samoan for weaving standards.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chant and hālau standards are held by kumu; temple-ritual records touch sacred matters. Student distress and eating disorders need care; never assign perfectionism to a group. The Niʻihau speech is the community's own.
- **Held for review:** empty; the craft, arts and school pointers were checked, and no temple, scholar or community needed setting aside.
