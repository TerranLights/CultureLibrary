# Hawaii: Criticism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese for community replies. **Concept in one line:** pointing out faults or judging the merits of people, work, institutions or oneself, and the forms this takes: feedback, critique, complaint, reproach, satire, dissent and self-criticism.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: check who may correct whom in families, hālau and civic clubs, and the line of public dissent from the 1890s press to today's sovereignty groups.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check *hansei* and indirect correction across generations; test, do not assume.
- Ilocano and Visayan Filipino residents: check how criticism moves in town associations and the community press.
- Chinese and Korean societies and churches: check how internal disputes were aired or kept inside.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check teasing and ethnic jokes as correction in camp memories, and who resented them.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: check who may criticize a pastor or chief, and where (*fale aitu*, family meetings).
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how public criticism of their communities is answered and by whom.
- Haole newcomers and remote workers: check whether they report being told their criticism is too blunt, and how locals describe the exchange.
- Military families and Red Hill's neighbors in Moanalua, ʻAliamanu and Hālawa: compare their accounts of the leak and its handling.
- Lahaina residents: check criticism of officials and of the rebuilding after 2023, each as an account.
- Niʻihau: criticism of outsiders' access is the community's own to voice; ask its spokespeople.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press, cartoons and public testimony; mixed for satire; thin for family and workplace criticism, which lives in memoir and talk.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the 1890s opposition press and chiefly histories; Samoan for *fale aitu*; Japanese for self-criticism in family writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Bishop trust dispute, Red Hill and Lahaina are contested; give each side. Correction inside a hālau is the kumu's. Online attacks on Micronesian residents are hurtful; quote little, cite much.
- **Held for review:** empty; the press, satire and online pointers weighed here needed no holding.
