# Hawaii: Health (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through clinic interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the state of body and mind that a culture counts as well, and how it is kept, lost and restored, by whom, and at whose cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare families who use *lāʻau lapaʻau* and *lomilomi* with those who rely on clinics, and check how each explains the cause of an illness.
- Kalaupapa residents and their families: hear the Hansen's disease history only through the community and its own groups.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check how food, rest and *yōjō* are discussed at home; never assume long life as a trait.
- Chinese descendants: check memory of the 1900 fire and the use of herb shops by generation.
- Ilocano and Visayan Filipino residents: compare *hilot* with nursing careers in the same families.
- Korean residents: check herbal clinics and church health ministries.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check blessing cures and the remedies remembered from camp days.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check *fofō* and *faitoʻo* alongside church health programs.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: sources are thin; look to clinic and community reports in Kalihi, Hilo and Kona.
- Niʻihau families: distance from care; ask only through their spokespeople.
- Military families and haole newcomers: compare the military health system and the visitor wellness trade with residents' care.
- Rural Kaʻū, Hāna, Molokaʻi and Lānaʻi: check distance to hospitals and how people manage it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemics, Kalaupapa and measured health; mixed for folk healing; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau health in print.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for remedies and Kingdom health records; Cantonese for herb-shop practice; Ilocano, Samoan and Tongan for healers' own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing knowledge, *ʻanāʻanā* and burial matters are family-held or restricted; ask practitioners and Papa Ola Lōkahi's healers' council, never work around them. Kalaupapa records hold patients' private lives. The plague-fire blame and the "genocide" framing of epidemics are contested; give each account.
- **Held for review:** empty; clinic, healer and epidemic sources were weighed for this concept, and none fell under a held category.
