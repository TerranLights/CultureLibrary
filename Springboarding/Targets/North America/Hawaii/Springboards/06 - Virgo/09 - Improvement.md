# Hawaii: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** the Hawaiian Islands (the main islands with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese with community help. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare loʻi and fishpond restoration (Heʻeia, Molokaʻi, Waipiʻo) with the schemes that drained them; check who calls what betterment.
- Japanese descendants: set Okumura's program and the wartime campaigns beside nisei memories of them.
- Okinawan descendants: check whether uplift campaigns addressed them apart, and their answers.
- Chinese residents and families moved by the Kukui project: check their own accounts.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check what *asenso* means here and in houses built back home (comparison only).
- Korean residents: check self-development through church and school.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: trace the climb from camp to owned house in family accounts.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: check how *alualu i luma* and *fakalakalaka* are used in youth programs.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how others' plans to "help" them are received; print is thin.
- Haole residents: compare planters' and developers' talk of improvement with newcomers' wellness retreats.
- Military: check base expansions called improvements, and neighbors' views.
- Waikīkī, Kakaʻako, Hilo and Lahaina residents: remade places, and who decided what "better" meant.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public works, plans and mission records; mixed for personal self-improvement and change across generations; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for chiefly histories and replies to the mission; Japanese for Okumura's writings and nisei responses; Ilocano and Tagalog for *asenso* stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mission "improvement", the taro patents and redevelopment are contested; give each side. The taro dispute cites the Hāloa genealogy; read it as its Kānaka Maoli tellers give it, not as a crop question. Lahaina's rebuilding after the 2023 fire: give residents' and officials' accounts. Patent, ordinance and land-title texts go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** on betterment across generations, a single source was left out of the table and stands here by category: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands.
