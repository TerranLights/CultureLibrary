# Hawaii: The domain: partnership and justice (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of Hawaii and Pidgin; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, including the Niʻihau speech; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** what a fair bargain and a good partnership are: self and other, bonds between equals, contracts and treaties, open enemies, settling disputes, civility, marriage and gender ways, and the letter of the law.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in homestead areas (Waimānalo, Nānākuli, Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua): check how Hawaiian Home Lands lease and succession rules shape marriage choices.
- People of Niʻihau, on the island and in Kekaha: ask through the community itself how quarrels and marriages are handled on a privately owned island.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants (Hilo, Waipahu, Mōʻiliʻili): compare go-between marriage with later choices, by generation.
- Ilocano and Visayan Filipino families (Waipahu, Kalihi, Lānaʻi City): check marriages arranged across the ocean against present practice.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: compare society rules for disputes with church-centered ones.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families (Upcountry Maui, Hilo): check godparent ties as bonds between equals.
- Samoan and Tongan families of Lāʻie, Kalihi and Waiʻanae: check what chiefly titles and church councils settle before courts do.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents (Kona, Hilo, Kalihi): check which disputes stay within clan and church; print is scarce.
- Haole residents and military families (Schofield, Kāneʻohe): check the state's courts against military justice and family advocacy.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for written law and Kingdom legal history; mixed for marriage customs by group; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau dispute ways.
- **Lands:** the tracker carries only the USA as pre-war land, so Hawaii's exact lands remain to be settled; the Libra sheets take the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea as the core and draw no lines.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kingdom codes and court files, which sometimes differ from the English; Japanese for marriage notices in camp-era papers; Samoan for *fono* minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow, annexation and the Kingdom's legal status are contested; give each reading as an account. Genealogy claims and burial disputes involve family-held knowledge; go through the families.
- **Held for review:** a wedding-rite pointer was set aside as a category (Buddhism); the church and civil rites beside it were kept.
