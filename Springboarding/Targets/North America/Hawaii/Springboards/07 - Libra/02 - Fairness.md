# Hawaii: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli taro farmers of Waiāhole, Waikāne and Keʻanae: check how ditch water is divided in practice beside what the water code says.
- Kānaka Maoli applicants for homestead leases, on Oʻahu and on Molokaʻi: compare readings of the waitlist settlement.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check how the 1909 strike and its equal-pay demand are retold in families and in group histories.
- Filipino residents, especially Ilocano and Visayan families on Kauaʻi and Maui: check memory of the 1924 wage campaign and of later union contracts.
- Portuguese, Puerto Rican, Chinese and Korean descendants: check where each stood on the plantation pay ladder and how that is remembered.
- Micronesian residents (Chuukese, Marshallese) in Hilo, Kona and Kalihi: check experience of equal treatment in clinics and schools; community groups hold most of the record.
- Surfers of the North Shore, Waikīkī and Makaha: compare local and visiting surfers' accounts of wave turns.
- Haole and kamaʻāina families: check views of Hawaiian preferences in schools and trusts, without assuming any one view.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, labor and water records; mixed for everyday turn-taking and inheritance; thin for Micronesian, Tongan and Niʻihau practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the fishing laws and nūpepa letters on water; Japanese for 1909 strike leaflets; Ilocano for 1924 strike papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Hawaiian preferences, the homestead queue, water diversion and Lahaina land deals are contested; present each account as one. Some ʻauwai rules are kept by farming families; ask before recording.
- **Held for review:** none; the inheritance and wage pointers considered drew on church, union and family records, so nothing was set aside under the standing rules.
