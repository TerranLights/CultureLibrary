# Hawaii: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families: check how *hoapaʻa* and *hui* are used today against their older senses, in rural Hāna and Molokaʻi and in town.
- People of Niʻihau: check through the community how marriage and partnership are arranged on a closed island; nothing public should be assumed.
- Chinese-Hawaiian and Japanese-Hawaiian families: compare what intermarriage meant for land, language and church in each.
- Okinawan and Japanese families: compare picture marriage, *nakōdo* matches and later free choice by decade and island.
- Filipino residents: separate Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog sponsor networks; check whether *kumpare* bonds cross groups.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: check godparent ties in Upcountry Maui and Hilo parishes.
- Samoan and Tongan Latter-day Saint and Congregational families: compare marriage as covenant with marriage as a bond between kin groups.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check church weddings and clan consent; print sources are few, so start with congregations.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and ʻUlupalakua: compare ranch partnership with marriage alliance.
- Haole residents, including mission descendants and newcomers: compare old business partnerships with present couples' arrangements.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marriage statistics, business history and intermarriage studies; mixed for ritual kin; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau marriage.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for hui deeds and chiefly alliance accounts; Cantonese for firm and society records; Korean for picture-bride letters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chiefly genealogies and *punalua* touch family-held knowledge; go through the families and their own institutions. Temple sealing records are held under church rules.
- **Held for review:** none; the co-parent and sponsor bonds weighed for this concept come from Catholic and Protestant parishes, which the standing rules leave open.
