# Hawaii: Balance (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; English of Hawaii and Pidgin; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** the even weighting of two or more forces, parties or demands so that none overwhelms the others; and the steadiness that results, in a person, a household, a society or the land.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli practitioners and stewards (Heʻeia, Keʻanae, Waipiʻo, Hāʻena): check how *lōkahi* is used in restoration work against its use in health programs.
- Kānaka Maoli and astronomy workers on Hawaiʻi Island: compare how each describes the balance struck on Mauna Kea, without merging them into two camps.
- Upcountry Maui farmers and Hāna taro growers: compare readings of the 2018 water decisions.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check food-as-medicine practice, and the plantation day's long hours against rest days, by generation.
- Chinese descendants: check hot-cold food ideas in Chinatown families.
- Ilocano and Visayan Filipino families: check *pasma* and the role of healers alongside clinics.
- In Samoan and Tongan households of Lāʻie, Kalihi and Waiʻanae, check whether *vā* language is used for keeping church, family and work in balance.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check health-balance ideas through clinic staff and community workers; little is published.
- Haole and military households: check work-leave patterns and how deployment strains them.
- Residents of Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi: compare a single landowner's island and a low-tourism island for balance of jobs and land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, water rulings and election data; mixed for health and body balance; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau ideas.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the moon-night *kapu* and 1887 debates in the nūpepa; Cantonese for herbal-shop practice; Tongan for *tā-vā* writings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** healing practice (*lāʻau lapaʻau*) is often family-held; go through practitioners' councils. Mauna Kea, East Maui water and 1887 are contested; present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** none; balance-in-the-body pointers from Chinese, Filipino and Okinawan healing were all kept, as no standing rule bars them.
