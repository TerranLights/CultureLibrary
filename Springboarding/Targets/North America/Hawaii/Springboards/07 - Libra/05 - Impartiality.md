*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: check how OHA, the Hawaiian Home Lands commission and land trusts handle conflicts of interest where many people share kin ties.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the memory of the territorial courts (the 1928 case, wartime provost courts) with later service on the bench.
- Filipino residents: check *palakasan* in workplaces and civil service, separating Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog speakers.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: check how society and church leadership seats are filled.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: compare who rose to foreman on the plantations, from personnel files and oral records.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check how *matai* standing or church rank plays in public jobs and community boards.
- Chuukese and Marshallese residents in court: check interpreter availability by island; ask the courts and community groups, since numbers are seldom published.
- Neighbor-island residents (Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, Kauaʻi): check recusal where every judge, juror and party knows each other.
- Haole residents, newcomers and military families: compare how they describe being outsiders to island networks, without generalizing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for ethics rules, court rules and scandals; mixed for everyday favoritism; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau experience of courts and offices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kingdom jury laws and court reports; Japanese for 1928 trial coverage; Tagalog for *palakasan* talk; interpreter-certified Chuukese for court access.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** OHA governance, the Kealoha case and resident pricing are argued over; present each side as an account. Living families from the 1928 case and the Kealoha case should not be approached without care.
- **Held for review:** none; the court-access pointers considered for interpreters and juries raised no item to set aside.
