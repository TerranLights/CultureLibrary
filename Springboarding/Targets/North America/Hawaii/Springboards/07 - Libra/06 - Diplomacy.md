# Hawaii: Diplomacy (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; English of Hawaii and Pidgin; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Samoan and Tongan; Tagalog and Ilocano; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** managing relations and disagreements between parties by talk, tact and negotiation rather than force: between nations and peoples, and in everyday social life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli sovereignty groups and Hawaiian civic clubs: compare positions on federal recognition, Naʻi Aupuni and independence, each in its own words.
- Kānaka Maoli residents near military training land (Mākua Valley in Waiʻanae; Pōhakuloa on Hawaiʻi Island): check how they describe talks with the Army.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare community ties to Japan and to Okinawa prefecture, and how relief and exchange are remembered.
- Samoan and Tongan communities: check how *talanoa* and *soalaupule* language enters dealings with churches, schools and the state.
- Filipino residents, Ilocano and Tagalog speakers: check the consulate's place and hometown associations' links to the Philippines.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: check the independence-era diplomacy of the Korean community and the Chinese societies' role as go-betweens with the Kingdom.
- Compact renewals as followed by Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check who speaks for them locally in the talks; few published sources.
- Military families and haole residents of Kailua and ʻEwa: check how base-community councils describe relations.
- The people of Niʻihau: check through the community's own representatives whether and how it deals with outside agencies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kingdom diplomacy and the overthrow inquiries; mixed for state international ties; thin for everyday tact by group and for Micronesian voices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian and French for the 1843 records; Japanese for the 1881 and 1886 correspondence; Samoan for the 1887 embassy's reception.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow, recognition and sovereignty, Red Hill and Mauna Kea are contested; present every account as an account. Protocols of arrival and entry belong to hālau and families.
- **Held for review:** none; the consular and exchange pointers weighed here did not involve any group or source under the standing rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Hawaii State Archives catalog: "Foreign Office and Executive" series, 1843 and 1887 folders.
2. Library catalog or HathiTrust: "Blount report" 1893 and "Morgan report" 1894; read the two summaries side by side.
3. Federal Register: 43 CFR part 50 (2016), with public comments from the islands.
4. East-West Center site: "Pacific Islands Conference of Leaders" meeting reports.
5. Library catalog: Okinawan community history of Hawaii, "pigs" 1948 relief.
