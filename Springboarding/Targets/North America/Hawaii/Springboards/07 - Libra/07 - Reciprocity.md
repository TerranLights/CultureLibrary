# Hawaii: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Korean; Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli fishing and farming families (Molokaʻi, Kaʻū, Keʻanae): compare how shares of a catch are returned with what the 1994 report recorded.
- Kānaka Maoli in protocol settings: check what *hoʻokupu* now means at gatherings and who receives it, through the groups who give it.
- Japanese descendants: compare *kōden* and return-gift customs among Christian and other families, by generation.
- Okinawan descendants: check *moai* membership by age and neighborhood.
- Filipino residents (Ilocano, Visayan): check how a debt of gratitude is returned to the sponsor who brought a family over.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: check labor exchange in plantation-era households and favor-keeping in society life.
- Portuguese families: check the Holy Ghost feast's free meal as repayment of a vow.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check the weight of contributions to family events on households in Lāʻie and Kalihi.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check obligations to kin in the home islands; sources are few, so start with church leaders.
- Paniolo, haole and military households: check how newcomers learn, or miss, island return customs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the treaty and Kingdom history; mixed for household exchange; thin for Micronesian, Korean and Niʻihau customs.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *kuapo* and *hoʻokupu* in the nūpepa; Japanese for kōden ledgers and society minutes; Uchinaaguchi terms for *moai* talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the treaty's role in annexation is contested; present each reading as an account. *Hoʻokupu* in protocol and in hālau belongs to its keepers. Family ledgers are private.
- **Held for review:** a funeral-rite pointer on return gifts was set aside as a category (Buddhism); the ledgers kept by families of every faith were retained.
