# Hawaii: Indecision (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of Hawaii and Pidgin; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Tagalog and Ilocano; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** difficulty or failure in reaching a decision: weighing too long, wavering, putting a choice off, or leaving it to others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli across sovereignty positions: compare how the recognition question has been kept open, and whether that is called a choice or a failure, each in its own words.
- Kānaka Maoli of Hawaiʻi Island near Mauna Kea and Kona: check how waiting on the telescope is described by different families.
- Japanese descendants: check whether family choices on caring for a parent are made by the eldest, all siblings, or a family meeting.
- Okinawan descendants: check use of *yuta* and of family councils by generation.
- Filipino residents (Ilocano, Visayan, Tagalog): check how hard choices are framed in family and church settings.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check whether chiefs, elders or pastors are expected to settle a pending family choice.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check clan-elder decision ways as they apply in Hawaii; published material is scarce.
- Patients' families at Kalaupapa and in Honolulu hospitals: check how surrogate consensus works in practice.
- Haole residents, military families and Portuguese descendants: compare views of the rail decision across Kapolei, Kāneʻohe and town.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public decisions, votes and statutes; mixed for family decision ways; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kamakau's original and the Privy Council's Hawaiian minutes; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi for *yuta* accounts; Tagalog for family-choice language.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recognition, the telescope and Kakaʻako Makai are contested; present each as an account. *Yuta* consultations and family end-of-life choices are private; *kilokilo* is a recorded practice, not a pointer to use.
- **Held for review:** none; the end-of-life and consultation pointers weighed here came from statutes, hospice records and family accounts, and none fell under a held category.
