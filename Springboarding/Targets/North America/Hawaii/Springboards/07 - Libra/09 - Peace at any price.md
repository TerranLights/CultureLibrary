# Hawaii: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese; Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare readings of 1893 and 1895 (yielding as wisdom, as sacrifice, or as loss), and the 2019 *kapu aloha* as a discipline of nonviolent conduct rather than giving way.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare views of the Emergency Service Committee among veterans' families and families of the interned.
- Chinese descendants: check family memory of the 1900 claims and what was accepted.
- Filipino residents (Ilocano, Visayan): check accounts of plantation strikes, when to hold out and when to settle.
- Korean descendants: check how independence-era factions settled or kept quarrels inside the community.
- Among Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants, check what workers' kin say was left unsaid to plantation bosses.
- In Samoan and Tongan congregations, check when a grievance is set aside for the group's sake; ask through church leaders.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check whether discrimination is reported or absorbed; ask community health workers, since figures are rare.
- Military families and residents of Waiʻanae: compare the Mākua settlement as each side tells it.
- Haole residents of old firms and newcomers: check how workplace complaints are raised or dropped.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1893–95, wartime and legal settlements; mixed for everyday conflict avoidance; thin for Micronesian, Korean and Niʻihau practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the 1887 press and Makahiki accounts; Japanese for the committee's community papers; Cantonese for fire-claims petitions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the queen's yielding, the 1887 constitution and wartime accommodation are contested; give each as an account. Abuse and violence records concern living survivors; use published reports only.
- **Held for review:** wartime closures of language schools and of one religious community's institutions were weighed; the latter was set aside as a category (Buddhism), and the language-school records were kept.

## 5. First queries
1. Hawaii State Archives: Liliʻuokalani's 17 January 1893 statement and 1895 abdication; then the Blount and Morgan files for each side's reading.
2. UH Mānoa Library: Hawaii War Records Depository, "Emergency Service Committee" folders.
3. Hawaii State Archives: "Court of Fire Claims" 1901 case files.
4. Department of Health: Hawaii PRAMS data reports, partner-violence items.
5. Hawaii DOE: Hawaii Administrative Rules, Chapter 19, current and earlier versions.
