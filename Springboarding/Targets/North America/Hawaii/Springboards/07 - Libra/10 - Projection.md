# Hawaii: Projection (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English of Hawaii and Pidgin; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** seeing in others (partners, rivals, enemies, outsiders) what one disowns in oneself: one's own faults, impulses, force or fears; and how a culture places its own unwanted traits and troubles on an outside "other".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli: compare how the overthrow and land loss are laid at the doors of missionaries, planters or the U.S., and how the churches answered.
- Japanese descendants: compare accounts of wartime suspicion by Issei, Nisei veterans and the families of internees.
- Okinawan descendants: check memory of being othered by other Japanese immigrants on the plantations, from the community's own histories.
- For Chinese descendants, check what families recall of the 1880s laws and of the disease once named for them.
- Filipino residents (Ilocano, Visayan): check how plantation-era blame for strikes and crime fell on them, and their own counter-accounts.
- Ask Marshallese and Chuukese organizations how their members describe being cast as a public cost.
- Haole residents, from kamaʻāina families to recent arrivals: check how the "transplant" label is used and received.
- Military families: compare how military and civilian neighbors describe each other after Red Hill.
- Lahaina residents and Upcountry Maui farmers: compare whom each blames for the fire and the water, by their own accounts.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check the images of them in island crime news and what community leaders answer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, hearings and official reports; mixed for folk blame and gossip; thin for Micronesian, Niʻihau and Korean material.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa accounts of Cook and of *ʻanāʻanā* cases; Cantonese for 1880s petitions; Marshallese and Chuukese for replies to cost reports, which few outsiders have read.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ʻanāʻanā* and *kahuna* matters are restricted or family-held; use only what is published. Cook's death, Compact costs, Red Hill and Lahaina are contested; present each as an account, and do not name individuals blamed in the fire.
- **Held for review:** one pointer was set aside as a category (South Asian-heritage scholars).
