# Hawaii: The domain: death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands, Papahānaumokuākea included), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (with the Niʻihau speech); the English of Hawaii and Pidgin; Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** how a people faces death, power and what is hidden: dying and the dead, change of self, sex ways, what is pooled and handed down, and what is kept secret.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families keeping *iwi* on family land (Kaʻū, Molokaʻi, Hāna): compare home burial with church cemeteries, through the families.
- People of Niʻihau: ask through community members in Kekaha whether island funeral and inheritance ways differ from Kauaʻi's.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants (Hilo, Waipahu, Kona): compare grave visits and family records across generations.
- Chinese descendants: check who still tends the society-run Mānoa graves in spring.
- Korean descendants: check how *jesa* sits inside Christian households.
- Ilocano (Waipahu, Kahului) and Visayan (Kauaʻi, Lānaʻi) families: set the nine nights beside home-province practice.
- Puerto Rican and Portuguese families in Hilo and Makawao: check anniversary masses and black dress by generation.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: check who pays and whether a body is flown home; Micronesian print is scarce.
- Paniolo (Waimea, ʻUlupalakua) and military families: set ranch graveyards beside Puowaina and base chapels.
- Haole residents of Kailua and Kīhei: check the "celebration of life" and sea scattering against older island forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Kānaka Maoli death lore and vital statistics; mixed for sex ways and pooled goods; thin for Micronesian, Niʻihau and Korean customs.
- **Lands:** no settled lands yet (the row carries only the USA), so where Hawaii ends is still the author's call; every Scorpio sheet works from the main islands and Papahānaumokuākea.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *kanikau* and nūpepa death notices; Japanese for issei gravestones; Ilocano for wake prayers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *iwi kūpuna*, burial places and some mele maʻi are family- or hālau-held; go through the burial councils, families and hālau, never around them. Honokahua is contested; give each side's account.
- **Held for review:** grave-visiting pointers from Japanese temple practice were set aside as a category (Buddhism); family and society cemeteries were kept.
