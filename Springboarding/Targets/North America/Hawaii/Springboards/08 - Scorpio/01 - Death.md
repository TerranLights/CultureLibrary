*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli on Molokaʻi and in Waiʻanae: compare deaths of the young with elders' deaths, and who leads the mourning in each.
- The Niʻihau community: check through its members whether the island's dead are buried there or on Kauaʻi.
- Japanese descendants in Hilo and Kona: compare the family grave with niches and sea scattering across three or four generations.
- Chinese descendants: check whether the spring grave visit still draws grandchildren born after statehood.
- Korean descendants: check how church funerals fit the honorific language of death.
- Ilocano families of Waipahu and Visayan families of Kauaʻi: check how long a death keeps a household in mourning.
- Portuguese families of Upcountry Maui: check prayer for the dead and mourning dress among the old.
- Samoan and Tongan families of Lāʻie: check how church schedules shape the days between death and burial.
- Chuukese families of Kalihi and Hilo: little in print; start with pastors and community groups.
- Military families at Schofield and Kāneʻohe: compare deaths on duty with civilian deaths in the same households.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality data and the war dead; mixed for grief practice by group; thin for Chuukese, Korean and Niʻihau mourning.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the *kanikau*; Ilocano for wake prayers and graveside talk on Undas; Marshallese for repatriation arrangements.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** burial places and *iwi* are family-held; the Mōkapu return and the 1853 death toll are told differently by different parties, so give each as an account. Youth suicide data need care in how they are shown.
- **Held for review:** a pointer on summer rites for the dead in Japanese families was set aside as a category (Buddhism).
