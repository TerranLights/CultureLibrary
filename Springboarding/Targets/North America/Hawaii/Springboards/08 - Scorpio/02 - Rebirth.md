# Hawaii: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; island English and Pidgin; Tagalog and Ilocano; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan and Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and the return of the dead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Hilo and Puna: compare 1830s revival records with families' own memory of conversion.
- Kānaka Maoli in recovery on the Waiʻanae Coast and Windward Oʻahu: check whether "going back to the land" and church play the same part.
- Niʻihau families: accounts describe their speech as continuous; check, through the community, how revival elsewhere is viewed.
- Okinawan descendants (Waipiʻo, Hilo): check who learns Uchinaaguchi now, and why.
- Japanese descendants: compare Christian converts of the plantation era with families who did not convert.
- Korean descendants: check church-based conversion stories against the first arrivals' church founding.
- Filipino families (Ilocano and Visayan): check Holy Week customs against born-again congregations drawing the young.
- Samoan and Tongan families of Lāʻie: check moves between churches and how a family receives one who changes.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check church recommitment after moving to Hawaii; sources are scarce.
- Haole newcomers: check the move to the islands told as a fresh start, and how locals receive it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mission records and language revival; mixed for recovery and reentry; thin for Micronesian and Korean conversion stories.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for church discipline books and nūpepa testimony; Uchinaaguchi and Japanese for club records; Samoan and Tongan for sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *kākūʻai* and *ʻūniki* belong to families and hālau; go through them. Mission accounts of "conversion" are one side; Hawaiian accounts may read the same years as loss. Treatment and prison data concern identifiable people.
- **Held for review:** island temples' teaching on lives that return was the one pointer withheld, kept as a category (Buddhism); the church and Hawaiian sources stand.
