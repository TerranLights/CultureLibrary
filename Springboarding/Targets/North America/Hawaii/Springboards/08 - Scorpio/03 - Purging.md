# Hawaii: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; the English of Hawaii and Pidgin; Japanese; Cantonese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli healers on Hawaiʻi Island and Molokaʻi: check, through practitioners, which cleansing practices are taught openly and which within families.
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae and Nānākuli: compare how residents speak of landfill siting with how the city frames it.
- Niʻihau and Kauaʻi residents: check views on the Lehua poison drop, through community members.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: compare year-end cleaning and Shinto blessings across four generations.
- Chinese descendants: check whether pre-New Year cleaning survives in mixed households.
- Korean families: check church fasting and prayer retreats as cleansing.
- Ilocano and Visayan Catholics in Waipahu and Kahului: compare *pabasa* and Lenten fasts with home-province custom.
- Puerto Rican and Portuguese parishioners of Pāʻia, Makawao and Hilo: check who still keeps the Lenten fast, by age.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check how church discipline handles a member's wrongdoing; print sources are few.
- Military families: compare base cleanup notices with what neighbors near Puʻuloa hear.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for environmental cleanup and border control; mixed for household and church cleansing; thin for Micronesian and Korean practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for plant-medicine texts; Tagalog for *pabasa* texts; Cantonese for New Year customs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some healing knowledge stays in families; go through practitioners. The Republic's loyalty purge and Kahoʻolawe's cleanup are contested; give each account. School discipline data must not be read as a judgment on any group. Eating disorders are a silence to note, not to probe.
- **Held for review:** a pointer on temple purification at year's end was set aside as a category (Buddhism); household and Shinto cleaning stand.
