*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli royalist descendants and annexation-era families: check how each family tells which ancestor stood where in 1893–98.
- Kaʻū and Kona families: compare local tellings of Keōua's death.
- Niʻihau's people: check, through them, how loyalty to the island's owners is spoken of, without assuming its form.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check which families speak of 1941 informing and which keep it closed.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare *pangangaliwa* talk in church groups with radio advice shows.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: check whether old society quarrels still mark who speaks to whom.
- Portuguese families of Hilo and Upcountry Maui: check how infidelity was handled among the plantation lunas' households and workers' households.
- Tongan and Samoan families: check church and family handling of a broken promise to the congregation.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check how the 1996 cut is told in community meetings; sources are scant, so start with community groups.
- Haole and military families: check how the overthrow story is taught to newcomers, and what they make of it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the overthrow and its rival accounts; mixed for infidelity talk; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau views.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *kumakaia* and *moekolohe* in the nūpepa; Japanese for internment-era family letters; Tagalog for church counsel.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow, the Home Lands program and the Compact are contested; set each account beside its rival. Informing in 1941 and adultery cases name real families; handle through their descendants.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; every candidate weighed for betrayal (chiefly histories, royalist and annexationist papers, internment memory, federal reports) fell outside the standing rules.
