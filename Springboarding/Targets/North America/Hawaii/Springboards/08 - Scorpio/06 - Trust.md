# Hawaii: Trust (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; Ilocano and Tagalog; Japanese; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Tongan and Samoan; Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** willingness to rely on a person or an institution, accepting the risk of being let down, in the expectation of not being harmed; loyalty is studied here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families with hereditary *kahu* duties: check, through the families, what a trust held across generations requires.
- Waiʻanae Coast residents: compare trust in the community clinic with trust in large hospitals.
- People of Niʻihau: check, through them, how far trust runs to those outside the island.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants of plantation camps: compare memories of the camp store with family stores.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check whom a new arrival trusts first: kin, church or sponsor.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: check how trust in family firms passes to grandchildren.
- Families who ran or used small neighborhood stores in Kula, Makawao and Hilo (many Portuguese and Puerto Rican): check whether buying on credit from a neighbor survives.
- Tongan and Samoan families: check trust in church treasuries against banks.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check trust in clinics, schools and landlords; written sources are few.
- Military families in base housing: compare their trust in the command before and after the 2021 water crisis.
- Haole newcomers: check how long locals say it takes before a newcomer is trusted.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** deep on the Kingdom court and the 2021 water crisis; uneven on everyday trust in strangers; slight for Micronesian and Niʻihau practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for court accounts and hymnals; Marshallese and Chuukese for clinic trust; Ilocano for sponsor relations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** where chiefs' bones rest is kept secret on purpose; never seek it. Gibson's role and the water crisis are contested; give each account.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; no candidate for trust (court histories, clinics, polls, roadside custom) came under a standing rule.
