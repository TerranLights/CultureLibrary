# Hawaii: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; Japanese; Cantonese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Portuguese; Samoan and Tongan; Chuukese and Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong, and the ways of ending it instead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of West Maui (Olowalu, Lahaina): check how the 1790 killings are remembered locally against the published accounts.
- Kauaʻi and Niʻihau families: compare local memory of 1824 with Oʻahu-written histories; reach Niʻihau through its members.
- Kānaka Maoli of Puna: check present-day tellings of Pele stories that turn on a slight returned.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare what vendetta films meant to issei audiences and to their grandchildren.
- Chinese descendants: check Chinatown theater memories and how revenge plots were received.
- Korean descendants: check church teaching on grudges across generations.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check how a family insult is answered or settled, through community leaders.
- Portuguese families: check feud memories between neighboring camps and their endings.
- Samoan and Tongan families: compare *ifoga* and *fakalelei* in Hawaii with what elders describe from home.
- Chuukese and Marshallese residents: check how churches end quarrels; print sources are few.
- Military families: check the "Remember Pearl Harbor" theme in base life and commemorations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for chiefly history and wartime press; mixed for everyday payback; thin for Micronesian and Korean practice.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Dibble and the nūpepa; Japanese for theater programs and reviews; Samoan for *ifoga* accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sorcery used to pay back a wrong (*ʻanāʻanā*) is restricted knowledge; point to practitioners' and families' own protocols, never around them, and never tie it to any group. Violent-death data must never be read as a trait of a group. Olowalu and 1824 are told differently by different parties.
- **Held for review:** for revenge no pointer needed holding; the one delicate area, sorcery as payback, is flagged above rather than held.
