# Hawaii: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin and island English, Tagalog, Ilocano and Cebuano, Japanese, Cantonese, Korean, Portuguese, Samoan, Tongan, Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order) and, by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli Congregational families (Waimea, Hāna, Molokaʻi) and hālau members: check how church hymn and chant are kept side by side.
- Niʻihau families: ask, through the community, whether faith practice differs from Kauaʻi churches; expect little to be shared.
- Japanese and Okinawan families (Kalihi, Hilo): separate shrine amulets from Okinawan *yuta* consultations; generations differ.
- Chinese families: check clan-society shrines against the Chinese Christian congregations of Honolulu.
- Korean families: set the first churches' faith beside newer Korean evangelical arrivals.
- Visayan and Ilocano families (Waipahu, Kahului, Lānaʻi City): compare Santo Niño devotion with *Iglesia ni Cristo* members.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants (Hilo, Makawao): check which devotions passed to grandchildren.
- Samoan and Tongan families: separate Latter-day Saint, Wesleyan and Congregational households' prayer habits.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check which island churches came with them.
- Haole residents and military families: compare base chapels with the islands' unaffiliated.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mission-era faith and Hawaiian hymnody; mixed for current practice; thin for Micronesian, Niʻihau and Okinawan household faith.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Fornander's pule and Kalawao letters; Cebuano and Tagalog for feast programs; Samoan for family prayer.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** pule and some chants are family- or hālau-held; Kalaupapa needs a permit and patients' consent; Mauna Kea and Lahaina are contested; give each account as one.
- **Held for review:** home altars and memorial rites in Japanese families were set aside as a category (Buddhism); shrine practice was kept.
