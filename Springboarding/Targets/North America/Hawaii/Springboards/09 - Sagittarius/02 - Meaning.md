# Hawaii: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin, island English, Japanese, Uchinaaguchi, Ilocano, Tagalog, Korean, Cantonese, Portuguese, Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in homestead and rural districts (Waimānalo, Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua on Molokaʻi): check whether land work and family *kuleana* are named before job or faith.
- Niʻihau families on Kauaʻi's west side: note whether any community voice speaks to this at all.
- Japanese descendants (Hilo, Waipahu): set "okage sama de" sayings beside career talk across generations.
- Okinawan families: check *ikigai* as used in the islands against its use in self-help books.
- Ilocano and Visayan families (Waipahu, Kahului, Lānaʻi City): compare first-generation and island-born answers on remittances and children's schooling.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: check how old independence and clan causes still figure in family stories.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families (Hilo, Upcountry Maui): check what the old trades meant after the plantations closed.
- Samoan and Tongan families: separate *matai* duties from church callings.
- Paniolo families (Waimea, ʻUlupalakua) and military households: compare ranch work and service as stated purposes.
- Haole arrivals in Puna and Haʻikū: compare why they say they came with the reasons haole kamaʻāina families give for staying.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in Hawaiian origin accounts and fiction, thin in surveys with Hawaii-level meaning items.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Hāloa tellings; Ilocano for GUMIL writing; Tongan for *tauhi vā* terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** genealogies tied to Hāloa are family-held; suicide data needs care with small island counts.
- **Held for review:** one candidate was set aside under the standing rule, kept only as a category: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands.
