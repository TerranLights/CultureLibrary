# Hawaii: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin, island English, Japanese, Korean, Tagalog, Ilocano, Cantonese, Portuguese, Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese, Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in sovereignty groups and outside them (Waiʻanae, Keaukaha, Molokaʻi): separate *ea* as collective freedom from personal liberty, and note who uses which.
- Niʻihau families: ask how coming and going to the island is arranged, and who may say.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check contract-era leaving and wartime restriction in family memory, by island.
- Chinese descendants: check whether family stories name leaving the plantation as the start of a free life.
- Korean families: set the independence cause beside later immigrants' views.
- Filipino families (Ilocano, Visayan, Tagalog): compare *kalayaan* talk among sakada descendants and recent arrivals.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants: ask whether moving off the camps is told as release or as loss.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: check how free movement to the islands is spoken of at home.
- Military families and paniolo families (Waimea, Upcountry Maui): compare "freedom" as service talk and as open-range life.
- Haole residents in Puna and on the Windward side: check off-grid newcomers against long-settled haole families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** deep archives on contract labor and the movements; few polls on what residents mean by liberty; Micronesian and Niʻihau voices scarce.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *ea* in the nūpepa; Japanese for JACAR files; Korean for association records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** sovereignty and statehood are contested: give each account as an account; Compact debates touch living families; Niʻihau access is the community's to describe.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; the labor files, presses and movement papers weighed for this sheet raised no item under the standing rules.
