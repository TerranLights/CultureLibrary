## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli scholars, *kūpuna* and families (Hāna, Waiʻanae, Kaʻū): set written history against genealogy and family moʻolelo as evidence.
- Niʻihau speakers: check whether their Hawaiian marks kinds of story differently; ask through the community.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare wartime memory in Honolulu with that in Kona and Hilo.
- Chinese descendants: check older Chinese-language papers against English papers on the same events.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check which outlets and radio hosts they treat as reliable.
- Korean families: compare the community's independence-era papers with present news habits.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families (Hilo, Kula): check parish bulletin against secular press as a source of facts.
- Samoan and Tongan families: compare church and family as places where accounts are settled.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check what interpreted information is trusted and why.
- Military families and haole newcomers: compare reliance on command or mainland news with local sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** the 1893 dispute and Hawaiian historiography fill shelves; news-habit data is patchy; Micronesian and Niʻihau material is nearly absent.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for 1890s editorials; Chinese for the community press; Samoan for story kinds.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1893, annexation, Mauna Kea and Red Hill are contested: give each side as an account. Genealogies used as proof are family-held.
- **Held for review:** on accounts of the past, a single item is withheld from the table and named here only by category: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands.
