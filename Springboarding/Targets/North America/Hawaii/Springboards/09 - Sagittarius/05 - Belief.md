## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families (Hilo, Puna, Kaʻū): set Pele belief against church membership in the same households.
- Niʻihau families: note only what the community chooses to say about omens and spirits; do not press.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: separate *yakudoshi* parties from *mabui* and *yuta* practice.
- Chinese descendants: compare feng shui use among older families and recent buyers from Asia.
- Korean families: check how far evangelical belief has replaced older household practice.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare first-generation and island-born accounts of *aswang* and *kulam*.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants: ask whether evil-eye cures are still sought, and from whom.
- Samoan and Tongan families: check whether spirit accounts are told differently in Lāʻie's church community than in Kalihi.
- Micronesian families in Kona and Kalihi: check which beliefs they name to clinicians and which they keep.
- Haole newcomers and military families: compare New Age belief with base-chapel belief.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** strong in folklore and ghost lore; uneven in surveys; thin for Portuguese and Micronesian belief.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Pele chants; Ilocano and Cebuano for *aswang* tales; Samoan for *aitu*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Pele practice is living religion, not folklore, to its keepers; the Hagoth tradition is a church matter, give it as taught and as questioned; spirit beliefs can carry stigma in clinics.
- **Held for review:** for the ill-starred-age customs, the temple-side purification pointers were put aside as a category (Buddhism); the shrine side and the parties stay in the table.
