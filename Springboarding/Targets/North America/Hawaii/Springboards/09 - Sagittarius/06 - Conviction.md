## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli activists and *kūpuna* (Molokaʻi, Waiʻanae, Keaukaha): separate long-haul campaigners from those drawn in by one event.
- Niʻihau families: note whether any public stand by the community is on record, and leave the rest to them.
- Japanese descendants: compare Watada's supporters and critics by generation and by island.
- Okinawan families: check how stands taken in Okinawa are echoed or ignored in Hawaii clubs.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: set old homeland causes beside current civic stands.
- Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog speakers: check *paninindigan* in labor and church settings.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants: ask whether stands were taken through the parish, the union, or neither.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: check how church leaders and members spoke in 2013, and whether they spoke alike.
- Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check who speaks for the community at the Capitol.
- Military families, haole surfers and paniolo families: compare service, surf and ranch-country stands on land and sea.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** good for protest history; thin for inner conviction and for Micronesian voices.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for oath-refusal notices; Korean for relief-society records; Tagalog for parish materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kahoʻolawe, the overthrow, military land and the 2013 hearings are contested; give each side as an account and describe violence, never endorse it.
- **Held for review:** empty for this sheet: neither the 2013 testimony nor the peace and surf campaigns produced a source the standing rules would hold back.
