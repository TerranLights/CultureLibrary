## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families (Waimānalo, Hāna, Molokaʻi): set *kūpono* as taught at home beside its use in church and school.
- Niʻihau families: let the community say how fakes of their shell work affect them.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check *shōjiki* and *makoto* in elders' advice and business stories.
- Chinese descendants: check old Chinatown shopkeepers' reputations as families tell them.
- Korean families: compare small-business trust among first-wave and newer families.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check how *palabra de honor* is invoked in family loans and deals.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants: ask how a spoken promise was held to in camp stores, lunas' dealings and ranch hiring.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: compare how church money is reported and questioned.
- Marshallese and Chuukese newcomers: check which dealings they report as unfair, and to whom; avoid framing them as either cheats or victims.
- Haole residents, paniolo families and military households: compare base, ranch and resort settings for expectations of candor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** complaints and press coverage are easy to find; how people talk about everyday honesty is mostly oral.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for ʻIwa and Kamakau; Tagalog and Ilocano for forwarder warnings; Samoan for church reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Niʻihau craft is the community's to describe; Lahaina dealings are contested; avoid naming any group as dishonest.
- **Held for review:** for this sheet the scams, swap-meet and church sources gave nothing to set aside under the standing rules.
