## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families: compare Kamehameha Schools, Hawaiian-focused charter schools and regular public schools on what the families say school is for; the admissions policy is contested.
- Niʻihau families on Kauaʻi: check Ke Kula Niʻihau o Kekaha's role through the school itself.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: set the postwar rush into degrees beside today's choices by generation.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: check community language schools against private-school routes.
- Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog families: compare first-generation students' paths at UH and the community colleges.
- Azorean, Madeiran and Puerto Rican descendants: check schooling across generations from family records, with no assumption about outcomes.
- Samoan and Tongan families: separate the Lāʻie university route from public-school paths elsewhere.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check what parents and students report about school entry and language support.
- Military and haole families: compare private-school choices in Honolulu with neighbor-island options.
- Paniolo and rural families (Waimea, Kaʻū, Molokaʻi): check distance and boarding as barriers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** institutional records and school data are plentiful; families' own views are thinner, Micronesian views thinnest.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Royal School letters; Chinese and Korean for community-school records; Chuukese and Marshallese for family interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kamehameha admissions and the Hawaiian-language school history are contested; give accounts. Hālau and *ʻuniki* teaching are held by kumu; go through them.
- **Held for review:** pointers withheld while scouting this sheet are kept as a single category: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands.
