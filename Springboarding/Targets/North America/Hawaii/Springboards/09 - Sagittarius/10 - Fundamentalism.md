# Hawaii: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin, island English, Japanese, Korean, Cantonese, Tagalog, Ilocano, Portuguese, Samoan, Tongan, Chuukese, Marshallese. **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli Christians and hula practitioners: check how church members and kumu speak about the 1830s campaigns today, and whether they agree.
- Niʻihau families: note only what the community shares about church strictness, since outsiders' claims are unreliable.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: compare Christian converts with members of the new religions on how each was labeled.
- Chinese and Korean congregations: check whether the word "fundamentalist" is claimed, rejected or never used.
- Ilocano, Visayan and Tagalog families: compare Pentecostal converts with Catholic kin on how conversion is talked about.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican Catholics: check traditionalist worship against parish practice.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: compare how members and leavers describe doctrine and discipline, without labeling the churches.
- Marshallese and Chuukese churches: check which mission lineages came with them and how strict they are said to be, by members.
- Haole evangelicals, military chapels and secular newcomers: compare who uses "fundamentalist" as an insult and of whom.
- Muslims and other small faiths in Honolulu: check how they meet the word in public.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin and scattered; most material is press coverage of campaigns and outsiders' labels; little is written by members.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the 1860s press; Korean and Cantonese for church usage; Chuukese and Marshallese for Pentecostal preaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the label itself is an insult to many; never apply it to a group; give each group's own words. The 1998 and 2013 marriage campaigns are contested.
- **Held for review:** two pointers kept out of the table and named here by category alone: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands; and Buddhism.
