# Hawaii: The domain: authority and duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii, meaning the Hawaiian Islands from Hawaiʻi Island to Papahānaumokuākea; region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi with Niʻihau speech; Pidgin; Hawaii English; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese; Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** who commands, who obeys and what is owed: career, status, reputation, authority and responsibility, with the folkways of age, time, rank, order and power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of prominent lines and homestead families (Waimānalo, Nānākuli, Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua): check how ancestry and office are weighed in claims to speak.
- The people of Niʻihau: ask through Kekaha intermediaries who holds authority on a privately owned island; print is mostly outsiders'.
- Japanese and Okinawan families (Hilo, Wahiawā, Upcountry Maui): separate prefecture-club leadership from political careers after 1954.
- Filipino families (Ilocano in Waipahu and Kahului, Visayan on Kauaʻi): trace who became *luna* or labor contractor, and what that standing led to.
- Chinese, Korean and Portuguese descendants: compare the society, church and benefit-club offices each used to rank members (Cantonese, Korean and Portuguese records).
- Samoan and Tongan families: test whether a *matai* title or Tongan noble rank carries weight on an island job site.
- Compact migrants (Marshallese, Chuukese): check who represents them before the state, and by what right.
- Old haole families and recent continental arrivals: compare inherited board seats with standing earned through jobs.
- Military and paniolo families (Waimea, Kula): set the chain of command beside the ranch foreman's word.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** deep for chiefly rank and the planters' rule; patchy for today's status ladders; scarce for Niʻihau and Compact migrants.
- **Lands:** no lands are settled in the tracker and there is no scope file (the row's only pre-war land is the USA); the nation's exact lands remain open, and the sign is written for the island chain with Papahānaumokuākea.
- **Languages needed:** Marshallese to read community reports unfiltered; Ilocano for contractor ledgers; Japanese for prefecture-club minutes; Hawaiian for the nūpepa's rank words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** chiefly genealogies are family-held and rank claims disputed; go through families and the royal societies, never around them. *Kauwā* descent wounds. The overthrow and homestead disputes are contested; give each account as one.
- **Held for review:** no item; regalia, clubs, the plantation clock and service-member briefings were the candidates, and all stayed.
