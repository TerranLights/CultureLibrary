# Hawaii: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Pidgin and Hawaii English first; then Japanese, Ilocano, Tagalog, Samoan, Tongan, Cantonese, Korean, Portuguese, Marshallese and Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli on Kauaʻi's west side and in Kalaupapa families: compare remembered defiance of health officers with the official files.
- Kānaka Maoli on Mauna Kea and in Hilo and Waimea: check how protest leaders, kūpuna and Native Hawaiian officers each name the authority they answer to.
- The people of Niʻihau: who may grant leave to visit, and how residents speak of the owners' rule; ask through Kekaha, not around it.
- Japanese and Okinawan families of Hilo and Waipahu: check how language-school fights shaped who parents thought should command a child.
- Filipino families (Ilocano speakers in Waipahu, Visayan on Kauaʻi): separate the *po* and *opo* of the home from deference at work.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations in Lāʻie and Kalihi: compare a pastor's authority with a titled elder's, and when one yields to the other.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families in Kalihi and Kona: check who they turn to first when police or child-welfare officers arrive; records are thin.
- Military families on Schofield and Kāneʻohe: set the command structure on base beside base residents' dealings with county police.
- Haole residents of Puna and Kaʻū: check off-grid settlers' refusals of county authority, in their own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** kingdom offices and plantation command are well documented; police-trust figures rest on small survey samples; Compact communities and Niʻihau will need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** Piʻilani's 1906 text and congregational minutes are in Hawaiian; the school-case papers partly Japanese; island church-council records Samoan or Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kalaupapa families' memories and patients' records are protected; Mauna Kea and the overthrow are contested ground, each telling given as one; child-discipline studies touch living families.
- **Held for review:** in the 1927 school case, the temple-run schools' side is held as a category (Buddhism); the court record and the territory's files are used.
