# Hawaii: Responsibility (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the English of Hawaii and Pidgin; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese for community sources. **Concept in one line:** being answerable: taking charge of a task or a person, and answering for the results, good or bad.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae and Puna: compare how land and fishing stewards describe their charge with how state managers describe theirs.
- Kānaka Maoli in Lahaina and Kula: check how fire survivors assign responsibility among utility, county, state and landowners, each telling apart.
- Japanese descendants of Hilo's Shinmachi and Waiākea: test how 1960 tsunami survivors speak of fault, theirs or others'.
- Filipino families (Ilocano in Waipahu, Visayan in Kauaʻi camps): check which child is made answerable for younger siblings and remittances.
- Samoan and Tongan families (Lāʻie, Kalihi): find out who answers for a youth's offense, the youth or the family's titled head.
- Chuukese and Marshallese families in Kalihi and Hilo: look at school and clinic reports that blame families, set against the families' own account.
- Chinese, Korean and Portuguese descendants of Honolulu and Hilo: find old merchant and society rules on who stood surety for a member's debts.
- Paniolo families (Waimea, Kula, Molokaʻi): find who carried the blame when a horse or a herd was lost.
- Haole newcomers and military families: ask whether short stays change who is expected to clean up, repair and report.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plentiful on disasters and corporate fault; moderate on household responsibility; sparse for Micronesian and Tongan families.
- **Languages needed:** Kamakau's serials need Hawaiian; wartime community papers need Japanese; school and clinic outreach needs Chuukese and Marshallese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Lahaina fire and the U.S. role in 1893 are contested; present each party's account. Child-support and youth-offense data touch living families; use aggregates.
- **Held for review:** empty; blame and answerability here were traced through courts of opinion (press, settlements, report cards), so no item fell under the rules.
