# Hawaii: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Pidgin and the English of Hawaii; ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Japanese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan and Tongan; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Chuukese and Marshallese where schools and churches keep records. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in hālau and canoe clubs (Hilo, Waimānalo, Molokaʻi): compare training regimes kept by kumu and coaches with school discipline codes.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants (Hilo, Waipahu, Upcountry Maui): check how *gaman* and dojo training are remembered by Nisei and by grandchildren.
- Ilocano- and Visayan-speaking parents: ask how the *palo* they received compares with what they now do, and with what island teachers expect.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: check whether language-school and church regimes are recalled as strict, and by whom.
- Samoan and Tongan households in Waiʻanae and Lāʻie: map three sources of correction (church youth leaders, parents, family court) and where they clash.
- Chuukese and Marshallese families in Kalihi, Kona and Hilo: ask how they read island school rules; little is written, so begin with family liaisons.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants (Kula, Hilo): look for discipline in parish schools and ranch work as their memoirs tell it.
- Military families at Kāneʻohe and Schofield: set barracks discipline beside how service parents correct children off base.
- Haole families in Kailua and Kona: compare "gentle parenting" talk among newcomers with older local ways.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** plantation regimes and school policy leave paper trails; home correction is talked about more than counted; Micronesian, Tongan and Niʻihau households have almost no written record.
- **Languages needed:** reading Kawelo's training in the original needs Hawaiian; prewar dojo founders wrote in Japanese; correction words at home need Tagalog, Ilocano, Samoan and Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hālau training is held by each hālau, so ask only what the kumu chooses to share; child-welfare and abuse sources touch living families and are contested between parents and agencies.
- **Held for review:** meditation and temple-run youth programs considered for self-discipline were set aside as a category (Buddhism).
