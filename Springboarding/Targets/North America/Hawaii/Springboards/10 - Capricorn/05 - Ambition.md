# Hawaii: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin and Hawaii English; Cantonese; Japanese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Korean; Samoan and Tongan; Portuguese; Chuukese and Marshallese for student and church groups. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli on homestead lands and in Hāna and Molokaʻi: compare ambitions tied to staying on the land with those that require leaving.
- Nisei and Sansei of Japanese and Okinawan descent: test the story of a climb from cane field to law office against those who stayed in farming, fishing and stores.
- Chinese descendants of Honolulu's Chinatown and Mānoa: check how early merchant fortunes shaped later career choices.
- *Sakada* grandchildren set beside newly arrived Ilocano and Visayan professionals: ask what each generation was told to aim for, and by whom.
- Korean Honolulu, first wave and post-1965: look at which professions parents push and which are frowned on.
- Samoan and Tongan youth: weigh the football route against church office and college as routes to standing, and who chooses.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican lines on Maui and Kauaʻi: note which jobs (police, fire, teaching, trades) became family careers, and why.
- Haole newcomers on Maui and Hawaiʻi Island: separate those who came to retire or step back from those who came to build something.
- Military families: check how a posting fits a career ladder, and how spouses' ambitions fare on Oʻahu.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** careers of chiefs, merchants and politicians are well recorded; young people's own aims are surveyed only patchily; Micronesian and Niʻihau youth are nearly absent from print.
- **Languages needed:** the kingdom's scholarship letters ran in Hawaiian papers; Afong's circle left Cantonese records; nurses' recruitment stories are in Tagalog and Ilocano media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family genealogies behind merchant and chiefly fortunes are held by descendants; sovereignty-minded and statehood-minded accounts of who rose after 1893 differ, so give each.
- **Held for review:** for ambition, one pointer is held as a category only: South Asian-heritage communities and scholars in the Hawaiian Islands.
