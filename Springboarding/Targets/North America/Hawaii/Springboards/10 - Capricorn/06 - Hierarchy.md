# Hawaii: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; Hawaii English; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Cantonese and Hakka; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese; Chuukese. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of chiefly descent and homestead families: check where genealogical rank still governs seating, speaking order and ceremony, and where it is ignored.
- Niʻihau families living on Kauaʻi's west side: learn, only as far as they wish to tell, whether the island's rank order differs from Kauaʻi's.
- Naichi and Okinawan lines: date the ranking between them, from camp-era slights to intermarriage, and note who says it has ended.
- Chinese descendants: separate Hakka and Punti families, and Christian from society-hall families, in marriage and membership.
- Island Filipino organizations: list who heads them, *sakada* grandchildren or doctors and lawyers who arrived after 1965.
- First-wave Korean churches: test whether old *yangban* standing carried into elder and deacon posts.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants (Kula, Hilo, Kauaʻi): trace their placement between haole and Asian groups in jobs and census labels.
- At Samoan weddings and Tongan birthdays in the islands: note the serving order of titled, noble and untitled guests.
- Haole boardrooms and clubs: count missionary-line names against newcomers and retired officers over the decades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** maps, wage tables and court protocol document the planter and kingdom orders well; today's class lines show mostly in pay and housing data; Chuukese and Marshallese clan rank in the islands is barely written.
- **Languages needed:** Hakka as well as Cantonese for society minutes; Uchinaaguchi phrases in Okinawan interviews; Hawaiian for eating-house rules; Tongan for mourning reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** ethnic labels include slurs; describe, never reuse. Chiefly descent claims are disputed among families. Okinawan–Naichi and Hakka–Punti accounts are told by one side at a time; gather both.
- **Held for review:** temple seating and membership order were weighed as a rank source and set aside as a category (Buddhism).

## 5. First queries
1. Library of Congress Sanborn maps: Hawaii towns; download Waipahu and Pāʻia sheets and mark camp names.
2. HathiTrust: "Report of the Commissioner of Labor on Hawaii" 1901–1915; copy wage tables by nationality.
3. SEC EDGAR: latest DEF 14A for Hawaiian Electric Industries and Bank of Hawaii; record "pay ratio".
4. UH Center for Oral History and the Hawaii United Okinawa Association: "Naichi" in interview indexes.
5. Friends of ʻIolani Palace and the Archives' Foreign Office files: "state dinner" and "order of precedence", Kalākaua's reign.
