## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of the homestead neighborhoods (Papakōlea, Waimānalo, Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua): check how each homestead association decides who speaks for it.
- Kānaka Maoli of rural districts (Hāna, Kaʻū, Puna, Waiʻanae, Molokaʻi): set kin-based help beside formal clubs.
- The people of Niʻihau and their kin in Kekaha: weigh whose account of the island's community is in print.
- Plantation-era descendants (Japanese, Okinawan, Chinese, Ilocano and Visayan, Korean, Portuguese, Puerto Rican): test whether camp, ancestry, church or union is named first.
- Haole residents: separate families of several generations from recent arrivals.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations (Kalihi, Lāʻie, Hilo) and Marshallese and Chuukese families (Honolulu, Kona): find their own associations before accounts written about them.
- Military families (Schofield, Pearl Harbor–Hickam, Kāneʻohe Bay) and paniolo families (Waimea, Upcountry Maui): compare base, ranch and parish (Congregational, Catholic, Latter-day Saint) as candidate units.
- Lahaina households after the 2023 fire: follow how displaced residents regroup, through their own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for kingdom-era and plantation associations and for movement history; thin for Tongan and Micronesian groups and for Niʻihau.
- **Lands:** the tracker settles no lands and there is no `00 - Scope.md`; the row's only pre-war land is the USA, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. This sign is written for the Hawaiian Islands with Papahānaumokuākea.

- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow, sovereignty, Mauna Kea and Lahaina are contested; give each side's account as an account. Niʻihau's community is reached only through its families' own channels.
