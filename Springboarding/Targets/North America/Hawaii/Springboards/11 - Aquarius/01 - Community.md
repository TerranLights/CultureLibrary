# Hawaii: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Tagalog and Ilocano; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of the Molokaʻi homesteads (Hoʻolehua, Kalamaʻula): compare homestead life with the old ranch town of Maunaloa.
- Kānaka Maoli of Nānākuli and Waiʻanae: check how canoe clubs, churches and homestead associations divide a coast's shared work.
- Niʻihau families in Kekaha and Waimea on Kauaʻi: check how a community kept apart re-forms in a mixed town, through families only.
- Plantation-town descendants (Pāhala, Pāʻia, Waialua, Hāmākua): test whether the old town, the camp or the ancestry group is "my community" after closure.
- Ilocano and Visayan newcomers in Kalihi and Waipahu: set hometown associations against island parishes.
- Samoan and Tongan households of Kūhiō Park Terrace, Lāʻie and Hilo: weigh congregation against housing project as the unit of shared life.
- Chuukese and Marshallese families (Kalihi, Kona, Hilo): print is scarce; start with clinics and pastors, then island-of-origin groups.
- Haole residents of the North Shore and Kailua: check where "locals only" lines run at surf breaks and in neighborhood groups.
- Military families in base housing and paniolo families of Waimea: check how far base, ranch and town overlap.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Honolulu tracts and plantation towns; thin for Niʻihau, Lānaʻi and Compact families.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *mele pana*; Uchinaaguchi and Japanese for the Okinawa Center's records; Samoan for congregation bulletins; Ilocano and Cebuano for fiesta programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some *mele* belong to hālau and families; ask their keepers. Flood and housing disputes involve neighbors still living side by side.
- **Held for review:** none; place, church and hometown pointers raised no rule, and no religious-community pointer was set aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Census data portal: ACS 5-year tables B25003 and B25038 for every Hawaii tract; map owners and long-settled households.
2. Library catalog: Lind "An island community" 1938; Pak "The watcher of Waipuna".
3. Kokua Kalihi Valley website: history and annual reports; then Hoʻoulu ʻĀina's program pages.
4. Bishop Museum archives: search *mele pana* recordings for Hilo, Hāna and Kaʻū.
5. *The Garden Island* archive, April–June 2018: "Wainiha" "Hanalei" supply boats and volunteers.
