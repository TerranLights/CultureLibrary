# Hawaii: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese with interpreters. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the group is weighed against the person, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and what members are expected to do together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli civic, royal and sovereignty bodies (Honolulu, Hilo, Molokaʻi): check how each decides (vote, consensus, elders) and who may leave.
- Kānaka Maoli of Niʻihau: whether a group view exists in print at all; ask families before quoting outsiders.
- Naichi and Okinawan families (Hilo, Waipahu, Kona): track the boundary between them across three generations.
- Chinese descendants: separate Four Counties and Zhongshan lines in the association records.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare membership in lodges, hometown groups and unions by plantation.
- Korean descendants: weigh the 1910s–1940s factional split against later church-based groups.
- Portuguese, Puerto Rican and Spanish plantation families: check when each was counted with or apart from the other groups.
- Samoan and Tongan households and Marshallese and Chuukese families: start from congregations and youth groups; studies of the Compact families are few.
- Haole residents, military families and paniolo crews: compare how a ranch crew, a military unit and a newcomer's circle draw insiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** strong for labor and ancestry associations up to statehood; mixed for schools and online; weak for Compact and Tongan groups.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Hui Kālaiʻāina reports; Cantonese for association rolls; Ilocano and Spanish for lodge rules; Korean for the faction papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** lodge rites are private to members; the school-conflict accounts concern children and are disputed, so report each source's account only. Wartime suspicion and internment remain painful for the families concerned.
- **Held for review:** nothing; lodges, unions, county societies and wartime liaison bodies were weighed, and none needed setting aside.
