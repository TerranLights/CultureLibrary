# Hawaii: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli in Waiʻanae, Nānākuli and Keaukaha: compare school-complex results with Kailua and Mānoa, and ask families how they read any difference.
- Kānaka Maoli on Molokaʻi and in Hāna: check how equality is weighed against a people's prior claim, in the community's words.
- Niʻihau families: check what is in print on standing between islanders and the island's owners, then go through Kekaha families.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: trace pay and officeholding from field wages onward, strike by strike and island by island.
- Filipino families (Ilocano, Visayan): check pay, officeholding and school results by generation.
- Chinese, Korean, Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: compare when each group's pay and political standing changed.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese residents: check complaints, school results and housing by group; sources are few and newer.
- Haole residents of Kahala, Lanikai and Kona's resorts: compare their counts with the tax-class tables; read old-family and newcomer accounts apart.
- Military families and māhū and transgender residents: set the base's equal-opportunity rules beside island life, and seek māhū voices through their own groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for plantation pay and strikes and for present-day income and school data; thin for gender diversity and Micronesian experience.
- **Languages needed:** Japanese for the Higher Wage Association; Hawaiian for the 1839 text; Ilocano for later strike leaflets; Pidgin to hear the comedy.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** admissions preference, sovereignty claims and land are contested; present each side's account. Māhū and transgender residents' stories are theirs to tell.
- **Held for review:** nothing; plantation pay, suffrage, schools and comedy needed no hold, and no faith pointer was in play for this concept.
