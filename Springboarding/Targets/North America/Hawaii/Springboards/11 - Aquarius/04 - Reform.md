# Hawaii: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Tagalog and Ilocano; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli congregations of the kingdom era and today's Hawaiian churches: compare who led reform of drinking and conduct, then and now.
- Kānaka Maoli land and water activists (Kahoʻolawe, Waiāhole, Mauna Kea, East Maui streams): separate reform within the state from calls for independence.
- The people of Niʻihau: ask through their families how change on the island is weighed and by whom; print offers little.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants (Hilo, Waipahu): check how far "Americanization" campaigns were accepted, bent or refused, by generation.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare uplift movements with union-led change on each plantation.
- Chinese descendants: follow the reform and revolutionary societies into later civic groups.
- Korean and Portuguese families: check whether church or lodge carried their reform drives.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities: check which reforms were asked by them and which were done to them, through their own groups.
- Haole residents, military families and Lahaina households: compare newcomers' campaigns, the services' changes and the fire survivors' demands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the kingdom's factions and for twenty-first-century policy; thin for Filipino uplift movements and Pacific Islander reform drives.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for temperance societies and *Ka Leo o ka Lahui*; Cantonese for the Baohuanghui; Ilocano and Visayan for the Federation's members.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "reform" in 1887–1893 is a loaded word tied to the overthrow; present each faction's account. Lahaina recovery and military land use are live disputes. Recovery-program stories belong to the people in them.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the faith-led reform pointers were Christian or civic, and no pointer was set aside under the standing rules.
