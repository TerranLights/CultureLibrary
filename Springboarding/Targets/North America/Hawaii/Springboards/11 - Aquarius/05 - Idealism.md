# Hawaii: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality, including visions of a better society.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli educators and voyagers (Waimānalo, Keaukaha, Hilo, Molokaʻi): compare ideals written into school charters with those spoken by canoe crews.
- Kānaka Maoli kūpuna in Kaʻū and Hāna: check how *ka wā kahiko* is invoked, and when it marks an ideal and when a plain past.
- Niʻihau families: most print about the island's ideals is by outsiders; check whose words those are, then go through families.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare the ideals named in prefectural clubs with those in peace observances.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check ideals of a calling (nursing, teaching) by generation, from their own associations.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: weigh exile-era political ideals against later civic ones, using family papers.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check whether parish feasts carry stated ideals, and which.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese households: compare how *faʻa Samoa*, *anga fakatonga* and island-of-origin ideals are invoked by elders and by the island-born.
- Haole newcomers (Kauaʻi's north shore, Puna) and military families: compare newcomers' own accounts of why they came with those of short-tour families posted by orders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for the paradise image and for Native educational and voyaging movements, thin for Filipino, Korean and Micronesian ideals in their own words.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *mele inoa* and *Ka Hae Hawaii*; Uchinaaguchi for the peace saying; Samoan and Tongan for church ideals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some *mele inoa* belong to families or hālau; use published versions and ask keepers. Michener's and Hollywood's images draw criticism from island writers; present it as their account.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the calling and peace pointers chosen were secular or Christian, so no faith or heritage rule applied.
