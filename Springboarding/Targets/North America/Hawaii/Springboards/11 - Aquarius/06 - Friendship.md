# Hawaii: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae and Mākaha: compare surf, canoe and church circles as places friends are made.
- Kānaka Maoli of Molokaʻi and Kaʻū: check hunting and fishing partnerships against kin-based help.
- Niʻihau families on Kauaʻi: friendship ties across the channel are a family matter; ask, don't assume.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: set social clubs beside *moai* and track how each changes with age.
- Ilocano and Visayan youth: compare *barkada* among the island-born and the newly arrived.
- Chinese and Korean descendants: check sworn-brother pacts and church fellowship groups in family memoirs.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican families: compare *compadre* ties with plain friendship.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese youth: check friendships across island-of-origin lines in schools, through counselors and community groups.
- Haole newcomers, military families on short tours, and paniolo crews: compare how fast each makes friends, and with whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** thin in surveys for the islands; rich in oral histories, fiction and club records.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Hiʻiaka tale; Samoan for *soa*; Uchinaaguchi and Japanese for *moai* and club records; Tagalog and Ilocano for *barkada* studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *aikāne* touches sexuality and is read differently by different scholars and families; present each reading. Hunting spots and fishing grounds are guarded knowledge; never publish locations.
- **Held for review:** nothing; activity clubs, *moai*, youth circles and local fiction were weighed, and none fell under the standing rules.
