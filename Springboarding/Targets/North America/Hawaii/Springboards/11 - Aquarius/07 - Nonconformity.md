# Hawaii: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Tagalog and Ilocano; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the departure is tolerated, punished or prized.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli māhū and their families (Waimānalo, Waiʻanae, Hilo): check how standing in hālau and families differs from treatment at school or work.
- Kānaka Maoli practitioners on Kauaʻi's north shore: compare their view of Kalalau campers with the state's.
- Niʻihau families: departures from the island's ways would be reported only by the families concerned; do not seek them out.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check how an "odd one" is spoken of in family memoirs, generation by generation.
- Filipino families (Ilocano, Visayan): seek *bakla* and transgender residents' accounts through their own groups, not others' talk.
- Samoan and Tongan communities: seek *faʻafafine* and *fakaleitī* voices through their own associations and churches.
- Chinese, Korean and Portuguese descendants: look for black-sheep figures in family histories and obituaries.
- Marshallese and Chuukese youth: check whether island-born young people's music and dress draw elders' objections; sources are scarce.
- Haole newcomers (Maui, Puna, Kauaʻi), military families and paniolo: compare who is called "weird" in each setting, and by whom.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for māhū history and for the 1960s–70s seekers, thin for punk, Pacific Islander and Micronesian nonconformity.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kamapuaʻa serials and the Kapaemahu story; Samoan and Tongan for community terms; Tagalog for *bakla* writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gender identity terms are self-names and can wound when used by others. Kalalau is a protected area; never publish routes or campsites. The Kapaemahu story has keepers; follow their telling.
- **Held for review:** nothing; for nonconformity the church, scene and census pointers were enough, and no item was set aside.
