# Hawaii: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Tagalog and Ilocano; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; some forms are prized (calm, objectivity, equanimity) and others condemned (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli families of Kaʻū, Molokaʻi and Waiʻanae: compare how grief is shown at funerals now with the kingdom-era accounts.
- Kānaka Maoli hālau and canoe clubs: check how composure is trained under a *kumu* or a steersman.
- Niʻihau families: reserve toward outsiders is reported by outsiders; check the source before using it.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare *enryo* and *gaman* in memoirs of the Issei, Nisei and later generations.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check how *bahala na* is used by the newly arrived and by the island-born.
- Korean and Chinese descendants: look in family memoirs for how grief and anger are handled in public.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese communities: compare funeral and church settings where feeling is shown with workplaces where it is held back.
- Haole residents and military families: check how newcomers describe island warmth or reserve, as their accounts only.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Kula: check the ranch ideal of the quiet, steady hand in oral histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich in memoirs and fiction, thin in measured data on emotional style for the islands.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for mourning accounts and *mele*; Japanese for Issei writing; Tagalog and Ilocano for *bahala na*; Samoan and Tongan for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** claims about any group's emotional style turn easily into stereotype; record them as someone's claim. Funeral customs are family matters.
- **Held for review:** one pointer, on a religious teaching of non-attachment, is held as a category (Buddhism).

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog and Ulukau: "kumakena" OR "kūmākena" mourning; note each describer and date.
2. Huapala.org or a Hawaiian songbook index: songs with "mehameha"; list composers and years.

4. United States Elections Project: Hawaii turnout of eligible voters, 1980–2024.
5. Hawaiʻi County Civil Defense and *Hawaii Tribune-Herald*, August 2018: "Hurricane Lane" Hilo flooding.
