# Hawaii: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese and Uchinaaguchi; Ilocano and Tagalog; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Samoan; Tongan; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of the homestead neighborhoods and of Waiʻanae: compare hopes spoken at sovereignty gatherings with hopes for a house and a job.
- Kānaka Maoli of Lahaina: follow how hope for the town is voiced in their own organizations after the fire.
- Niʻihau families: their hopes for the island's future are theirs to state; go through Kekaha families only.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: set the first arrivals' hopes beside their grandchildren's, using anniversary books.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare *sakada* hopes with those of present-day arrivals, generation by generation.
- Chinese, Korean and Portuguese descendants: check what hopes for children were written in family histories and church booklets.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: compare hopes for health care and schooling with what families report finding; studies are scarce.
- Haole newcomers and military families: compare the hopes they state on arrival with what they report a few years on.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Kula: check hopes for keeping ranch land in the family.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for migration stories and song, thin for measured hope among residents and for Micronesian families' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *wānana* and the Queen's Prayer; Japanese for the *Gannenmono*; Ilocano for *sakada* letters; Marshallese and Chuukese for migration hopes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1893–1895 events and Lahaina are contested and raw; give each account as an account. The Aliamanu deaths touch grieving families. Crisis-line data are aggregated only.
- **Held for review:** nothing; year-turning, church and migration pointers needed no hold for hope, and none was set aside.
