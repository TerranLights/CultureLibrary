# Hawaii: Compassion (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** being moved by another's suffering and wanting to relieve it; mercy toward those in one's power.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae, Waimānalo and Keaukaha: compare help through *ʻohana* and homestead associations with help taken from agencies, and check which feels like pity.
- Kalaupapa residents and their Topside kin: check how they want care for the exiled told, and by whom.
- Niʻihau families: sources on how they care for one another are scarce; reach them only through Kekaha kin.
- Japanese descendants: compare the *omimai* gift with formal charity, and generations against each other.
- Ilocano and Visayan families of Waipahu and Lānaʻi City: compare hometown funds with parish relief.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families of Honolulu and Kona: check help from churches and kin against what clinics and shelters report.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations and Latter-day Saint wards of Lāʻie: compare church welfare routes.
- Portuguese descendants of Upcountry Maui; Korean and Chinese church members: compare parish charity.
- Haole residents and military families: compare base relief societies with civilian giving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for houselessness policy and charity history; mixed for family aid; thin for Niʻihau and for Micronesian families' own words.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the Pele tales in the nūpepa; Ilocano and Tagalog for club records; Marshallese and Chuukese for migrants' accounts of the safety net.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** houseless camps, Compact migrants' health and Kalaupapa touch living people; quote residents' own organizations, never describe a group as needy. Prison programs need consent.
- **Held for review:** one pointer, on charity done in devotion to a figure of mercy in island Buddhist practice, is held as a category (Buddhism).
