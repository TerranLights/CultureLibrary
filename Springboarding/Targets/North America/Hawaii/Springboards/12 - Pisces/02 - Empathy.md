# Hawaii: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Korean; Cantonese; Samoan and Tongan; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Hāna, Kaʻū and Molokaʻi: check whether *naʻau* talk differs from Honolulu use, and whether *uwē* survives at funerals.
- Niʻihau speakers: compare their Hawaiian words for feeling with the dictionary's; sources are scarce and reached through families.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare unspoken reading of others across generations; do not assume the Japan frame holds.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: test *pakikiramdam* against Ilocano terms; separate recent arrivals from plantation-era kin.
- Korean families: check *nunchi* usage in church and family settings.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: compare *vā* talk with what pastors and youth leaders say.
- Marshallese and Chuukese patients: check what interpreters say goes unread by clinicians.
- Haole newcomers and military families: check how they describe learning to read local silences.
- Portuguese and Chinese descendants: look for family memoirs on being understood or misread as "local".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich for hospital measures and Japanese and Filipino frames, thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *uwē* accounts; Japanese and Tagalog for the frames; Chuukese and Marshallese interpreters for patients.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Lahaina is raw; quote residents' own organizations. Ethnic humor names groups: describe audiences' reactions, never the groups. Funeral wailing in families is private.
- **Held for review:** none; the comfort and listening pointers came from churches, schools and hospitals, and no religious or other pointer was set aside for empathy.
