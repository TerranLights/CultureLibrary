# Hawaii: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan; Spanish of Puerto Rican families; Portuguese; Cantonese; Korean; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of the homesteads and of rural Molokaʻi and Kaʻū: compare sacrifice for *ʻohana*, for *lāhui* and for church in songs and family accounts.
- Niʻihau families: sources are few; ask through Kekaha kin what they are willing to share.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: set the war generation's service beside parents' sacrifice for children; separate the two groups.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare money sent home with time given to elders here.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations of Lāʻie, Kalihi and Hilo: weigh church giving against family obligations; check pastors' and youth's accounts.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check what parents say they gave up to bring children for school and care.
- Puerto Rican, Portuguese, Korean and Chinese descendants: look for family histories that name the first generation's losses.
- Military families at Schofield, Kāneʻohe Bay and Pearl Harbor–Hickam: compare spouses' and children's accounts with service members'.
- Paniolo households of Waimea and Kula: ask which child stayed on the ranch and gave up schooling or a trade, and how siblings speak of it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war dead, missionaries and memorials; mixed for family sacrifice; thin for Puerto Rican, Micronesian and Niʻihau accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Malo, the Kekela letters and the 1893 *mele*; Japanese and Ilocano for family papers; Samoan and Tongan for church records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** temple offerings of human life are a painful subject for descendants; quote the historians and present each reading as a reading. The overthrow and Red Hill are contested; give accounts as accounts. Gold Star and Lahaina families are grieving.
- **Held for review:** no item; the vow, fast and offering pointers came from Hawaiian, Christian and Latter-day Saint sources, so nothing about giving oneself up had to be held.

3. DCAS website: download war-casualty files; filter home of record = Hawaii; group by town.
4. Hawaiian Mission Houses archive: "Kekela" Marquesas letters, 1853–1900.
5. OPTN data reports: living donors by state of residence, Hawaii, 2000–present.
