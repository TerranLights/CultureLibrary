# Hawaii: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi with Niʻihau speech; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan; Cantonese; Korean; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self losing its edges in something larger, and the ending of bonds, groups and identities.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of homesteads and rural districts (Keaukaha, Hoʻolehua, Hāna): compare how the kingdom's end and language loss are told in each.
- Niʻihau speakers: check what their community says about keeping a language unbroken; reach them through their own families.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare dementia care and language loss by generation; separate the two groups.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: test how fast Ilocano fades in Waipahu against Kalihi or Lānaʻi City.
- Plantation-camp descendants of Waialua, Kaʻū and Puʻunēnē: ask what dissolved with the mill (camp, union, store) and what did not.
- Lahaina families of every background: follow how a scattered town keeps or loses its ties, through its own groups.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations and Marshallese and Chuukese families: check which languages and church forms carry over to island-born children.
- Haole residents, military families and Portuguese, Korean and Chinese descendants: compare intermarriage and language loss with plantation-era groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the kingdom's end, depopulation, language data and intermarriage; mixed for ecstatic worship; thin for Micronesian accounts and Niʻihau.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for the *haka* accounts and 1890s *hoʻohui* debates; Japanese for family dementia accounts; Ilocano, Chuukese and Marshallese for language-loss interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** possession and mediumship are restricted knowledge; use only what was published, and point to kumu and families for the rest. The overthrow, annexation and population estimates are contested; give each account as an account. Lahaina is raw.
- **Held for review:** for crowds that move as one, a dance pointer from Buddhist practice was put aside as a category (Buddhism); the worship and plantation pointers stay.
