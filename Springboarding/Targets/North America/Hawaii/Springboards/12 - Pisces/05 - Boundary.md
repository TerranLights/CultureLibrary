# Hawaii: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi with Niʻihau speech; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Cantonese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Samoan and Tongan; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of homestead and rural districts (Waimānalo, Nānākuli, Keaukaha, Molokaʻi): compare who sets *kapu* on family land or fishing spots, and who respects it.
- The people of Niʻihau and their Kekaha kin: the closed island is the sharpest boundary in the nation; read only what they choose to share.
- Chinese, Japanese and Native Hawaiian descendants of Chinatown and Kakaʻako: compare family memory of the 1900 cordon.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: test inside/outside talk across generations and between the two groups.
- Ilocano and Visayan households and Samoan and Tongan congregations: compare whose home is open to whom.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check how housing rules and neighbors draw lines around them, in their advocates' words.
- Haole residents of gated and beachfront estates, and military families behind base gates: compare their account of privacy with neighbors'.
- Paniolo families of Waimea: check fence lines and open range in ranch memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land lines, biosecurity and the 1900 cordon; mixed for personal boundaries; thin for Niʻihau and Micronesian accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for kapu markers; Cantonese and Japanese for 1900 accounts; Ilocano and Samoan for household norms; interpreters for Chuukese and Marshallese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** heiau protocols and kapu places belong to their *kahu*; follow posted rules and ask. Niʻihau's closure is its people's choice. Abuse cases need survivors' consent. Land and access disputes are contested; give accounts as accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; kapu, quarantine, estate and church-training pointers were weighed for drawing lines, and none came under the standing rules.
