# Hawaii: Escapism (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Cantonese; Samoan and Tongan; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** seeking relief from a reality felt as harsh, dull or confining, through fantasy, intoxication, entertainment, travel, withdrawal or disappearance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae, Puna and Molokaʻi: check what counts as rest and what as escape in families' own accounts and Native Hawaiian health programs.
- Niʻihau families: thin in print; their community's own view of the outside world's pull is theirs to give.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare homeland television and travel as relief across generations, and between the two groups.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare homeland media with church and fiesta life as relief from long work hours.
- Korean, Chinese and Portuguese descendants: check family memoirs for what counted as rest and what as idleness.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: check what church youth programs offer as alternatives; studies are scarce.
- Haole newcomers: compare "rock fever" accounts with those who stay.
- Military families: compare R&R memory with present-day base leisure and leave.
- Paniolo families of Waimea and Kula: check hunting, rodeo and ranch work as escape or as duty.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for tourism image, substance-use surveys and treatment data; mixed for everyday leisure; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau views.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for *ʻawa* records; Japanese, Tagalog and Korean for homeland media; Chuukese and Marshallese for youth programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** never tie drinking, drugs or gambling to a group; report rates by place and survey, and quote treatment and recovery groups in their own words. *ʻAwa* in ceremony belongs to its practitioners.
- **Held for review:** none; the intoxication, travel and media pointers here are secular, Christian or Hawaiian, and the rules set none aside.

## 5. First queries
1. Library catalog: Malo and Kamakau indexes for "awa"; then early visitors' journals (Ellis, Bingham) for the same word.
2. SAMHSA website: NSDUH substate estimates, Hawaii regions, latest three cycles.
3. Hawaii Department of Health: "Alcohol and Drug Abuse Division" annual reports; tabulate admissions by island.
4. UH Mānoa Library and Hawaii State Archives: "Hawaii Calls" scripts and recordings; Hawaii War Records for "R&R" 1966–1972.
5. Capitol website: "lottery" OR "casino" bills, 2015–present; read testimony lists.
