# Hawaii: Spirituality (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Korean; Tagalog and Ilocano; Samoan and Tongan; Cantonese; Portuguese; Spanish of Puerto Rican families; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** a person's or group's relation to what they take as sacred, transcendent or ultimate, inside or outside organized religion (the experience and practice; faith and its institutions are Sagittarius's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli practitioners in Hilo, Puna and Kaʻū (near Pele) against Honolulu: compare offerings, chant and church life.
- Kānaka Maoli Christians of Congregational, Catholic and Latter-day Saint churches: check how they join *pule* and hymn.
- Niʻihau families: their spiritual life is their own; sources are scarce and reached through Kekaha kin.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: separate Shinto practice from other household rites, generation by generation.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare home altars and parish life by island.
- Korean congregations; Tongan, Samoan, Marshallese and Chuukese churches: compare daily, weekly and choir-centered devotion.
- Chinese families: check folk and household worship against Christian conversion in family memoirs.
- Portuguese and Puerto Rican descendants: check devotion to saints; Spiritism is thin in print, so start with parish and family sources.
- Haole seekers and military families: compare retreat-center and "spiritual but not religious" accounts with church members'.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Hawaiian chant, Christian and Shinto practice; mixed for New Age; thin for Puerto Rican, Micronesian and Niʻihau spiritual life.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for prayers and chant; Japanese for shrine records; Korean, Tagalog, Tongan and Samoan for congregations; interpreters for Marshallese and Chuukese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** hula altars, *pule* and Pele practice belong to *kumu* and families; use what they share and follow their protocols. Mauna Kea and spiritual tourism are contested; give accounts as accounts.
- **Held for review:** two pointers, one on contemplative sitting and one on a large public memorial rite, came from Buddhist practice and are held as a category; Shinto, Christian and Hawaiian pointers stand.
