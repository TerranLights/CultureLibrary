# Hawaii: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Korean; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters; Samoan and Tongan; Cantonese; Portuguese. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher, with release and letting go.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Kauaʻi and Niʻihau against those of Hawaiʻi Island: compare how 1810 is remembered on each side.
- Kānaka Maoli sovereignty groups and Kānaka Maoli in civic office: compare readings of the 1893 yielding.
- The people of Niʻihau: they have their own memory of Kauaʻi's chiefs; ask only through their families.
- Issei and Nisei of the war years, Japanese and Okinawan: set what the 1945 papers printed beside what families say they accepted, and when.
- Kalaupapa families and Topside kin: compare acceptance accounts.
- Puna households (Kapoho, Leilani, Kalapana): compare sellers and holdouts.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check how displacement and resettlement stories are told in the islands.
- Ilocano and Visayan, Korean, Samoan and Tongan families: compare end-of-life choices as each community's own studies record them.
- Haole residents and military families: compare views of quitting and of hospice with plantation-era families'.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1893, 1945 and end-of-life data; mixed for family release practices; thin for Marshallese and Niʻihau accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kamakau, the hymnal and Liliʻuokalani's papers; Japanese for 1945 press; Marshallese interpreters for Bikini memory; Ilocano and Korean for end-of-life studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the overthrow and abdication are contested; give each account as an account. Family hoʻoponopono is private. Kalaupapa and lava-zone families are living people; quote only what they have published or agreed to.
- **Held for review:** one letting-go rite tied to Buddhist practice is held as a category; the hymn, hoʻoponopono and hospice pointers stand.
