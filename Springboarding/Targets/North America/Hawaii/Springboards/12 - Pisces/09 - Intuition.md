# Hawaii: Intuition (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Korean; Cantonese; Tagalog and Ilocano; Satawalese and other Micronesian terms only through the navigators themselves; Samoan and Tongan; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** knowing or judging without conscious reasoning: the gut feeling, the hunch, the sense for people and situations.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Puna, Kaʻū and Molokaʻi against Honolulu: compare dream and *ʻaumakua* accounts; family protocols differ.
- Niʻihau families: little is published; their knowledge of sea and weather is their own to share.
- Voyaging crews of the Polynesian Voyaging Society and island canoe clubs: compare navigators' and paddlers' accounts of feel against instruments.
- Japanese and Okinawan families: compare premonition stories with Okinawan practices; keep the two apart.
- Korean, Chinese and Filipino (Ilocano, Visayan) families: compare dream-reading across generations, from each group's own writing.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: check whether navigators or elders among them are known in the islands; sources are scarce.
- Haole newcomers and military families: ask whether they credit hunches to "vibes", to God or to training, and how island-raised neighbors put it.
- Paniolo families of Waimea: check reading of horses, cattle and weather in ranch oral histories.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for navigation and wartime warnings; mixed for dream practice; thin for Micronesian, Samoan and Tongan intuition and for Niʻihau.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for nūpepa dreams; Japanese, Korean and Cantonese for family dream accounts; Ilocano for Filipino studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *ʻaumakua* and dream knowledge are family-held; never press for them. Navigation teaching has its own protocols set by navigators. Psychic trade is described, never endorsed.
- **Held for review:** none; dream books, navigators' testimony and wartime records were weighed for hunches and premonitions, and nothing was set aside.
