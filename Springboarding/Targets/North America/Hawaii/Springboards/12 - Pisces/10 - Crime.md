# Hawaii: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** what the culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. (Wrongdoing as a moral and social category; codes and courts go to Libra, legal culture to Capricorn.)

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Waiʻanae, Nānākuli, Puna and Molokaʻi: compare *hewa* and *pono* talk in families and churches with how outsiders' crime coverage frames their districts.
- Niʻihau families: check whether and how wrongs are handled inside the community; very little is in print and it is theirs to give.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: check whether family disgrace figures in accounts of a wrongdoer's relatives, and how that differs by generation.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: check how sin and crime are told apart in parish and family life.
- Samoan and Tongan congregations: check how churches and families respond when a member is charged, in their own accounts.
- Marshallese and Chuukese families: compare their accounts of being policed with what agencies report; sources are scarce.
- Haole residents, military families and recent newcomers: compare which local wrongs they condemn and which they find foreign.
- Fishing families of Hāna, Kaʻū and Molokaʻi: compare gathering rules kept by families with state rules.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for victim advocacy, mission texts and press history; mixed for wrongdoer accounts; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau perspectives.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for catechisms, *puʻuhonua* and *pōwā* tales; Japanese and Ilocano for family shame accounts; Samoan and Tongan for church responses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** never tie a wrong to a group; report by place and by source. Trafficking, family violence and missing-women cases involve living survivors; use only published reports. The syndicate era involves living families; quote the press as press.
- **Held for review:** none; the sin lists, victim groups, enforcement records and fiction raised no item under the standing rules for this concept.
