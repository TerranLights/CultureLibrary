# Hawaii: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Hawaii (the Hawaiian Islands), region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi; Pidgin; the English of Hawaii; Japanese; Ilocano and Tagalog; Samoan and Tongan; Korean; Cantonese; Portuguese; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the culture's ways of naming, showing, managing and using it, with worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kānaka Maoli of Molokaʻi, Puna and Kaʻū: compare fear of sorcery, lava and places with Honolulu accounts; follow family protocols.
- Niʻihau families: what they fear and avoid is theirs to tell; reach them through Kekaha kin.
- Japanese and Okinawan descendants: compare wartime fear (suspicion, detention) with present-day disaster worry; separate the two groups.
- Ilocano and Visayan families: compare children's fear figures and night customs with the Philippine homelands, as families tell it.
- Samoan, Tongan, Marshallese and Chuukese families: compare fear of disaster and of being treated as outsiders; scarce sources.
- Hilo and Kona households: compare tsunami, lava and dengue fear across one island.
- Haole residents and military families: compare fears of crime, sharks and missiles with island-raised neighbors'.
- Paniolo families of Waimea: check fear of drought, wildfire and Pōhakuloa's training noise.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disaster, preparedness and wartime fear; mixed for supernatural fear; thin for Micronesian and Niʻihau accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Hawaiian for Kālaipāhoa and *makaʻu* sources; Japanese and Ilocano for family lore; Marshallese and Chuukese through interpreters.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kālaipāhoa and *ʻanāʻanā* are restricted knowledge; use published accounts and point to families and practitioners for the rest. Pōhakuloa, Compact migrants and the missile alert are contested; give each account as an account, and never describe a group as feared or fearful.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the ghost, shark, disaster and outsider pointers here were weighed for fear, and none was set aside under the standing rules.
