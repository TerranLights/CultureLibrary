# Midwest Republic: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German and Low German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish, Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it shows itself: personhood, the body, appearance and naming ways.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Anishinaabeg (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi):** check, community by community, whether clan, spirit name or place comes first in self-description, and what is public at all.
- **Dakota and Ho-Chunk:** check personhood through kin terms; the published life stories differ by editor and era.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements:** check dress and photograph rules by settlement, since each Ordnung differs.
- **Hmong Minnesotans and Wisconsinites:** compare generations on clan surname, given name and adopted English name; the naming rite may persist or change.
- **Somali Minnesotans:** check how lineage recitation and given names are used by first and second generations.
- **Arab Americans of Dearborn and Chaldeans of Detroit:** separate them; check self-labels across census boxes, church, mosque and family.
- **German-, Scandinavian-, Polish- and Czech-descended Midwesterners:** check name anglicizing and name-day survival by county and parish.
- **Black Midwesterners of Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee and Gary:** compare naming and portrait habits of Great Migration arrivals and their grandchildren.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for English-language records and panel studies; mixed for Black and Latino self-description; thin in print for Menominee, Meskwaki and Myaamia. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass treats the Great Lakes states and upper Mississippi Valley as the core and the edges as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** Anishinaabemowin and Hmong for naming rites; Arabic and Neo-Aramaic for teknonyms; Polish and Czech for name days.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** spirit names, clan teachings and Hmong soul-calling are not public by default; the census race box for Arab Americans is contested.
- **Held for review:** naming customs of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands; Buddhist naming and ordination rites in some refugee communities, held under the open question on Buddhism.
