# Midwest Republic: Identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German, Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish, Polish; Spanish; Arabic and Chaldean Neo-Aramaic; Hmong; Somali; Bosnian; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Oneida, Myaamia. **Concept in one line:** what a person or group takes itself to be: the answer to "who are you?"

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **St. Louis and Chicago:** compare the introductory questions (school, parish) to see what each city's people sort by.
- **Yankee-, German- and Scandinavian-settled counties in Wisconsin, Iowa and Minnesota:** check whether church or ancestry gives the first answer, county by county.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check where "Latino" is accepted and where the national label is kept.
- **Black Midwesterners with Southern roots:** check whether "home" names the South or the northern city across generations.
- **Appalachian out-migrants in Uptown Chicago, Cincinnati, Dayton and Ypsilanti:** check how they named themselves against outsiders' labels.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis:** check which of the two Bosnian labels is used, by whom, and when.
- **Myaamia, Oneida and Shawnee:** check how removal and return shape membership; Shawnee histories now sit mostly outside the region.
- **Odawa, Potawatomi and Ojibwe bands in Michigan:** check how recognition history shapes self-description.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for white ethnic and urban histories and census data; mixed for Arab, Chaldean and Hmong; thin in print for Menominee, Meskwaki and Ho-Chunk self-description.
- **Languages needed:** Polish, German and Norwegian for the old ethnic press; Neo-Aramaic for Chaldean sources; Oneida for the WPA texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** false claims to Native identity, and enrollment rules (enrollment law goes to Libra); census race boxes for Arab Americans; clan identity among Somalis.
- **Held for review:** none set aside; the ancestry tables are pointed to whole, so no single community's study had to be weighed under the rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Open the Chicago Foreign Language Press Survey and search "Americanization" and "our nationality" in the Polish, Czech and Swedish sections.
2. Pull ACS ancestry tables for Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa and Michigan counties; map the top write-ins.

4. Contact the Myaamia Center for its published community-history materials.
5. Search "Oneida Language and Folklore Project" and list which texts are translated.
