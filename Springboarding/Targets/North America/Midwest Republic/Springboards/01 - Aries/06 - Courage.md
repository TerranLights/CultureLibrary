# Midwest Republic: Courage (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch and German; Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** acting rightly in the face of fear, danger, pain or social cost.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quakers and Black congregations of Indiana and Ohio:** compare who is named as brave in Underground Railroad memory, and who is left out.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check conscientious objection records (World War I camps; Civilian Public Service) as the courage of refusal.
- **Meskwaki, Ho-Chunk and Ojibwe veterans:** check how each nation honors service at its own gatherings, as each shares it.
- **Dakota and settler descendants in Minnesota:** read accounts of 1862 side by side; each names different acts as brave, and each account is contested.
- **Finnish, Croatian and Italian mining families in Copper Country and the Iron Range:** check which strike leaders and rescuers are remembered.
- **Black Chicagoans of the 1950s–60s:** compare the Till case and the 1966 open-housing marches for what moral courage meant to participants.
- **Hmong and Somali Minnesotans:** compare how war service and flight are framed across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Underground Railroad, wars and disasters; mixed for Native veterans' own accounts; thin for Somali and Arab Detroit material on bravery.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Croatian for strike sources; Hmong for veterans' testimony; Meskwaki for the code-talker history from inside; German for nonresistance writings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1862 and the Mankato executions; veterans' trauma; some honor songs and warrior societies are not public.
- **Held for review:** none; veterans' and award sources were weighed and none named a held category.
