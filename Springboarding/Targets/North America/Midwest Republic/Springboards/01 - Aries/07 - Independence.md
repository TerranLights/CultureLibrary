*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **German- and Norwegian-descended farm families:** compare letters, memoirs and farm-crisis accounts for how owning land and debt were spoken of.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check church aid in place of insurance and public programs, settlement by settlement.
- **White Earth, Red Lake and Menominee:** check each nation's own framing of collective self-sufficiency; histories of allotment differ.
- **Hmong households in the Twin Cities, La Crosse and Wausau:** check gardening and hunting as provisioning, and the record of hunting conflicts with neighbors as a contested account.
- **Young adults in rural Iowa and in Chicago or the Twin Cities:** compare when each leaves home and pays their own way.
- **Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check the range of views on the island's status within one community.
- **Black Chicago and Detroit self-help institutions:** check churches, credit unions and religious business programs, each by its own account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler letters, farm sources and federal statistics; mixed for Native food sovereignty in print; thin for Arab and Somali household self-reliance studies.
- **Languages needed:** German for letters; Spanish for Puerto Rican movement papers; Anishinaabemowin for food-sovereignty terms; Somali for festival programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Puerto Rican independence history includes prosecutions and surveillance; Hmong hunting conflicts in Wisconsin are a painful contested account; public assistance carries stigma.
- **Held for review:** none; the food-sovereignty and festival pointers raised nothing under the standing rules.
