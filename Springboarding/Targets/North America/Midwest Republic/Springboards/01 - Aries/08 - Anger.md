# Midwest Republic: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Chicagoans and Detroiters:** compare how 1919, 1943 and 1967 are named and explained by narrators of different ages.
- **White ethnic neighborhoods on Milwaukee's South Side and Chicago's Southwest Side:** check counter-protest accounts from residents themselves, as accounts.
- **Rural Wisconsin and Madison or Milwaukee:** compare where resentment is aimed and in what words.
- **Flint residents:** check meeting transcripts and oral histories for whose anger was heard.
- **Amish and Mennonites:** check what their own periodicals ask of a person who is angry.
- **Mexican Chicagoans:** check folk-healing sources for the link between anger and illness, and whether it holds in the second generation.
- **Hmong families:** check how clan leaders mediate household disputes.
- **Dakota and settler descendants in Minnesota:** read 1862 accounts side by side; each account is contested.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, inquiries and labor violence; mixed for everyday household anger; thin for Somali, Arab and Hmong emotion vocabularies in local sources.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish for folk-illness terms; Hmong for mediation; Dakota for 1862 narratives; Polish and Czech for confession guides.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1862 and the Mankato executions; Detroit 1967; killings at Kent State; debates around Dearborn. Criminal law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; folk-healing and religious sources on anger were weighed and none fell under the open questions.
