# Midwest Republic: Recklessness (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; German, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** courage or initiative pushed past its useful range; acting without regard for danger or consequence.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish youth in Elkhart-LaGrange and Holmes County:** check, by settlement, how risky behavior before baptism is handled; outside portrayals are contested.
- **Rural Wisconsin and Minnesota counties:** compare drinking, ice and snowmobile data county by county rather than by reputation.
- **Detroit-area car communities:** check which racing was praised and which was punished, and by whom.
- **Farm families of Iowa, Illinois and Indiana:** check how injuries are spoken of in farm papers and safety campaigns.
- **Irish Chicagoans:** check how the 1871 blame story was answered within the community.
- **Mexican Chicagoans:** check when *aventado* is praise and when blame, by generation.
- **Lutheran congregations of the ELCA, Missouri Synod and Wisconsin Synod:** compare sermons and youth materials on drinking, gambling and dancing (Concordia Historical Institute, St. Louis *check*), since synod teaching differs.
- **Dutch Reformed West Michigan:** check church discipline records (Heritage Hall, Calvin University *check*) for what was named reckless living.
- **Red River Valley towns such as Moorhead and East Grand Forks:** check 1997 flood records for when staying to fight the water was praised and when called reckless.
- **Anishinaabeg:** check how each community frames teaching stories about rash acts, and when they may be told.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for injury statistics, insurance and safety history; mixed for folk belief; thin for Arab, Hmong and Somali community sources on youth risk.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Polish for the Lomax recordings; Spanish for youth outreach; Anishinaabemowin for teaching stories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** alcohol rates can feed regional stereotypes, so present them as data with context; Prohibition gangs are often glamorized; some Anishinaabe stories are seasonal. Traffic and gambling law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the gambling and drinking pointers were weighed and none touched a held category.
