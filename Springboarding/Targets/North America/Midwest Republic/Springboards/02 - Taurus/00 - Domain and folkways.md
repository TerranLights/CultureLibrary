# Midwest Republic: Possessions and value, domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** the American English of the Midwest (Inland North, North Central and Midland speech); Pennsylvania Dutch; Midwestern German and Low German; Norwegian, Swedish and Finnish; Polish; Czech; Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin (Ojibwe), Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee and Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value (what is worth having, and how is it held?), with the folkways of food, dress and wealth.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chicagoland against downstate Illinois and Iowa:** check whether catalogs, fairs and spending tables point the same way in city and farm counties.
- **Dearborn and Detroit's Chaldeans:** separate Lebanese, Yemeni, Iraqi and Chaldean records, since their stores and foods sit in different archives.
- **Anishinaabeg (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi), Menominee and Ho-Chunk:** check which harvests and goods each nation itself names, before any outside ethnography.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements (Elkhart-LaGrange; Kalona, Iowa; Arthur, Illinois):** compare church districts, whose dress and household rules differ.
- **Upper Peninsula and Iron Range:** check Finnish, Cornish, Italian and Slovene mining households, where company stores set what could be bought.
- **St. Louis and Kansas City:** check Bosnian, Hill Italian and Westside Mexican food records outside the Great Lakes core.
- **Hmong and Somali Minnesotans:** compare the 1970s-80s and 1990s-2000s arrivals, recorded by different agencies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cities, land-grant states and European-descended settlers; mixed for Black Midwesterners; thinner for Ho-Chunk, Meskwaki, Somali and Bosnian households, where community archives come first.
- **Languages needed:** German and Norwegian for nineteenth-century press; Polish and Czech for parish records; Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali for living households; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for the nations' own words for food and goods.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** regalia, ceremonial food and giveaways belong to the nations that hold them; use only what they publish. Debates around Dearborn are contested; cite them as accounts. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** for the domain's food and dress customs, the pointers drawn from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands wait under the standing rule, as a category.
