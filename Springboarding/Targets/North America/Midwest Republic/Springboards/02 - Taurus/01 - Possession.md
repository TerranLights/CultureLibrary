# Midwest Republic: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German and Pennsylvania Dutch; Norwegian and Swedish; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Bosnian; Anishinaabemowin (Ojibwe) and Dakota. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Norwegian and Swedish settlers against Polish and Czech city parishes:** check what each carried and kept, since trunk collections and letter archives survive unevenly.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota and Ho-Chunk:** separate each nation's account of dispossession, removal and returned objects; their histories differ in date and place.
- **Japanese American resettlers and Bosnian refugees:** compare what forced movers say they saved, from sources each community controls.
- **Detroit and Gary against Iowa farm towns:** check how abandonment, storage and secondhand trade differ where population fell and where it held.
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonite households:** check household inventories against church rules on goods, district by district.
- **Hmong and Somali families in the Twin Cities:** check what is kept for weddings, funerals and New Year, and who keeps it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inventories, letters and museum holdings of European settlers; mixed for Black Midwesterners and Japanese Americans; thin and community-held for Somali, Hmong and Bosnian keepsakes.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Norwegian for letters; French for the colonial inventories; Ojibwe and Dakota for possessive grammar; Somali, Hmong and Bosnian for living memory.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** objects of the dead, sacred objects and anything under repatriation; enslaved people listed as property in French and early American records; Japanese American losses.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist teaching on nonattachment and the nation's Buddhist communities, as a category only (an open question for the author).
