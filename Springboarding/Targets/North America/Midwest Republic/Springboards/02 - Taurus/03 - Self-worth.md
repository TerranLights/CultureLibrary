# Midwest Republic: Self-worth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Norwegian; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin (Ojibwe) and Dakota. **Concept in one line:** the value a person holds themselves to have, and what that value is believed to rest on.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lutheran, Reformed and Catholic congregations:** check sermons and catechisms on pride, humility and grace, since teaching on worth differs by synod and denomination.
- **Laid-off autoworkers of Flint, Kenosha and Detroit against Iowa farm families of the 1980s:** check how each crisis was narrated by those who lived it.
- **Anishinaabeg and Dakota boarding-school survivors:** check survivors' own testimony, school by school (Tomah, Mount Pleasant, Pipestone, Morris).
- **Black Midwesterners of Bronzeville and Detroit:** check the press, parades and church records that honored and defended worth.
- **Hmong elders and their children:** check how standing is described across generations and between women and men.
- **Arab, Chaldean and Somali Midwesterners:** compare Dearborn, West Bloomfield and Cedar-Riverside sources on honor and shame terms.
- **Amish church districts:** check how *Hochmut* is named and corrected, district by district.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for working-class oral history and religious writing; good youth and cohort surveys; thin for Somali and Ho-Chunk self-description outside community organizations.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania Dutch and German for teaching on pride; Arabic, Somali and Hmong for honor and face terms; Ojibwe and Dakota for teachings on humility and respect.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and hopelessness data; boarding-school testimony (survivors decide its use); post-2001 surveillance of Dearborn, a contested account.
- **Held for review:** none; the cohort and youth surveys and the scholars named were all checked against the standing rules.
