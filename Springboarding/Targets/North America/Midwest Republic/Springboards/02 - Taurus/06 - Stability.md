## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Century-farm families of Iowa and Wisconsin against Chicago and Twin Cities renters:** check tenure and moving data, since stability may be measured differently in each.
- **Amana and Amish communities:** check how communal and church rules keep routines, and where they let change in.
- **Ojibwe, Odawa and Myaamia communities:** check each people's own account of continuity through removal and seasonal life.
- **Black Detroit and St. Louis:** check clearance records alongside residents' oral histories, block by block.
- **Iron Range and Upper Peninsula mining towns:** compare Finnish, Slovene and Italian accounts of booms and closures.
- **Hmong, Bosnian and Somali arrivals:** check how first and second moves are recorded and remembered, family by family.
- **Mexican and Tejano families who settled out:** check Michigan, Ohio and Wisconsin town records for when seasonal work became settled life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land, newspaper and census records; mixed for refugee and migrant families, where studies date quickly.
- **Languages needed:** German for Amana records; Finnish and Slovene for range towns; Polish for farm-settlement parish books; Hmong, Bosnian and Spanish for arrival stories; Ojibwe and Myaamia for seasonal and community words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** removal histories; freeway and renewal clearances are contested; trait rankings by state risk stereotype and must be framed as published accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; the tenure, mobility and refugee-resettlement sources considered for staying put came from outside every held category.
