# Midwest Republic: Wealth (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Prairie Avenue, Summit Avenue and Grosse Pointe households against their Irish, Swedish and Black domestic staff:** check servants' letters and memoirs, which show wealth from below.
- **Black entrepreneurs of Indianapolis, Detroit and Bronzeville:** check business records and the press for how fortunes were built under segregation.
- **Chaldean, Lebanese and Yemeni merchant families:** separate their store histories, since each came by a different route.
- **Gaming nations and nations without large casinos:** compare Shakopee Mdewakanton, Ho-Chunk, Menominee and Meskwaki reports, nation by nation.
- **Iowa and Illinois landowners against tenants:** check land-value and tenure data together, since farm wealth sits mostly in land.
- **Hmong clans:** check council guidelines against families' own wedding and funeral records.
- **West Michigan Reformed families:** check their own statements on wealth and faith, rather than outside commentary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for elite houses, land values and tax data; mixed for immigrant merchant wealth; tribal wealth must come from each nation's own reports.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic and Sureth for merchant families; Hmong for bride-wealth debates; Somali for wealth in herds and trade; German and Polish for nineteenth-century fortunes; Dakota and Ho-Chunk for each nation's terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal per capita payments and enrollment; the Grosse Pointe screening and other exclusions are contested; bride wealth is debated inside the Hmong community.
- **Held for review:** a single wealth-data source is withheld under the South Asian rule; category only: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
