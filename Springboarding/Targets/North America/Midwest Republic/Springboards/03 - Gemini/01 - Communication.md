# Midwest Republic: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (Upper Midwest and Detroit-Chicago speech), Low German, Polish, Mexican and Puerto Rican Spanish, Arabic and Sureth, Hmong, Somali, ASL, Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media, and the ideas held about what good communication is and is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Lutheran Scandinavian and German congregations of Minnesota and Wisconsin, Catholic Polish and Irish parishes of Chicago and Milwaukee:** check how liturgy, testimony and parish meetings set who speaks and in what order.
- **Black Baptist, Methodist and Pentecostal churches of Detroit, Chicago and St. Louis:** check the sermon as training for public speech outside the church.
- **Deaf communities around the state schools:** check ASL norms of attention, turn-taking and storytelling in their own accounts.
- **Amish and Mennonite affiliations of Indiana, Ohio and Iowa:** check how telephone rules and circle letters shape communication at a distance.
- **Anishinaabeg, Meskwaki and Ho-Chunk:** check listening and careful-speech teachings, and code-talker memory, each as its own nation tells it.
- **Dearborn's Yemeni and Lebanese households and Detroit's Chaldeans:** check who interprets for whom at clinics and schools, and in which language.
- **Michoacán- and Guerrero-origin families in Chicago, Puerto Ricans of Humboldt Park:** check address forms and talk about frankness separately.
- **Hmong and Somali families:** check whether sung and recited poetry still carries messages between generations, and how this differs by city.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Black Detroit and Chicago speech, Chicago Latino families and recorded interviews; mixed for Arabic and Sureth; thin for Ho-Chunk, Menominee and Bosnian talk norms, where tribal language departments and community oral histories come first.
- **Languages needed:** Spanish, Arabic and Sureth for the clinics and churches; Hmong and Somali for oral poetry; ASL for Deaf sources; Anishinaabemowin for elders' texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** code talkers' recordings belong to their Nations and families; ask before quoting. Black Hawk's *Life* passed through an interpreter and an editor, so whose voice it carries is debated.
- **Held for review:** a free-speech case from Des Moines schools (*Tinker*, 1969) was set aside as law, for Libra; no other pointer was held.
