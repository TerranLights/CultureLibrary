*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black readers in Chicago, Detroit, Kansas City and St. Louis:** check the Black press against the white-owned dailies over the same years.
- **German, Polish, Czech, Norwegian and Swedish readers:** check when each language's press closed and what took its place.
- **Amish and Mennonite affiliations in Ohio, Indiana and Wisconsin:** check scribe papers and circle letters against each group's limits on phones and computers.
- **Farm counties of Iowa, Illinois and Indiana and the Red River Valley:** check Extension, farm radio and commodity apps as rival sources.
- **GLIFWC member bands, the Myaamia, the Menominee:** check how each governs its own data and which knowledge it keeps restricted.
- **Dearborn's Arab and Chaldean residents:** check trust in official data and willingness to answer surveys, by origin and generation.
- **Flint and Detroit residents:** check community testing and organizing as information sources in the water crisis.
- **Spanish-speaking Chicago and southwest Detroit; Hmong and Somali households:** check in-language access to public-health and census information.
- **Northwoods, Upper Peninsula and the Iron Range:** check broadband gaps and closures of local papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the press (Chronicling America, state historical societies' newspaper microfilm); mixed for farm information; thin for Somali, Hmong and Menominee media archives, where community radio stations and the Hmong Archives in St. Paul are the next stop.
- **Languages needed:** German, Polish and Czech for the old press; Arabic for Dearborn; Hmong and Somali for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Midewiwin birchbark scrolls and other restricted Anishinaabe knowledge are not to be sought out; use only what the communities publish. Flint and post-2001 Dearborn are contested ground: read each account as an account.
- **Held for review:** state open-records acts and the Flint lawsuits are Libra's letter-of-the-law material, so they stay out.
