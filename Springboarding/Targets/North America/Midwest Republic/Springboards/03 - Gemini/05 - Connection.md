# Midwest Republic: Connection (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Norwegian and Swedish; Polish; Bosnian; Mexican and Puerto Rican Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** the ties that link people to one another, and the work of making, using and keeping them: keeping in touch, introducing, networking, and the felt sense of being connected (or not).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Norwegian, Swedish and Finnish heritage societies; German Turners; Polish and Czech fraternal orders:** check what each association did to keep ties to the home district and among members.
- **Appalachian families in Dayton, Hamilton, Detroit and Uptown:** check weekend trips home and storefront churches as tie-keepers.
- **Great Migration families of Bronzeville and Detroit:** check the clubs and churches that linked them to Mississippi, Alabama and Georgia.
- **Mexican hometown federations in Illinois (Michoacán, Jalisco, Guanajuato, Zacatecas):** check projects funded back home and who leads them.
- **Puerto Ricans of Humboldt Park and Lorain, Ohio:** check back-and-forth ties to the island, by generation.
- **Hmong clans, Somali families, Bosnians of St. Louis, Chaldeans from Telkaif and nearby villages:** check how each keeps overseas ties, without mapping clan or lineage onto people.
- **Anishinaabe doodem, Dakota thiyóšpaye, Ho-Chunk clans:** check how far kin ties reach, as each nation describes it.
- **Rural Iowa and Wisconsin counties and Chicago's isolated elders:** check isolation measures side by side.
- **Detroit across from Windsor, the Twin Ports, the Upper Peninsula across the Mackinac Bridge:** check bridges and crossings as everyday links (Canada as comparison only).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for immigrant associations and Black Chicago; mixed for Mexican hometown clubs; thin for Bosnian and Somali internal ties, best approached through community organizations and their own histories.
- **Languages needed:** Norwegian and Polish for society minutes; Spanish for hometown clubs; Hmong and Somali for kin networks; Arabic and Sureth for Chaldean and Dearborn families.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Somali money transfer after 2001 and clan affiliation are both sensitive; do not chart clans. Hmong clan disputes are internal matters.
- **Held for review:** Chicago patronage ("clout") and the court decrees against political hiring were weighed as ties-for-advantage, then passed to Capricorn's merit and legal-culture sheets.
