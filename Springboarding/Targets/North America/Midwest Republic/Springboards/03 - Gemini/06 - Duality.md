# Midwest Republic: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English (including Upper Peninsula usage); Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Finnish; Polish; Chicago Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** how a culture lives with two-ness: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how it treats people who show two faces.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **German Americans of Cincinnati, Milwaukee and St. Louis:** check how wartime pressure on a second language played out city by city.
- **Amish youth of Elkhart-LaGrange and Holmes County:** check whether the years between church and world run alike in both.
- **Mexican, Puerto Rican and MexiRican Chicagoans:** check which heritage each names first, and when.
- **Dearborn's Lebanese and Yemeni families and Detroit's Chaldean Catholics:** check loyalty questions in community-led reports, by origin and faith.
- **Ho-Chunk, Meskwaki and Sauk:** check ceremonial halves, only as each nation chooses to describe them.
- **Myaamia of Indiana and Oklahoma; Potawatomi bands of Michigan and Wisconsin:** check nations divided by removal (the western parts as comparison only).
- **Two-Spirit Anishinaabe, Dakota and Ho-Chunk people:** check their own terms rather than English glosses.
- **Yoopers and Trolls; Minneapolis and St. Paul; St. Louis and East St. Louis:** check joking and serious paired identities.
- **Black writers of Dayton, Chicago and Detroit:** check the mask and double consciousness across generations.
- **Hmong 1.5 and second generations:** check language and clan duties held alongside public school and work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for German Americans and Black literature; mixed for Latino mixed heritage; thin and old for Ho-Chunk and Sauk dual organization, so begin with the Nations' cultural departments.
- **Languages needed:** German for wartime and school records; Spanish for Chicago families; Arabic for Dearborn; Hmong; Anishinaabemowin for Two-Spirit terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Two-Spirit identity is for Two-Spirit people and their nations to define. Post-2001 Dearborn and the removals are contested; present each as an account.
- **Held for review:** Wisconsin's 1889 school-language statute (the Bennett Law) was weighed for the bilingual-school story and passed to Libra.
