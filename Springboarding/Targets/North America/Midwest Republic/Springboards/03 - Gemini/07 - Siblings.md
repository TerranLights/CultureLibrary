# Midwest Republic: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German and Norwegian (farm-family records); Polish; Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how a culture understands the sibling bond: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonite families:** check older children's work roles and which child takes over the farm.
- **German and Norwegian farm families of Wisconsin, Minnesota and Iowa:** check how brothers and sisters divided or kept the land, by county.
- **Great Migration families of Chicago and Detroit:** check siblings who came north one after another, and those who stayed South.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago families:** check eldest daughters as caregivers and interpreters, by generation.
- **Hmong and Somali families:** check who counts as a brother or sister, and how far the term reaches.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota and Ho-Chunk:** check sibling terms by age and sex and whether cousins are counted as siblings, nation by nation.
- **Arab and Chaldean family businesses of Detroit:** check siblings as co-owners and successors.
- **Catholic women's orders and UAW locals:** check how non-kin "sisters" and "brothers" use the words.
- **Boarding-school survivors and their families:** check siblings separated, in the nations' own accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for twin research, longitudinal surveys and literature; mixed for immigrant caregiving; thin for Meskwaki and Ho-Chunk kin usage today and for Bosnian families, where tribal language programs and community centers come first.
- **Languages needed:** Hmong and Somali for who counts as kin; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for kin terms; Spanish and Arabic for caregiving studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** twin-study participants are protected; work from published results. Sibling violence and boarding-school separations involve living people.
- **Held for review:** partition suits among heirs over family farms are court matters, so they went to Libra.
