# Midwest Republic: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; German; Norwegian; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; French (colonial treaty records). **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision; more broadly, the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mennonites and Amish of Elkhart-LaGrange:** check church reconciliation against the secular programs that grew from it.
- **Catholic parishes (Walloon Belgians of northeast Wisconsin, Mexican Chicagoans, Polish Milwaukee):** check devotions to intercessors and priests as go-betweens.
- **Lutheran congregations of several synods:** check how pastors are asked into family disputes.
- **Black Chicago and Detroit:** check churches, block clubs and street mediators, and how the 1919 and 1967 commissions are read today.
- **UAW locals and Gary steelworkers:** check stewards and state mediators in grievances.
- **Iowa and Minnesota farm families:** check farmer-lender mediation from the farmers' side.
- **Hmong clans:** check *mej koob* and clan elders in marriage and quarrels.
- **Somali elders in Minneapolis and Columbus:** check elders' councils beside city agencies.
- **Dearborn imams and Chaldean priests:** check clergy and *wāsṭa* in family and business disputes.
- **Anishinaabe, Dakota, Ho-Chunk and Meskwaki nations:** check peacemaking and circle practice in each nation's own description.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history and restorative-justice programs; mixed for urban peacemaking; thin for Somali and Hmong mediation, which is largely internal, so community organizations' public materials come first.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial council records; Hmong and Somali for elders' practice; Arabic for Dearborn; Spanish for shrine and parish sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** mediation around domestic violence and gang conflict involves people at risk; treaty councils and the 1919 and 1967 reports are contested accounts.
- **Held for review:** tribal peacemaker courts were noted for Libra, being courts; one scholarly pointer was set aside under the rule on South Asian-heritage scholars and stands here only as that category.
