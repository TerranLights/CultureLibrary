# Midwest Republic: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** English; German; Dutch; Norwegian and Swedish; Polish; Czech; Mexican and Puerto Rican Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; rumor (unconfirmed reports passed from person to person) is researched here too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small towns of Iowa, Indiana and central Illinois, and Chicago neighborhoods:** check fiction's small-town talk against what correspondents actually printed.
- **West Michigan Dutch Reformed and Norwegian Lutheran congregations:** check church discipline for slander beside everyday talk.
- **Amish settlements:** check how talk relates to church discipline, using only what Amish sources publish.
- **Black Chicago and Detroit:** check rumor in 1919, 1943 and 1967 as residents and officials each recorded it, and barbershop and salon talk today.
- **Somali Minneapolis:** check how community health workers answered rumors, in their own reports.
- **Hmong St. Paul:** check call-in radio and clan reputation.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago:** check *el qué dirán* and *chisme* across generations.
- **Dearborn:** check sermons on *ghība* and reputation in close neighborhoods.
- **Anishinaabeg and Dakota:** check teachings on speaking of others, as each nation states them.
- **Men's gathering places (taverns, supper clubs, deer camps, co-op coffee groups):** check men's talk about others under other names.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in literature, radio and newspapers; mixed in rumor research; thin in refugee communities, where talk stays internal and community organizations are the respectful route.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch and Norwegian for church minutes; Spanish for *chisme*; Somali and Hmong for community media; Arabic for sermons.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1943 and 1967 riots and the 2017 outbreak are contested accounts; old columns and church minutes name real families.
- **Held for review:** libel suits over small-town papers are Libra's material and stayed out.

## 5. First queries
1. Search a Wisconsin or Iowa rural weekly for correspondents' columns from one township, 1900–1950.

4. Read Lee and Humphrey's rumor chapter beside the Detroit press of June 1943.
5. Pull Midwestern states' YRBS bullying items and the tightness scores together.
