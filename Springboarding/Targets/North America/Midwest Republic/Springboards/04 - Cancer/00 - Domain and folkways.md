# Midwest Republic: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English (Inland North, North Central, Midland); Spanish; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish; Czech; Dutch; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** the sign's domain, home and family, with its folkways (building ways, family ways, child-rearing ways): what is home, and who is family?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements** (Elkhart-LaGrange, Kalona) beside their non-Plain neighbors: check where the household line falls when grandparents move into the attached house.
- **Black Midwesterners of the Great Migration** (Bronzeville, Detroit's former Black Bottom): compare census households with lodger and kin-chain accounts.
- **Dearborn's Lebanese, Yemeni and Iraqi families; Detroit's Chaldeans**: check whether a source pools them.
- **Hmong of St. Paul, Wausau and La Crosse**: check whether clan is recorded beside the household.
- **Somali Minneapolis** (Cedar-Riverside): check how *reer* maps onto a census household.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida of Wisconsin, Meskwaki**: check each nation's own publications for who counts as a relative, on reservation and in Minneapolis or Chicago.
- **German-, Scandinavian-, Finnish-, Polish-, Czech- and Dutch-descended families**: check house types by settlement (Driftless, West Michigan, Iron Range, Upper Peninsula).
- **Appalachian out-migrant families** (Cincinnati, Dayton, Chicago's Uptown): check the "back home" tie in their records.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago** (Pilsen, Little Village, Humboldt Park): check godparent ties in parish records, kept apart by origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census-based household counts (IPUMS, ACS) and for the big immigrant groups; thinner for Meskwaki, Menominee, Ho-Chunk and Chaldean households, where the nations' and communities' own channels come first. The Midwest Republic's exact lands are still to be settled; this pass writes for the Great Lakes states and the upper Mississippi Valley, and treats Appalachian Ohio, western Pennsylvania, the Plains, the CSA and New England as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** German, Pennsylvania Dutch and Scandinavian languages for settlers' household papers; Polish, Czech and Dutch for parish records; Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali for living families; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Anishinaabe clan and ceremonial knowledge and birchbark records only as the communities share them; boarding-school separations; family law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** one household-study candidate drew on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands and was set aside as a category.
