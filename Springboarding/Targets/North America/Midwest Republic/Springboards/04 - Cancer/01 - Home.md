# Midwest Republic: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Spanish; German; Polish; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Czech; Dutch; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota; Ho-Chunk. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Owners and renters in Chicago's two-flats and Milwaukee's Polish flats**: check how renting a floor of a relative's house is counted.
- **Black homeowners in Detroit's neighborhoods and Chicago's West Side**: check how home is described where houses were bought on contract or later stood vacant (the Rust Belt account is contested).
- **Dearborn's Southend (Yemeni) and east Dearborn (Lebanese)**: check whether "home" in interviews points to the house or to the village of origin.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis's Bevo Mill area**: check how buying and repairing old houses after the 1990s war is described.
- **Hmong of St. Paul's East Side; Somali of Cedar-Riverside**: check home altars and the move from apartments to houses.
- **Ho-Chunk, Dakota, Meskwaki, Menominee**: check each nation's own words for homeland against reservation and town.
- **Iron Range and Upper Peninsula mining families**: check company "locations" and the camp or cabin as second home.
- **Amish of Elkhart-LaGrange**: check house-building and the grandparents' move within the farmstead.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (housing censuses, HOLC maps, house catalogs, a deep novel shelf); thinner for what home means to the Meskwaki, Menominee and Ho-Chunk outside their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Czech for building-and-loan papers on buying houses; Norwegian and Swedish for homesick letters; Arabic, Hmong and Somali for today's homes; Dakota and Ho-Chunk for homeland place names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** homeland place names and sites only as the nations share them; eviction and contract records name private families; housing law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; the pointers weighed for house altars and house blessings came from communities the standing rules allow, so nothing on this sheet was set aside.
