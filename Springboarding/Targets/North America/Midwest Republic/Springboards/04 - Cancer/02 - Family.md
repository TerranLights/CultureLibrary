# Midwest Republic: Family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Spanish; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Polish; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Bosnian; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** the people a person counts as their own, by blood, marriage, adoption, ritual or choice, and what they owe one another.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Midwesterners in Chicago, Detroit and Milwaukee**: check kin networks across several households against census family counts.
- **Polish families in Chicago and Hamtramck, Czech families in Cedar Rapids**: check how first-generation letters and later reunion booklets differ in who is counted.
- **Amish and Mennonite families** (Elkhart-LaGrange, Kalona, the Driftless): check how far *Freindschaft* reaches in visiting records.
- **Hmong clans in St. Paul and Wisconsin**: check where clan mediation sits beside the nuclear household.
- **Chaldeans, Lebanese and Yemenis around Detroit**: check family enterprise and village ties as separate threads.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans**: check godparent ties in baptism records and in interviews.
- **Ho-Chunk, Dakota and Anishinaabe families**: check clan and adoption in each nation's own publications.
- **Iowa and Illinois farm families**: check succession and who keeps the home farm.
- **Bosnian families in St. Louis; Appalachian out-migrant families in Cincinnati**: check kin left behind and kin brought over.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (the region hosts major family panels and huge genealogical holdings); thin for Chaldean and Meskwaki family structure outside community sources.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Pennsylvania Dutch for letter collections and scribes' columns; Hmong and Somali for clan terms; Arabic in Yemeni and Levantine forms; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for kin terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan membership and adoption are the nations' own business; DNA and adoption records touch living people; custody and adoption law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; family-law pointers on adoption and custody were sent on to Libra, not held, and no family-study pointer needed setting aside.
