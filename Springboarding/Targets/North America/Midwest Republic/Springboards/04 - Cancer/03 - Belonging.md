# Midwest Republic: Belonging (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Spanish; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Polish; Norwegian; Bosnian; Arabic; Somali; Hmong; Oneida; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota; Ho-Chunk; Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** being accepted as part of a group or a place, and feeling so: being "one of us".

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Peninsula and downstate Michigan; Chicago and downstate Illinois**: check how each side names the other, since belonging may be drawn inside a state.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities and their "English" neighbors**: check membership by baptism against residence.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida and Meskwaki**: check each nation's own statements on who belongs, and how urban members in Chicago, Milwaukee and Minneapolis are counted (citizenship rules are the nations' own business).
- **Dearborn's Lebanese, Yemeni and Iraqi residents; Detroit-area Chaldeans**: check how belonging to Dearborn, to a church or mosque and to a home village interact.
- **Somali Minnesotans and Hmong Minnesotans and Wisconsinites**: check second-generation accounts against their parents'.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis**: check *komšiluk* in a new city.
- **Black Midwesterners with Southern roots**: check homecomings "down South" as a second belonging.
- **Small-town Iowans and Hoosiers who left and came back**: check what return is called.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fraternal, parish and immigrant-society records; mixed for felt belonging, which needs interviews and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** Norwegian and Polish for society minutes; Oneida for the WPA stories; Arabic, Somali and Hmong for community media; Pennsylvania Dutch for church-district terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal citizenship and enrollment disputes; Dearborn debates and urban segregation are contested ground, presented as accounts.
- **Held for review:** none; the welcoming-city, newcomer and congregation pointers weighed for belonging all came from groups the standing rules allow.
