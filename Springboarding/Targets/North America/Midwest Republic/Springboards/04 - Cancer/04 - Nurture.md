## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Anishinaabe and Dakota families**: check teaching by watching and doing, as each nation's schools and elders describe it, against outsiders' early records.
- **German- and Czech-descended towns** (Watertown, St. Louis, Cedar Rapids): check kindergarten and gymnastics societies as two routes to raising children.
- **Amish and Mennonite families of Elkhart-LaGrange and Iowa**: check school readers and chores against public-school curricula.
- **Black families in Bronzeville and Detroit**: check community-wide institutions (parades, church youth departments) alongside parents.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans**: check *consejos* and *educación* in parenting programs, by origin and generation.
- **Hmong, Somali and Arab American families**: check parents' and second-generation accounts separately, since they may describe nurture differently.
- **Iowa, Indiana and Wisconsin farm families**: check 4-H and Extension records for how chores were framed.
- **Swedish, Finnish and Dutch settlers**: check parish and temperance youth work in Minnesota, the Upper Peninsula and West Michigan.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (the region produced landmark early-childhood studies and long-running surveys); thinner for Somali and Meskwaki child-rearing outside community sources.
- **Languages needed:** German for kindergarten and Turner papers; Pennsylvania Dutch alongside English for Amish schooling; Somali and Hmong for parent interviews; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for teaching terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coming-of-age teachings of Native nations only as they share them; child-welfare and schooling law (including Amish schooling) goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** none; the child-development research centers weighed here were named as institutions, not through individual scholars, so no one had to be set aside.
