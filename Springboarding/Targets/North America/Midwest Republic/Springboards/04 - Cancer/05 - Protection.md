*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Anishinaabeg of White Earth, Leech Lake, Bad River and Fond du Lac**: check who speaks as protector of water and rice, and how that is chosen.
- **Dakota communities (Lower Sioux, Prairie Island, Shakopee Mdewakanton, Upper Sioux)**: check how 1862 is told in each, against settler-descendant accounts in New Ulm.
- **Amish and Mennonites**: check what protection means where force is refused, by settlement.
- **Black Detroiters and Chicagoans**: check accounts of defending homes and blocks, decade by decade.
- **Flint residents**: check parents' and pediatricians' accounts against officials'.
- **Hmong veterans' families in Minnesota and Wisconsin**: check how the war in Laos is told as protecting kin.
- **Upper Peninsula Finns and Italians; Iron Range miners**: check strike-era accounts of guarding families.
- **Red River Valley and Iowa river towns**: check flood fights as a community duty.
- **Rural Wisconsin and Michigan hunters and suburban Chicagoans**: check what "home defense" covers in each.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters and newspapers; mixed for felt protection; contested accounts (1862, sundown towns, Detroit, pipelines) need both sides' own words.
- **Languages needed:** German for Mennonite nonresistance papers; Finnish and Italian for Copper Country records; Hmong for veterans' oral histories; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for protector terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Dakota 1862 descendants on both sides; protective ceremonies of Native nations only as shared; ongoing pipeline disputes.
- **Held for review:** none; the gun-law and policing pointers this concept draws were passed to Libra and Capricorn, not held.
