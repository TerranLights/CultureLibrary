## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ojibwe communities of Red Lake, Leech Lake and Lac du Flambeau**: check care during epidemics and boarding-school years as Ojibwe historians tell it.
- **Lutherans of different synods (Norwegian, Swedish, German)**: check deaconess work and funeral-lunch customs by synod, since they may differ.
- **Catholic parish communities (Polish, Mexican, Irish, German)**: check sisters' hospitals against parish sodalities as sources of care.
- **Black churchgoers in Chicago and Detroit**: check nurses' guilds and church mothers as a parallel care system.
- **Hmong families in St. Paul and Wisconsin**: check who decides on care, the household or the clan.
- **Somali Minnesotans; Arab Americans of Dearborn; Bosnians of St. Louis**: check paid family caregiving and its words in each.
- **Amish of Elkhart-LaGrange and Iowa**: check care of the old within the farmstead.
- **Rural Iowa, the Northwoods and the Upper Peninsula**: check distance to care against neighbors' help.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (major aging panels based in Michigan and Wisconsin, deep hospital archives); thinner for care inside Somali, Hmong and Meskwaki families outside community studies.
- **Languages needed:** German and Norwegian for deaconess records; Polish and Czech for benevolent-society minutes; Somali, Hmong and Arabic for family caregivers' own words; Anishinaabemowin for healing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Native healing knowledge only as shared; patient records; health-care law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** none; care-ethics theorists and healer traditions were weighed, and only Midwestern institutions and community sources were kept, with nothing set aside.
