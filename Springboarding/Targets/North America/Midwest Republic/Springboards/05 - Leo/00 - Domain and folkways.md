# Midwest Republic: Domain and folkways (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English (Inland North, North Central, Midland); Pennsylvania Dutch; German, Low German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish, Czech, Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Hoocąk (Ho-Chunk), Menominee, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** how people shine, play and take pleasure (romance, children, celebration, fame, gambling, sport ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chicagoland against downstate Illinois:** check whether club records and fair-and-church records describe different leisure worlds.
- **Detroit against West Michigan:** dance-club sources beside Dutch Reformed Sabbath sources and Holland's Tulip Time.
- **The Upper Peninsula, Iron Range and Northwoods:** Finnish, Cornish, Italian and Slovene mining towns need their own sauna, dance-hall and hunting-camp sources.
- **Milwaukee, Cincinnati, Cleveland:** German Turner halls, Polish fests and Cleveland-style polka are separate trails.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements (Elkhart-LaGrange, Holmes County, Kalona):** separate youth fun, church limits and display for tourists.
- **Black Midwesterners:** Bronzeville, Detroit's Paradise Valley and St. Louis's Ville one by one; the archives are city-specific.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans; Mexican Americans of the Twin Cities and Kansas City:** compare parade and *quinceañera* records.
- **Dearborn's Arab Americans and Detroit's Chaldeans:** check whether celebration sources differ by origin and church.
- **Hmong, Somali and Bosnian communities:** short paper trails; oral history is the route.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Meskwaki, Oneida:** compare powwow circuits, games and giveaways nation by nation, as each shares them.
- **Lutheran synods and Catholic parishes:** check what each permits at picnics, fish fries and bingo.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cities, sport, fairs and festivals; thinner for the newest communities and for Native ceremonial life, much of it not public.
- **Lands:** the Midwest Republic's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Great Lakes states and the upper Mississippi Valley, with Appalachian Ohio, the Plains, the CSA and New England as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Polish, German, Norwegian and Czech for the old ethnic press's festivity pages; Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali for current celebration media; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for dance and game words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some ceremonial songs and dances are not for outsiders; powwow photography rules vary; Amish photographs need consent; gambling data touch on addiction.
- **Held for review:** new-year festival pointers for Buddhist Southeast Asian-heritage Midwesterners were set aside as a category (an open question for the author), as was a festival-calendar pointer for South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
