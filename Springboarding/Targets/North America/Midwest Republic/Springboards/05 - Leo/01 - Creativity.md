# Midwest Republic: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Norwegian, Polish, Czech, German; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Michigan against Detroit:** furniture design (Grand Rapids, Zeeland) against auto styling and techno; each claims a different kind of invention.
- **Chicago's Black musicians and Detroit's:** compare who is credited as author in blues, gospel, house and techno histories.
- **Pilsen and Little Village against Humboldt Park:** Mexican and Puerto Rican murals and their sponsors may need separate sources.
- **Amish and Mennonite makers (northern Indiana, Holmes County, Kalona):** check where pattern and color are set by church and where by buyers.
- **Norwegian, Czech and Polish craft circles:** check how revival classes balance copying old pieces and inventing new ones.
- **Anishinaabeg, Ho-Chunk and Menominee makers:** check quillwork, black-ash basketry and beadwork nation by nation, and who may use which designs.
- **The Myaamia, Shawnee and Potawatomi histories:** check how removed peoples' arts were kept or rebuilt far from Ohio and Indiana.
- **Hmong, Somali and Bosnian Midwesterners:** check the gap between makers trained before arrival and those schooled here.
- **Dearborn's Arab Americans and Detroit's Chaldeans:** check which arts programs and galleries carry their work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for architecture, design, music and institutions; mixed for home crafts; thin for Somali oral composition in Minnesota.
- **Languages needed:** Norwegian and Czech for craft-revival newsletters; Hmong for needlework vocabulary; Somali for poets' own terms; Anishinaabemowin for words of making.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some birchbark scrolls are sacred and closed; Native designs carry rules of use; credit and royalty disputes in music involve living families.
- **Held for review:** intellectual property and repatriation law came up under "who owns what is made" and is left to Libra.
