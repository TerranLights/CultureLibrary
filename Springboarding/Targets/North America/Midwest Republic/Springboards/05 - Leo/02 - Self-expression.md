# Midwest Republic: Self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch; Norwegian; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** showing one's inner self (thoughts, feelings, tastes, beliefs, identity) outwardly, in speech, appearance, art and action.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Inland North and Midland speakers:** check whether Chicago, Detroit and Cleveland speakers and Columbus, Indianapolis and St. Louis speakers describe their own speech differently.
- **Old Order Amish, conservative Mennonites and assimilated Mennonites:** check where each draws the line between church dress and personal choice.
- **Black Chicagoans and Detroiters:** compare church, club and street as places to speak and dance, with care for class and generation.
- **LGBTQ Midwesterners in Chicago, the Twin Cities and small towns:** check whether archives exist outside the cities.
- **Muslim women of Dearborn, Hamtramck and Minneapolis:** check head covering and dress as accounts from the women themselves, varying by origin and generation.
- **Hmong elders and younger writers:** check sung poetry against print and online writing.
- **Somali women and men:** check which poetic forms belong to whom, as the community explains.
- **Anishinaabe, Dakota and Ho-Chunk youth:** check regalia and language revival after the boarding-school era, as each nation shares it.
- **Norwegian Lutheran and Irish and Polish Catholic letter writers:** check whether faith shaped what got written home.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check *el qué dirán* in family accounts on each side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, music and the ethnic press; mixed for everyday private expression, which needs interviews and diaries.
- **Languages needed:** Norwegian for nineteenth-century letters; Polish and Arabic for community newspapers; Hmong and Somali for oral poetry; Anishinaabemowin for oratory terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school records hold children's names and abuse; LGBTQ archives contain living people's private papers; Native regalia is not for casual photographing.
- **Held for review:** campus speech fights and school dress-code lawsuits came up and their legal side is left to Libra.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Blegen's letter collection and note each letter's expressions of feeling and complaint.
2. Search *DARE* for "show-off", "uppity" and "big-headed" with Midwestern locations.
3. Search the Gerber/Hart and Quatrefoil catalogs for newsletters from outside Chicago and the Twin Cities.
4. Search the National Archives catalog for boarding-school files from Tomah, Pipestone and Mount Pleasant.
5. Search *The Arab American News* archive for "hijab" and "dress code".
