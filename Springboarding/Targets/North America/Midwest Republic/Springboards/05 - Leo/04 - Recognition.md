## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check how service is acknowledged without individual praise, against the town-paper culture nearby.
- **Dakota, Ojibwe, Ho-Chunk, Meskwaki and Menominee:** check naming, honoring songs and veterans' recognition, as each nation shares them.
- **Black Chicagoans and Detroiters:** check the DuSable and other naming campaigns from community accounts, not only city records.
- **Italian, Polish and Mexican Chicagoans:** compare Columbus Day, Pulaski Day and Mexican hero commemorations as group recognition.
- **Hmong Minnesotans and Wisconsinites:** check veterans' and refugees' recognition across generations.
- **Somali, Bosnian and Arab professionals:** check credential and title recognition in the job market, from their own organizations.
- **Ohio's industrial cities and Iowa's farm towns:** compare halls of fame and service-club awards as local systems of credit.
- **Arts Chicago against arts Grand Rapids and Minneapolis:** juried prizes against public votes.
- **Lutheran and Catholic congregations:** check lay honors (long service, altar societies) and who is thanked from the pulpit.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for awards, halls of fame and naming disputes; mixed for workplace and family recognition; thin for Somali and Hmong internal honors, which are oral.
- **Languages needed:** Dakota and Anishinaabemowin for names and thanks; Hmong and Somali for praise forms; Polish, Czech, German and Finnish for lodge and parish honors.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** honoring songs and names belong to the nations; renaming disputes are live; veterans' records hold personal data.
- **Held for review:** federal acknowledgment of tribes and professional-licensing rules are law and go to Libra (letter) or Capricorn (spirit).
