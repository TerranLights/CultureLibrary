## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ojibwe, Odawa and Potawatomi communities:** check lacrosse and the moccasin game as each community revives and frames them.
- **Ho-Chunk and Menominee:** check trickster stories and games against outsiders' records, with each nation's say first.
- **Wisconsin's German-descended towns and Michigan's and Indiana's mixed towns:** compare sheepshead and euchre as markers of settlement.
- **Dutch Reformed West Michigan and Catholic Detroit:** compare Sunday play in each.
- **Amish settlements of Indiana, Ohio and Iowa:** check youth play by settlement and by affiliation.
- **Chicago's immigrant neighborhoods across a century:** check field-house programs for Polish, Czech, Black and Mexican children.
- **Hmong and Somali Minnesotans:** compare traditional games and adopted sports by generation.
- **Arab Americans of Dearborn:** check coffeehouse games and youth sport as separate worlds.
- **The Northwoods, the Iron Range and the Upper Peninsula:** winter play and camp culture apart from the metros.
- **Gamers in college towns (Madison, Ann Arbor, Bloomington):** check tabletop and video-game clubs against panic-era accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for organized games, industry and parks; mixed for children's free play; thin for Somali and Hmong play in print.
- **Languages needed:** German and Pennsylvania Dutch for game names; Hmong and Somali for community sport; Anishinaabemowin for game vocabulary; Spanish for parish-fair bulletins.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Native games carry ceremonial meaning and are not for outside play or filming; check with each community whether trickster stories are kept for winter telling.
- **Held for review:** pinball and gambling statutes and gaming-disorder rules go to Libra.
