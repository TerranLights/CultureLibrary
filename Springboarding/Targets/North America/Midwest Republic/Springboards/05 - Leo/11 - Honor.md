# Midwest Republic: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Polish, Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Somali; Hmong; Dakota; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Thâkîwaki (Sauk; *check* spelling), Meskwaki, Ho-Chunk and Dakota:** check honoring of warriors and veterans, nation by nation, as each shares it.
- **Upland South settlers of southern Ohio, Indiana and Illinois against Yankee and German settlers further north:** check whether county studies split along the Midland and Inland North line.
- **Out-migrant Appalachian communities in Cincinnati, Dayton, Chicago and Detroit:** check honor and respect accounts from community sources, not outsiders' labels.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** compare *honra* and respect in family and street accounts.
- **Arab Americans and Chaldeans of metro Detroit:** check honor vocabulary as people use it, varying by origin, faith and generation.
- **Somali Minnesotans:** check *sharaf* and clan standing from community sources.
- **Hmong families:** check face and family standing across clans and generations.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check the given word in trade against outside contracts.
- **Polish, Czech and German lodges:** compare oaths and the honor of membership.
- **Black Chicagoans and Detroiters:** check respect and reputation in church, union and street accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for veterans, gangs and commemoration; mixed for family honor, which needs interviews; thin and sensitive for honor in Somali and Hmong families.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic, Somali and Hmong for family honor words; Dakota and Anishinaabemowin for honoring terms; German, Polish and Czech for lodge oaths; Spanish for *charro* codes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family-honor topics invite stereotype; use members' own words; 1862 commemorations are grieving; eagle feathers and honor songs belong to the nations.
- **Held for review:** one scholarly pointer was set aside under the South Asian rule (category: South Asian-heritage scholars); clan customary law and dueling statutes go to Libra.
