# Midwest Republic: The domain, work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English, plus the community and Native languages in section 1. **Concept in one line:** the domain of work, service, daily routine and health: what is good work and a well-kept life?

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm households and factory towns:** check whether livestock and seasons or the shift whistle set the day, and how mechanization changed it.
- **Inland North, Midland and Upland South settlement areas:** check where settlers' origins shaped the Sabbath, the work bee and housekeeping, since Fischer and Woodard draw the lines differently.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Meskwaki and the Oneida of Wisconsin:** check each nation's own seasonal round on its lake, river or prairie lands.
- **Amish and Mennonite districts:** check how church-district rules set the working week.
- **Great Migration and Appalachian out-migrant neighborhoods:** check which southern rhythms of work and church were kept in Chicago, Detroit, Cincinnati and Dayton.
- **Newer arrivals (Hmong, Somali, Bosnian, Mexican, Puerto Rican, Arab, Chaldean):** check how shift work met religious calendars and family meals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for farm, labor and time-use sources; thinner for each Native nation's own account and for smaller immigrant groups, which need community archives. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign is written for the Great Lakes states and the upper Mississippi Valley as the core.
- **Languages needed:** Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for seasonal terms; German, Norwegian, Finnish and Polish for the immigrant press; Arabic, Hmong and Somali for present household routines.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Anishinaabe ceremonial calendars and Midewiwin knowledge are not public; use only what the nations share.
- **Held for review:** national syntheses of American work were weighed; none set aside, though most are written from the East Coast and need Midwestern testing.

## 5. First queries
1. In IPUMS-ATUS, pull household work, paid work and sleep for the Census Bureau's East North Central and West North Central divisions (the bureau's definition) against national figures.
2. Search the Ball State Middletown collections for "housework", "leisure" and "working class".
3. Search GLIFWC publications for "manoomin season" and "sugarbush".
4. Search DARE for "chore", "redd up", "frolic" and "bee".
5. Search Iowa and Minnesota state fair premium books for "canning", "bread" and "domestic arts", 1900 to 1960.
