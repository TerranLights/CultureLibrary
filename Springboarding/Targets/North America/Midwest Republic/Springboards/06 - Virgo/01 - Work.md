# Midwest Republic: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Pennsylvania Dutch, Polish, Czech, Finnish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali community usage; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Auto, steel and glass towns (Flint, Detroit, Toledo, Gary, Cleveland):** check how line jobs were ranked against skilled trades, before and after layoffs.
- **Mining districts (Iron Range, Copper Country, southern Illinois coalfields):** check how danger and company control shaped the meaning of a miner's work.
- **Family farms in Iowa, Wisconsin's Driftless Area and the Red River Valley:** check whether farm work is spoken of as a job, a way of life or a debt.
- **Mexican American and Tejano migrant families in Michigan and Ohio fields:** check how seasonal work, school and settlement were balanced.
- **Black workers of the Great Migration:** check how access to skilled work, unions and self-employment differed in Chicago, Detroit and Milwaukee.
- **Anishinaabeg, Ho-Chunk, Menominee and Meskwaki:** check how each nation names subsistence work against wage work, in its own terms.
- **Amish and Mennonite communities:** check what the church districts allow in factory and shop work.
- **Hmong, Somali and Bosnian workers:** check how the communities themselves describe factory, packing-plant and small-business work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for industrial and union history; mixed for farm work after 1950; thin for each Native nation's own terms for work, which need tribal language programs.
- **Languages needed:** Anishinaabemowin and Dakota for work verbs; Finnish and Polish for the socialist and parish press; Spanish for migrant-labor materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Rust Belt decline and the Detroit 1967 account are contested; treat each as an account. Labor law (right-to-work, collective bargaining) goes to Libra or Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** union-produced histories were weighed as possibly one-sided; kept, but to be read beside company and community sources.
