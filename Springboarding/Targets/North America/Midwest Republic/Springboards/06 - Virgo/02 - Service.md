*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Pullman porters, waiters and domestic workers of Chicago and Detroit:** check how service jobs were held as both livelihood and a site of protest.
- **Catholic women's orders and Lutheran deaconesses (Valparaiso):** check how vowed or consecrated service differed between the two traditions.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee and the Oneida of Wisconsin:** check how each nation honors its veterans and its helpers at gatherings, as the nations describe it.
- **Small-town volunteer bodies in Iowa, Indiana and Wisconsin:** check who staffs fire, ambulance and church-kitchen service as towns shrink.
- **Hmong veterans of the war in Laos and their families:** check how past military service is claimed in Minnesota and Wisconsin.
- **Amish and Mennonite disaster volunteers:** check how relief service fits churches that avoid state service.
- **Arab, Chaldean, Somali and Puerto Rican community agencies:** check how each frames service to its own members and to outsiders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for service clubs, settlement houses and Catholic orders; mixed for tipped restaurant work; thin for Native nations' service roles outside their own papers.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Norwegian for fraternal records; Arabic and Somali for agency outreach; Anishinaabemowin for honor terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** veterans' honor songs and ceremonies belong to the nations; describe only what they publish. Pullman and domestic-service histories involve racial hierarchy; read company records beside workers' own accounts.
- **Held for review:** Rotary's and Lions' self-published centennial histories were weighed as promotional; kept as sources of the clubs' own framing only.
