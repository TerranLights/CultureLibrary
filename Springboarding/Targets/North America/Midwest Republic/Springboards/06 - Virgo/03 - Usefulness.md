# Midwest Republic: Usefulness (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch, German, Norwegian, Polish, Spanish, Finnish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali community usage; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** being of practical value to others or to a purpose; for a person, contributing and being needed; for a thing or a piece of knowledge, practical value.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm and town households in Iowa, Indiana and Wisconsin:** check how children's tasks and the label "useful" changed as farms mechanized and families moved to town.
- **Amish and Mennonite districts:** check how thrift, reuse and the refusal of some goods are framed by the churches themselves.
- **Hmong, Somali, Mexican and Arab families:** check how elders' roles and children's help are described inside each community.
- **Anishinaabeg and Menominee:** check how each nation describes the right use of forest, rice and plants, in its own terms.
- **Laid-off industrial workers in Flint, Youngstown-area Ohio and Gary:** check how job loss is described as loss of a role; the Rust Belt story is a contested account.
- **Disabled workers and self-advocates:** check how sheltered workshops are defended and opposed in Ohio, Minnesota and Missouri.
- **Black and white rural grandparents raising grandchildren:** check where kinship care rose and how families speak of it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for catalogs, farm-youth groups and federal data; mixed for poor-farm records, which vary by county; thin for Native nations' own words for usefulness.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania Dutch and German for thrift writings; Hmong and Somali for elders' roles; Anishinaabemowin and Menominee for land-use terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** treaty "civilization" programs and the labeling of land as unused are contested; give the nations' accounts first. Disability and old-age records involve privacy; use published or anonymized data.
- **Held for review:** company-written histories (Deere, the catalog firms) were weighed as promotional; kept only as evidence of how firms argued usefulness.

## 5. First queries
1. Compare the farm-equipment and kitchen sections of a Sears catalog from 1900, 1930 and 1960.

3. Pull ACS county tables on grandparents responsible for grandchildren for the core states.
4. Search state archives' finding aids for "county poor farm" and "register of inmates".
5. Read the Menominee Tribal Enterprises and College of Menominee Nation publications on forest management.
