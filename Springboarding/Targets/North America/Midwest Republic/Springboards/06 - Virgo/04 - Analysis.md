## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **University towns (Ann Arbor, Madison, Champaign-Urbana, Iowa City, Hyde Park):** check how campus research cultures are seen by surrounding farm and factory towns.
- **Insurance and finance centers (Chicago, Des Moines, Columbus, Bloomington, Illinois):** check how actuarial and trading work is named and ranked locally.
- **Farm operators working with extension services:** check whether record keeping and soil testing are framed as science, habit or both.
- **Anishinaabeg working with fisheries and wild-rice science:** check how tribal biologists and elders set their methods side by side.
- **Black neighborhoods mapped by lenders and planners:** check how residents' own accounts answer the analysts' maps.
- **Amish and Plain communities:** check how reckoning and accounts are learned in their own schools.
- **Arab, Chaldean and Hmong families in small business:** check how bookkeeping is taught and shared within families.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; the region holds major survey, data and statistics institutions. Thin for Native and immigrant groups' own terms for reasoning and testing.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic, Hmong and Somali for current community material; Dutch, Swedish and Czech for older school and church records; Anishinaabemowin for knowledge terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tribal health data are governed by the nations; use published summaries only. Redlining maps and the Dearborn counting debates are contested ground.
- **Held for review:** the analytic-holistic thinking-style literature was weighed and left out here, since it sorts whole peoples by style; take it up only after the regional data exist.
