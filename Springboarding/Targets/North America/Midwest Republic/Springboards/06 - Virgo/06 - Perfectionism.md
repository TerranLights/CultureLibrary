# Midwest Republic: Perfectionism (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch, German, Swedish, Finnish, Polish, Dutch, Spanish, Arabic and Hmong community usage. **Concept in one line:** holding oneself, others or one's work to a standard of flawlessness, and the striving, checking and distress that come with it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Auto and parts workers in Detroit, Toledo and Kokomo:** check how quality drives were received on the shop floor, against how management told them.
- **Amish and Shaker-descended craft traditions:** check where exact work is praised and where it is warned against as pride.
- **Wisconsin cheesemakers and brewers of German and Swiss descent:** check how certification and family method compete as standards.
- **Hmong needleworkers and Anishinaabe beadworkers:** check how each group's makers describe flaws and deliberate breaks, in their own words.
- **Students in selective high schools and campuses (Ann Arbor, Evanston, Hyde Park):** check how academic pressure is talked about by students and counselors.
- **Arab American and Chaldean families in Dearborn and Sterling Heights:** check how standards of work tie to religious teaching in sermons and schools.
- **Black musicians and producers in Detroit and Chicago:** check how studio standards were set and remembered.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for corporate quality campaigns and craft collections; mixed for perfectionism measures by region; thin for Native makers' own terms, which need the nations' language programs.
- **Languages needed:** Hmong for needlework terms; Arabic for teaching on *itqān*; Pennsylvania Dutch for pride and humility; Anishinaabemowin for craft words not listed here.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** eating disorders, anxiety and suicide among high achievers need care and published data only. Some Anishinaabe objects are ceremonial; describe only what the makers share.
- **Held for review:** the "humility block" in quilts was weighed as possible folklore; kept only as a claim to test with makers.
