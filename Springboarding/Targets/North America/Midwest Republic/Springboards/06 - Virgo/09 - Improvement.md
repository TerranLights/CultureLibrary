# Midwest Republic: Improvement (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Dutch, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali community usage; Anishinaabemowin and Myaamia. **Concept in one line:** making something better than it was (oneself, one's work, a home, land, a community, a people), and the belief that betterment is possible, desirable and owed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Native nations (Anishinaabeg, Meskwaki, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Myaamia, Potawatomi):** check how each answers imposed "improvement" and frames its own renewal of language and land.
- **Immigrant workers under Ford-style programs (Polish, Hungarian, Italian, Arab):** check how inspection and English classes are remembered in family and community accounts.
- **Farm families in drained and plowed country (Ohio, Indiana, Iowa):** check how land "improvement" and later restoration are spoken of.
- **Recovery communities in Akron, Minneapolis and Chicago:** check how twelve-step language spreads beyond addiction.
- **Black Chicago and Detroit:** check how "uplift" and urban renewal are told by those moved or helped.
- **Hmong, Somali and Mexican adult learners:** check what each community counts as getting ahead.
- **Amish and Mennonite districts:** check where they welcome and refuse "better" methods.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for self-help, reform and land records; mixed for immigrant adult education; boarding-school records are growing but painful.
- **Languages needed:** Myaamia, Anishinaabemowin and Meskwaki for renewal terms; Polish and German for Americanization-era sources; Spanish, Hmong and Somali for current adult education.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding schools, the removals under the 1830 act titled *"An Act to provide for an exchange of lands with the Indians residing in any of the states or territories, and for their removal west of the river Mississippi"* and urban renewal are contested; survivors' and the nations' accounts first. Graves and records at boarding-school sites are restricted.
- **Held for review:** the Fox Project's own reports were weighed as outsiders' self-assessment; kept only beside Meskwaki accounts.
