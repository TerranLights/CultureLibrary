## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Inland North against the upland-settled south of Ohio, Indiana and Illinois:** check whether marriage and court records differ between the Yankee and Upland Southern settlement streams that county histories record separately.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements (Elkhart-LaGrange, Holmes County, Kalona):** check how church discipline and the *Ordnung* sit beside state courts in marriage and disputes.
- **Anishinaabeg (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi), Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida, Meskwaki:** check each nation's own codes, courts and treaty histories separately; for the Myaamia and Shawnee, the histories of removal.
- **Black Midwesterners of the Great Migration (Bronzeville, Detroit):** check marriage and court records in the Black press and community archives, not only in city records.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check *compadrazgo* and hometown clubs as partnership forms.
- **Dearborn's Arab Americans, Detroit's Chaldeans; Hmong, Somali, St. Louis Bosnians:** check religious marriage contracts and community mediation for each.
- **Out-migrant Appalachians in Cincinnati, Dayton, Chicago's Uptown and Ypsilanti:** check them as Midwesterners where they live; Appalachian Ohio itself, the Plains and New England are comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for court, census, church records and the ethnic press; thinner for smaller refugee communities and for Native nations' internal processes, which belong to them.
- **Languages needed:** German, Polish, Czech and the Scandinavian languages for the 1880-1940 press layer; Arabic, Somali and Hmong for living communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the U.S.-Dakota War and the Mankato executions, the removals of the 1830s and Detroit 1967 are contested; present each as an account. Midewiwin knowledge is restricted. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled.
- **Held for review:** this sheet weighed general moral-psychology and economic-game studies and left them for the concept sheets, where they meet a specific question.
