## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Farm couples in Iowa, southern Minnesota and Wisconsin's dairy counties:** check how farm women's work is recorded in partnership agreements, deeds and oral histories.
- **Amish and Old Order Mennonite couples around Elkhart-LaGrange and Kalona:** check marriage within the church district and the place of a couple in its work.
- **Finns of the Iron Range and the Upper Peninsula:** check co-op membership rolls as a partnership record distinct from church records.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican families in Chicago and its satellite cities:** check godparent choices across communion, *quinceañera* and wedding.
- **Dearborn's Lebanese, Yemeni and Iraqi families; Detroit's Chaldean families:** check contract-signing customs and family roles in matchmaking for each.
- **Hmong families in St. Paul, Wausau and Sheboygan; Somali families in Minneapolis:** check how go-betweens and payments appear in community and court records.
- **Anishinaabe, Ho-Chunk and Dakota communities:** check clan and kin rules on marriage as each nation shares them.
- **Black families of Bronzeville and Detroit:** check marriage and boarding records through the migration years.
- **German Catholic Stearns County, Minnesota, and Dutch Reformed Holland, Michigan:** check church-court records on marriage for comparison of the two.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on marriage law, co-ops and fraternal societies; thin on business partnerships inside refugee communities, best reached by community business associations.
- **Languages needed:** Hmong and Somali for marriage negotiation; Finnish and Norwegian for co-op and lodge minutes; Arabic for contract terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan and ceremonial marriage knowledge belongs to each nation; Hmong marriage customs have been publicly disputed, so present accounts, never a verdict on a people.
- **Held for review:** couples' research labs outside these states were weighed and not used; Midwestern panels carry the couple data here.
