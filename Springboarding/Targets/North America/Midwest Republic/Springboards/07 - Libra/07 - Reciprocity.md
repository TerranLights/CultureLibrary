# Midwest Republic: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Polish; Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish; Bosnian; Spanish; Arabic; Somali; Hmong; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements of Elkhart-LaGrange, Holmes County and Kalona:** check how church aid is assessed and how a family that cannot repay is treated.
- **Norwegian- and Swedish-descended Upper Midwesterners:** check whether *julebukking* and return-thanks survive or have become heritage events.
- **Finns of the Iron Range and the Upper Peninsula:** check *talkoot* records in co-op and hall minutes.
- **Black families of Bronzeville and Detroit:** check burial-society and church-aid records across the migration generations.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check *tandas* and store credit separately, since their histories differ.
- **Dearborn's Lebanese and Yemeni families; Detroit's Chaldeans:** check obligations of hospitality and favor for each.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis, Somali Minneapolitans, Hmong of St. Paul:** check how each pools money for funerals, weddings and newcomers.
- **Potawatomi, Ojibwe, Ho-Chunk and Dakota communities:** check giveaways and offerings only as each nation chooses to share them.
- **Rural Iowa against Chicago:** check whether neighbor help measured in surveys differs by county type.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in folklife and ethnography; national surveys measure favors only coarsely; informal finance among Somali and Hmong families is little written down.
- **Languages needed:** Somali, Hmong and Bosnian for community finance and help; Finnish and Norwegian for hall and church minutes; Anishinaabemowin for harvest offerings.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** offerings and giveaways can be ceremonial; Hmong funeral practice can meet city zoning and health rules; present any dispute found as an account.
- **Held for review:** gift-exchange game experiments were weighed and set aside, since none known to the agent sampled these communities.
