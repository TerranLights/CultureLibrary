# Midwest Republic: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Polish; Swedish, Norwegian; Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Somali; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mennonite, Brethren and Amish communities:** check how nonresistance is taught and how each handles wrongs inside the church, keeping principled peace and silence apart.
- **Shawnee, Myaamia and Potawatomi histories of the removal era:** check accommodation and resistance as each nation's historians tell them.
- **Dakota communities after 1862:** check how survivors and descendants describe the years of keeping quiet and the later commemorations.
- **Catholic dioceses of Milwaukee, St. Paul-Minneapolis and Chicago:** check investigation records diocese by diocese.
- **Black Midwesterners in sundown-town regions and in Bronzeville:** check debates over "respectability" in the Black press as accounts, not as a portrait of a group.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans:** check whether immigration status affects reporting, comparing citizens and noncitizens.
- **Dearborn's Arab Americans; Somali and Hmong families:** check how community counselors weigh family honor against reporting.
- **German- and Polish-descended families in Wisconsin and Michigan:** check family letters and parish records for idioms of keeping the peace.
- **Copperhead strongholds in southern Ohio, Indiana and Illinois:** check county voting and press against the northern tier.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, court and Civil War records; thin and private for silence inside families; the best sources are advocates' reports.
- **Languages needed:** Somali, Hmong and Arabic for family-violence work; German and Polish for family letters; Pennsylvania Dutch for church teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases involve living victims; sundown towns, the removals of the 1830s and 1862 are contested; respect closed church and tribal records.
- **Held for review:** the national studies on the "spiral of silence" were weighed and left out; they do not break results down by these states.
