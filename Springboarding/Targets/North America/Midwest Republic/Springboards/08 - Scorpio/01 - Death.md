# Midwest Republic: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; Swedish, Norwegian; Polish; Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Bosnian; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Menominee. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Detroit and Bronzeville congregations:** compare televised homegoings of famous Detroiters with ordinary ones.
- **Lutheran farm towns in Minnesota and Wisconsin:** read funeral sermons and memorial books to see who is praised at a life's close.
- **Polish Chicago, Milwaukee and Hamtramck:** check whether *wypominki* and candlelit graves at All Souls persist past the third generation.
- **Mexican, Puerto Rican and Guatemalan families:** compare the deaths of infants and young people in records and rites, since the *angelito* tradition may be kept or dropped.
- **Dearborn's Shia and Sunni families and Detroit's Chaldeans:** check how quickly burial follows death and where, since cemetery space is a practical lead.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis:** check how the dead of the 1990s war are remembered far from their graves.
- **Hmong families:** compare funerals with *qeej* and those without, since conversion splits clans.
- **Somali Minnesotans:** check deaths of young men by violence and how mosques and families respond.
- **Anishinaabeg of Michigan and Minnesota, Dakota, Menominee:** each nation's own published accounts of feeding and remembering the dead.
- **Iron Range and Copper Country mining families:** check memorials to mine deaths as local dates of mourning.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mortality counts, obituaries and European-descended rites; mixed for Hmong, Somali and Bosnian practice; thin in print and partly restricted for Native mourning.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and Polish for old death notices; Arabic and Bosnian for mosque announcements; Hmong for funeral songs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and overdose deaths; Mankato 1862 and accounts that disagree; Native mourning songs and ceremonies; children's deaths.
- **Held for review:** a Midwestern Santa Muerte shrine was considered, but none is known well enough here to name; left for the research stage.
