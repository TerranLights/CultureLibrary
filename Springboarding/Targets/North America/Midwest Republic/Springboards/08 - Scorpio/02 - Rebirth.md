# Midwest Republic: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Swedish, Norwegian; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Myaamia, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery, redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival and beliefs in the return of the dead.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican Pentecostals against Catholic parishes:** check how converts describe leaving the family's church, since testimony is recorded and Mass practice is not.
- **Black Christians and Black Muslims of Detroit and Chicago:** compare name changes and testimonies as markers of a new life.
- **Amish youth in Elkhart-LaGrange and smaller settlements:** check baptism ages and leaving rates in published studies.
- **Swedish Covenant and Norwegian Haugean Lutherans against high-church synods:** check whether conversion language appears in congregational histories.
- **Hmong households:** compare Christian converts with households keeping shamanic healing.
- **Second-generation Dearborn families:** check how young people describe returning to religious practice.
- **Myaamia, Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Menominee and Ho-Chunk:** each nation's revival in its own words and on its own schedule.
- **Detroit's long-time residents and newcomers:** compare who tells the "comeback" story and who disputes it.
- **People leaving prison in Chicago and Milwaukee:** check which churches and employers take part in reentry.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for revivals, recovery and urban renewal; good for Native revival where nations publish it; thin for Hmong and Somali conversion.
- **Languages needed:** Swedish and Norwegian for revival periodicals; Spanish for Pentecostal testimony; Myaamia and Ojibwe materials through the nations' own programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding schools as forced remaking (Mount Pleasant, Michigan; Harbor Springs; Pipestone; Tomah); relapse; the "comeback" account of Detroit is contested.
- **Held for review:** the region's psychedelic and wellness retreats were weighed as pointers but none is established enough here to name.
