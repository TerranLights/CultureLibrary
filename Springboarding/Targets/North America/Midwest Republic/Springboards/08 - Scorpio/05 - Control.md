## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old Order Amish, New Order and Beachy groups in Indiana and Ohio:** check how each limits technology, since the line moves by affiliation.
- **Amana's descendants and the Bishop Hill heirs:** compare how communal control ended and what was kept.
- **Auto, steel and packinghouse families in Detroit, Gary and Chicago:** check what employers once checked at home and how workers recall it.
- **Dutch Reformed West Michigan and German Lutherans:** compare elders' home visits and pastoral oversight.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican households:** check who holds the household money, through surveys that ask, not assumptions.
- **Hmong families:** compare clan mediation with courts and social services in family disputes.
- **Dearborn's Arab Americans:** check how planning and *qadar* are paired in sermons and advice.
- **Anishinaabeg and Menominee:** compare control of rice beds and forest under each nation's own rules.
- **Dakota descendants:** check how Fort Snelling's new exhibits were made and with whom.
- **Small-town Lutherans of Minnesota and Wisconsin:** check memoirs for neighbors' oversight, by decade.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for company towns, water engineering and domestic-violence work; mixed for household control; protected for Amish and clan matters.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania Dutch and German for Amish and Amana sources; Hmong for clan mediation; Ojibwe for rice governance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coercive control and partner violence; the Fort Snelling camp; Amish privacy. Legal discipline and authority go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** Michigan's militia and survivalist groups were weighed as the far end of control; none is named until reporting on them can be weighed.
