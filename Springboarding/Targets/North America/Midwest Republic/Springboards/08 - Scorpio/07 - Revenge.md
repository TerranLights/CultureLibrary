# Midwest Republic: Revenge (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English (including Chicago street speech); German; Polish; Finnish; Czech; Spanish; Arabic; Bosnian; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Sauk. **Concept in one line:** harming someone in return for a harm they did, to get even, restore standing or deter the next wrong; forgiveness and payment for wrongs as its alternatives.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black neighborhoods of Chicago's South and West Sides:** compare blocks with violence-interruption workers and those without, from the programs' own evaluations.
- **Little Village and Humboldt Park:** check how Mexican and Puerto Rican community groups describe stopping retaliation.
- **Ojibwe bands of northern Wisconsin and protest groups of the 1980s:** collect both sides' accounts of the boat landings.
- **Ojibwe and Dakota:** set Warren's account of their wars against Dakota-side histories.
- **Hmong hunters and rural landowners in Wisconsin:** check how the 2004 and 2007 hunting-land killings were told in Hmong media and in local papers.
- **Amish of Indiana and the 2011 Bergholz beard-cutting case** (eastern Ohio, at the Appalachian edge, comparison only): check how Amish writers answered retaliation inside the faith.
- **German anarchists, Czech and Polish workers of 1880s Chicago:** check each language's press on Haymarket.
- **Detroit 1967 witnesses:** compare Black residents', officers' and Guardsmen's accounts.
- **Bosnians of St. Louis:** check how justice for war crimes is spoken of when revenge is out of reach.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, labor violence and gangland history; mixed for everyday petty revenge; sensitive and partial for current street violence.
- **Languages needed:** German for 1886; Ojibwe and Dakota for war traditions; Hmong for the hunting cases.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** current gang conflict and named young people; the 1919 and 1967 riots; the spearfishing years; the hunting killings. Private vengeance against the state's punishment goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** news coverage of the Wisconsin hunting killings was kept out of the pointer table; press accounts should first be balanced with Hmong and local sources.
