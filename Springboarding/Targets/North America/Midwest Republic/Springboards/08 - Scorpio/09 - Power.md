# Midwest Republic: Power (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English (Chicago political speech); Pennsylvania Dutch; Czech; Finnish; Polish; Spanish (Mexican and Puerto Rican); Arabic; Somali; Hmong; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota. **Concept in one line:** the capacity to make things happen and to affect others, with or against their will, and how its holders, holding and use are judged.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muncie and other mid-sized Indiana and Ohio towns:** compare the Lynds' "X family" with later restudies, since a single study may freeze one moment.
- **Chicago wards by ethnicity, 1920s-1980s** (Irish, Czech, Polish, Black, Mexican): check patronage payrolls ward by ward for who received city jobs, rather than relying on reputation.
- **Gary, Cleveland and Detroit:** compare what Black mayors could and could not change, decade by decade.
- **Finnish cooperatives of the Iron Range and German Lutheran farm towns:** compare cooperative and church-led local power.
- **Amish districts:** check how bishops chosen by lot exercise authority, from members' own writings.
- **Puerto Rican and Mexican Chicago:** compare the Young Lords' path with Mexican American electoral campaigns in Pilsen.
- **Dearborn, Hamtramck and Detroit's Chaldeans:** check how each community enters city politics and how residents of other backgrounds respond.
- **Somali Minneapolis and Hmong St. Paul:** compare routes from community groups to elected office.
- **Anishinaabeg, Dakota, Ho-Chunk and Menominee:** each nation's own account of its government, elected and traditional.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for machines, strikes and urban politics; mixed for small towns; thin and nation-controlled for Native governance.
- **Languages needed:** Czech and Polish for ward-era press; Arabic for Hamtramck and Dearborn; Ojibwe and Dakota for traditional leadership terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** living politicians and current disputes in Dearborn and Hamtramck; clan and religious authority in immigrant communities. Legitimate authority and power ways go to Capricorn; election law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** tribal gaming compacts in Wisconsin, Michigan and Minnesota were weighed as a shift of economic power; left for the research stage, to be read through each nation's own reports.
