# Midwest Republic: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Pennsylvania Dutch; Finnish, Polish, Dutch; Spanish (Mexican and Puerto Rican); Arabic, Sureth (Chaldean Neo-Aramaic); Hmong; Dakota, Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven: God, gods, spirits, a sacred order; by extension, secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Iowa and Minnesota Lutheran parishes versus Chicago and Twin Cities suburban megachurches:** measure how the language of personal faith differs.
- **Polish Catholics of Hamtramck and Chicago's northwest side versus Mexican Catholics of Pilsen and southwest Detroit:** compare devotions (Divine Mercy; the December 12 processions) and vows.
- **Puerto Rican Pentecostal and Catholic congregations of Humboldt Park:** check how switching between them is described within families.
- **Black Baptist, Methodist and Holiness congregations of Detroit, Gary and St. Louis:** compare testimony and song as carriers of faith through hardship.
- **Amish affiliations in Holmes County and Elkhart-LaGrange:** check how baptism into the church is framed as a choice.
- **Dearborn's Shia Lebanese and Iraqi and Sunni Yemeni communities; Detroit's Chaldeans:** each on its own terms, by rite and generation.
- **Hmong families of St. Paul and Wausau, traditional and Christian:** how both faiths are kept or negotiated at funerals.
- **Dakota and Oneida Christian congregations and traditional practitioners:** each nation's account of faith after removal and mission.
- **Upper Peninsula Finns, Laestadian and Suomi Synod lines:** compare who counts as a believer.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic and Protestant devotion and for survey measures; mixed for Muslim and Chaldean life (community sources, some in Arabic and Sureth); thin and community-held for Native faith.
- **Languages needed:** Sureth and Arabic for Detroit-area liturgy and oral history; Hmong for the conversion question; Finnish for Laestadian sermons; Dakota for the hymnals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clergy-abuse cases in Midwestern dioceses bear on lost faith but involve survivors; conversion is painful in some Hmong and Native families; mission texts are outsider sources.
- **Held for review:** devotional pointers were weighed across every faith present; nothing was set aside, since each pointer chosen is held and published by the community it concerns.
