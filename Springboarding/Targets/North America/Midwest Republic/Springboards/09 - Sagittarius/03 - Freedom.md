# Midwest Republic: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish, Czech; Spanish (Puerto Rican and Mexican); Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Bosnian. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state; and freedom as a belief and a political ideal. Law on liberties is for Libra (the letter) and Capricorn (the spirit).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Yankee northern counties of Illinois, Indiana and Ohio versus their upland-Southern southern counties:** check how "free soil" and anti-war "Butternut" rhetoric divided in the 1850s-1860s press.
- **Forty-Eighters of Milwaukee, Davenport and St. Louis versus rural German Catholics and Lutherans:** whether *Freiheit* carried the same charge in both.
- **Black communities of the Ohio River towns and of Great Migration Chicago and Detroit:** freedom told as escape, arrival and disappointment; compare accounts by period.
- **Sauk, Meskwaki, Dakota, Ho-Chunk and Potawatomi histories of removal and return:** each nation's own framing of sovereignty and land.
- **Amish, Mennonites and Hutterite memory of the First World War:** check what freedom of conscience meant to communities pressed over military service.
- **Polish and Czech fraternal societies:** compare homeland independence and freethought as their freedom causes.
- **Puerto Rican and Mexican Chicago:** separate nationhood debates from immigration accounts.
- **Arab Dearborn, Hmong St. Paul, Somali Minneapolis, Bosnian St. Louis:** freedom as asylum, in each community's own telling.
- **Northwoods and Driftless rural counties versus metro cores:** measure how the open land, guns and hunting enter freedom talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for abolition, labor and immigrant press; mixed for recent refugee groups; Native accounts exist but must be found through each nation's own publications.
- **Languages needed:** German for Forty-Eighter newspapers; Polish and Czech for fraternal press; Spanish for Humboldt Park sources; Arabic and Somali for recent community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1862 war and executions at Mankato, the 1830s removals (under the 1830 act whose published title begins *"An Act to provide for an exchange of lands with the Indians"*), Haymarket and the Dearborn disputes are contested; quote each account as an account. Puerto Rican independence politics involve living people and prosecutions.
- **Held for review:** court cases on conscience and schooling came up and were moved out, as they belong to Libra and Capricorn; no other pointer was set aside.
