# Midwest Republic: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish, Czech, Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Oneida. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it (scripture, elders, scientists, the press, one's own experience), and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Anishinaabe, Oneida and Dakota oral historians set against treaty journals:** check how each nation describes the standing of remembered agreements.
- **Survivor families of the Midwestern boarding schools:** compare their accounts by school and by church or federal operator.
- **Detroit residents of different neighborhoods and generations:** how each names and dates 1967.
- **Missouri Synod, Wisconsin Synod and ELCA Lutherans; Christian Reformed and Reformed Church in America:** compare their stated sources of doctrinal truth.
- **Rural news-desert counties versus metro media markets:** where people say they get facts.
- **Spanish-, Arabic-, Hmong- and Somali-language audiences:** separate by the media each uses.
- **University towns (Ann Arbor, Madison, Champaign-Urbana, Iowa City) versus their surrounding counties:** test trust in science by place, without assuming the result.
- **Readers of the Black press across eras:** check how the *Defender* and the *Michigan Chronicle* positioned their accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for press, church and government records; mixed for language-community media (much not archived); Native accounts rich in places but controlled by the nations.
- **Languages needed:** Anishinaabemowin, Dakota and Oneida for treaty and oral accounts; German for synod controversy; Arabic, Hmong and Somali for current media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school records involve deaths and survivors; Detroit 1967 and the Coughlin broadcasts are contested; some treaty-era knowledge is kept by the nations for their own use.
- **Held for review:** the national misinformation literature was weighed and kept out unless it has Midwestern cases; the Dearborn Independent's 1920s campaigns were noted as a possible further pointer for the author to decide on.
