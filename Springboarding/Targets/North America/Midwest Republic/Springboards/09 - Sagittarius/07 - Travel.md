# Midwest Republic: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; French (colonial records); Swedish, Norwegian; Pennsylvania Dutch; Spanish (Mexican); Bosnian; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Ho-Chunk, Potawatomi. **Concept in one line:** going far from home: long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration, and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler (Gemini keeps short journeys).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ojibwe bands of Madeline Island, Leech Lake and Mille Lacs:** check how each tells the migration story and which parts are kept for ceremony.
- **Potawatomi bands that stayed (Pokagon, Forest County) and those removed (Citizen Potawatomi):** compare accounts of the same journey from each side.
- **Swedish Chisago County, Norwegian Decorah and Finnish Upper Peninsula settlers:** compare how their letters describe the crossing and the return trip.
- **Great Migration families of Bronzeville and Detroit's North End:** trace trips "down home" and back over generations.
- **Amish of Holmes County and Elkhart-LaGrange:** check rules on vehicles and hired drivers per affiliation.
- **Mexican Chicago by home state:** separate Michoacán, Jalisco and Guanajuato networks.
- **Hmong, Somali and Bosnian refugees:** compare first-generation flight with children's visits to the old country.
- **Twin Cities and Chicago snowbird retirees versus small-town elders who stay:** measure who winters away.
- **Latter Day Saint descendants who stayed in Missouri and Illinois:** how the Community of Christ tells the departures it did not join.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for European emigrant letters and Latter Day Saint archives; mixed for Mexican and refugee networks (much in community hands); the Ojibwe migration story is shared in parts.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial and fur-trade contracts; Swedish and Norwegian for letters; Spanish, Hmong, Somali and Bosnian for current networks.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** removals and the Trail of Death are contested and painful; parts of the miigis migration story are ceremonial; refugee flight narratives involve trauma.
- **Held for review:** Dearborn's Hajj travel agencies and Catholic pilgrimages to Holy Hill were weighed as pilgrimage pointers and left for the research stage, since no published study of either came to mind with confidence.
