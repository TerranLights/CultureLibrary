## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **German, Norwegian, Swedish, Dutch and Finnish descendants:** check whether the shared "honesty lasts longest" proverb survives in each community's speech, or only in old readers.
- **Amish and Mennonite makers of Holmes County and Shipshewana versus their non-Plain neighbors:** how both sides describe trust in trade.
- **Chicago, Detroit and Milwaukee political wards versus suburban and rural towns:** compare what each press calls corruption, without importing a reputation.
- **Mexican and Puerto Rican newcomers in Chicago and Milwaukee:** check reported experiences of fraud and bribery in the home country and here.
- **Dearborn's Arab merchants and Chaldean grocers of Detroit:** how each community's own business associations talk about trust.
- **Hmong and Somali families:** check how clan and elder mediation handles a broken promise, in their own accounts.
- **Ojibwe, Odawa and Potawatomi communities:** each nation's own teaching texts on honesty.
- **Black Chicago and Detroit, past and present:** how the Black press has covered dishonesty by landlords, contract sellers and officials.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for commerce, journalism and school readers; mixed for immigrant experiences of fraud (legal-aid and press sources); thin for Hmong and Somali discussion in English.
- **Languages needed:** Arabic for sermons; Spanish for notario-fraud materials; Hmong and Somali for mediation accounts; Anishinaabemowin for teaching terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** corruption reputations attach to cities and groups; quote watchdogs and prosecutions as accounts, not verdicts on a people. Fraud victims are often undocumented.
