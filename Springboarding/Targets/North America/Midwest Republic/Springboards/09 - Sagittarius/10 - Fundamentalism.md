# Midwest Republic: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Pennsylvania Dutch; Dutch; Norwegian, Finnish; Spanish (Puerto Rican and Mexican); Arabic (for the press's use of the label). **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore it and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Minneapolis, Chicago and Wheaton evangelical institutions:** check where separatist and "new evangelical" lines fell, institution by institution.
- **Christian Reformed, Protestant Reformed and Reformed Church in America congregations of West Michigan and northwest Iowa:** compare how each states its confessional boundaries.
- **Missouri Synod, Wisconsin Synod, ELCA and Laestadian Lutherans:** separate their fellowship rules rather than ranking them.
- **Old Order, New Order and Swartzentruber Amish; conservative Mennonites:** check how each describes separation, and whether members reject the fundamentalist label.
- **Puerto Rican and Mexican Pentecostal congregations in Chicago:** how holiness codes are described by members and by those who left.
- **Small strict colonies past and present (Zion, House of David, Bishop Hill's Janssonists):** compare their founding claims and their endings.
- **Catholic traditionalist chapels and Latin Mass communities in the region's dioceses:** check whether members or critics use the word.
- **Ojibwe and Dakota Christian congregations:** check their own writers on mission-era separation teaching; do not apply the label to traditional ceremony.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the 1910s-1930s Protestant hubs (archives, histories); mixed for current strict congregations; thin and sensitive for leavers' accounts.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch for Reformed controversy; Finnish for Laestadian sources; German and Pennsylvania Dutch for Lutheran and Plain writing; Spanish for Pentecostal congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "fundamentalist" is an insult in most usage; quote who applies it. Outside commentators have applied the label to Muslim Dearborn in contested public debates; treat those as accounts about the commentators' claims, never as a finding. Leavers and abuse cases involve living people.
- **Held for review:** comparative pointers on strict currents in non-Christian faiths were weighed; only those tied to Midwestern communities' own publications would be kept, and none came to mind with confidence, so the table stays with the Christian and colony cases.
