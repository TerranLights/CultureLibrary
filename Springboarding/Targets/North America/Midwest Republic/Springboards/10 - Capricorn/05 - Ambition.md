# Midwest Republic: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish; Czech; West Michigan Dutch; Chicago Spanish; Arabic (Dearborn) and Chaldean Neo-Aramaic (Detroit suburbs); Somali; Hmong. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black Midwesterners of the Great Migration and their descendants:** compare migration-era letters with later memoirs to see how aims for children shifted.
- **Amish and Mennonite owners in Elkhart-LaGrange and Holmes County:** check where church limits on business size are drawn in each affiliation.
- **Chaldeans and Arab Americans of Detroit and Dearborn:** separate Chaldean, Lebanese and Yemeni accounts of family business; their histories differ.
- **Norwegian, Swedish and Finnish Upper Midwesterners:** check whether the leveling and contentment words live in speech or only in heritage writing.
- **Appalachian out-migrants in Dayton, Middletown, Cincinnati and Chicago's Uptown:** compare their own accounts with the outside portraits.
- **Hmong and Somali Minnesotans:** look at how political candidacy and college-going are discussed across generations.
- **Myaamia, Shawnee and Potawatomi nations removed from the region:** read each nation's own revival plans for ambitions tied to homelands.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, newspapers and mobility studies; thin for Somali and Chaldean aspirations in scholarship, so start with community media.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and Czech for immigrant letters; Spanish and Arabic for community press; Chaldean Neo-Aramaic for older Detroit family talk; Somali for youth media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *Hillbilly Elegy* and the Rust Belt account are politically charged; present each as an account. Business success of one group can feed stereotypes; record each community's own framing.
- **Held for review:** two data pointers from the concept block were set aside under the South Asian rule and stand here only as the category of South Asian-heritage scholars; nothing else was set aside.
