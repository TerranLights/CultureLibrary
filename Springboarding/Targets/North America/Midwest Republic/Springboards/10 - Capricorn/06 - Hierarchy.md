# Midwest Republic: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Old-stock Yankee and German families against later Slavic and Italian arrivals in mill towns:** check lodge and club rosters for who was admitted where.
- **Black Midwesterners in Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee and St. Louis:** compare redlining, covenant and housing-authority records city by city.
- **Grosse Pointe, Lake Forest, Edina and Ladue against their nearby cities:** check suburban screening and zoning records for how rank was written into place.
- **Irish, Polish, German and Czech Catholic parishes:** look at how each related to the bishop and to one another.
- **Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi and Ho-Chunk clan systems:** read each nation's own account; clan roles differ by nation.
- **Hmong clans and Somali *qabiil* in Minnesota and Wisconsin:** check community-led sources only; whether clan rank matters in city life is a question, not a given.
- **Lebanese, Yemeni and Iraqi Arab Detroiters and Chaldeans:** compare how each places village and family origin.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for housing, labor and community studies; mixed for clan systems, where outsiders' accounts outnumber community ones.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial Detroit and Illinois; German and Swedish for immigrant ranks; Hmong, Somali and Arabic for kin and lineage terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** redlining and segregation are contested ground; Somali clan talk can be divisive; Grosse Pointe's scheme names families, so cite the record, not individuals.
- **Held for review:** none. *Castas* and Northwest Coast feast ranks belong to other lands; French Detroit's ranks stand in for the colonial layer here.
