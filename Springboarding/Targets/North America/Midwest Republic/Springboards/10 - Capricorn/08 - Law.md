# Midwest Republic: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; French (colonial records); Pennsylvania Dutch and Mennonite German; Norwegian; Polish; Chicago Spanish; Dearborn Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota; Ho-Chunk. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey, use, bend or avoid it (Capricorn takes the spirit; the letter is Libra's).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ojibwe bands of Wisconsin, Minnesota and Michigan:** compare treaty-harvest codes and court rulings band by band; the 1837 and 1842 treaties differ.
- **Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida of Wisconsin and Meskwaki courts:** check each judiciary's place for custom and elders.
- **Dakota communities in Minnesota:** read their own accounts of 1862 beside court and army records, as accounts.
- **Amish and Mennonite districts:** check which ones accept courts, insurance and state codes, and which refuse.
- **Chicago neighborhoods (Bronzeville, Pilsen, Little Village, Humboldt Park):** compare records of trust in police.
- **Arab Americans and Chaldeans of Dearborn and Detroit; Somali Minnesotans; Hmong clans:** check how religious or customary mediation meets civil courts.
- **St. Louis County municipalities and Kansas City:** compare court fines and fees records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Chicago and Detroit policing, tribal-court rulings and treaty cases; thin and private for clan and elder mediation.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial civil records; Mennonite German for nonresistance teaching; Arabic, Somali and Hmong for customary mediation; Ojibwe and Ho-Chunk terms as each nation publishes them.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1862 trials, the spearfishing conflict, Ferguson and Detroit's STRESS unit are contested; debates over religious law in Dearborn are politically charged, so record each side as an account.
- **Held for review:** one ethnographic pointer was set aside under the South Asian-heritage scholars rule; held only as that category.
