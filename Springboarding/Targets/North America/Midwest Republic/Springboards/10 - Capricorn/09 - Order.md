# Midwest Republic: Order (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German; Swedish; Polish; West Michigan Dutch; Chicago Spanish (Mexican and Puerto Rican); Dearborn Arabic; Somali; Hmong; Anishinaabemowin; Dakota. **Concept in one line:** things and people being where and as they should be: the arrangement that holds off chaos, in the cosmos, society, the home and the self.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Chicago's South and West Sides against North Shore suburbs:** check complaint, policing and zoning records for different meanings of a well-kept street.
- **Detroit neighborhoods against Grosse Pointe and Dearborn:** compare demolition, code enforcement and policing records across the city line.
- **Southern Illinois and Indiana small towns:** check sundown-town records town by town; each is a separate claim.
- **Dakota and Ojibwe communities:** read each nation's own accounts of balance and place, not one "Native view".
- **Dutch-heritage West Michigan and German-heritage Milwaukee and Cincinnati:** compare festival and civic-improvement records rather than assume a household style.
- **Somali, Hmong and Arab American neighborhoods:** check how community meetings and police talk about safety and disorder.
- **Minneapolis after 2020:** read city, state, police-union and community accounts side by side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for riots, planning and neighborhood-disorder research; thin for how Somali and Hmong communities frame order in their own terms.
- **Languages needed:** Dakota and Anishinaabemowin for place and balance terms; Polish and Dutch for festival records; Arabic and Somali for safety talk.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1919, 1967, 1968 and 2020 are contested; sundown-town listings can name living communities; some Dakota sites and teachings are sacred.
- **Held for review:** which present nations claim descent from Cahokia is for the research to ask; no nation's statement is named here.
