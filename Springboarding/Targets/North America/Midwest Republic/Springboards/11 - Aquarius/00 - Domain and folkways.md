# Midwest Republic: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America (written for the Great Lakes states and the upper Mississippi Valley). **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch; Midwestern German and Plautdietsch; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish; Czech; Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what they hope for (associations, ideals toward strangers, networks, progress, causes, tolerance, and the ways of settling and neighboring).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Upper Midwest (Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan) and the lower Midwest (Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Missouri):** check whether settler origins (New England, Upland South, continental Europe) show in the associations each builds; Woodard's *American Nations* (2011) and the Census Bureau's Midwest region cut these differently and are sources' definitions only *check*.
- **Big-city neighborhoods and farm townships:** check which institutions carry community life in Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee or Cleveland against Iowa towns or the Driftless Area.
- **Anishinaabeg (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi), Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida of Wisconsin, Meskwaki:** check each nation's own account of what makes a people, apart from settler categories.
- **Amish and Mennonite settlements (Elkhart-LaGrange, Kalona, Arthur):** check how the church district sets the bounds of the group.
- **Later arrivals:** Hmong in St. Paul, Somali in Minneapolis, Arab Americans in Dearborn, Chaldeans in Detroit's suburbs, Mexican Pilsen and Little Village, Puerto Rican Humboldt Park, Bosnians in St. Louis: check whether hometown, clan or faith bodies organize community life, by generation.
- **Black Midwesterners (Bronzeville, Detroit, Kansas City's 18th and Vine):** check church, club and fraternal records apart from citywide figures.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nineteenth-century associations and European immigrant societies; mixed for post-1975 arrivals.
- **Languages needed:** German, Norwegian, Swedish, Polish and Czech for society records before about 1920; Spanish, Arabic, Somali and Hmong for today's community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the Great Lakes states and the upper Mississippi Valley, and Appalachian Ohio, the Plains, New England and the CSA appear only as comparison. Anishinaabe and Dakota ceremonial societies hold knowledge that is not public.
- **Held for review:** nothing set aside; national civic-life studies with no state or metro breakdown were weighed and left out as too coarse for a regional domain view.
