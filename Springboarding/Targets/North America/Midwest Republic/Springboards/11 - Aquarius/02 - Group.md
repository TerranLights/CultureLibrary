# Midwest Republic: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Finnish; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; how the person is weighed against the group, where "us" and "them" are drawn, and how members are expected to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Union towns (Flint, Toledo, Gary) against towns that never organized:** check how strike memory shapes who counts as "us".
- **Old Order, New Order and Swartzentruber-type affiliations among Amish settlements:** check which affiliations share communion with which, as the clearest written record of group lines.
- **Finnish church, temperance and socialist halls; Polish clerical and nationalist societies:** check each camp's own newspapers rather than one side's account of the other.
- **Dakota bands (Bdewakantunwan, Wahpekute, Wahpetunwan, Sisitunwan):** check band-level sources and the Dakota spellings each uses.
- **Black fraternal orders (Prince Hall Masons, Elks) in Chicago, Detroit, St. Louis and Kansas City:** check lodge records as a group history separate from white orders.
- **Hmong clan structures and Hmong Christian congregations:** check how each decides disputes, using community sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, fraternal orders and service clubs; mixed for gangs (mostly outside accounts); thin for clan decision-making.
- **Languages needed:** Finnish and Polish for hall and alliance newspapers; Hmong and Somali for clan and youth-group media; Dakota orthographies for band names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** gang histories touch living people and contested accounts of Chicago and Milwaukee policing; Somali clan affiliation is a sensitive topic and should be left to community sources; Anishinaabe clan teachings are shared selectively.
- **Held for review:** Somali clan bodies were weighed as pointers and kept out of section 2 until community sources say how, and whether, they wish to be studied.
