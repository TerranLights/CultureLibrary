# Midwest Republic: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch; German; Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish; Polish; Dutch; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; French (colonial records). **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Quaker settlements of eastern Indiana and western Ohio (Richmond, Wayne County):** check meeting minutes on equality against neighboring towns settled from the Upland South.
- **Scandinavian Lutheran towns of Minnesota and Wisconsin:** check whether leveling sayings appear in their own press or only in outside caricature.
- **Mexican and Tejano farmworkers in Ohio and Michigan:** check the Farm Labor Organizing Committee (Toledo) *check* records on pay and housing.
- **Chicago's South and West Sides and suburbs such as Cicero:** check the accounts of each side of housing conflicts as accounts, not as descriptions of residents.
- **Menominee and Oneida of Wisconsin:** check their own sources on distinct standing as nations.
- **Hmong and Somali communities in the Twin Cities:** check community sources on women's standing and public office, by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for race, housing and suffrage; mixed for class attitudes in small towns; thin in public sources for leveling customs inside Hmong and Somali communities.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial Illinois; German and Swedish for nineteenth-century radical and Lutheran press; Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali for present-day media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** redlining, segregation in Chicago and Milwaukee, and Detroit's housing history are contested ground: present each account as an account; laws on race and rights go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** under the rule on South Asian-heritage scholarship, one pointer that would otherwise sit in section 2 is held here only as that category.
