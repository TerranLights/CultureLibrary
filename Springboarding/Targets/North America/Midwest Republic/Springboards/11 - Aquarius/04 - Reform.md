*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Progressive Wisconsin and Minnesota against machine cities (Chicago, Kansas City under Pendergast):** check how reform and patronage were argued in each city's own press.
- **Social Gospel Ohio and Catholic reform in Chicago and St. Paul:** check which reforms each church body backed and which it opposed.
- **Dearborn's Arab American and Chaldean institutions (ACCESS, from 1971 *check*):** check how service agencies and mosques frame change from within.
- **Native nations as targets and authors of reform:** check the Menominee, Meskwaki and Saginaw Chippewa accounts separately; each nation's history differs.
- **Steel and auto towns (Gary, Youngstown's edge, Flint):** the Rust Belt account of reforms and plant closings is contested; check workers' and residents' own tellings first.
- **German freethinkers and Lutheran synods:** check which reforms (temperance, school language) split them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Progressive Era, temperance and settlement houses; mixed for Native nations' own records of imposed reforms; thin for Somali and Hmong debates on change in published form.
- **Languages needed:** German and Swedish for temperance and socialist debates; Dutch for the 1857 church split; Spanish and Arabic for present-day organizing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** boarding-school histories are survivors' and nations' testimony first; the removals of the 1830s and the Dakota War of 1862 are contested ground; laws produced by reforms belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the 2020 Minneapolis protests were weighed as a reform case and held for a later pass built on local and community archives, not national coverage.
