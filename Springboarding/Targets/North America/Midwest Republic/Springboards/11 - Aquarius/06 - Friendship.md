# Midwest Republic: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English; Pennsylvania Dutch and German; Swedish, Finnish; Polish, Czech; Spanish (Mexican and Puerto Rican); Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Myaamia. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Men in industrial towns (taverns, union halls) and men on farms (card clubs, deer camps):** check where adult men's friendships form in each, using oral histories.
- **Mexican compadrazgo in Pilsen and Little Village, and Puerto Rican friendship terms in Humboldt Park:** check each community's own usage; do not merge them.
- **Lebanese, Yemeni and Iraqi Arab Americans in Dearborn, and Chaldeans in Oakland County:** check how friendship and family overlap in each community's sources.
- **Amish youth groups in Elkhart-LaGrange:** check how young people's friend groups are organized and named before baptism.
- **Dakota, Ojibwe and Myaamia alliance traditions:** check each nation's account of making relatives and friends across peoples.
- **Black Midwesterners in Chicago and Detroit:** check social clubs and church auxiliaries as friendship institutions, by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for colonial and treaty-era alliance language and for national friendship surveys; mixed for regional cuts; thin for Somali and Bosnian friendship in published sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for colonial alliance records; Pennsylvania Dutch (to read "friend" correctly); Spanish, Arabic, Hmong and Somali for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Dakota and Lakota relative-making ceremonies are partly restricted; treaty speeches come through interpreters and clerks and need the nations' own readings.
- **Held for review:** Deloria's account was kept, but she wrote from Dakota communities farther west; how well it fits Minnesota's Dakota communities is held for the researcher to judge.
