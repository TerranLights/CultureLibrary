*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Amish who stay and Amish who leave (Elkhart-LaGrange, Holmes County edge, Kalona):** check leavers' memoirs and community responses as two accounts.
- **College towns (Madison, Ann Arbor, Yellow Springs, Iowa City) and their surrounding counties:** check local papers for how each side names the other.
- **Black religious movements in Detroit and Chicago (Nation of Islam, Moorish Science Temple):** check members' own publications beside outside accounts; never let one stand for the movement.
- **Two-Spirit Anishinaabe and Dakota people:** check Twin Cities and Wisconsin Two-Spirit societies' own statements *check*.
- **Juggalos (from Detroit's Insane Clown Posse):** check fans' accounts against the 2011 federal gang classification, treating each as an account.
- **Polish National Catholics and Roman Catholic Polish parishes:** check the split as each side recorded it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, freethinkers and Anabaptist dissent; mixed for youth subcultures; thin for Hmong, Somali and Arab American nonconformity in published sources.
- **Languages needed:** German and Czech for freethinker records; Pennsylvania Dutch for Amish youth terms; Norwegian for Haugean sources; Spanish and Arabic for present-day youth media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Amish leavers' stories involve living families; Two-Spirit teachings are each nation's to state; gang classifications and religious-movement histories are contested.
- **Held for review:** the Juggalo case stays in section 3 only, because its main sources (a federal classification and fan media) have not been weighed for reliability.
