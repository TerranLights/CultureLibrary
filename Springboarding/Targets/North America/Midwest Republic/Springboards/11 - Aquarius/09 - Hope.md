# Midwest Republic: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern English (including Black Chicago and Detroit usage); Norwegian, Swedish; Polish; Spanish; Arabic; Hmong; Somali; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and trusting, or acting as though, it can come, for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Black families of the Great Migration in Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee and Gary:** check how hopes are told by the generation that came and by its grandchildren.
- **Mexican Chicagoland pilgrims and Puerto Rican Humboldt Park:** check each community's own rituals of petition and thanks; do not merge them.
- **Farm counties in Iowa and southern Minnesota through the 1980s farm crisis:** check farm-press letters and extension records for hope and despair.
- **Auto and steel towns (Flint, Toledo, Gary):** the Rust Belt account of lost hope and revival is contested; check residents' own voices before planners'.
- **Myaamia, Dakota and Ho-Chunk language programs:** check each nation's own statements of what renewal is for.
- **Somali youth in Minneapolis and Hmong families in St. Paul:** check whether hopes are framed around the family, the clan or the person, in community media.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the Great Migration and for European emigrants' letters; mixed for present-day hope in survey form (mostly national or regional); thin for Bosnian St. Louis.
- **Languages needed:** Norwegian, Swedish and Polish for emigrant letters; Spanish and Arabic for religious petition; Somali and Hmong for youth and family hopes; Anishinaabemowin and Dakota terms checked with each nation's dictionaries.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths-of-despair data involve suicide and overdose; some manoomin practice is ceremonial; Detroit 1967 and the city's later decline are contested ground, told differently by different residents.
- **Held for review:** prosperity preaching in the region's megachurches was weighed as hope sold for money; with no source on it that could be placed with confidence, it is left for later research rather than named.
