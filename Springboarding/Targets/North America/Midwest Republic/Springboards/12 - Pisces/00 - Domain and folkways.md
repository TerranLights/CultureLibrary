# Midwest Republic: Domain and folkways, the hidden and transcendence (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch, German, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong, Somali; Scandinavian languages, Finnish, Czech, Dutch; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the hidden and of transcendence: how suffering, the unseen, confinement and the wish to escape are met, and where the line falls between religion, magic and science (the folkway of magic ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Anishinaabeg (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi): check what each community publishes on spirits and healing, and keep it apart from what it keeps.
- Dakota, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Meskwaki and Wisconsin Oneida: check each nation's own materials separately, since missionary records blur them.
- Amish and Mennonite affiliations (Holmes County, Ohio; Elkhart-LaGrange): check whether charm-healing is kept, refused or argued over.
- Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicagoans: check fright illness against spiritism; the frameworks have different homelands.
- Hmong of St. Paul, Wausau and Milwaukee: check shamanic healing beside clinic care, by generation and church.
- Somali Minneapolis, Arab Dearborn (Lebanese, Yemeni, Iraqi) and Detroit's Chaldeans: check where recited healing meets the clinic.
- Black Chicago and Detroit: check spiritual churches and practice carried north in the Great Migration.
- German, Polish, Czech and Irish parishes; Czech freethinkers as contrast.
- Appalachian out-migrants in Cincinnati, Dayton, Detroit: check what folk medicine traveled north. Appalachia and the Prairie Federation themselves: comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for confinement and mortality; mixed for magic ways (strong older collections, little recent fieldwork); thin for Somali and Bosnian practice.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet takes the Great Lakes states and upper Mississippi Valley as the core.
- **Languages needed:** French and some Latin for the *Relations* and colonial Detroit; German, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong, Somali, Anishinaabemowin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Midewiwin scrolls and ceremonies; asylum patient files; boarding-school records; possession cases involving minors.
- **Held for review:** a paranormal-tourism directory of "haunted Midwest" sites was weighed and left out as unsourced lore; the Mansfield reformatory's ghost tours stay a lead, not a pointer.

## 5. First queries
1. Hyatt, *Folk-Lore from Adams County*: work its index under "cure", "ghost", "sign", "witch".
2. CDC WONDER: suicide and overdose deaths by county, 1999 on, for the Great Lakes states; set beside County Health Rankings.
3. Archives of Michigan and Minnesota Historical Society: "state hospital" and "poor farm" finding aids, with access rules.
4. Ojibwe People's Dictionary: "manidoo", "jiibay"; then tribal college libraries for community-published teachings.
5. HathiTrust full text of the Thwaites *Relations*: "songe" and "jongleur" near Michilimackinac and La Pointe.
