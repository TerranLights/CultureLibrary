# Midwest Republic: Empathy (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English (Inland North and Midland speech); German, Spanish, Polish, Swedish, Norwegian, Arabic, Hmong, Somali; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** feeling or grasping what another person feels or thinks, from their side.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Inland North and Midland speech areas: check how distress is hinted and read, since reading others depends on local talk norms.
- Black Chicago and Detroit beside white ethnic parishes: check accounts of cross-line understanding sought or refused in the open-housing years.
- Arab Dearborn and Chaldean Detroit after 2001: check the museum, interfaith and media efforts aimed at outsiders, and how they were received.
- Hmong and Somali patients with Midwestern clinicians: check interpreters' accounts of being understood or missed.
- Amish and Mennonite writers: check how understanding of outsiders is taught, from their own publications.
- Anishinaabeg and Dakota: check talking circles and storytelling as listening forms, as communities describe them.
- Rural men in farm towns: check where feeling-talk does or does not happen, and who disputes the picture.
- Generations: check the online "empath" identity among the young against older usage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for clinical history (Chicago is a center) and media archives; mixed for survey data with regional cuts; thin for Somali and Anishinaabemowin terms.
- **Languages needed:** German for the *Einfühlung* line; Hmong for liver idioms; Arabic for Dearborn press; Anishinaabemowin via language programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** therapy session records (consent); Black Hawk's *Life* as a mediated text; talk-show guests' privacy.
- **Held for review:** a widely shared "walk a mile in his moccasins" poem, often credited to a Native source, was weighed and left out; its attribution is doubtful and it would put words in a Native mouth.
