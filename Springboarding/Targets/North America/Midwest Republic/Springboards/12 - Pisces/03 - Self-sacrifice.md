# Midwest Republic: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Pennsylvania Dutch, Dutch, Norwegian, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong; Ho-Chunk and Anishinaabemowin through community sources. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Amish and Mennonite affiliations: check how martyr stories are taught to children now, from their own publications.
- Lebanese and Iraqi Shia of Dearborn beside Yemeni Sunni and Chaldean Catholics: check each community's own martyrs and days.
- Polish, Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago: check the self-giving mother ideal in each, and where daughters' own writing contests it.
- Hmong families of St. Paul and Wisconsin: check veterans' service and how its recognition is argued over.
- Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Oneida and Anishinaabe communities: check veterans' honors as each nation describes them.
- Black Chicago and Detroit: check how Great Migration parents' sacrifices are told by children and grandchildren.
- Farm families of Iowa and southern Minnesota: check who gave up land, schooling or marriage to keep a farm.
- Labor towns (Gary, Flint, Toledo): check the strikers' and their families' accounts.
- Out-migrant Appalachian families in Dayton and Cincinnati: check what parents gave up to work in the plants.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, donation and caregiving data; mixed for religious sacrifice practice (good for Catholics and Shia, thin elsewhere); thin for Somali and Bosnian sources here.
- **Languages needed:** Dutch, German and Polish for older devotional and press sources; Arabic for Ashura materials; Hmong for veterans' testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** political uses of *shahīd*; Haymarket as contested memory; parts of the war-in-Laos record still restricted; casualty families' privacy.
- **Held for review:** Lincoln's Bixby letter, the best-known American condolence on sacrifice, was weighed and left out: its recipient was a Boston widow, outside these lands.
