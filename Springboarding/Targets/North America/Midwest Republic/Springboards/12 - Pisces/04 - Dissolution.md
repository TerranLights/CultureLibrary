# Midwest Republic: Dissolution (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German (including Amana and Wisconsin German), Swedish, Finnish, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong; Myaamia and Anishinaabemowin through revival programs. **Concept in one line:** the coming apart of a fixed form: the self's edges lost in something larger (a person, a crowd, nature, the divine), and the ending of bonds, groups and identities (working definition proposed; the author decides).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Amana, Bishop Hill and the Shakers beside Amish settlements: check what each group did to hold together or let go.
- Black Chicago and Detroit: check the dance floor and ecstatic worship as participants themselves describe them.
- German, Polish, Czech, Dutch and Scandinavian descendants: check language shift by generation and county.
- Native Chicagoans and Twin Citians from relocation families beside reservation communities: check city-made community against dispersal.
- Myaamia and Shawnee: check removal and reawakening, as each nation tells it.
- Ethnic Catholic parishes (Polish, Slovak, Hungarian, Italian): check which mergers were fought, and how.
- Arab Dearborn, Chaldeans and Bosnians of St. Louis: check pressure to assimilate and the counter-pressure to keep apart.
- Hmong elders and youth: check what is said of lost language and lost soul together.
- Mill and plant towns (Flint, Gary, Toledo): check residents' words for a town coming apart, beside the outside Rust Belt account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for communal-society archives, aging studies and language-shift data; mixed for dance and worship experience (mostly oral); thin for Sufi practice and Hmong soul-loss in print.
- **Languages needed:** German (Amana's and Wisconsin's), Swedish (Bishop Hill), Polish and Slovak (parish fights), Myaamia through the Center.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relocation and removal histories (nations' voices first); dementia data (consent); "ruin" imagery as a contested portrayal of Detroit.
- **Held for review:** anonymous festival-goers' online trip reports were weighed and left as a later lead; they cannot be placed in any region.
