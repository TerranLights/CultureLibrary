# Midwest Republic: Boundary (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch, German, Dutch, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Hmong; Anishinaabemowin, Dakota, Meskwaki. **Concept in one line:** the lines a person or group draws between self and others, and between groups, places and categories, and how firmly they are held.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Amish affiliations (Swartzentruber, Old Order, New Order): check where each sets the line with "the world" and how shunning is used.
- Black Detroiters and Chicagoans beside white ethnic parishioners: check each side's account of the same neighborhood line.
- Ojibwe bands of Wisconsin's ceded territories and their non-Native neighbors: check accounts of the boat landings from both sides.
- Dakota, Ho-Chunk and Meskwaki: check homeland against treaty lines; the Meskwaki land bought in 1857 is its own case.
- Arab Dearborn (Lebanese, Yemeni, Iraqi) and Chaldeans: check privacy and modesty lines as members describe them; outside debates as accounts.
- Hmong households: check ritual limits on entry and visiting.
- Upper Peninsula and Lower Peninsula: check the state-line identity the Toledo War left.
- Border towns (Detroit, Sault Ste. Marie, International Falls): check crossing as routine (port-of-entry counts exist).
- Farm families of Iowa and Illinois: check line-fence and drainage quarrels as neighbors tell them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for maps, surveys and segregation studies; mixed for everyday privacy norms; thin for Hmong and Somali household rules in print.
- **Languages needed:** Pennsylvania Dutch and Dutch for church-world lines; Arabic for Dearborn debates; Dakota and Anishinaabemowin for homeland names.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** treaty lines and ceded territory (nations' accounts first); Birwood Wall, redlining and open housing as contested memory; modesty debates around Dearborn.
- **Held for review:** the township "fence viewer", who settles line-fence quarrels in several Midwestern states, was weighed and left for Libra as a legal office.
