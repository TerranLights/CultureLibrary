# Midwest Republic: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; Pennsylvania Dutch, German, Dutch, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Somali, Hmong; Sauk, Meskwaki, Potawatomi and Dakota accounts through each nation. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher (God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death); release and letting go included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- AA groups in Ohio and Minnesota beside Native-led wellness circles in Minneapolis and on reservations: check how each frames giving up control.
- Amish and Mennonite affiliations beside Lutheran synods: check yieldedness against Lutheran teaching on God's will.
- Black Chicago's gospel churches: check songs and testimony of surrender, congregation by congregation.
- Polish, Italian, Irish and Mexican Catholics: check novenas and petitions as letters of handing over.
- Dutch Reformed of West Michigan: check catechism teaching against members' own accounts of trial.
- Arab Dearborn, Somali Minneapolis, Bosnians of St. Louis: check trust in God's will in sermons and in illness.
- Hmong families: check funeral rites that send the dead on, as families describe them.
- Sauk and Meskwaki, Potawatomi, Dakota: check how each nation remembers surrenders forced on it.
- Iowa and Illinois farm families of the 1980s farm crisis: check accounts of giving up the farm.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for recovery history, end-of-life data and Catholic devotion; mixed for Protestant and Muslim practice; thin for Hmong and Somali writing on letting go.
- **Languages needed:** German and Dutch for hymn and catechism lines; Pennsylvania Dutch; Arabic for sermons; Dakota and Potawatomi through each nation's materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** removals, the Black Hawk War and 1862 as contested accounts; AA anonymity; patients' end-of-life records.
- **Held for review:** Reinhold Niebuhr, raised in Missouri and a Detroit pastor in the 1920s, is credited with the Serenity Prayer, but where and when he wrote it has been debated; held until the attribution is checked.
