## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Indiana and Ohio Quakers beside Catholic Jesuit-educated circles: check how each tells a true prompting from a false one.
- Farm families of Iowa, Illinois and Indiana: check weather lore and dowsing against extension-service advice.
- Amish settlements: check almanac use and what is said against "signs" and fortune-telling.
- Black Chicago: check dream and number lore in its own setting, including churches that opposed it.
- Anishinaabeg and Dakota: check only what each community publishes on dreams and sky knowledge.
- Hmong and Somali families: check dreams as told in families, and who interprets them.
- Arab Dearborn and Chaldean Detroit: check dream-telling among Muslims and Eastern Christians separately.
- Nurses, firefighters and police in Chicago, Detroit and Milwaukee: check union and training records on trusting the hunch.
- Spiritualists of Indiana and Michigan: check mediums' own accounts of "knowing".

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for psychology of judgment (Minnesota is a center) and folk forecasting; mixed for religious discernment; thin for Somali and Hmong dream lore in print.
- **Languages needed:** German for almanacs; Arabic for dream manuals; Hmong and Somali for family dream-telling; Anishinaabemowin for dream teachings shared publicly.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Anishinaabe dream and vision knowledge (communities decide what is shared); spiritualist clients' names; profiling disguised as "gut feeling".
- **Held for review:** the *Old Farmer's Almanac* and its secret forecasting formula were weighed; it is a New Hampshire publication, so it stays a contrast to the Ohio-printed Amish almanac, not a pointer.
