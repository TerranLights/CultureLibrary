# Midwest Republic: Crime (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English (Chicago and Detroit usage included); Pennsylvania Dutch, Dutch, German, Polish, Spanish, Arabic, Somali, Hmong; Native-language terms only as nations publish them. **Concept in one line:** what counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how those who commit it are seen. Law itself is researched only in Libra and Capricorn; this sheet takes the idea of wrongdoing and the wrongdoer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Chicago neighborhoods (Bronzeville, Little Village, Back of the Yards, the North Shore suburbs): check who neighbors name as wrongdoers against what surveys record.
- Black and white Detroiters: check how 1967 is named and remembered on each side.
- Italian American organizations of Chicago: check how they have answered gangster imagery in film and press.
- Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago: check church and outreach accounts of leaving gangs.
- Ojibwe, Dakota, Menominee and Ho-Chunk: check harm and repair as each nation shares it, beside the missing-relatives work.
- Amish affiliations: check how wrongs inside the church are handled and how attacks on Amish people are spoken of.
- Somali Minneapolis and Hmong St. Paul: check press portrayals against community accounts.
- Small-town Wisconsin and Iowa: check local fascination with home-county cases.
- Appalachian out-migrants in Cincinnati and Uptown Chicago: check how the press of their day labeled them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (Chicago is the birthplace of much crime sociology; strong city open data); mixed for small towns; thin for Somali and Hmong accounts in their own languages.
- **Languages needed:** Polish and German for older press crime columns; Spanish for Chicago community press; Arabic, Somali and Hmong for community responses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Detroit 1967 and redlining-era violence as contested ground; victims' families' privacy; juvenile records; MMIR testimony.
- **Held for review:** one work by a South Asian-heritage scholar was set aside under the standing rule and stays off this sheet until the author decides.
