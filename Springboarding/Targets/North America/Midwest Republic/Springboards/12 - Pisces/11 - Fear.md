# Midwest Republic: Fear (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Midwest Republic, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Midwestern American English; German, Pennsylvania Dutch, Polish, Czech, Spanish, Arabic, Somali, Hmong; Anishinaabemowin. **Concept in one line:** the emotion felt at danger, real or imagined, and the ways of naming, showing, managing and using it; worry and anxiety as its diffuse forms.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Farm and small-town families of Iowa, Illinois and Indiana: check storm fear and drills against what elders recall.
- Black Chicagoans and Detroiters: check fear of violence and fear of being taken for a threat, both, in their own words.
- Jewish Skokie and Twin Cities suburbs: check survivors' and descendants' accounts.
- Arab Dearborn, Chaldean Detroit, Somali Minneapolis: check fear of suspicion and surveillance after 2001, as accounts.
- Mexican and Puerto Rican Chicago: check fear tied to immigration enforcement and the bogeymen of childhood tales.
- Hmong families: check night terrors and sudden deaths as families, healers and doctors told them.
- Anishinaabeg: check winter tales and their teaching use, only as communities share them.
- German, Polish and Czech descendants: check which old-country bogeymen survived in English.
- Indiana and Michigan teenagers: check legend trips and online legends as the young tell them.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, folklore archives and horror literature; mixed for fear-of-crime survey data at state level; thin for Somali sources.
- **Languages needed:** German, Polish and Czech for children's lore; Arabic for Dearborn press; Hmong for night-terror accounts; Anishinaabemowin for tales shared publicly.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1919 riot, Cabrini-Green and post-2001 Dearborn as contested accounts; Hmong deaths (families' privacy); *wiindigoo* stories (seasonal and community rules).
- **Held for review:** online "creepypasta" legends set in Michigan and Ohio were weighed and left as a later lead; they cannot be traced to tellers or places.

## 5. First queries
1. Indiana University Folklore Archives: finding aid; search "legend trip", "haunted bridge", "cemetery".
2. NOAA Storm Events Database: deaths by county since 1950; then local press on siren systems.
3. HathiTrust: the 1922 Commission report; list the testimony on fear and rumor.
4. Library catalog: Adler (2011), notes on Hmong communities and the CDC's 1980s reports.
5. Household Pulse Survey: anxiety estimates by state, Great Lakes states, 2020-2024.
