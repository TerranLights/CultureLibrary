# Quebec: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and regional speech), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, East Cree (Eeyou), Kanien'kéha, Anishinaabemowin, Atikamekw, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Mi'gmaq, W8banaki, Wendat, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what a self is here and how it asserts itself: personhood, the body, appearance, naming, beginnings and initiative.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophones of Montreal against those of the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, the Beauce and the Gaspé: check whether naming fashions and regional self-labels move together or apart.
- Anglophone Quebecers of the West Island, the Eastern Townships and the Gaspé coast: compare census self-labels and memoirs.
- Inuit of Nunavik: namesake relations may define a person differently from the civil registry; check both.
- Eeyou, Innu and Atikamekw communities: compare how wellness, land and personhood are linked in each people's own terms.
- Wendat of Wendake and Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: compare dress and regalia groups as self-presentation.
- Acadians of the Magdalen Islands: check how a few common surnames shape the use of bynames.
- Montreal's Haitian, Hasidic and Moroccan Sephardic communities: compare naming practices and dress codes at worship and school, each from its own sources.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for francophone naming and parish records; mixed for Anglophone and immigrant self-presentation; thinner and community-held for Indigenous personhood. The nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for Quebec as the province stands, with Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Quebec French for most archives; Inuktitut and East Cree for naming and wellness terms; Yiddish and Haitian Creole for community self-descriptions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous names and naming ceremonies are shared only as the nations share them; Jesuit accounts are outsiders' readings; dress codes at work and worship touch the contested secularism debate (point to Libra and Capricorn for the law).
- **Held for review:** for this domain, no naming-statistics, portrait or ethnographic pointer had to be set aside under the standing rules.
