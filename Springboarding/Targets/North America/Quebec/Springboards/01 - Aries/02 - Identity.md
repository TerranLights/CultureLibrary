*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophones who answer *Québécois* and those who still answer *Canadien français*: check age, region and family history before reading either as a type.
- *Bleuets*, *Beaucerons* and Gaspésiens against Montrealers: compare regional self-labels with national ones.
- English-speaking Quebecers of the Townships, the Gaspé coast and Montreal's West Island: check how each frames belonging.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: membership debates differ by community.
- Wendat of Wendake, Innu of the Côte-Nord, Atikamekw of the Mauricie, Anishinaabeg of the Outaouais and Abitibi: compare each people's land-based self-naming.
- Nunavik Inuit and Eeyou: compare collective identity under their regional bodies.
- Self-identified Métis groups in Quebec: their claims are contested; check how First Nations and Métis bodies respond.
- Montreal's Vietnamese, Italian, Haitian and Jewish communities: compare first- and second-generation memoirs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for francophone national identity; mixed for minority and newcomer identities; Indigenous membership questions are held by each nation and partly closed.
- **Languages needed:** French for most scholarship; English for Anglophone archives; Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut and Innu-aimun for self-names and their senses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *de souche* and *pure laine* are contested; the language laws, the secularism law and the referendums are contested accounts, to be read as social events here; Indigenous membership is each nation's own matter.
- **Held for review:** among the identity surveys and immigrant-community memoirs considered, none had to be withheld under the standing rules.
