## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophone families of the Saguenay and the Beauce against Montreal's: check the age of leaving home and who decides schooling.
- English-speaking communities of the Townships and the Gaspé: compare how they framed control of their own schools.
- Montreal's Hasidic communities of Outremont and Boisbriand: check accounts both by members and by people who left; a sensitive subject.
- Maghrebi, Lebanese, Haitian and Latin American families: compare first and second generations on marriage and career choice.
- Kanien'kehá:ka, Eeyou, Atikamekw, Nunavik Inuit and Wolastoqiyik: collective self-rule takes a different form in each; compare their own documents.
- Women of different generations: compare memoirs from before and after the Quiet Revolution, reading its account of the *Grande Noirceur* as one account.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for family sociology and psychology of motivation; mixed for immigrant families; Indigenous self-government documents are public, but internal debates are each nation's own.
- **Languages needed:** French for commissions and family studies; Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut and Atikamekw for governance terms; Yiddish for Hasidic community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** end-of-life choice, abortion and leaving religious communities are sensitive; the James Bay agreements and the secularism law are contested; the law itself belongs to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the end-of-life and motivation literature was scanned for pointers to set aside, and none was.
