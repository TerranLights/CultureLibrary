## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Beauce and the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean: check regional business-formation data against local lore about founders before accepting either.
- Gaspé and Magdalen Islands fishing communities: compare cooperative and private starts.
- Nunavik Inuit co-ops and Eeyou firms: compare collective with individual initiative in each people's own accounts.
- Innu, Atikamekw and Anishinaabeg youth: compare Wapikoni films by community for who starts projects.
- Montreal's Lebanese, Greek, Italian, Portuguese and Haitian small-business owners: check immigrant-business studies by community and generation.
- English-speaking business families of Montreal and the Townships: compare their founding stories with francophone ones.
- Rural women's circles (*Cercles de fermières*) and parish men's associations: who launched what.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for cooperatives, colonization and business statistics; mixed for immigrant enterprise; Indigenous enterprise data sit with each nation's corporations.
- **Languages needed:** French for co-op and colonization archives; English for Montreal business history; Inuktitut and East Cree for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "enterprising region" lore can turn into regional stereotype; treat it as a claim to test. The James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement is contested ground.
- **Held for review:** among the business surveys and founder biographies looked at for this concept, none called for setting aside.
