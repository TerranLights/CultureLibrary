## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Ironworking families of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: compare their own histories with outsiders' magazine portraits.
- Francophone veterans and conscription resisters: compare the memory of both in the same towns.
- Log drivers of the Mauricie, the Outaouais and Charlevoix: check union and parish records alongside songs.
- Madelinots and Gaspé fishers: compare sealing and sea-rescue memory between francophone and English-speaking families.
- Inuit Rangers of Nunavik and Innu hunters of the Côte-Nord: compare what each counts as a hard trial in their own words.
- Haitian exiles and Montreal's Holocaust survivors: check testimony collections for how each frames endurance.
- Women and men: check whether award citations and memoirs praise the same acts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, hero-cult and award records; mixed for working trades; Indigenous warrior traditions are held by each nation.
- **Languages needed:** French; English for ironworkers and Anglophone veterans; Kanien'kéha for the Warrior Society's own terms; Yiddish for testimony.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990 Kanehsatà:ke resistance and the Warrior Society are contested ground, to be pointed to only as social events and each account kept as an account; the seal hunt is contested; trauma in testimony needs care.
- **Held for review:** the war memoirs and bravery citations raised no pointer that the standing rules would set aside.
