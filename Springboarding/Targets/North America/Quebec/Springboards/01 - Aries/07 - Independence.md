## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal renters and homeowners in the regions: check how Moving Day and home ownership shape the age of leaving home.
- Farm families of the Mauricie and the Saguenay: compare the 1898 monograph and the 1996 history on self-provisioning.
- Villages of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Gaspé hinterland: compare those that organized to stay with those that closed.
- Eeyou hunters, Innu families going *nutshimit* and Nunavik Inuit hunters: compare each people's programs and stories of living from the land.
- Elders and disabled people: compare their own groups' words for independence with the state's.
- English-speaking Gaspé communities and Montreal's Hasidic communities: check how each runs its own institutions, from their own sources.
- Haitian, Filipino and Latin American households in Montreal: check how remittances figure in household accounts.
- Referendum memory: compare by region, age and language without reading any result as a group's nature.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for rural household history and statistics; mixed for immigrant households; Indigenous land-based programs are documented, but harvest data may be restricted.
- **Languages needed:** French; East Cree, Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for land-based terms; Haitian Creole for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the referendums, the sovereignty debate and the James Bay agreements are contested ground; welfare words stigmatize; harvest locations can be restricted.
- **Held for review:** the household-economy studies and remittance research were weighed against the standing rules, and nothing had to be withheld.
