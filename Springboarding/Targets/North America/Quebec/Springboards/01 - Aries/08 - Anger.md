# Quebec: Anger (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, Wendat, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut, East Cree, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of grievance and the urge to strike back or set things right, and how it is expressed.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophones of different regions and classes: check whether the strength felt in each oath differs between Montreal, Quebec City and the Saguenay.
- English-speaking Quebecers: compare the 1990s "Angryphone" label with Anglophone community groups' own accounts.
- Kanien'kehá:ka, Wendat and Eeyou: compare each people's own teaching on clearing anger with health-board program language.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare the regional survey's categories with community wellness workers' terms.
- Haitian and Latin American families of Montréal-Nord: compare community and media accounts of 2008.
- Women and men in working-class Montreal: check the play's reception against oral histories.
- Students of different regions in 2012: compare who joined the *casseroles* and where.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for swearing, aggression research and public unrest; mixed for private, everyday anger; Indigenous ceremonial knowledge is shared only as each nation chooses.
- **Languages needed:** Quebec French with its colloquial registers; Kanien'kéha and Inuktitut for teaching terms; Haitian Creole for folk-illness terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conjugal violence, police deaths and riots are painful and contested; the 1990 Kanehsatà:ke resistance is contested ground and belongs here only as a social event; the Condolence ceremony is not for outside use.
- **Held for review:** the swearing studies and aggression research carried no item that needed holding back.
