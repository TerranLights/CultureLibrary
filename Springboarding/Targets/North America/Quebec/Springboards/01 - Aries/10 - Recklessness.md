## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Young drivers of rural regions (Abitibi, Bas-Saint-Laurent, the Beauce) and of Montreal: compare crash and survey data before reading any pattern as a type.
- Snowmobilers and ATV riders of the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Côte-Nord: check coroners' reports and clubs' safety rules together.
- Nunavik Inuit and Eeyou hunters: compare what each counts as heedless on changing ice, in their own words.
- Ice canoe crews of Quebec City and Isle-aux-Coudres: check how crews talk about danger and tradition.
- The *bòlèt* among Haitian Montrealers and the provincial lottery: check gambling research by community, not by reputation.
- Street youth of Montreal and the West Island's English-speaking youth: compare harm-reduction records and novels.
- Women and men: check the youth survey's breakdowns before assuming who takes which risks.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for road, injury and gambling statistics; mixed for cultural meanings of daring; Indigenous land-safety knowledge is partly public through regional bodies.
- **Languages needed:** French for coroners and agencies; Inuktitut for ice terms; Haitian Creole for gambling terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** drug use, suicide-adjacent risk and deaths need care; coroners' reports name people; the legends' devil figures are folklore, not findings.
- **Held for review:** the risk surveys and gambling research were reviewed for anything the standing rules would set aside, and nothing qualified.
