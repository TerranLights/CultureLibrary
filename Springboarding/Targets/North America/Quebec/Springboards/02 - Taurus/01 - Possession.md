# Quebec: Possession (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Mi'gmaq, Wendat, East Cree (Eeyou), Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, W8banaki, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Italian. **Concept in one line:** having and holding things: the bond between people and the things they keep, use, carry, store, lend, lose and give away.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's many tenants against owner households of Chaudière-Appalaches: check whether renting changes how much is kept and where it is stored.
- Mi'gmaq of Gespeg, Gesgapegiag and Listuguj: compare Le Clercq's account with each community's own telling of gift and keeping.
- Wendat of Wendake: check how Wendat writers today describe the goods of the dead against the Jesuit report.
- Eeyou communities of Oujé-Bougoumou and Whapmagoostui: compare objects held by the institute with objects kept in families.
- Inuit of Nunavik's coastal villages: check where personal property ends and shared hunting gear begins.
- Italian Montrealers of Saint-Léonard and Little Italy: compare family memories of 1940 with the archival record.
- Haitian families of Montréal-Nord and Saint-Michel, and Vietnamese families of Côte-des-Neiges: check what each sends abroad and what it keeps.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for museum collections and missionary texts; mixed for household surveys of belongings; thin and scattered for hoarding and lost property.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Le Clercq and Brébeuf; Mi'gmaq and Wendat for the peoples' own terms; Italian and Haitian Creole for community memories.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grave goods and the Feast of the Dead touch Wendat ancestral remains; repatriated objects are each nation's to describe; hoarding cases involve private health data.
- **Held for review:** the museum and oral-history pointers for possession were checked against the standing rules, and nothing had to be set aside.
