## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Descendants of seigneurial *censitaires* in the Richelieu valley against Townships freehold families: check whether two tenure histories left different words and feelings about owning land.
- Madelinots: compare family memories of tenancy under the proprietors with land records.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kanehsatà:ke, Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: each community's land history differs; separate them before comparing.
- Innu of Mashteuiatsh, Essipit and Nutashkuan against Innu communities outside the treaty talks: check how each frames title.
- Inuit landholding corporations of Kuujjuaq, Puvirnituq and Inukjuak: compare how each village manages common land.
- Montreal renters of Hochelaga and Parc-Extension against suburban owners of Laval and the South Shore: check tenure, not attitudes.
- Families expropriated at Mirabel and Forillon: compare how each group tells its loss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for seigneurial records, tenure statistics and expropriation history; mixed for Indigenous title, which is negotiated and documented in each nation's own sources.
- **Languages needed:** old notarial French for land rolls; Innu-aimun and East Cree for territory words; Kanien'kéha for Kanehsatà:ke's own records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Kanehsatà:ke, the James Bay agreements and the treaty talks are contested; property law and the Civil Code belong to Libra, and the purpose of property law to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** land-tenure, cooperative and public-finance pointers were all weighed under the standing rules, and none needed holding back.
