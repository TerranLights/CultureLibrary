# Quebec: Security (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Dairy farmers of the Montérégie hit by the 1998 ice storm against Montreal apartment dwellers: check how each group says it got through.
- Retirees on public pensions against seasonal workers of the Côte-Nord and Gaspé: compare what security means with and without a steady job.
- Inuit of Nunavik's Ungava and Hudson coasts: compare store prices, country-food sharing and freezer programs.
- Innu of Matimekush and Naskapi of Kawawachikamach: separate the two peoples' accounts of the caribou closure.
- Atikamekw of Wemotaci and Opitciwan: check how each community negotiates its land and harvest with forestry firms.
- Jewish free-loan borrowers and Haitian *sòl* members: compare two community forms of mutual help, each in its own records.
- Unionized public-sector workers against gig workers in Montreal: check what each counts as security.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for pensions, unions and food-bank reports; mixed for informal savings in immigrant communities; Indigenous food security is well studied but best read in each nation's own reports.
- **Languages needed:** French for most records; Inuktitut, Innu-aimun and Naskapi for food and land documents; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** food insecurity in Nunavik and the caribou closure are hard subjects for the communities; pension and labor law belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** pension, union and food-aid pointers were reviewed for this concept under the standing rules, and none had to be put aside.
