## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Farm families of the Beauce and the Lac-Saint-Jean against suburban households of Laval and the Rive-Sud: check years in the same home and in the same parish.
- Old Anglophone families of the Eastern Townships and the Gaspé coast against Anglophones who left after 1976: compare who stayed, and how each tells it.
- Eeyou of Chisasibi and Oujé-Bougoumou: compare two different stories of moving and settling.
- Inuit elders and younger Nunavimmiut: separate memories of camp life from village life.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke against those of Akwesasne: compare the Seaway's effects on each.
- Italian families of Saint-Léonard and Portuguese families of the Plateau: check how long each has stayed in its first neighborhood.
- Innu and Naskapi near Schefferville: compare their accounts of the mine's opening and closing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for parish genealogy, rural literature and heritage lists; mixed for urban displacement; Indigenous relocations are documented in inquiry reports and community histories.
- **Languages needed:** French for most sources; East Cree and Inuktitut for relocation testimony; Italian and Portuguese for neighborhood memory projects.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** relocations and the sled-dog killings are painful histories; the *survivance* reading of rural Quebec is one account among several, as is the Quiet Revolution's account of what came before.
- **Held for review:** for stability, the genealogical and heritage pointers were checked against the standing rules, and nothing was set aside.
