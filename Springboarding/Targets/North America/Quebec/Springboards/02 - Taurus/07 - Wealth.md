## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Westmount and Mount Royal Anglophone families against Outremont francophone business families: check how each displays or hides wealth, in memoirs and the press.
- Côte-Nord mining wage earners against Gaspésie and Bas-Saint-Laurent households: compare regional income data before reading attitudes.
- Gaspé fishing families of Paspébiac and Grande-Rivière: check merchant ledgers against family memory of debt and wealth.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: separate supporters' and critics' accounts of gaming revenue.
- Innu of Essipit against Innu of Unamen Shipu and Pakua Shipi: compare economic plans; sources for the smaller Lower North Shore communities are scarce, so start with their band offices.
- Inuit beneficiaries in Kuujjuaq and smaller villages: check how compensation wealth reaches each community.
- Montreal's Jewish, Lebanese and Haitian communities: compare each community's own histories of entrepreneurs and donors, without generalizing from a few names.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income statistics, elite histories and business press; mixed for attitudes toward the rich; Indigenous economies are documented mostly by each nation and its corporations.
- **Languages needed:** French and English for business and elite sources; Innu-aimun, Inuktitut and Kanien'kéha for community plans; Arabic for Lebanese press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** named families' private lives need care; the Plan Nord and gaming debates are contested; tax law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** rich-list, philanthropy and Indigenous-enterprise pointers were weighed against the standing rules for this concept; nothing needed setting aside.
