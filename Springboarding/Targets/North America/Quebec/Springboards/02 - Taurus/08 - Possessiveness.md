# Quebec: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, East Cree (Eeyou), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Arabic (Maghrebi communities), Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Innu families of the Côte-Nord, Atikamekw families of the Haute-Mauricie and Eeyou tallymen of the James Bay coast: compare how each people describes holding and sharing a family hunting territory.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how sharing rules for game are explained by elders and by younger hunters.
- Laurentides and Mauricie sport-fishing communities: compare memories of the private-club era with those of the ZEC era.
- Francophone and Anglophone teens in the PAJ study: check whether control items differ by language group or region, as the data show.
- Haitian, Maghrebi and Latin American Montrealers: check each community's own organizations for how they discuss jealousy in couples, without importing outside generalizations.
- Rural Mauricie and urban Montreal audiences: compare how *western* songs and city pop treat jealous love.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fiction, partner-violence surveys and the private-club history; mixed for everyday possessiveness over objects and spots; thin for small communities' own terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for most sources; Innu-aimun and Atikamekw for territory words; Haitian Creole and Arabic for community discussions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner violence and control involve victims' safety and privacy; hunting-territory knowledge may be family-held; criminal law on coercive control belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** partner-violence, folklore and hunting-territory pointers on possessiveness were weighed under the standing rules; none needed holding.
