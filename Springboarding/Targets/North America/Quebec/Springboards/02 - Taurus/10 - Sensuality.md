## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lanaudière and Charlevoix *veillée* traditions against Montreal club culture: check how dance and music are described as pleasure in each.
- Gaspé and Magdalen Islands seafood feasts against Beauce maple feasts: compare seasonal food pleasures by place.
- Wendat of Wendake and Innu of Mani-utenam: compare how each nation shares its food and music with outsiders.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how country food is spoken of as pleasure and as health, in Inuit health materials.
- Montreal's Ashkenazi, Portuguese and Haitian communities: compare the foods and feasts each describes as its own.
- Parishes that took the temperance pledge against those that did not: check parish records rather than reputations.
- Older and younger Quebecers: compare time-use data on meals and rest.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for food history, music and church condemnation of pleasure; mixed for touch and comfort norms; thin for some Indigenous communities' food-pleasure terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for church letters and food media; Innu-aimun, Wendat and Inuktitut for the peoples' own words; Portuguese and Haitian Creole for community feasts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the clergy's account of pleasure belongs to the contested account of the *Grande Noirceur*; read it as one source. Some Indigenous ceremonies with food and song are not public. Sexuality itself belongs to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** food, music and spa pointers for sensuality were weighed under the standing rules, and none was held back.
