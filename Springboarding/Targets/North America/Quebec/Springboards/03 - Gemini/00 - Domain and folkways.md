## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, Beauce and Gaspé speakers: check whether older glossaries and recent corpora record regional speech the same way.
- Madelinots: compare Acadian-rooted island speech and schooling with mainland usage.
- Anglophones of the West Island, the Townships and the Gaspé coast: compare how each learns French and how each school network has shrunk or held.
- Innu, Eeyou and Atikamekw: check which language each nation's schools teach first, and in which grades.
- Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: compare language-program choices in three Kanien'kehá:ka communities with different borders.
- Inuit of the Hudson and Ungava coasts: compare Inuktitut schooling village by village.
- Haitian, Italian, Greek, Portuguese and Maghrebi Montrealers: check which language each generation uses at home, from each community's own sources.
- Deaf Quebecers: separate LSQ and ASL users first.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for census language data, corpora and school reform; mixed for neighborhood life; thinner and community-held for Indigenous languages. Quebec's exact post-war lands are still to be settled, so this sign's sheets take the province as it stands; Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario serve only as comparison.
- **Languages needed:** French for most official work; English for Anglophone and ASL sources; LSQ through interpreters; each First Nation's language for its own teaching materials; Inuktitut syllabics.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the language laws (Bill 101, Bill 96) and residential schools are contested; read each account as an account. Some stories are told only in season.
- **Held for review:** the speech, schooling and commuting sources weighed for this overview raised no case under the standing rules; nothing was set aside.
