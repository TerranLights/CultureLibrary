# Quebec: Communication (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual* and regional speech), Quebec English, Langue des signes québécoise, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, Eeyou (East Cree), Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Maghrebi Arabic, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the exchange of meaning between people, by speech, writing, gesture, silence and media; and what good communication is held to be and what it is for.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and Montreal francophones: check *tu* and *vous* choice in shops and workplaces against each other, from recorded data rather than impressions.
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Gaspé speakers: compare how directness and teasing appear in local radio and dialect corpora.
- West Island and Townships Anglophones: check how each handles bilingual service encounters, from survey data.
- Innu and Atikamekw: compare what each community's radio carries, and in what share of Indigenous-language airtime.
- Nunavik Inuit set beside the Eeyou: compare two northern broadcasting histories and how each reaches far-off villages.
- The three Kanien'kehá:ka communities (Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke, Akwesasne): check what each one's radio and public notices carry, and in which language.
- Haitian, Maghrebi, Vietnamese and Latin American Montrealers: check where children act as interpreters, family by family, in each community's studies.
- Deaf LSQ users and hearing children of Deaf parents: compare who interprets at home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for broadcasting archives, sociolinguistics and digital-use surveys; mixed for everyday gesture and silence; Indigenous broadcasting archives are partly community-held.
- **Languages needed:** French for nearly all conversation research; Inuktitut and East Cree for northern radio; LSQ for Deaf sources; Haitian Creole and Darija for families' own accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recorded private conversations raise consent questions; free-speech limits and the language laws belong to Libra and Capricorn; some Indigenous broadcast archives are restricted by their communities.
- **Held for review:** interpreting and broadcasting pointers were weighed against the standing rules; none needed holding back.
