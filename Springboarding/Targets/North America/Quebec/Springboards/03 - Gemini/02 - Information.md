## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Readers of the Saguenay, Mauricie and Estrie regional dailies: check how each region's news supply changed after the cooperative takeover.
- Côte-Nord and Abitibi-Témiscamingue towns: compare broadband, radio and local-paper access, using federal and provincial connectivity maps.
- Anglophones of the Townships and the Gaspé: check where they get provincial news in English and whether it covers their region.
- Eeyou, Inuit and Kanien'kehá:ka readers: compare what their own press reports against the province-wide press on the same events.
- First Nations under the Quebec–Labrador commission's surveys: check how each community decides what data leaves it.
- Montreal's Haitian, Vietnamese and Lebanese households: compare reliance on home-country media and group chats, each in its own language.
- Older and younger readers across regions: separate paper, radio, television and phone use before comparing trust.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for newspapers (much of it digitized), media-use studies and the press crisis; mixed for community-language media; Indigenous data are governed by OCAP and released on each nation's terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for nearly all press and survey material; English for Anglophone and northern papers; Inuktitut and East Cree for parts of the northern press; Yiddish and Italian for community archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** privacy and access-to-information law belong to Libra; health and residential-school records are restricted; data about First Nations requires their consent.
- **Held for review:** press, library and data-governance pointers were sifted for this concept; none fell under a standing rule.
