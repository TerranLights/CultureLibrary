## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Mauricie and Bas-Saint-Laurent farm families of the 1900s: check memoirs of *écoles de rang* against school inspectors' reports.
- Anglophone South Shore and West Island families: compare immersion enrollment and outcomes across decades.
- Innu, Eeyou and Atikamekw children: check how each nation sets time on the land into its school year.
- Wendat of Wendake and W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak: compare revitalization routes for languages with few or no first-language speakers.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare youth in the villages with those who study in Montreal.
- Hasidic families in Outremont and Boisbriand: check, from community and press sources alike, how home, religious and provincial schooling are combined; one contested question among several.
- Haitian and Vietnamese families in Montreal: check who helps with homework and in which language, in each community's own studies.
- Newcomer children in *classes d'accueil* in Montreal, Quebec City and Sherbrooke: compare the move into regular classes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for textbooks, early-childhood surveys and immersion research; mixed for apprenticeship and on-the-job learning; Indigenous land-based learning is documented on each nation's terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for nearly all; old notarial French for apprenticeship contracts; Innu-aimun, East Cree, Inuktitut and Wendat materials for land and language programs; Yiddish for traditional school records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential and day schools are contested; follow survivors' own accounts first; Hasidic schooling is a live dispute; school law and education as an institution belong elsewhere (Libra, Sagittarius).
- **Held for review:** early-learning, apprenticeship and language-revival pointers were weighed for this concept; no item met a standing rule's threshold.
