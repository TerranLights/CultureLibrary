# Quebec: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Mi'gmaq, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the wish to know, to find out and to explore; and how far it is encouraged, channeled, limited or punished.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City families: check museum-going and science-club joining against those of the Côte-Nord and Abitibi, where institutions are far apart.
- Gaspé and Estrie communities hosting Miguasha and Mont-Mégantic: compare how locals and visitors use them.
- Innu and Naskapi of the Côte-Nord and Kawawachikamach: check how each community now answers the early ethnographers, in its own publications.
- Inuit of Inukjuak and other Hudson-coast villages: compare views of outside filmmakers and researchers across generations.
- Mi'gmaq of Listuguj and Gesgapegiag: check what each asks of researchers studying fisheries and land.
- Anglophone Montrealers: compare natural-history clubs and museums with francophone ones before 1960.
- Haitian, Vietnamese and Jewish Montreal families: check, from each community's parenting sources, how children's questions are answered and which questions are off limits.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for natural-history and science-outreach history and cultural-practices surveys; mixed for everyday nosiness, which leaves few records; Indigenous views of research are documented mainly in their own protocols and testimony.
- **Languages needed:** French for the naturalist and museum record; English for McGill and Anglophone sources; Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for community responses to research; Haitian Creole and Vietnamese for family accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** early ethnographies and films carry outsider labels; restricted knowledge and sacred sites are closed to outside curiosity; research on First Nations needs their consent.
- **Held for review:** museum, ethnography and research-ethics pointers were checked for this concept; nothing among them had to be held.
