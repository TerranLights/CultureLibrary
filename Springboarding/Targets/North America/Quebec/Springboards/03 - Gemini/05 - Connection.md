*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophone families of the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Beauce: check how family associations and godparent choices compare across the two regions.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue and Côte-Nord mining towns: compare how newcomers build ties where few families go back more than two generations.
- Scottish, Irish and English Montrealers: check each society's records separately, not as one Anglophone bloc.
- Eeyou and Anishinaabeg communities: compare how travel and gatherings tie together villages far apart.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check adoption and naming ties across villages, from Inuit sources first.
- Jewish, Greek, Portuguese and Italian Montrealers: compare hometown and parish networks, each in its own records.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how family networks spanning Montreal, Haiti and the U.S. are described in community studies.
- Isolated elders in Montreal and in small rural parishes: compare who reaches them, from charity and public-health data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations, genealogy and patronage history; mixed for loneliness measures by region; Indigenous kin ties are documented on each nation's own terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for patronage and family-association records; English for Anglophone societies; Yiddish, Greek and Portuguese for community archives; Inuktitut and East Cree for northern kin sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** adoption and kin records are private; the Duplessis era and the 1960s networks are contested history; patronage law and inquiries belong to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** association, kin and isolation pointers were set against the standing rules; there was nothing to hold.
