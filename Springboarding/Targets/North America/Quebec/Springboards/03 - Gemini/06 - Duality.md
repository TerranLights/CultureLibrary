# Quebec: Duality (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English (and the mixed speech called *franglais*), Wendat, Innu-aimun, Mi'gmaq, Acadian French of the Magdalen Islands, Haitian Creole, Italian, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** how two-ness is lived: holding two identities, languages, loyalties or views at once; dividing the world into pairs; and how people who show two faces are treated.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal francophones and Anglophones of the West Island: check how each describes bilingual daily life, from interview studies on both sides.
- Pointe-Saint-Charles and Verdun working-class neighborhoods: compare the mixed French-English record with that of the middle-class suburbs.
- Eastern Townships Anglophones: check how long-settled English families and newer francophone neighbors account for living side by side.
- Wendat of Wendake: separate the community's own creation-story telling from the missionary record.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam: compare writers' accounts of reserve and city with those of elders.
- Madelinots, Natashquan and Île-aux-Grues residents: compare Mi-Carême customs across three islands and coasts.
- Italian, Haitian and Maghrebi Montrealers: check, community by community, how language choice is split across generations.
- Two-Spirit people in Montreal and in First Nations communities: what the term means is theirs to say, and only through their own organizations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for literature, commissions and bilingualism debates; mixed for everyday two-ness at home; Wendat and Mi'gmaq concepts are documented on each nation's terms.
- **Languages needed:** French and English in equal measure; Wendat and Mi'gmaq for their own concepts; Haitian Creole for *marasa* and family sources; Italian and Arabic for community studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1980 and 1995 referendums, the language laws and the "two founding peoples" thesis are contested ground; read each as an account. Vodou and Two-Spirit topics are for their communities to frame.
- **Held for review:** the literary and commission pointers on two-ness were weighed under the standing rules; none were set aside.
