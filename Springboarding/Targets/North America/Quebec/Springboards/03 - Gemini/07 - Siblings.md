# Quebec: Siblings (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, East Cree (Eeyou), Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Italian, Arabic, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** brothers and sisters, and how the sibling bond is understood: who counts as a sibling, what siblings owe one another, and how they rank, compete and care.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Beauce farm families: check how land passed to one son and what the others received, from notarial and oral sources.
- Montreal working-class families of the Plateau and Hochelaga: compare shared-room, chores and older-sister care in memoirs and oral histories.
- Anglophone Protestant families of the Townships: compare family size and inheritance with nearby francophone parishes.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check sibling terms and namesake ties from Inuit speakers, not from outside kinship charts.
- Kanien'kehá:ka and Eeyou families: ask each community how clan or kin terms extend "brother" and "sister".
- Vietnamese and Haitian Montrealers: compare older siblings' duties as interpreters and earners, each from its own studies.
- Italian families of Saint-Léonard: check how brothers shared migration costs and businesses.
- Children in youth protection: compare placement data on keeping siblings together, across regions.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fertility history and family television; mixed for everyday sibling life; Indigenous kinship is documented on each nation's own terms; youth-protection files are closed.
- **Languages needed:** French for demography and media; Inuktitut for kin terms and naming; Vietnamese, Italian and Haitian Creole for community family studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Duplessis orphans and youth-protection cases are painful and contested; residential schools separated siblings and need survivors' accounts first; inheritance law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** demography, twin-study and media pointers on brothers and sisters were sorted under the standing rules; none were held back.

## 5. First queries
1. Read Henripin's monograph for family size by province and origin, 1941 to 1961.
