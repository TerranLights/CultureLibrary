# Quebec: Mediation (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Atikamekw, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Spanish (Latin American workers), Haitian Creole, Arabic, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a third party helping two or more sides settle a conflict or reach an agreement without imposing a decision; and the go-between who stands between parties (brokers, interpreters, intercessors).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Rural Bas-Saint-Laurent and Mauricie parishes: check whether priests or notaries settled more quarrels, from church and notarial records.
- Montreal and Quebec City unions: compare how labor disputes reached conciliators in the two cities.
- Each of the three Kanien'kehá:ka communities: compare whom it sends, or accepts, as a go-between with Quebec and Ottawa.
- Atikamekw of Manawan and Wemotaci, and Inuit of Nunavik: compare their own conflict-settling bodies with the provincial services nearby.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check how the Cree-Quebec relationship assigns go-between roles in practice, from Eeyou sources.
- Haitian, Maghrebi and Vietnamese families in Montreal: check who steps into family and school quarrels, from community workers' studies.
- Hasidic and Orthodox Jews in Outremont: check how neighborhood disputes are brokered, from both community and borough sources.
- Guatemalan and Mexican farmworkers in Montérégie and Lanaudière: compare recruiters' and advocates' accounts.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for family mediation, ombudsman reports and the crises of 1970 and 1990; mixed for everyday go-betweens; Indigenous conflict-settling practices are recorded on each nation's own terms.
- **Languages needed:** French for nearly all public records; Kanien'kéha for Peacemaker teachings; Atikamekw and Inuktitut for community justice reports; Spanish for farmworkers' accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the October Crisis and the 1990 Kanehsatà:ke resistance are contested ground; youth-protection cases are confidential; Longhouse teachings may be restricted; mediation law belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** go-between, ombudsman and community-justice pointers were sifted with the standing rules in mind; none was set aside.
