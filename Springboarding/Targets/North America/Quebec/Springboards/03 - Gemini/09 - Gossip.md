# Quebec: Gossip (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Italian, Portuguese (Azorean), Vietnamese, Arabic, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** talk about absent people, especially their private lives and conduct; and rumor, unconfirmed reports passed from person to person.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Plateau-Mont-Royal and Hochelaga working-class streets: check balcony and stairway talk in memoirs against the suburbs after 1960.
- Drummondville and other Centre-du-Québec mill towns: compare how French and English townspeople's talk is recorded in community studies.
- Gaspé Anglophone villages and Magdalen Islands communities: check what local histories and fiction say of small-island and small-coast talk.
- Innu, Atikamekw and Eeyou communities: check how each community's own organizations discuss gossip and lateral harm, without generalizing across them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare older channels (radio, visiting) with Facebook groups, from Inuit sources.
- Haitian, Italian, Portuguese and Vietnamese Montrealers: check how reputation travels in each community's churches, media and clubs.
- Montreal's Orthodox and Hasidic Jewish communities: check how teaching on *loshn-hore* is set out in each community's own educational material.
- Cégep and university students: compare anonymous-page cultures across Montreal, Sherbrooke and Rimouski.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fiction, celebrity press and folklore; mixed for everyday gossip, which leaves few records; thin and sensitive for Indigenous and small-community talk.
- **Languages needed:** French, including colloquial Quebec French, for nearly all; English for Townships and Gaspé sources; Haitian Creole for call-in radio; Yiddish and Hebrew for *loshn-hore* teaching.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 2020 denunciations named real people and remain disputed; small-community gossip can identify individuals; defamation law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** fiction, press and online-call-out pointers on gossip were reviewed under the standing rules; none needed holding.
