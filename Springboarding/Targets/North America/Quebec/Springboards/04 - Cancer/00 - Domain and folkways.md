# Quebec: The domain: home and family (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and the speech of the Saguenay, the Beauce, the Gaspé and the Magdalen Islands), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Eeyou (East Cree), Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** what is home, and who is family: household forms, roots, fathering and child-rearing, family memory, hospitality, and the ways houses are built and families ordered.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean families: test whether dense regional genealogies change how far kin is counted, against Montreal.
- Montreal plex tenants against the bungalow suburbs of Laval and the South Shore: compare who shares a roof.
- Eastern Townships Anglophone farm families: check whether Loyalist house types carry their own idea of the homestead.
- Madelinots and Gaspé Acadians: check how family names cluster by village and how houses are placed on the land.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare households in public housing with the camp households *Sanaaq* describes.
- Eeyou and Innu families: compare the bush camp and the village house as places of home, from each nation's sources.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check how clan belonging cuts across the single-family house.
- Hasidic households of Outremont and Boisbriand: check household size and the Sabbath boundary as community sources describe them.
- Haitian and Vietnamese Montrealers: compare how sponsored parents and grandparents are housed, from community studies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for genealogy, house history and fathering programs; mixed for immigrant households; Indigenous household life is best read in each nation's own writing. Quebec's post-war lands are not yet settled; until they are, this sign treats the province as it now stands as the target, and Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario are comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** French for notarial and heritage sources; English for Townships and Montreal housing history; Inuktitut syllabics for *Sanaaq*; Cree and Kanien'kéha for dwelling words; Yiddish for Hasidic household sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential schools broke up households in Eeyou Istchee and on the Côte-Nord; survivors' accounts first. Hasidic family life is private. Inheritance goes to Scorpio, household law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the genealogical, architectural and parental-leave pointers weighed for this domain gave no reason to set anything aside.
