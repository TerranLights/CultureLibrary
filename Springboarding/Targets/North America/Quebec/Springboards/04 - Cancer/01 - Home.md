# Quebec: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including Saguenay and Gaspé speech), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, East Cree (Eeyou), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Portuguese, Italian, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal renters in plexes and Quebec City's old Haute-Ville: check how tenancy and the yearly lease shape the feeling of a home.
- Company-town families of the Saguenay and the Mauricie: compare houses given by employers with self-built rural houses.
- Abitibi and Témiscamingue settlers' descendants: check memoirs of first houses on cleared lots.
- Madelinots and Gaspé coast families: check how houses are placed against wind and sea, from local heritage studies.
- Innu of Pessamit and Uashat mak Mani-utenam: compare the reserve house with the inland camp, in Innu writing.
- Anishinaabeg of Kitigan Zibi and Abitibiwinni (Pikogan): check what housing departments and elders say about home on and off reserve.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare the public-housing unit with family and camp memory.
- Portuguese and Italian homeowners: compare buying patterns, from community studies.
- Townships Anglophones near the Vermont line: check how cross-border ties enter household memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for architecture, housing policy and disaster coverage; mixed for immigrant homeownership; Indigenous ideas of home as land are best taken from the nations' own writers and councils.
- **Languages needed:** French for urban history and disaster records; English for Townships and Gallant; Innu-aimun and Anishinaabemowin for territory terms; Portuguese for community housing sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** slum clearance and expropriation are remembered differently by those removed; flood victims' claims may be in dispute. The rental-board side of housing is law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** housing-history, flood and house-museum pointers were looked over for home, and none fell to be set aside.
