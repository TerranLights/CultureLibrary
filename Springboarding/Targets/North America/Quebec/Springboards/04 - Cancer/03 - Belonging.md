*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lac-Saint-Jean, Beauce and Magdalen Islands people: check how regional names and local-versus-outsider speech mark belonging, in each region's own media.
- Montreal francophones and newcomers in Saint-Michel or Côte-des-Neiges: compare how long "becoming a Quebecer" is said to take.
- Anglophones of the West Island, the Gaspé coast and the Lower North Shore: separate their accounts, which differ in size and isolation.
- Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check who speaks in each community's debate over who is a member, and in which forum.
- Inuit in Montreal and Nunavimmiut at home: compare ties kept across the distance.
- Innu and Anishinaabeg living in towns like Sept-Îles and Val-d'Or: check what friendship centers report about belonging off reserve.
- Black Montrealers of long standing and Haitian arrivals: separate the histories before comparing.
- Jewish Montrealers (Ashkenazi, Moroccan Sephardic, Hasidic): check how each relates to francophone and Anglophone institutions.
- Chilean, Salvadoran and Lebanese Montrealers: compare exile and choice as starting points.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nationalist writing and minority histories; mixed for regional insider-outsider dynamics; thin and scattered for Latin American exiles.
- **Languages needed:** French for national history and the Hérouxville case; English for Anglophone and Black Montreal history; Kanien'kéha for membership terms; Inuktitut for urban Inuit sources; Spanish for exile memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the referendums, the language laws and the secularism law shape belonging talk and are contested accounts, to be read as social events; membership in First Nations is a nation's own business and highly charged; Groulx's writing raises questions of exclusion.
- **Held for review:** the nationalist essays, minority histories and membership debates gathered here were weighed under the standing rules, and none was withheld.
