# Quebec: Nurture (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, Innu-aimun (Ilnu of Mashteuiatsh), Anishinaabemowin and Atikamekw, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Italian. **Concept in one line:** raising another toward growth, above all a child: feeding, warming, teaching and shaping them toward who they should become.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophone families of the Mauricie and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: check how grandparents' role in raising children shows up in regional oral histories.
- Montreal working-class mothers of the early 1900s: compare milk-depot records with the household-science school model.
- Innu of Mashteuiatsh and Pessamit: check what novels and councils say about grandmothers raising children on the land.
- Atikamekw and Anishinaabe families: check cradleboard use and its revival, from community makers.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare daycare practice in Inuktitut with home practice, village by village.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check how immersion families describe the choice.
- Italian, Vietnamese and Portuguese families: compare what each wanted children to keep through heritage-language classes.
- Anglophone families of the West Island and the Townships: check youth associations and church programs as nurturers.
- Haitian families of Montréal-Nord and Saint-Michel: check lullabies and household teaching, from community memory.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for public-health guidance and infant-feeding history; mixed for immigrant child-rearing; Indigenous nurture is taught by each nation, often orally.
- **Languages needed:** French for public-health and school sources; English for Anglophone youth work; Innu-aimun, Atikamekw and Anishinaabemowin for cradle and kin words; Inuktitut for daycare materials; Vietnamese and Italian for family interviews.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential schools cut the passing of nurture in Eeyou Istchee, the Côte-Nord and Abitibi; survivors' accounts first. Debates on spanking touch on law (Libra) and discipline (Capricorn).
- **Held for review:** the lullaby collections, feeding charities and heritage-language programs were weighed for nurture under the standing rules; none needed holding.
