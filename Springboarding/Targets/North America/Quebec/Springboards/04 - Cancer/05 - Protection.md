## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale: check how protective devotions grew around sieges and epidemics.
- Rural brigades of the Beauce and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: compare volunteer protection with city services.
- Anglophone Montreal charities and francophone Catholic ones: separate who defined "the vulnerable" in each.
- Wendat of Wendake: check how the community reads the 1760 safeguard today.
- Anishinaabeg of Barriere Lake and Kitcisakik: compare how each frames guarding land and children.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check how community protection is organized in each.
- Innu, Atikamekw and Inuit families: compare testimony to the national inquiry, nation by nation.
- Haitian and other Black Montreal families: check youth-protection experiences against the commission's report.
- Italian Montrealers: check the parish's role as protector across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for youth-protection reports and family-violence surveys; mixed for folk protective customs; Indigenous self-protection and land defense are recorded mostly in contested and community sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for youth-protection and survey sources; English for 19th-century charities; Kanien'kéha and Anishinaabemowin for community terms; Inuktitut for testimony; Italian for parish history.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** child abuse, the 1989 killings and the national inquiry's testimony are traumatic; the 1990 Kanehsatà:ke resistance and the warrior societies are contested accounts. Policing and treaty law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** no source on guarding children, women, elders or land had to be withheld under the standing rules.
