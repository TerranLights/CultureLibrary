## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's Centre-Sud and the Quebec City lower town: compare rooming houses and shelters as the rungs above the street.
- Val-d'Or, Chibougamau and La Tuque: check how far shelter users come from reserves and northern villages, and why.
- Inuit in Montreal and Nunavimmiut at home: compare crowding in the north with homelessness in the south.
- Anishinaabeg of Kitcisakik and Lac-Simon: check housing histories from the communities' own sources.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Pessamit: check how housing shortages on reserve drive moves to towns.
- Haitian and other asylum seekers arriving in 2017 and after: compare reception by community groups and the state.
- Jewish refugees of the 1920s and 1940s and later Maghrebi and Syrian arrivals: compare who did the sheltering.
- Youth leaving foster care in Montreal, Laval and the Laurentides: check where they land.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Montreal shelters and the provincial counts; thinner for regional towns; Indigenous housing data are held by councils and housing bureaus.
- **Languages needed:** French for counts and shelter reports; English for Anglophone missions and refugee agencies; Inuktitut and Anishinaabemowin for community housing sources; Haitian Creole and Arabic for asylum seekers' accounts; Yiddish for older refugee files.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** deaths on the street and the asylum crossings are charged events; shelter residents' identities are protected. Refugee and housing law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the shelter, asylum and housing-bureau pointers were sifted with the standing rules in view; no item called for being withheld.
