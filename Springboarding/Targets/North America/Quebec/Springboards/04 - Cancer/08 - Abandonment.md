# Quebec: Abandonment (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Côte-Nord speech), Quebec English, Atikamekw, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** leaving someone or something one was bound to care for or stay with; and the fear, and the experience, of being left.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Unwed mothers and their children in Montreal and Quebec City: compare the two cities' homes, and adoptees' own accounts.
- Lac-Saint-Jean and Côte-Nord company towns: check how people of Val-Jalbert and Gagnon tell the closing of a town.
- Gaspé and Bas-Saint-Laurent parishes: compare villages slated for closure with those that held on.
- Eastern Townships farm families who took Home Children: check letters and inspection reports from both sides.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check what families say about relatives sent south and never returned.
- Atikamekw of Manawan and Wemotaci and Innu of the Côte-Nord: check families' testimony on missing children, nation by nation.
- Men in the 1990s debate on absent fathers: separate Corneau's readers, fathers' groups and their critics.
- Haitian families reuniting in Montreal: check how children left with kin describe the wait.
- Anglophones of the Gaspé coast and the Lower North Shore: check whether emptied communities speak of being left behind.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church maternity homes and company towns; mixed for adoptees' searches (files closed); Indigenous families' accounts are documented in testimony and community campaigns.
- **Languages needed:** French for foundling and planning records; English for Home Children files; Atikamekw, Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for families' testimony; Haitian Creole for reunification accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** missing Indigenous children, tuberculosis evacuations and residential schools are wounds still open; survivors' and families' words first. Adoption files are closed by law (Libra).
- **Held for review:** the foundling-home, ghost-town and missing-children pointers were weighed under the standing rules, and nothing in them was set aside.
