# Quebec: Motherhood (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, W8banaki (Abenaki), Haitian Creole, Arabic (Lebanese), Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** being a mother, and the role, standing and ideals attached to it: bearing, raising and standing for a child; and the mother as a figure of the culture.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Francophone mothers of the Beauce and the Saguenay: compare the midwife and the parish doctor in local birth memories.
- Montreal mothers of the 1910s-1960s: separate working-class from middle-class experience in the medicalization records.
- Inuit of the Hudson and Ungava coasts: check how births and the *amauti* are taught where midwives practice and where they do not.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check what clan mothers do, from each community's own teaching.
- Innu, Atikamekw and W8banaki women: compare mothers' roles as their own filmmakers and writers present them.
- Lebanese, Haitian and Maghrebi mothers: compare mothering across a migration, from each community's fiction and studies.
- Women of the 1970s feminist movement and the Catholic women's federations: separate their models of the mother.
- Anglophone mothers in the West Island and the Townships: check birth and maternity services in English.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the history of birth and feminist writing; mixed for immigrant mothers; Indigenous motherhood is taught by each nation and partly reserved.
- **Languages needed:** French for history, theatre and fiction; English for Anglophone services; Inuktitut for midwifery materials; Kanien'kéha and Innu-aimun for kin and role words; Arabic and Haitian Creole for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clan mothers' roles and birth teachings may be restricted; residential schools and evacuations for birth separated mothers and babies; the 1980 referendum is a contested account. Abortion goes to Aries; birth law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the midwifery histories, feminist texts and shrine records were reviewed for this concept; none was set aside under the standing rules.
