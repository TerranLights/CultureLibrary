# Quebec: The domain: creativity and self-expression (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and the speech of the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, the Beauce, the Gaspé and the Magdalen Islands), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Eeyou (East Cree), Kanien'kéha, Atikamekw, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Mi'gmaq, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Italian, Greek. **Concept in one line:** how people shine, play and take pleasure: festivity, romance, children as joy, fame, display, games of chance and the ways of sport.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale against Montreal: check how each city's festivals present it.
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Abitibi-Témiscamingue: compare the junior-hockey town and its stars with the big-city sports market.
- Gaspé Peninsula and the Magdalen Islands: check song festivals and kitchen parties in fishing villages, and how Acadian Madelinots mark their own occasions.
- Anglophone Montrealers and Eastern Townships Anglophones: compare club sport and the fall fair with francophone parish festivity.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check lacrosse and the community's own account of it.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Atikamekw of the Haute-Mauricie: compare feasts and drum dances as each nation describes them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check games, songs and contests as Nunavimmiut present them, at home and at circumpolar events.
- Haitian, Italian and Greek Montrealers: compare parades, saints' feasts and dance halls as community events.
- Hasidic communities of Outremont and Boisbriand: check community sources on celebration and public display.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, arts funding, hockey and television; mixed for gambling and courtship; thin for some smaller Indigenous nations' games outside their own publications. Quebec's post-war lands are still to be settled; these sheets write for the province as it now stands, with Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** Quebec French including colloquial and stage registers; English for club sport and Anglophone comedy; Inuktitut and Kanien'kéha for games and their names; Haitian Creole for carnival sources; Yiddish for celebration in Hasidic writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous songs and dances belong to families or ceremonies and are shared only with consent. Sex goes to Scorpio, food pleasure to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** the festival, sport and survey sources weighed for the domain gave nothing that the standing rules set aside.
