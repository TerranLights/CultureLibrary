# Quebec: Creativity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with rural Côte-du-Sud and Charlevoix craft vocabulary), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, W8banaki, Wendat, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** making something new that others find valuable or fitting, in the arts, in crafts, in solving problems and in everyday life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Côte-du-Sud and Charlevoix carvers and weavers: check how village crafts became heritage and tourist goods.
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean: compare self-taught painters and their reception with Montreal's art schools.
- Mile End and Plateau Montreal: check how the indie music and game scenes describe originality.
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale: compare its theater makers with Montreal's on who is called an innovator.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Wendat of Wendake and W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak: check, from community sources, how each tells the history of making for sale.
- Inuit of Nunavik (Puvirnituq, Inukjuak, Salluit): compare printmaking and carving as artists and cooperatives describe them.
- Anglophone Montrealers and Townships writers: check how English-language circles defined new work apart from the French scene.
- Yiddish-speaking and later Jewish Montreal: compare first-generation writers with their children's English-language work.
- Haitian Montrealers: check painters', musicians' and writers' own accounts of making in exile.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for fine arts, theater and the creative industries; mixed for rural crafts; thin for some First Nations' own terms for making, which sit with community language programs.
- **Languages needed:** French for craft and arts-council sources; English for Anglophone little magazines; Inuktitut for artists' statements; Yiddish for poets' own writing; Kanien'kéha for beadwork terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** designs owned by families or nations, and objects held by museums, carry consent questions; the souvenir-art literature speaks of colonial markets. Who owns a work goes to Taurus, its legal protection to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for creativity, the craft, art-world and creative-industry pointers were weighed and none was held back.
