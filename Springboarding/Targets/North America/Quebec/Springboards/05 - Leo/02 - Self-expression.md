## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's Village against small towns in the Beauce and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: check how coming out is told in each.
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale: compare talk radio's place in public venting with Montreal's media.
- Outaouais: check how a border region with Ottawa discussed religious dress at school.
- Atikamekw of Wemotaci and Manawan and Innu of the Côte-Nord: compare youth film and music as each community frames them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how artists explain choosing a language for a song.
- Haitian and Maghrebi Montrealers: compare what second-generation youth say about family limits on display.
- Anglophone Montrealers: check how English-language songwriters were received by francophone critics.
- Wendat of Wendake: check how the community reads the missionary record of its ancestors' festivals.
- Hasidic Montrealers: check what community writing says about dress, modesty and visibility.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the arts, Pride and the dress debates; mixed for everyday emotional display; thin for most Indigenous languages' own words for speaking out.
- **Languages needed:** joual and spoken French for poetry and talk shows; Inuktitut and Innu-aimun for songs; Atikamekw for Wapikoni films; Haitian Creole and Maghrebi Arabic for second-generation accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** religious dress and the secularism law are contested; present each side as an account. Coming-out stories are personal; use published ones.
- **Held for review:** while weighing sources on visible expression, one pointer connected to South Asian-heritage communities in Quebec's lands was set aside under the standing rule.
