# Quebec: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with Saguenay and Beauce speech and the Acadian French of the Magdalen Islands), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Italian. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean: check whether the region's "kingdom" self-image shapes pride in its own history.
- The Beauce and Chaudière-Appalaches: compare pride in local business-founding with the Montreal business press's account.
- Capitale-Nationale: check pride as capital city and as former hockey town.
- Magdalen Islands and the Gaspé: compare Acadian pride with pride in being Québécois, in islanders' own words.
- Anglophone Montrealers, Townships and Gaspé Anglophones: check which emblems and parades each group uses.
- Innu of Pessamit and Uashat mak Mani-utenam: check how role models and festivals carry community pride.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare pride in language and in hunting skill as community sources describe them.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Akwesasne: check how a community split by borders marks pride in its own nationhood.
- Haitian and Italian Montrealers: compare pride in origin with pride in Quebec, generation by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for national symbols, nationalism and surveys; mixed for regional pride; thin for Indigenous words for pride, which need community language staff.
- **Languages needed:** French, including nineteenth-century church writing; English for Irish and Anglophone parade records; Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for community framings; Haitian Creole and Italian for diaspora sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** national pride touches the 1980 and 1995 referendums; present each side as an account. Pride in New France's heroes is disputed by First Nations writers. Honor goes to concept 11.
- **Held for review:** surveys, flags and parades were weighed for pride; none of them fell under a standing rule.
