# Quebec: Recognition (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** others' acknowledgment of a person's or group's worth, achievements or standing, from a word of credit to a formal honor or title.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and the regions (Abitibi-Témiscamingue, Côte-Nord, Gaspé): check whether medals and prizes reach the regions in proportion.
- Capitale-Nationale: compare honors given by the state's seat with those given by Montreal institutions.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check how communities responded to renaming and acknowledgments near them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare recognition through restored names and photo identification with state honors.
- Innu and Eeyou communities: check which forms of thanks and naming each community uses for its own people, from its own sources.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how community honors differ from mainstream awards in whom they name.
- Vietnamese and other refugee communities: compare how arrivals honored the families and parishes that sponsored them.
- Anglophone Quebecers: check whether separate award systems run beside the francophone ones.
- Women in rural Quebec: compare recognition of farm and household work with paid work, from farm-women's groups.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for honors lists, prizes and toponymy; mixed for workplace recognition; thin for Indigenous forms of naming and thanks outside community sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for prizes and occupational research; eighteenth-century French for La Potherie; English for Anglophone awards; Kanien'kéha and Inuktitut for names and naming.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** renaming and acknowledgments are disputed; give each account as an account. Photographs of the dead may need community consent. State apologies are also treated under humiliation and dignity.
- **Held for review:** for recognition, the honors registers, workplace studies and renaming records gave no pointer to set aside.
