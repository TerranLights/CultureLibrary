# Quebec: Being seen (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Italian, Greek, Arabic (Lebanese and Maghrebi), Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** being noticed and perceived as one really is; the need to be witnessed, and the rules for who may be visible, when and to whom.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Anishinaabeg of Abitibi-Témiscamingue (Lac-Simon, Pikogan, Kitcisakik) and of the Outaouais (Kitigan Zibi): check their own accounts of being overlooked.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: compare self-made images with news images from 1990.
- Inuit in Montreal against Inuit in Nunavik: check how visibility differs between the city street and the home village.
- Côte-Nord and Magdalen Islands sealing families: check how they describe being filmed and judged by outsiders.
- Eastern Townships and Gaspé Anglophones: compare rural English speakers' visibility with Montreal Anglophones'.
- Haitian and other Black Montrealers: check accounts of being watched by police against being missing from screens.
- Italian, Greek, Lebanese and Maghrebi Montrealers: compare customs about displaying good fortune, from community sources.
- Seniors living alone in rural parishes and in Montreal: check who notices them, through local visiting programs.
- Small-town parishes of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Beauce: compare older church-step sociability with today's online visibility.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for media controversies and police data; mixed for representation counts; thin for Indigenous terms and for evil-eye beliefs, which sit in community and folklore sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for media, police and parish sources; English for Townships and urban Indigenous sources; Anishinaabemowin for community responses; Italian, Greek and Arabic for evil-eye lore.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** street checks, blackface and urban homelessness are contested; give each account as an account. Images of Inuit in public space need care. Privacy and surveillance as control go to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** the media, census and street-check pointers on being seen were checked; there was nothing to put aside.
