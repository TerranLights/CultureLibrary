# Quebec: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, Haitian Creole, Spanish (Latin American Montrealers). **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Gaspé and Bas-Saint-Laurent: check the dignity language of the 1970s parish-closing protests against today's regional development debates.
- Montreal's Hochelaga, Saint-Henri and Montréal-Nord: compare anti-poverty groups' own accounts of welfare and respect.
- Mauricie and Abitibi survivors of the La Tuque and Amos residential schools: check survivors' statements, community by community.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how dignity in health care far from home is described in Inuit sources.
- Atikamekw of Manawan and Wemotaci: compare community statements on respectful hospital treatment.
- Black Montrealers, Caribbean and Haitian: check student, worker and community accounts of respect, from 1969 onward.
- Latin American Montrealers: compare refugees' and workers' accounts of decent treatment on arrival.
- People with disabilities in the regions and in Montreal: check self-advocates' own words on choice and risk.
- Elderly francophone and Anglophone Quebecers: compare how dignity in old age is discussed in each language's press.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor, poverty and end-of-life records; mixed for slavery (scattered records); survivors' testimony is extensive but must be read with care.
- **Languages needed:** French, including New France records for slavery; English for Concordia and Black community archives; Kanien'kéha for teaching terms; Innu-aimun, Atikamekw and Inuktitut for survivors' own words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential schools, slavery, sex work and medical aid in dying are painful and contested; give survivors' and objectors' accounts as their own, and keep identifying details out. Any legal reading goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the slavery histories, poverty groups and end-of-life reports were weighed under the standing rules for this concept, and nothing was withheld.
