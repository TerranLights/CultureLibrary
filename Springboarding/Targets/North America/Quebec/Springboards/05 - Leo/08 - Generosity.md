## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Capitale-Nationale and Montreal: compare where telethons and volunteer programs began and how each city gives.
- Rural parishes of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Beauce: check older customs of the blessed bread and New Year's gifts against today's local fundraisers.
- Anglophone Montrealers and Townships Anglophones: compare founding bequests with today's community foundations.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee and Innu of the Côte-Nord: check feasts and sharing of game as each nation describes them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check what Inuit researchers say about food sharing and its obligations.
- Jewish Montrealers, Ashkenazi, Sephardic and Hasidic: compare their charitable bodies, which run separately.
- Muslim Montrealers of Maghrebi and Lebanese origin: check how giving is organized through mosques and associations.
- Haitian Montrealers: compare collective work and family support with formal charity.
- Wealthy and modest households across regions: check donor data beside informal giving, which the tax data miss.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for formal charities, telethons and tax data; mixed for informal giving; thin for Indigenous sharing outside community research.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Champlain; French for parish and charity records; English for Square Mile and Jewish federation archives; East Cree, Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for sharing terms; Arabic for mosque notices.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous feasts are shared as each nation chooses; charity can shame its receivers, so give their accounts. Reciprocity as an exchange rule goes to Libra; wealth to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** the giving data, charity archives and religious almsgiving pointers were reviewed; there was no item to set aside.
