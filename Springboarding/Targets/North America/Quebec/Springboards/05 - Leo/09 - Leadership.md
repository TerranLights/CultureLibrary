# Quebec: Leadership (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, East Cree, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, W8banaki, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** guiding a group so that others follow willingly, by example, persuasion, vision or standing; distinct from the formal right to command (authority, Capricorn).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Rural parishes of the Mauricie and Chaudière-Appalaches: check how the curé, the mayor and the cooperative manager shared leadership.
- Montreal and the Capitale-Nationale: compare municipal leadership styles in the press of each city.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check how hunting leaders and elected Grand Chiefs relate, from Eeyou sources.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare camp leadership, cooperative leaders and regional government, in Inuit accounts.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check how the Longhouse and elected councils each describe their leaders; keep each account separate.
- Wendat of Wendake and W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak: compare leadership in small nations close to French-speaking towns.
- Innu and Atikamekw communities: check what the words for leader cover in each language, with community language staff.
- Haitian, Italian and Greek Montrealers: compare community leaders in churches, associations and politics.
- Union and business leaders across regions (Saguenay aluminum towns, Abitibi mining towns): check who is followed at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political and union figures; mixed for business; thin for Indigenous governance terms outside nations' own publications.
- **Languages needed:** French for militia, church and political sources; English for Eeyou and Inuit biographies; Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, East Cree, Kanien'kéha and Inuktitut for leadership words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** elected and traditional leadership in Kanien'kehá:ka communities are in dispute; give each side's account. Sovereigntist and federalist leaders are judged differently by each camp. The right to command goes to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** leadership scholarship, biographies and political records were reviewed for this concept; none was set aside.
