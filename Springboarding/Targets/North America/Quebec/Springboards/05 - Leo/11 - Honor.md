# Quebec: Honor (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with Outaouais and old seigneurial vocabulary), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Wendat, Haitian Creole, Greek, Arabic (Lebanese), Italian. **Concept in one line:** worth held in others' eyes and one's own, won and kept by conduct (keeping one's word, courage, loyalty, sexual propriety) and lost by insult or failure; it can be defended.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Old seigneurial parishes of the Côte-de-Beaupré and the Richelieu valley: check precedence disputes recorded in parish histories.
- Outaouais: compare the Montferrand legend with how lumber-camp honor is told elsewhere (Mauricie, Saguenay).
- Montreal Anglophone regiments and francophone regiments: check how each keeps its battle honors.
- Kanien'kehá:ka, Wendat and W8banaki: compare how each nation tells the alliance promises of the 1700s.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check what community sources say about a hunter's or a leader's good name.
- Haitian Montrealers: check the greeting of honor and respect in family use across generations.
- Greek, Italian and Lebanese Montrealers: compare family honor and respect as each community's writers describe them, without assuming one pattern.
- Junior-hockey towns (Rimouski, Val-d'Or, Victoriaville): check local views on fighting and respect.
- Supporters and opponents of conscription in 1917-1918 and 1942-1944: compare how each side spoke of honor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military, fraternal and seigneurial records; mixed for street and sports codes; thin for Indigenous words for honor outside community sources.
- **Languages needed:** French for seigneurial and legend sources; English for regiments and Masonic archives; Kanien'kéha and Wendat for alliance terms; Haitian Creole, Greek, Arabic and Italian for family honor in community writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** family honor and violence must be researched in the words of those who lived it, never assumed for any group; conscription and alliance memory are contested. Revenge goes to Scorpio, duty to Capricorn.
