# Quebec: Joy (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, East Cree, Atikamekw, Haitian Creole, Yiddish and Hebrew, Vietnamese, Portuguese. **Concept in one line:** the emotion of gladness and delight, and the larger sense of being glad to be alive; how a culture names, feels, shows, seeks and shares it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue and the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean: check what draws people out in summer and winter, from local festival and press sources.
- Rural parishes of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and Lanaudière: compare older wake and holiday customs with today's celebrations.
- Montreal and Quebec City: check how each city's winter festivals are described by residents rather than tourism offices.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check community celebrations of children's milestones, in Eeyou publications.
- Atikamekw of Manawan and Wemotaci and Wendat of Wendake: compare powwows as each community frames them.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check holiday games and feasts in village sources.
- Haitian, African and Vietnamese Montrealers: compare public festivals with family celebrations such as Tết.
- Hasidic and other Jewish Montrealers: check which festivals call for public rejoicing and how neighbors see them.
- Portuguese and Italian Montrealers: compare saints' feasts and family celebrations across generations.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for festivals, happiness indexes and surveys; mixed for religious joy; thin for Indigenous words for joy outside community language programs.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the Jesuits' journal; French for indexes and press; Inuktitut and East Cree for community celebrations; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish festival sources; Vietnamese for New Year celebrations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Indigenous celebrations include ceremony shared only as communities choose; "joie de vivre" is a stereotype to test, never a finding. Escape goes to Pisces, grief to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** happiness data, festival records and religious-celebration pointers were weighed for joy; none was set aside.
