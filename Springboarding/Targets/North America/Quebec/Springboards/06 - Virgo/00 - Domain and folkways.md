# Quebec: The domain: work and service (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and the speech of the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, the Beauce and the Gaspé), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Eeyou (East Cree), Atikamekw, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Cantonese. **Concept in one line:** what good work and a well-kept life are: work, service, the daily round, health, the kept house and craft.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's old mill districts (Pointe-Saint-Charles, Saint-Henri) and the West Island suburbs: check how the working day and commute are told in each.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue and the Côte-Nord: compare mine shifts with the farm and forest round of the Bas-Saint-Laurent.
- The Beauce: check local accounts of small-factory work and the sugar season.
- The Gaspé and the Magdalen Islands: compare fishing seasons and the off-season as Gaspesians and Madelinots describe them.
- Eeyou and Innu: check how each nation sets time on the land beside wage work.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare village jobs, mine rotations and harvesting in Nunavimmiut sources.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check how work far from home appears in community histories.
- Townships and Gaspé Anglophones: compare farm and mill work in English-language local histories.
- Chinese, Jewish, Haitian and Portuguese Montrealers: check which trades each entered and how its own sources explain the path.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor history, statistics and occupational health; mixed for the daily round and the kept house; thin for smaller First Nations' seasonal rounds outside their own publications. The nation's exact lands are not yet settled; this sign is written for the present province, with Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** French, including old notarial vocabulary; English for Montreal industry; Innu-aimun, Eeyou, Atikamekw and Inuktitut for seasonal rounds; Cantonese and Yiddish for trade-community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wage-work histories in First Nations and Inuit communities touch residential schools and relocations; healing knowledge is shared only as healers allow. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn, the clock to Capricorn, money to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** the labor-history, time-use and health pointers weighed for the domain raised nothing the standing rules hold back.
