# Quebec: Work (Virgo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and Abitibi and Gaspé speech), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Mi'gmaq, Yiddish, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** purposeful effort that produces, keeps up or provides something of value, paid or unpaid, and the place that effort holds in a life.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Eastern Townships textile towns (Magog, Sherbrooke, Drummondville): check how mill work is remembered in local museums and union records.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue: compare mining-town accounts of shift work with accounts from the region's farm parishes.
- Mauricie and Saguenay paper towns: check whether one employer set the town's working calendar.
- The Gaspé, the Côte-Nord and the Magdalen Islands: compare seasonal fishing and tourist work in the words of those who do it.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check how ironwork families describe the work and the absences it brings.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Mi'gmaq of Listuguj: check how mine, port and salmon work appear in each community's own histories.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare village wage work with harvesting as Nunavimmiut sources count both.
- Jewish Montrealers of the garment era: check Yiddish union papers beside French ones.
- Italian, Portuguese, Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check how community papers and oral histories describe the first job after arrival.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, strikes and labor statistics; mixed for unpaid and informal work; thin for First Nations' wage-work histories outside community publications.
- **Languages needed:** French, including union and shop-floor jargon; English for Montreal's industrial records; Yiddish for the needle trades; Kanien'kéha and Inuktitut for the terms of work in those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** strike histories and the 1972 Common Front carry partisan accounts; present each as an account. Labor law goes to Libra and Capricorn; wages and wealth to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** the union histories, films and oral-history archives weighed for work turned up nothing that a standing rule removes.

## 5. First queries
1. Search the Voyageur Contracts Database for contracts signed in Montreal and tally parishes of origin.

4. Pull census occupation tables for the Côte-Nord and the Beauce and compare the leading trades.
5. Search Concordia's oral-history archive for interviews tagged "travail" or "work".
