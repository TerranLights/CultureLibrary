*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** work or effort done for another (a person, a household, a customer, the public, a nation, a cause or God), as a role or as an act, paid or unpaid.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale: check how a civil-service city speaks of public service as a career.
- Rural parishes of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and Beauce: compare memories of the priest's housekeeper and the parish's unpaid helpers.
- West Island and Townships Anglophones: check church auxiliaries and service clubs in English-language records.
- The Gaspé and the Haute-Mauricie: compare how volunteer emergency services are recruited in small places.
- Kanien'kehá:ka, Wendat, W8banaki and Mi'gmaq: check how each community honors its veterans, and how service to the Crown is debated there.
- Innu and Eeyou: check what serving the community and elders means in each nation's own sources.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare community helpers named in Nunavimmiut sources with paid service jobs.
- Caribbean and Filipino women in household service: check their organizations' own accounts against employers'.
- Haitian and Jewish Montrealers: compare how each community's agencies and congregations describe serving newcomers, then and now.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for religious orders, the public service and war; mixed for volunteering by region; thin for domestic workers' own voices before the 1970s.
- **Languages needed:** French for parish and civil-service records; English for Legion and Anglophone club papers; Tagalog and Haitian Creole for workers' and congregations' sources; Yiddish for the older benevolent societies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** conscription and the 1918 riots carry rival accounts; First Nations military service touches the Crown relationship; Vodou service to the spirits is described only as its practitioners allow. Care goes to Cancer, duty to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the domestic-worker, volunteer and veterans' pointers considered for service included nothing a standing rule would hold.
