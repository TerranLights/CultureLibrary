*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Beauce and the Bois-Francs: check how local histories describe the handy farmer and small inventor.
- The Lower North Shore and the Gaspé: compare how fishing villages speak of being needed when the work leaves.
- Montreal and Quebec City retirees: check which roles retirement groups offer and how members talk about them.
- Atikamekw of Manawan and Wemotaci: check how makers and elders are named and honored as useful to the nation.
- Innu and Eeyou: compare skills on the land with wage skills in community sources.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how elders' teaching roles are described at regional gatherings.
- Italian and Portuguese Montrealers: compare backyard food work across generations, in gardeners' own words.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how children's help at home and in translation appears in family studies.
- Disabled Quebecers and their organizations: compare advocates' accounts of useful work with employers'.
- Anglophones of the Townships: check English-language accounts of farm thrift and making do.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disability and labor statistics; mixed for repair and thrift; thin for Indigenous terms for capable people, which need community language staff.
- **Languages needed:** French, including farm and workshop slang; Atikamekw and Inuktitut for makers and elders; Italian and Portuguese for garden studies; Haitian Creole for family sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "burden" in end-of-life records is painful and must be read with care; disabled people's worth is not to be judged by output. Self-worth goes to Taurus, independence to Aries.
- **Held for review:** the disability, repair and elder pointers weighed for usefulness gave no item that falls under a standing rule.
