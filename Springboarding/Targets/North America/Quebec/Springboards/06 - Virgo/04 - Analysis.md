## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City university milieus: check whether Laval's and Montreal's social-science traditions framed analysis differently.
- Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Côte-Nord: compare engineering and plant-floor analysis in aluminum and mining towns with university accounts.
- Eastern Townships: check how English and French schools and colleges (Bishop's, Sherbrooke) taught science.
- Eeyou of Chisasibi and Waswanipi: check how hunters' observations are recorded and who in the community is asked to interpret them.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord: compare Innu classification of animals and plants with school science in Innu schools.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how ice and weather knowledge is shared with scientists, and on whose terms.
- Mi'gmaq of Listuguj and Gespeg: check whether "two-eyed seeing", a term from Mi'kmaw elders in Nova Scotia, is taken up in Quebec Mi'gmaq schools.
- Anglophone Montrealers: compare McGill's English-language science culture with francophone institutions.
- Francophone newcomers from the Maghreb and Haiti: check how foreign engineering and science degrees are assessed and used.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science institutions, statistics and test results; mixed for everyday reasoning; thin for Indigenous analytic terms without community language staff.
- **Languages needed:** French for scientific and school vocabulary; English for McGill and Townships sources; Eeyou, Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for land knowledge.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Indigenous land knowledge is shared under research protocols and may be restricted; some knowledge-holders decline to have it set beside school science. Curiosity and learning go to Gemini, truth to Sagittarius.
- **Held for review:** the statistics, science-institution and land-knowledge pointers weighed for analysis contained nothing that a standing rule sets aside.
