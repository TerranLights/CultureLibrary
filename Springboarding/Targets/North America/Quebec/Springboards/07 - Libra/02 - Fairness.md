# Quebec: Fairness (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and rural speech of the Beauce and the Bas-Saint-Laurent), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** even-handed treatment in everyday dealings: getting one's due share, taking turns, and being held to the same rules as everyone else.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Farm families of the Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Beauce: check how farm transfers to one child were balanced against the others' shares.
- Montreal and Quebec City renters and daycare seekers: compare complaints over waiting lists in the city and the regions.
- Nunavimmiut of Hudson Bay and of Ungava Bay: check how beluga quotas differ by coast and how each coast argues its share.
- Eeyou hunters and Abitibi sport hunters: compare how each side describes the harvesting priority.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check community debates over who shares in revenues and housing.
- Haitian, Filipino and Latin American Montrealers in care and service work: check what pay-equity and tipped-wage rules mean in their own accounts.
- Hasidic families of Outremont and Boisbriand: check how inheritance and business shares are handled under religious and civil rules.
- Anglophone Quebecers in the Eastern Townships: check how English-speaking families recorded farm inheritance.
- Workers of the Côte-Nord mining towns: compare union seniority rules with fairness talk in town papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, pay-equity records and notarial deeds; mixed for children's and household rules; thin for Inuit and Eeyou sharing rules beyond what communities publish.
- **Languages needed:** early-modern French for ordinances; legal French for statutes; Inuktitut and Eeyou for harvest shares; Yiddish and Hebrew for religious inheritance rules; Haitian Creole for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** harvest quotas and sharing in Inuit and Eeyou communities can be contested between villages and with Ottawa; present each side as an account. Equality as an ideal goes to Aquarius, merit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** among the inheritance, pay and quota pointers weighed for fairness, none was set aside under the standing rules.
