# Quebec: Partnership (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a committed bond between two or more parties who share aims, risks and returns as (more or less) equals: in love and marriage, in work and business, and between peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City couples: check how married, civil-union and common-law shares differ by city and neighborhood.
- The Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Gaspé: compare marriage alliances in parish records with Montreal's.
- Farm families of the Montérégie and the Centre-du-Québec: check how cooperative membership and family partnership overlap.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how marriage, adoption and hunting partnerships are described in Nunavimmiut sources.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Mashteuiatsh: compare how each community explains its business partnerships to its members.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check how the Grande Alliance is debated in each community.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how couples formed in Haiti and in Montreal describe their union.
- Italian and Portuguese Montrealers: check wedding customs and family firms across three generations.
- Same-sex couples in Montreal's Village and in the regions: compare the use of civil union and marriage.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, census and parish records; mixed for couple life and business partnerships; thin for First Nations' and Inuit partnership customs beyond what they publish.
- **Languages needed:** French and English for law and press; eighteenth-century English and French for fur-trade papers; Inuktitut and Innu-aimun for community accounts; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for community marriage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Cree and Innu agreements are debated inside each nation; present each side as an account. Violence inside couples goes to the domain sheet and to Scorpio, sex ways to Scorpio, family to Cancer.
