# Quebec: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (political and legal vocabulary), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Small municipalities of the Gaspé and Abitibi-Témiscamingue: check how hiring and contracts are watched where everyone is related.
- Montreal and Laval: compare municipal ethics rules and inquiry findings in two large cities.
- Quebec City: check how the civil service's capital describes merit and favoritism in its own press.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kanehsatà:ke and Kahnawà:ke: check how each community's election rules and appeals are written and argued.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord: compare custom codes and federal-act elections across communities.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how regional bodies (Makivvik, the regional government) set conflict-of-interest rules.
- Anglophones of the Townships and the West Island: check how English-language media covered the Bastarache and Gomery inquiries.
- Haitian Montrealers: check community accounts of access to public jobs and how connections are discussed.
- Jewish Montrealers: check how community bodies set rules for fair allocation of aid and seats.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inquiries, statutes and ethics rulings; mixed for everyday favoritism, which leaves few records; thin for First Nations' internal election disputes beyond court files and what communities publish.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for the Bigot trial; legal French for codes and rulings; English for federal inquiries; Kanien'kéha and Innu-aimun for community codes; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for community accounts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inquiry findings name living people; cite rulings, not rumors. Never tie favoritism to an ethnic group. Corruption as legal culture and policing go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** in weighing inquiry, election and ethics-code pointers for impartiality, the standing rules held nothing back.
