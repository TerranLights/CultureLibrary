# Quebec: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Kanien'kéha, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Spanish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Anishinaabeg of Kitigan Zibi and Lac-Simon: check how gift exchange with the French is told in community histories.
- Innu of Unamen Shipu and Pakua Shipi: compare feast and sharing customs as each Lower North Shore community records them.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Akwesasne: check how land-sharing teachings are applied across a border.
- Farm parishes of Charlevoix and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: check how mutual help between neighbors is told in local histories.
- Quebec City's Saint-Roch: compare time-bank members' accounts with older parish charity.
- Haitian, Vietnamese and Latin American Montrealers: check how each community's savings clubs set and enforce turns.
- Montreal's Jewish communities: check how loan and charity societies keep their books.
- Anglophones of the Gaspé and the Townships: check how English-language mutual societies and lodges recorded what members owed.
- Students from France in Montreal and Quebec City: compare how they and local students describe the fee agreement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for French-regime accounts, statutes and bilateral agreements; mixed for neighbor help and savings clubs, which keep few records; thin for Anishinaabe and Innu exchange customs beyond what communities publish.
- **Languages needed:** old administrative French for C11A; legal French for the code; Innu-aimun, Kanien'kéha and Anishinaabemowin for teachings; Haitian Creole, Vietnamese and Spanish for savings clubs; Yiddish for loan societies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** wampum teachings and feast customs belong to their nations; use what they publish. Revenge goes to Scorpio, generosity to Leo, giving without return to Pisces.
- **Held for review:** a single reciprocity pointer is held back under the standing rule: South Asian-heritage communities in Quebec's lands, as a category only.
