# Quebec: Peace at any price (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha. **Concept in one line:** keeping peace or harmony by giving way, staying silent or paying whatever it costs, even when the cost is one's own (or someone else's) rights, truth or justice.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Rural parishes of Lotbinière and the Centre-du-Québec: check how the Aurore case is remembered where it happened.
- Montreal and Quebec City: compare how strikes ended by special laws were reported in each city's press.
- Workers in the Côte-Nord and Abitibi mines: check harassment and safety complaints against the silence described in oral histories.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how community bodies speak about family violence and reconciliation in their own publications.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord: check how women's associations describe silence and speaking out.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check community peacemaking customs as the nation's own teachers describe them.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how the proverb of the blow and the scar is used in talk of family and politics.
- Hasidic and other Jewish Montrealers: check community debate over domestic harmony, divorce and speaking out.
- Anglophones of the Gaspé and the Townships: compare English-language accounts of wartime conscription and goodwill campaigns.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, settlements and press; mixed for family conflict avoidance; thin for Atikamekw and Anishinaabe peacemaking customs outside their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French and English for the capitulation; legal French for the codes; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for community debate; Inuktitut, Innu-aimun and Kanien'kéha for community reports.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases and shelter data involve survivors; use published reports only. The capitulation, conscription and special laws carry rival accounts; present each as an account. Revenge and secrecy go to Scorpio, surrender to Pisces, anger to Aries.
- **Held for review:** among the capitulation, harassment-law and abuse-settlement pointers for peace at any price, nothing came under the standing rules.
