# Quebec: The domain: death and transformation (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and rural speech), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Kanien'kéha, Wendat (in revitalization), Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** how the culture faces death, power and what is hidden: death and rebirth, sex, shared resources, inheritance and secrets, with power ways as a runner-up.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's francophone Catholics and Anglo-Protestants: compare the two mountain cemeteries' registers and plot sales.
- Rural parishes of the Beauce, the Bas-Saint-Laurent and Charlevoix: check when the funeral parlor replaced the home viewing.
- Acadians of the Magdalen Islands and the Gaspé: compare island cemeteries and fishing families' wills with the mainland's.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how names of the dead pass to the newborn, as Inuit-authored sources describe it.
- Eeyou and Innu: compare graves on the hunting lands with burial in the villages, and who speaks for each.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check condolence and mourning teachings only as their keepers choose to share them.
- Wendat of Wendake: compare the repatriation account with the colonial descriptions of the Feast of the Dead.
- Ashkenazi, Moroccan Sephardic and Hasidic Montrealers: check each burial society's own records of mourning rules.
- Haitian, Lebanese, Greek and Maghrebi Montrealers: check where the dead are buried, at home or abroad, and why.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church records, vital statistics and cemetery registers; mixed for sex ways and couples' money; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and W8banaki death ways beyond their own publications. Quebec's final lands are still to be ruled on; this sign takes the present province as its core, with Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario as outside comparisons only.
- **Languages needed:** old French for Jesuit and notarial sources; Inuktitut, Innu-aimun and Eeyou for naming and burial; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for exile funerals.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** First Nations and Inuit burials, remains and mourning ceremonies belong to their keepers; residential-school deaths and the search for missing children are grieving, contested ground. Estate law goes to Libra; power is concept 9 here.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the cemetery, burial-society and estate sources considered for this domain all passed the standing rules.
