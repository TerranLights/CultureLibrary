# Quebec: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Atikamekw, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Greek. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how a culture understands dying, the dead and what death means, with grief and mourning.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Gaspé and Magdalen Islands fishing parishes: check how the sea's dead are named and remembered when no body comes home.
- Quebec City and the Capitale-Nationale beside Montreal: compare hospice access and place of death in regional health records.
- Anglophone Montrealers: check whether palliative-care history is told differently in English and in French sources.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue and Côte-Nord mining towns: check union and town records of mourning after mine deaths.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare how deaths far from home, in southern hospitals, are handled and told.
- Atikamekw of Manawan, Wemotaci and Opitciwan, and Innu families of the Côte-Nord: check their own accounts of children who died away from home.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check how deaths on the land and in the villages are spoken of in Cree media.
- Ashkenazi, Sephardic and Hasidic Montrealers: compare how each keeps the year of mourning.
- Greek, Italian, Portuguese and Lebanese Montrealers: check fixed-day memorial services and bodies sent home.
- Muslim Quebecers of Quebec City and Montreal: check where they bury and how the mosque shooting changed that.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for palliative care, aid in dying and suicide research; mixed for grief customs; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and W8banaki mourning beyond what each nation shares.
- **Languages needed:** sixteenth- and seventeenth-century French for Cartier and the *Relations*; Inuktitut and Atikamekw for deaths away from home; Yiddish, Hebrew and Greek for memorial rites.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and the missing Indigenous children are raw ground; First Nations and Inuit mourning ceremonies are their keepers'. The afterlife is concept 2; the aid-in-dying law is Libra's.
