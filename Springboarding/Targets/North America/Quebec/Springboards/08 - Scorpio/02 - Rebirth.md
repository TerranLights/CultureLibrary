# Quebec: Rebirth (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Wendat (in revitalization), Haitian Creole, Hebrew and Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a new life that comes through an ending: conversion, recovery and redemption, renewal after disaster, cultural revival, and beliefs that the dead return.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Bas-Saint-Laurent towns rebuilt after fire against the Gaspé's closed towns: check what decided whether a place was rebuilt.
- Francophone Protestant families of the Richelieu valley and the Eastern Townships: check how converts and their descendants tell the change.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee and Anishinaabeg of Abitibi-Témiscamingue: compare accounts of returning to burned land.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check whether the namesake bond is spoken of as a return, in Inuit-authored sources.
- Wendat of Wendake and W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak: compare how each nation's language revival describes its own aims.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check how Longhouse revival and church membership are each described by their own members.
- Haitian, Latin American and African-born Montrealers in evangelical churches: compare testimony styles across congregations.
- Hasidic and other Orthodox Jews of Outremont and Côte-Saint-Luc: check how returnees to observance are received.
- Francophone converts to Islam and to evangelical churches: check how converts describe family reactions, in their own words.
- Anglophone Montreal and West Island recovery groups beside francophone ones: compare meeting literature and language.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church history, the Quiet Revolution debate and disaster history; mixed for recovery and reentry; thin for First Nations ceremonies of renewal, which their keepers may not share.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the *Relations*; Inuktitut for naming; Wendat manuscripts and linguists' notation; Haitian Creole for testimony; Hebrew and Yiddish for return to observance.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** forced conversion and residential schools are grieving, contested ground; the *Grande Noirceur* account is one account among others; Inuit naming and First Nations renewal rites belong to their holders.
- **Held for review:** in reincarnation and Montreal temple sources, Buddhist rebirth teaching was set aside as a category (Buddhism, an open question for the author).
