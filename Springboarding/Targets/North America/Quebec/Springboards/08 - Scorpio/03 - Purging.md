# Quebec: Purging (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Hebrew, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** getting rid of what is felt to be polluted, poisonous or corrupt, in a body, a mind, a group, an institution or a place, so the whole can be clean or healed again.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City: compare the two cities' vice cleanups and whose names the press printed.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue: check how Rouyn-Noranda's neighborhoods next to the smelter speak of the arsenic, by street and by generation.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare cleanup decisions in Ungava Bay and Hudson Bay communities.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Lac-Saint-Jean: check which healing practices each community chooses to make public.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check the accounts on each side of residency and membership disputes, without taking one as the community's voice.
- Jehovah's Witnesses and other small Christian groups: check their own histories of the 1940s and 1950s.
- Gay and lesbian Montrealers of the 1970s: compare accounts in French and English community papers.
- Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check how cleansing baths and church deliverance prayers each answer misfortune.
- Hasidic and Modern Orthodox Jews, and Maghrebi and Lebanese Muslims: compare the place of ritual washing in each community's own guides.
- Quebec English speakers and francophone language activists: check how each describes campaigns against anglicisms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inquiries, censorship files and press; mixed for folk purgatives and cleansing rites; thin for Innu and Atikamekw healing beyond what communities publish.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the brandy quarrel; 1950s legal and press French; Innu-aimun for healing programs; Haitian Creole, Hebrew and Arabic for ritual washing.
