# Quebec: Betrayal (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City against Montreal: compare how each city's press told the Nordiques' departure and the Sun Life move.
- Gaspé mining and fishing towns: check how strikebreakers and their families were treated after 1957, from both sides.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Lac-Saint-Jean: check Innu historians' reading of Pastedechouan beside the Jesuit one.
- Wendat of Wendake and Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: compare how each nation tells the 1760 shift of alliance.
- Inuit of Inukjuak and other Nunavik villages: compare family accounts of the relocation with the government record.
- Anglophone Montrealers: check English-language accounts of the 1976-1980 departures, and whether "betrayal" is used of those who left or of those who stayed.
- Sovereigntist and federalist writers: compare memoirs of 1981 and 1995 as rival accounts, not as group traits.
- Haitian Montrealers of the first exile generation and their children: check how talk of informers changed across generations.
- Hasidic and other Orthodox Montrealers: check whether and how *mesirah* (informing to outside authorities) is discussed in their own publications.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political memoirs, speeches and press; mixed for infidelity (surveys exist, talk is private); thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Mi'gmaq accounts of broken promises outside what they publish.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Le Jeune; nineteenth-century French for Lartigue and Mercier; Inuktitut for Inukjuak testimony; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for informer vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1981 patriation, the 1980 and 1995 referendums, the October Crisis and the Kanehsatà:ke land question each carry rival accounts and are presented as accounts; treaty teachings belong to their nations. Treason as law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for betrayal, the infidelity, informer and treaty sources considered left nothing to set aside under the standing rules.
