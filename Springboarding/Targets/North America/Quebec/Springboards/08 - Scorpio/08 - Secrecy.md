# Quebec: Secrecy (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** keeping knowledge hidden from others on purpose, and how a culture judges what may, must or must not be kept hidden; privacy is one of its facets.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and small parishes: check whether gossip and privacy are described differently in city and village memoirs.
- Saint-Hyacinthe and other diocesan towns: compare girls' and boys' private writing in the nineteenth century.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check how each community's cultural center handles requests about restricted objects.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Mashteuiatsh: compare what each community publishes about restricted practices.
- Anishinaabeg of Kitigan Zibi and Lac-Simon: check their own statements about ceremonies not open to outsiders.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check Avataq and Makivvik rules on sharing elders' recordings.
- Gay and lesbian Montrealers and those in the regions: compare memoirs of the closet by decade and region.
- Families of adoptees born in Quebec City, the Saguenay and Montreal crèches: check how disclosure was received in each.
- Federalist and sovereigntist camps: compare their accounts of 1995 spending as rival accounts.
- Haitian and Jewish Montreal families: check, in their own memoirs, which topics were kept from children or from outsiders, without assuming any.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for inquiries, archives and press; mixed for family secrets; thin by design for restricted Indigenous knowledge, which is not to be researched beyond what keepers release.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the devout societies; nineteenth-century French for the diary; Innu-aimun and Kanien'kéha for protocol statements; Inuktitut for research rules.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the shaking tent, medicine masks and similar knowledge are restricted; adoption and abortion histories involve living people; the 1995 referendum is contested ground. Confidentiality law goes to Libra; gossip to Gemini.
- **Held for review:** in weighing confession, lodge, adoption and restricted-knowledge sources for secrecy, no pointer had to be set aside.
