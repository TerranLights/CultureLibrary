# Quebec: Crisis (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** a turning point of acute danger or breakdown, when things cannot go on as they were and must change, for a person, a family, a community or a country.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay villages and Côte-Nord towns: check how landslide and flood survivors in each describe moving away or staying.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue: compare settlers' memoirs of the Depression colonization with the Anishinaabeg accounts of the same years.
- Quebec City and Montreal in 1832 and 1918: check parish and hospital records for each city's epidemic response.
- Irish Montrealers of Pointe-Saint-Charles and Griffintown: check how the famine dead are kept in community memory.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kanehsatà:ke and Kahnawà:ke: compare their own accounts of 1990 with journalists' and police accounts.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how Kangiqsualujjuaq and other villages speak of the 1999 avalanche and later safety measures.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee and Innu of the Côte-Nord: compare accounts of evacuations for fires and floods, in their own media.
- Haitian Montrealers of Saint-Michel, Montréal-Nord and Laval: check how families describe 2010 and the years after.
- Anglophone and francophone Montrealers: compare how each press told the 1980 referendum as a crisis or not.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for epidemics, disasters and political crises; mixed for personal and family crises; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and W8banaki disaster memory beyond what they publish.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the 1663 accounts; Kanien'kéha for 1990 testimony; Inuktitut for the avalanche; Haitian Creole for the 2010 diaspora.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1990 Kanehsatà:ke resistance and the 1980 and 1995 referendums carry rival accounts, presented as accounts; the avalanche and landslide involve grieving families. Emergency law goes to Libra; stability and security to Taurus.
- **Held for review:** the epidemic, landslide, avalanche and referendum sources weighed for crisis did not call for setting any pointer aside.
