# Quebec: Faith (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Eeyou, Kanien'kéha, Haitian Creole, Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** trust in and commitment to what can't be seen or proven (God, gods, spirits, a sacred order), and by extension secular faith in people, progress, science or the nation.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal francophones and Beauce and Saguenay parishes: check whether Mass-going and shrine visits fell at one pace.
- Quebec City's Catholic institutions beside the Eastern Townships' Anglican and United churches: compare closures and reuse of buildings.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kanehsatà:ke and Kahnawà:ke: separate Catholic, Methodist and Longhouse accounts of faith and of switching between them.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: compare Anglican, Pentecostal and Catholic congregations, and when Pentecostal revival reached each community.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Atikamekw of Manawan, Wemotaci and Opitciwan: check how each nation describes the church and its own ceremonies today.
- Italian, Portuguese, Greek and Lebanese Montrealers: compare how home-country saints and rites keep or lose their hold in the second generation.
- Haitian Montrealers: separate Catholic, evangelical and Vodou practice, and check how families move among them.
- Ashkenazi, Moroccan Sephardic and Hasidic Montrealers: compare what "observant" means in each community's own institutions.
- Maghrebi and other Muslim Quebecers of Montreal and Quebec City: check mosque records and community statements, not outside commentary.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Catholic practice and its decline, with surveys back to the 1980s; mixed for immigrant parishes; thin for Naskapi, Wolastoqiyik and Algonquin Christianity, where community histories are the main route.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for the hospital annals; Inuktitut syllabics and East Cree for mission records; Italian, Portuguese and Haitian Creole for immigrant parish life; Hebrew and Yiddish for congregational minutes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the clergy-abuse cases, the residential schools and the 2017 mosque attack are grieving ground; the secularism debates are contested and go to Libra and Capricorn for their law.
- **Held for review:** among the immigrant congregations weighed for faith practice, Buddhist temples were held as a category only (an open question for the author).
