# Quebec: Meaning (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and Gaspé fishing speech), Quebec English, Eeyou, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what makes life, work and suffering worth it: the sense that life has a point, and where that point is found.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Charlevoix, the Saguenay and the Bas-Saint-Laurent beside Montreal: check whether land, parish and family are named as sources of purpose at different rates in interviews.
- Acadians and other fishing families of the Magdalen Islands and the Gaspé coast: compare what each says after a fishery closes.
- Abitibi-Témiscamingue mining families: check how fly-in and long-shift work is spoken of as worth it or not.
- Inuit of Nunavik: separate the survey's community results, since the Hudson and Ungava coasts may differ.
- Eeyou, Innu and Atikamekw: compare what each nation's own programs say about life on the land and the young.
- Anglophone Quebecers of the West Island and the Townships: check whether staying or leaving after 1976 is told as a loss or a fresh purpose.
- Haitian, Italian, Vietnamese and Latin American Montrealers: compare first and second generations' accounts of what the move was for.
- Hasidic communities of Outremont and Boisbriand and secular Jewish Montrealers: check what "purpose" means in each group's own writing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for suicide statistics, health surveys and literature; mixed for direct survey items on meaning; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik, where their own councils' publications are the place to start.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Bourgeoys; Inuktitut and Eeyou for elders' recordings; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for exile writing.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide, above all among Inuit and First Nations youth, needs care and community consent; elders' recordings may carry access rules; the end-of-life law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the men's-group, spiritual-care and suicide-surveillance sources weighed for meaning all cleared the rules.
