# Quebec: Freedom (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Eeyou, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** being able to act, speak, believe and move without constraint by others or by the state, and freedom as a belief and a political ideal.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal francophones and Montreal Anglophones: check how each community's press uses *liberté* and "freedom" in the language debates, read as rival accounts.
- The Outaouais and the Gaspé, near other provinces: compare whether freedom of movement across a provincial line comes up in local media.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Akwesasne, Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: separate each community's own accounts of border, land and movement.
- Eeyou of Whapmagoostui and Chisasibi and Inuit of Kuujjuarapik: compare how each speaks of moving freely on rivers and coast changed by dams.
- Innu, Atikamekw and W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak: check how each council writes of the freedom to hunt and travel.
- Hunters and anglers of Abitibi, the Mauricie and the Laurentides: compare how access to Crown land is argued region by region.
- Black Montrealers of long standing (Little Burgundy) and Haitian Montrealers: separate the histories of slavery, exile and arrival.
- Vietnamese, Latin American and Maghrebi Montrealers: check which freedoms each community's own papers name as reasons for coming.
- Hasidic and secular Jewish Montrealers: compare how each speaks of religious freedom and of leaving a closed community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for political and protest history and for the press; mixed for polling with Quebec breakouts; thin for Naskapi, Wolastoqiyik and Mi'gmaq views of freedom beyond their councils' statements.
- **Languages needed:** early modern French for Lahontan; Kanien'kéha and Eeyou for movement and land terms; Haitian Creole, Vietnamese and Yiddish for exile and labor presses.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1970 crisis, the referendums, the language laws and the Kanehsatà:ke resistance of 1990 are contested; treat each as an event with rival accounts; the law on rights belongs to Libra and Capricorn; the 1703 dialogue speaks for a Wendat leader through a French author.
- **Held for review:** nothing; the slavery, labor, refugee and polling sources weighed for freedom passed the standing rules.
