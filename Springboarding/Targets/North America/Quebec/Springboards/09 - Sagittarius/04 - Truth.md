# Quebec: Truth (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and the speech of the Magdalen Islands), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Eeyou, Innu-aimun, Atikamekw, Haitian Creole, Italian, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what is so, and how a culture decides what is so: whom it trusts to say it, and how it settles disputes over facts.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and the Capitale-Nationale: check whether trusted news sources and talk radio differ between the two cities.
- The Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Abitibi-Témiscamingue: compare reliance on regional dailies and community radio after newsroom closures.
- Acadians of the Magdalen Islands and Gaspé fishing towns: check how island and coast communities test rumor and official word.
- Innu and Atikamekw: compare their own land-use evidence with how outside inquiries received it.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Wendat of Wendake: check who may read and interpret a wampum record in each community.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou: compare whose account of the past schools carries weight in each community's own publications.
- Haitian, Italian and Greek Montrealers: compare ethnic media as trusted sources against the francophone and Anglophone mainstream.
- Anglophone Quebecers: check whether the English-language press and the French-language press report the same disputes as fact.
- Young and old: check news-source and conspiracy items by age before reading any regional gap.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school history disputes, inquiries and media; mixed for direct surveys of trust in science in Quebec; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik oral records outside their councils.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the Ragueneau life; Inuktitut, Eeyou and Atikamekw for testimony and place names; Haitian Creole, Italian and Yiddish for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** survivors' testimony needs care and consent; wampum and oral records belong to their keepers; the *Grande Noirceur* account and the 2020 statue are contested ground. Oaths in court go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the curriculum, ethnic-radio and misinformation sources weighed for truth raised no issue under the standing rules; nothing was put aside.
