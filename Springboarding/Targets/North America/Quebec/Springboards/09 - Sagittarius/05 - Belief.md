# Quebec: Belief (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with Saguenay and Beauce speech), Quebec English, Innu-aimun and Naskapi, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Wendat (in revitalization), Haitian Creole, Arabic (for Lebanese and Maghrebi Montrealers). **Concept in one line:** holding something to be true about God, the world, people, and right and wrong, religious or secular, and the warrant people want for it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Beauce: compare how long folk beliefs and Easter duty held in each region's records.
- Montreal's francophone core and the West Island: check whether "no religion" and "spiritual but not religious" are chosen in different shares.
- The Eastern Townships and the Laurentides: compare how rural towns received new religious movements that settled near them.
- Innu and Naskapi of the Côte-Nord and Kawawachikamach: separate their own accounts of hunting belief from Speck's.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how Inuit authors handle older beliefs in Christian communities, and what they choose not to print.
- Wendat of Wendake and Atikamekw: compare each nation's own writing about belief with the mission record.
- Lebanese Maronite, Greek Orthodox and Armenian Montrealers: check where belonging to the church and personal belief come apart.
- Maghrebi and other Muslim Quebecers: check community and mosque sources on what young believers say they hold.
- Haitian Montrealers: separate Catholic, Protestant and Vodou belief, as each group states its own.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for catechisms, surveys and folklore; mixed for new religious movements; thin for Naskapi and Mi'gmaq belief outside older ethnographies and their own councils.
- **Languages needed:** the French of the 1630s for Le Jeune's report; Innu-aimun and Naskapi for hunting belief; Inuktitut for Qumaq; Arabic and Haitian Creole for immigrant belief.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Inuit and First Nations beliefs may be restricted by their keepers; the 1994 deaths touch grieving families; older ethnographies carry outsiders' labels to read critically. Belief at the heart of the secularism law is contested ground and its law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** Buddhist groups, met among the new and immigrant movements weighed here, are held back as a category (the author has not yet ruled on them).
