# Quebec: Conviction (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a firm, settled belief that a person holds to and acts on: the strength of a belief, not its content.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's student milieu and the regions: check whether 2012 strike votes differed between Montreal colleges and those of the Saguenay, Abitibi or the Mauricie.
- The Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Côte-Nord: compare how river and dam campaigns rallied townspeople and fishermen.
- Valleyfield, Sherbrooke and other mill towns: check how labor stands are remembered in each town's own history.
- Inuit of Puvirnituq, Salluit and Ivujivik beside the other Nunavik communities: separate the dissidents' accounts from those of the signers.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: compare council, Longhouse and Warrior accounts as rival voices, not as one.
- Innu of Ekuanitshit and Uashat mak Mani-utenam: check how each council states its stands on dams and rivers.
- Anglophone Montrealers and Townships residents: compare their associations' records of principled refusal in the language debates, as accounts.
- Haitian, Greek and Latin American Montrealers: check exile and solidarity groups' papers for which causes each held to.
- Hasidic communities and Montreal's secular Jewish left: compare what each calls steadfastness in its own writing.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for labor, student and independence movements; mixed for religious conviction after 1960; thin for Wolastoqiyik, Naskapi and Atikamekw stands outside their councils' statements.
- **Languages needed:** French of New France for Le Ber; Inuktitut for the Nunavik dissidents; Kanien'kéha for Warrior and Longhouse terms; Haitian Creole for the exile press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Kanehsatà:ke resistance of 1990, the October Crisis, the referendums and the 2012 strike are contested ground with rival accounts; Warrior Society writings are disputed inside the communities. Rights and their law go to Libra and Capricorn.
