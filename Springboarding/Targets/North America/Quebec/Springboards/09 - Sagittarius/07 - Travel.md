# Quebec: Travel (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with Magdalen Islands Acadian speech), Quebec English, Innu-aimun and Naskapi, Eeyou, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Spanish (for seasonal farmworkers). **Concept in one line:** going far from home (long journeys, exploration, pilgrimage and migration) and how the culture regards the road, the foreign and the traveler.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City travelers: check passport and outbound-trip data for differences of class and region before reading culture into them.
- The Beauce, the Eastern Townships and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: compare parish records of emigration to New England in the 1800s.
- Acadians and Anglophones of the Magdalen Islands: check how islanders speak of leaving and returning, by language group.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Matimekush–Lac John, and Naskapi of Kawawachikamach: compare the train's role in each community's own accounts.
- Eeyou of inland and coastal communities: separate the effects of the road on those it reached and those it did not.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check how air travel costs and medical travel to Montreal are described by Inuit organizations.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: compare cross-border travel for work and family, in their own accounts.
- Haitian, Vietnamese and Lebanese Montrealers: compare return trips, who goes, and what is carried home.
- Mexican and Guatemalan seasonal workers in Montérégie and the Laurentides: check their own testimony against employers' and recruiters' records.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for emigration history, tourism statistics and youth programs; mixed for pilgrimage today; thin for First Nations seasonal rounds as told by the nations, where their own cultural centers come first.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for La Vérendrye; Innu-aimun and Naskapi for the railway communities; Spanish for farmworkers; Haitian Creole and Vietnamese for return travel.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** temporary workers' conditions are contested; Inuit medical travel touches health and family separation; exploration narratives carry colonial framing. Border and visa law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** for travel, the remittance, farmworker and snowbird sources weighed raised nothing that the standing rules put aside.
