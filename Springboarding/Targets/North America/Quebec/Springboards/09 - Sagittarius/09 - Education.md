# Quebec: Education (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Eeyou, Innu-aimun, Wendat (in revitalization), Yiddish and Hebrew, Haitian Creole. **Concept in one line:** formal and higher learning (schools, universities, degrees, scholarship and lifelong study) and the standing of the educated person.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal, with several universities, and regions served by one campus (Abitibi, Rimouski, Chicoutimi): check attainment and who leaves to study and who returns.
- The Gaspé and the Magdalen Islands: compare how far students travel for college and how many come back.
- Francophone and Anglophone systems: separate cégep and university paths in each language before comparing.
- Wendat of Wendake, Innu and Atikamekw: compare post-secondary participation and what each nation's education body states as its aims.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou: check how each region's school board and students describe studying in the south.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check the community's own schools and colleges against the provincial path.
- Ashkenazi, Moroccan Sephardic and Hasidic Montrealers: compare religious, Yiddish and French schooling as each community chose it.
- Haitian, Vietnamese and Maghrebi Montrealers: check first- and second-generation attainment in the data before reading any pattern.
- Men and women: check degree attainment by sex and region in the indicators, and how the press frames any gap.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for official indicators, college and university histories and the Parent era; mixed for first-generation and immigrant paths; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik post-secondary data, where their councils and CEPN come first.
- **Languages needed:** Latin and French for Jesuit-college records; Inuktitut for Nunavik student voices; Yiddish and Hebrew for Jewish school archives.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential schools, the *Grande Noirceur* account of church schooling and the language-of-instruction rules are contested ground; school law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the university, school-board and student-press sources weighed for education raised no case for holding anything back.
