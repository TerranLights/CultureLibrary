# Quebec: Fundamentalism (Sagittarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Yiddish and Hebrew, Arabic, Haitian Creole, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Spanish (for Latin American congregations). **Concept in one line:** the insistence that one truth, usually a sacred text or founding doctrine read literally, is complete and binding, and the movement to restore and defend it against modern change; whether the word fits secular certainties is itself contested.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and Montreal Catholics: compare where traditionalist and Marian movements gathered and how each diocese answered them.
- The Laurentides and Outremont: check how Hasidic communities and their neighbors each describe separation and its frictions, as accounts.
- The Gaspé and the Saguenay: compare how small high-control groups were received in remote places, in local press.
- Francophone evangelicals in Montreal, Quebec City and the Mauricie: check their own histories and how outsiders' use of *secte* affected them.
- Haitian, Latin American and African Montrealers in Pentecostal churches: compare each congregation's statement of faith before using any label.
- Maghrebi, Lebanese and other Muslim Quebecers: check mosque statements and researchers' interviews for how they meet the word *intégriste*.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou: check how charismatic revivals in their communities are described by members, not by outside observers.
- Secular campaigners and religious minorities: compare who calls a secularist "fundamentalist" and who uses the word of believers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for nineteenth-century ultramontanism and the accommodation debates; mixed for evangelical and traditionalist groups; thin for small groups and for leavers' own accounts outside press reports.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth- and nineteenth-century French for church letters and Tardivel; Yiddish and Hebrew for Hasidic sources; Arabic for Muslim scholarship; Haitian Creole and Spanish for Pentecostal congregations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the word is an insult in most uses; Hasidic, Muslim and evangelical Quebecers have each been targeted by it, and the secularism law, the accommodation debate and the 2017 mosque attack are contested or grieving ground; youth-protection and secularism law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the strict Catholic, Jewish, evangelical and new-movement pointers weighed here passed the standing rules; none was set aside.
