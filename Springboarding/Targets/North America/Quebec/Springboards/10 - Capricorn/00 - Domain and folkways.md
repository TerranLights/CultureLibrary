## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City: check whether address and office hierarchy differ between the metropolis and the civil-service capital.
- The Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and the Beauce: compare where local standing comes from (family firm, parish, union).
- The Magdalen Islands and the Basse-Côte-Nord: check how Atlantic time shows in local schedules.
- Anglophones of the Eastern Townships and the Gaspé: compare English accounts of status with French ones from the same counties.
- Innu, Atikamekw and Anishinaabeg: check each nation's own texts on chiefs and elders.
- Eeyou and Inuit of Nunavik: compare how each regional body publishes the roles of elders, youth and elected leaders.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check what each publishes on clan titles beside its elected council.
- Haitian, Vietnamese and Maghrebi Montrealers: check how credential recognition shaped careers, through their own organizations.
- Ashkenazi, Moroccan Sephardic and Hasidic Montrealers: compare where each seeks communal authority.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, state and protocol records; mixed for cross-cultural indexes, which may lack a Quebec row; thin for Naskapi, Wolastoqiyik and W8banaki sources beyond their own publications. The tracker lists no settled lands yet, so the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign takes the province as it stands as the core, with Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario as comparison only.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for Franquet; administrative French for protocol and pension texts; the Indigenous languages above for elder and chief terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the oath dispute and the Quiet Revolution's account of the *Grande Noirceur* are contested; give each as an account. Clan and title knowledge belongs to the nations that hold it. The spirit of law is this sign's concept 08; its letter is Libra's.
