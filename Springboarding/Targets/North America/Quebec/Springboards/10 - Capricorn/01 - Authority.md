# Quebec: Authority (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the recognized right to direct others, decide for them or speak for them, and others' acceptance of that right.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City: check whether challenges to police and protest rules took the same form in both cities in 2012.
- The Beauce and the Bas-Saint-Laurent: compare parish-era memoirs on the curé's word with Montreal working-class memoirs.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Akwesasne, Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check how each community's bodies describe their own mandate, not how outsiders rank them.
- Eeyou and Inuit of Nunavik: compare how regional policing is described in each people's own reports.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam and Atikamekw of Manawan: check band and community publications on accepted and refused outside authority.
- Hasidic, Modern Orthodox and secular Jewish Montrealers: compare where each looks for a binding word.
- Haitian, Maghrebi and Latin American parents: check each community's own guides on schools and youth protection.
- English-speaking Quebecers of Pointe-Claire, Sherbrooke and the Gaspé coast: compare letters to the editor on the 2020 health orders in English and French papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, protest and pandemic records; mixed for permission and correctional measures, which must be assembled; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik sources on authority.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the *Relation*; Kanien'kéha for Longhouse sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for Hasidic texts; Haitian Creole for community radio.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse cases need survivors' consent and published sources only; the 2012 laws and Longhouse matters carry rival accounts. Spirit of law is concept 08; Libra keeps its letter.
- **Held for review:** in looking for religious and community authority pointers, nothing turned up that a standing rule would hold.
