# Quebec: Duty (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with the rural speech of the Gaspé and the Lac-Saint-Jean), Quebec English, Mi'gmaq, Atikamekw, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Vietnamese, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** what a person owes and must do because of a role, a tie or a promise, whether or not they wish to.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Gaspé, the Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Lac-Saint-Jean: check how parish duties (tithe, pew rent, corvées) were recorded in each diocese.
- English-speaking Quebecers of the Townships and the Pontiac: check their own war memorials and Legion records against francophone ones nearby.
- Mi'gmaq of Listuguj and Gesgapegiag: compare how each community frames fishing duties in its own rules.
- Atikamekw of Manawan, Wemotaci and Opitciwan: check whether each community speaks of Nitaskinan duties the same way.
- Eeyou of the coast and of the inland communities: compare families' accounts of time owed to the land.
- Vietnamese, Haitian and Lebanese Montrealers: check each community's caregiving arrangements through its own associations.
- Hasidic communities of Outremont and Boisbriand: check how spokespeople explain communal duties beside civic ones.
- Nurses and orderlies in Montreal, the Outaouais and Abitibi hospitals: compare TSO grievances by region and hospital.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, statute and memorial records; mixed for caregiving and volunteering, where surveys are national; thin for Naskapi and W8banaki statements on duty beyond their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth- and eighteenth-century French for tithe and *fabrique* books; Mi'gmaq and Atikamekw for land texts; Vietnamese for family-care sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clerical nationalism and the Crown's duty to Native nations carry rival accounts; give each as an account. Land teachings are the nations' to share. Duty as law's spirit is fine here; the letter of statutes belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the caregiving, memorial and land-duty sources weighed here gave no cause to hold any back.
