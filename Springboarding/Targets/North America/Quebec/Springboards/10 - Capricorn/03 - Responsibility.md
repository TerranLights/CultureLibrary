## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's East End and the West Island: check whether young-carer and support-payment studies sample both, and in which language.
- The Bas-Saint-Laurent and the Côte-Nord: compare how local press assigned blame after L'Isle-Verte and after the 2023 fires.
- The Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean and Abitibi: check how forestry towns speak of company, state and household responsibility for fire risk.
- Eeyou of Mistissini and Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam: compare each community's own statements on fire, land and evacuation.
- W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak and Wendat of Wendake: check each nation's environmental offices for how they word stewardship.
- Inuit of the Hudson coast and of Ungava Bay: compare recovery and healing programs and who runs them.
- Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check community groups' guides on parents' duties under youth-protection law.
- Hasidic and Sephardic Montrealers: compare how each community's institutions describe answering for members' conduct.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, inquiries and fire records; mixed for the mental load and young carers, studied in scattered theses; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik sources on stewardship beyond their own sites.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the 1648 *Relation*; administrative French for inquiries; W8banaki, Innu-aimun, Eeyou and Inuktitut for land and healing terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** coroner files name the dead; use published reports. Addiction and family records need care. Responsibility as legal culture is in scope here; statutes' letter is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** among the inquiry, welfare and stewardship pointers gathered for responsibility, none raised a standing-rule concern.
