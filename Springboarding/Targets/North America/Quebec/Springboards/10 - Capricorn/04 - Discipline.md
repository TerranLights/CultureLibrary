# Quebec: Discipline (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Maghrebi Arabic, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** training and keeping behavior to a standard, whether imposed by others (parents, teachers, armies, employers, churches, the state) or by oneself (self-discipline, self-control, willpower).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and the Capitale-Nationale: check memoirs of the strap from French Catholic and English Protestant schools in each city.
- The Eastern Townships and the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean: compare how the two monasteries' neighbors speak of the monks' rule.
- The Mauricie and the Outaouais: check regional collections for local names of the bogeyman figure.
- Anglophone Montrealers and Irish Catholic schools: compare their discipline memoirs with francophone ones.
- Inuit of Nunavik and Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee: check each people's own accounts of training children for the land and for school.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Atikamekw of the Haute-Mauricie: check survivors' and elders' accounts of discipline at home and in institutions, community by community.
- Haitian, Maghrebi, Vietnamese and Latin American parents: compare studies across communities, not within one alone.
- Hasidic Montrealers: check community schooling sources on study discipline.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for institutions, monasteries and court records; mixed for parenting practice, studied unevenly; thin for Naskapi and W8banaki sources on child-rearing beyond their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for colonial military sources; Inuktitut for Avataq recordings; Haitian Creole and Arabic for community sources; Yiddish and Hebrew for Musar texts.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** reform schools, residential schools and the strap involve survivors; their accounts come first and contested accounts are given as accounts. Parenting studies must not be turned into claims about any group. Disciplinary law as spirit fits here; its letter is Libra's.
- **Held for review:** in looking for fasting and religious-discipline pointers across Montreal's faiths, Buddhist communities were held as a category (an open question for the author).
