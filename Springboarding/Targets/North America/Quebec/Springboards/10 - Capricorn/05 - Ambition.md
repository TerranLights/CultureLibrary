# Quebec: Ambition (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including Quebec City and Montreal working-class speech), Quebec English, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Spanish (Latin American Montrealers), Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the wish and drive to rise, achieve or become more than one is: in work, wealth, standing, skill or service.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- The Beauce and the Saguenay: check whether the "entrepreneurial region" label holds up in each area's business registers.
- Quebec City's Lower Town and Montreal's Saint-Henri: compare novels and memoirs of leaving a working-class district.
- Anglophone Montrealers who stayed and those who left after 1976: check memoirs and surveys on careers and departure.
- Eeyou of Eeyou Istchee and Inuit of Nunavik: compare their own accounts of the James Bay years and of the ambitions their leaders set.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Anishinaabeg of Abitibi: check community publications on graduates and new enterprises.
- Haitian, Maghrebi, Latin American and Vietnamese Montrealers: compare educational-aspiration studies community by community.
- Ashkenazi and Moroccan Sephardic Montrealers: check each community's own histories of mobility from Saint-Urbain or from arrival in the 1960s.
- Women and men in the same professions: compare where each group's ambitions are described as admirable or suspect.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for business, survey and media sources; mixed for attitudes to ambition, which are seldom asked directly; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik aspirations beyond broad surveys.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century administrative French for ennoblement files; Eeyou and Inuktitut for community accounts of James Bay; Haitian Creole and Spanish for community studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the James Bay project and the language-driven departures of anglophones carry rival accounts; give each as an account. "Entrepreneurial" regional labels are claims to test, not facts.
- **Held for review:** one mobility pointer for ambition was set aside under the standing rule: work by scholars of South Asian heritage (category only).
