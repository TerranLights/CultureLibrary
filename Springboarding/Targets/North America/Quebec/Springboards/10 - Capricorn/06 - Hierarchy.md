# Quebec: Hierarchy (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (including *joual*), Quebec English, W8banaki, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Italian, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the ordering of people, groups, places or beings into higher and lower ranks, with differences of power, honor and access.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's West Island and East End: check whether address forms and workplace language mark rank differently in each.
- Company towns of the Mauricie, the Saguenay and the Côte-Nord: compare memoirs from managers' and workers' neighborhoods in the same town.
- Anglophones of Westmount and of the Gaspé coast: compare two English-speaking groups at opposite ends of the income scale.
- Eeyou of Eastmain and Waskaganish: check community histories of the trading-post era against the company's journals.
- W8banaki of Odanak and Wôlinak and Wendat of Wendake: compare how each nation discusses status registration and membership.
- Inuit of Kuujjuaq and of smaller villages: check whether the regional center and the villages describe southern staff differently.
- Italian, Portuguese and Greek Montrealers: check each community's own histories of labor agents, foremen and owners.
- Ashkenazi and Moroccan Sephardic Montrealers: compare how each community describes its own institutions' leadership.
- Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check credential and job-grade studies, never a single community's data alone.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for church, commission and census sources; mixed for lived rank at work; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik sources on status beyond their own sites.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century English for HBC journals; Eeyou (James Bay Cree) and Inuktitut for community histories; Italian for early immigrant sources; French for commissions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the "ethnic class" and language-of-work accounts are contested; give each as an account. Federal status registration and band membership are sensitive within communities. The welfare slur is recorded, never used as a label.
- **Held for review:** the class, status and deprivation sources considered for hierarchy raised no standing-rule concern, so none was held.
