# Quebec: Merit (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French, Quebec English, Wendat (in revitalization) and Innu-aimun for colonial-era terms, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** deserving reward, rank or praise because of one's own effort, ability and results, rather than birth, connections or luck.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and the regions (Rimouski, Rouyn-Noranda, Saguenay): check how far the cote R and private schools shape access in each.
- Quebec City and Sherbrooke: compare school-award practices in public and private schools of each city.
- English-speaking Montrealers: compare McGill and Concordia admissions history with francophone universities'.
- Ashkenazi and Sephardic Montrealers: check each community's account of admission barriers and later mobility.
- Eeyou, Innu and Atikamekw students: compare post-secondary funding rules community by community.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check what Nunavik students say about southern college admission, in their own publications.
- Haitian, Maghrebi and Latin American newcomers: compare credential-recognition studies across communities.
- Hockey families of the Saguenay, the Beauce and Montreal suburbs: check birth-month patterns in local elite teams.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for admissions, immigration and union records; mixed for school awards, scattered in local press; thin for First Nations funding rules, held by each community.
- **Languages needed:** the Jesuits' French of the 1600s; the administrative French of admission and immigration rules; Eeyou, Innu-aimun and Atikamekw for community sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the admissions quota and immigrant credential debates carry rival accounts; give each as an account. Students' records stay private.
- **Held for review:** of the admissions, school-award and credential pointers gathered for merit, none needed holding back.
