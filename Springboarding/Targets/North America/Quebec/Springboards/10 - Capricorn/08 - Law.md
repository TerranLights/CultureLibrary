# Quebec: Law (Capricorn): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (legal and everyday), Quebec English, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Scottish Gaelic (for nineteenth-century Townships sources), Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the binding rules of a community, and how people relate to them: what law is for, whose it is, whether it is just, and whether to obey it, use it, bend it or avoid it. Here, the spirit of the law; its letter belongs to Libra.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and Quebec City: check whether the ADAJ surveys find different trust in courts in the two cities.
- The Eastern Townships: compare English- and Gaelic-language and French accounts of the Morrison manhunt.
- Rural Abitibi, the Gaspé and the Côte-Nord: check how far courts and police are, and how people settle things meanwhile.
- Eeyou of the coast and inland communities: compare how each applies the Cree Nation's own laws.
- Inuit of Hudson Bay and Ungava Bay villages: check how Saqijuq-type work differs by village.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check what each community publishes on its own laws beside provincial law.
- Muslim Montrealers of several origins and Jewish Montrealers: compare how each community describes religious rules beside civil law, in its own words.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for legal history, codes and court rulings; mixed for everyday legal consciousness, studied mainly through ADAJ-type surveys; thin for Innu, Atikamekw and W8banaki legal orders beyond what each nation publishes.
- **Languages needed:** nineteenth-century legal French and English; Scottish Gaelic for Townships sources; Eeyou and Inuktitut for self-government texts; Arabic for religious-arbitration sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the notwithstanding clause, the secularism law and the religious-tribunals debate are contested ground; present each account as an account. Indigenous legal orders are the nations' to describe. This sheet is the spirit of law; the text of statutes goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** the court, code and self-government pointers weighed for the spirit of law needed no holding back under the standing rules.
