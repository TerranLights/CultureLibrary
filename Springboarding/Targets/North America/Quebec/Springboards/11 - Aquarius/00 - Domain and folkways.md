# Quebec: The domain: community and ideals (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and regional speech), the Acadian French of the Magdalen Islands, Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Mi'gmaq, Haitian Creole, Italian. **Concept in one line:** what binds people into groups, and what do they hope for: friends, associations, humanitarian ideals, settlement and neighboring, and the future.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's Plateau beside the West Island: check whether people join through the same kinds of groups (cooperatives, sports clubs, churches).
- Saguenay and Beauce towns: compare how their associations began (parish, cooperative, family firm), through anniversary histories.
- The Magdalen Islands and the Gaspé coast: check how fishing-village gatherings are recorded beside farm parishes'.
- English-speaking Quebecers of the Townships and the Gaspé: compare lodges, Women's Institutes and church halls with francophone ones nearby.
- Innu of the Côte-Nord and Naskapi of Kawawachikamach: check each nation's sources on seasonal gatherings.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Kanehsatà:ke: check what each publishes on clan and longhouse beside elected councils.
- Inuit of Nunavik: compare community-wide events village by village.
- Haitian, Italian, Lebanese and Vietnamese Montrealers: check how associations differ by arrival period.
- Hasidic, Moroccan Sephardic and older Ashkenazi Montrealers: compare the institutions each built.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for associations and cooperatives; mixed for friendship and hope, often surveyed only for Canada; thin for Wolastoqiyik, W8banaki and Naskapi sources. No lands are yet settled in the tracker, so Quebec's exact lands remain open; the sheets of this sign write for the present province, pointing to Labrador, the Maritimes and Franco-Ontario only to compare.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the founders; Italian for club notes; Innu-aimun and Kanien'kéha for each nation's terms of belonging.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** interculturalism, the referendums and the secularism debate are contested; present each as an account. Clan and ceremonial knowledge belongs to the nations that hold it.
