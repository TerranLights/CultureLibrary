# Quebec: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with the speech of the Gaspé and the Bas-Saint-Laurent), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Wendat in revitalization, Portuguese, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Gaspé fishing villages and Bas-Saint-Laurent farm parishes: check how each told the threat of closure, through local and parish histories.
- Côte-Nord company towns (Gagnon, Fermont, Port-Cartier): compare residents' accounts of a town built for a mine or a mill.
- Pointe-Saint-Charles and Milton-Parc: check how each neighborhood's own organizations define who belongs.
- English-speaking Quebecers of the Pontiac and the Lower North Shore: compare community organizations run in English where francophone ones dominate.
- Wendat of Wendake: check how council and family histories record the community's moves.
- Nunavimmiut of the Hudson Bay villages and of Ungava Bay: compare how each village records its feasts and its sharing out of food.
- Eeyou inland and coastal communities: check which community events each Cree Nation government lists.
- Azorean and mainland Portuguese Montrealers: compare parish and club membership.
- Côte-Saint-Luc, Hampstead and Outremont households: check which Jewish institutions each district supports.
- Vietnamese Quebecers of Côte-des-Neiges and Brossard: compare how associations formed after the 1979 arrivals.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Montreal's community sector and the Eastern Quebec closures; mixed for disaster recovery, where reports exist but follow-up is uneven; thin for Naskapi, Wolastoqiyik and W8banaki community records beyond their own sites.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for the Confrérie; Portuguese for parish bulletins; Inuktitut and Eeyou for northern village records.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** grief in Lac-Mégantic is recent; use published surveys and community-chosen accounts. The Wendat Nation keeps its own history and decides what is shared. The legal form of northern villages belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** of the parish, cooperative and disaster sources considered for community, none fell under the standing rules.
