# Quebec: Group (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Innu-aimun, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Haitian Creole, Spanish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a set of people who see themselves, or are seen by others, as a unit; here, how a culture weighs the group against the person, draws the line between "us" and "them", and expects members to act together.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- CEGEP and university students in Montreal, Sherbrooke and Rimouski: check whether strike votes and initiation rites differ by campus.
- Union members of the Côte-Nord mines and the Mauricie mills: compare how locals recorded strike solidarity and those who crossed the line.
- English-speaking voters of the West Island, Pontiac and Townships: check how often they vote as a bloc and for whom.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check what each community says of clan and family when work crews and teams are formed.
- Innu, Wendat and Anishinaabeg: check each nation's own memory of seventeenth-century alliances.
- Nunavimmiut of the larger and smaller villages: compare the 2011 vote by community.
- Chilean, Salvadoran and Colombian Montrealers: check how political exile shaped each group's associations.
- Haitian youth groups in Rivière-des-Prairies and Laval: check community organizations' accounts of gang prevention against police accounts.
- Jewish youth movements of the interwar decades and today's day-school networks: compare what each asks of members.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for unions, elections and student movements; mixed for street gangs, where sources are partial and often police-led; thin for Abitibi and Côte-Nord group life outside unions.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Champlain; Spanish for exile associations; Yiddish for interwar youth-movement papers; Inuktitut for referendum materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the 1970 arrests, the 2012 strike and the referendums are contested; present each as an account. Gang research involves minors and living people. Kahnawà:ke membership rules are a community matter, and their letter belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** among the union, gang and youth-movement pointers weighed for group life, none needed holding back under the standing rules.
