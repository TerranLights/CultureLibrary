# Quebec: Equality (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, Yiddish, Vietnamese, Maghrebi and standard Arabic. **Concept in one line:** the idea that people are of equal worth or standing and should be treated alike in some respects; which respects, and among whom, is where cultures differ.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's francophone east end and the old Square Mile: check how each district's histories describe the income gap between language groups.
- Saguenay, Abitibi and Côte-Nord towns: compare how regional coalitions framed equality with the cities.
- English-speaking Quebecers after 1977: check whether their own groups describe a reversal of standing, and how others answered.
- Innu women of the Côte-Nord: check their own accounts against Leacock's thesis.
- Nunavimmiut: compare survey gaps village by village, not only Nunavik as a whole.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check what community sources say about women's equal standing under clan teaching and under federal rules.
- Jewish Montrealers of the interwar decades: compare the quota years with the founding of Jewish hospitals and schools.
- Black Montrealers of Little Burgundy and Haitian Montrealers: separate the older railway-era community from later arrivals in any equality record.
- Vietnamese and Maghrebi Quebecers: check how each community's press reports hiring and housing barriers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for income, language and sex gaps; mixed for ethnic discrimination, where surveys are national and samples small; thin for Wolastoqiyik, W8banaki and Naskapi equality sources beyond their own publications.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for Bougainville; Yiddish for interwar community press; Inuktitut for Nunavik survey reports; Arabic and Vietnamese for community media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "two founding peoples" erases First Nations and Inuit in many readings, and the language laws are contested; give each as an account. Equality law (charters, pay equity) is for Libra.
- **Held for review:** for equality, the survey, cooperative and movement sources considered here gave no reason under the standing rules to set any aside.
