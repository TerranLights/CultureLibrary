# Quebec: Reform (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (nineteenth-century political French included), Quebec English, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, Haitian Creole, Yiddish and Hebrew. **Concept in one line:** deliberate change meant to improve a society's institutions, practices or people, short of overturning the whole; and the figure of the reformer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Lower Canadian parishes of the Richelieu valley and the Quebec City region: check how each answered the reform and rebellion choices of the 1830s-1840s.
- Montreal's working-class wards and the suburbs: compare which municipal reform parties won where.
- English-speaking Quebecers of Westmount and the Townships: check which reform causes their women's councils and churches carried.
- Eeyou of Chisasibi, Mistissini and Waswanipi: compare what each community's graduates and elders say the Cree School Board changed.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check Kativik board histories against families' own accounts.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check how community-run schooling is told as reform by the community's own institutions.
- Residential-school survivors from Quebec schools: their testimony comes first when a reform was imposed.
- Reform, Orthodox and Hasidic Jewish Montrealers: compare what "reform" means in each.
- Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check which community campaigns sought reforms, through their own archives.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1830s-1840s politics, the Quiet Revolution and Montreal reform; mixed for health reforms, where accounts are partisan; thin for Naskapi, W8banaki and Wolastoqiyik reform sources beyond their own sites.
- **Languages needed:** nineteenth-century political French for the *Résolutions* and LaFontaine; Inuktitut and Eeyou for school-board records; Hebrew and Yiddish for congregational papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** residential schools were presented as reform; survivors' testimony comes first and some is restricted. The Quiet Revolution's account of the *Grande Noirceur* is contested; give it as an account. Statutes of reform go to Libra; what law is for, to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** the religious, municipal and school-reform pointers weighed for reform were all within the standing rules; nothing was set aside.
