# Quebec: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Spanish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality; it includes visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Back-to-the-land settlers of the Bas-Saint-Laurent, the Gaspé and the Townships in the 1970s: check how their memoirs and local histories describe arrival and departure.
- Former Marxist-Leninist activists in Montreal and Quebec City: compare memoirs with Warren's account.
- English-speaking Montrealers of the 1960s: check whether counterculture ideals ran through different networks than francophone ones.
- Innu readers in Ekuanitshit, Unamen Shipu and Pessamit: compare Kapesh's vision with later Innu writers' visions of the land.
- Anishinaabeg of Pikogan, Kitcisakik and Kitigan Zibi: check how each community states *mino-bimaadiziwin* in its own materials.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check community responses to Kateri Tekakwitha in fiction and in devotion.
- Nunavimmiut: compare Plan Nunavik's goals with village councils' own priorities.
- Haitian, Chilean and Salvadoran Montrealers: check how exile politics shaped ideals for the home country.
- Laurentian Jewish farm families and Montreal's Yiddish left: compare ideals of land and of labor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for 1960s-1970s movements and literature; mixed for volunteering abroad; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and W8banaki statements of ideals in print.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Sillery; Innu-aimun for Kapesh; Yiddish for farm-colony papers; Spanish for exile groups.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** clerical-nationalist ideals, the *Grande Noirceur* and the 1970s left are contested; give each as an account. Missionary idealism must be read with the receiving peoples' voices first.
- **Held for review:** across the movement, literature and mission sources weighed for idealism, nothing met the standing rules for setting aside.
