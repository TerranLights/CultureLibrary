# Quebec: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Italian, Portuguese, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Men in Saguenay and Abitibi hunting and fishing camps: check how friendships formed there are described in local writing and outfitters' records.
- Montreal and Quebec City office workers: compare *5 à 7* customs and who is left out of them.
- Newcomers in Quebec City, Sherbrooke and Rimouski: check how their accounts of making friends differ from Montreal's.
- English-speaking Montrealers and Townships Anglophones: check cross-language friendship in school and work memoirs.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne: check what each community's teachers say of paired friendships, set against Lafitau's report.
- Eeyou and Nunavimmiut youth: check how regional programs describe peer friendship in small communities.
- Italian and Portuguese families: compare how co-parent choices changed from the first generation to the third.
- Haitian Montrealers: check whether *konpè* and *kòmè* ties are kept in Montreal parishes and households.
- Jewish Montrealers of Côte-Saint-Luc and the Plateau's older Yiddish milieu: compare friendship institutions across eras.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for films, time-use and loneliness surveys; mixed for ritual friendship among immigrants; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik friendship terms and customs in print.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for Lafitau; Italian, Portuguese and Haitian Creole for co-parent studies; Inuktitut and Eeyou for youth programs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Haudenosaunee teachings on friendship belong to the nations that keep them; youth peer-support work in Nunavik touches suicide prevention and must use published, community-approved material only.
- **Held for review:** the film, survey and co-parenthood pointers weighed for friendship brought nothing under the standing rules, so none was held.
