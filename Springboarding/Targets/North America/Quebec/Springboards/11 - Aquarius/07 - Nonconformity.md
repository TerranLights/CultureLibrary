# Quebec: Nonconformity (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual* and Saguenay and Beauce speech), Quebec English, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Haitian Creole, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** departing from a group's norms of belief, dress, conduct or way of life, by choice or by nature; and how far the culture tolerates, punishes or prizes the departure.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Saguenay and Beauce youth scenes against Montreal's: check whether metal, punk and festival crowds met different local reactions.
- Mile End, the Plateau and Hochelaga artists: compare how each district's artists describe rents and their neighbors.
- Rimouski and Gaspé readers of alternative papers: check what counts as dissent far from the metropolis.
- English-speaking Montrealers in the zine and indie-music worlds: compare their accounts of crossing into French-language scenes.
- Two-Spirit people of Kahnawà:ke, Wendake and Mashteuiatsh: only their own published words, never outsiders' labels.
- Inuit women of Nunavik: check who leads the tattoo revival and how elders speak of it.
- Innu and Atikamekw youth: check how community radio and festivals carry music that breaks with older forms.
- Haitian and Maghrebi Montrealers: check how young people describe pressure from family and from the wider society at once.
- Hasidic Montrealers who leave their communities: check published memoirs and support groups, with care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for the arts, Pride and the 1960s-1970s counterculture; mixed for regional scenes, mostly in press and fan sources; thin for Naskapi, W8banaki and Wolastoqiyik views on difference in print.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Duchesneau; Gauvreau's invented "exploréen" language; Inuktitut for the tattoo revival; Yiddish for leavers' sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Two-Spirit teachings are the nations' to share; gender and bullying data involve minors; people who leave closed religious communities may be at risk if named.
- **Held for review:** for nonconformity, the counterculture, Pride and youth-survey sources weighed here held nothing that the standing rules would set aside.
