# Quebec: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Eeyou (James Bay Cree), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes; cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal's single-person households and those of rural parishes: check who lives alone in each and why, through census and local studies.
- Young voters in Montreal and in the Bas-Saint-Laurent: compare turnout studies and the reasons given.
- English-speaking Montrealers and francophone fans: check how each press described a calm goalie or player.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke, Kanehsatà:ke and Akwesasne: check what each community writes about restraint and non-interference.
- Eeyou families: check how Cree health and school materials describe a child's freedom to learn by watching.
- Inuit hunters of Nunavik: compare how calm on the ice is taught, from Inuit-run sources.
- Abitibi and Côte-Nord mining families: compare fly-in schedules' effects as spouses and workers tell them.
- Haitian and Vietnamese Montrealers: check how each community's writers describe the reserve they met on arrival.
- Hasidic and secular Jewish Montrealers: compare what each counts as proper composure in mourning and public life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for living alone, turnout and professional training; mixed for emotional style, where studies are few and samples small; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Mi'gmaq sources on restraint.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French and Latin for Jogues; Kanien'kéha and Eeyou for teachings on restraint; Haitian Creole and Vietnamese for arrival memoirs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Jogues's death is told as martyrdom in Catholic sources and differently by Kanien'kehá:ka; give each as an account. Isolation of elders and burnout involve living people. Impartiality as a fairness norm belongs to Libra.
- **Held for review:** a meditation-center pointer for non-Christian contemplative practice was set aside under the standing rules, kept as a category only: Buddhist communities in Quebec's lands.
