# Quebec: Hope (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with the Acadian French of the Magdalen Islands), Quebec English, Innu-aimun, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Vietnamese, Spanish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line:** wanting a good that is uncertain, and believing or acting as though it can come; for oneself, one's family, one's people or humanity.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and Montreal seafaring families of the past: check what sailors' chapels and ex-votos record in each port.
- Young climate marchers of Montreal and of regional CEGEPs (Rimouski, Jonquière, Rouyn-Noranda): compare signs and statements by region.
- Supporters of both sides in 1980 and 1995: check how each side's memoirs speak of hope after the vote.
- English-speaking Quebecers of the West Island: check how their press marked the 2020 rainbows and the 1980 vote.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam: compare Fontaine's novel with community writing on schooling.
- Inuit of Inukjuak and other Hudson Bay villages: check how land programs are described by the men who run them.
- Vietnamese and Cambodian Quebecers: check how arrivals of 1979-1980 tell their hopes, and what sponsors' parish records say.
- Haitian and Latin American Montrealers: check what churches and community radio say in times of disaster at home.
- Older Catholic francophones and younger secular ones: compare who still posts thanks to saints.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for songs, slogans and church practice; mixed for measured hope, often surveyed only at Canada level; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik sources on hope in print.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for chapel records; Vietnamese for refugee memoirs; Innu-aimun and Inuktitut for community programs; Haitian Creole for church media.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the referendums are contested; present each side's account as an account. Families of missing children and youth programs in Nunavik must be approached through published, consented material only.
- **Held for review:** the shrine, survey and refugee-sponsorship sources weighed for hope gave no ground under the standing rules for holding any of them back.
