## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Innu of Pessamit and Uashat mak Mani-utenam: check what Innu writers say outsiders fail to grasp.
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Akwesasne and Kahnawà:ke: compare their own histories of adoption with captivity narratives.
- Inuit in Montreal for health care: look at what patients and escorts report about being understood in hospitals.
- Yiddish- and French-speaking Montrealers: check translation projects that tried to carry one community's voice to the other.
- Greek, Italian and Portuguese Montrealers: compare how each community's press frames understanding across generations.
- Saguenay and Outaouais families: check whether empathy programs reach regional schools as in Montreal.
- English-speaking Quebecers in the Gaspé: compare local accounts of feeling understood, or not, by Quebec City.
- Haitian Montrealers: check how community health workers describe bridging clinic and family.
- Atikamekw patients at Joliette and Wendat families at Wendake: compare accounts of being listened to in care.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health-care training, school programs and literature; mixed for everyday empathic speech; thin for Naskapi, Wolastoqiyik and Mi'gmaq sources on reading others' minds.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century English and French for Radisson; Yiddish for the theater archive; Innu-aimun and Inuktitut to find each people's own terms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** inquiry testimony is traumatic; autism research must give autistic people's own accounts first; Radisson's captivity is told differently by Kanien'kehá:ka. Trust goes to Scorpio.
- **Held for review:** the theater, school and clinical-training leads on perspective-taking were all usable under the rules; none needed holding.
