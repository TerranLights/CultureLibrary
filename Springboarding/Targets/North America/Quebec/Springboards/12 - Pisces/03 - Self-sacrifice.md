# Quebec: Self-sacrifice (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Innu-aimun, Wendat, Haitian Creole, Yiddish, Vietnamese, Italian, Portuguese, Arabic. **Concept in one line:** giving up one's own interests, comfort, health or life for another person, a group, a cause or God.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke: check how the community and the shrine each tell Tekakwitha's penances.
- Wendat of Wendake: compare Wendat historians' accounts of 1649 with the Jesuit martyr cult.
- Innu of Uashat mak Mani-utenam: check Innu responses to *Ashini* and to other settler novels of Innu life.
- Beauce towns and Montreal's English-speaking regiments: compare how each remembers war dead.
- Saguenay and Mauricie caregivers: look at regional caregiver groups' reports for unequal burdens.
- Haitian Montrealers: compare Catholic and Protestant women's groups' accounts of the *poto mitan*.
- Vietnamese and Lebanese Montrealers: check memoirs of parents who left professions behind.
- Hasidic Montrealers (Outremont, the Tash community at Boisbriand): set inside teaching on giving one's life for the faith against how French-language press reports it.
- Magdalen Islanders and Gaspé fishermen's families: check what is said of those lost at sea for a family's living.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for martyr cults, regimental and missionary archives; mixed for caregiving and parents' sacrifice; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik sources on giving for the people.
- **Languages needed:** the Latin and French of a 1690s hagiography; Hasidic Yiddish and Hebrew for *mesires-nefesh*; Haitian Creole for the *poto mitan* literature.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** martyrdom stories cast Wendat and Haudenosaunee as killers in Catholic accounts; give each as an account. Hunger strikes and caregiving involve living people. Duty goes to Capricorn; courage to Aries.
- **Held for review:** no missionary, regimental or martyr source had to be held back under the rules.

## 5. First queries
1. Find Chauchetière's *Vie* (manuscript or edition) and read Greer's chapter on it.

5. Look up "poto mitan" on Érudit and in Haitian community newspapers, and note who uses the phrase as praise and who as critique.
