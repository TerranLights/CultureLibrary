## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Westmount, Baie-D'Urfé and Saint-Bruno: compare demerger campaigners' language with those who accepted merger.
- Anglophone and allophone Montrealers: check census language-transfer rows by origin before reading any trend.
- Atikamekw, Kanien'kehá:ka and Anishinaabeg of Kitigan Zibi: compare language transmission figures and each nation's account of why they differ.
- Irish-descended families of Quebec City and the Beauce: check how each tells its absorption into French-speaking parishes.
- Haitian Pentecostal and Catholic congregations: compare how ecstatic worship is welcomed or kept out.
- Hasidic and Moroccan Sephardic Montrealers: compare devotional gatherings, from each community's own sources.
- Mauricie and Saguenay dementia carers: compare home care with alternative centers.
- Religious women of Montreal's orders: check how members describe the closing of their houses.
- Young ravers of the 1990s and today's festival crowds: compare press framing across decades.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for mergers, language and demography; mixed for ecstatic religious and dance experience; thin for Wolastoqiyik and Naskapi sources on language loss.
- **Languages needed:** seventeenth-century French for Colbert's letters; Haitian Creole, Hasidic Yiddish and Maghrebi Arabic for ecstatic practice in members' words.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Allan Memorial experiments harmed living people and are before the courts; demolinguistic figures are argued over in the language debate (Bill 101, Bill 96), so give each reading as an account; Vodou and Sufi practice must be described by practitioners.
