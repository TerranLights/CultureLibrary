## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Stanstead and other Townships border villages: compare how English- and French-speaking residents describe crossing.
- Hasidic Outremont and its francophone and Anglophone neighbors: read each side's statements in the window and worship disputes.
- Mi'gmaq of Listuguj and Gesgapegiag: check how each community speaks of provincial and federal lines through Gespe'gewa'gi.
- Wolastoqiyik and W8banaki: compare how small nations with scattered members define who belongs where.
- Naskapi of Kawawachikamach and Innu of Matimekush: check each nation's own sources on shared and separate ground.
- Gaspé Anglophone villages (New Carlisle, Gaspé town) and francophone neighbors: compare cemeteries, schools and churches kept apart.
- Italian, Vietnamese and Haitian Montrealers: check how family privacy is discussed in each community's press.
- Young and older Quebecers: compare greeting kisses and touch by generation before reading any regional pattern.
- Suburban Laval and Plateau renters: check how hedges, fences and shared stairs are argued over.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disputes, school curricula and press; mixed for touch and personal space; thin for Naskapi and Wolastoqey sources, which need the nations' own offices.
- **Languages needed:** early modern French for Champlain's legends; Mi'gmaq and Wolastoqey for land names; Yiddish for modesty norms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the Hasidic disputes and Bill 21 are contested ground (Bill 21 goes to Libra and Capricorn as law); #MoiAussi names living people; Indigenous territory is each nation's to describe, and no border is settled here.
- **Held for review:** neither the border, worship-zoning nor consent leads tripped a standing rule; nothing was held.
