# Quebec: Surrender (Pisces): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Kanien'kéha, Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Italian, Spanish, Arabic, Vietnamese. **Concept in one line:** ceasing to resist; giving oneself, one's will or one's control over to something stronger or higher: God, fate, a lover, an enemy, illness, death; release and letting go too.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Quebec City and Montreal: compare how each city's histories tell 1759 and 1760.
- People who were behind the Kanehsatà:ke barricades and soldiers who faced them: find each group's later testimony on the night the standoff ended and the words chosen for it.
- Inuit of Nunavik: check whether elders' sayings of acceptance are used in health and youth programs.
- Haitian Montrealers: compare how pastors and Vodou practitioners speak of accepting misfortune.
- Maghrebi and Lebanese Montrealers: check what *maktūb* is used for in community radio and press.
- Bas-Saint-Laurent and Abitibi farm families: compare farm-succession data and families' own accounts.
- Montérégie and Gaspé palliative homes: check whether volunteers' accounts of acceptance differ by region.
- Montreal veterans' families: compare francophone and Anglophone memory of capture at Dieppe and Hong Kong.
- Older Italian and Portuguese Montrealers: look at how moving into a residence is discussed in community papers.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war history, school and gambling data; mixed for spiritual abandonment and end-of-life acceptance; thin for Atikamekw, Wolastoqiyik and Naskapi accounts of giving up land or ways of life.
- **Languages needed:** eighteenth-century French for Ramezay; Inuktitut for elders' interviews; Arabic and Vietnamese for sayings of fate.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** 1990 is contested ground; present each account as an account. Medical aid in dying is law (Libra) and an end-of-life matter (Scorpio). Gambling and recovery involve private people. Outsiders' labels of "fatalism" for any group are claims to record, never adopt.
- **Held for review:** the war, palliative and recovery pointers for this concept raised no rule; nothing was set aside.
