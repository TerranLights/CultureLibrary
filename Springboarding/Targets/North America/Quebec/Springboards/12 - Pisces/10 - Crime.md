*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** Quebec, region North America. **Language(s) for searching:** Quebec French (with *joual*), Quebec English, Anishinaabemowin (Algonquin), Inuktitut of Nunavik, Haitian Creole, Spanish, Yiddish. **Concept in one line** (named by the author): what a culture counts as a serious wrong against the community, deserving public condemnation and punishment, and how it sees those who commit it. Law itself is researched under Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit); this sheet takes the idea of wrongdoing and the wrongdoer.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Montreal and the Laurentides: compare 1899 press coverage of the Viau case in city and rural papers.
- Anishinaabeg of Lac-Simon and Kitcisakik and Eeyou in Val-d'Or: check each community's own statements after 2015.
- Inuit of Kuujjuaq, Puvirnituq and Salluit: set each village's justice committee reports beside regional police summaries, without ranking the villages.
- Black Montrealers and historians of New France: check how Angélique is remembered in each community's events.
- Lac-Mégantic residents and railway workers: compare accounts of who bore the blame.
- Abitibi and Côte-Nord hunters: check how poaching is talked about at home against in wardens' reports.
- Montréal-Nord residents of Haitian and Latin American origin: look at community groups' own words on policing and wrongdoing.
- Hasidic and other Jewish Montrealers: compare community responses when a member is accused, from their own press.
- Quebec City and Montreal true-crime audiences: check which local cases each city's media returns to.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for police statistics, famous cases and press; mixed for everyday tolerated wrongs; thin for Naskapi, Atikamekw and W8banaki views of wrongdoing, which belong to those nations.
- **Languages needed:** the court French of 1734 for Angélique's interrogations; Anishinaabemowin and Inuktitut for words of harm and repair; Haitian Creole and Spanish for Montréal-Nord community radio.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Val-d'Or allegations and the Montréal-Nord events are contested; give each as an account. Victims and accused are living people. No group is to be linked with a kind of crime. The letter of criminal law goes to Libra; its spirit to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** in weighing victim, trial and folk-song leads on wrongdoing, none met a standing rule that would hold it back.
