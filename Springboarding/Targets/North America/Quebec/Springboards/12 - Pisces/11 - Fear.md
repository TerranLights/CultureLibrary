## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- Innu of Pessamit and Mashteuiatsh and Eeyou of Waswanipi: compare how each nation publishes its cannibal-being tales, and for whom.
- Inuit of Nunavik's coastal villages: check whether *qalupalik* is taught in schools and by elders alike.
- Saguenay and Charlevoix residents: compare fear of flood, landslide and quake in local memory.
- Mi'gmaq of Listuguj and Gesgapegiag: look at community responses to Barnaby's films.
- Montreal women of different neighborhoods: compare the 1990s safety audits with recent ones.
- Rural parishioners born before and after 1960: check memories of mission sermons by age.
- Haitian, Maghrebi and Jewish Montrealers: compare fear after attacks on their communities, from their own organizations.
- Anglophone Montrealers who stayed after 1976 and those who left: compare later accounts of worry over language laws (Bill 101, Bill 96), as accounts.
- Teen respondents in Montreal, the Outaouais and Abitibi: check EQSJS anxiety items by region before naming any pattern.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for disasters, horror film and youth surveys; mixed for fear of God and collective fear; thin for Atikamekw, Naskapi and Wolastoqiyik fear figures, which only those nations can share.
- **Languages needed:** Innu-aimun and Eeyou for the forest tales; Inuktitut for the sea-ice tale; Haitian Creole and Yiddish for night fears.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the cannibal-being stories carry teachings some nations keep for their own; outsiders' psychiatric use of "windigo" has harmed. The language laws and the fear-of-disappearing debate are contested ground; give each reading as an account. Attacks on communities are grieving ground. Crime is concept 10; courage and recklessness belong to Aries.
- **Held for review:** nothing was set aside; the films, hazard maps and fright tales gathered for fear all sit within the rules.
