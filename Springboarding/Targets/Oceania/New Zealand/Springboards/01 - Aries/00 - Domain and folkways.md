# New Zealand: The domain: self and identity (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (New Zealand English), te reo Māori, New Zealand Sign Language; Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Tokelauan, Mandarin, Cantonese and Korean for the larger communities; Moriori (ta re Moriori) for Rēkohu. **Concept in one line:** the domain of the self and identity: what a self is in this place, where it is located, how it is named, embodied and presented, and how it asserts itself.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi, separately rather than as "Māori":** Ngāpuhi, Waikato-Tainui, Ngāti Porou, Ngāi Tūhoe, Te Arawa, Ngāi Tahu (Kāi Tahu), the Whanganui river iwi; each places the self in its own landscape and descent line.
- **Urban Māori without strong marae ties:** identity built through urban authorities and schools, not a home marae.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu (Chatham Islands):** a separate people whose self-naming was long denied.
- **Pākehā by region:** Otago and Southland (Scottish settlement), Canterbury, the West Coast; regional self-images may differ.
- **Pacific peoples:** Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Tokelauan, Fijian, Tuvaluan; split New Zealand-born from island-born.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** old Cantonese families from the gold-field era against recent Mandarin-speaking arrivals.
- **Korean, Filipino, Dutch and Dalmatian-descended communities:** own naming and dress practice.
- **Deaf New Zealanders; takatāpui and Pacific gender-diverse people (fa'afafine, fakaleiti):** identities that cut across ethnicity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich (bilingual official sources, Māori and Pacific scholarship, panels); thinner for small migrant groups and Moriori.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (with dialects), Samoan and Tongan at least; Mandarin, Cantonese and Korean for those communities.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whakapapa is often tapu and held by iwi experts; tā moko and Pacific tattoo designs have owners; toi moko (preserved heads) are a repatriation matter, not a research object; approach Moriori history through the Trust.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the Aries set is written for the core lands (Te Ika-a-Māui, Te Waipounamu, Rakiura, Rēkohu).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. In Te Ara, read the entries on whakapapa, tā moko and personal names; list their sources.
2. In Stats NZ, find the census ethnicity and iwi-affiliation question wording for each census since 1991, plus Te Kupenga's identity items.
3. Search the NZAVS publication list for "ethnic identity", "national identity" and "self".
4. In Papers Past, search the Māori newspapers for "pepeha"; in the English papers, search "New Zealander" with "type" for early self-labels.
5. Pull the Births, Deaths and Marriages name lists for 2000 to the present and mark the Māori and Pacific names.
