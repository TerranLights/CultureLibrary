# New Zealand: Self-assertion (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan; Mandarin and Korean for newer communities; NZSL. **Concept in one line:** declaring and acting on one's own existence, wishes and claims in the presence of others.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi kawa (marae custom):** speaking order and who may stand differ between iwi; compare a Te Arawa marae with a Ngāti Porou one and a Ngāi Tahu one before generalizing.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** rank and age set who may speak in the fono or family meeting; New Zealand-born children meet a school that grades "participation".
- **Taranaki iwi (Te Āti Awa, Taranaki, Ngāti Ruanui):** the Parihaka inheritance shapes how claims are pressed.
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei:** the Bastion Point experience of asserting a claim in the largest city.
- **Pākehā rural against urban:** farm districts and the cities may draw the line between "straight talk" and "skiting" in different places.
- **Unionized workforces** (meat works, wharves, nursing): assertion as a group, against individual negotiation.
- **Chinese and Korean students and parents:** classroom speaking norms that may clash with school expectations.
- **The Deaf community:** the push for NZSL as a language of its own, a collective claim to be heard.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed to rich. Strong sociolinguistic and protest-history sources; little direct survey work on complaining or negotiation.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for whaikōrero and whakataukī; Samoan and Tongan for church and family speech.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** marae kawa is set by each marae and may not be recorded; occupations and police raids remain painful for the families involved.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
