## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāi Tūhoe:** the clearest case of an iwi seeking to manage its own land and people.
- **Waikato-Tainui and the Kīngitanga:** autonomy organized around a monarch since the 1850s, a different model from Tūhoe's.
- **Rēkohu (Chatham Islands):** Moriori and the island community's distance from mainland control.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** career, marriage and church choices made with the 'āiga or kāinga, especially for New Zealand-born children.
- **Chinese and Korean families:** study and career choices weighed with parents; second generation against first.
- **Rural Pākehā farmers:** autonomy on the land against regulation, the "nanny state" debate.
- **Disabled New Zealanders:** self-direction in care.
- **High-control religious groups** (Gloriavale on the West Coast, for example): the far end, where members' choices are tightly held.
- **Young people in Auckland against small towns:** how freely they move and decide.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for collective Māori autonomy and youth surveys; thinner for adult personal choice and family decision-making outside Pacific studies.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan for family decisions.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** high-control groups involve survivors and court cases; bodily-autonomy debates (abortion, end-of-life choice, vaccination) are divisive; their legal side belongs to Libra and Capricorn, not here.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
