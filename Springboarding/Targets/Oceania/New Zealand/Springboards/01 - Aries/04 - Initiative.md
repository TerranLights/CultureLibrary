## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Porou and the East Coast:** land-development initiative under Ngata, and its later iwi enterprise.
- **Ngāi Tahu and Waikato-Tainui as post-settlement iwi corporations:** initiative through collective investment, a different shape from the lone founder.
- **Canterbury after the earthquakes:** students, farmers and neighbors who acted first; compare suburbs.
- **Rural Pākehā farming districts:** the make-do inventor in farm workshops.
- **Chinese New Zealand families:** market gardens and shops as first businesses.
- **Dalmatian (Croatian) families of Northland and West Auckland:** gum fields to vineyards.
- **Samoan and Tongan churches:** fundraising, building and youth programs started inside congregations.
- **Korean and other recent migrant small-business owners:** self-employment as a route in.
- **Wellington's public servants against Auckland's private sector:** who is expected to act unasked at work.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for volunteering, earthquake response and business statistics; thinner for initiative inside families and workplaces.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan for church records; Chinese and Croatian for older community papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Canterbury testimony involves deaths and trauma; iwi business data can be commercially restricted.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
