## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Māori Battalion companies:** A (Northland), B (Rotorua and Bay of Plenty), C (East Coast), D (the rest, including the South Island); each district kept its own memory.
- **Waikato-Tainui under Te Puea:** resistance to First World War conscription, a courage of refusal that contrasts with the Battalion story.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu:** Nunuku's covenant against killing, which raises the question of courage without fighting.
- **Taranaki iwi:** the Parihaka tradition of facing troops unarmed.
- **Conscientious objectors and Quakers:** moral courage against national war memory.
- **West Coast and Waikato miners:** danger at work, with mine disasters as tests.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Fijian New Zealanders:** war dances before rugby (siva tau, sipi tau, cibi) and service in the armed forces.
- **Women:** wartime nurses, resistance agents and rescue volunteers; whether their courage is told the same way.
- **Rural search-and-rescue volunteers against urban professionals.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war and bravery awards; moral and emotional courage need more digging in oral history and media.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan and Fijian for Pacific sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** war and mine disasters (Pike River, 2010) involve grieving families; some iwi war histories are held locally; haka are owned by iwi and should not be performed or reproduced.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
