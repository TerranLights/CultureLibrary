# New Zealand: Independence (Aries): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori (including the Kāi Tahu dialect), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori; Mandarin and Korean. **Concept in one line:** not depending on others; self-reliance and self-sufficiency, for persons, households and peoples.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāi Tahu:** mahinga kai as a long southern tradition of providing for oneself from land and sea.
- **Northland hapū (Ngāpuhi and neighbors):** home ground of most of He Whakaputanga's signatories; political independence read locally.
- **Rēkohu (Chatham Islands):** a remote island community that must supply much of its own needs.
- **High-country and back-country Pākehā:** station families, deer cullers, possum trappers.
- **West Coast towns:** how households coped after mines and mills closed.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands Māori households:** several earners and remittances, interdependence as normal practice.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** community self-help, and whether it held across generations.
- **Disabled people and their organizations:** independence redefined as chosen support.
- **Young adults in Auckland:** house prices keeping them at home longer than in smaller towns.
- **Cook Islands, Niue and Tokelau:** self-governing or dependent ties to New Zealand; outside the core lands, comparison only.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for welfare history, statistics and Māori food sovereignty; thinner for emotional independence.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (with Kāi Tahu forms); Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands Māori.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the meaning of He Whakaputanga and of tino rangatiratanga is a live political and legal dispute, so record it, do not settle it; mahinga kai sites may be restricted; welfare dependence carries stigma for the people studied.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Read the NZHistory pages on He Whakaputanga and list the signatories' iwi and hapū.
2. Pull census living-arrangement tables for ages 20 to 34 by ethnic group and region.
3. Search Kā Huru Manu for mahinga kai entries in one district, for example Murihiku (Southland).
4. Search the *New Zealand Journal of History* for "self-reliance", "dole" and "pioneer".
5. Search NZ On Screen for documentaries on back-country life and possum trapping.
