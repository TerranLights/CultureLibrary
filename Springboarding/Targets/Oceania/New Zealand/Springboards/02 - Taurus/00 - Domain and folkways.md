# New Zealand: Domain and folkways (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (New Zealand usage), te reo Māori (including the Kāi Tahu dialect), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Chinese (Cantonese and Mandarin). **Concept in one line:** the domain of possessions and value: what is worth having, and how it is held (money, resources, food ways, dress ways, wealth ways).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kāi Tahu / Ngāi Tahu (South Island):** pounamu and mahinga kai; their magazine *Te Karaka* documents both.
- **Waikato-Tainui and Taranaki iwi:** land lost by confiscation, against iwi that lost land by sale.
- **Ngāti Porou (East Coast) and Tūhoe (Te Urewera):** longer-held tribal lands and farming trusts, against urban iwi.
- **Moriori (Rēkohu / Chatham Islands):** a separate people with their own foods and goods.
- **Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands, Niuean and Tokelauan communities:** mats, barkcloth and quilts as ceremonial wealth; remittances home.
- **Chinese New Zealanders (gold-rush Cantonese families vs. recent Mandarin-speaking arrivals):** two histories of trade and property.
- **Otago and Southland Scots Presbyterians vs. West Coast miners vs. Canterbury runholders:** different settler habits of thrift, display and land.
- **Dalmatian (Croatian) families of Northland:** kauri-gum digging, vineyards and close ties with Māori.
- **Auckland vs. provincial and rural towns:** house prices split holdings and worries.
- **Comparison only:** New Zealanders in Australia (diaspora, outside the nation).

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for settler history and spending statistics; mixed for Pacific exchange and Chinese family records; thin for Moriori.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (and older 19th-century spelling without macrons), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Cantonese.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** taonga and mātauranga are held by iwi and hapū, and some knowledge is tapu and not for open sharing; seek iwi consent. Ownership in law (land titles, Treaty settlements, the Wai 262 claim's findings) goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Te Ara: search "food", "clothing", "Māori economy" and "shops"; note each bibliography.
2. Stats NZ: download the latest Household Expenditure Statistics tables by region and ethnicity, and the CPI basket review notes.
3. NZETC: find Best's text with Ranapiri's *hau* statement and read it beside Firth's chapter on exchange.
4. Papers Past: search "auction" and "price list" with decade filters 1860s, 1900s and 1930s.
5. Te Papa collections online: search "korowai"; read how each cloak came to the museum.
