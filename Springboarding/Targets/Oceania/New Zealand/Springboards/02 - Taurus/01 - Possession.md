*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Arawa (Rotorua):** carvers and guides who made objects to sell to visitors since the 19th century; compare objects made for sale with those kept.
- **Northland hapū (Ngāpuhi, Te Rarawa):** heirlooms held by hapū and whānau, against personal belongings.
- **Moriori (Rēkohu):** the carved kōpi trees (rākau momori) mark living things; ask how Moriori sources speak of holding them.
- **Samoan and Tongan families in South Auckland:** fine mats and barkcloth stored now for a future funeral or wedding.
- **Cook Islanders:** quilts that pass between kin at haircutting rites, weddings and burials.
- **Filipino and Korean newcomers:** what is packed, sent home and kept from the old country.
- **Canterbury households after the quakes:** loss and salvage set them apart from the rest of the country.
- **Farm families vs. urban renters:** sheds full of kept gear against renters who move often and keep little.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; rich on grammar, heirloom traditions and quake accounts, thin on everyday keeping and lending.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Cantonese for older family papers.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** taonga tuku iho are held by whānau and hapū and some are tapu; their stories need consent. Ancestral remains are not belongings and stay out of scope. Theft and property in law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
