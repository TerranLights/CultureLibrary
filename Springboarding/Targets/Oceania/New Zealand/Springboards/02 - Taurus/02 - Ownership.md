*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei (Tāmaki Makaurau):** a small land base in the country's richest city, regained after occupation; ownership amid urban land values.
- **Waikato-Tainui and Taranaki iwi:** land taken by confiscation (raupatu), and how "ours" is spoken of after return.
- **Tūhoe (Te Urewera):** Te Urewera as a heartland held as a whole; compare with iwi whose holdings are scattered titles.
- **Moriori (Rēkohu):** their own claim histories after the land changed hands in the 1830s and later; keep them apart from Māori.
- **Pacific families:** titles and land back home held by the extended family, while houses here are owned by couples or rented.
- **Young Aucklanders vs. older owners:** flatting and lifelong renting against mortgage-free homes; a generational split in owning.
- **Farm families (Waikato, Canterbury, high country):** freehold farms and Crown pastoral leases; succession tensions.
- **Gloriavale (West Coast) and other communal groups:** members hold nothing personally; a margin case, handle with care.
- **Chinese, Korean and newer migrant buyers:** home purchase as arrival; the foreign-buyer debate.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on housing, fiscal and co-op data; mixed on Pacific tenure in diaspora; deep Māori sources need iwi-by-iwi work.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (incl. 19th-century land testimony), Samoan, Tongan, Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land loss and Treaty claims are raw and unresolved for many; consult iwi. Land titles, the Māori Land Court, Treaty settlements, foreshore and seabed and the legal status of rivers go to Libra (letter) and Capricorn (spirit).
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Stats NZ: download *Housing in Aotearoa: 2020* and the census tenure tables with the family-trust category.
2. OECD and Stats NZ: pull general government expenditure as a share of GDP since 1970, and local authority spending.
3. ISSP data archive: open the NZ Role of Government files and list the ownership items.
4. Ngā Taonga: search "Bastion Point" and "Land March 1975".
5. Ngāi Tahu site: read three years of annual reports for how members' collective ownership is described.
