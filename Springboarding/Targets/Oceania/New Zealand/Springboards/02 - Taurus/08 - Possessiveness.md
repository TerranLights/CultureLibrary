# New Zealand: Possessiveness (Taurus): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (New Zealand usage), te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Fijian, Chinese. **Concept in one line:** holding on to what one sees as one's own, including people, beyond what the bond or the thing warrants, and the urge to keep others away from it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **West Coast whitebaiters:** check how stands pass within families and are guarded each season; a classic site of "my spot".
- **Surf towns (Raglan/Whāingaroa, Piha, Gisborne):** localism toward outsiders, and the iwi whose shores the breaks are.
- **Provincial rugby supporters:** pride in holding a trophy; compare unions with long and short Shield tenures.
- **Coastal hapū (e.g. in Northland and Tairāwhiti):** closing beaches and kaimoana grounds for the group against outside takers.
- **Fijian, Samoan and Tongan households:** ask how kin claims on goods shape what may be held back; compare by community.
- **Chinese New Zealand families:** inheritance and sibling claims, as families themselves discuss them; compare older and newer arrivals.
- **Young couples (Youth surveys, school programs):** phone checking and location sharing, a new arena.
- **Rural neighbors vs. city neighbors:** boundary fences, water and stock vs. parking and trees.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed; partner control is well surveyed, everyday territoriality lives in local news and forums, and pre-modern sources are story-based.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Fijian, Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** partner control and violence are traumatic; use published survey and program material only. Rāhui are tapu restrictions set by hapū; do not frame them as greed. Fishing and whitebait rules go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Williams's dictionary: look up hae, puhaehae, apo and kaiapo; list the example sentences.
2. Search the NZ Family Violence Survey papers for "controlling behaviours" tables.
3. Orbell's encyclopedia: read "Kae" and "Tinirau", then follow its sources to iwi versions.
4. Papers Past: search "whitebait stand" and "possie" for older disputes.
5. RNZ archive: search "rāhui ignored" for recent cases and iwi statements.
