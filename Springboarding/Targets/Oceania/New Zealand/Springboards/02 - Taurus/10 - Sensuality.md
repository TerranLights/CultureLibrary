## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Arawa (Rotorua):** check how hot water figures as everyday comfort at home rather than as resort luxury.
- **Rakiura Māori and Kāi Tahu tītī harvesters:** muttonbird season and its tastes and smells as seasonal pleasure; ask harvesters directly.
- **Samoan and Tongan churchgoing families:** ask how Sunday feasting and rest sit beside fasting and church discipline; compare denominations.
- **Cook Islanders:** dance and drumming at festivals; compare islands of origin (Rarotonga, Aitutaki, the northern atolls).
- **Latter-day Saint and Seventh-day Adventist communities:** food and drink rules that set aside some pleasures.
- **Invercargill and parts of Dunedin and Auckland once "dry":** local histories of refusing the bar.
- **Wine regions (Marlborough, Central Otago):** taste as an industry and a local identity.
- **Cantonese families and Auckland's Dominion Road:** yum cha and food streets as places of shared enjoyment.
- **Beach and bach culture across Pākehā families:** summer bodily ease in togs and jandals; test class differences.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on food, drink and festivals; mixed on touch and bodily comfort; Pacific material sits mostly in theses and community media.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rongoā knowledge can be tapu or held by particular practitioners; ask before recording. Sex and sexual health go to Scorpio. Liquor licensing law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
