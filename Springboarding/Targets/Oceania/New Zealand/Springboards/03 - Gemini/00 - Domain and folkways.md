## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāpuhi (Northland) and Waikato-Tainui:** large iwi, different dialects and marae speech customs.
- **Ngāti Porou (East Coast) and Tūhoe (Te Urewera):** long-kept home use of te reo, against urban Auckland Māori.
- **Kāi Tahu (South Island):** a dialect being rebuilt; a southern language history.
- **Moriori (Rēkohu / Chatham Islands):** a separate people and speech; island-bound short journeys.
- **Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands, Niuean and Tokelauan communities:** separate stories of keeping a language through church and family.
- **Deaf New Zealanders:** a signing community with its own schools and clubs for talk and learning.
- **Southland and Otago vs. the North:** the "Southland burr" and other regional speech.
- **Chinese (old Cantonese families vs. recent Mandarin speakers), Korean and Filipino communities:** weekend language schools and home-language shift.
- **Comparison only:** New Zealanders in Australia, whose children's language and schooling sit outside the nation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for New Zealand English, te reo counts and schooling; mixed for Pacific languages and neighbors; thin for Moriori.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (and older spelling without macrons), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, NZSL, Cantonese and Mandarin.
- **Scope:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; the sheets of this sign are written for the core lands.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whakapapa, tribal oratory and some song texts belong to iwi and hapū; ask before quoting. Language status in law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
