## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Arawa vs. Ngāti Porou:** rules on whether women speak on the paepae are said to differ between iwi; test it hapū by hapū.
- **Waikato-Tainui:** oratory at Kīngitanga gatherings, where speaking turns follow their own protocol.
- **Samoan and Tongan congregations:** honorific registers and orators' roles carried into church and funeral talk in Auckland and Porirua.
- **Deaf NZSL users:** eye contact, touch and turn-taking set by a visual language.
- **Farm and rural service towns vs. Auckland and Wellington offices:** where plain speaking and hedging are expected; check by setting and class, not by stereotype.
- **Chinese and Korean migrants in Auckland workplaces:** studies of directives and small talk across first languages.
- **Young urban Māori and Pacific speakers:** a youth register of slang and particles, spreading beyond its origin.
- **Comparison only:** New Zealanders in Australia, whose talk is heard against Australian English.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for New Zealand English pragmatics and workplace talk; mixed for Pacific oratory in the nation; thin for Chinese family speech and for Moriori speech.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, NZSL; Mandarin and Korean for migrant workplace studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whaikōrero texts and recordings may carry tribal knowledge; ask iwi before use. Free speech and hate speech in law go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
