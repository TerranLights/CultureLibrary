## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi with their own radio stations and newsletters (e.g., Ngāti Kahungunu, Ngāti Porou, Te Arawa):** a news system that runs beside the national one; compare whose word is trusted.
- **Tūhoe:** the 2007 police raids in Te Urewera and their coverage, a test of how a community sets its own account against the media's.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands families:** church notices and Pacific radio as news routes, with older and younger members using different channels.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** a parallel channel in Chinese-language apps; compare trust with English-language media.
- **Rural districts (West Coast, King Country, Northland):** local paper closures and broadband gaps.
- **Chatham Islanders (Moriori and Māori of Rēkohu):** distance and delay in news and records.
- **Deaf New Zealanders:** access to emergency information through captions and NZSL.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for trust in news, internet use and press history; mixed for Pacific and Chinese channels; thin for Moriori and for small-town word of mouth.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (19th-century spelling in the niupepa), Samoan, Tongan, Mandarin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some mātauranga is held by iwi and not for open use; data about Māori falls under the Māori data sovereignty principles. The Official Information Act, privacy law and broadcasting rules go to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
