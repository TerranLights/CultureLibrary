## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Raukawa, Te Āti Awa and Ngāti Toa:** a tribal plan from the 1970s for rebuilding learning and language, leading to their own wānanga.
- **Kāi Tahu:** Kotahi Mano Kāika, a strategy of learning te reo in the home; compare household against school learning.
- **Ngāti Awa:** a tribal wānanga at Whakatāne; tertiary learning on iwi terms.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands families:** Aoga Amata, akoteu and language nests run through churches; learning by service at home and church.
- **High-country and island families:** children taught at home through Te Kura, often by a parent.
- **Deaf learners:** the shift from oral-only teaching to NZSL in the deaf schools of Auckland and Christchurch.
- **Chinese and Korean families in Auckland:** after-school tutoring and weekend language schools.
- **Refugee-background communities (e.g., Somali, Ethiopian, Colombian):** adults and children starting English and school late.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for early literacy, Māori-medium education and statistics; mixed for trades and Pacific early childhood; thin for learning by pitching in at home.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, NZSL; Mandarin and Korean for tutoring studies.
