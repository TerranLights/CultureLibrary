# New Zealand: Curiosity (Gemini): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi whose ancestral remains and taonga were collected:** consent, return and the end of outside curiosity about their dead.
- **Ngāpuhi and Hokianga communities:** Kupe traditions tied to their harbor; exploration stories held locally.
- **Waikato-Tainui voyaging crews:** the revival of ocean wayfinding as active exploring.
- **Moriori:** a people long described by outsiders' guesses; how they now set the questions about themselves.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** whether children may question elders; test by family and church, not by group.
- **Chinese and Korean families in Auckland:** enrichment classes and competitions; test how far they serve curiosity for its own sake, family by family.
- **Canterbury and Otago amateur naturalists and fossil hunters:** a settler tradition of collecting rocks, bones and birds.
- **Rural and coastal communities:** onlookers at strandings and storms; curiosity drawn by nature, not by museums.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for science engagement, museums and research ethics; mixed for children's questioning at home; thin for nosiness as a social sanction.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan for family studies.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kōiwi tangata and some lore are tapu; tribal narratives of knowledge belong to their iwi and some versions are restricted. Research ethics law goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Te Papa: search "Karanga Aotearoa" for the program's history and its repatriation lists.
2. Library: Smith's *Decolonizing Methodologies*, the opening chapters on research and imperialism.
3. Biodiversity Heritage Library: browse the *Transactions* indexes for "moa" and "Maori" in the 1870s.
4. NZETC: search "Tawhaki" and "kete" and compare the versions.
5. Curious Minds: list the community projects funded and the regions they cover.
