## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kāi Tahu:** many members of mixed Māori and early whaler or settler descent; how two lines of descent are spoken of.
- **Ngāti Porou:** home iwi of Ngata, whose saying on two worlds is often quoted; compare its use at home and in national speeches.
- **Moriori and Māori of Rēkohu:** two peoples on one island with a painful shared history.
- **NZ-born and island-born Samoans and Tongans:** labels and teasing that sort people by birthplace and language.
- **Afakasi and other mixed Pacific families:** belonging to two or more groups at once.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** long-settled families and recent arrivals, each with its own sense of being between.
- **Dutch post-war migrants:** families who kept Dutch at home but switched to English outside; quiet two-ness.
- **North Island vs. South Island ("the Mainland"):** a regional pair voiced in sport and jokes.
- **Takatāpui:** identities defined from inside.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for biculturalism, multiple-ethnicity data and Māori philosophy; mixed for Pacific youth identity; thin for Dutch and other European migrant two-ness.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Dutch, Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Māori cosmological knowledge is tapu; teasing labels and old blood-quantum words are hurtful, describe, do not repeat; takatāpui identity is self-defined. The Treaty's legal meaning goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
