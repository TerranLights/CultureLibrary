*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tūhoe and Ngāti Porou against Ngāpuhi:** strong rural home bases against an iwi whose people mostly live away from Northland; land-tied households against city ones.
- **Kāi Tahu (Ngāi Tahu):** a large tribal whakapapa registry and their own dialect change how family records are found.
- **Urban Māori with weak iwi links** (West Auckland's Waipareira networks): whānau built around place and need.
- **Pākehā settlement streams:** Otago Scots Presbyterians, Canterbury Anglicans, West Coast Irish Catholic miners; house plans and family sizes may diverge.
- **Pacific peoples** (Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Tokelauan, Fijian): larger, church-centered households with transnational kin.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** gold-era and market-garden lines against recent arrivals.
- **Northland's Dalmatian-descended families and Dutch postwar migrants:** two European minorities with their own hosting ways.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu (the Chatham Islands):** household history needing separate sources.
- **Farm against city households; Rainbow families:** farm succession; chosen family forms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich. Census, cohort studies and Te Ara are deep; older Māori and Moriori household history is uneven and partly filtered through Pākehā recorders.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (with southern forms), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Chinese; NZSL.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whakapapa is often held within whānau and iwi, not for open use; stories told on marae belong to their owners.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; this sign's sheets are written for the core lands of the North Island, the South Island and their nearby islands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only).

## 5. First queries
1. Pull Stats NZ's 2018 and 2023 census tables on household composition and family type, by ethnic group and region.
2. Read Te Ara's "Whānau" entry, then Metge's *New Growth from Old*, listing every household form named.
3. Search the NZ Electronic Text Collection for Te Rangi Hīroa and John White with "whare", "kāinga", "whānau".
4. Search Pacific Islands Families Study and Growing Up in New Zealand reports for "household size" and "parenting".
5. Search Papers Past for "home hints" columns, 1900 to 1950, and for "Aunt Daisy".
