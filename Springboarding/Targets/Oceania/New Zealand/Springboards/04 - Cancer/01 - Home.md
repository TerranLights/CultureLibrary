# New Zealand: Home (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese, NZSL. **Concept in one line:** the place a person or group lives from and returns to, and what it means to them: a dwelling, a household's base, and the wider place called home.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei:** an urban iwi whose home ground was nearly lost inside a city; papakāinga rebuilt there.
- **Tūhoe of Te Urewera:** home as a whole landscape, not a single house.
- **Kāi Tahu and Rakiura muttonbirders:** seasonal homes on the tītī islands, a cycle of places rather than one address.
- **Pākehā of settler descent:** the shift from "Home" meaning Britain to home meaning here, by generation and region.
- **Christchurch red-zone and East Coast flood households:** home lost to disaster and buyout, and how each region framed it.
- **Pacific families in South Auckland and Porirua:** shared and crowded houses; home split between Aotearoa and island villages.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** gold-era families whose 老家 was in Guangdong, against recent arrivals buying in Auckland suburbs.
- **Renters against owners, and holiday-town locals against bach owners** (Coromandel, Queenstown Lakes): who gets to make a place home.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for housing and ownership; mixed for home as feeling; thin for Moriori and smaller Pacific groups.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** land loss and occupation histories are contested and live; disaster interviews involve grief.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category only, standing rule).

## 5. First queries
1. Download *Housing in Aotearoa: 2020* and pull tenure and crowding by ethnic group.
2. Search the National Library catalog for "papakāinga" and for Te Matapihi reports.
3. Search Papers Past for "going Home" and "Home letters", 1860 to 1950, to date the British usage.
4. Search QuakeStudies for "red zone" and "our home" interviews.
5. Search NZ On Screen and Ngā Taonga for "Bastion Point" and for bach documentaries.
