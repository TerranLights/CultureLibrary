# New Zealand: Shelter (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese, and the languages of refugee-background communities. **Concept in one line:** cover and refuge from danger and the elements: a roof, a safe place for those who have none, and the duty to give it.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kāi Tahu:** seasonal nohoanga and rock shelters; travel shelter as an old way of life.
- **Ngāti Kurī at Kaikōura and East Coast iwi:** marae opened to strangers after quake and cyclone.
- **People sleeping rough in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch:** who they are is a question for the counts; three cities with different mission traditions.
- **Pacific families in crowded South Auckland houses and garages:** taking in kin as shelter.
- **Refugee-background communities** (Syrian, Somali, Colombian and others): refuge received and then given to later arrivals.
- **Chinese New Zealanders of gold-era descent:** a heritage of shelter under hardship.
- **Trampers, hunters and backcountry families:** the hut ethic.
- **Women and children who left violence:** refuge, and the barriers to reaching it.
- **Northland's rural whānau in caravans and sheds:** substandard shelter outside cities, to be checked against data.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for homelessness counts and housing quality; thin for older Māori travel shelter beyond archaeology and iwi histories.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese, Arabic, Somali, Spanish.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** refuge locations are confidential; refugee and homeless people's stories need care; rock-art sites may be wāhi tapu. Tenancy rules and asylum law go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category kept for review).

## 5. First queries
1. Read the latest severe housing deprivation report and note its categories.
2. Search news archives for "marae opened" with "Kaikōura" and "Gabrielle".
3. Search the National Library catalog for "huts" and for *Shelter from the Storm*.
4. Search MSD and press archives for "emergency housing" and "motel" since 2016.
5. Ask Te Ana or the Rock Art Trust what they publish on shelter sites.
