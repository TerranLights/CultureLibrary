# New Zealand: Care (Cancer): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand (Aotearoa), region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Tagalog, Chinese. **Concept in one line:** attending to the needs and well-being of another, above all one who can't meet them alone: the attitude, the work and the relationship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Māori whānau caring for kaumātua at home or on marae-linked housing:** against rest-home care; what each iwi says about it.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** care of elders and the sick as a household and church duty, a question to test against data.
- **Filipino care workers:** paid care for others while kin are abroad.
- **Chinese and Korean elders brought on parent visas:** who looks after whom in three-generation homes.
- **Disabled people and their families:** care received, refused or redefined by self-advocates.
- **Pākehā elders in retirement villages:** care bought in a growing market.
- **Rural and remote districts** (West Coast, East Cape, the Chatham Islands): care far from hospitals.
- **Men caring for wives or parents:** a smaller, less studied group.
- **Rongoā users:** care outside, or alongside, the public system.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for unpaid care, disability and aged care; mixed for rongoā, where knowledge is guarded.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Tagalog, Chinese, Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rongoā knowledge belongs to its holders; care-home neglect and psychiatric history are painful; pay and court matters go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category, per the standing rule).

## 5. First queries
1. Pull census unpaid-activities tables for "looking after a member of own household who is ill or has a disability", by ethnicity.
2. Read Durie's Te Whare Tapa Whā and the Fonofale model side by side, listing each part of care they name.
3. Search Carers New Zealand's reports for "carer wellbeing" and "respite".
4. Search the National Library catalog for "rongoā" and for Riley's book.
5. Search the Kai Tiaki archive for "aged care" and "home care", 1990 to now.
