## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Rarawa and Hokianga communities:** Whina Cooper's home ground, to see how a mother figure is honored locally against nationally.
- **Tūhoe and other iwi with recorded birth lore:** old whare kōhanga practices against today's revivals.
- **Waikato-area Māori mothers at Whāingaroa:** placenta burial and birth customs reclaimed.
- **Pākehā mothers across the Plunket century:** rules from experts against current gentle advice.
- **Solo mothers:** stigma and support since the 1973 benefit.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands mothers:** church honors and kin who share the mothering.
- **Chinese study mothers:** mothering away from husbands.
- **Farm mothers:** isolation and rural women's networks.
- **Lesbian and takatāpui mothers; mothers who lost babies:** forms of motherhood often left out.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich: maternity reports, Plunket history, women's organizations; thinner for Pacific mothers' own words and for Moriori traditions.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Cantonese and Mandarin, NZSL.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** birth karakia and placenta customs may be held by whānau; stillbirth and postnatal illness need care; abortion goes to Aries; benefit and adoption law to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category under the standing rule).
