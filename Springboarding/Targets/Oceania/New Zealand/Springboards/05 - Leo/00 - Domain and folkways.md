## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi, not one Māori voice:** Te Arawa (performance tourism since the 1800s), Ngāti Porou (kapa haka and their own kawa), Waikato-Tainui (Kīngitanga gatherings), Kāi Tahu (southern dialect), Tūhoe (Te Hui Ahurei a Tūhoe festival *check*), Ngāpuhi (large and dispersed).
- **Moriori of Rēkohu:** their own arts and gatherings, not folded into Māori.
- **Pākehā regions:** Otago-Southland (Presbyterian, temperance history), the West Coast (mining, pub life), Canterbury (racing), Wellington (arts institutions), Auckland (scale).
- **Pacific communities:** Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands, Niuean, Tokelauan, iTaukei Fijian; church and dance life differs.
- **Asian New Zealanders:** goldfields-era Chinese families against recent Chinese, Korean and Filipino arrivals.
- **Rural and urban; generations; the Deaf community; rainbow communities and takatāpui.**

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: strong statistics, an open encyclopedia, digitized newspapers. Thinner for smaller Pacific groups and Moriori.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (with dialects), Samoan, Tongan; NZ English idiom for slang.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some haka, waiata, carving and moko designs belong to particular iwi and hapū, and some knowledge is restricted; Best needs critical reading; drinking and gambling touch public-health debates. The nation's exact lands (for example, the realm's Pacific territories) are still to be settled; this sheet is written for the core islands.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
