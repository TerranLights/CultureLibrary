## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Arawa:** tourism created a market for carving early; where commerce shaped form.
- **Ngāti Porou:** meeting houses built under Āpirana Ngata's revival; tradition rebuilt on purpose.
- **Kāi Tahu:** pounamu carving on the West Coast; a stone with tribal ownership, and the line between craft and souvenir.
- **Tūhoe and Whanganui iwi:** less tourist-facing; whether making stays closer to community use.
- **Pacific communities:** Cook Islands quilters, Tongan ngatu makers, Samoan tattooists; women's and men's crafts differ and are passed on differently.
- **Pākehā regions:** Southland and rural sheds (engineering tinkering), Dunedin's music scene, Wellington's film and effects industry.
- **Asian New Zealanders:** Chinese calligraphy and opera societies, Korean and Filipino community arts; little studied.
- **Deaf community:** NZSL poetry and storytelling as visual art.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for Māori arts and national art history; mixed for Pacific crafts (strong museum holdings, thin written study); thin for Asian community arts and everyday improvising.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori craft vocabulary (much is technical); Tongan, Samoan, Cook Islands Māori for Pacific forms.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whakairo and moko designs carry whakapapa and some belong to specific hapū; tapu rules govern some making; museum holdings may be disputed. Copying debates (souvenir "tiki", corporate use of Māori motifs) are live.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Te Ara: search "whakairo", "raranga" and "Māori art" for the origin accounts, noting which iwi tells each.
2. Library catalog: Mead's *Te Toi Whakairo* and the *Te Māori* exhibition literature.
3. Waitangi Tribunal site: download *Ko Aotearoa Tēnei* and read its taonga-works chapter for the making questions only.
4. Te Papa Collections Online: search "tīvaevae", "ngatu" and "hiapo" with makers' names and community groups.
5. Papers Past: search "number 8 wire" and "Kiwi ingenuity" to date the phrase and see who used it.
