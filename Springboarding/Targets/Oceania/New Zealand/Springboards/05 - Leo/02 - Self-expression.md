## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Marae by marae:** speaking rights on the paepae and the karanga differ (some East Coast marae, Ngāti Porou among them, are reported to let women speak formally *check*); expression is governed locally.
- **Tūhoe:** political speech as identity in Te Urewera.
- **Pacific families:** brother-sister restraint and respect for titled elders may limit open speech at home; compare island-born and New Zealand-born youth.
- **Pākehā:** Presbyterian Otago-Southland against cosmopolitan Auckland and Wellington; rural men and men in trades.
- **Chinese and Korean New Zealanders:** language shift between generations changes what can be said to whom.
- **Rainbow communities and takatāpui:** coming out across different family systems.
- **The Deaf community:** a whole language as expression.
- **Teenagers:** where schools require uniforms, check whether style moves to hair, shoes and phones.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: strong on Māori and Pacific body art and oratory, and on mental-health campaigns; thinner on everyday expressiveness as measured, beyond the national surveys.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (oratory and chant genres), Samoan and Tongan (respect vocabulary).
- **Sensitive or restricted:** moko designs carry whakapapa; whaikōrero protocol and gender rules are debated and locally owned; mental-health and coming-out stories need care. Free-speech law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
