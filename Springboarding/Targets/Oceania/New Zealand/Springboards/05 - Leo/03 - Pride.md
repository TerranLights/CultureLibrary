# New Zealand: Pride (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan for the largest Pacific communities. **Concept in one line:** feeling and showing that oneself, one's work or one's own people have worth, from healthy self-respect to arrogance.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāpuhi:** many hapū, strong local loyalties; pride may sit with the hapū before the iwi.
- **Kāi Tahu:** tribal enterprise after settlement; pride in rebuilding an economic base.
- **Ngāti Toa:** custodians of a haka the whole nation performs; pride in authorship against national use.
- **Waikato-Tainui:** the Kīngitanga as a focus of collective standing.
- **Moriori:** revival after long denial; pride in survival itself.
- **Tongan and Samoan New Zealanders:** flag and team displays; compare church, village and national loyalties.
- **South Island against North Island:** "the Mainland" self-image in Canterbury and the South; rural Southland and the West Coast.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** goldfields descendants and recent arrivals may draw pride from different histories.
- **Rainbow communities:** Pride as a public claim, and its divisions.
- **Farmers and trades:** pride in work and land, and wounded pride when they are lost.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: survey items, a historical dictionary, a century of sport and regional records, and live Pride debates.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (proverbs, pepeha), Tongan and Samoan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** haka ownership and use are contested; Moriori history is painful and disputed; Pride's internal disputes are recent and charged. Avoid treating modesty as a national trait; test it group by group.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: Mead and Grove, *Ngā Pēpeha a ngā Tīpuna*; index the sayings on boasting, humility and the proud brought low, with iwi.
2. Data archive: ISSP National Identity, New Zealand rounds; pull the pride items by ethnicity.
3. Papers Past: search "skite" and "skiting" from 1860 to 1940 to see who was called a boaster.
4. Papers Past: search "Originals" with "All Blacks" for 1905-06 national-pride coverage.
5. News archives: "Mate Ma'a Tonga" with "Auckland" and "flags", 2017.
