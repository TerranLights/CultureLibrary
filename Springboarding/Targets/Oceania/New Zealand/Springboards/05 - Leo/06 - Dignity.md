# New Zealand: Dignity (Leo): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** New Zealand English, te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan. **Concept in one line:** the worth a person has as a person, and the bearing and treatment that honor it; usually held to be inherent rather than earned.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi caring for kaumātua on marae:** compare iwi health providers in Te Tai Tokerau and Tairāwhiti with urban providers.
- **Taranaki iwi:** Parihaka's legacy of non-violent bearing as a living teaching.
- **Pacific families:** care for elders at home against residential care; church roles in dignified funerals.
- **Pākehā retirees:** small-town and rural old age, the "dignified retirement" claim, and its gaps.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** long-settled families' memory of exclusion; elders in family care.
- **Disabled people and their families:** institutional histories (Kimberley Centre *check*) against self-advocacy.
- **Beneficiaries and the working poor:** dignity at the welfare counter and the food bank.
- **Refugee-background communities:** dignity in resettlement.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in policy and health literature; mixed for everyday courtesy norms; thin for some Pacific and refugee groups.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (tapu, mana), Samoan and Tongan respect vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** kōiwi tangata and toi moko are tapu and handled under iwi protocol; dying and disability cases are personal; abuse-in-care history overlaps. Law (assisted dying, rights codes) goes to Libra and Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Download *Whakamana Tāngata* and index its submissions by group.
2. Te Papa site: search "Karanga Aotearoa" and list repatriations by iwi and year.
3. Library: Te Rangi Hīroa, *The Coming of the Maori*, chapters on tapu and rank.
4. LiLACS NZ: find its reports on Māori and non-Māori elders' views of independence and care.
5. Health and Disability Commissioner: search decisions for "dignity" and sort by care setting.
