## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi elders who went to Native Schools:** the generation punished for speaking Māori, against their grandchildren in Kura Kaupapa Māori.
- **Waikato-Tainui and Taranaki iwi:** confiscation after war; memory of defeat and of land taken.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** shame tied to family standing and church; island-born parents against New Zealand-born children.
- **Pākehā boys' schools and rugby clubs:** initiation and ribbing traditions; where they turned cruel.
- **Rainbow and takatāpui youth:** bullying rates reported separately in youth surveys.
- **Asian New Zealanders:** street harassment and accent mockery; compare long-settled and recent families.
- **Rural towns and small communities:** where everyone knows, and gossip lowers a family.
- **Gender:** the "Roast Busters" case as a test of how girls' and boys' humiliation are judged.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for school bullying and historical collective humiliation; mixed for everyday ribbing, which needs fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (whakamā and related terms have no clean English match); Samoan and Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse songs and curses may be tapu or hapū-owned; sexual-humiliation cases involve minors and suppressed names; Native Schools memories are painful. Avenging humiliation goes to Scorpio, legal remedies to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
