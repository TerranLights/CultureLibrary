## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Kurī and other marae communities:** how a marae decides to open its doors to strangers in a crisis.
- **Ngāti Raukawa:** the iwi of the hau text (*check*); how it reads its ancestor's words today.
- **Tūhoe and Ngāti Porou:** giving on remote home marae against urban giving by members who live away.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** obligation, church and remittance; compare first and later generations.
- **Cook Islands and Niuean communities:** hair-cutting and other gift-giving ceremonies (*check* names).
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** red packets, clan associations and mutual aid, from goldfields societies to recent groups.
- **Christchurch and Kaikōura residents:** giving after earthquakes as a local memory.
- **Farmers and small towns:** neighborly help against formal charity.
- **Wealthy donors:** large family foundations against everyday giving.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: strong anthropology of Māori and Pacific giving, open charity data and remittance figures. Informal and marae giving are undercounted.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (koha, hau and the Ranapiri text in the original), Samoan, Tongan, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the hau text has been read and reread by outsiders, so set iwi views beside it; family obligation and remittance burdens are private; reciprocity as a system goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
