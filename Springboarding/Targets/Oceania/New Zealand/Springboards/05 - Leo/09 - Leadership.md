## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Waikato-Tainui:** a hereditary monarch (Kīngitanga) with elected tribal bodies beside it.
- **Ngāi Tahu:** a corporate tribal model; elected representatives from each papatipu rūnanga.
- **Tūhoe:** leadership tied to prophets (Rua Kēnana) and to independence in Te Urewera.
- **Ngāti Porou:** leadership through education and Parliament (Ngata).
- **Ngāti Tūwharetoa:** an ariki line at Taupō; inherited senior status.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands communities:** chiefly titles, church office and elected office; who leads when they differ.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** clan and community associations as the old leadership, against new professional networks.
- **Pākehā:** farm, union and local-body leaders against corporate leaders; small-town mayors.
- **Women:** three female prime ministers (Shipley, Clark, Ardern) set beside women's informal leadership on marae and in churches.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: biographies, tribal documents and political history are abundant; GLOBE and board data give measures.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (leadership terms and whakapapa), Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** succession within iwi and the Kīngitanga is internal business; matai titles involve family disputes; formal authority and command go to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Te Ara: search "rangatira" and "leadership" for the traditional qualities list; trace it to Firth or Best and to iwi sources.
2. Library: King's *Te Puea* and *Whina*; note how each woman gathered followers.
3. Iwi Chairs Forum and Ngāi Tahu sites: download governance documents on choosing leaders.
4. Library: Meleisea on the matai system; then search "matai titles New Zealand-born".
5. Find the GLOBE New Zealand chapter and list its leadership profile scores.
