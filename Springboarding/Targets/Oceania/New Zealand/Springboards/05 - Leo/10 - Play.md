## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Porou and East Coast iwi:** games tied to their own traditions (mū tōrere among them *check*) and strong kapa haka play.
- **Taranaki and Waikato iwi:** kī-o-rahi revival in schools and at marae gatherings (*check* which iwi lead it).
- **Samoan and Tongan communities:** kirikiti, church youth groups and fiafia nights; play joined to church life.
- **Cook Islands and Niuean families:** dance and drumming as play for children.
- **Pākehā:** rural children's freedom against urban supervised play; tramping and hunting families; beach and bach summers.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** mahjong and family games; generations playing differently.
- **Christchurch children after the earthquakes:** play in a broken city and the new playgrounds built since.
- **Gamers:** a cross-cutting group; online play by age.
- **Older adults:** bowls clubs and bridge.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: an unusually strong national play-history literature (Sutton-Smith), early-childhood policy and survey data. Thinner for adult play outside sport and gambling.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (game names and chants), Samoan and Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some whai chants and games may carry iwi stories with protocol; colonial records of games need critical reading; gambling harm touches public health. Competitive sport sits closer to Aries.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
