## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tūhoe:** Matariki revived from the iwi's own star knowledge; Te Hui Ahurei a Tūhoe as a festival of return.
- **Rātana followers:** joy in worship, with brass bands, across several iwi.
- **Ngāti Porou:** action songs from wartime as morale and delight.
- **Samoan and Tongan congregations:** church celebrations, choirs and feasts; compare denominations.
- **Cook Islands communities:** drum dance and celebrations on Constitution Day (*check* New Zealand observance).
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** Lunar New Year and lantern festivals across generations.
- **Pākehā:** summer holidays at the bach or crib against northern-style Christmas; temperance and church traditions in the South.
- **Young people:** festivals, raves and New Year; joy and its risks.
- **Older people and people with depression:** where joy is hard; the mental-health side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich: strong wellbeing statistics and policy frameworks, plus festival and song histories. Thinner on everyday expressions of delight, which need fieldwork.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (song texts, Matariki knowledge), Samoan, Tongan, Mandarin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some Matariki and star knowledge is held by tohunga and iwi with protocol; drinking and festival harm touch public health; depression needs care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
