## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Porou and Tairāwhiti:** C Company of the Māori Battalion drew heavily from here; how war service is held in local memory.
- **Te Arawa:** a long role as hosts for tourists and dignitaries; service to guests as paid work and as duty.
- **Whanganui iwi:** the river settlements tied to Aubert's mission; religious service seen from both sides.
- **Waikato-Tainui and the Kīngitanga:** service to the King movement and its poukai gatherings, against service to the Crown.
- **Moriori and Ngāti Mutunga on Rēkohu (Chatham Islands):** enslavement after 1835; any study of servitude must begin with the Moriori account.
- **Samoan families:** tautua and the matai path, kept or changed in city life.
- **Tongan and Fijian congregations:** service to church and kin; the weight of contributions.
- **Rural Pākehā towns:** volunteer brigades, RSA clubs and women's institutes as the main service bodies.
- **Hospitality workers in Queenstown and Auckland:** migrant and working-holiday staff who serve tourists.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for military service, honors and volunteering statistics; mixed for marae service (guides exist, practice varies by iwi); thin for colonial and modern domestic work and for Pacific church service in writing.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan and Fijian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the enslavement of Moriori; pre-colonial captivity; Māori war service and land grievances (returned soldiers' land schemes passed Māori over, a topic to check). Conscription law goes to Libra; duty and obligation as moral ideas to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
