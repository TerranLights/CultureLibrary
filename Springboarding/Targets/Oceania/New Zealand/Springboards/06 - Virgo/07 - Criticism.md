## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāpuhi at Waitangi:** host iwi of the yearly challenge to the Crown; who speaks for whom and when.
- **Ngāti Porou and Te Arawa:** differing rules on who may stand to speak on the marae, and so on who may rebuke whom.
- **Samoan congregations:** the pulpit and the church committee as sites of critique, and limits on criticizing ministers.
- **Tongan families:** fakamā and the weight of shame when a member is criticized in public.
- **Wellington's political and media class:** criticism as a trade, close-knit and personal.
- **Rural Pākehā communities:** whether cutting down the boastful is felt more strongly in small towns.
- **Auckland's Chinese-language media and community groups:** criticism of New Zealand politics in another language and register.
- **Workplaces with many migrants:** clashes between direct and indirect feedback styles.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for satire, cartoons, inquiries and complaint bodies; mixed for marae and church criticism (well described, varying by iwi and denomination); thin for workplace feedback beyond practitioner claims.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (chant language is archaic); Samoan; Tongan; Mandarin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some kaioraora name ancestors and remain hurtful; Waitangi Day disputes; the Cartwright patients' privacy. Defamation and free-speech law go to Libra; authority and its legitimacy to Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.

## 5. First queries
1. Library: *Ngā Mōteatea*, index of pātere and kaioraora; note the occasion and target of each.
2. Ombudsman site: annual reports, complaints by agency and topic over two decades.
3. NZ Cartoon Archive: search "Muldoon", "Lange" and "Waitangi" for patterns of mockery.
4. Google Scholar: "tall poppy" "New Zealand" survey or experiment.
5. Media Council and BSA sites: decisions by topic, to see what audiences criticize.
