## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tūhoe and Ngāti Kahungunu:** where Te Wheke came from; whether two Māori models reflect two upbringings.
- **Te Arawa:** geothermal bathing and healing places, and their loss to and recovery from the state.
- **Rātana followers across Whanganui and Rangitīkei:** faith healing beside medicine.
- **Samoan families in South Auckland and Porirua:** fofō and taulāsea used beside clinics; the 1918 epidemic remembered.
- **Tongan and Cook Islands communities:** church-centered health programs; faito'o and ta'unga practice.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** Chinese medicine clinics and herbal shops, and whether older and newer migrants use them alike.
- **Farming families:** rural isolation, farm injury and the wellbeing programs aimed at them (Farmstrong, *check*).
- **Deaf and disabled New Zealanders:** health defined by those who live with impairment.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statistics, inquiries and health history; mixed for rongoā and Pacific healing (growing literature, much oral); thin for Chinese and Korean community practice.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori; Mandarin.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rongoā plant and karakia knowledge belongs to practitioners and may be withheld; the 1918 Samoan deaths; mental health and suicide need care. Health law and the Tohunga Suppression Act's text go to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
