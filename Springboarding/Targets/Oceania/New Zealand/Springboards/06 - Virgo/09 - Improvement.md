## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Waikato-Tainui:** gardens once improved by their own engineering; land confiscated, later a settlement plan for renewal.
- **Ngāi Tahu:** a long-range tribal development plan; how members judge whether things got better.
- **Ngāti Porou:** heartland of Ngata's schemes; how descendants assess them.
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei:** Bastion Point and its aftermath; reclaiming land as betterment.
- **Pacific families from Freemans Bay and Ponsonby moved to Ōtara and Māngere:** urban "renewal" seen from below.
- **Glen Innes state-house tenants:** a second round of the same pattern.
- **Canterbury and Waikato dairy regions:** intensification as progress or as harm to water.
- **Canterbury's earthquake survivors:** competing ideas of a better city.
- **Chinese and Korean migrants:** night classes, tutoring and credential re-training as routes to betterment.
- **Conservation volunteers in predator-trapping groups:** improvement as restoration.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for land, ecology, reform and housing history; mixed for Māori and Pacific accounts of imposed improvement (strong recent work, older records written by officials); thin for personal self-help culture.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan; Mandarin and Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** assimilation policies and confiscation; displacement through renewal; predator control methods (1080 poison) are hotly disputed. Land and settlement law goes to Libra.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
