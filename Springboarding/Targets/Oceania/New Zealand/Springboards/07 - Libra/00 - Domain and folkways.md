## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Māori, by iwi:** Ngāpuhi (the 1835 and 1840 signings), Waikato-Tainui (Kīngitanga alliance-making), Ngāi Tahu (long claim before settlement), Tūhoe (non-signatory history); each holds a different founding relationship with the Crown.
- **Pākehā regional strands:** Presbyterian Otago and Southland against Anglican Canterbury; farming districts against the cities; church heritage may have shaped marriage rites and divorce attitudes.
- **Pacific peoples:** Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Niuean, Tokelauan and Fijian communities, each with its own exchange at unions.
- **Asian New Zealanders:** long-settled Cantonese families against recent Chinese, Korean and Filipino arrivals; family say over partners may differ by generation.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu (Chatham Islands):** a separate tradition of handling conflict, in their own voice.
- **Rainbow and takatāpui communities:** the civil-union and marriage-equality debates turned on them.
- **Diaspora (comparison only):** New Zealanders in Australia, whose partnership rights trans-Tasman rules set.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich: open legislation, strong statistics, published tribunal reports, digitized newspapers. Pre-contact marriage and dispute custom comes through oral tradition filtered by 19th-century collectors.
- **Lands:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled; these sheets are written for the core islands, the Chatham Islands included.
- **Languages needed:** English; te reo Māori (Tribunal evidence, niupepa, tribal histories); Samoan and Tongan; Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** whakapapa and tribal knowledge held by iwi; marriage and tangihanga tikanga vary by hapū and some is not for outside publication; family violence data needs care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
