## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Waikato-Tainui and Ngāi Tahu:** the first large settlements with formal Crown apologies; compare what each tribe asked redress to do.
- **Tūhoe:** the 2007 Urewera police raids and the later police apology make a live case of policing, justice and a people's standing.
- **Taranaki iwi and Parihaka:** arrests and detention without trial in the 1880s as a historical-justice claim.
- **Samoan and Tongan communities:** *ifoga* and church-led apology beside sentencing; how courts weigh them.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** the poll tax as a non-Treaty redress claim.
- **Survivors of state and church care:** a cross-ethnic group whose claims turn on institutions.
- **Rural districts against the cities:** distance from courts and reliance on community patrols.
- **Victims' families against reform advocates:** split views on punishment cut across ethnicity.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for state courts, statistics and inquiries; mixed for customary justice, whose 19th-century sources come through collectors and Native Land Court records.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for tikanga terms and hui records; Samoan for *ifoga*.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** imprisonment and abuse-in-care material involves living survivors; gang-related data; some tikanga on wrongs is held by iwi and not for outside publication.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
