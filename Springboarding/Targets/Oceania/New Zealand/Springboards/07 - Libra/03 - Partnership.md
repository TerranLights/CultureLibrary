## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Whanganui iwi and Tūhoe:** the two best-known legal-person settlements put partnership into joint bodies; compare their terms.
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei:** an urban iwi partnering with Auckland's council after the Bastion Point occupation.
- **Te Arawa and Waikato-Tainui:** marriage alliances and the Kīngitanga show partnership made through kin ties.
- **Dairy districts (Waikato, Taranaki, Southland):** sharemilkers and owners, often of different generations or origins.
- **Married and de facto couples:** the law treats them alike after three years; check whether the couples do.
- **Takatāpui and Pākehā rainbow couples:** different routes to recognition.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Chinese families:** how far kin take part in choosing a partner, by generation.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for law, settlements and parliamentary debate; mixed for courtship and couples' private arrangements; tribal alliance histories rich but held in iwi publications and oral tradition.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for tribal histories and settlement documents; Samoan, Tongan and Chinese for family sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** "Treaty partnership" and "co-governance" are live political disputes; present each side in its own words. Whakapapa behind alliance marriages is held by iwi.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
