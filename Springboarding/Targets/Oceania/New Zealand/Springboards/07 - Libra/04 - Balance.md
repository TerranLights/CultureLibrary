## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Whanganui iwi and Waikato-Tainui:** both settlements put a river's health first; compare how each states balance between use and wellbeing.
- **Ngāi Tahu:** *mahinga kai* (food-gathering places) claims tie balance to access across the South Island.
- **Tūhoe and Ngāti Porou:** maramataka and gardening knowledge kept and revived in different ways.
- **Dairy districts (Waikato, Canterbury, Southland):** intensification and water quality set farm income against rivers.
- **Pacific communities:** Fonofale and church life set the frame for family and work demands.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** Chinese medicine and food ideas of hot and cold, by generation.
- **Shift workers (meat processing, ports, health care):** balance where the working day does not follow the clock.
- **Muslim communities:** Ramadan in a Southern Hemisphere year, against the work calendar.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for health models, environmental law, constitutional debate and work-hours statistics; mixed for household practice; maramataka knowledge is held by iwi and hapū and varies by place.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (maramataka, tikanga); Samoan and Tongan for Pacific health; Chinese for medicine and food.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some seasonal and tapu knowledge is restricted; freshwater and predator control are contested; describe the disputes, not a verdict.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
