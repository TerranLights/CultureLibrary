# New Zealand: Impartiality (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English, te reo Māori, Chinese, Korean, Samoan. **Concept in one line:** judging or treating people without favoring anyone for who they are or how they are tied to the judge: no favorites, no prejudice, no special treatment for rank, wealth, kin or friendship.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Small towns and island communities (West Coast, Chatham Islands, East Cape):** dense ties make recusal and conflict rules hard to apply.
- **Māori public servants and board members with iwi duties:** whanaungatanga and political neutrality meet in the same person.
- **Iwi who fought beside the Crown and iwi who fought against it:** the shifting meaning of *kūpapa* shows how neutrality itself was judged.
- **Graduates of elite schools (Auckland, Christchurch, Wellington):** the old-boys network as the familiar charge.
- **Pacific church communities:** ministers' standing in disputes within a congregation.
- **Chinese and Korean business circles:** connections as a resource, seen from inside and outside.
- **Sports communities:** home-ground bias as everyday talk.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for statutes, codes, ombudsman and regulator decisions; thinner for everyday favoritism, which needs surveys, forum material or interviews.
- **Languages needed:** English; te reo Māori for Tribunal and land-court records; Chinese and Korean for business press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** *kūpapa* is a painful label in some iwi histories; use each iwi's own account. Named officials in bias complaints may be living.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
