## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi with different marae speaking orders:** mapping *pāeke* and *tauutuutu* districts shows where protocol itself differs.
- **Ngāpuhi:** the 1835 and 1840 negotiations and today's disputed settlement mandate show diplomacy inside one large iwi.
- **Waikato-Tainui and the Kīngitanga:** the King movement's dealings with the Crown and other iwi as a standing diplomatic institution.
- **Samoan *matai* and Tongan nobles' kin in Auckland:** imported orator traditions in church and community meetings.
- **Cook Islanders and Niueans:** citizens of the realm with their own governments; dual standing in talks.
- **Chinese and Korean business communities:** face and indirection in trade with home countries.
- **Wellington's policy and diplomatic community against regional New Zealand:** who conducts diplomacy, and who watches it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for foreign policy, settlements and marae protocol; mixed for everyday tact, though workplace sociolinguistics helps.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (oratory, songs); Samoan and Tongan (orator traditions); Chinese for trade press.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some whaikōrero, songs and peace agreements belong to particular iwi and hapū; mandates in settlement talks are disputed.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
