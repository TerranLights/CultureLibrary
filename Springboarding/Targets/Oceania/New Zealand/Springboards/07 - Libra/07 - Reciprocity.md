# New Zealand: Reciprocity (Libra): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English, te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese, Tagalog. **Concept in one line:** returning what one receives, in kind: gifts for gifts, help for help, harm for harm; the give-and-take that binds people and keeps accounts between them.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Muriwhenua iwi (Ngāti Kurī, Te Aupōuri, Te Rarawa, Ngāti Kahu and others):** *tuku* readings of early transfers make reciprocity a land-claim question.
- **Ngāi Tahu:** South Island food exchange tied to seasons and places.
- **Iwi with large tangihanga circuits (for example in Waikato and on the East Coast):** koha flows across many marae.
- **Samoan and Tongan households:** church and family giving with large remittance obligations.
- **Filipino workers:** *utang na loob* between employers, recruiters and kin.
- **Chinese families, old and new:** *renqing* in business and family life, by generation.
- **Rural Pākehā districts:** working bees, "bring a plate" and shared machinery.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in classic anthropology, Tribunal reports and surveys; contested readings of *utu* and *hau*, so pair outside scholarship with Māori scholarship.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (Ranapiri's text, niupepa, Tribunal evidence); Samoan and Tongan (church and family giving); Tagalog and Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** koha amounts and tangihanga customs are private and vary by marae; church-giving pressure is sensitive in Pacific communities.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
