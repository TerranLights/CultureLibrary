# New Zealand: Death (Scorpio): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English, te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Cantonese and Mandarin. **Concept in one line:** the end of a life, and how dying, the dead and the meaning of death are understood; grief and mourning included.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Porou and other East Coast hapū:** long tangihanga on rural marae, with kin returning from the cities; travel itself is part of grieving.
- **Waikato-Tainui:** the public tangihanga of the Māori monarchs at Tūrangawaewae, set against a whānau tangi.
- **Te Arawa and Tūwharetoa:** volcanic-plateau traditions of omens and mass death.
- **Ngāi Tahu / Kāi Tahu:** southern practice and dialect, with fewer people living near their marae.
- **Moriori:** the dead of Rēkohu and their memory after the 1835 invasion.
- **Pākehā:** Presbyterian Otago, Catholic Westland, secular Wellington; the spread of the "celebration of life".
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands families:** how long a body lies at home, church choirs, and the senses of a Pacific vigil.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** Qingming at the Chinese sections of old cemeteries.
- **Jewish and Muslim communities:** burial within a day, and burial societies.
- **Rainbow communities and the Deaf community:** grief outside family rules; signed eulogies.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on numbers and on Māori mourning; mixed on Pākehā private grief; thin on afterlife belief outside church surveys.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (honorific and oratorical registers), Samoan and Tongan respect registers, Cantonese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide reporting follows media guidelines; child deaths are case material; whaikōrero to the dead belongs to the marae and is not copied without leave.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, as a death-practice unit.
