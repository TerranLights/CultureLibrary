## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Toa Rangatira:** custodians of Ka Mate, with their own account of its meaning.
- **Ringatū and Rātana followers:** two Māori faiths of deliverance with different centers (East Coast and Bay of Plenty; Whanganui and Rangitīkei).
- **Moriori:** revival after near-erasure, apart from any Māori story.
- **Tūhoe, Ngāi Tahu and Waikato-Tainui:** iwi rebuilding after settlement, each on its own model.
- **Gang whānau:** Mongrel Mob and Black Power chapters, where leaving and reforming differ.
- **Pacific Pentecostal and Latter-day Saint congregations:** sudden conversion against lifelong membership, in South Auckland and Hastings.
- **Pākehā rebuild towns:** Napier, Christchurch and Kaikōura, set against towns that did not recover.
- **Chinese and Korean churches:** conversion within migrant families.
- **Rainbow New Zealanders:** gender transition as people themselves describe it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich on language revival and the Christchurch rebuild; mixed on recovery; thin on afterlife return beliefs.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (including revival-movement vocabulary), Samoan and Tongan church registers, Korean and Chinese church usage.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Ka Mate's use is guarded by Ngāti Toa; prophetic texts of Ringatū and Rātana belong to their faiths; recovery accounts are personal.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands, for conversion and revival.
