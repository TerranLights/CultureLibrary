## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Waikato-Tainui and Ngāti Maniapoto in 1917-1918:** refusal to fight for the Crown that had taken their land; compare with iwi who volunteered (Te Arawa, Ngāti Porou) and why.
- **Te Rarawa and Te Aupōuri at the land march's starting point:** the far north as a source of long-haul resolve.
- **Te Wai o Hua and Makaurau Marae at Ihumātao:** a generational split over one stand.
- **Otago Presbyterian and Quaker pacifists:** the Baxter family and their circles.
- **West Coast and central North Island timber and mining towns:** conviction about jobs and livelihood set against conservationists'.
- **Temperance districts (Invercargill and other former "dry" areas):** a moral cause that shaped local life.
- **Conservative Christian networks (Destiny, Brethren) versus liberal church activists:** opposite convictions with similar intensity.
- **Pacific youth activists:** climate and church conviction joined.
- **Rainbow campaigners of 1985-1986 and 2012-2013:** sustained public conviction across two reforms.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; protest history and conscientious objection are well documented in archives and film.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan for Pacific activism.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** objectors' families may still carry stigma; the Ihumātao settlement split whānau; living activists should be approached through what they have published.
- **Held for review:** none.
