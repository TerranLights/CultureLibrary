## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi by founding waka:** each tells its own arrival, route and landing; no single national version.
- **Rakiura Māori and the tītī (muttonbird) islands:** a seasonal journey to harvest grounds, as the families describe it.
- **Ngāti Kahu, Te Rarawa and other Northland voyaging families:** the center of the navigation revival.
- **Te Arawa:** hosts of tourists for generations.
- **Moriori:** their own arrival traditions on Rēkohu.
- **Ni-Vanuatu, Tongan and Samoan seasonal workers versus settled Pacific families:** the temporary traveler against the migrant who stayed.
- **Chinese goldfield descendants versus recent Chinese migrants:** two migration eras.
- **Dalmatian (Croatian) families of Northland and West Auckland.**
- **Young urban graduates on the OE versus rural youth who stay:** who travels is a class and place question.
- **New Zealanders in Australia:** comparison only, to show what leaving means from the home side.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; migration and tourism statistics are strong, and voyaging tradition is well published.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan; Cantonese and Mandarin; Croatian.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** some waka traditions and navigation knowledge are held by families or iwi; the origin debate can belittle ancestors; RSE workers' conditions are a current controversy.
- **Held for review:** none.
