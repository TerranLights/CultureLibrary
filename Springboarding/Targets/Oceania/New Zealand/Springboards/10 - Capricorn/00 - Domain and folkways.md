## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāpuhi (Northland):** signatories of 1835; their reading of what 1840 did and did not hand over.
- **Waikato-Tainui and the Kīngitanga:** a Māori monarchy since 1858, an authority line beside the Crown's.
- **Tūhoe (Te Urewera):** *mana motuhake* as a long refusal of outside command.
- **Kāi Tahu (South Island):** a statutory tribal council (1996), showing duty inside a corporate iwi.
- **Moriori (Rēkohu, Chatham Islands):** their own line of authority and its fate under invasion and colonization.
- **Pākehā regions:** Presbyterian Otago, Anglican Canterbury, the mining West Coast; three founding ideas of who leads.
- **Urban Māori authorities (Te Whānau o Waipareira, West Auckland):** authority grounded in shared city life, not descent.
- **Pasifika communities (Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands, Niuean, Fijian, Tokelauan):** chiefly titles and church offices carried into Auckland suburbs.
- **Chinese, Korean and Filipino New Zealanders:** family and workplace authority, old Cantonese families against recent arrivals.
- **Farming districts against Wellington:** rural self-reliance beside the capital's public-service world.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich; archives are heavily digitized, and authority is argued in public around Te Tiriti.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori in northern and southern forms; Samoan and Tongan for chiefly and church offices; some Chinese and Korean.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** recited genealogies and installation rites belong to the iwi and hapū that hold them; who holds sovereignty under Te Tiriti is an open political quarrel (record each side, settle none).
- **Lands:** which islands and territories the post-war nation holds is not yet fixed by the author; this sign's sheets are written for the North and South Islands, Rakiura and Rēkohu.
- **Held for review:** set aside by category only: South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
