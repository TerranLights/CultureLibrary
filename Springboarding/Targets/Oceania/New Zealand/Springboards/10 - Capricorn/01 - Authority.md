## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Kīngitanga iwi (Waikato, Ngāti Maniapoto, Hauraki) against northern iwi who stayed outside:** an elected-by-hui monarchy beside the Crown, accepted by some iwi and not others.
- **Ngāti Tūwharetoa (Taupō):** a paramount-chief line still recognized; hereditary authority in practice.
- **Taranaki iwi (Parihaka):** non-violent refusal as an authority tradition of its own.
- **Ringatū and Rātana followers:** prophetic and church authority that crosses tribal lines.
- **Urban Māori authorities against iwi rūnanga:** two claims to speak for Māori in the cities.
- **Samoan and Tongan congregations:** the minister's and the matai's word in the diaspora, and the New Zealand-born generation's response.
- **Pākehā Presbyterian, Catholic and secular households:** separate sources of household authority.
- **Wellington public servants against rural districts:** central authority seen from its own offices and from far off.
- **Chinese and Korean families:** parental authority across first and second generations.
- **The Deaf community (NZSL users):** authority of hearing institutions over Deaf schooling, and the community's own leaders.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for political and Māori authority; thinner for workplace authority and Asian households.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (19th-century manuscripts and newspapers), Samoan, Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tohunga knowledge and chiefly succession are matters for iwi; the 2007 Urewera police raids and the armed-police debate are raw; COVID-19 orders remain divisive.
