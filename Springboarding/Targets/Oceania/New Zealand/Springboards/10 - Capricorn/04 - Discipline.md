## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Māori who went through Native Schools against kura kaupapa graduates:** two school regimes, two ideas of training.
- **Survivors of state and church care (many Māori and Pacific):** discipline from institutions in place of family.
- **Samoan, Tongan and Cook Islands church families:** correction tied to church teaching, and young people's changing view of it.
- **Chinese and Korean families in Auckland:** after-school tutoring and long study hours as household discipline.
- **Patched gang whānau:** rank discipline outside the state, and the families caught between.
- **Otago and Southland Presbyterian families:** an often-described strictness, to test against records.
- **Rugby, rowing and netball communities:** team discipline as a public ideal.
- **Deaf New Zealanders:** generations schooled under oralism against those schooled in NZSL.
- **Military towns (Waiouru, Burnham, Linton):** army routine in family life.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich; New Zealand has long cohort studies, a national inquiry and a public referendum on this concept.
- **Languages needed:** NZSL for Deaf testimony; Samoan and Tongan for church parenting material; te reo for kapa haka and wānanga sources.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** abuse in care and the Native Schools are traumatic histories; gang sources need care; family violence data identifies vulnerable people.
- **Law:** the letter of section 59 and school rules goes to Libra; this sheet keeps the purposes argued for discipline.
- **Held for review:** under the standing rule, the category of South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
