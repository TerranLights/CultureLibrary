## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Iwi and hapū:** Waikato-Tainui (seat of the Kīngitanga), Ngāpuhi, Tūhoe, Ngāti Porou, Ngāi Tahu, Te Arawa, Ngāti Kahungunu: marae, rūnanga and gathering customs differ by tribe.
- **Urban pan-tribal Māori** (Te Whānau o Waipareira, West Auckland): group life built on shared city neighborhoods.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu:** their own covenant tradition and modern revival.
- **Pākehā district cultures:** Presbyterian Otago, Anglican-planned Canterbury and Catholic West Coast mining towns each left a different web of halls, lodges and parishes.
- **Pacific communities:** Samoans and Tongans in South Auckland and Porirua, Niueans, Tokelauans and Cook Islands Māori; for each, the church is the hub of collective life.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** goldfields-era clan associations set against newer Mandarin-speaking networks.
- **Smaller settler streams:** Northland's Dalmatian Croatians, Puhoi's Bohemians, Dutch reformed congregations.
- **Recent arrivals and refugees:** Korean, Filipino, Somali, Ethiopian, Cambodian, Syrian; how they are received varies by town.
- **The Deaf community;** rural districts against the two big cities.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich: strong official statistics, a large te reo archive and local newspapers. Pacific and recent-migrant associations are thinner in print.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori above all; Samoan and Tongan for congregational records; Cantonese for early clan associations.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** the nation's exact lands are still to be settled. Marae tikanga and some whakapapa are not for outsiders; Māori data sovereignty (Te Mana Raraunga *check*) governs use of Māori data. The 2019 Christchurch mosque attacks, gangs and closed communities such as Gloriavale need care.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (as a category; for the domain, their own associations and gatherings).
