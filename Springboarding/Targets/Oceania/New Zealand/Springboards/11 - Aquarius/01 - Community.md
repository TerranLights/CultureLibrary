# New Zealand: Community (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English, te reo Māori; Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Tokelauan, Korean and Chinese for the matching communities. **Concept in one line:** the bond among people who share a life (a place, a faith, a trade, an origin, an interest, a cause), recognize one another as members and owe one another something; and the felt quality of that bond.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural service towns against Auckland suburbs:** halls and A&P shows against turnover and commuting.
- **Ngāti Porou's coastal settlements (Ruatōria, Tikitiki):** small, te reo-strong communities centered on several marae each.
- **Tūhoe of the Ruātoki and Waimana valleys:** community bound to Te Urewera, with its own history of state raids.
- **Ngāi Tahu rūnanga:** a large tribe spread thin across the South Island, held together by many small papatipu rūnanga.
- **Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei:** an urban hapū that lost and rebuilt its home village.
- **Pacific towns within towns:** Tokelauans of Porirua and the Hutt, Cook Islands Māori of Tokoroa, Samoans and Tongans of Ōtara and Māngere: church-based communities with different ages and sizes.
- **Mining and timber towns** (Blackball, Waihī, Kawerau): community built around one employer and its closures.
- **Christchurch's eastern suburbs:** neighborhoods emptied by the red zone after 2011.
- **Chatham Islanders:** Moriori, Ngāti Mutunga and Pākehā families on one small island group.
- **Korean and Chinese suburbs** (North Shore, Howick): association and church life in other languages.
- **Closed religious communities** (Gloriavale on the West Coast): total community, and those who leave it.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for cities and Māori communities; thin for small towns between censuses and for Pacific church records.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for marae and rūnanga papers; Tokelauan, Samoan and Tongan where the parish is the village; Korean and Chinese for the newer suburbs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** marae and hapū matters belong to their people; some marae histories are held orally and shared by permission only. Gloriavale and Centrepoint involve abuse findings and survivors. The Christchurch red zone is still raw for many.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category; as place and faith communities).

1. Download the latest Quality of Life Survey report and compare the "sense of community" items across cities.

4. Search nzhistory.govt.nz and Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei sources for "Ōkahu Bay 1951".
5. Find Sargisson and Sargent's book and list the communities it covers.
