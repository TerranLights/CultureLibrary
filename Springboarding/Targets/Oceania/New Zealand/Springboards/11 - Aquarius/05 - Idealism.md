# New Zealand: Idealism (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English, te reo Māori, Moriori; Samoan and Chinese for specific communities. **Concept in one line:** living and judging by ideals (principles, and visions of how things ought to be) held above present interest or practicality; it includes visions of a better society (utopias).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Taranaki iwi and Te Āti Awa:** heirs of Parihaka; nonviolence as an inherited ideal.
- **Moriori:** a covenant against killing, and what its keeping cost.
- **Tūhoe:** Maungapōhatu as a built vision of self-rule, and its forcible end.
- **Whanganui River iwi:** hosts to a Pākehā poet's commune on their land; whose ideal was it?
- **Golden Bay and the Coromandel:** Pākehā alternative-lifestyle districts since the 1970s.
- **Riverside Community (Moutere):** Christian pacifists living by principle since 1941.
- **Young climate strikers against older voters:** generational ideals measured in survey waves.
- **Pacific church youth:** ideals of service within church and family, and of activism outside them.
- **Farmers and the clean-green ideal:** a group often caught between the national image and its own costs.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for named movements (Parihaka, nuclear-free, communes); thin for private idealism and disillusion, which needs memoirs and oral history.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for Parihaka, Maungapōhatu and the prophets; Moriori sources through Hokotehi; Chinese for the friendship circles.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Parihaka and Maungapōhatu involve state violence and are tied to Treaty settlements and Crown apologies; their teachings are guarded by descendants. Moriori history is contested and painful. Some commune histories involve abuse.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (category; their own visions and movements are not scouted here).
