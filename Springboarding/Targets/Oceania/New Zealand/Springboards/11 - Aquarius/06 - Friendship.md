# New Zealand: Friendship (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (NZ slang), te reo Māori, Samoan, Tongan, Cook Islands Māori, Korean, Chinese. **Concept in one line:** a voluntary, personal bond of liking, loyalty and help between people who are not, or not only, kin or partners.

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Rural Pākehā men:** friendship through the shed, the club and the rugby field; loneliness on isolated farms.
- **Māori whānau networks:** cousins as closest friends; whether "friend" and "whanaunga" separate at all.
- **Samoan and Tongan youth:** church and school friendships, and the line between uso and friend.
- **Cook Islands Māori and Niueans:** small communities where friends and relatives overlap heavily.
- **British and South African migrants:** two large English-speaking groups that report different ease in making local friends.
- **Korean and Chinese international students and families:** friendship inside and across language lines.
- **Older widowers:** the target of men's sheds and visiting schemes.
- **Rainbow and takatāpui communities:** friends as chosen family.
- **Deaf clubs:** friendship organized around NZSL.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: good cohort and youth surveys, a strong literature on Pākehā mateship, but little on women's friendship, Pacific friendship and adult friendship in other languages.
- **Languages needed:** NZ English slang across generations; te reo Māori and the Pacific languages wherever friend and relative share one word; Korean and Chinese for students; NZSL for Deaf clubs.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's loneliness and suicide (handle with current reporting guidelines); cohort-study data are restricted; soldiers' letters may involve living families.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (a category, with nothing named; friendship pointers for them wait on the author).
