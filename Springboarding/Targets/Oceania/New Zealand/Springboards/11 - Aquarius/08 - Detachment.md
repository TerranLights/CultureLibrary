# New Zealand: Detachment (Aquarius): springboard

*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*
*Use: a starting point for research, not its limit. Know why each pointer is here, check it, then follow every relevant lead beyond it (RUNBOOK, "Research from the springboard").*

**Target:** New Zealand, region Oceania. **Language(s) for searching:** English (NZ idiom), te reo Māori; Samoan, Chinese and Korean for family emotional styles; Thai for one Buddhist community. **Concept in one line:** keeping distance (emotional, personal or social) from people, from one's own feelings, or from outcomes. Cultures value some forms of it (calm, objectivity, impartiality, equanimity) and condemn others (coldness, indifference, withdrawal).

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **High-country and back-country men:** the stoic, solitary ideal at its strongest; also where isolation is most dangerous.
- **Māori at tangihanga:** open public grief, against whakamā as private withdrawal; two rules, not one.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** silence before elders as respect, set beside expressive worship.
- **Chinese and Korean immigrant families:** restraint norms between generations, as each community writes about them.
- **Exclusive Brethren:** distance from outsiders written into church teaching on separation, so what to study is a rule, not a temperament.
- **Remote islanders** (Chatham Islands, Stewart Island/Rakiura) and West Coast towns: distance as a fact of life.
- **Journalists, doctors and referees:** detachment as a professional duty.
- **Older people living alone, and young people reporting loneliness:** opposite ends of the age range.
- **Thai and Cambodian Buddhists:** non-attachment as religious teaching inside a migrant community.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** mixed: strong literary and film criticism on Pākehā reserve, good census and turnout data, but little measured on emotional display; Māori emotional concepts need Māori-authored sources.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori for whakamā and waiata; Chinese and Korean for family writing; Thai for the monastery's own materials.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** men's mental health and suicide (follow the media guidelines on suicide reporting); tangihanga practice belongs to each marae; the Brethren rarely speak to outsiders.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (kept to a category: on reserve and solitude among them, nothing is named).
