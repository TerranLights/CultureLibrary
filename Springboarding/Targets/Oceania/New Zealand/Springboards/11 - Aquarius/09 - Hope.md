## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāi Tahu:** its motto names descendants not yet born; set its tribal plans and reports beside that motto.
- **Rātana followers (Rātana Pā, Rangitīkei):** hope carried by a healing church and its yearly January gathering.
- **Māori language whānau:** kōhanga families betting on a language's future.
- **Pacific migrants and seasonal workers** (Samoan Quota; Recognised Seasonal Employer workers from Tonga, Vanuatu and elsewhere): hope tied to work and remittances.
- **Refugee families resettled through Māngere:** a new life as the hope, and what they report finding.
- **West Coast families** of the Pike River miners: hope as waiting for recovery.
- **Christchurch residents:** hope and fatigue during a decade-long rebuild.
- **Young people:** home ownership, climate and the Big OE.
- **Chinese and Korean parents:** a hope expressed as tuition, music lessons and university plans, often the reason for migrating at all.
- **Farmers:** hope tied to weather and prices from season to season.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** likely rich for expectations polling, mental-health inquiry material and the Matariki revival; thin for private religious hope outside the big churches.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori (dialects matter: Kāi Tahu forms); Samoan and Tongan for sermons and radio; Chinese and Korean for parents' hopes.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** suicide and despair (follow suicide-reporting guidelines); Pike River families' grief; Matariki and maramataka knowledge is held by tohunga and iwi, some of it not shared openly. New Zealanders in Australia are a comparison only.
- **Held for review:** South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (as a category alone; the hopes behind their migrations wait for the author's review).
