## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Tūhoe and Ngāti Porou marae:** tangihanga protocols differ by iwi, so the forms of shared weeping and speech to mourners are worth keeping apart.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** vā-based relating may make attunement a duty of position, not a private feeling; test it.
- **Pākehā rural men:** where the pressure to "harden up" meets newer talk of men opening up.
- **Autistic New Zealanders:** whose own testimony tests the "lacks empathy" label.
- **The Deaf community:** face and hand reading as the everyday channel.
- **Mandarin- and Korean-speaking Aucklanders,** first generation against New Zealand-raised: different expectations of what goes unsaid.
- **The Dawn Raids generation and their children:** memory of being misread.
- **Health and care workers:** where empathy becomes paid labor.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in Pacific and Māori writing on relating; mixed for measured empathy (no national empathy scale known *check*); good patient-experience data.
- **Languages needed:** NZSL (visual sources), te reo Māori, Samoan and Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** tangihanga are private family events, studied only through published or shared accounts; Dawn Raids testimony is personal.
- **Held for review:** pointers on South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands (empathy across group lines).
