*Scouting, not research. From the agent's own knowledge; nothing here is verified. Every name is marked *check*.*

## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Porou and the East Coast:** heavy enlistment and the Ngata-era case that service earns equal standing.
- **Waikato-Tainui:** conscription resisted under Te Puea; whose sacrifice the Crown could claim.
- **Taranaki iwi at Parihaka:** sacrifice by non-violent endurance.
- **Moriori of Rēkohu:** a vow against killing kept through invasion.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** tautua and fatongia, service to kin and church as a lifelong giving-up.
- **Seasonal workers' home villages and New Zealand hosts** (Hawke's Bay, Central Otago, Marlborough): who bears the cost of the absence.
- **Defence towns** (Waiouru, Burnham, Linton): military families' own language of sacrifice.
- **Coastal and alpine volunteer-rescue communities:** risk taken for strangers as local custom.
- **Chinese New Zealanders:** goldfield men who sent earnings to Guangdong villages for decades.
- **Quakers and other peace churches:** sacrifice as refusal.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for war service (Cenotaph, battalion records); mixed for caregiving and remittances; thin for Pacific in-family sacrifice in English.
- **Languages needed:** Cantonese for old letters; te reo Māori; Samoan and Tongan.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** Moriori history of the 1835 invasion is painful and contested between peoples; war dead are tapu to whānau; organ donation touches tikanga on the body.
- **Held for review:** migrant sacrifice stories from South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
