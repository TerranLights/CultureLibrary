## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Ngāti Maniapoto and Te Rohe Pōtae:** the only region with a remembered border closed to settlers.
- **Te Tai Tokerau iwi against Te Arawa and Ngāti Porou marae:** kawa on who may speak and where differ; the boundary itself varies.
- **Samoan and Tongan households:** respect avoidance between siblings as a family line.
- **Chinese and Korean Aucklanders in multigenerational homes:** privacy inside the household.
- **Auckland apartment dwellers against rural sheep and beef families:** shared walls against fenced paddocks and distance.
- **501 deportees:** people placed inside a border they did not grow up within.
- **New Zealanders abroad during MIQ:** citizens kept out by their own line.
- **Pākehā suburban households:** the section, the fence and keeping to oneself.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich for marae protocol and recent border policy; mixed for personal space (whether New Zealand sits in the cross-national personal-space samples *check*); thin for Pacific in-home boundaries in English.
- **Languages needed:** Samoan and Tongan for kin avoidance; te reo Māori, including each iwi's kawa vocabulary.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** rohe overlaps tie into live disputes between iwi; rāhui details belong to the iwi that set them. Fences, land title and public access rules go to Libra or Capricorn.
- **Held for review:** household and group lines among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
