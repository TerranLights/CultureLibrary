## 3. Who to compare inside the nation
- **Te Rarawa, Ngāti Kahungunu and other iwi with active maramataka:** each calendar is tied to its own coast and river, so reading signs differs by place.
- **Scots-descended families of Otago and Southland:** second-sight stories brought from the Highlands and Islands.
- **Samoan and Tongan families:** dreams of the dead taken as guidance, set against clinical readings.
- **Sheep, beef and dairy farmers:** stock sense and weather sense from long practice.
- **Commercial and customary fishers** (Hauraki Gulf, Bay of Plenty, Kaikōura): reading sea and birds.
- **Hospital nurses and whānau:** gut worry against protocol.
- **Scientists and mātauranga practitioners:** two traditions of knowing in public dispute.
- **Chinese New Zealanders in Auckland:** siting and numbers in buying homes.

## 4. Flags
- **Data:** rich in te reo and iwi sources for maramataka and tohu, and in hospital safety documents; mixed for belief in psychics (no national poll known *check*); thin for Scots lore after settlement.
- **Languages needed:** te reo Māori, Scottish Gaelic (old letters), Samoan, Tongan, Chinese.
- **Sensitive or restricted:** maramataka and matakite knowledge is held by iwi experts and taught in wānanga; the 2021 science debate is heated; psychic shows touch real victims' families.
- **Held for review:** hunches, signs and luck among South Asian-heritage communities in the nation's lands.
