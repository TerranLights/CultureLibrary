## Do the sheets exploit the nation's depth?
**Mostly yes.**
- **Coverage across the lands:** the sheets work across all four provinces, not only Shanghai and Suzhou. They reach into Huizhou, the Huai plain, Wenzhou, the Zhoushan islands and northern Jiangsu (Xuzhou), and they bring in less-known groups:
  - the *duomin* (堕民) of Shaoxing and Ningbo;
  - the nine-surname boat people (九姓渔民);
  - the Hangzhou, Nanjing and Zhenjiang banner garrisons;
  - the Shanghai Jewish refugees.
- **Pre-modern material** is strong throughout: Wu-Yue histories, Huizhou documents and lineages, Wang Yangming and the Zhedong school, Kunqu, the Jiangnan examination culture, and Song to Qing charity estates.
- **Search terms:** 128 sheets give Wu-language terms (Shanghainese, Suzhou, Wenzhou and so on), 69 give Classical Chinese, 61 give She terms, and 59 give Hui or Arabic and Persian terms.

**Weak spots:**
- **Mandarin and Hui dialects:** Jianghuai Mandarin terms (central Jiangsu and Anhui) appear in only 6 sheets, and Huizhou Chinese in only 9. That is thin for half of Anhui and Jiangsu.
- **Southern Min:** the Southern Min of Cangnan and Pingyang has no search terms in any sheet and is a compare group in only 8.
- **Romanization:** few Wu terms say which romanization they use (11 sheets).
- **She terms:** these are mostly given in Chinese characters for She words, with *check*. That is reasonable, since She has no standard script.
